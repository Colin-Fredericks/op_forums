                It has taken a little longer for me to come up with my take on the Perfected Kata Bracers than I intended, but here is what I have come up with.

                Perfected Kata Bracers (Artifact ••••)

                It has long been forgotten who forged the first of these bracers. While each reflects the Master who made them, they also reflect the simplicity of the first of their kind. Each takes the form of a prayer strip made from one of the magical materials (except Starmetal) that have been twisted to fit around the forearms or calves of an individual. Engraved into each are a series of sutras that have been inlaid with Starmetal. Sidereal Exalts are always considered to resonate with these bracers regardless of what material they are made from (as all of them use Starmetal). Attunement costs is 8 motes.

                Evocations of the Perfected Kata Bracers

                Blossoming of the Lotus
                Cost: 1wp; Mins: Essence 1
                Type: Supplemental
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Indefinite
                Prerequisites: None

                When the wearer of these bracers uses the Form Charm of a Martial Art, they can activate this Evocation at the cost of 1 willpower. Doing so allows the wearer to access the effects of the Mastery Keyword for any Charm within the style of the Form they are in for as long as they remain in that Form. Additionally, while this Evocation is active, the wearer may ignore any instance of the Terrestrial Keyword for any charm in their current Form’s style that might apply to the wearer.

                For those Exalts that can naturally access the effects of the Mastery Keyword, this Evocation instead allows the wearer to draw upon the natural Essence of the world around them to reduce the cost of all Charms within the style of the Form Charm by 2 motes (to a minimum of 1 mote per effect).

                When this Evocation is active, the Starmetal sutras glow with a bight light that forces the wearer’s Anima to flare to the Burning level of display. Should the wearer activate a Charm with an Anima cost that reduces the Anima display to the Dim level for longer than the end of their next turn, this Evocation’s effect ends and the wearer looses any benefit of their Form Charm until such time as they can activate it again. Otherwise, this Evocation lasts for as long as they remain under the effect of their current Form Charm (switching forms requires this Evocation to be activated again to gain its benefits with the new style’s charms).

                This Evocation is unlocked at no cost the first time an attuned wearer enters a Martial Arts Form.

                Perfected Kata Strike
                Cost: (Essence) motes (+1 wp); Mins: Essence 1
                Type: Simple
                Keywords: Decisive-Only
                Duration: Instant
                Prerequisites: Blossoming of the Lotus

                This Evocation allows the wearer to enhance an unarmed or style weapon strike with extraordinary precision while using a Form Charm. When the wearer prepares to finish off their opponent, they add their Essence rating to the Accuracy and Damage of a Decisive attack. The wearer can choose to do either Bashing or Lethal damage regardless of what damage type their attack normally does. If the wearer is fighting a Creature of Darkness (or a Fair Folk), they can spend 1 Willpower to make the damage done Aggravated.

                Special activation rules: This Evocation awakens at no cost when the wielder, who must have been attuned for at least one story, is engaged with a Creature of Darkness (or Fair Folk) with an Essence rating higher than theirs (maximum of 5) and defeats that foe or emerges from Initiative Crash that the foe caused them to suffer.



                Not entirely sure how well the second evocation compares with the effects of the Charms that a given Martial Artist will have thanks to the effects of the first evocation... so I may still tweek it as needed to better balance it (as in it is an alternative strike rather than a superior one). That it can deal Aggravated damage (to Creatures of Darkness and Fair Folk) I think makes it something a player would consider in some circumstances as don't recall many Martial Art Charms allowing for such damage in most uses. I may give it the Dual Keyword instead of the Decisive-Only and have the Decisive effect deal Aggravated damage regardless of the opponent... but in that case I think the cost of using the Evocation will need to be higher, at least when a Decisive attack is made. Might also increase the Essence minimum as well. As it is, it is intended to be more effective the more experienced the Exalt is and thus more costly to use (one can not choose to pay less for a lesser effect... it is all or nothing). Additionally, since the Evocation isn't part of any Style, it will not benefit from the reduced Charm cost that natural Masters get when using the first Evocation's effects.

                As you can see, overall this Artifact is useful to anyone who acquires it, whether they are natural Martial Art Masters or not... and does provide clear play style that many Exalt types would not normally have (specifically Mastery Keyword effects). One does have some hurdles to overcome to awaken the evocations, but the balancing point is the cost of using the Evocations rather than more hurdles to overcome to gain the rewards of owning so powerful an artifact.

                            I was honestly debating whether or not to treat the effects as innate abilities or as evocations. I opted for expressing them as evocations only because there are too few artifacts that have innate abilities that are powered through Essence (or Willpower), especially when they have more than one effect. Since I wanted to keep the feel of the original inspiration and add the secondary effect that allows access to the Mastery Keyword, evocations seemed the best way to display that.

                            That said, whether they actually are Evocations or just expressed through a similar mechanic is a matter of debate. While no two artifacts are exactly alike, quite a few have been copied and their effects replicated. True, most of these are of the 2 Dot variety, but the fairly simplistic design of the Perfected Kata Bracers suggests that they not difficult to make overall, but they do have to be... well... perfect. The reason they are considered 4 dots is due to the exacting precision needed to produce them... even the slightest flaw could ruin months, if not years of work making one (and very likely can only be constructed by those who are natural Masters of the Martial Arts)... plus the effects are substantial (though whether they are powerful is a subjective decision).

                            Even so, they do have limitations... namely that the effects only work when one is using a Form Charm and even then the effects only apply to charms that belong in the same Style as that Form Charm. In other words, you can't access the Mastery effects of Tiger Style when you are in Snake Form... or the Mastery effects of Snake Style when in in Tiger Form. That changing from one Form to another requires one to pay the Willpower cost to access the Mastery effects will put some limits on how powerful the user will be. Sure, they may still freely combine Martial Art charms as always, but they will only be able to benefit from the Mastery effects of the charms from the style they are currently in Form for. Lastly... glowing like a beacon for all to see will tend to make one a target of interest for most groups... and will likely be perceived as a threat by most opponents. This means that they are more likely to be attacked by multiple opponents (with the bonuses and penalties there in applying).

                            In short, it will be up to the Storyteller any player alike to take full advantage of everything these Bracers provide... both mechanically and from a role-playing perspective. It will be a balancing act of course, but when done right it will create situations where the player can truly shine (though when done wrong it can leave the player either feeling overwhelmed and unduly burdened or just not properly challenged). It is also very likely that at some point, other Martial Artists will learn of their ability and seek to challenge the owner... especially if they are suspicious of what they have in their possession. Basically, those who possess such an artifact are likely to lead some rather interesting lives.

                                                I do understand that my brainstorming of ideas was a little scattered and unrefined... which could easily lead to false impressions of intent. Brainstorming is kind of like that... I am just trying to figure out the effects I would like for the artifact, then once I know what I am after, then I figure out how to balance the effects so that the effort of getting it equals the benefit gained from it. Not sure how do describe this approach, but it works for me.

                                                Yes, I wasn't sure that going with Evocations was the right choice, but looking at the available artifacts so far, any that offer more than a single effect tend to present those effects as Evocations as opposed to innate effects.

                                                The Solar and Sidereal effect is designed to allow Exalts that have natural access to Mastery effects to still find some use from this artifact. Given the Anima flare that is produced when this effect is active, it made sense that they might be drawing on the natural Essence of the world to power their Charms... at least the ones that belong to the same Style as the Form they are in. It is a powerful effect, but with this limitation (plus the role-play opportunity of flare) I felt it was balance.

                                                As for why I allow the wielder a chance to flare their Anima after use is to allow them to maintain the effect without having to repay all the relevant costs. Remember, as stated in the evocation's description, not only is the Mastery effect lost, but the character is also forced out of their Form Charm (the commitment to the charm is released) when the effect ends. Most effects of this nature tend to allow a character the chance to re-establish the condition that maintains the effect before it is lost, so as not to be overly punishing to the player. To automatically end the effect without a chance to maintain it would tend to result in players not using charms with Anima costs as the Willpower cost (not to mention the significant mote cost of most Form Charms) is high (willpower doesn't regenerate during combat to the same degree that Essence motes do). Thus, I felt giving a character until the end of their next turn (which is consistent with a lot of charms that require conditions to maintain) was fair. There may not be many Martial Art charms that have anima costs, but they do exist and thus a player using that style should not feel like they need to avoid those charms of their chosen style to maintain this effect.

                                                Besides, just because they have the chance to flare their anima to maintain the effect doesn't mean they have to. This provides them with a choice, where not allowing them this chance takes away any choice. It is up to the player to decide whether or not to take the opportunities given to them.

                                                It is because of the Willpower cost that I am allowing this evocation to both nullify the applicability of the Terrestrial keyword and to allow access to the Mastery keyword. Unlike mere Motes, Willpower is a very finite resource for players and doesn't regenerate with each round of combat like Essence motes do. It is best described as the Martial Artist focusing a lot of their power into manifesting this effect... sort of like how many Anime fighters are powering up (even causing an anima flare effect too)... and should do more than what simple motes would accomplish. Given that to date only one type of Exalt is even affected by the Terrestrial keyword (though we can presume that weaker Exigents are likely subject to this limitation as well), I don't see what they need an extra hoop to jump through when thematically, it doesn't add much to the experience except make things a little harder for the player playing a slightly weaker character. The limitations of playing a character subject to the Terrestrial Keyword is already enough of a limitation, as they likely have smaller Essence pools to work with to begin with. I generally come at my designs from the perspective of what will be fun to play... not how to make something cool hard to attain just because of the player's choice of character splat.

                                                The trade off is when a character can use Mastery effects (only while in a Form Charm's effect) and which effects are applicable (only charms from the style they are in the Form of). For characters who only know a single Martial Art, this isn't a problem as they are only likely to use that style anyways. This is a limitation in and of itself... one that opponents could potentially exploit (and any Storyteller worth their salt will find ways to exploit the flaws of a single combat strategy should it make sense to do so). In short, the character that relies on a single style becomes predictable to a degree. Now for characters that learn multiple styles, this still limits their access to Mastery effects (well... at least for non-Solars and Sidereal) as they now need to choose which style has the Mastery effects they want to take advantage of. They are still free to mix and match Martial Art charms as they see fit, but will only benefit from the Mastery of a single style's charms at any given time. It really does come down to the player choosing how they wish to play... and the consequences of their choices. A player is more likely to accept the consequences of their own actions when they had the freedom to choose how they hang themselves as opposed to not having a choice in what happens. More hoops to jump through to get more evocations for relatively minor benefits (a lot of Mastery effects are not substantially powerful... significant yes, but not overly powerful)

                                                You don't add a Weapon's accuracy bonus to Decisive attacks... but you can still add dice through Charms to the accuracy of a Decisive Attack (all this means is adding dice to the attack roll). A Dex 5 Martial Arts 5 character is still rolling a base attack pool of 10, even if they are not getting the +3 accuracy bonus from their Reaper Daiklave... and can still increase this pool by Charms (or Excellencies). Perhaps rephrasing it to say that it adds to the attack roll rather than to accuracy would be a better choice of words. I did choose to make the evocation Simple to limit the amount of stacking possible... you got that part right... this is meant to represent the finishing move of a battle... a Kamehameha if you will. I fully expect players to try to make it as powerful as they can, as it is thematically appropriate to do so (though within normal limits as I will specify the added dice are considered to be Charm dice). I see this as a fun little option that I want players to take advantage of... thus I wanted it to be an attractive option among other attractive options they might have access to. Of course the player doesn't manage to finish the fight with this attack (or any other attack they might try), then I will do my best to go full Freeza on their ass should the opportunity arise (and from time to time it has).

                                                As for the ability to combine other charms with Martial Arts, even Lunars do have a degree of limitations in this respect (not as much as Ability based Exalts mind you). That these additional charms will not benefit from the Bracers and will tax the Lunar's resources for combat (motes, willpower, initiative, anima) will place limitations of what they can achieve even with access of Mastery. Of course they can blow all their motes, willpower, imitative and even anima into a single powerful attack (any Exalt an do so in theory), but the consequences of doing so should be obvious. Any player who does that quickly learns that it may look cool, but often costs them the battle as they put too much power into a single effort when they should have been pacing themselves. Combat is a marathon where one needs to dole out their power over the long run rather than spend it all at once as if it were a sprint or a dash. I am not worried about players trying to make one powerful attack because only the inexperienced and foolish make that mistake more than once (and I take full advantage of such mistakes when players make them, to show them why such tactics are not the best course of action). I think this is one case where we are coming at things from two very different perspectives... you seem afraid that players might abuse an effect and try to make it so the effect can't be abused. I however approach things from the perspective that players will find a way to abuse any mechanic, thus I let them show me their exploit and then show them why it may not be the advantage they think it is... I let them be the powerful demi-gods they are and let the consequences of often ill thought out plans show them their error rather than try (and likely fail) to reign in their power. Yes, there are times when they actually found an exploit that is both powerful and has few drawbacks or consequences for me to exploit in return... but if those turn out to be fun for everyone (I love it when players discover something I overlooked, even if it is often frustrating) we may keep it open. If it becomes a problem we work together to deal with it... sort of what you and I are doing now... so that it is something everyone can enjoy without anyone feeling like they are being punished for their creativity.

                                                To be honest though, your version of the Bracers was definitely inspirational... I saw what you were going for... and how costly it would be for characters using it. I may not agree with that approach, but it did help me refine the concept I was going for. You probably don't agree with a lot of my reasoning for the costs and benefits I put into my version of the Bracers... which is fine (to be honest I am not sure my reasoning is entirely sound at times), but that is what I like about these forums... most people can respectfully disagree and discuss their perspectives and hopefully come to an agreement... even if it just to agree to disagree. I think your example has been the most help I got as it actually provided me what I needed to know, even if I didn't agree with the direction you took. Different people will approach the same problem from different angles... and that can give a person insight they may not have considered from their own perspective. That was definitely the case here.
