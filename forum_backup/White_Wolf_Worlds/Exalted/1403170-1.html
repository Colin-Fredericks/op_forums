        A good general rule I tend to use is to face the players with combat encounters at least as powerful as them, and then do two things:

        1) grapple. In my experience, nothing scares the players as much as being grappled and then having other minions come over and try to kill deliver killing blows. This also gives great opertunities for the other players to rescue them, which can make for some interesting player and character dynamics with the whole "I saved you" VS "I totally had that under control" fun stuff

        2) you don't have to kill them outright... I don't recall the exact wording of the rules, but maiming blows are still a thing in 3rd, right? Instead of delivering a killing blow, chop off an arm or a leg. Or perhaps simply have the opponent disarm the player and demand their surrender. Similarly to the first point, basically put them in a position where the others have to save them.


        And ya, ambushes with put traps that forcibly separate them if they dont have athlethtics or fast crafting charms to dig their way out, those work great for this. If the players rely too much on magical or demonic transportation, have a sorcerer lie in ambush and counterspell or banish their transport out from under them. that's a great way to initiate a battle.

                Originally posted by limaxophobiac View Post

                The Rampharts sinkhole creates difficult terrain, not a death-pit. Reshape Battlefield resculpts the battlefield "before that battle" and there is no enemy battle group for the Earth Aspect to target with either effect so that's no help here.



                Per+Athletics -3. If anyone's sitting at average Per and no Athletics excellency even with a stunt and a WP they are not unlikely to eat the full 4 auto lethal and 7 dice bashing, after which they probably eat the bonfire due to wound penalties and then are out in short order.

                Being incapacitated in your sleep and then getting to watch the other players fight the big climatic battle without you sounds like a bummer, so I would personally try to avoid it as a possibility. If everyone's rocking Athetics(F) 5 it's another story.

                Also while I get wanting to cut down on rolling for noticing the hazard and then a second roll to avoid the damage, having Awarness play no part in noticing the hazard feels a bit arbitrary.
                "The players are all well spec'd for fighting"

                Exalted isn't a proscriptive RPG. Though Vance has clarified this is not the intent, I'd allow the use of Reflexive Charms which make sense. If you've got, say, the Dodge Charm Reflex Sidestep Technique (which lets you dodge attacks and traps you didn't see coming), it would be not unreasonable to cancel the penalty for being asleep -- even though it's not a Dodge roll and Evasion doesn't apply. Or allow the Awareness Charm Surprise Anticipation Method (which helps stop you being taken by surprise) to apply to the roll. Or let a Resistance Supernal Stunt how she toughs out the fall with Adament Skin Technique (which can stop uncountable damage from scenery effects). Or if someone is awake -- either keeping watch, or rising before sunrise -- they don't take the -3, and could even wake the others perhaps.

                Willpower and a level 2 Stunt gives you two successes and reduces the penalty to -1. (That's a 40% chance of success with Perception 2 and literally nothing else.) A noticeably athletic person of average perception (3+2) gets at least 3 successes 87% of the time.

                If that's still too difficult for your party then lower the difficulty. Or let them have it as Wits+Dodge or Dexterity+Awareness or some other "guarenteed 11" pool. Rule that their beds break the fall, reducing the guaranteed damage to Bashing and eliminate the rolled damage. This is just an idea, scale it to suit.

                The point of the fall is to soften them up. Either by getting them to spend precious motes and willpower or by giving them wound penalties. That's how the Wyld Hunt operates. Because the PCs are going to be basically unstoppable in any fair fight.

                Also Crippling injuries are a thing. Take one instead of being put out of the final fight.

                If the bonfire hazard is a problem, have the pitch burn out, or encourage the PCs to put the fire out with a Gambit. Or make the pit have patches of bonfire, but reduce the overall hazard to sandstorm levels.

                This is just one idea on how to challenge 3 combat invested Solars with two Dragon-Blooded.

                The list of Charms given in a splat book is not the sum total of all known magic the splat is capable of. The Dragon-Blooded can move earth around. Digging a fifty foot pit doesn't strike me as beyond the Princes of the Earth, whether there's an opposing battlegroup or not.

                Originally posted by limaxophobiac View Post
                I'm calling it heavy handed based on that the encounter mostly boils down to if everyone has the Athletics excellency or not, which yes most exalted tend to end up having but it's crazy punishing if you don't.
                Or a way of mitigating scenery damage.
                Or Ox Bodies.
                Or use your Crippling Injury.
                Or have Solar Medicine patch you up.

                A starting Solar can have 22+ health levels. Losing ~8 right off the bat isn't a death sentence. With Stamina 3 and two Ox Bodies, a Solar is still in their -2s. It is entirely within the realm of possibility that a Medium range fall can leave a starting Solar in their -0s.

                Then the ST decides whether they Decisive kill the PCs or use Gambits to take them alive (or just Wither them some more).

                I get that if you're playing Exalted as intended, with Tomb of Dreams style characters, then sure this is pretty harsh. But against reasonable optimized PCs... I don't see it.

                But your input is noted. This is just my idea, I'm not even the ST here.

                How would you run the encounter?

                        Session is tomorrow, so my/the villain's current plan is a variant of JohnDoe's devilry. The Air aspect issue a challenge, a three on three battle for the fate of the island, with the promise of executing lots of innocent bystanders if the PCs delay or try anything funny. He'll meet them in the town's execution square where the campaign began, where criminals/anyone who looks at him funny are locked in a pot and drowned by the incoming tide.

                        He'll be standing at the head of the square, along with the Earth aspect and a couple guards dressed as Immaculate Monks. As soon as the players appear, the Earth aspect will collapse the entire square, dropping the players through a short fall (5 levels of blunt damage, - 1/success on a Dex-Athletics roll) and into bondfires, with the golem battle group surrounding them. Meanwhile, the Air aspect will make ranged attacks from the edge of the pit, the Immaculate MONK battle group will rain poisoned arrows (using poison arrow frog venom), and the Earth aspect will throw boulders at anyone trying to climb out.

                        And if the fight goes on too long, the tide will start coming in...

                        All of this will be set up before the challenge is issued, so the players will have a chance to figure it out and turn the tables.

                                    Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                    Sounds neat. Let us know how it goes.
                                    Well, it didn't work out quite like how I expected (surprise).

                                    Last session, the party had kidnapped a noble and offered to ransom her for her weight in silver. The Dragonblooded baddie decided to try and turn the tables, disguising himself and his elites as common soldiers and bringing them all to the exchange (with the golems as the "only significant escort"). The players got warned by a spy they'd placed in his household, and skipped the meeting in favor of raiding their foes' barracks and defeating all the normal soldiers there.

                                    This session, then, they rushed back to try and catch the Dragonblooded on his way back, maybe manage some divide-and-conquer. The Air aspect had given up on his disguise at this point, but I think they forgot about the monks and Earth aspect, because they promptly started a fight-- despite the fact that the Eclipse in particular was low on motes. One brutal point-blank-range slog and two collapsed buildings later, the monks and both Dragonblooded were dead, the golems frozen without further orders, and the Eclipse and Dawn both sitting at 6 health levels of damage.

                                    In retrospect, maybe they weren't quite as well built for combat as I thought, but it was a pretty epic boss battle. The Eclipse freaked the hell out when the Air aspect started throwing lightning at her, which was delightful. (She then caught a bolt on her daiklave and redirected it straight up into the sky.)

                                    Many thanks to you guys for your help. I might not have been able to use JohnDoe's plan, but the discussion here made me a lot more confident about what three Solars can handle-- I definitely used charms and tactics a lot more ruthlessly than I might have otherwise, and the session was all the better for it.

                                    (edit: Also highly successful, for whatever it's worth: the players-roll-all-the-dice houserules I was talking about a little bit ago. I've been using them throughout the campaign, but they really proved their worth tonight. Four completely different enemies on the battlefield, and I had no more trouble than I would have running the encounter in, say, 5e D&D. And my goodness do the turns flow more smoothly)
