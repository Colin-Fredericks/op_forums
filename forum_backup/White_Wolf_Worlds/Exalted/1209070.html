    Some Storytelling Advice

    04-19-2018, 08:33 PM
    Alright, so I've mused about this elsewhere but I figured long form thinking it out and asking for help would be best on the forum.
    So, in my current Gothic Horror Mystery Game, one of the three big bads is an old and isolated sorcerer who has made contact with entities from the Upper Ocean(the region of the stars, that shields creation from the full brunt of the sun). He's engaged in research of prior worshipers of the Lovecraftian creatures that lurk in that strange sea, and has been conducting experiments on fusing these creatures with human beings to create an elevated form of life (at request of the local king, who wants an edge over a local rival dragonblooded nation), including himself. The eventual plan, plot wise, is for him to introduce more widespread mutation and terror in the capital itself.
    I've been considering whether this scope is something that should be kept as a unique entity--because of his contact with powerful and ancient spirits having warped him in someway, he's been turned into a stranger thing, a complete unknown or unique being in Exalted--or if he should be a Solar (the party is currently an Abyssal, 2 exigents, a lunar and a dragonblooded) to play off his nature as a fundamentally human pursuit of power and insight.
    Does anyone have any thoughts?

    For perspective, the other big bads are a circle of Lunar revolutionaires, and an infernal pirate crew plotting a coup.

                          I'd make him a Solar. I don't think making him Not A Solar is a bad idea either, but I notice a distinct lack of Solars mentioned in your game, most notably in the PC group. With no Solar Heroes, this is a great chance to make a Solar Villain whose reveal might be a genuine surprise, when they push him to his limit and he becomes a blasphemous flailing tentacle monster aflame with Ultimate Power. Your Abyssals will hate him extra-hard if/when he starts hypocritically rebuking them as CoD, especially if he threatens them with anything Holy. Do they have any respect for the UCS? Because this guy makes a complete and utter mockery of His divine authority.

                          Edit: Actually, how does your table feel about Solars, like, in general? I've been drilling it into my players that Solars are the very definition of Power for like 10 years but almost never make a Solar NPC, so when I do they shit their pants a little because they know it potentially means the biggest threat possible. But that's a veteran Exalted group. Newer players might not have a long-time respect for Solars as more than the standard PC choice. If that's closer to your group, then maybe don't make it a Solar.

                              re: Solars and Holy

                              The "hypocrisy" of a malevolent Solar using Holy vs an Abyssal (who one presumes isn't trying to murder everything) can be a good idea. It hammers home that Holy isn't "Lawful Good" and is instead more like "my daddy's the mayor - the cops are arresting you, not me". And truth be told, while I avoid Solars, they do make fantastic demonologists (precisely because of Holy - it isn't just combat - there were Holy tagged social charms, meaning a Solar has more mental/emotional sway over demons than others).

                              Hell, if you wanted to be "cruel" about it, play up to Theion-styled "big ball of glorious light flame" when Holy is used. It's less "I'm brow-beating you into subservience" and more "I remind you guys a lot of what you once were..."

                              re: Bonfires

                              Irrelevant! You don't need to be a Twilight to be a sorceror or dabble with horrors. A Night Caste could suppress their anima over and over.

                              Or introduce a hearthstone or item to hide it. There some Fire-themed Hearthstones that gave you a faux Fire-Aspect anima banner (great for masquerading along). By extension, I'm not saying "pretend to be a Fire-Aspect" but merely "don't feel like you NEED to reveal your anima banner".

                                              Ah, the change in Editions, lol.

                                              The point remains (somewhat). In Glories of the Most High - Sol - one of the problems it brings up is that Sol Invictus is of such extreme Virtue, that Virtues naturally contradict one another. Therefore, he has to suppress them.

                                              For example, Compassion compels him to save the world - but he suppresses that for a scene to go hide in the Jade Pleasure Dome. Awesome, he's no longer faced with suffering, so he doesn't have to suppress Compassion - but now Temperance is likely suppressed by virtue to his own addiction to the Games of Divinity. Etc, etc, etc, etc.

                                              IE, Sol isn't always consistent.

                                              Plus the Roll of Glorious Divinity has an ambassador to Maleas/YuShan god who has proven loyal for a few centuries.

                                              Plus - in 3E, Twilights explicitly have that new ability to bind a demon or elemental as a Familiar. Hardly "Sol disapproves", no?

                                              Ergo
                                              • Perhaps the Twilight was chosen for legit occult expertise? Only after (X) event later did one obsession follow another and they eventually went Lovecraftean.
                                              • Not all horrors are inhuman. Going to 2E, Malfeas' charms imply a permanent PTSD-nightmare with awareness charms "only good enough" to let you know a possible threat is nearby. Adorjan's compassion is crazy. Theion did conviction. The Ebon Dragon shuns Virtue as She Who Lives In Her Name takes them to impersonal extremes.
                                                • Malfeas and Cearr are a great example - the signature Slayer was literally Chosen after a battlefield where his whole tribe was exterminated between 2 larger armies and a monster. PTSD really fits. The day he gets "Nightmare Fugue Vigilance", he'll never sleep - but also gets permanent-nightmares as he's awake.
                                                • Adorjan has another example in her compassion charms - she can literally make herself "love' you (and in that, become better at teaching, helping or hurting you). The Twilight could be a victim who earned her unfortunate affections and was warped thereafter.
                                              Etc, etc.

                                              My mention of Holy for Demonology is less "Sol picked a Yozi cultists and gave him power" and more "Sol picked (X) hero who - upon realizing they could brow-beat demons with ease - ended up among them more often". I've built a few "pro-Demon" Exalts (Eclipses, Night, etc) - not loyal to the Yozis or anything - but merely prone to working with demons more often (since my charms guaranteed obedience). They stayed "on the good side" but had Adorjan showed up with charm-set, I wouldn't be surprised if they were wooed over.
