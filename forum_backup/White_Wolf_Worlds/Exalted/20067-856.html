                                                    Harmonious Academic Methodology is probably your best "learn fast" charm in Lore right now, since it lets you expand your Lore background (that is, the area you've studied) automatically every story, and when you succeed at a "moderately challenging" roll to introduce or challenge a fact.                                                             Originally posted by Frostav View Post
                                                            The situation I was thinking about was pretty extreme--a Twilight from "regular" Creation meeting with the Twilights of Gunstar Autochthonia (it's a long story) and having to cram super hard to learn all the advanced Essence science they used regularly. I thought that was a situation extreme enough that even a Twilight like her (who has like, Lore 7 Int 8) would have to use charms to speed up learning all of it.
                                                            Well, nobody ever really knows all of a science; that's why we need lots of scientists.

                                                            The sitcom Scrubs has not exactly aged well to me, but I'm still compelled by the moment where the crotchety old chief of medicine points out that he needs to regularly attend conferences to get updates on the latest knowledge because even somebody who's only been out of medical school five years learned a whole bunch of stuff that has been rendered obsolete.

                                                            See also PhD studies, which are less about raw information and more about ways of thinking and researching.

                                                            However, with that in mind, it's probably not really necessary for the character to be taught how to learn things, it sounds like they've already strongly internalised that. If introduced to base principles, provided with a suitable reference catalogue and given a research project to apply to, that's a more effective way of acquiring such technical knowledge than just cramming a lot of facts.

                                                            I think these things can make for the basis of an ongoing and compelling narrative more effective than just the idea of holing up in a room with a pile of books that are memorised and now you're an expert, not least because memorisation is not really necessary for developing expertise; everybody consults stuff (and colleagues) while they're working on something.

                                                            Harmonious Academic Methodology plays its part in making the expansion of such a knowledge base a more certain and slightly mystical thing than it might be assumed to be for mortals, but the important part should be interaction with other characters, making Lore rolls to introduce facts at appropriate times, engaging in projects with the subject that ought to consist of answering questions by raising new interesting ones that the chronicle can continue to work on answering (preferably in a form that characters have to go out and deal with rather than just sit in a study theorising about).
