    Being able to take the shape of gods and demons gives you a palette well outside what exists in nature, which has benefits if you want something that is hard to justify existing in the animal kingdom. It also assists in impersonation, despite being less useful than impersonating a person (especially fun if you keep material demons around, and can assume their shape). That said, it would work a hell of a lot better if you assumed that some abilities like "is constantly on fire" are natural effects rather than charms.

                Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                I suppose it's possible that just as every animal is designed with the idea in mind that a Lunar is going to turn into it, it's possible to design every spirit in the game with the same idea.

                I like the idea of a GENERAL Knack which allows you to inherit the artifact-attunements of any creature you kill, so you can instantly use their daiklaive. I figure this would be packaged together with the effect of stealing someone's Fate/Destiny, keeping their star from dimming in the sky and thwarting that avenue of Sidereal investigation.


                BUT -- is a demon sufficiently different from a god in terms of Lunar advantage that you should need two different Knacks for their forms? Demons are like gods in being naturally immaterial, capable of hearing prayers, having panoplies, and being immortal.

                Would either be sufficiently different from elementals, which are like mortals in being naturally material and incapable of hearing prayers?

                What about raksha or ghosts, which have odd relationships with materiality and cannot and can hear prayers?

                Well all of them with have their own unique powers. There are bound to be similarities, but they work differently on principle. Say you have the shape of a god that can heal using Presence, and you have the shape of a sesseljae who can heal with Craft. Both heal, but do it differently, and may have other side-effects or bonuses.

                    Maybe it's stupid, but I think that the best way to both give Lunars the power and flexibility they are supposed to possess, and balance things mechanically, is for them to be able to purchase the personal charms/abilities possessed by beings whose Heart's Blood they possess, with appropriate surcharges and/or limitations to avoid the whole "Eclipsoid" issue. Such as, for instance, Exalted charms drawing upon the Exaltation, of which the Lunar only possesses her own. Or certain Panoply charms being invested in a divine portfolio or spiritual dominion, and thus only remains accessible so long as no one else claims it.

                    You kill a Tyrant Lizard, you get to transform into a Tyrant Lizard. But if you want the full benefits of being a Tyrant Lizard, you have to invest the time and effort to master that form. Same goes whether you want to become a Storm Serpent or Ahlat or Octavian; there are no shortcuts to power.

                    Aretii: "As one of the players described it, 'After initiation, it was actually a relief seeing who had eyepatches or bandaged hands - you know what they sacrificed, instead of wondering what bits people cut out of their own souls to gain power.'"

                                    Heart-Theft of the Behemoth doesn't actually make sense.

                                    "Behemoth" is not a species. Mount Mostah and Proto Puma Prime and Arad the Hunter are not related.

                                    "Behemoth" is a catch-all term for the miscellaneous pile; those creatures who are entirely unique, being neither a member of a demon species, nor an elemental species, nor a god, or a raksha, or a goblin...

                                    If Lunars can learn one Knack to become essentially anything regardless of classification, then that one Knack would be able to substitute for any other form-acquisition Knack. Because if both Mostah and Prime become valid targets with one Knack, then that one Knack should surely be able to handle blood apes and Dragon Kings.

                                                            I agree that being able to blend in is an asset, especially in games where your ST is very serious about the wyld hunt. However what is common and unassuming in one local may not be so in another. A camel totem blends into the Southern territories just fine, but in the frigid north draws undo attention.
