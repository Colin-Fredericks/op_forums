        Anyway when I ran Ex3 I would typically break down the army by sections. You'd have the Infantry, you'd have the Elites, you'd have Archers, you'd have Cavalry.

        That could give you a Legion/Brigade sized force. And I'd say if you have two Divisions fighting each other with the PCs as part of it, then you only need to stat up the Brigade/Battalion level parts where the PCs are.

        During my campaign, the PC General controlled five Legions. Another PC accompanied one of those, which happened to be fighting at a certain point in the Battle. That Legion, and it's commander, and it's particular foes, got dice rolled in the battle system etc. The other four Legions were "off-screen" as it were, I rolled opposed command checks, and used them with adjustments for force multipliers etc to tell me who was winning.

        TLDR you only really need to worry about the section of the battle that a PC is directly involved in and in almost all situations that'll never be more than a few thousand men vs a few thousand men. I'd then break that down by unit type down to a maximum of 4-5 units, with a lot of them getting a cursory "I'm going to assume this unit commander always gets X successes and I don't need much more than that" just to speed bits up.

            Troops of different types should probably be in different battle groups, especially if it's relevant to the battle strategy. Like, if the general has 200 hundred archers whose job is to attack from range when the enemy advances, 1000 conscripts who are only really there to die or prevent the enemy from getting within melee range of the archers, and 100 elite troops who are expected to actually win the battle, it's better to have three battle groups because they have very distinct equipment, training, and rolls during the battle.

            If you just want an army for the PCs to fight, you can treat it as one battle group and say there are elite officers commanding the conscripts or archers shooting arrows from the back of the army without giving them separate mechanical effects.

                                                Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                                                My complaint was specifically about very disparate weapons, per the reference to BT infantry rules. I would break an extreme long-range attacker into a different unit from the close combat skirmishers, is what I'm saying. Doctrinally, the archers and slingers are going to be a way smaller group than the bulk of an Imperial or Lookshy legion, like a Size 2 auxiliary that accompanies the main Size 5 unit.
                                                I generally wouldn't.

                                                If a Realm force of 250 Heavy Infantry, 500 Light Infantry and 250 archers fights an Ice Walker force of 800 Light Infantry and 200 mounted slingers then I'd run each side as a single battle group.

                                                That number of troops is likely spread out over multiple range bands anyway, and their long range vs close combat capabilities are (more-or-less) balanced against each other. I might just knock two dice from their ranged attacks maybe (only if it became relevant because of PC actions).

                                                Likewise if it's 30 archers and two spearfighters vs 40 swordfighter then I don't think the two speaefighters are meaningfully contributing enough to the fight to justify a second battlegroup. Just stunt it.

                                                Looping right back to the OP though... if your army is 100 pressganged peasants with sicles and scythes and 12 elite super soliders geared out with micro howitzers, powered armor and airstriking drones vs a horde of 2000 horse archers... then it MIGHT be worth breaking your forces into two battlegroups.

                                                I probably still wouldn't. The 100 conscripts can be run as a diceless background element for you to Stunt with. Unless there's a PC with really good War Charms who is able to stack magical bonuses on the conscripts until they are just as deadly as the super soliders... but then if the two units are just as good as each other, I'd be sorely tempted to just run them as a single, larger battlegroup.

                                                Unless you are personally friends with the archers, and every one who falls in battle will be a meaningful, scaring loss, whereas your spearfighters are disposable human shields, the verisimilitude gained by breaking up close and long range attacks simply isn't worth it. In my opinion, your milage may vary.
