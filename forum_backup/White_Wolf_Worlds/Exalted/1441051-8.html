                                    To go back to the original thread topic, and about DB marriage specifically: remember for lengthy periods of human history in a number of cultures, marriage was (a) mostly a thing nobility did and (b) mostly about inheritance and alliances. That's not to say other people didn't couple up and form family units, but even then you might rely on matchmakers to find a spouse. It's something that still happens in Japan today, although it's a small minority of marriages now. (Heck, if we want to be extremely generous, what are OKCupid and the like other than modern-day matchmakers?)

                                    Still, not marrying for love (or at least, not marrying due to love) is not the same thing as "force two people who will not ever get along together", but it's also why even the current 3e Realm probably has more male-female couples who, if their orientations and preferences lie outside of that, take on lovers than same-sex couples who are formally married; from the Dynastic perspective so long as you tolerate your spouse and do your duty of producing children, what you do otherwise can be accepted. I imagine the 'happy' sort of Dynastic marriage involves two people who find each other acceptable and might actually grow love over the course of their marriage as they gain trust in their spouse.

                                    Reading between the lines while 2e never had any top-down "remove all the gay people!" mandate, John Chambers seemed to put Exalted way down his list of priorities and gave more direction to Scion. I imagine this left a lot of room for homophobic or just unthinking freelancers to roll back things that weren't caught. It certainly left a lot of room for absolutely ludicrous holes in the mechanics, after all. See the "Realm is a matriarchy" thing - no, the explicit word "matriarchy" was not used. However, it was stated to be "a lot like the modern world, but gender-flipped with a bias towards women". Most writers unthinkingly still went with Default Males, and I think the 3e use of matriarchy and matriarchs is extremely fair even if you still go "like our world but flipped". Patriarchy's still a thing, after all.
