        Going off what I said earlier about beast-form Evocations, I think that would be a good way to turn 'animal planet heroes' into Mythical Beast Heroes. Evocations should be based off themes and myths, so instead of a bear evocation be 'go be a bear', it should be like...


        The fierce northman lunar sees the Bear as the VIkings did - a terrifying combatant, something that crushes through the enemy, that acted as inspiration for the berserker. They develop evocations based on their belief of what a bear is. Their bear can resist wound penalties, can charge through a battlegroup to get to its commander, can ignore swords sticking into even their heart.

        The Realms-born lunar sees the Bear as Britain did - as Artaois, the legendary warrior and leader, whom's spirit King Arthur carried. They create evocations of glorious leadership, they gain bonuses for being in the thick of battle alongside their allies, they are preternaturally skillful, able hear arrows from ten thousand meters away, and avoid them.

        The Eastern Lunar sees the Bear as Artio, the Guardian Goddess of the Celts irl. They create evocations of protectiveness, they form attachments to their 'cubs' and move to defend them from any blow. She gains strength as her cubs are injured, but does not rush into battle needlessly.


        All three of these lunars are still a Bear Totem lunar. But all three have their own myths and legends about what a Bear is. The same can be done for many animals, though 'weirder' and more 'modern' animals (such as the ever popular pistol shrimp, which I've seen four lunars of) kind of resist the 'mythical beast' because... very few myths really 'exist' about these animals. In which case, make one up, duh.

                                            Even though dolphins have been seen both a guardians/saviours in Greek mythology but nasty little fair-folk tricksters by Amazonian tribes. In Arthurian myth, Sir Yvain both has a lion as a companion and has to fight one in his quests, the first representing the virtues of a christian knight and the 2nd representing the dark animalistic impulses of sin.

                                            What I'm trying to say is that an animal can have many different interpretations in mythology and different cultures.
