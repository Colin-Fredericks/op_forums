      Well, I think the whole idea has its merits as a setting, and third edition adds actually a lot of systems that are really interesting and actually add and play well with the sort of epic contect this sort of game is supposed to help the players and GM feel. That with a lot of flawed or outright failed ideas, of course.

      We have played exalted through the years, first, second and now third edition First was outright simplistic and very broken in the gameplay-story integration. Second, it helped a bit with the charms... but had a complete mess of a combat system that outright did not work. Thiird has I think the best streamlined system in general (but not yet quite in combat) and very thematic character types. The whole supernal skill concept for solars in particular I think it's a master stroke, making you able to feel ike 'the best' in a particular field. The lunars feel a lot more shapeshifting, the dragonblooded a lot more 'elemental benders', and so on, than in the previous editions.

      Still, the combat idea with the withering and decisive sort of attack, barely works and is still a bother in melee... and just simply does not work as a concept for ranged combat.I have had to heavily adapt it so everything is decisive and withering is more a secondary system to actually prepare for 'super hits', for instance. Again, solar charm trees, while most are brilliant and make a lot more sense than before, things like craft are a complete unworkable mess that I just simply had to mutilate heavily so my craft oriented player could actually enjoy and make sense of it.

      In general, we are running this 3rd edition campaign and we are enjoying it... which is quite the target, but I feel that 3rd edition could have done with a pair more years of heavy polishing and play testing that it simply could not have.

      Essence I looked at and... oversimplified stuff is just not my thing as a GM.

      So in general... yes, but it demands a bit of an effort to make it work for you, if you want to actually enjoy the setting and the themes that I feel are pretty unique to this 'world'.

              Originally posted by Gonzo View Post
              Hi, I'm curious, do you play Ex3?
              Yep. In two games at the moment.

              Bonus Question: Do you think Exalted is still worth playing?
              Well, considering I play it? Yes.

              My group prefers some of the house rules out there that I don't always like compared to the RAW or some other options, but nothing that's a deal breaker.

              That said, if Exalted is still owned by Paradox when it's finally time for 4e (or maybe a full 3.5e) so it still runs on the ST system, what I'd really love to see if Essence moved up to the core rules as the simplified version really does seem to suit how most people I know want to play the game for the most part, and then have the more complex 3e style subsystems available to add-on to fine-tune to the level of complexity your group wants.

              Essentially (no pun intended), all three core editions of Exalted have suffered one key flaw that's always plagued everything the game has tried to do: the core rules are written for normal humans and then have Exalted stuff slapped on top of them. While I enjoy a heroic mortal game personally, I think something Essence does right (and also something you see in things like Scion 2e) is that building the rules around playing super-powered beings from the get go makes everything easier to accomplish. I think that's why Godbound gets suggested so much (even if I think it's not really good at doing Exalted even if it's good at doing it's own version of epic fantasy RPG): Godbound is a game written for high powered demigods. By just tossing all the D&D rules that only matter to mortal adventurers and focusing on the kinds of epic stories Godbound wants to do, it's a lot closer to what many Exalted fans have wanted for a long time. This is part of what makes the Cortex homebrew for Exalted one of the better ones in my mind: Cortex can run more simply like Godbound, while fairly seamlessly scaling up the mechanical complexity to Essence or even core Exalted levels.

              It's a heck of a lot easier to start with a simple version of the rules and then add to it, then trim things down later.

                                  I am currently playing in an Essence-version reboot of an old Exalted group that went on a years-long hiatus after we were broken under the sheer weight of Ex3. We found that we spent twice as long on systems and dice rolling than actual roleplaying. To be fair, I see several very good ideas in Ex3, the system is simply too cumbersome for me to enjoy, and with the degree of house rules needed for me to enjoy it, I might as well create my own system from scratch.

                                  So far Essence seems to be have a balance between system and roleplaying far more to the liking of myself and my group. There are good ideas (I like ventures and milestones), a few flaws (I am... not a fan of the attributes, some minor annoyance about a few things that could be smoother, but needed to connect to Ex3) that are easily house ruled into the Void (attributes, I am looking at you!), and given that Ex3 felt like a chore to play, we are pleasantly surprised to find Essence overall enjoyable.

                                  I was convinced that Exalted was dead for me, and had it not been for Essence I would not consider Exalted worth playing any longer (and wouldn't even be lurking on this forum). The group and I are still in the early stages of our Essence-fueled game, so do not know how Essence works in the long run, but right now I am overall hopeful that Exalted has many good stories for us still.

                                  I am looking forward to seeing the Essence Companion.
