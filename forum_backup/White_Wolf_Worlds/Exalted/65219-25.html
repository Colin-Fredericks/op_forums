    The Five Stars of the Maidens are in fact planets that reflect their power and themes, much as the Silver Chair does for Luna.

    Mercury is a land of vast oceans, long island chains, and flying continents, each of which carries massive plains or titanic mountains. The very geography changes at all times, all the better to encompass more and more journeys. The residents are nomadic peoples with a strong oral tradition; the very land resists attempts to settle upon it, forcing them to build great ships and mighty caravan-cities. Of particular note is a great city built upon a massive wheel. The sorcerer king who raised the city did so after he was cursed by the fates to suffer eternal doom should he set foot in Creation ever again.

    Mars, of course, is a barren wasteland wracked by eternal battle; the soil has been stained a deep crimson by the blood spilled upon it. Those who live there are brutal and savage. The shadowy echoes of warriors long dead stalk the lands, and the gods of battle travel there to sake their primal bloodlust. A race of vicious giants calls Mars home; they were once the favored race of the Black Boar, before the Exalted exterminated them from Creation.

    Jupiter is a fog-shrouded land of bizarre geometries. The land and air speak of vast secrets and arcane lore beyond mortal comprehension. Vast orders of Mystics roam the land, cataloging that vast knowledge. They then horde in their mighty library-citadels, lest a rival order gain access to their vast trove of secrets; in the land of Jupiter, knowledge truly is power.

    Saturn is a massive graveyard world. Everything upon the land is naught but an epitaph to something that has died. Everything, from people, to nations, loves, ideas has some form of memorial on Saturn. Saturn is unique among the planets in that it holds no living inhabitants (though some of the memorials are convincing enough to fool most). Even the Neverborn are memorialized there, though their massive cenotaphs are too large to fit upon Saturn's surface. Instead, they orbit her, creating a deathly eerie planetary system.

    I'm not sure what to do with Venus, though.

                                        The All-Seeing Eye: During the latter part of the Shogunate era, the lord and families were hardly loyal to the Shogun. For this an Order was founded of the most honourable, capable and loyal Dragonblooded coming from all the houses. These were the agents and troubleshooters of the Shogun. They were supposed to handle banditry, gather proof of a lord’s disloyalty if there was, out crimes against the people. It served the Shogun and his laws. It started out with good intentions. Over time this Order developed its own spynetwork, the agents could not be everywhere. It answered only to the Shogun and a few appointed people. As the organisation grew, it left its ideals behind. Sometimes crimes of a lord against the people had to be covered as the shogun needed his army, or a rebellion under the rulership of the Shogun’s family would do great damage to the Shogun’s image. Still later the missions changed from gathering evidence to actually planting them, incriminating a lord that did not bow to the shogun’s wishes. When honor became less and less a requisite for agents of the Order, more and more certain houses got people of their family in the correct places. Not long after control of the order was split among factions.
                                        One faction later rename themselves house Iselsi. The Empress or one of her companions at the time, had been a high ranking agent of the Order or what was left of it in the Balorian crusade.
                                        Later what was left of the Order and swore loyalty to the Empress, was made into the Allseeing Eye.

                                        Inspiration: Magistrates from Legend of the Five Rings.
                                        Reason: Fleshing out of the Shogunate era, make room for the possibility of a group of players of Dragonblooded to work together while still giving, people stereotypes to work worth, due to regional lords being rather antagonistic against each other.
                                        Creation is broken and thus Yu-Shan is: Imagine if Creation is, in an abstract way, a finely tuned machine. Every part has its function. Creation is supported and upheld by the pillars, The Daystar, the Lunar Chair and the stars each have their function. They also help balance Creation against the tides of the wyld, scouring it’s influences. Then the Balorian crusade happens. First the influence of the Wyld floods in. Then the Sword of Creation is activated. Activated but not controlled perfectly. The finely tuned machine though resilient no longer works perfectly, it’s a bit off kilter. It’s damaged too hard. But the wear is obvious. No longer is the Wyld completely cleaned from Creation each time. The influence of the Sun, moon and stars and Heaven is muted. This affected the Spirits of Yu Shan, and not a way that almost anyone would be willing to admit. The corruption with which Heaven is rife is at least partially attributable to the polluting influence of the Wyld. This expresses itself as a disconnected feeling from creation.

                                        Celestial Incarna: the incarna are alien creatures. They also have a very different view on things compared to mortals. After longs ages of servitude, they broke free from the Primordials. They had paid their dues. They left creation to the Exalted. Heaven would keep running as it would. They view time in a different way. In their mind, they just sat down at the table. And only now the games are getting interesting. There’s Heaven, there are the Exalted. They grant blessings. They’re still just enjoying their freedom. The exalted are there, they had full authority (well almost) over creation. Why do the incarna need to intercede? Oh sure, there are problems. But Creation still exists. As an example: The Solar exalted made the Unconquered Sun turn his back on them, by making a few claims and get into a fight with the other Exalted. In the unconquered sun’s eyes, barely a moment of time passed before he forgave them. Even though that period was long enough for the first age to be considered legends or myths by most.
                                        Reason: Make the incarna appear less like addicts, return some of the mystery to them by making their ways of thinking alien. Also remove the idea that “waking up the Incarna from the Games will solve everything.” At most if one stood before the Unconquered Sun, getting his attention from the Games of Divinity, she would be granted a great boon, or a lecture of “I’m proud of you, but it is your responsibility.”, and then return playing.

                                        The Wyld in Creation: When the sword of creation was activated, it also fought against some of the changes in terrain that had been made by influxes of Wyld energies. Sometimes it restored these regions to creation acceptable terrains. Others were merely covered by a new creation compatible surface. This means that encased or localised pockets of Wyld energies can be found as close as the inner sea. This can result in randomly empowered creatures, or Faerie folk cut off from the wyld but still living since the Balorian crusade.

                                        Reason: introduce unexpected enemies, Leave reminders of the Balorian crusade in the environment. One finds a heap of first age ruins but almost no wrecks from the Crusade. Even though a first age creationspanning defense was activated which barely saved creation.

                                        Neverborn and adversaries with a connection to them: The minds of the Neverborn are hard to fathom. Mostly because there are almost no single cohesive minds of the Neverborn left. They are dead, their minds shattered. The pieces however are formed by the void, the oblivion of the underworld and their rage at the living and creation. Each Neverborns mind consists of several cognisant ideas and personas. Whispers (such as the background) are voices of personas too weak to make actually solidify or that cannot interact with creation or whose perception of it is too warped to understand anything. Just like a Primordial has hearts, and needs them to constitute and reinforce his identity, so does a Neverborn lack them and needs others to make sense of itself or reality. This is where the Deathlords come in. Some of the Deathlords took the offer to serve or make a bond with the Neverborn. Deathlords are a strange amalgam of the ghost of powerful Solar and soul a Yozi would have. Some souls manifest independently and have strange and alien agendas.
                                        Why: Separate Deathlords from Abyssals. Make Deathlords powerful, but also more alien. Develop the Neverborn a bit more, play up the fact that dying is not mentally healthy. If a Yozi needs his souls for his identity, and killing one can change it completely. Then the Neverborn need, or instinctively try to develop a likewise system. By further developing the errant souls other creatures can be introduced than either Ghost, Abyssal or Deathlord.

                                                    Creation was literally made out of narrative; as such, Physics aren't a Real Thing in Exalted. The loom upholds something similar to physics by weaving a never-ending and extremely compelling Story of Everything (literally of every single fate-bound thing in existence) working as it's supposed to (by our standards). This creates narrative consistency that is often confused for laws of physics (things fall down not because of gravity, but because it would be narratively inconsistent if they didn't. And that way lies the Wyld).

                                                    The Shinma are all aspects of narrative that allow this to be. (A narrative must have distinct characters, thus separation. For characters to be interesting they must be flawed, thus perfection/imperfection. etc.)

                                                    The reason for keeping the loom up-to-date is that as long as its narrative is close enough to "reality", it all works. When it starts diverging, plot-holes are introduced, as though the narrator of creation was getting progressively more drunk (or Shayamalayan).

                                                    The sidereals do their job to not have to deal with Nickolas Cage.
