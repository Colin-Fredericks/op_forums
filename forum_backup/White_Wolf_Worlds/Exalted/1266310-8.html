    ShinyGod: Hey Salem, remember how I talked a big game about life and death being a delicate balance which cannot be screwed with? I lied; you're immortal now, explicitly so you can't join your true love until you learn the lesson I want you to. Behold my compassion.

    Salem: So you CAN and WILL make people immortal?

    ShinyGod: Oh yeah, I just resent being told to do it on command. See ya!

    Act 2:

    GloomyGod: Lookit that, the problem we created came back to bite us in the ass - but not like, meaningfully, it's just kind've annoying. Time to destroy everyone in the world *but* Salem for Salem's temerity.

    ShinyGod: I can and will repeatedly bring people back to life, but nah, that seems about right.

    Act 3:

    ShinyGod: Hey Oz, remember how I talked a big game about life and death being a delicate balance which cannot be screwed with?

    Oz: No?

    ShinyGod: Well I did, and I lied. You have to go back.

    Oz: Nah, I'd rather stay here and wait for Salem.

    ShinyGod: Oh, also, here are four neat toys - don't bring them together until you achieve world peace or we'll destroy the entire world.

                                                    It depends on the circumstances, but yeah if an entity is so alien that you can't ascribe human morality to it then short of being made like said entity one wouldn't be able to learn its view of morality. Some series get away with the scenario by playing it more towards alien circumstances to things humans can understand. For example "Speaker for the Dead" had a race where part of their growing up involved being ritualistically gutted. This led to a few conflicts owing that one was a result of doing this to a human without knowing that humans work that way and another was a human refusing to gut the alien because the human wasn't aware that this was not a suicide request.

                                                    In other cases its a bit less effective. For example killing people doesn't seem to take much effort for either brother in RWBY, whereas the most effort we see in bringing anyone back was the light brother putting Ozma in the reincarnation cycle , and that's only because he wanted Ozma's permission on the front, to entities like that killing people could be viewed as significantly less immoral than it would be for a human since they could literally undo the fatality. Meaning for them they could view killing and raising a human with the same impact as flicking someone's nose. Granted that doesn't seem to be how it's played in show I'm merely making a point. The thing is entities like that trying to teach the importance of life is a bit tricky considering the entities teaching it are immortal with apparently easy and complete control over life and death.

                                                            Oh yes, absolutely. And the hell of it is, that's like... past Salem, who was almost certainly a much more cuddly person who valued human life more than the modern incarnation.

                                                            We can agree that, after watching the God of Destruction effortlessly No Sell the attack against him and then just as easily wipe out her army, Salem’s response should not have been to threaten to sacrifice another army to them for her own sake? Like, maybe she should have tried pleading with the brothers to bring back the people she had led to their deaths?
                                                            I can agree with this, yeah, although it's a bit sort of... the kind of person who is going to respond that way is probably the sort of person who doesn't assemble a murder army in the first place, yeah? If Salem actually cared about the army, she would, paradoxically, have probably not assembled it in the first place.

                                                            Like, you can argue that on a purely practical level, regardless of her ACTUAL feelings Salem should have immediately knuckled under to the Gods and told them whatever lies they wanted to hear and attempted to alter her behavior in whatever way they found most congenial in order to get to an out. But that's a practical argument, not a moral one.

                                                            Or taken any responsibility for it at all?
                                                            This part is tricky, because Salem isn't the only one with agency here. The Gods have it as well, as do the other people involved. Nobody had to sign onto Salem's agenda of "Murder the Gods. Topple their thrones. Steal their power, and make it yours." She was lying to them, but the lie itself isn't one that really makes you look good for responding to it with "Divine murders to make myself immortal? Oh fuck the hell yes. Sign me up!"

                                                            Similarly, the God of Darkness doesn't really look good for deciding "this pathetic attack, which did not, in fact, pose any kind of threat to me at all, warrants genocide and global disaster as a response." Whatever else Salem might have done, that's not on her.

                                                            That said, Salem had every reason to expect that even if they won, there would be massive casualties. She knowingly marched people into what was gonna be a slaughter for her own selfish reasons, using lies as a goad, and THAT'S something she absolutely should take responsibility for.
