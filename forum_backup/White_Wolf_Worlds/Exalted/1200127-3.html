    Now to see Nellens get its due.

    Given what I’m presuming of their status, it’s fun to see what I suppose is a combination of edifying family legend, and piece of popular narrative to cement their place.

    With the changes to Mnemon, the idea of her having been made an advocate for unExalted Dynasts… it doesn’t make less sense, but it would stand out less and is less necessary. So if House Nellens is going to be expanded, he fits nicely with that position.

    Good to see that they’re still run by that group of seven, plus that dynamic of outsiders assuming that the Dragon Blooded are in charge. I do wonder how Nellens Odem’s mortality is squared with membership in the Greater Deliberative.

    Oh good, they did end up implementing that idea that it’s possible for mortals to have superior breeding to Dragon Blooded.

    So it seems to me that, unlike the other Houses, Nellens doesn’t really have a designated position, so they capitalise in areas vital to the Realm’s function that the other Houses largely neglect. The different dynamic that gives to their Dragon Blooded is also fascinating, that distinct pressure that is put upon them.

    I know there’s always been the idea that Nellens spreads through the various minor industries and businesses of the Realm, but I think the take of that making them immensely wealthy is new. It’s a strong advantage to be sure, although I have to imagine that it being spread over so many ventures can make it hard to bring to bear on any singular instance (even if their methods probably rely on diffuse action anyway).

    I can see some interesting story possibilities in the idea of the House being in the early process of building up its military. In particular, something like a party of Nellens travelling Threshold warzones in search of experience and instructors. Maybe even some overtures to somebody in the margins of Lookshy.

    I enjoy the notion that Nellens responds to being widely despised with a kind of duplicitous turning of the other cheek, or otherwise taking it in stride while making their plans. So yeah, as was said to me before, House Sesus has issues with Nellens due to being named for their ancestor (which seems to come with the revision of the House not really even being descended from him… and also how he’s no longer the father of Ledaal, who isn’t even a biological daughter anymore), so that probably makes for a good history of almost open feuding that many of the others didn’t have. It’s also nice to have a prospective conflict based on something as simple as geographic proximity, combined with the issues that Ledaal is having in its own right, and thinking that Nellens would make for an easy win.

    And after everything built up with Ragara, it does warm my heart to see them being so heavily frustrated by a House consisting largely of mortals. They really need to find a way to make friends with Mnemon.

    Ah come now, what did Nellens do to deserve having assets reassigned to V’Neef? While Nellens and Tepet offer a nice example of a conflict between fresh new money and old money on hard times. And of course, the untapped resource that is the general mortal society of the Realm.

    Hmm, Juche… didn’t that used to be the centre of Realm manufacturing and mining? Eh, hardly anything was ever done with that, so might as well revise it as the Nellens headquarters. It’s also interesting for there to be a heavily fortified prefecture even before the Isle is in open war. Probably keeps things interesting for the Sesus who try infiltrating. It might also give them a leg up if it actually comes to war; between that and their weaker military, it gives the idea that they might at least spend the initial stage adopting a siege mentality, and letting the others fight it out for a while… although Ledaal might be equipped to break through such fortification anyway. Still, the possible strategic pictures it paints are compelling.

    Heh, what kinds of bookkeeping chicanery do they need to engage in to conceal their financial assistance to other satrapies? They’re probably too practically minded to do anything as silly as ensure that a House is repaid with its own money just because.

    Ahh, I love the contrast between Nellens’ advocacy for mortals, and undermining abolition of slavery. Sabine paints a fine picture of a powerful mortal situated among the Exalted.

    Oh God, the idea of taking the Righteous and Accountable Ministry of Weights and Measures, always held up as an overwrought and insignificant office, and making a bigger deal out of it… a hair in either direction, and it would be a very silly idea, but matched with Nellens and with this execution, it does its job. Here’s hoping that somebody will be able to take inspiration from it to get some actions out of the Bureaucracy Ability.

            Now, House Peleps. I've already gotten an impression from previews in how it's discussed in other House write-ups that it's currently a bit different from my preconception of a bit of a villainous House. It still has the nautical focus, but at the core of a general image of travel and adventure, plus an edge of righteous judgement, that apparently lends them a lot of domestic popularity. I’m inferring that, as one of the older and more prominent faces of the Realm abroad, the need to hold themselves to a higher standard of behaviour and accomplishment was at least partially driven by a sense of wanting to present a good face for the world at large.

            Ahh, and it’s counterbalanced by a particularly strict, meritocratic outlook. I’m already seeing some things that can be done with that, particularly for stories in which one might want to play a Dynast who is still a bit fringe.

            Exactly what the basis of Peleps in the Imperial Navy was is something that was always vague (and probably a bit inconsistent) in the past, so I appreciate the definition of it here, and think it’s a good choice to just commit to the connection. Even with that, I never would have expected the governing body of the navy and the ruling council of the House to be one and the same, nor for it to be elected. As something to make this House stand out, it feels a bit reminiscent to the nation of Sova to me. It’s a curious, although not altogether unfitting, notion that the navy of the Realm is basically a law unto itself, at least as administration goes. I’ll be interested to see if Peleps’ write-up casts any light on the history of that.

            And going along with the trend of Houses having interesting policies for the future is the prospect of Peleps taking its toys and establishing a new dominion elsewhere.

            So let’s see… that the Merchant Fleet had recently been reassigned from Peleps to V’Neef has always been in the game, and a sticking point between Houses, but key details have been changed. The Merchant Fleet has gone from the coastal defence of the Blessed Isle to the ships that escort tribute back to the homeland, and take a cut. That certainly makes more sense as to how it would materially benefit the new House, and given the changed setup of Peleps, how it was old and respected and financially secure, it makes the conflict have somewhat more substantive stakes.

            Hmm, they are still a bit villainous, but it’s certainly rooted in a clear context and purpose. Heh, it’s a kind of ironic twist on my initial read; they start off as projecting the positive public face of the Realm in the Threshold, and now they’re transitioning to being the early portent of renewed conquest and war. It feels a bit reminiscent of Tolkien’s Numenor.

            And it finally comes down to the plan that the Empress was always said to fear; just seceding altogether and establishing an independent power base in the West. I can imagine that the possibility of hundred or thousands of warships, decked out with innumerable Exalted, coming out of the east to seize control might be the kind of thing that would give even the Silver Prince pause.

            So Peleps got as large a share of the legions as Cathak did (although without the pre-existing ones to makes its land army truly massive), placed at the side of the Imperial Navy. I like that idea that they find the notion of directly appointing their members to officer positions within those legions distasteful, but necessary for rapidly securing control. The Navy is certainly massive, but the limited deployment and nature of the Realm’s land probably places the advantage with the rest of the Dynasty, if they should set differences aside.

            Ohh, now there’s a fascinating idea; that the image of the House, and its reputation with the Dynasty as a whole, might protect it from direct assault by enemies within, a protection that exists in tension with the ultimate treachery that secession would entail.

            It’s a very evocative idea, that Peleps, of all Houses, would have this stature and history among the Realm, and exist in a state of crisis very different from that of Tepet; that idea that it’s become too big to sustain itself in the long term, without drastic action.

            Jeez, everybody dislikes House Ragara, don’t they? With all of this build-up, I’ll be very interested to see it next, particularly since I was only recently struck by the point that Ragara may be the only son of the Empress to ever be granted a House, and what he needed to do to earn that.

            Anyway, I like the way it suggests that Cathak and Peleps together could be an unstoppable military force, and wonder at the meaning behind Peleps savouring the possibility of the Dynasty united against them.

            To go along with V’Neef representing a much deeper blow to the House, I like the way that the rival is elevated in stature and agency here to make for a more active and even feud.
            It’s funny, I always looked at the broken west of the Blessed Isle and saw it as the least desirable collection of prefectures, but with this revised status and format for Peleps, I can see new value in it (making a nice home for some evocative landscapes).

            And hey, they actually did include a form of the Realm stripping satrapies bare and leaving them to rot after all. I wonder if they were deliberately misdirecting me when I asked about that before.
            The relationship between the Realm and Wavecrest is already vastly more interesting.

            Wow, Peleps was the second daughter? That must make the House very old indeed. To just imagine how widely travelled she was… and at that age, she may very well have been part of the inception of those Imperial values that her descendants are considered exemplary of.

            By the by, I’ve noticed how many House founders get more elaborate interment than the prior Editions stating that Dynasts went for simple cremation. I wonder if this is a general change, or a privilege of founders.

            Peleps Lai must also be pretty damn old, and I enjoy the way that this makes her conservative in a manner that she actually believes that the Empress might come back. The notion that she dearly wants to directly conquer the West and then present it to the Scarlet Empress is positively cute, though.

            I don’t think we’ve yet seen many characters who entertain ambitions for the throne themselves, rather than being presented as supporters for other claims, and Peleps stature does make it seem like the type where at least one of the admirals would have such a vision; in a way, they feel like they’ve got a stronger claim than even Mnemon (which has to really complicate Mnemon’s desire for their support). I’ve got to admire her faith in Tepet, when there are possibly more immediately valuable prospective supporters.

            Heh, considering the Peleps disgust for Nellens, which may represent one of the best hopes for Tepet’s recovery, the idea of an alliance between the three Houses through Aramida’s pursuit of the position is rather inviting.

            God, the game has been missing the groundwork for such complex politics and strategies.

            And to top it off, an Immaculate missionary who intends to try and convert the Mask of Winters to the faith. Good luck to him.

            Peleps has risen dramatically in my estimations. It may be my second favourite House so far, and is vastly more vivid and alive and complex than ever it was in the past. This… this is a House of Water. I’m really amazed.

                    All right Ragara, you’ve gotten a lot of build-up; dazzle me.

                    A comparison to the Imperial Mountain seems like a suitably ominous introduction, given the note of how there are freaky horrors beneath it.

                    Being the Imperial Bank is an old mainstay, but the emphasis has been a bit adjusted what with how this is giving them tremendous capital over the other Houses. Nice to see that Banoba is still the boss, particularly if Ragara himself is going to be developed more. Aaaand even more ominousness with the idea that the House wants everything.

                    Ah, so that’s rooted a bit more specifically in the idea of them being particular ardent in the seeking out of powers and Artifacts. I’ve often had a thought of Realm military forces going on expeditions in the Threshold to pick that stuff up, so it’s good to see that given more roots in the agenda and remit of a specific House.

                    Oh, but they’re plumbing some greater depths than even that. Diabolism, the Underworld, association with the Anathema… I had an impression that Ragara was going to be the more villainous House, but this is going further than even my expectations. Mind, it creates an intriguing avenue to undermine their otherwise seemingly implacable power… or confirm it.

                    Hmm, so like Mnemon, Banoba also has an Inner Circle to go along with his singular authority, but the relationship is more… let’s say reciprocal, and they’re less about administering the House, and more about managing the research into forbidden arts. And they’re studying Exaltation itself, jeez, I’m getting some real “Modern Age take on Infernals” vibes from them.

                    What the devil is the Final Realm-Controlling Utterance? Is that a specific thing, or is it a term for a hypothetical pursuit?

                    Heh, it’s funny that the description of their social standing is comparatively straightforward. Guess that’s the benefit of being old and reliably powerful, while most of their issues are kept in the dark.
                    So, it would seem as though Ragara’s technically official remit is in locating and mining valuable minerals, which provides the security behind the immense wealth that they leverage into financial chicanery such as Threshold land-sharking in the interests of imperialism. Heh, I’m suddenly reminded of how Akira Toriyama said that one of his seminal villains was based on Japanese land sharks of the 80s. And it’s not just about money, but funding their pursuits and research.

                    Hmm, it’s interesting how their philosophy and practices informed their approach to acquiring a military, basically flipping it. Also interesting how, for all of their power, this is presented as a less than successful strategy. It’s another interesting possible weak spot.

                    I like how it starts off saying that the House has no real friends among the others, but as well as their relations being complex and detailed, they do see a practical need to make overtures to at least a few other Houses. I also like that final note on how one of Banoba’s plans is something that might leave them in a vulnerable position if it actually succeeds.

                    Maybe it’s just my bias arising from Masters of Jade, but I’m getting real Masters of Jade vibes from the Stepped Palace particularly; like a kind of combination of the headquarters of the Timeless Order and Coindelving. The typical Ragara policy towards satrapies represents an interesting contrast to their domestic activities.

                    Jau Dei is a neat location with its unique qualities and manner of living, the complications this creates for the House, and the stakes it represents for them.

                    So, Ragara: generally familiar with his whole thing of enormous ambition and hating his family, and the policy of his kill switch being tied only to one sister. Under other circumstances, I might be critical of the idea of giving an explanation for his longevity, but it has always been an elephant in the room, and if it’s in the form of a neat and ominous Artifact… yeah, I’m good with it. Mind, given some of the other references, I’m surprised that Blood Zenith turned out to “just” be a daiklave. I was always fine with the idea that Ragara was at peace with his retirement, but if the implication here is that he’s clinging to life in hopes of the House’s experiments bearing some fruits, I’m okay with it. It would still technically leave him a bit on the sidelines.

                    No word on how he founded the House, that will probably come up in The Realm.

                    Hmm, Banoba’s write-up here is short, but he has already gotten a lot of details, so this seems to serve as mostly wrapping things up with his private life. I note that some of Banoba’s details have been changed, such as no longer having a public persona of being dull (which probably wouldn’t fit with the new context of the House), and his relationship with Heral seems a lot more open and committed, so… good for him.

                    Ahh, nice to finally see Szaya get her due. She’s got a strong basis in the current construction of the House, which contrasts interestingly with her having a more national loyalty.

                    And finally, Iuna strikes me as a Ragara who has a more altruistic take on their mission, if she’s focused primarily on preserving culture, while still possibly being involved in something a bit sinister. That actually puts me in mind of another possible vulnerability of the House; that they might bite off more than they can chew.
