        Sijan in truth is not as dour as a traveller may tell you, it is however absolutely concerned with the affairs of death and the dead. The city itself is alive with contrast of colours, deep and rich, spanning all manner of funerary rite. Arrays of candles, incense, and pyre-flames burn constantly, giving the city a warmth and strong scent, both masking the cold and the smell of the dead. Decorations of orchids and flowers grown in the nearby shadowlands are abound the city, often including flowers of other funerary rites of many a people. Talismans of protection of the dead, and symbols of the dead are also frequent, be they prayers written on strips of paper, skulls decorated with paint and jewels, or chimes made of bone. The city has a din of sound at all times, the sounds of prayers and mourning, or somber celebrations. The semblances and echoes of life and warmth give comfort to the dead, and the great rites and prayers to remember them give the dead who live in Sijan of the Underworld great wealth and prosperity.

        Rumor has it that Sijan has a patron guardian spirit, a forgotten woman who died long ago where Sijan now sits. This spirit, sometimes honored as the Lady of the Dead, watches over the spirits, people, and lands of Sijan. She appears as a motherly woman of white skin and long raven hair. Her face is that of a skull, and her body is entwined with all manner of richly coloured vine and flower.

            Denandsor's allure of mystery and treasures bring many Scavenger Lords and other interested parties near it, but few enter it for long. As such around the old city many small towns, some cities to themselves, have become hotspots and congregations of those who proclaim themselves Scavenger Lords. All manner of people looking for fortunes and work, or to sell their finds come here. Looking like something between a bazaar and an archaeological dig site, all manner of antiquities and oddities are here, often haphazardly about in a very schizophrenic display. Hired guards and mercenaries protect individual shops, claims, and the scavenger lords themselves. Traders of goods come here to taste the wealth of the scavengers, and many establishments seek to pray on the the scavenger vices. The Guild has a major presence here, and supports the place's existence fervently, controlling much of the market, often so they can have first claim to many wonders. Lookshy has a contingent of scholar soldiers stationed out here, keeping an eye out for First Age weapons that could be useful or too dangerous to use. Laws are determined by the Scavengers Code, and are really only followed publicly. There is very little of a centralized government, moreso individual parties and camps, and very wealthy individuals.

            There is however a commonly celebrated time, a game of sorts, where daring individuals go into Denandsor and try to find something of worth. Some of these people are sponsored, their sponsors laying some claim to whatever they find, others make bets on how long such ventures last before the scavenger flees the city, or who will find the object of greater worth. More fortunes have been made betting on the scavengers rather than the scavengers have discovered. Certain popular characters have become well know City Scavengers whose repeat attempts and efforts have given them a semblance of fame, though any fool who calls themself a Scavenger Lord is welcome to try and best the city.

            Rumor has it there are certain spells or incantations that could disperse the magic that lays on the city, no such things have been found but there is a small business in selling false information to the gullible.

                Travel by road in Creation can both help or hinder your travels, depending on the spirits of the road. Many roads, often connected ones, have formed Road Courts to themselves, pooling their power and offerings together to be a much more formidable force. Many of these have alliances with traders, most certainly with the guild. Some get intermediaries to bully uncooperative spirits into service. These roads or "highways", if their price is paid, can significantly speed your journey along despite travelling at no faster speed. These highways don't very well exist on the Blessed Isle in this capacity as the spirits cannot extort or pool their power there without retribution.

                    The Exalted were beaten, battered, tortured and killed many thousands of times over in the War. They could be tricked, swayed, subverted, converted. This happened more times than a god can count. None of these explain the utter violation of will that Thrice-Damned Gorol represented.

                    For you see, Gorol was the first one to break. Oh, certainly many had "broken" under the interrogation of the deva lords, but their essential will and spirit was never gone for long. What Gorol did, and what he saw, and the revelation seared into his mind? To be suitable for the Investiture, a being must have heaped so much horror and despair and torment upon their own head that the unconquerable soul cracks. That is the nature of an akuma.

                    The empty shell, devoid of all will or motive, that will die of exposure or thirst if not given Titanic purpose; that is their true self. What remains of it. A being with drive and motivation enough to walk and talk and pray will never be a malleable enough clay for the Yozi's hands.

                    Reincarnation offers a chance to heal the damage, slowly, over lifetimes, but the fissure never disappears entirely.
