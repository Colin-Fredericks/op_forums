            Crane

            Essence to Master: 4(!)
            Armor: None
            Weapons: War fan, hook sword, unarmed. Lacks the normal “unarmed can do lethal” rider.

            Shtick: Defend all your allies, and reply with enhanced counterattacks to anything that comes their way.

            Charms:
            1. A Withering attack is more accurate and damaging against someone who has attacked you (or a Ward you were Defend Othering).
            2. +2 Parry when Defending Other.
            3. Form. +1 Parry. Cost for Full Defense reduced by 1i. Respond to any attack on you or your ward with a counterattack.
            4. Make a roll; bank successes for later use in raising Parry or making counterattacks.
            5. Counterattack out to the range of the attack made on you.
            6. Fly for 1-2 actions, reducing falling damage at the end if still airborne. Double-10s on Decisive damage rolls.
            7. Add stunt dice to Decisive damage rolls. If you do enough damage, create Ties in the target.
            8. Double-10s on Decisive damage. Enemy’s Defense is penalized by his Intimacies as per Resolve.
            9. Your counterattack throws an enemy two range bands, or disarms at a discount, or returns an attack to the attacker.
            10. When you Decisively counterattack your enemy’s Decisive attack, add his Initiative to your own.
            Empowering Justice Meditation (#1) is pretty uninspiring in its default mode. Compare 4m on an Excellency to 3m on this; the Excellency will hit at times that this misses, and if they do both hit, the Excellency does just as much Withering damage unless soaked to minimum. The math is considerably better in the secondary “if-they-hit-you” mode, or if you’re a Solar.

            Same thing with Fluttering Cry of Warning (#2), actually – the default mode is only 1m better than what you could do with just an Excellency. Again, the Mastery benefit helps a lot – a reflexive Defend Other is a big deal – but so far, the default Celestial-level effect of this style fails to impress. (Actually, the mastery benefit is really good – I’d probably take that over War Lion Stance.)

            Crane Form (#3) seems to assume that there are default rules for when a counterattack is made. Which, it makes perfect sense that there would be such rules, but I don’t think there actually are. Reflexive activation is hard here – you have to defend in a way that drops the attacker’s Initiative below your own.

            Actually… ha. Actually, are there any rules saying you can’t activate multiple counterattack effects in response to a single attack? Can I respond to an attack with Solar Counterattack and Solar Cross-Counter and Crane Form and Crossed-Wings Denial (#4)? I don’t see anything detailing general counterattack rules at all.

            Kindly Sifu’s Quill (#8) is vicious with the Mastery tag: double-7s on a Decisive damage roll, against which your opponent has something like -4 Defense, if you can exploit a Defining Intimacy. That’s a fight-ender for 5m.

            *snerk* The image of what I assume is Mournful Crane’s Cry (#9) is just ridiculous – ragdoll bodies and a pale, shrieking central character. Not a fan. Edit: Wait, no, this has to be from Silver-Voiced Nightingale. No Cranepic?

            Okay, what is “her enemy’s Initiative” for the purposes of Wisdom of the Celestial Crane (#1)? Is it the enemy’s pre-attack Initiative, or if they hit, is this their reset-to-base 3i? The latter seems like the natural reading, but that’s a pretty expensive Charm.

            The major thing here seems to be the reflexive Counterattacks – which is a big deal! – and the various counterattack-enhancing Charms… but that just drives the lack of any real counterattack rules home, and absent that, power is hard to evaluate. Otherwise, the Celestial-level defense here is lackluster, though some of the Mastery upgrades are quite nice. I’m afraid I’ll always have some affection for Neph’s mad original vision, here, with its combo-free Charms and its 0m perfect defense and its instant-training effect. This comes close, when it gets into Intimacy manipulation and other nifty tricks, but it just doesn’t have that same wow-factor.

            My suspicion is that, for a group who stay in relatively close proximity, this is going to be a fairly lethal add-on: whenever anyone gets attacked, the Crane stylist Defends Other on them and then replies with a counterattack. My suspicion is also that this is going to be a pretty glass-cannon strategy; the Parry buffs are limited, there’s no Onslaught negation, and there’s nothing to help with soak or mitigating Decisive attacks in a build that can't wear armor. Forget the sidebar telling you to favor social stuff, you need Resistance - one good hit and you’re going to be minus one Crane.


            Silver-Voiced Nightingale Style

            Essence to Master: 3
            Armor: Light or none
            Weapons: Your enhanced kiai.

            Shtick: Strike down your enemy with your resounding song; Bollywood dance number optional. Buff allies to make big cooperative attacks. Make Performance-based influence rolls in combat.

            Charms:
            1. Your shouts are light ranged weapons, permanently. You can flurry them with Performance rolls at no penalty.
            2. Reroll (but not recurring reroll?) all 1s on Join Battle for you and all allies who can hear you.
            3. Inspire fear in your enemy, forcing him to move away from you. If you also hit him with your kiai, treat him as if he had Intimacies of fear for you.
            4. Form. +1 Evasion. +Performance to your Withering damage. Gain 3i when someone pays WP to resist your influence.
            5. +(Performance/2) to Evasion vs. a Decisive. If successful, reflexively Read Intentions with bonus dice.
            6. Against one enemy, you take no Evasion penalties, gain double-9s to Rush or Disengage, and gain 1i when he gains Initiative.
            7. Make a Performance roll vs. all enemies within earshot; those overcome must pay 1wp or take -3 Defense from despair.
            8. All enemies in short range are hit by a sonic environmental attack; all who fail to resist can be hit by an extra-accurate Withering kiai the next turn.
            9. An ally’s attacks gain double-10s (Decisive) or double-9s (Withering) on damage. Bonus damage if you then attack the same target with your kiai.
            10. You and all allies gain 1 WP each turn, which cannot be banked or spent on Charms. Wow!
            11. Make a kiai gambit on the target. Each success delays his turn by 1 tick and adds 1i to the damage of all Decisives made on him until his next turn.
            Voice of the Night Bird (#1) is a pretty great dip – or would be, without the Ability requirement. Never be without a ranged weapon! Social attack via your killing song!

            Terrifying Battle Shriek (#3) plus Tiger Style? Terrify your enemy into running away, then run him down and devour him.

            Why do all these Martial Arts Charms have a semicolon after their Mins? We’re often told that rules text “isn’t programming code” – is this, in fact, a bit of C++ that’s crept into the book? (Probably not.)

            Hearing the Heart’s Song (#5) is a pretty lousy defensive Charm, but it may not be a bad Read Intentions booster.

            Haunting Heart-Rending Melody (#7) is unlikely to actually stick on anyone – a -3 penalty to Defense is pretty huge. On the other hand, if five guys all spend WP to resist, that’s a big swing to your Initiative, thanks to the form!

            Ooh, Resounding Songbird’s Cry (#8) is an environmental attack! We haven’t seen that space explored yet. That looks like a pretty potent way of getting around many enemies’ primary defenses – not a lot of damage, but it sets you up for a big follow-up, and damage is damage.

            Aria of Victory (#9) is really stunning; it might as well read “You and all allies gain 1 non-Charm success to one action every turn.” That’s a huge bonus. Shattering Discord Cacophony, on the other hand, took me a moment to get. It seems like the best thing to do with this is to drop, say, 10i for +4i to all allies. If you can do that right after your enemy last acted, and so significantly delay when he next acts, that means all your allies (and you!) might get 1-2 chances to make Decisives with +4i. With three allies, that could be something like seven attacks, for +28i (less what you spent on the gambit) – more, if you can afford to chain on Flashing Blade Harmony. That’s not half bad, but it’s still a pretty expensive power requiring a lot of set-up – you might get one really good opportunity to use this per fight, and then only if you’ve got a solid group.

            Nightingale feels like it should play well with Crane – lots of Simple effects here that would eat up your turn buffing allies, while Crane adds an enormous pile of counterattacks; neither of them can abide much armor. Crane wants to parry, though, while this wants to dodge – hm.

            On balance, this is a style that feels more about being helpful than about achieving big things on your own. There’s some social bonuses, some reliable ranged weapons, and some big party buffs – but not much in the way of meat-and-potatoes. You could get a lot out of this, if you’re social-heavy, but I wouldn’t want it to be my only line of defense (or offense, for that matter).

                                                            Righteous Devil Style

                                                            Essence to Master: 3
                                                            Armor: Medium, light, or none.
                                                            Weapons: Firewands and other flame-based weapons.

                                                            Shtick: Call out the wicked and let them repent or burn. Armor won’t save them; numbers won’t save them; health levels for darn sure won’t save them.

                                                            Charms:
                                                            1. Double-10s on Decisive damage. Use firewands out to medium range.
                                                            2. Your Aim actions also allow you to reload your weapon.
                                                            3. Add your Aim dice to a Withering attack’s damage, or one die to a Decisive’s damage.
                                                            4. Form. Intimidate all enemies; anyone affected takes a penalty to run or hide. Reflexively Aim at anyone affected. Your firewand is a melee weapon.
                                                            5. Remove some or all of the target’s soak, depending on how much cover he has.
                                                            6. Everyone at close range is treated as standing in a bonfire.
                                                            7. Cheap Withering or Decisive damage booster.
                                                            8. Hit up to 6 enemies in a 90-degree arc, doing full Decisive damage to each(!).
                                                            9. Target repents of his wicked deeds or takes [Essence]A levels of damage.
                                                            I’ve always wanted to build a Righteous Devil character – but, y’know, Scroll of the Monk. Third time might be the charm, here.

                                                            The first three Charms here are all very Aim-heavy: Blossom of Inevitable Demise (#1) requires an Aim to function at Medium range, and Cloud of Ebon Devils (#2) and Kiss of the Sun Concentration (#3) both require an Aim to do anything. That’s a problem for a short-range weapon, because you wouldn’t normally spend turns Aiming at all – since Aim can’t be flurries, any turn you spend doing that you don’t actually get to attack. And then we get to the Form (#4), and everything clicks: if you intimidate your targets well enough, you get to Aim for free! That’s a pretty potent effect – it’s effectively +3 non-Charm dice to all your attacks for the scene, plus whatever other Aim upgrades now become reflexive as well. It also sets tone for the style pretty well – this is an art for calling the wicked out and putting them down, not for defense.

                                                            (It’s also nice to see the Equilibrium-style “fight with my pistols” thing lifted from 2e's Golden Exhalation Style.)

                                                            Don’t much care for the form activation condition – if you’re losing a turn to Aim anyway, you might as well just spend that turn activating the form (and then Aim reflexively). The form of the roll here (Cha + Pres + Ess) is almost never repeated in the Charms chapter; there, we’d say “(Cha + Pres) roll, adding (Essence) bonus dice.” I’m very happy with the more compact form, as long as we all understand what it means – I assume the (Ess) dice count towards your die cap?

                                                            Azure Abacus Meditation (#5) is a vicious little charm – in most close-range encounters (i.e., your default use case) you’re obviating their soak completely. This is both cheaper and more powerful than Snake’s soak-negation – plus it works at Short range! I know it isn’t original to this edition, but I do love the anima targeting reticule.

                                                            In general, this style (together with, maybe, Single Point) has some of the strongest visuals – I can very clearly see the gunslinger standing in the heart of the fire he’s set.

                                                            Dancing Devil Trigger Finger (#8) requires tracking all enemies “in a 90-degree arc” out to Medium range. Maps, game! You need maps! Suggest using maps. This is very close to the most dangerous multiattack so far – maybe Melee’s “hit everyone” powers are better, but those only work at close range. If you can actually set this up on three or four enemies – and you should be able to! – with a decent amount of Initiative, you could pretty easily end a fight in one shot. It’s expensive, but looks very much worthwhile – and of course, you could stack it with Caress of 1000 Hells (#9), Kiss of the Sun Concentration, and Phoenix Flies on Golden Wings (#7) for an extra-accurate attack dealing an extra [Ess] levels and 6 dice of damage to everyone in the area, on top of your full base Initiative damage – unless they repent, of course, which is also a solution to a persistent Wyld Hunt.

                                                            (I like Caress’s “REPENT OR BURN” theme in general. Solar Judge Dredd is a totally viable concept, complete with armor.)

                                                            This is probably one of my favorites so far. Don’t ask Righteous Devil for defense – it’s not there. On the plus side, you can wear armor, and you could pretty easily stack Resistance or Dodge – or, heck, Melee; don’t tell me you can’t stunt that. But for pure, unfiltered offense, with high-accuracy, high-damage Decisives, this style has you covered.


                                                            Black Claw Style

                                                            Essence to Master: 3
                                                            Armor: None.
                                                            Weapons: Unarmed only. (Well, and stolen weapons – see #7.)

                                                            Shtick: You are an innocent, defenseless dove. You were attacked unjustly, and the crowd is turning to your side – even your enemy is compelled to love you, and his ability to fight you falters. Then you rip out his still-beating heart and burn it to ash.

                                                            Charms:
                                                            1. If anyone beats you at Join Battle, he is perceived as starting the fight – both by others and by himself, unless he beats your Guile.
                                                            2. When attacked, counterattack socially to create positive Ties to yourself and steal Initiative. With the form active, also affects bystanders.
                                                            3. Poison your enemy with a long-lasting Initiative-draining venom. Gain the Initiative it drains.
                                                            4. Form. +1 Evasion; disengage more easily and at lower cost. Penalize enemy defenses if they have positive Ties to you.
                                                            5. An attack on you loses (Guile + positive Tie strength) dice.
                                                            6. Grab and hold an enemy without savaging him. If he is poisoned, turns of grappling don’t count against the poison’s duration.
                                                            7. Counterattack with a Disarm gambit, stealing your enemy’s weapon. It is a Black Claw style weapon as long as you carry it.
                                                            8. When attacked Decisively, counterattack with social influence against everyone in earshot, subtracting your wound penalties from Resolves. Anyone affected turns on the attacker – violently, with appropriate Ties to you.
                                                            9. Rip out your enemy’s heart. Double damage if he likes you; triple damage if he loves you.
                                                            The Mastery effect of Open Palm Caress lets you grapple as an ambush. Well, that’s the fight. (What does happen if you ambush someone who then beats your Join Battle?) I like that this Charm – and, it looks like, the style in general – reverses the normal combat incentives: “Oh, you spent 10m to win Join Battle? Hope you like being the bad guy!”

                                                            Heck yeah, Righteous Devil lady. I don’t know who you are, but you look awesome.

                                                            This is the second style to have “successfully defend against an attack that drops your enemy’s Initiative below your own” as a form trigger. It seems like the only way to make that fire, barring some Dodge Charm shenanigans, is to defend with exactly 1 less Initiative than your attacker – right? That’s awfully selective of a trigger.

                                                            Okay, let’s see. Practically speaking, a stylist here is going to have Guile 5-6 – right? So Doe Eyes Defense (#5) is somewhere between 5-10 dice subtracted for 4m1i; if you’re a Solar, at least the 1i is likely refunded, and maybe more. That’s very solid – it’s better than a non-Charm bonus of the same size would be, for instance. It seems like this could be used to stave off an enemy Decisive really effectively – the enemy has to substantially outspend you just to have a reasonable chance of hitting. Lesson here seems to be "Crash the Black Claw stylist first, try to actually kill her second."

                                                            Storm-Calming Embrace (#6) is just great. You can’t hit the enemy, of course, but you can hold on to him while the poison kills him for you. Hm. Although he can swing back now, so this is not nearly as deadly as it would have been in 2e – okay, now I’m uncertain whether this feels worth the cost. Baseline grappling is already so lethal, you have to give a compelling reason to do something different with it.

                                                            I’m now picturing a Black Claw stylist deliberately picking fights just so she can steal weapons of particular types. “Sorry, I need a firewand to be a form weapon!” It doesn’t seem like that should break anything, but it might lead to some kind of odd combinations, given that the stolen weapon appears to be in-form indefinitely at no committed cost.

                                                            Black Claw has one of the tightest themes so far. Everything works together to create the narrative of the fight; all the Charms push you towards the upper-level stuff, where all those Intimacies cash in and reward you for being such a poor, innocent soul. There’s very little direct offense here (until the pinnacle), but the defensive tricks are pretty solid – and the social counterattacks move the front line somewhere many fighters won’t be well-defended.
