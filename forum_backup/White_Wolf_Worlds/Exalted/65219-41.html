                                                            The True Great Curse:
                                                            The Great Curse did not fall on the Exalted host, for the same reason a man impaled on a sword doesn't curse the sword, or a man dying from AIDS after getting it from his lover isn't going to curse the virus. No, the dying Primordials were not capable of viewing the Exalted truly as beings. Instead, they directed their fading might and power on those they felt were the perpetrators: the gods.

                                                            Gods are myopic, selfish beings, but they were always part of Creation, and tended it. After the Curse, their tendencies to think of personal advancement, or of protecting their dominion at the expense of all else, became exaggerated. The power and persuasion of the United Exalted Host held this mostly in check. In the absence of Exalted nnity and the Solars, the Sidereals (of all factions) and the Empress pour more and more resources into the Immaculate Order every year, and only barely do they make advances.

                                                            For the Incarnae, a more poetic Curse was laid. The Great Geas was extended to cover the Exalted themselves, thus preventing the Incarnae from directly taking action against their Chosen.

                                                            They play the Games of Divinity not (just) out of addiction, but to play for time. Even a mighty Lunar or Solar can't fight their way through a hundred Celestial lions, so they stay in the Jade Pleasure Dome, enjoying the game and talking quietly among themselves about how they might solve this problem.

                                                            Commentary: This headcanon/houserule accomplishes a few things my group finds fun and desirable. First, the Incarnae are not callous dicks. They are prudent, or maybe just cowards (which isn't important to us). But, what they aren't doing is interfering in the doings of the Exalted, at least not directly. No matter how many of her "beloved" Chosen die at the hands of the Dragon-Blooded pogrom, Luna isn't going Crinos on the Dynasts. The Maidens aren't going to up and karate kick Chejop one day when they have had enough of him. This also justifies any major world-ending issues needing to be solved by the Exalted themselves.

                                                            Second, the follies of the Exalted are theirs to bear in full. The Solars became mad tyrants because they did. The Sidereals screwed up the Loom because they chose to. Etc.

                                                            Sidereal Charms:
                                                            Sidereals don't have a bunch of charms off-limits due to the Maidens. Rather, they are limited due to their own hubris. Must like the Maidens are bound by their nature, their duty to Fate, so are the Sidereals. Only when they act in accordance with the needs of Fate, accepting their place, rather than seeking their own agenda, can they access some of their Charms (or Greater Astrology). The squabbles of the Bronze and Gold Factions, the jockeying in committee for position and to advance personal agendas, all this pollutes the Sidereals.

                                                            Commentary: This means Siddies can unleash Greater Astrology, and powers like it, in times of great need. It also means they don't need to ask "Mother May I?" of the Maidens. It does mean they need to get their personal sh*t together. Essentially,if you ever wanted an Essence Three Chosen of Endings to grab Ayesha and Chejop, knock their heads together over and over and yell "Stop it!" This lets that happen. Does it limit Sids? Yeah, but they have always been limited. This at least makes the limit self-imposed, rather than arbitrarily done by the Maidens.

                                                            The Usurpation:
                                                            Was actually a war. The Sids didn't just bring Dragon-Blooded into it. They had several Lunars, gods, and even a few Solars in on it. Each party was told whatever they wanted to hear to get them to participate. A young Solar who balked at the excesses of his Elders, for example, might be told this is just a purge of the worst offenders, after which a new Deliberative will form with young, bright minds like theirs at the front. The plan was to always kill these otehr , of course, after they were used/weakened.

                                                            The result was a Creation-wide Charlie Foxtrot. The DBs traitors were not able to insinuate themselves into every Gens, and the result was entire armies of Dragon-Blood duking it out. Lunars fought each other and Solars Alliances shifted and were broken, and in the end a majority of the Elders of every type of Chosen were dead, and the Solars were almost all dead and their Exaltations imprisoned. Oops.

                                                            Lunar Empires:
                                                            I never really cared for how under-represented the Lunars were. In my mind, they ruled vast nations in many places. They are powerful shapeshifters, after all, and the Wyld Hunt doesn't reach everywhere. I imagine Lunars murdering and replacing loyal princes who pay tribute to the Realm, resisting quietly, waiting for the moment to strike. In the deep West, a Dragon-Blooded Admiral is actually a Lunar who relies heavily on Sorcery and unique Gifts. She is helping foment the Realm civil war

                                                            Finally, I never could justify personally the Lunars not running the Guild (it fits too much), so they do, more or less. The Lunars involved don't agree on much, so they mostly agree to spread the Guild to counter the Realm and help newly Exalted Lunars escape. They hide from the Hunt and the Sids by acting through proxies and not all simply sitting on the board. They are simply major players here and there, who collectively can steer things where they "should."

                                                            Chejop Kejak:
                                                            Is fated to live exactly to the moment he realizes his plan for Creation failed. Then he will die.

                                                            Commentary: Fate is a bitch.

                                                            The Empress:
                                                            Is just plain gone. She isn't the Scarlet Bride, or a Deathlord, or just waiting in the wings for the right moment to make a comeback. What happened to her will remain forever a mystery, even to the Sidereals, even to the Maidens. Why? Because she has too much narrative power as a wildcard to squander by showing up. The children of the Realm will need to put on their big boy/girl jade britches and fix their nation, or die trying.

                                                            Celestial Exaltations:
                                                            Don't just carry memories from previous hosts. They actually develop what might be called taste. If a particular type of individual was particularly successful, the Exaltation might find someone similar the next time. If a host really didn't work out well, the Exaltation might head in another direction. Since Exaltations are mostly inchoate miracles, little understood, perhaps even by their creators, this isn't a predictable process.

                                                            This allows for fun stories. For example, the same Lunar Exaltation might continue to choose the descendants of a great kingdom, long after that kingdom has fallen to dust. There seems to always be one Chosen of Battles who is a total pacifist.

                                                            Additionally, Exaltations require a great deal of power from the being creating them. The Incarnae are all weaker personally for having invested in their Chosen, and the Elemental Dragons sleep at least partially because the blooding of their Chosen is a constant drain on their Essence (but a sustainable one).

                                                            Neverweres:
                                                            The Neverborn are not unkillable Chthulu things. They do "create" the Labyrinth, and their voices taint the Nephwraks, and make all sorts of horrible things. What they don't do is dictate terms to the Deathlords, or visibly manifest power. They don't get to play anymore. This leads to:

                                                            Deathlords:
                                                            Are powerful ghosts, and not all from the Usurpation. They are all former Exalts who gained their power by hearing and mastering the whispers of the Neverborn, taking the essence into themselves as a "substitute" for being Exalted. They are content to enact their passion plays, intrigues, and conquests to eternity. Some of them really want to end the world, but most of them just want to conquer it, or enact vengeance, or whatever. There's at least one former Sid, at least a few Lunars, and the rest are Solar Ghosts, most (but not all) from the Usurpation. They coordinated long enough to get and corrupt the Solar Exaltations, some of which escaped. At this point, though, they could erupt into war at a moment's notice.

                                                            The purpose of this is much the same as the Great Curse change. it pushes the powerful, bossy things into the background. Thus, Deathlords can be terrifying Necromancers, etc, and not look incompetent at destroying the world. Abyssals also gain more freedom of action, particularly if you change monstrances...

                                                            Monstrances:
                                                            Can be used to "invert" a Solar into an Abyssal, but can't actually contain or control an Abyssal. If an Abyssal dies, the Exaltation returns to the Monstrance. If the Monstrance is destroyed, the Exaltation just finds a dying person and Exalts them. Deathlords have to use other methods to control their Deathknights, such as gaining loyalty, credible threats, education, material wealth, etc.

                                                            This allows for truly independent Abyssals, and for Deathlords to bribe, cajole, or manipulate them into service.
