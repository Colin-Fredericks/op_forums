    Does Moonlit Huntress work?

    11-28-2017, 08:12 PM
    Arms of the Chosen has inspired me to look back at a number of older Artifacts that I'd like to get the same treatment. One of the things that intrigued me about the paragraph on moonsilver potentials was "cyclic change", which reminded me of Moonlit Huntress, in the 3rd Edition corebook.

    But after really looking at the attunement bonus, I have misgivings.


    The bow gives you a +1 non-Charm success to Stealth rolls when you're suffering Initiative Crash. That's a neat idea. Kind of useless if there's no cover to use for an attempt at establishing concealment, though. But at least it makes some sense that if your opponent crashes you (or if you're trying and failing so hard to Disengage that you Crash yourself), you might want to enter stealth and hide for the three turns necessary for your Initiative to reset to base.

    Though it would seem much easier just to shoot your opponent with a withering attack. So you might be able to score an Initiative Shift.

    (Incidentally, where is the rule that tells us attacking from concealment reveals you? I can find that rule alluded in Observer-Deceiving Attack, but it's never actually stated anywhere in the rules on Stealth. I have taken the matter to the Ask The Devs thread.)

    Anyway, taking the Thrown Charm as gospel, Moonlit Hunter forces you to choose between hiding for three rounds -- possibly in the same range band as the person you were struggling to Disengage from -- during which you cannot simply attack to regain Initiative, because the bow offers no way to attack while remaining concealed. And this is assuming that you had any cover in which to conceal yourself, possibly while at close range with a Melee-focused enemy.

    Oh, and remember that you can't flurry an action to hide (again) with anything else, such as the attack action that revealed you. Not without magic that this bow doesn't provide.


    Assuming you had cover and managed to hide for three turns, you reset to base Initiative. At which point the bow gives you +1 non-Charm success to Disengage actions.

    Personally, I think that if you haven't managed to use your concealment to Disengage already, or got lucky enough to have your enemy leave the range band to look for you elsewhere, you're going to be hurting for the loss of that Stealth bonus. It's not Disengage actions that take penalties while you try to move while hidden; it's the Stealth action that takes the penalty.

    Also, Disengaging costs 2 Initiative, succeed or fail. So, you could get out of the Stealth-boosted Initiative crash, only for your bow to encourage you to put yourself right back into Initiative Crash as you attempt to run away from a swordsman who already put you into Initiative Crash before.

    Has anyone tried using this Artifact? Has this worked out for them?


    Okay, so what about the next level, Half Moon, which is where anyone should be when first rolling Join Battle.

    +2 to Awareness rolls. Which you only get after making possibly the most important Awareness roll of the entire fight. Not even necessarily the Join Battle roll itself, but perhaps an Awareness check beforehand to notice an impending ambush against yourself.

    The level of ironic "Fuck You, Player" is beyond words to describe.

    Even aside from that, is the bonus actually useful in a fight in progress? If you've just rolled into combat, any ambushing attacker has probably revealed himself with the opening attack. If you've instead fought your way back up through Initiative Crash and base Initiative, you're hopefully coming out of Stealth, and in the wildest of dreams, you're still in stealth. Being concealed, and not yet having a strong advantage, you're probably wanting more to remain concealed than trying to find anyone else who is concealed, especially because the swordsman beating your ass probably doesn't need to try hiding from you.


    The next level is Waxing Moon, which offers an even better bonus to disengaging, and an equal one to rushing.

    Why does an archer want to rush anyone? Also, rushing is impossible in stealth without magic, and the bow offers no magic to handle that restriction.

    Also also, why did Disengaging stop being important between the Waning Moon and Waxing Moon stages? Why was Awareness more important in the Half Moon stage?


    Finally, the Full Moon stage lets you reflexive Aim in order to attack targets at long range.

    Wait, is this bow trying to help you achieve safety through range, or stealth? If it was that concerned with range, why the Awareness check half-way through, and why does it give you a Stealth bonus in Crash instead of something more useful for running away, like discounting the Initiative cost of the Disengage action?

    Also, why would the Waxing Moon stage give you the option to rush an opponent when the Full Moon stage encourages you to attack from as far away as possible?



    Obviously, this thing is the rushed and poorly considered afterthought of the Corebook, as I've said before. Everything about it is a joke, especially its single boring Evocation, and I can't imagine why the Lunar thought it was worth hiding from the Wyld Hunt. Except maybe to conceal her shame.



    Okay, so what ideas can be salvaged from this?

    The basic idea of a bow that grows from concealing darkness to shining strength is workable; make the bow's string a thing of moonlight that disappears when you're Crashed, forcing you to hide without the option of shooting arrows to wither back some Initiative. After you reset, the stealth bonus ends because the string of light has reappeared and you can start making withering attacks again, and the string is brightest and tightest when you have the most Initiative to use for a Decisive shot.

    The attunement bonus' essential idea would make an excellent Artifact cloak. Something that helps conceal you when you're Crashed, but which makes you extremely intimidating when you've got plenty of Initiative for making a lethal Decisive attack.

      Incidentally, I looked at the leaked copies of the Corebook back when it was under production. I'm sorry, I'm weak.

      I knew very early on that there wasn't going to be a moonsilver artifact in the Core, and I was fine with that.

      Sure, I was disappointed, but I wasn't upset. I know there's only so much space in a Corebook, and that coming up with good ideas for Lunars and their metal has proven difficult in the past. Lunars weren't even coming out until after the Exigents, so there was plenty of time for good Lunar/moonsilver stuff later.

      Moonlit Huntress upsets me. Because someone thought that shoving a poorly developed afterthought into the book at the metaphorical last minute was acceptable, when that effort and word-count could have been spent polishing content which was actually worthwhile. Or fixing the disappointing paragraph on the potential of moonsilver.

      This isn't even a recent sentiment, on my part. I'm more disappointed now than before, but it was always obvious how poorly Moonlit Huntress compared to the others. I always knew it was newer than them, as well as shorter, and more boring.

      Sure, it took until now to see just how broken this poor thing is (though anyone who tried to use it out of the box would have figured it out then rather than later), but did no one think we would NOTICE right away that it was pretty poor compared to the other 3-dot Artifacts? Did they think we wouldn't care if the token moonsilver thing was halfhearted, as long as a token of any sort was included?
