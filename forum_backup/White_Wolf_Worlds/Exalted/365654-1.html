    The big thing to consider is that we basically have no rules whatsoever for Fate Error. It is strongly suggested that they can cause by massive expenditures of Essence. What shows up in the Loom would be highly dependent on what the Kilomote is used to accomplish.

    The Thousand-Forged Dragon can absorb energy from a Manse and the surrounding area to release a multi-kilomote explosion. The effect being 100 dice of damage in a (manse rating) miles area. The results should show up in the Loom as the threads of everything in the blast radius being cut short. The destruction of the demesne and mangled dragon lines should also show up prominently.

    The Five-Metal Shrike on the otherhand only uses a half-kilomote and does infinite damage in a 25 yard radius and 50 levels of damage in a 500 yard radius. Less energy, smaller radius, but really far more damaging. Again death and destruction on a truely massive scale.

    For comparison the Adamant Sorcery Spell Total Annihilation only costs 65 motes. This spell does 50 Levels of damage in a 50 yard radius and expands for Essencex5 ticks at a rate of 10 yards/tick doing 25 Levels of damage/action.

    The most notable thing here is the obvious loss of efficiency as mote expenditure goes up. A Kilomote might be pretty, but I doubt it will show up on the Loom in an appreciably greater way than the Total Annihilation spell.

                                                    Originally posted by Mercury View Post
                                                    Okay. I'm seeing a number of questions along these lines, and I understand. The book says things like 'Essence use disturbs destiny' and all that, so it's pretty easy to make assumptions along the lines of 'The raw power radiating out damages the giant fate machine!'

                                                    What you have to understand is that 'What happens if 1000 motes' isn't an interesting question because the volume of essence is only tangentially related to the Loom. The Loom of Fate has descriptive and predictive functions, and it's totally capable of tracking enormously powerful things. Greater Elemental Dragons, mountain-sized monsters, entire cities of sorcerers- those aren't automatically disruptions in the lines of destiny.

                                                    What disrupts the Loom of Fate is when a things happen which had basically no chance of happening. If your Solar casts Rain of Doom and melts a city to nothing, that came out of nowhere. The disruption is equal exactly to the Loom being wrong about everything in that area suddenly melting in a torrent of acid. Using the proper magic, or planning, you can mitigate that entirely by letting Heaven know that someone is going to melt everything with acid rains.

                                                    If you use those thousand motes to temporarily look really cool, then you'll get virtually no discernible disruption at all. If you use the thousand motes to transform an entire nation into a pillar of fire which spews out fire-birds to fly around and drop fire-shit on people, it will be a massive disruption (although not something the Loom hasn't seen before). If you use it to create a creation-spanning megaplague which kills 100% of everyone, you essentially break the Loom.
                                                    Honestly, I was reading through the Sidereals book and found the "high essence expenditures mess up the loom" to be pretty stupid and confusing. Looking at it as a "high mote expenditures are often correlated with highly implausible/unexpected happenings" makes the paragraphs about the loom make much more sense.
