    I wrote a backstory for Ma-Ha-Suchi

    04-24-2018, 09:08 AM
    So the current game I'm in has become pretty Lunar-centric for the time being, and we're spending Calibration in the Nameless Lair. The version of Ma-Ha-Suchi that we've been interacting with is a liiiittle different from the book canon - he's still pretty crazy, but most of the worst parts (cannibalism, breeding pits) have turned out to be Realm propaganda (whew).

    This focus and a more general "need-to-write" itch got me thinking about who he might have been in the First Age before becoming The Wolf with the Red Roses. Things got a little out of hand as I got thinking what it might be like to be a mortal in the First Age, and then suddenly find yourself as one of the most powerful beings in Creation, wildly overqualified for even the biggest problems you'd faced up to that point.

    So this was fun to write, even though it ended up with an element of "and then things got worse".

    C&C welcome, this was pretty much just for fun!

      Part 1 - Childhood

      The winter woods stretched on to the north, and then to the fae wyldlands hundreds of miles beyond. Now and then, the march of birch and pine parted to reveal a vast sea of frozen white, flat but for the wind-drive dunes like the caps of ocean swells. Regarding this untouched landscape beneath the grey sky, the boy was, for the moment, alone.

      His companions shouted in the woods behind him as they played, their voices muffled by snow and cold and easy to ignore. Less easy to dismiss was the object of his attention – the field was dominated by a single tree, taller still and stranger than any he had ever seen, looking as though it had grown merely to challenge Mount Meru itself. He imagined that he could sense its roots spreading all beneath the frozen land, forbidding the lesser trees to approach its mighty domain.

      Slowly, he stepped out onto the field. He moved at first with reverence, then after one step and then another he was charging across the snow. His cloak billowed behind him like an unfurled sail as he flew until the drifts deepened, reaching over the tops of his boots and then nearly to his hips. He bared his teeth and pressed on, the sound of his friends now entirely lost to his ears.

      When finally his leather-gloved hand slapped against the goal, he paused a moment to catch his breath. Still, this colossus of the north demanded tribute, and as his racing heart slowed, his gaze traveled up the tree and towards the heavens – surely no tree in Creation could be larger than this improbable behemoth.

      Quickly, he peeled off his glove and touched bare hand to the trunk, fine fingernails digging into the gaps between the bark it wore like plates of heavy armor. Blue eyes sparked with a wild fury, and he began to heave himself upwards when laughter rang out behind him.

      “What in the Sun’s light are you doing?”

      The boy’s grip faltered and he turned back, seeing another standing at the edge of the deepening snow, a grin on his face. He glanced between the runaway and the others as they struggled to catch up, picking their way across the field.

      There was really no answering that question. Spellbroken and abashed, he bent to collect his discarded glove and moved to meet Caj. The snow and ice caked to his legs had grown heavy, and he moved slowly, grimacing as he picked his way back through the half-collapsed trail he’d created.

      “Don’t tell me you’d thought to climb that monster,” he continued, giving the tree a cursory look before turning his eyes back. “I wouldn’t carry you home if you broke your leg.”

      The boy laughed, short of breath, clapping his bare hand on the other’s shoulder as he finally reached him. “No, I suppose you wouldn’t.” He straightened and pulled his glove back on, glancing back towards the tree. For the life of him, he could not recall what, or if at all he had been thinking as he charged it with such fervor. Already he knew he would suffer for this on the next day.

      Together they crossed the remainder of the field to where the others had given up completely.
      *

      Teeth chattering as his mother’s handmaid helped him out of his sodden clothes, the boy tried and failed to avoid the matriarch’s stare. She had watched this affair in silence, her expression unreadable to his eyes until he was safely tucked within the folds of a warm robe.

      “Lise says that you tried to climb that tree,” she said, finally.

      “She lied!” he began, then stopped, casting his eyes aside and calming his voice, “I mean, I didn’t really. Mostly I only wanted to see it up close.”

      “How old are you now?” his mother asked, rising and indicating that he should take her chair as she held her hand out to receive a hairbrush from the maid.

      “Twelve years,” he answered, omitting that this was only as of a month ago. He sat down, glancing at the handmaid from beneath his eyelashes. She smiled reassuringly.

      His mother circled behind him, placing a hand atop his head as she began to brush out his short, but nonetheless disheveled blond hair. “You’re getting a little old to be playing that way,” she chided, although her voice was not unkind.

      “I don’t think he’s all that old yet, Amalia,” the maid countered, a bit of a laugh in her voice, “he still sounds like a little bird, clearly a child.”

      “Oh, whose side are you on, Maja!” the mother cried, but she too giggled. He flushed, holding a hand up to hide his face only to have it quickly swatted away. “Don’t let him fool you! Point being, my darling,” she continued, her tone sobering, “you shouldn’t be trying to climb such a monster anyway. You would fall to your death, and then what would we do?”

      “I’m sorry,” he answered softly, “…is Father angry?”

      “No; why should he be? We only want you to be careful-“ she trailed off, the end of her statement rising uncertainly.

      “…and to be aware of your station,” Maja continued, meeting the mother’s eyes. “Some things just do not do for a growing lord. Although,” she glanced from one to the other, “twelve is still young enough to forgive such things, ma’am.”

      “With consideration to-?” her eyebrow raised.

      “Aye, ma’am, true.”

      His attention drifted around the room as their conversation trailed off. They tried not to discuss it around him, problems that concerned the adults of a household could not be fully hidden from the children. Between the whispers when he was beneath notice, comments spoken with the expectation he’d not heed them, and letters left out openly, he knew that their house was in troubling times. Prosperity of late made the city an attractive bit of land for the young Dragonblooded of certain Gens who wished to cut their teeth on something rather risk-free, and mortal government had little power against the whims of the Exalted host.

      The worry within the house was plain if one would only see it, and their conversation now was not the first of its kind – the undercurrent of all being that he, as heir to their governance, must soon grow and stand with their father to better present a strong front. Such pressure of age was not put on his friends or even his siblings, but, again, they did not live under the same axe of responsibility. He felt it keenly, even if he did not understand it entirely.

      “I’m sorry,” he said again, “I will temper myself in the future.”

      “Such a darling!”

      “You hush, he has you wrapped around his finger, hasn’t he?” His mother sighed. “Are you warmed up now, dear? If so, please do hurry to your room and dress – your father wishes to see you before dinner.”

      The boy rose from the chair and, turning, gave a short bow at the waist to his mother and her handmaid. “Of course – thank you,” he said, before disappearing into the hallway.

      *

      The clothing of the house differed from the standard garb of the north which his playclothes mimicked. Within the household of the governor, family and staff alike dressed in ways that lent a nod towards the Gens, which in turn flirted with the styles of Meru. In present circumstance it seemed a rather contradictory style decision, but it was all a part of the theatre of position which had in recent years been their primary concern.

      It was a fine line they walked, keeping the Gens at arm’s length while also not burning bridges. Doubtless the governor held some vain hope that one or another of his children might prove to have the blood of Dragons and save them from this ongoing struggle, but such dreams paid out rarely enough as to not warrant any thought at all.

      For the boy’s part, this theatre was what he had always known, and so the grandeur of the house meant little to him. He was as at home in the hides and furs of his peers as the silk and lace of his family, each seeming only different costumes for different occasions, and his movement and manners shifted fluently to suit either.

      Clutching his leatherbound portfolio, the boy hurried down the hallway to the large double-doors at the end. Though made of dense carved hardwood, they nonetheless were well-maintained and opened easily to his hand. He slipped past and into his father’s offices, pulling the door closed behind him.

      This room reflected the rest of the household, combining the modern fashions with the close comforts of the north. A large fire burned at one side underneath trophies of the hunt, bringing light to the rather dark décor. The governor’s desk had pride of place within the chamber, and the man himself behind it looked up as the boy came in.

      “Mother said you wished to see me,” he said softly as he approached the desk, shifting the portfolio to both hands. “I thought to bring my schoolwork; I assumed you meant to see it.”

      His father beaconed him forward. “Good evening – I heard you had quite a day. But yes, yes, let’s see what I’ve been paying your tutor for.”

      A small smile crossed his face as he moved around the desk, unwrapping the tie from the book before placing it in front of his father and opening it. “We’ve been working on maths and the old texts – some fiction, but mostly historical. He has not so much had me practice music lately,” a detail he found aggravating but could live with, as needs were.

      Placing a hand on the boy’s shoulder, Gerhard reviewed the papers in silence. The pages turned slowly as he appraised the work, then, finally, gave a nod.

      “Hm, well done. Your penmanship needs some work – you write too slowly, the ink bleeds into the paper and blurs. And you’ve mixed your ‘Ma’s and ‘Hi’s in a few places.”

      “Teacher had already marked those,” he said quickly, but still lowered his head in acknowledgement. “I will continue to work on it.”

      “Of course. I’d like you to write me a letter, weekly, so that I can see your progress. No less than a page, and in formal tone. Now then,” his father said, closing the book and handing it back to him, “what is this that Lise says you’d gotten up to today?”

      He hid his frown – was she telling everyone? – and kept himself composed this time. “We had been playing outside of city to the north, in the forest. I found an enormous tree in the middle of a clearing, and only wished to see it more closely.”

      “Ah, so you were out there,” Gerhard said, almost to himself. “Were you satisfied with your inspection?”

      “Not hardly, I have never seen anything so tall, let alone growing by itself. The wind across the field should have toppled it before it grew a quarter – no, a tenth so large. How can it be?”

      “Maybe the clearing was not always a clearing. Maybe this behemoth merely claimed that land for itself, and it was all the other, weaker trees that died.”

      The boy’s questions died in his mouth, his mind suddenly full again with thoughts of the Dragonblooded. His father seemed to notice the change, and continued.

      “That land has a Dragonline – you know of these – a rare one, run here all the way from the East. It’s why our lands are so green, cold as they are, and where it rises to the surface, that tree grows.”

      “So it is only that tree’s luck, then,” he replied, tone morose.

      “This is life. It is not for us to know the caprice of the gods,” his father said, his voice firm, dressed in finality. The boy’s grip on his portfolio tightened, but he bowed his head.

      “I will see you at dinner then, Father?”

      “Yes, of course. You are dismissed.”

      “Good evening,” he said with a second bow, more formal, before excusing himself from the room.

          Part Two - Against the Dragons

          The sun had begun to melt through the snow, and in bits and patches, the grass was visible and beginning to revive. The warm seasons were too short, but celebrated when they arrived – even if the regional joke was that the ‘warm season’ was more aptly called the ‘mud season’. It was almost as much due to this as not wanting to rush the afternoon that the pair strolled so slowly between the trees, arm in arm.

          Over the past years, the household’s tense situation had worsened, and at this stage they had only to thank how comparatively slowly time seemed to move for the Dragonblooded. Five years or more meant very little to those who measured their lifespans in centuries. Still, the noose was tightening, and it was no longer a matter of ‘whether’ but ‘how long’ the family would retain governance of the city.

          This was never outside of the boy’s mind, but it was currently tucked into some deeper corner, a black spot that he chose not to prod at least for today. Lise spoke freely, and even a bit too much, as they picked their way through the forest. Her voice flitted in and out of his thoughts as he tried to recall the northward path from their childhood, guiding them both around the wetter parts of the trail, wool cloaks gathered up in their free hands to keep the hems safe.

          As the path opened finally to the clearing, the girl paused in her speaking. Looking from the field to her companion, she let out a surprised giggle. “Here? Really?”

          He inclined his head slightly, the hint of embarrassment rising on his face. “I quite like it here…if you promise not to tell, this time.”

          “You still hold that against me?”

          In place of answer, he released her arm, striding out into the field. Its openness had allowed the snow to disappear completely, and so it looked as vibrant and summer-green as he had hoped, but was rather squashier than he had accounted for. Nevertheless he walked on for several paces before turning back to her with a somewhat pained smile. He held a hand out to her, and she laughed again.

          “Your shoes are ruined!”

          “You don’t need to tell me. Are you coming, or no?”

          She hesitated, then took a few tentative steps out to him, taking his hand. He grinned, turning and leading her further into the field. She followed, and as shoes and stockings alike fell victim to the water and mud, she eventually dropped the hem of her cloak to let it fall where it might. Though he kept his wrapped over his arm, his pace hurried towards the tree at the center of the field, his eyes fixed on it.

          Since that day, the tree had grown monolithic in his mind, though he could not say the why of it. It was a place of power not just for the Dragonline on which it grew, but because its very existence felt both a blessing and an affront of the highest order. It made him feel very large and very small in turn, and that strangeness drew him towards it over and over again. He went to it often, and thus it seemed natural that he should bring her here now, as she stirred a similar contrast within him.

          “Don’t tell me you mean us to climb it!”

          “By the gods, no, and don’t you start that rumor again,” he groaned as they finally reached the tree. As he grew taller, the impression of its size had never once faltered. He reached out, placing a hand to the bark, and she did the same.

          “It feels…more alive than I expected,” Lise said, after a moment.

          “Warm,” he agreed. She lowered her head and smiled.

          They stood in silence for several moments, silence that was not entirely awkward, but not perfectly comfortable. She twisted her gloves in her hands, and he gave the pretense of gazing up towards the top of the tree, though occasionally stealing a glance towards her.

          “You have brought us far from home,” she said finally, and he turned.

          “I was under the impression that the disruptions of my household irritated you. And so…here we are, quite safe from them.”

          She turned, a look of mock-scandal on her face. “I never implied any such-“ she began, then yelped and stumbled. He leapt forward half a pace, catching her. Looking down, he saw her stockinged foot raised above the mud, the empty shoe stuck fast beneath it.

          “Lean on the tree,” he said, kneeling to pry her shoe from the muck. She covered her mouth and laughed.
          “What a state we are in!”

          “Give me your foot!”

          She held out her foot daintily, giggling as he held out the mud-caked shoe. Putting a hand to the back of her calf, he slipped it onto her foot, and her giggling faded. Slowly putting her foot back down, she blushed and looked away. He rose and she cast him a quick, shy glance, eyes traveling down to his muddy knees, and then she laughed again – a short, anxious gasp of a thing.

          The boy raised a hand to her cheek, streaking it with mud, but her eyes held no shock or anger as he drew her to her toes. The moment had seemed to both of them as alive as the tree they stood under, and she returned his kiss without surprise or hesitation.

          *

          Their return to the city drew no small amount of attention, as covered in mud as they were. Initially those who saw them were concerned that some accident had befallen them, but it didn’t take long for the rumors to move in another direction. Oblivious or heedless, the boy returned to his home, succeeding in evading his family in order to shed his ruined clothes and bathe away the evidence of the day’s travels.

          Robed and combing out his long hair, he was congratulating himself on his stealth when a member of the household staff let herself in after a cursory knock, bowing her head as she took a half-step through the doorway to prevent it being shut on her just as quickly.

          “Your father wishes to see you.”

          He mouthed a curse before looking at her over his shoulder. “Does time permit I dress myself?”

          “I expect so, however, he does ask that you come straightaway.”

          The boy gave a nod, and she retreated. He laid the comb down, strongly suspicious of just what his father wanted to discuss. Frankly, he thought to himself, it was none of his business, but it would not do to go before him indignant and irritated – that only gave his father the advantage.

          Shedding the robe, he dressed – the household fashion’s resemblance to Meru’s had not changed over the years. If anything, it had grown more strict even, a substantial line item in the budget. They still walked the line of keeping both the locals and the Gens comfortable with their leadership. It was not permitted to defy the Gens openly, but if the local population loved their Governor and the Governor in turn seemed in line with what the Gens wanted…well, it had gotten them this far, and the visual warfare was one of the simplest aspects of it all.

          He understood it now far better than he had in his younger days, and could appreciate the scope of his father’s wisdom. However, the more he realized, the more the cracks began to show, making the futility of it all that much more apparent – they were all only kidding themselves at the end of the day. Dragonblooded always get what Dragonblooded want, and if they wanted this city, which they did, they would have it sooner or later.

          For this reason, he was torn between continuing the fight, and just throwing his arms up in frustration with it all. It was readily apparent that they were fooling themselves more than they were fooling the Exalted.

          Still, he considered, as he sat and tilted his mirror to get a better look before taking up a cushion of powder, frustration was weakness. He had his father’s blood and tenacity, and so it was not within him to show his belly to the Gens. His household’s mask of bravery was an excellent start, and the mask must lead to a solid counter-attack.

          Of course this was illegal, but the social efforts of the house weren’t quite as easy to prosecute as it would be if they had instead chosen to take up torches and pitchforks. To have both parties favor them was a good start, but favor is where his father’s plan began and ended. This had worked so far, but eventually when the goals of one or the other differed, the plan would fall apart.

          Leaning forward and opening his mouth just so, he painted his lips. He wondered if the Gens would conquer them within his lifetime, and, if so, his life would change significantly. Doubtless a bloody takeover would be both unnecessary and unpopular with the citizens, but it seemed unlikely that their new Dragonblooded governor wouldn’t install his own men in the family’s place. The Exalted host had no need to be so generous.

          His gaze flickered towards the clock, then back to the mirror. Making a small, “hmph,” sound back in his throat, he lined his eyes. Some time had passed since he had been told to hurry, but if he could not confront his father with anger on the matter of his earlier whereabouts, he would express his displeasure by minutes.

          At last he rose, untucking his hair from the collar of his coat and giving himself a final glance before leaving his room to suffer his scolding.

          *

          The old doors which closed off his father’s study were, at this moment, attended. The measured strike of the boy’s heels against the hardwood skipped by half a beat, breaking time, but he forced himself to recover and continue on. Gerhard’s doors were only rarely watched by household staff, and this could only imply matters of state. He began to dread that he had also misstepped in the figurative sense.

          Bowing his head, the servant pushed the door open just enough to allow himself to slip inside the study, leaving the boy waiting outside. Brief moments stretched on before he reappeared, holding the door aside and bowing. With bile in his throat, the boy stepped into the room, head raised as though he did not at this moment wish to disappear.

          He met Gerhard’s eyes first, not glowing with the inner coals of wrath, but underneath a creased brow that communicated wordlessly that they would be speaking privately later. Those eyes shifted just slightly towards the left, and the boy traced their path to the back of an armored man who bore the insignia of the Gens embroidered on the cape thrown over his shoulder. This man was just beginning to turn, and the boy stretched one foot out, sweeping a deep bow before the turn had been completed.

          In measured tones, his father introduced them to each other. The visitor, Hachiro, was fortunately not himself a Dragonblooded, although he held the rank of Sochei underneath the Shozei Sei Arata, who was a member of the Exalted host. This made Hachiro a representative of the Gens, a detail which failed to bring the boy any comfort.

          He rose from the bow as Hachiro was lowering the fist he’d raised to his left breast in reciprocation. Though Hachiro couldn’t have avoided noticing the faux pas the boy had committed, he was well-bred enough to ignore it, turning his attention back to Gerhard. The boy did the same, hoping that his face was not too red.

          The meeting that followed was exactly what their house had long been dreading. Sei Arata wished to take a more direct role in these lands, was the precise way it was stated, and their city would be his ideal staging point. This was not a takeover, Hachiro readily assured them, but rather a state of mutual cooperation. The governor would help Sei Arata settle in and teach him the ways of the city and its people, and in turn, the Dragonblooded would lighten Gerhard’s workload considerably.

          Father and son did not meet eyes during this discussion – such breaches of etiquette were anyway unnecessary. There was little that either could say. Hachiro was only the messenger, the advance guard, and their usurper would arrive sooner or later regardless of what transpired in this conversation.

          Once the message had been received to the Sochei’s satisfaction, Hachiro refused an invitation to dinner, insisting that he must return straightaway. Gerhard bid his son wait in the office as he saw Hachiro out.

          Left alone, the boy looked around the room again. The comfortable dark wood and clutter of the room suddenly felt stifling, heavy, and oppressive. Books on their shelves, papers and ledgers on the desk, the sum of generations spent keeping this city, all come to nothing in one day. How they had struggled, how he had done his part all his years, and now this one star-blessed fuck could just sweep them all aside. Anger rose, souring his mouth and blackening the edges of his vision. It boiled up, a scream held behind his teeth, but he fought it down. To howl his rage in the face of the Dragon would accomplish less than nothing – if their actions were in vain, how much less were their cries?

          Raising his eyes briefly to the door and listening for footsteps, he moved behind his father’s desk and lifted the paper that Hachiro had brought. It was a heavy linen paper, not the vellum or pressed pulp more common in the north. The ink on this thing was richly pigmented, still smelling of soot, and had been placed on the page by a deft hand above an embossed stamp that shone with, was it…yes, white jade dust.

          Flicking his wrist, he threw the document back onto the desk. The takeover would be exceedingly gradual, some sort of crab-boiling scheme. Sei Arata wished to avoid rebellion, even though it would be his right and duty to quash any such notions with any force he deemed necessary. Sei Arata was either a gentle man or an anxious one, not yet blooded much in combat of the physical or social varieties. With his selection of their location in particular, the boy assumed the latter.

          The door opened, and he looked back up, experiencing a moment of almost-vertigo as Gerhard approached the desk that he, for once, stood behind. Lowering his head, he moved back out, switching places again with his father. The older man dropped to his seat with a sigh, picking up the paper and giving it the briefest of glances before letting it fall.

          “The Sochei waited for an hour. This will make it back to Sei Arata in the form of a report on our internal weakness. You have not only disrespected, but also undermined me today.”

          The boy did not raise his eyes. There was no argument to be made. Any explanation would come off as a petulant excuse, and honestly, the only reason he had to offer was no better than that. His father was silent for many moments, seemingly debating with himself before he spoke again.

          “End your affair with Lise. They’ll see it was weakness, distraction, and, hell, it could be. Silver Lady forbid you got her with child, that is the least thing we need right now.”

          Breaking his penitent stance, the boy looked at his father incredulously. “That is-“

          Gerhard waved a hand, cutting his son off. “Don’t even argue. Our days of freedom are over, and we must turn our efforts towards finding a place for ourselves under Sei Arata.”

          The boy settled back into silence, his thoughts circling the events of the day. He was not foolish enough to think that his delayed appearance at the meeting had the slightest effect on its outcome – that had been decided long before the meeting occurred – but his actions had affected their options going forward. His error was in assuming the purpose of his father’s summons, and as much as he could blame the staff for not alerting him, responsibility fell on his shoulders.

          This guilt began to form into the bones of an idea. “What if-“ he began, and Gerhard paused from organizing the papers on his desk.

          “Yes?”

          He paused, then shook his head. “No, nothing. I’m sorry.”

          Gerhard shrugged. The meeting seemed to have take a lot of the strength out of him, and the boy wasn’t sure that he wanted to begin to dissect the issue in his father’s presence.

          “May I leave?”

          “You may.”

          Bowing his head, the boy returned to his room.

              Part Three - Exaltation

              If at the beginning they had considered that Sei Arata’s plan followed a slow pace, in reality it had been glacial. Five years had seen Gerhard still enjoying his title, although by now he was governor in name only. His family still held their lands, courtesy of what they were careful to refer to as the Dragonblooded’s generosity.

              Month by month their existing power was stripped away, and it was certain that Gerhard would soon retire without passing the title down to any of his children. The younger ones had not had the training that the governance would require, and his eldest son, formerly most promising of them all, had all but defected to Arata’s camp. Taken in with the ways and fashions of Meru, his loss was a blow to the family.

              At the least, it seemed that everything would work out for him – it was no secret that he’d formed a close relationship with the former Sochei, now Chuzei Hachiro. The mortal who had first brought the news of the axe’s fall had frequently returned to handle the logistical concerns of moving in Sei Arata and his attachés. This ended up becoming a collaborative effort between himself and the governor’s son, who had volunteered readily to ensure the process flowed as smoothly as possible. “For the good of our people,” he had said, having little luck in improving his family’s opinions. “Our time may be done, but they don’t need to suffer.”

              Whether or not they could see the value in this way of thinking, his family made him nearly an outcast within his own house. Servants were hard to secure the help of, his siblings gave him a wide berth, and even his parents’ voices tended to fall silent when he entered the room. It wasn’t unexpected then that he began to spend less and less time with the household, and more time with Hachiro, and the only question was when he would give up the pretense and move in with the Gens entirely. Still, he hung on with a tenacity either born of obliviousness to the hostility around him, or some futile hope that they would eventually see things his way.

              The last warmth was fading from the season, leaving their land in the grip of the ice-laden air which held sway for most of the year. In darkness he had slipped away from his family’s house without any great difficulty – they willfully ignored his comings and goings, and even if he had been caught they would likely assume he was meeting Hachiro. Gerhard’s once eagle-eyed powers of observation had failed him in these last years, and it would have made no difference to his assumption that his son had snuck out in the hides and furs of the north, not the silks of his new ‘allies’.

              This decision on his part was a risk he could hardly shoulder, but now, even with all of this drawing to a close, his nerve began to waver and he fled into the cloudless night, seeking that constant behemoth of his childhood.

              It rose in defiance as it always had, longer than the history of his family spanned. Steadfast friend and taunting adversary this tree had been, springing from the Dragonline that tenaciously ran its course over thousands of miles from the east. This tree, born from the same blood that gave Sei Arata’s line its power. As he had done many times since his youth, he pulled his gloves off and set his hands against the bark plates, digging his fingers into the gaps between, and wondered how he might drink some of this beast’s might and tenacity for himself.

              A futile dream, at his age. No-one joined the host of the Dragonblooded in adulthood. That hope had died, and along with it he was half tempted to let Arata carry through with his plan to tap the damn tree for a manse.

              He frowned, and his fingernails dug into the tree’s bark. Still, this thing had not been useless to him. There were always cracks, even in the armor of the mighty. Sei Arata would never have his manse – hell, Arata’s household whispered of his ineptitude behind his back. He couldn’t hope to accomplish anything of great scale if he could not even bring mortals’ lands under his grasp within these past years.

              Their struggle was almost at an end, and the Dragonblooded may have never realized who he wrestled with at all. The man smiled, although his expression wavered a little. If he won, how long would it last? Perhaps he was a fool for thinking the Gens would leave. The Dragons had power that mortals couldn’t begin to understand, and this little rebellion could all be quashed in moments. He might even be executed, despite his contingencies.

              He couldn’t let it frighten him. He had to keep moving forward until he no longer could, one way or another.

              The tree was warm beneath his hands, but the warmth hardly seemed to reach beneath his skin as it once had. He had made enemies of friends and friends of enemies, and none of it true – for a wild moment he wondered if this was why the tree had not blessed him, and why its warmth no longer reached his false heart. But that was a stupid thought, he reasoned, the kind of thinking that betrayed a man wasting time trying to make sense of a past he could no longer exercise control over.

              The wind, however, seemed entirely too willing to embrace him. He shuddered against the cold, taking his hands off the tree and pulling his furs tighter around his shoulders. The night was clear and cloudless, the moon and her stars holding court far above even the reach of the tree, the Fickle Lady and Fateful Maidens.

              He did not know if they cared at all for the struggles of mortals, but some conceit caused him to fix his gaze upon these gods. His breath was harsh with anxiety and frustration, wreathing him in white as he stared into the sky. Gradually, his fear began to lose ground. The luck of the damn Dragons was all that had put him here. With all things equal, his family would have won with far less effort than he had invested to this point – Arata was blessed with the position granted by his blood but was otherwise lacking.

              Anger flooded him at the thought of all his family’s work, his work, undone. Digging his nails into his palms, he screamed into the night – an indignity that he’d never have allowed if he wasn’t sure he was alone. The sound echoed around the clearing, the stillness of the air amplifying it and carrying it off into the forest.

              A small wind rose, coming back to him as if in answer. As it approached the clearing, the rustling of the trees was almost the sound of hushed whispers, giggling at his indiscretion. His heart jumped, and he turned, startled, scanning the empty field and finding nothing, of course.

              He lowered his hands, relaxing his fingers. The stress was affecting him now, and he figured he had better go back to Hachiro and at least sleep before confronting Arata. If he didn’t force himself to go, he would be here all night.

              He inhaled, held the breath, then breathed out. As he took a step away from the tree, he felt his hair gathered up, then beginning to slowly cascading down to his back as if running from someone’s fingers. Again he spun, feeling the rest of his hair fall free, away from the grasp of the smirking woman with outstretched hands.

              If before his heart had raced, now it seemed to stop, and his legs faltered, sending him to a knee even as he kept his eyes on her. There was no question that she was not human, nor Dragonblooded. Though he had never seen a Fae, there was something to her manner that brought them to his mind when her smirk deepened as she knelt and raised her hands to cup his face. He was immediately aware, and horrified, that his jaw had fallen open, and he was fairly panting with awe. Either indifferent or pleased by this, she put her thumbs in his mouth, running them over his teeth, his tongue.

              Before he could decide if he dared to be upset by this, she had let him go and risen back to her feet. He followed her example, and she turned from him, placing her hand to the tree and closing her eyes for several moments while he waited.

              “The fight is not over for thee,” she said, her voice crystalline, accented in the tones of a language that had passed from living speech, “But neither is thy suffering ended. Thou shalt see victory, and thy heart shall be loved again if thou canst bear the weight of thy joys and thy sorrows in the years to come.”

              She opened her eyes and turned her head, tilting it slightly to appraise the man fully.

              “Thou wilt stand alone and thou wilt stand with many, but thou wilt always be my creature. Thou art soft with hidden teeth, my love, and with claws to rend thy foes. To thee I will give much, but I will take much as well, and thou shalt gnash thy fangs and curse me in the same breath that thou blest me.”

              “But if thou art strong as I suppose, thou wilt have no fear in thy struggles. Thou wilt accept these things that I give and I take, and thou wilt move forward with thy wits and thy silvered tongue.”

              Her smile had grown grave, but here it touched the corners of her lips again.

              “Thou art beautiful – the passion of thy heart bloomed red compels me to love thee, Ma-Ha-Suchi.”

              He shuddered, transfixed by the silver light that bled from her eyes. He did not know that name or title, but the power of her voice seemed to tattoo it upon his soul. He raised a hand, clasping it to his chest.

              “You are-“

              She strode forward, laying her hand over his and leaning in to press a soft kiss beside his lips. The glow of her silver light brightened until he was forced to close his eyes.

              When he opened them again, it was to darkness. The moon and her stars were distant as the night moved on to the earliest hours of morning, and the cold began to settle again within his bones. It was only now a handful of hours until his plan would bear fruit, either sweet or bitter.

              *

              Sei Arata’s office was in every way different from his father’s. Spatially, it was far more open, but it felt claustrophobic for its insistence on ceremony. Tapestries of the Gens hung on the walls lest you ever forget where you were, armor and weapons stood on display lest you ever forget why. If a space lacked for inanimate reminders, it was occupied by staff of every type – record-keepers, guards, advisors, servants, and more besides.

              The Dragonblooded himself had pride of place, a desk foregone for a low table the likes of which were uncommon this far into the Threshold. It dominated the center of the room in a way that Arata no doubt wished to, and perhaps once had. The man found that his racing pulse calmed once he had entered the room ahead of Hachiro, and he found himself wondering if the Dragonblooded had always looked quite so small and weary.

              For his own part, he hoped that his own exhaustion didn’t show. Sleep had been scarce in the weeks leading to this day, and events had taken on a hazy, surreal quality, making his memory untrustworthy. To be honest, some part of him was not certain if he was at this meeting as opposed to just dreaming it after spending so much time planning.

              Even so, he tried to give no sign of his feelings as he bowed to the would-be governor – straight from the waist, in the style of this house – and then knelt before the table once Arata deigned to extend his arm in invitation. A servant moved forward in absolute, unintrusive silence to pour tea for both men. No one spoke before the Dragonblooded, and even the Chuzei waited by the man’s elbow, papers gathered up in his arms and ready to be placed on the table.

              But Arata wanted to take his time. The Dragonblooded studied him, and he wasn’t sure whether he saw some glint of suspicion – or even Essence – in the Shozei’s eye, or if it was just his imagination. Certainly Arata had reason enough to expect duplicity, but in all these years he had never once taken action to block the governor’s son.

              At last, the Shozei greeted him, his lack of title conspicuous in this place that built its entire hierarchy upon them. A mortal who did not belong to the Gens would rarely meet with someone of Sei Arata’s blood, but his own position within this territory gave him this slight chance. As much as he was beneath notice, he was even less a threat, and no doubt it served the Dragonblooded’s reputation well that he met personally with those who would hand their lands off to him.

              He bowed his head.

              “Shozei, you had wished to discuss the progress of transferring the territories to your control. It is known that, in your wisdom and generosity towards those that live here, you have acted with a soft hand and slow footfalls. However, after many years, leadership of the land is still split between yourself and the governor, which is logistically problematic.”

              Arata looked to Hachiro, who bowed and knelt, spreading his papers over the table. He cleared his throat before joining the conversation.

              “Regrettably, Shozei,” he began, and the man glanced over, seeing an almost imperceptible quiver to his hands, “the populace reports little confidence in the Gens ability at this juncture. Polling indicates that they see the Gens as a complicating factor in area politics, with little benefit to offer.”

              “Consequently,” he held out a document to Arata, and here his hand visibly shook, “I have a letter from Kazei Ran Moriko, co-signed by Taimyo-tuva Yuu Suzu requesting that definitive action be taken with regards to this city, or that you step down.”

              The Dragonblooded snatched the paper from Hachiro’s hands and examined it. His sneer turned to a look of incredulity after a few moments.

              “Give this land back to the mortals?” he demanded, throwing the letter down on the table. “Why in the hell would Suzu want such a thing?”

              “With respect, Shozei,” the man began, pressing forward although his throat had gone dry, “the entire value of this land is based upon that demense to the north. The office of the governor is prepared to grant exclusive use of it, geomantic rights included, to the Gens. This is, of course, pursuant to your removal.”

              “And if I choose the other option, to make a show of force?”

              “If you do,” he lowered his head in acknowledgement, then looked up and met Sei Arata’s eyes, “then the people will resist. Yes,” he interjected as the Dragonblooded began to interrupt, “they will be put down, if not by you then by the state. The Gens, however, would disapprove of such a result, and the area would need to be repopulated. A minor inconvenience in the grand scheme, but the lack of people would slow down manse development.”

              “It is worth adding,” the man continued, trying to keep his breathing level, “that the confidence of your men wavers, and may crumble if you choose such an action instead of taking steps to save face.”

              The Shozei’s jaw worked, teeth grinding, but the man thought that he saw fear in the Dragonblooded’s eyes. He did not push, letting Arata weigh the options before him. Hachiro was the first to break protocol, glancing towards the governor’s son, hesitating, and then placing a hand on his shoulder.

              The man only had a moment to register a pang of guilt for the Chuzei before Arata sprang up, this small act of treachery the last straw. The governor’s son managed to plant one foot beneath himself to begin to rise, but in that small amount of time Arata had seized the daiklave from his guard and held it beneath his opponent’s jaw.

              Panic swept over and through him like a cold wave, and then he was calm. His field of vision narrowed to include only Arata. As he caught his breath, his eyes flicked around the room. Arata’s guards had their hands on their blades, the weapons half-drawn.

              He took in a breath, and the air seemed to vibrate around him, in his throat and lungs, growing warm within his blood. He exhaled, and the feeling seemed to flood from him, wreathing Arata’s men in the sympathetic hum. Ever so slightly, their eyes turned towards him.

              The man smiled, feeling the tips of his canine teeth press into the flesh of his lower lip.

              “This is most indecorous, Arata.”

              Arata’s eyes widened, the daiklave falling from his hand as he stepped back. The man was almost taken aback at this extreme reaction, but his confusion only deepened when Arata dropped to his knees, pressing his face to the floor.

              The feeling in the room was broken as the guards averted their eyes, and even Hachiro pushed himself away from the governor’s son, horrified. Confusion swelled and blossomed into indignation, and the man’s face twisted in injured anger, “What is this?”

              A door opened behind him, hurried footfalls entering the office before a hand landed on his shoulder, squeezing and half-restraining.

              “Clear the room,” an unfamiliar voice commanded, and the Dragonblooded and his household left within moments, none of them as much as stealing a glance at the man.

              “Terribly sorry to arrive so late. We have a bit to discuss, you and I.”

              *

              Whatever else he could say about Her, Luna in Her divine wisdom had spoken the truth. The days to follow were some of the best and bitterest of his life, and he had soared with elation to be admitted to the ranks of the Celestial host, but he had also strived to claw Her spark from his chest upon hearing the effects this would have on the life he had been living.

              This conflict was to be expected, his Sidereal advised him. Even to the mightiest of Exalts, this transition was sudden and traumatic. But, he was reassured, the feeling would pass. It always did. His training would take much of his time and leave no space for sorrow, and then he would be introduced to Meru, a world beyond mortal imagining.

              Surely then, he would shed no tears.

              This training, to use the gifts Luna had blessed him with, would take him far away from his familial lands. There was no need for him to worry, though – his family would be well provided for, and Gerhard’s continued governance was assured until he chose to retire, as he was the clearest stand-in for the claim his Lunar son exercised over the lands. Once Gerhard no longer wished to lead, a representative would be appointed from the Gens. Naturally, the son himself would be too otherwise occupied to rule such a small land personally.

              What could he do but laugh? He had won, decisively, but that victory was ashen and hollow – in it, he had accomplished nothing at all.

              All that was left to him was to let thoughts of those lands fall from his fingers like gathered snow, or he would go mad. Madness, he learned in these early years, was not amongst the many troubles that Lunars were fortunate enough to possess an immunity for.

              It was a pity.

              In time, the city of Meru welcomed him, insulating him from the worries and tragedies of the Threshold. The mortal world, measured in short years, slipped away from his grasp and he was content to turn his eyes away from it at last, allowing himself to be enveloped in the much grander world of the Exalts with all of their art and adventure. The Wolf with the Red Roses embraced it fully and found that the Sidereal had spoken the truth – there was distraction enough for him here.

              “Have I lost you?”

              Ma-Ha-Suchi’s eyes cleared, shifting and focusing back on the Solar across the table from him.

              “For a moment, my apologies. You were speaking of your campaign to the north, I believe,” He smiled, showing his wolf’s fangs as he un-clenched his hands from his glass, reaching across to place one over the Solar’s hands. “Continue, please.”
