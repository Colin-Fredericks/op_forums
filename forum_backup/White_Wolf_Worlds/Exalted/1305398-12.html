    I skipped Earth for now, because after Air’s incredibly poor showing, I was really curious how Fire – which I believe to be the weakest of the five styles – would fare.

    Fire Immaculate:

    Attributes: Str 3 Dex 5 Sta 3 |Wits 4
    Abilities: Fire Dragon 5, Athletics 4, Awareness 4
    Charms:
    Fire Dragon: Flash-Fire Technique, Searing Edge Attack, Flame-Flicker Stance, Perfect Blazing Blow, Fire Dragon Form, God-Immolating Strike
    Brawl: Become the Hammer
    Awareness: Precision Observation Method
    Resistance: Ox-Body Technique x2

    All in all, a very conservative spread. Fire Dragon offers no incentives for unusual attribute spreads, and it has very few out-of-style Charms worth having on a 10 Charm budget. Blade-Deflecting Palm would be a potent complement to Flame-Flicker Stance’s post-roll shenaniganery, but it yanks us right out of aura, negating the benefit we’d want to use it for. Ox-Body Technique has strongly diminishing returns past the second purchase, and the rest of the Resistance Charms are not so hot either. This leaves us with the profoundly mediocre God-Immolating Strike – mid-combat healing is really not common enough among spirits for the aggravated damage to do anything useful 99% of the time, and between 3d flux damage per turn and Searing Edge Attack, there are more than enough ways for a stylist to ensure that her initiative is higher than her opponent’s at any time she’d want to launch a decisive, so this is just 5m for 2 dice of delayed damage, which is… a worse Falling Scythe Flash until you hit Essence 4 (and even then you could argue that FSF pulls ahead by virtue of its broader applicability). Still, decisive damage for motes and nothing else is not terrible, I'll take it.


    The combat:

    To anybody with even the most rudimentary math skills, an alpha strike will seem like a terrible idea. I’m way too lazy to do the math on Flash-Fire Technique, but I doubt it would reliably get us more successes than the average plus 1 standard deviation – meaning it would yield 9.5 successes on a full excellency. Even if we round up to 10, this only gives us 13 initiative. We’re not in fire aura yet, so we can’t get a damage bonus from Perfect Blazing Blow, so even in our best-case scenario, we can only expect a total damage pool of 20 dice (13 initiative, plus 5 threshold successes from Flash-Fire, plus 2 from God-Immolating Strike). Even with the excellency rerolls, this averages out to 8.8 damage, just shy of being able to kill even a single blood ape, and setting us up for four attacks in retaliation while we’re hanging at base initiative. Way too risky.

    On the other hand, if we manage to win Join Battle, we get a reflexive form activation, allowing us to raise our anima by 1 and giving us a free non-Charm autosuccess on almost every attack roll we make in the scene. This pays for itself in 4 attacks even if the apes launch no decisives in the scene.

    Since the apes only have 6 dice on Join Battle and we’re not planning to go for a turn 1 kill, consistency works in our favor. So we buy 2 auto-successes from the excellency and let the dice gods sort out the rest.

    …And of course now we roll 8 successes on 10 dice, bringing us up to 13i. Ape #1 rolls 7 for 10i, while ape #2 is slacking with 3 successes for 6i. We get to reflexively activate Fire Dragon Form, raising our anima banner to glowing.

    TURN 1

    Our rough strategy is to go in hard, crash one of the apes right out of the gate, then play defense until our flux cooks him alive. It’s 9 dice of free damage over the course of a crash, we’d be stupid to do anything risky like attacking early and setting ourselves up for an easy crash or even an initiative shift if we get really bad luck on the damage roll.

    Fire Dragon moves in with blades whirling, striking at ape #2 with Searing Edge Attack and 2 motes on the excellency, rolling 18 dice vs a Parry of 3. Anima goes up to burning, and we’re entering fire aura at the end of our turn thanks to SEA. This is paramount, because we really need the post-roll activation on Flame-Flicker Stance in order to not hemorrhage motes while the apes pound on us with their four attacks.

    18 dice roll 7 successes, 4 threshold goes to damage for a total post-soak pool of 7 dice. We get exactly 4 successes, SEA burns away the remaining 2 initiative, crashing ape #2. Fire Dragon at 23 initiative, ape #2 at 0. Things are looking up.

    Ape #1 comes in with the Principle of Motion flurry. We spend 3m on Flame-Flicker Stance and 2m on the excellency, raising our anima banner to bonfire. Roiling flames explode from the Fire Dragon stylist, scorching demonic flesh! We get 3 flux damage against ape #1 and a rather pathetic 1 against ape #2. Too bad, I was hoping to get a wound penalty off of this.

    First attack rolls 6 successes, fails, and inflicts no onslaught.

    We’re throwing 2m at the excellency against the second attack. The chances of him rolling no 1s at all are rather slim (0.9 on the power of 11, roughly 31 percent), so we can afford to skimp on our second point of Parry even though we want to activate Flame-Flicker Stance for the onslaught negation no matter what.

    Second attack rolls 12 successes. Not even FFS is gonna save us from this, and we don’t even get the onslaught negation. 5 threshold successes go into damage, and even with medium armor, we’re looking at 12 dice post-soak. Ouch.

    He rolls 5 successes, bringing him up to 13i while Fire Dragon goes down to 18. If we hadn’t gotten our 3 damage off of our anima flux earlier this round, he’d have a pretty good chance to fire off another two attacks before we get to act next turn, if ape #2 rolls even a single successful attack.

    Ape #2 goes for a PoM flurry as well against the same defense as before. 4 successes vs. Parry 6, reflexive Flame-Flicker Stance negates the onslaught because we want to avoid his next attack so he stays in crash and suffers lethal flux damage next turn.

    Same attack goes up against the same defense once more. Ape #2 rolls a baffling 3 successes, Flame-Flicker stays in our back pocket because we’re gonna reset onslaught next action anyway.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, everybody regains 5 motes, flux damage! Ape #1 takes 1 damage, ape #2 eats 2 and is dropped into -1s.

    TURN 2
    Fire Dragon at initiative 18, ape #1 at 12, ape #2 at 0.

    Turn begins with a quick cost-benefit assessment. Keeping ape #2 around costs us 10 motes per turn (4m on the excellency against both attacks, plus 6m on using Flame-Flicker Stance on ape #1’s last and ape #2’s first attack to negate onslaught); moreover, twice as many opponents making twice as many attacks against us doubles the chances of an anomalous occurence ruining our carefully-laid plans. Flux damage did its job, but the remaining 6 dice are unlikely to finish the ape off, in which case we’re gonna have to decisive him anyway, so we may as well get it out of the way now while we’re ahead in initiative. Our full combo of 18 initiative +2 dice of damage from Perfect Blazing Blow +2 from God-Immolating Strike has a nearly 97% chance to eat his remaining 6 health levels with the excellency rerolls. I’m willing to gamble on that.

    (Some would say PBB + GIS are overkill; we’re paying an 8m 1wp premium on increasing our kill chance by ~9%. However, failure would set us up for 8 attacks – 10, if the wounded ape gets an initiative shift – before we get to act again, and we won’t even be able to negate onslaught at initiative 3. I’m willing to pay that premium for my peace of mind.)

    So we’re spending 1m excellency + 3m 1wp PBB + 5m GIS against Parry 4 -1 wound -1 onslaught. The ape tosses in a willpower in a futile attempt to stave off the inevitable, for a grand total of 3.

    Our 13 dice rolls 10 successes after taking the PBB doubles into account. We have a base damage pool of 20, rerolling 6s until they fail to appear. We get 8 damage; the ape is dead.

    Now we reset and face the prospect of 4 attacks from ape #1 before we can act again. And our onslaught negation no longer works. Crap.

    Ape #1 comes at us with the PoM flurry. First attack rolls 11 dice vs. our Parry of 6; we spend 4m on the excellency because Flame-Flicker’s onslaught negation no longer works, and we really don’t want to get crashed so the consistency is preferable to the potential for saved motes. He rolls 3 successes. Fire Dragon feels kind of stupid.

    Second attack! Our defense is the same as before, but onslaught brings us down to Parry 7 total. He gets 4 successes; we’re off the hook once more.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, everybody regains 5 motes, anima flux fails to deal damage.

    TURN 3
    Ape #1 at initiative 12, Fire Dragon at 3.

    Another Principle of Motion flurry comes our way. We pump 2m into the excellency; the difference between the ape’s chances to hit parry 5 and parry 6 is nearly 22%, and we can almost certainly use Flame-Flicker Stance to bridge that gap if we need to.

    Sadly, the ape rolls exactly 6 successes. Not even Flame-Flicker Stance can save us now. We’re looking at 8 dice of post-soak damage.

    The ape rolls two successes. We’re hanging at 1i; he has 15 and another attack left to make.

    2m brings us up to Parry 4; our logic is the same as before, save that the gap is over 25% now.

    Ape #1 cares not for our carefully calibrated probabilistic reasoning and proceeds to roll 2 successes.

    Fire Dragon’s turn! She puts 1m into the excellency and slashes at her opponent’s 4 points of Parry with 15 dice. Ape #1 considers dumping a WP into enhancing it, because keeping her at 1i is very tempting, but decides that being able to make another PoM flurry is probably more valuable (and also 15 dice has a really good chance to hit Parry 5 still).

    Fire Dragon’s 15 dice yield 8 successes, 4 threshold results in a post-soak damage pool of 7. She successfully reaps 3 initiative from her opponent.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, everybody regains 5m, anima flux deals 3 withering damage to ape #1.

    TURN 4
    Ape #1 at initiative 9, Fire Dragon at 5.

    Ape fires off his last Principle of Motion flurry, Fire Dragon uses a 2m excellency. His 11 dice garner exactly 7 successes, but he rolls no 1s! Flame-Flicker Stance is shut off.

    Then he proceeds to roll 1 success on 7 dice of post-soak damage. Mediocre!

    Next attack vs. a Parry of 6 after a 2m excellency rolls 5 successes, fails to connect.

    Fire Dragon goes for another 1-mote wonder, rolling 15 dice vs. Parry 4, hits with 5 successes, then rolls 2 successes on her 4d post-soak pool, making this the second turn when she snatches up the exact same amount of initiative her opponent managed to build.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, everybody regains 5m, anima flux deals 2 damage, bringing the ape down to 7.

    TURN 5
    Ape #1 and Fire Dragon sit at 7 initiative both.

    Ape knows he has no chance to win a clash, so he takes a full defense. Fire Dragon spends 3m on Searing Edge and 1m on the excellency, rolling 15 dice against a parry of 5 and hits with exactly 5 successes. 4 dice of damage garner 2 successes; her dreams of crashing him remain unrealized.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, everybody regains 5, anima flux deals 1 damage.

    TURN 6
    Fire Dragon at 10, Ape #1 at 3.

    She rolls the same 15d plus SEA combo against his Parry of 4, and somehow manages to miss. He rolls against an unenhanced parry of 6 and also misses.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage deals 2 withering.

    TURN 7
    Fire Dragon at 10, Ape #1 at 1.

    Fire Dragon tosses a 1m excellency at him; rolls 5 successes vs Parry 4, then proceeds to gain zero successes on 5 dice of post-soak damage. Sensing weakness, the ape Brutal Ape Pounces on her and succeeds with 7 against Parry 6, rolling only a single 1 and thus preventing her from Flame-Flickering the attack away. However, his 8 post-soak damage dice only roll 3 successes, so the knockdown and the decisive do not trigger.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage deals 2 withering.

    TURN 8
    Fire Dragon at 8, Ape at 3.

    Fire Dragon launches a 1m +SEA attack against him, succeeding with 2 threshold and causing 2 withering damage on 5 post-soak dice. Searing Edge Attack ensures the crash. Ape attempts another pounce and fails.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage deals 1 decisive, ape is feeling surprisingly comfortable despite the flames enveloping him and charring his flesh.

    TURN 9
    Fire Dragon at initiative 16, Ape at -1.

    Her 1m excellency’d attack hits with 3 threshold, stealing another 3 points of initiative; his pounce attempt fails yet again.

    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage deals 1 decisive, putting the ape on his first -1 health level.

    TURN 10
    Fire Dragon at initiative 20, Ape at -4.

    Time for the killing blow: another 1m excellency + PBB + GIS for 10 successes vs Parry 2.

    PBB only managed to double a single die, so 21 dice of damage… resulting in 9 successes before even starting to reroll the 6s. The ape is dead.


    Final result: 2 dead apes and an unharmed Immaculate with some motes and WP left in the tank. Everything went better than expected!

    Notes:
    - Searing Edge Attack got us 2 crashes we couldn't have snatched otherwise. Even if we dumped an extra 4m into the excellency, that's just 2 threshold successes, resulting in 1.11 damage on average. It would've been enough to ensure the crash in the second case (at a 1m surcharge compared to SEA), but not the first. This is a niche effect, but far from useless - being able to dump decisive flux damage on an opponent 1 turn earlier than we normally could is a very potent benefit.
    - I actually found myself really enjoying playing with Flame-Flicker Stance. It introduces a lot of moment-to-moment decisionmaking into how the style plays (can I afford to take the risk of letting the attack through if it potentially saves me 2 motes? can I afford to not excellency at all and hope that my opponent rolls two 1s? should I launch this decisive now and give up my onslaught negation, or is it better if I hold back?), and provides a massive benefit in saved motes. I probably should've relied on it even more, despite the fact that it failed me at least twice.
    - For a Fire Dragon stylist, Perfect Blazing Blow is actually a really good Charm! It compares favorably to White Reaper's Falling Scythe Flash + Bleeding Crescent Strike in half as many Charm purchases and at a considerably lower mote cost. I'd rather blow a WP than 7 motes with the teensy-tiny pools I have as a deeb.

                    Earth Dragon!

                    Earth Immaculate:

                    Attributes: Str 5 Dex 3 Sta 3 | Wits 4
                    Abilities: Earth Dragon 5, Athletics 4, Awareness 4
                    Charms:
                    Earth Dragon: Stone Dragon's Skin, Force of the Mountain, Stillness-of-Stone Atemi, Unmoving Mountain Stance, Earth Dragon Form
                    Awareness: Precision Observation Method
                    Brawl: Become the Hammer
                    Resistance: Ox-Body Technique *2, Impervious Skin of Stone

                    „Earth Dragon really wants to max out Strength and Stamina” is a meme. Deeb ox-bodies only give you -1 and -2 health levels, so you’ll pretty much never need to negate a full -3 penalty with Stone Dragon’s Skin. Unmoving Mountain Stance does receive a significant benefit from maxed Stamina if you’re up against an opponent who can knock you away, but let’s face it, if you’re up against a Solar brawler with Heaven Thunder Hammer, you’re pretty much screwed no matter what. As for its other use… meh? Spending 6m to deny your opponent initiative on each attack is not a sustainable strategy, not to mention that you probably won’t even need to do so due to your sky-high soak. UMS is in the same category as Fire Dragon’s Searing Edge Attack – it’s a powerful niche effect, but the thing about niche effects is that you probably shouldn’t tailor your build to maximize their benefit.

                    The thing you do want, however, is Strength. Strength 5 gives you +3 dice on smashing attacks (which, given your predilections, may as well read „every attack”) and it turns Force of the Mountain into a terrifying +5 withering damage for 3m. Oh, and it also gives you +5 soak in the Form, but that’s a red herring – if you went for Str 3 Sta 5, you’d have the exact same soak in the Form and 2 more out of it. The important part is that you get to make 17d raw damage unarmed withering attacks with +4 charm dice on top for 4m a pop, and that’s terrifying.

                    With the issue of attribute distribution settled, time to turn out attention to Charm selection. I ditched Ghost-Grounding Blow because it’s just a shittier, more expensive Spirit-Chaining Strike, and picked up Impervious Skin of Stone instead. With our low-ish Stamina, this is actually not necessarily an amazing Charm (4m for +3 soak is a really inefficient way to use your motes, even with the post-roll activation taken into account), but once you get your infinite soak engine up and running, it provides an amazing second line of defense against unexpectedly high attack rolls resulting in damage overflow from threshold successes. When your opponent rolls 6 dice of damage, spending 4m to cut that in half suddenly seems like a much better deal.

                    However, I did fuck up in an important respect: I really should’ve ditched one of the Ox-Bodies for Effortlessly Rising Flame. With a -2 mobility penalty, any idiot can run circles around the poor Earth Immaculate. Blood apes lack ranged attacks and as such, can’t really take advantage of this, but in a wider context, this is something that can lose her the fight.

                    Last but not least is the issue of weapon selection. Unarmed, she has a stunted Parry of 5, 4 after using a smash attack. The blood apes can comfortably hit this, and it’s not worth for her to bother spending motes on the excellency to avoid that when her soak reduces them to 1 die anyway. This means she may as well swing a tetsubo and eat the additional -1 Defense penalty; the +1 raw damage they get from the threshold success is unlikely to change anything.


                    The combat:

                    Our battle plan is similar to how we played with the Fire Aspect: go for a consistent join battle result by adding auto-successes (we want to win Join Battle and let one of them hit us so we can activate the Form off the back of that hit), then move in, crash a dude with our initial attack, execute said dude with a followup attack, and trust that our soak will grant us the means to weather the following 4 blows over two turns.

                    This battle plan is immediately shot to hell when she proceeds to roll a single success on her Join Battle, bringing her up to 6i, with the apes at 7i and 6i respectively.

                    TURN 1
                    Ape #2 at 7i, Ape #1 at 6i, Earth Dragon at 6i.

                    Ape #2 moves in with a Principle of Motion flurry! First attack rolls 11 dice against a Parry of 4 after stunt. Knowing that she’s incredibly vulnerable until she manages to fire up Earth Dragon Form and the soak bonus from bonfire, and that she won’t have a chance to activate EDF if Ape #2 lands even a single point of damage on her, Earth Dragon drops her tetsubo and pumps a full excellency into her Parry, bringing her up to 7.

                    11 dice garner 5 successes, the attack fails. Second attack at 11 dice has a good chance to hit her full excellency’d Parry of 6, so she tosses in a Willpower as well for Parry 7. Ape #2 rolls 4 successes, his second attack fails as well.

                    Ape #1 and Earth Dragon act on the same tick! Ape #1 knows fully well that he has no chance to win a clash against a full excellency’d attack and that the 3 automatic levels of post-soak damage withering clashes get by default would ensure that Earth Dragon shoots up to the top of the initiative order and can get a reflexive form activation off of any attack thrown at her, so he decides to stay at short range and use Shattering Roar instead. Thwarted, Earth Dragon goes for a withering attack against ape #2 instead.

                    Order of operations suddenly becomes really important, because if she gains any initiative off of the attack, she can activate the Form when Ape #1’s roar hits her. The rule says ties go to the better stunt, which is Earth Dragon’s. She gets to fire off her attack before the roar hits.

                    Confident in her abilities (and the +4 accuracy bonus from light weapons), Earth Dragon tosses Force of the Mountain and a 2m excellency into her attack, raising her anima to glowing and entering Earth Aura at the end of her turn. Dex 3 + Earth Dragon 5 + accuracy 4 + excellency 2 + stunt 2 brings her up to 16 dice vs Parry 4. Ape #2 really wants to deny her form activation, so he spends a Willpower to bring his Parry up to 5.

                    She rolls 6 successes – base damage 12 +1 threshold +2 FotM -7 soak is 8 post-soak damage dice, rolling 5 successes. The ape’s ears are ringing under her relentless onslaught.

                    Meanwhile, the other ape rolls a single success on 9 dice against her Parry of 5, and misses the roar. Oops.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5.

                    TURN 2
                    Earth Dragon at 12i, ape #1 at 6i, ape #2 at 2i.

                    She launches another attack with 2m + FoM, raising her anima to burning and rolling 16 dice vs parry 3. 10 successes means a total raw damage pool of 24, 17d10 post-soak for a total of 7 successes. Ape #2 is crashed, and Earth Dragon has more initiative than she knows what to do with.

                    Ape #1 moves in, PoM flurry; first attack rolls 5 successes against her Parry of 5, hitting exactly. Earth Dragon Form activates reflexively, and her anima rises to bonfire. Flux damage fails to gather any successes against either demon.

                    15 base damage vs. soak 18, ape #1 is reduced to 1 die and rolls no successes.

                    Second attack rolls 13 successes vs. Parry 4! 9 threshold goes into damage for a total raw damage pool of 24. Earth Dragon activates Impervious Skin of Stone and cuts this in half; 3 damage dice roll a single success.

                    Ape #2 also flurries, tossing 11 dice vs Parry 3 on the first attack and hitting exactly. He is reduced to a single damage die and rolls a failure. Second attack is 11d vs Parry 2, rolling 7 successes and boosting his damage to 20… of which 2 gets past her soak. He rolls 4 successes, and barely manages to pull out of crash.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage! Earth Dragon pays 2i to hopefully knock ape #1 prone, but her flux fails to deal damage. Then she also proceeds to roll no successes against ape #2.

                    TURN 3
                    Earth Dragon at initiative 17, ape #1 at 8, ape #2 at 1.

                    She swings her mighty weapon for a smash attack against ape #1, enhanced by 1m and FoM. She rolls 8 successes and deals 9 damage. Ape #1 is crashed and prone; the demons are starting to re-evaluate their life choices.

                    At this point, if this was an actual game, the apes would activate Hurry Home instead of waiting for her to unload her infinite initiative, but let’s keep going and see what happens!

                    Ape #2 PoM flurries; his first attack hits with 1 threshold and rolls a single damage success; his second one somehow manages to fail against a Parry of 3.

                    Ape #1 forgoes PoM and instead uses a regular flurry to rise from prone and get an attack in. 4 dice to rise rolls the required 2 successes; he is up and running, attacks, hits and rolls a 10 on his single die of post-soak damage.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                    TURN 4
                    Earth Dragon at 27i, Ape #2 at 3, Ape #1 at 1.

                    Earth Dragon rolls a decisive smash with 2 excellency dice and Force of the Mountain against Ape #1, who spends a WP but is no match for her 7 successes on 15 dice.

                    25 damage dice with double 10s and rerolling 6s results in 21 levels of damage and a very, very dead blood ape.

                    Ape #2 is out of Willpower. He pounces because there’s literally nothing else he can do with his motes, succeeds on the attack, gets reduced to a single die and fails to roll any successes.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                    TURN 5
                    Ape #2 at 4i, Earth Dragon at 3.

                    Ape pounces, hits, and rolls no successes once more.

                    Earth Dragon smashes with 1m excellency + 3m FotM, maybe out of a misplaced desire for fair play as she brings herself down to 1i in the process.

                    However, her attack connects, rolls 11 successes on 17 post-soak damage dice, and crashes ape #2 once again. It’s been three turns since last time, so she also reaps an initiative break from him.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                    TURN 6
                    Earth Dragon at 20i, Ape #2 at -6.

                    She rolls to finish her prone opponent off with her signature FotM +1m excellency attack, and scores a hit against Parry 2 with 11 successes.

                    20 dice of damage with double 10s and rerolled 6s get 10 successes; another ape bites the dust.


                    Final result: 2 splattered blood apes and a profoundly bored Earth Immaculate. (No, seriously, she had more than half of her peripheral pool left by the end – after being on the receiving end of one of the worst opening rounds I’ve ever seen.)

                    Notes:
                    - what is this I can’t even
                    - Earth Dragon OP, pls nerf
                    - seriously, the fight was effectively over by the time I realized I should be spamming base initiative decisive PoM flurries in the hope that maybe those would do something
                    - not that they would achieve anything against Earth Immaculates in the form they’re encountered in the wild, which is to say bedecked in artifact heavy armor with Hardness 10

Deeb ox-bodies only give you -1 and -2 health levels, so you’ll pretty much never need to negate a full -3 penalty with Stone Dragon’s Skin. Unmoving Mountain Stance does receive a significant benefit from maxed Stamina if you’re up against an opponent who can knock you away, but let’s face it, if you’re up against a Solar brawler with Heaven Thunder Hammer, you’re pretty much screwed no matter what. As for its other use… meh? Spending 6m to deny your opponent initiative on each attack is not a sustainable strategy, not to mention that you probably won’t even need to do so due to your sky-high soak. UMS is in the same category as Fire Dragon’s Searing Edge Attack – it’s a powerful niche effect, but the thing about niche effects is that you probably shouldn’t tailor your build to maximize their benefit.

                        The thing you do want, however, is Strength. Strength 5 gives you +3 dice on smashing attacks (which, given your predilections, may as well read „every attack”) and it turns Force of the Mountain into a terrifying +5 withering damage for 3m. Oh, and it also gives you +5 soak in the Form, but that’s a red herring – if you went for Str 3 Sta 5, you’d have the exact same soak in the Form and 2 more out of it. The important part is that you get to make 17d raw damage unarmed withering attacks with +4 charm dice on top for 4m a pop, and that’s terrifying.

                        With the issue of attribute distribution settled, time to turn out attention to Charm selection. I ditched Ghost-Grounding Blow because it’s just a shittier, more expensive Spirit-Chaining Strike, and picked up Impervious Skin of Stone instead. With our low-ish Stamina, this is actually not necessarily an amazing Charm (4m for +3 soak is a really inefficient way to use your motes, even with the post-roll activation taken into account), but once you get your infinite soak engine up and running, it provides an amazing second line of defense against unexpectedly high attack rolls resulting in damage overflow from threshold successes. When your opponent rolls 6 dice of damage, spending 4m to cut that in half suddenly seems like a much better deal.

                        However, I did fuck up in an important respect: I really should’ve ditched one of the Ox-Bodies for Effortlessly Rising Flame. With a -2 mobility penalty, any idiot can run circles around the poor Earth Immaculate. Blood apes lack ranged attacks and as such, can’t really take advantage of this, but in a wider context, this is something that can lose her the fight.

                        Last but not least is the issue of weapon selection. Unarmed, she has a stunted Parry of 5, 4 after using a smash attack. The blood apes can comfortably hit this, and it’s not worth for her to bother spending motes on the excellency to avoid that when her soak reduces them to 1 die anyway. This means she may as well swing a tetsubo and eat the additional -1 Defense penalty; the +1 raw damage they get from the threshold success is unlikely to change anything.


                        The combat:

                        Our battle plan is similar to how we played with the Fire Aspect: go for a consistent join battle result by adding auto-successes (we want to win Join Battle and let one of them hit us so we can activate the Form off the back of that hit), then move in, crash a dude with our initial attack, execute said dude with a followup attack, and trust that our soak will grant us the means to weather the following 4 blows over two turns.

                        This battle plan is immediately shot to hell when she proceeds to roll a single success on her Join Battle, bringing her up to 6i, with the apes at 7i and 6i respectively.

                        TURN 1
                        Ape #2 at 7i, Ape #1 at 6i, Earth Dragon at 6i.

                        Ape #2 moves in with a Principle of Motion flurry! First attack rolls 11 dice against a Parry of 4 after stunt. Knowing that she’s incredibly vulnerable until she manages to fire up Earth Dragon Form and the soak bonus from bonfire, and that she won’t have a chance to activate EDF if Ape #2 lands even a single point of damage on her, Earth Dragon drops her tetsubo and pumps a full excellency into her Parry, bringing her up to 7.

                        11 dice garner 5 successes, the attack fails. Second attack at 11 dice has a good chance to hit her full excellency’d Parry of 6, so she tosses in a Willpower as well for Parry 7. Ape #2 rolls 4 successes, his second attack fails as well.

                        Ape #1 and Earth Dragon act on the same tick! Ape #1 knows fully well that he has no chance to win a clash against a full excellency’d attack and that the 3 automatic levels of post-soak damage withering clashes get by default would ensure that Earth Dragon shoots up to the top of the initiative order and can get a reflexive form activation off of any attack thrown at her, so he decides to stay at short range and use Shattering Roar instead. Thwarted, Earth Dragon goes for a withering attack against ape #2 instead.

                        Order of operations suddenly becomes really important, because if she gains any initiative off of the attack, she can activate the Form when Ape #1’s roar hits her. The rule says ties go to the better stunt, which is Earth Dragon’s. She gets to fire off her attack before the roar hits.

                        Confident in her abilities (and the +4 accuracy bonus from light weapons), Earth Dragon tosses Force of the Mountain and a 2m excellency into her attack, raising her anima to glowing and entering Earth Aura at the end of her turn. Dex 3 + Earth Dragon 5 + accuracy 4 + excellency 2 + stunt 2 brings her up to 16 dice vs Parry 4. Ape #2 really wants to deny her form activation, so he spends a Willpower to bring his Parry up to 5.

                        She rolls 6 successes – base damage 12 +1 threshold +2 FotM -7 soak is 8 post-soak damage dice, rolling 5 successes. The ape’s ears are ringing under her relentless onslaught.

                        Meanwhile, the other ape rolls a single success on 9 dice against her Parry of 5, and misses the roar. Oops.

                        DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5.

                        TURN 2
                        Earth Dragon at 12i, ape #1 at 6i, ape #2 at 2i.

                        She launches another attack with 2m + FoM, raising her anima to burning and rolling 16 dice vs parry 3. 10 successes means a total raw damage pool of 24, 17d10 post-soak for a total of 7 successes. Ape #2 is crashed, and Earth Dragon has more initiative than she knows what to do with.

                        Ape #1 moves in, PoM flurry; first attack rolls 5 successes against her Parry of 5, hitting exactly. Earth Dragon Form activates reflexively, and her anima rises to bonfire. Flux damage fails to gather any successes against either demon.

                        15 base damage vs. soak 18, ape #1 is reduced to 1 die and rolls no successes.

                        Second attack rolls 13 successes vs. Parry 4! 9 threshold goes into damage for a total raw damage pool of 24. Earth Dragon activates Impervious Skin of Stone and cuts this in half; 3 damage dice roll a single success.

                        Ape #2 also flurries, tossing 11 dice vs Parry 3 on the first attack and hitting exactly. He is reduced to a single damage die and rolls a failure. Second attack is 11d vs Parry 2, rolling 7 successes and boosting his damage to 20… of which 2 gets past her soak. He rolls 4 successes, and barely manages to pull out of crash.

                        DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux damage! Earth Dragon pays 2i to hopefully knock ape #1 prone, but her flux fails to deal damage. Then she also proceeds to roll no successes against ape #2.

                        TURN 3
                        Earth Dragon at initiative 17, ape #1 at 8, ape #2 at 1.

                        She swings her mighty weapon for a smash attack against ape #1, enhanced by 1m and FoM. She rolls 8 successes and deals 9 damage. Ape #1 is crashed and prone; the demons are starting to re-evaluate their life choices.

                        At this point, if this was an actual game, the apes would activate Hurry Home instead of waiting for her to unload her infinite initiative, but let’s keep going and see what happens!

                        Ape #2 PoM flurries; his first attack hits with 1 threshold and rolls a single damage success; his second one somehow manages to fail against a Parry of 3.

                        Ape #1 forgoes PoM and instead uses a regular flurry to rise from prone and get an attack in. 4 dice to rise rolls the required 2 successes; he is up and running, attacks, hits and rolls a 10 on his single die of post-soak damage.

                        DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                        TURN 4
                        Earth Dragon at 27i, Ape #2 at 3, Ape #1 at 1.

                        Earth Dragon rolls a decisive smash with 2 excellency dice and Force of the Mountain against Ape #1, who spends a WP but is no match for her 7 successes on 15 dice.

                        25 damage dice with double 10s and rerolling 6s results in 21 levels of damage and a very, very dead blood ape.

                        Ape #2 is out of Willpower. He pounces because there’s literally nothing else he can do with his motes, succeeds on the attack, gets reduced to a single die and fails to roll any successes.

                        DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                        TURN 5
                        Ape #2 at 4i, Earth Dragon at 3.

                        Ape pounces, hits, and rolls no successes once more.

                        Earth Dragon smashes with 1m excellency + 3m FotM, maybe out of a misplaced desire for fair play as she brings herself down to 1i in the process.

                        However, her attack connects, rolls 11 successes on 17 post-soak damage dice, and crashes ape #2 once again. It’s been three turns since last time, so she also reaps an initiative break from him.

                        DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5, flux does nothing.

                        TURN 6
                        Earth Dragon at 20i, Ape #2 at -6.

                        She rolls to finish her prone opponent off with her signature FotM +1m excellency attack, and scores a hit against Parry 2 with 11 successes.

                        20 dice of damage with double 10s and rerolled 6s get 10 successes; another ape bites the dust.


                        Final result: 2 splattered blood apes and a profoundly bored Earth Immaculate. (No, seriously, she had more than half of her peripheral pool left by the end – after being on the receiving end of one of the worst opening rounds I’ve ever seen.)
