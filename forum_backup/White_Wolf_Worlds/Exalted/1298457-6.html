                                As for whether or not you would need Terrestrial vs Celestial level working in order for a complete sex transformation I think your millage is going to vary based on Storyteller fiat more then anything else.

                                Personally I think you are going to get more value by considering what type of story you want to tell through the use of a transgender characters or npcs and how the access to these forms of magic shapes how the story, rather then using some source book or another to justify your answer. After all each persons gaming table is going to have unique quirks that suit people playing. You and your group should pick the one that appeals most to the group as a whole and helps you tell the stories that you find most interesting and evocative regardless of cannon interpretations, Similarly there is little point in trying to force your personal opinion on the cannon interpretation of the point onto other groups, as there is nothing really to be gained by the argument.

                                Here's how I would look at this particular problem
                                As a transwoman myself, who has face some amount of adversity due to my identity, I personally identify strongly with characters that have had struggles due to their marginalized status especially members queer communities. As such I enjoy stories that allow me through my characters to overcome that adversity in a safe environment, which is one of the things I enjoy about tabletop role-playing. Settings and rules which ignore LGBT struggles by hand waving them away with easy access to magic or "enlightened" culture about gender don't necessarily appeal to me as it feels like they scrub away the struggles of LGBT people in a way that ignores our existence and personal experiences.

                                However,
                                I have also played with other members of the LGBT community in my group that have told me that their feelings are the exact opposite of mine. That role playing games are one of the ways that they escape from some of the hardships and discrimination that they have faced, and that the game serves as a meaningful method of self expression and wish fulfillment where they are able to explore what it would be like to lives as gay/trans/etc in a world that was accepting of their status, and that easy access to sorcery or other magic that could change their gender didn't give them a feeling of erasure of personal struggle, but a genuine way to explore their identity in a way they cant in reality. So when hosting games for these people I simply made changes to the setting that would make the games more enjoyable for them.

                                I think that both of these ways of looking at the problem are valid, but will be preferred by different people, but the goal should ultimately be to maximize the enjoyment of your table.
                                However at the point where we are arguing over the reproductive capabilities of transgender people in regards to sorcery order to win an internet argument about what the so called canonical state of a games magic system, rather then discussing how we can use the setting in way that that can improve the enjoyment and engagement of our players. I wont call it offensive but it does seem to be in a bit of poor taste.

                                                Without a massively-detailed system of mathematically-rigorous magical "physics" for Creation, far beyond not only the scope of the game but beyond the ability of the writers to actually create, it is literally impossible to say definitively what level of working "should" be able to accomplish what level of transformation. All people can do is argue by analogy, and argue which analogies are more relevant, and it's pointless because there's no underlying reality to point at to test the "should".

                                                With no available underlying reality, what magic can do at what level is instead dictated by "story". And we know the story here is one where the Precedent of Rawar is important, meaning that transgender Dynasts actually need there to be a social precedent for using sorcery to reproduce because their marriages are otherwise going to be barren.

                                                Now, it is perfectly fair to believe that story sucks.

                                                But in the context of that story, there are two possible reasons I can see for such a precedent being needed. The first is that gender-affirmation sorcery is not regularly used by transgender Dynasts. The second is that the kind of gender-affirmation sorcery regularly used by transgender Dynasts is not complete enough to make them able to reproduce without further sorcerous intervention.

                                                The first is defensible; there could quite plausibly be a social taboo against using sorcery for gender-confimation so strong that few transgender Dynasts ever undergo it. It would have to be a pretty severe one, though.

                                                The second also has a patina of plausibility. For example, ovaries, testes, and adrenal glands all make testosterone, and all make estrogen, but only ovaries make ova while only testes make spermatozoa. So magically tweaking the ratio of those hormones produced by someone's gonads (or removing them while tweaking their adrenal glands to start producing more of the desired hormone) can look like a smaller change than transforming them into their counterpart. It's not iron-clad logic from the descriptions of workings that the difference is Terrestrial-versus-Celestial instead of the difference between levels of Ambition or Finesse within Terrestrial workings, but there is plausibly a difference.

                                                So, which explanation feels like the "better" one for canon given the existing "Transgender Dynasts" boxed text?
