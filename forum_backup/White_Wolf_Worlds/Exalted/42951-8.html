    Originally posted by Mindworm Jim View Post
    I can see potential for Lunars to have either Castes or Aspects. It's mostly a question of which direction the writers choose to take them.

    Personally, I feel like Aspects work a little better than Castes. An Aspect is some core piece of the Exalt that never changes, and that is exactly what a Lunar needs. They might be a mighty warrior one day, and a king the next. Or perhaps the day after that they are a merchant. A Caste doesn't make sense, but an Aspect creates an anchor. Something for the Lunar to fall back upon, something that relates to everything they do, no matter what they do. It also makes the Chimera a little more horrifying, as they lack such an anchor. As their desires and whims pull them in every direction their personality fragments. Of course, I have no idea what those Aspects might actually be, so ... uh ... I'll get back to you on that.

    In addition, a Caste is an extension of what you already are. However, some of the earlier comments on Lunars called them, "monsters with some humanity in them, or humans with a monster inside", which seems to imply (to me anyway) that a character would not necessarily be the same after their Exaltation. You could make Castes fit, but having what is essentially different kinds of roles for different kinds of monsters ... doesn't really feel right. Whereas an Aspect feels like it could describe what kind of monster you are. You aren't compelled to act like a monster, but it's sitting down there, inside you.

    Now I'm just blabbering. Hopefully some of this was useful.
    What's the difference between "roles for different kinds of monsters" and "what kind of monster you are"?

            Originally posted by Paradim View Post
            What's the difference between "roles for different kinds of monsters" and "what kind of monster you are"?
            Hmm, yeah, that didn't really come out very well. Let's see if I can do better ...

            Castes are fixed. You do not fall out of Castes. It is not expected that a Dawn will be something other than a fighter. Castes align with the person you were before Exaltation. Characters can deviate from Castes, but it is very rare that they simply cease to operate within their definitions.
            The way I read this description, Castes strike me as something of an external feature. Your Caste is a circle in the ground around you. How you operate or what you do within that circle can vary greatly, and you may occasionally step out of the circle for some reason or another, but you will eventually return. It's too ... I guess 'structured' is the best word ... for what are supposed to be primal and savage beings caught somewhere between human and monster.

            An Aspect connotes very little beyond an idea of what you will be like. Aspects imply things about demeanor, appearance, and (often latent) skills. They also demonstrate the nature of your power, such that it is essentially different from that of another Aspect. It has almost nothing to do with your expected role.
            This, on the other hand, strikes me as very internal. The Aspect is a starting point, nothing more. You can do whatever you want, but whatever you do will be built upon the foundation the Aspect gives you. Coloring it, supporting it, undermining it, whatever. This strikes me as a superior method to represent the human/monster dichotomy that they seem to be aiming for with Lunars. Your monstrousness does not force you into one particular path, but it influences the outcome of any path you walk.

            Of course, this is all based off of one comment that may have changed, or may simply not have been very important. So who knows.

                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The symbols and metaphors of each Aspect need no longer be shoehorned into the Caste roles of Warrior, Trickster, and Loremaster.
                The whole point is that the Full Moon no longer would need to be limited to that set-up. Instead of coming at it from the perspective of "we need some moon-symbolism to tie into fighty-type Caste roles", Lunar Aspects could be approached from a perspective of "here are the themes and symbolism suggested by this aspect of the moon. What kinds of characters follow from that?"

                Originally posted by Paradim View Post

                This sounds incredibly chaotic, which is not conductive to the systemic grouping with clear definitions that enable players to point to the specific Exalt type division that they want to express with their character. We have to keep in mind that this is for a Roleplaying Game, with mechanics and rules to present a structure that enables the players to tell the stories they want, and if people scratch their heads in confusion to what defines the specifics of an Exalt type, then that will only be harmful to the express purpose of designing a game.

                Lunars have been screwed over hard in the past by having an incoherent theme to them. At least the three Castes provided you with structure to base a character around.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                the light of the Full Moon has been be associated with the following by various cultures on Earth, in no particular order: revelation, lunacy, madness, berserker rage, lust, longing, passion, beauty, guidance, justice, thieves, magic, love.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The Full Moon has aspects of revelation and mysteries unveiled, as well as madness and violence and powerful emotions.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The changing of the moon can stand for liminality, in-between states, twilight, mercuriality, deceptiveness, whimsy, transformation, the unexpected, spontaneity, etc.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The Changing Moon includes aspects of the walker-between-worlds and the Deadly Beastman Transformation as well as the shapeshifting trickster.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The new moon can take on connotations of doubt and uncertainty, the unknown and the fear thereof, mysterious shapes lurking just beyond the light, the wonders and horrors to be found off the beaten path, away from the safe and the known and the named.
                Originally posted by Exploding Frogs View Post
                The No Moon includes aspects of the unnameable horror, the Monster, the blood-soaked Beast, as well as the bogeyman and the mischief-making spirit and the keeper of secret lore.
                As you can see, there's plenty of structure here to base a character around. I may have hammered on the point that any of the three proposed Aspects could play the role of any of the Castes, but do not mistake this for meaning that they are interchangeable or indistinguishable. Each brings its own approach, its own characteristic style and tone, to the table.

                The Full Moon, as a Warrior, is a furious berserker or a hot-blooded avenger or a self-appointed dispenser of justice. As a Trickster, he is a seducer, relying on animal magnetism and sheer audacity to get away with his escapades. As a Lorekeeper, he's the one who shines light into dark places and rips away lies and deception...or else tears away the veils concealing That Which Man Was Not Meant To Know. There's always a sense of energy, of power, of force and motion and vitality in the way he does things.

                The Changing Moon Warrior is fluid and flowing, both in motion and in form. He doesn't restrict himself to just one war-form--no, he's the one who will slip between a dozen shapes over the course of the fight, and whose flesh flows out of your grasp just as you think you've finally got him. And he's a Trickster in the tradition of Coyote or Raven or Br'er Rabbit, a shapeshifting thing from the wild who plays impossible pranks and jokes just because he can and who's always one step ahead of anyone who thinks they've managed to catch him. And as a Lorekeeper he's the outsider, the walker-between-worlds, the shaman, neither wholly of this world nor wholly of the other.

                The No Moon Warrior is the monster you're afraid of. He's a thing unseen and unknown, a nightmare come to life, striking from the shadows and vanishing before you catch more than a terrified glimpse. Or he's the one hunting such a monster: the lone hunter who knows the secret weakness of the unstoppable beast. As a Trickster he's a bogeyman, a thing of fairy tales and campfire stories, something from outside the world of the comfortable and the known that plays by rules and judges by standards and acts for reasons unknown and unknowable to you, a thing strange and inexplicable and mysterious. And the Lorekeeper No Moon knows all of the secrets and mysteries and wonders and horrors that lie hidden beyond the campfire's light, and might choose to share some of them. Or not.

                        Some of the prior lore about how Lunar Castes work, combined with some assumptions about new priorities in this Edition, feel like they might imply some things about suitability of Castes for Lunars.

                        There's the idea that the way Lunars used to work was that they Exalted first and then got a Caste later as it naturally affixed itself to their talents, which changed into the Castes breaking up a bit and needing to be fixed by tattoos, and there are indications that there's going to be an idea towards greater emphasis on Lunar relations to Solars in their original form as contrasted against their new context.

                        Now, one thing I could take from the unfixed nature of the Lunar Caste is that it could imply the roles carried by such a Caste are a bit different to other ones. A Solar can be somebody who was a great warrior (or had a strong inclination towards pursuing or resolving things through contest and the like) and then they're Exalted to be the Caste that includes warriors as part of an overall martial excellence archetype because Solars are built around that general Appolonian arete thing. A Sidereal destined to aid the progression of notable conveyances gets the Journeys Caste, an Alchemical made out of lives of inspirational leadership and high-minded experimentation becomes an Orichalcum Caste*. They're roles defined by the general context of what that Exalt does.

                        So it could be that, with Lunars, if part of what makes them up is that storm of persona and wild energy that makes for the whole "monster/good person" dichotomy, well... it might be that the role you're suited for as a Lunar can only really become apparent after you've spent some time being a Lunar, because it might be that Lunars have the biggest transition between what they were before and after they Exalted.

                        I'm just thinking, right now, it would seem fairly suitable to the differences between Lunars and the others, especially Solars, as well as for helping character concepts be a bit more open. I mean, the monster inside a good person inside a monster thing feels interesting, but it also feels like it would be fairly limiting if you had to assume that the only people who became Lunars were, well, Holden made the comparison to Dexter once.

                        I look at Seven Devil Clever and think "what if she did that same impressive ballsy thing that got Luna's attention, and then got Exalted out of it, and then she's got this monstrous storm inside of her", because Seven Devil Clever is fun and straightforward and her current origin story doesn't really imply anything of being Dexter.

                        And then she's got this new characteristic, and has to feel it out before anybody can figure out what Lunar Caste suits her.

                        In the First Age, this probably would have included how she relates to and synergizes with her Mate, and that leads into the whole big thing of Solar-Lunar issues right there.

                        In the Second Age, it might come out of what aptitudes and proclivities she directs towards the usurpers and other enemies of the Silver Pact, in which case, given what we might gather of the Lunar methodology there, "Warrior, Trickster and Shaman" seems a fairly pertinent divide. Might also help give some weight to the idea that tinkering around with their Exaltations was an achievement and a big deal for the Silver Pact.

                        If Lunars had Aspects, I'd say they'd have to have them set up from the start, and maybe even bridge them off of what actual phase of the moon you Exalted under (time of day notwithstanding), and then build up from there. Because the ultimate difference between Aspect and Caste seems to be what side of the character generation they're on**; you start with Aspect and then figure out what the person makes of it, whereas you start with aptitudes and achievements and thus assign a Caste based on that.

                        * Incidentally, Alchemical Castes are assigned the same way that every other Caste is in Autochthonia, and they're Castes built around the unique and necessary place Alchemicals have in Autochthonian society. The Octet needs its inspirational visionaries and personable industrial czars as much as it needs its therapists and clerics and laborers.
                        ** I mean this as an analogy rather than a literal approach to character creation.
