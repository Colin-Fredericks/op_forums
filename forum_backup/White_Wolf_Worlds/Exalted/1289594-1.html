                                        Many good responses here. The gods' reasons were, I think variable. I'll describe some others I find plausible:

                                        Personal loyalty will be a big one. The Unconquered Sun in particular is the general triumphant of Heaven, grin a flash, always on the march, under a sovereign that disdains to command his own legions. Many, many gods will owe him their personal loyalty - "Where is your loyalty to The Monarch, Tah-na-pa?" "My loyalty is to the Ever-Victorious". Many will believe that he is the rightful and legitimate sovereign of Heaven (with the Mandate of errr.. Heaven).

                                        Authority loyalty is baked into the hierarchical structure of the bureaus. The Bureau of Fate in particular, would seem to be especially likely to fall in line with the Maidens, if they decreed that it must be so. The whole mission is to put into place the visions of what the Maidens decree must be so.

                                        Gods may have been won by the prospect of promotion and individual status - "The first ones to fall in line with the rebellion will be rewarded with rank in the new order of the world".

                                        The prisoner gods in the Primordials' jails, languishing for horrific offences against the order of the world, would be won by a promise of amnesty and freedom in the new world.

                                        Ambition must have been important as well. We've talked about wealth, but the Unconquered Sun was likely promising a glorious age of high civilization; the gods of what would become the Bureau of Humanity would be persuaded by promises of opulent cities and rich networks of trade under the purview. It's likely the the Primordials kept much of their domains on a pretty tight leash to prevent risk and threat (barring exceptions like the Lintha, a low population of sapient beings, without much glory or magic).

                                        (I like this one as it emphasizes the gods as a knowing and intentional partner in the First Age, although a junior one to the genius of the Solar Exalted).

                                        War gods would be motivated by the chance for glorious struggle alone. The Aerial Legion perhaps by the chance to test their mettle against their creators.

                                        Beyond all these considerations, consider survival instincts alone on the part of the gods who would be neutral. "What do you think they'll do to us, if the Incarna lose? Do you think they'll trust us again? Do you think they'll let us exist? If the Incarna win, do you think *they'll* let us live?". Once enough gods are on side that conflict becomes inevitable, they had to pick a side. The whole thing is much like a coup; you win the heads of the army who command loyalty of the others, and then you make it clear that anyone else who doesn't falls into line is for the chop.

                                        Remember finally that by the Second Age we have a sampling of the gods who won out the end of the war and the First Age successfully. This may not be a sample to represent the gods as a whole.
