            Have Your People Contact Mine: 4
            Bring the Liver to Smoke at her Shop: 3

            This is Nexus. There’s always someone living upstairs or next door who might have heard you through thin walls. Aside from nosy or sharp-eared neighbors, the Guild has spies, the Council has spies, various criminal syndicates have spies…it’s usually dangerous to assume you’re not being eavesdropped on at any given point in time. Well, nobody eavesdrops on random street rats like you, but on wealthy alchemists? The odds are good.

            “Bringing a couple of guards into my neighborhood might be a bad idea,” you smirk. “It’d just attract the wrong sort of attention.” You flash Golden Smoke your best dangerous-rogue sort of smile.

            “I see.”

            “Listen. We don’t want to get ahead of ourselves here, this might not even actually be the liver. I’d like you to look it over, appraise it, and then make an offer.”

            She nods. “That makes sense…would you be able to bring it here?”

            “Normally yes, but given how valuable you tell me it is, I’d rather ensure safe storage if you decide not to buy or if I decide not to accept your offer. I’ll have someone contact you later today to arrange a meeting and an appraisal.”

            Smoke seems dubious. “I’m not sure I’m comfortable meeting in an unknown location on your say-so.”

            “The meeting will be under the roof of a legitimate Guild merchant I do some business with from time to time. If I’m making such a highly specialized trade I’m going to want their expert advice anyway.”

            “…very well. I will look forward to your messenger, and in the meantime I’ll discuss the matter with Blue Root.”

            “Thank you. Say…you’re an alchemist. Do you know anything about Raptor Cats?”

            “Their scales make excellent purses.” She shrugs. “I’ve heard you can use their musk as the main ingredient in an aphrodisiac? But there are plenty of aphrodisiacs out there that don’t call for dangerous voyages into the eastern jungles. Why do you ask?”

            “Ran into one yesterday, and a friend says since it scratched me it’ll probably be waiting for me to get sick from the wound and stalking me for a while. I was thinking it might be profitable to trap it, if its corpse would be as valuable as the River Dragon.”

            “You ran into a Raptor Cat? In Nexus?” She’s raising an eyebrow in disbelief.

            “I’d heard rumors of it and hadn’t given them much thought myself,” you nod. “But it’s real, alright. The story I hear says some Merchant Prince had it captured for a pet and it got loose.”

            “Well…I’ll look into it, but honestly I wouldn’t know what to do with most of a Raptor Cat. You don’t see many of them. River Dragons wash up dead sometimes, or get tangled up in fishing nets and drown themselves and then float up to the surface. And sometimes the Dragonblooded go hunting for them. It’s rare, but it’s happened often enough for people to figure out how to make use of them. Raptor Cats…don’t get brought back to civilization very often. It’s probably worth more alive than dead.”

            “Hm. I guess that Merchant Prince from the rumor might pay to get it back…something to think about!” You shrug. “Thank you for your help, Golden Smoke. I’ll look forward to seeing you again later today.”

            You head out, the little bell ringing again behind you.

            You calmly walk past the shuttered windows of Blue Root’s alchemical shop.

            You calmly walk around the corner and duck into an alley.

            You do a little dance involving lots of skipping and silently but enthusiastically pumping your arms. Four. Hundred. Dinar!


            It’s still early, and the trip back to the Blackwall neighborhood is reasonably quick and uneventful. You check several times along the way to make sure you’re not being followed from the alchemist’s shop. You take a couple of odd and inconvenient routes and spend a few minutes hiding in odd spots to lose pursuit, just in case. You’re pretty good at that-pretending to be a boy out on the street does get you less unwelcome attention than people knowing your gender…but it doesn’t prevent that sort of thing entirely, and so you’ve gotten quite familiar with losing creepy but determined tails.

            This time, you don’t think you’re being followed. Not by people, anyway. But everywhere you go, you notice there’s at least one and sometimes three or four raitons perched nearby, looking as studiously nonchalant as it’s possible for a bird to look. When you duck into alleys or hide, you can hear them croaking and cawing to one another, and the flapping of wings as they take flight. When you come back out of your nooks and hiding spots, you find them circling high overhead, and they usually slowly come down and land on clotheslines or rooftops nearby to continue watching you after a few minutes.

            When you get home, White Moth is awake. She’s brewing some tea and reading a book. The cover is black and the title is in some sort of silver embossed ribbon. You recognize some of the words from last night: the big words on the front of the book say “The Spirits of the Departed,” and then under that there’s some smaller words you don’t know, and then “Dead.” Then at the bottom there’s another couple of words you don’t understand. So…probably a book about ghosts?

            “Good morning!” Moth says to you as you come in the door. “Have you already had breakfast?”

            “Ate some rice.” You nod. “With a little vinegar.”

            “Ugh. Rice again? I need to get some vegetables or something…”

            “You have…money?”

            “…no.” She sighs. “No vegetables. Maybe I should research dead criminals and see if I can find a pirate or something whose ghost I can summon to ask where he hid his silver…”

            “You can…do that?”

            “Well, yeah. I’ve got the summoning part down pretty well, but I’m only allowed to do it when the Master of Nine Black Chains is around. I’m, uh, not so great with warding or binding yet.”

            “What’s that mean?”

            “It means I could probably call up a pirate ghost, but he might cut my throat and drink my blood and then ride around town drinking and fighting using my dead body, though.”

            “That sounds…less like a good plan then.” You’re finding yourself regretting being all intimidating with White Moth. She doesn’t seem to know it, but she’s legitimately frightening. People shouldn’t talk about evil spirits possessing corpses so casually! It’s bad luck!

            “Yeah…thanks for your help with the prayer strips last night. I’m gonna bring them over to the Master this afternoon. He says just after noon is the best time to research in dangerous places, the sun keeps most of the really bad stuff hiding until after dark. If you want I could introduce you.”

            “Thanks. But I have someone I have to meet.” You take what you hope is a liver and not some other less valuable organ out of its clay jar, pat it dry with one of your rags, wrap it in another rag, and put it in your carrying bag.

            “Oh, gross.”

            “The Princess of the Umbral Infinitudes is grossed out by a random organ?” You tease. “I’d assume you’re used to a little blood and guts.”

            “Ick.” She wrinkles her nose. “Tavi, I have literally cut open dead guys, and I’m telling you most of them smelled better than that thing, and looked more appetizing. You’re not gonna....eat that, are you?”

            “Nope. Gonna sell it. Good luck with your prayer strips!” You head for the door. “I hope you catch plenty of ghosts!”


            Your prize in hand, you make your way to the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants! It’s almost noon by the time you arrive, and Yinhang Square is bustling with Guildsmen hurrying this way and that in search of profit. You give the grizzled mercenary guarding the door a nod, and to your surprise he grins and nods back as you approach.

            “Tavi, right?” He says.

            “Um. Yes.” You smile. Smiling is a good thing to do when you’re unsure of social context.

            “I appear to have you at a disadvantage, Tavi! I’m Toad in Spring. I try to remember our clients.” He grins right back at you. “You run out already, kid? Coming back to withdraw your money?”

            “…no?” You shake your head, slightly confused. “I’m actually looking for help setting up a business deal I hope is going to make me some more money.”

            “Well shit!” he laughs. “Looks like I owe Liu a bit of silver!”

            “How so?” You’re not liking the turn this conversation is taking.

            “Kid like you? I bet him you’d be back the very next day to try and pull most of your money out to spend it on one thing or another. He bet me not only would you not do that, you’d be back within the week to put more money in. Said something about you reminding him of a young Merchant Prince he once knew.”

            “Hm. How much do you make if I go in there and pull all my money out? It might be worth doing, if I split your winnings with you.”

            “Ha!” The mercenary pats your back and almost knocks you over. “Old Liu’s a good judge of character alright! A nice thought, kid, but no. That’d be cheating, and that that would take all the fun out of gambling.”

            He waves you in. You’re not sure you understand the…allure… of gambling.

            You wait for a middle-aged woman and what you quickly surmise are her two burly teenage sons to drop off a small sack of silver (you gather from their conversation that she runs a small stall in the market nearby, though you’re unable to make out what she sells) and make her way out. She doesn’t pay you much mind, but her sons give you wary looks and puff themselves up a bit. Your new clothes make you look more professional, but you still apparently look like trouble.

            You explain your intent to the clerk at the front desk, who asks you to wait a few minutes while he ducks into the back office, and then Fifteen-Fingers Liu comes out to meet you. He’s wearing a completely different set of fancy silk robes from yesterday-these ones are deep green, and have little dancing grasshoppers embroidered on them in yellow and gold, playing tiny instruments. Just as yesterday, you find yourself taking a comfortable seat in Liu’s office, admiring his tapestries of armored frogs at war.

            “Feng tells me you are looking for our help to broker a sale, young man.” Liu strokes his long white beard, an expression of slightly satisfied amusement on his face. “Would I be wrong to guess that you’re selling more, what was it…alchemical supplies?”

            “You’d be right.”

            “I was under the impression that you sold your previous goods using Dwi as your broker. Why is it that you are coming to us?”

            “I knew Dwi knows several alchemists she’s worked with in the past. I wanted to see who she trusted enough to work with. She’s a pretty good judge of character.”

            “She recommended you come to us to open your accounts, so I suppose I can’t argue.” Liu smiles. “And once you knew which alchemists she preferred to work with, you decided to cut out the middleman?”

            “I figured I’d make more if I sold directly to the alchemist, yes.”

            “Then why come to us?”

            “My goods are, um. Worth more than I thought. I’d prefer to make the exchange here, with Guild backing and your security nearby. And I’d prefer to put the money into my accounts right away rather than having to walk across Nexus with that much silver on me.”

            Silently, Fifteen-Fingers Liu stands up, walks to the open door of his office, closes it, and returns to his seat. Sitting down again, he steeples his fingers under his chin and leans forward until his long beard is almost brushing the polished surface of his desk.

            “How much silver are we talking about, Tavi?”

            You gulp. Liu is pretty old, but this is a bank-he has mercenary guards pretty close by. You consider the possibility that maybe your banker will just kill you and take the goods…but no. Dwi is, after all, a really good judge of character.

            You put the bag on his desk. Liu gingerly opens it, sees the wrapped wrags, wrinkles his nose at the smell, and looks at you quizzically.

            “That is-I hope-a River Dragon liver. My alchemist buyer says fresh out of the Dragon it’d be worth two and a half Dirham. She says that if it’s a couple days old and a bit pickled, which this is…she’s willing to offer a single Dirham.”

            Liu whistles. “That’s a hefty sum for lizard guts.”

            “It is. She’s going to want to appraise it to make sure it’s actually the liver and that it’s in good enough shape for the potions she wants to make-potions that make your skin as hard as River Dragon scales. Apparently Merchant Princes might want them for their guards or gladiators.”

            The old man nods. “Makes sense…and the only person who has offered you an appraisal of the value of this liver is…the woman you’re selling it to?”

            “What do you-…oh.”

            “Heh. I suspect she might lowballing you, my boy. Pick a number that seems really big to shake ‘em up, cut that number down a bit to a still hefty number to make it seem realistic because nobody trusts their own good luck, and they won’t even think about how that first number you pulled out of the air maybe ought to have been even bigger to start with. It’s an old trick when you’re in a specialized industry dealing with somebody unfamiliar with the trade. Did she tell you that you ought to sell right away because it’s going down in value fast?”

            “…she did.”

            “Well…I’m not an alchemist. Maybe she was being perfectly honest with you! But if I were you, I’d want an appraisal by a separate alchemist unrelated to the deal. This is just an old man’s hunch, but…well, there’s a lot more than a couple of Dirham riding on gladiatorial matches sometimes. We might be able to sell this thing for a lot more if you get a couple different alchemists bidding on it.”

            “That…makes sense.”

            “There’s a hitch, though.” Liu frowns. “When we broker a deal like this, we just take a flat five percent cut of the sale, so you can’t lose any money on the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants…but I don’t know a River Dragon Liver from a cow liver. Alchemists don’t appraise things for free. So if we hire an appraiser and our appraiser says your goods are worthless, you’re out his fee and you don’t have a sale to make it up. And if you skip the appraiser and trust your buyer…well, there’s a chance she tells you your goods are almost worthless and buys ‘em for a pittance and screws us over.”

            Now you’re frowning.

            “Liu…how much would an alchemist normally charge for an appraisal?”

            “Last time I had to have my usual man appraise something, he charged four Dinar.”

            “Ouch…I want that money for rent…but even if it’s not as valuable as I’m hoping for, if it’s River Dragon bits I’ll probably make the money back.”

            “…I hope so. Even if it’s not worth a Dirham, it’s probably worth something. I’ll send Feng to call over my alchemist friend., then.”

            “Thank you.”

            “Don’t mention it. Say, Tavi…”

            “Yes?”

            “I don’t often encounter young men your age blessed with your head for business. You said yesterday you’re looking to work as a canal boatman? I hope I’m not offending you saying this, but I think that’d be a terrible waste.”

            “…are you…offering me a job, sir?”

            “I’m trying to get you to ask me for a job, kid.”

            “I see.” You keep your expression as serious as you can…as you steeple your fingers and lean across the desk, mimicking Liu’s demeanor when you mentioned large amounts of silver. “Tell you what, Liu. You say you normally take a five percent cut of my sale? How about you take one percent instead, and in exchange I work as an apprentice for you for a while, see if I’m suited for the work.”

            “Hrm. Assuming three percent of two and a half Dirham…” Liu’s fingers nimbly clack scarlet beads along the rails of his abacus.

            “Thirty Dinar,” you say, a split second before he does.

            He raises an eyebrow. “For one of my clerks, that’s about eight months’ pay.”

            You do some more math in your head. That’s…significantly more than a boatman would make.

            “Then, assuming we make the sale, in exchange for you only taking a two percent cut, what if I work for you for three months for free? After that we can decide based on my work whether you keep me on and start paying me.”

            “Seven months free.”

            “Four.”

            “Tavi, you’re a smart kid, but you’re a still just a kid. I couldn’t pay you that much more than a trained clerk, it wouldn’t be fair to-”

            “Liu, you’re talking as though we sell this thing for price I’ve been quoted for an unblemished fresh liver. If we’re talking only one Dirham, which might be a lowball but might be an honest and fair price, three percent is only, um…twelve Dinars. So you’d be saving…two Dinars, I think? Besides, none of your trained clerks remind you of a certain young Merchant Prince you once knew.”

            You wink.

            “…who said that?”

            “Toad in Spring. Your guard at the door.”

            Liu chuckles. “Tavi, you haggle like my grandmother, and that’s a compliment. Tell you what-how about we make the deal, I charge you five percent as normal, and then afterwards...we talk about the possibility of my hiring you for a position that would be paid right away.”

            You nod. “That sounds pretty reasonable.”


            It takes a couple hours for Red Clover, Liu’s alchemical appraiser of choice, to arrive and complete his appraisal. It’s good news! You do, in fact, have a River Dragon liver! Well…part of one. Clover informs you that what you have is the front quarter or so of the liver, the rest must have been cut away and gone to someone else during the chaotic butchering process. He advises you that the way you stuffed other organs inside and tied it shut probably helped preserve the organ, as did the smoking and the pickling. After poking about a bit with a long wooden spoon, he declares that the organ probably has enough quality bile to produce one skin hardening draught, perhaps two if you’re lucky. He declares to you and Liu that while one of those potions would probably sell for somewhere between two and five Dirham, the process to make them is expensive and requires lots of other unusual ingredients in addition to River Dragon bile. For what you have, he tells you a fair price would be around half a Dirham.

            That’s two hundred Dinar.

            Red Clover actually offers to buy it from you on the spot for one hundred and ninety six Dinar, just taking his appraisal fee off the cost of the goods, and you almost spit and shake his hand on the spot, but Fifteen Fingers Liu puts his hand on your shoulder and shakes his head, and you instead tell Clover that you have another buyer, and you’d like to hear her offer before you accept his.

            Clover smiles ruefully. “Fair enough. I can stick around.”

            The Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants arrange a runner with a message, and about forty minutes after that Golden Smoke shows up, looking serious. The two of you sit across a gleaming table, you with Fifteen Fingers Liu standing slightly behind you on your right and Red Clover leaning on the wall nearby.

            Smoke greets you perfunctorily and examines the goods.

            “This…is in pretty bad shape, and it’s far from the whole liver, Tavi.”

            “That’s what our Red Clover, our appraiser tells me,” you sigh. “I was hoping it would be in nearly ideal shape and I’d be able to sell it to you for most of the two and a half Dirham you said you’d offer for perfect condition. In the condition it’s in…he said he’d only be willing to offer me two thirds of a Dirham for it.”

            In the corner, Red Clover…manages to turn the noise he’s making into a plausible sneeze.

            “…I see.” Golden Smoke sighs. “I wasn’t expecting an auction, young man. I’ll offer you three quarters of a Dirham. That’s as high as I’m going. Red Clover?”

            “Too thin a margin for me, lass.” Clover gives her a rueful grin. “That’s the advantage of dealing in bulk like Master Blue Root, eh?”

            “I suppose so.” Smoke nods, and gets out a gleaming black leather account book embossed with silver lettering. “To whom should I make out the Cheque?”

            “You can make it out to Tavi,” Fifteen Fingers Liu declares, eyeing her ledger the way you’ve seen Rat sometimes eye a clumsy foreigner with a fat purse. “You’re with the Silver Falcon Consortium?”

            “That’s right. Master Blue Root’s sister-in-law works as one of their auditing clerks.” Smoke shrugs. “He says he gets the family rates.” She pulls her necklace out of her shirtfront to reveal the small seal dangling on it, inks the seal, and stamps the cheque with the image of a tree depicted with branching roots and starts hanging in its branches.

            “I see. I won’t try to seduce him away from them, then.” Liu winks, and accepts her Cheque. “I’ll just deposit this into your account, young Master Tavi.”

            “Thank you, Liu.” You can’t help smiling, but you try to keep it a cool collected sort of smile. You’ve just made three hundred Dinar. Well, two-eighty-one with the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants’ cut and the appraisal fee for Red Clover, but still!

            Some brief additional haggling and a small bidding battle between Golden Smoke and Red Clover sees you selling your remaining River Dragon fangs and pickled bits for an additional twelve Dinar. The alchemists gather the supplies and take their leave…and you are left behind in a rather fancy meeting room with Fifteen-Fingers Liu.

            “…I think Red Clover likes you, Tavi,” Liu chuckles.

            “That’s nice. Also, YES!” You jump to your feet and laugh in delight. “I didn’t think she was gonna buy that.”

            “It was bold.” Liu smirks, pulling out a long pipe and lighting it. “But with an offer already made there was no reason not to risk being bold. So, if my calculations are correct, after our five percent charge, you just made two hundred fifty two Dinars, young man. ”

            “Hm? Five percent of three hundred and twelve is a little under sixteen Dinars, Liu.”

            “True. But for the sale of alchemical ingredients, there’s a ten percent tax to pay to the Council of Entities as well.”

            You scowl. “…oh.”

            “Indeed.” Liu shrugs. “Cost of doing business.”

            “…I guess I wouldn’t be making any money at all if it weren’t for the Emissary.” You shrug, then break into a grin again. “It’s still a good day!”

            “Indeed. Tavi, have you had formal training in arithmetic?”

            “What do you mean?”

            “It’s just that you seem to have an excellent head for numbers. Most of my clerks would be breaking out the abacus for calculations you seem to be doing quite quickly in your head.”

            “I mean…my mother taught me a bit about money.” You shrug. “And Aunt Dwi is always talking about percentages, she takes a share of the profits each of her girls makes. So I guess I know how to figure those.”

            “Hm.” Fifteen Fingers Liu inhales slowly, and then lets curling wisps of smoke from his pipe roll out to twirl around his magnificent moustache. “I recommend that we deposit the proceeds from this deal into your interest bearing time-sealed account. It’s fixed at nine percent interest for the next nine months, so that will let you make back our fee and some of the cost of the tax.”

            “That leaves me with…five Dinars I can access, right? You took the four for the appraisal out of my other account before the deal?”

            “That’s right.”

            “Let’s put fifteen Dinars into my Cheque account. That way I have the money if I need it. You can put the rest into the time sealed account.”

            Liu nods.

            “I’ll do that. Now, Tavi, about the other matter we discussed…I think you have what it takes to be a Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchant. I’m willing to offer you a position as an apprentice clerk. You’d be expected to show up here looking clean and presentable just before dawn every morning and work until sunset. You’d be paid two Dinar each month. It’s not easy work. Our apprentice clerks are expected to do basic accounting and sales work, but they also handle a lot of the unpleasant tasks-most notably debt collection, which can sometimes be a bit dangerous. It involves going along with our mercenaries and tracking down people who owe us money and don’t wish us well to force them to pay up or get dragged off to forced labor. But if you’re as talented as I think you’re going to be, there will be chances to rise within the ranks. What do you say?”
            This is a life-changing decision. What do you say?

            Accept the Offer: This is a chance to join the Guild, albeit at the bottom rung of the ladder. Joining the Guild is a way to get rich and powerful. The Guild pretty much runs Nexus…but you’ll be a part-time bank clerk, part-time legbreaker. Remember back when we were picking what life path to go down and we opted to go get an apartment and put off that decision? This is one of those paths. It leads to a life of disciplined and vaguely-legitimized theft and regular violence against down-on-their luck people a lot like yourself as you were last week. Tavi's great at Bureaucracy but can't actually read or write, which complicates office work and accounting, so she's gonna end up spending a lot of time with Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants' hired Guild Mercenaries doing investigation, tracking, and roughing up of debtors if you go down this road.

            Decline the Offer: You just made a ton of money. More money than you can really hold in your brain right now. Enough to pay the rent every month for years and years! You don’t need to risk your life for two Dinar a month. You don’t have to jump at the first offer you get, you can afford to find a more appealing profession, or maybe go into business on your own. There are lots of other paths to take!

            Haggle: You don’t want to accept the offer as-is…but perhaps you can talk Liu into offering you a better deal. If you pick this option, you should tell me what you’re asking him for to get you to accept the position, and if it wins I’ll be rolling to see if you can persuade Fifteen Fingers Liu. If you succeed I'll count your vote as an Accept vote but modify the deal to include your specifications. If you fail I'll count it as a Decline vote.
