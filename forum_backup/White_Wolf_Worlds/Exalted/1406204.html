    Autochthonia and vehicles

    08-03-2020, 09:59 PM
    With 3rd Edition trying to move away somewhat from magitech and move artifacts toward being more rare, I had some thoughts about Autochthonia and vehciles that I wanted to share and see what other people thought.

    Autochthonia, with its somewhere-between-steampunk-and-techno-futurism aesthetic, made plenty of use of various artifact vehicles in 2nd edition, powering them with elemental cores and putting them in the hands of mortals of high rank and the military. With the typical such vehicle being rated somewhere around 4 dots, it made for quite a few potent artifacts lying around. To keep the aesthetic of hover cars, transport crawlers, and tunnel trams, perhaps what we'll see is that most such vehicles will be powered by one or more Municipal Alchemical Charms, such as a train station or an airport. The actual vehicles, rather than being made from the Magical Materials, would be ordinary steel, copper, and aluminum and tap into an energy field broadcast by the patropolis. With "mundane" vehicles in existence, artifact versions can then be special, custom-made creations intended for use by Champions.

    Thoughts?

                                                      Originally posted by Blackwell View Post

                                                      Airboats use their fuel for lift, yes. But the key thing is actually that the fuel is *kerosene*. Kerosene is what we use now for jet fuel. In our world, kerosene was invented 50 years or so after the internal combustion engine. It's a major step forward in energy availability, lighting, etc.
                                                      The first record of the distillation of kerosene was written in the 9th century, about a thousand years before the invention of the internal combustion engine. The Chinese were using it as a lamp fuel during the Ming Dynasty. Easier processes for the production of kerosene, first from coal and then from petroleum, is one of the things that contributed to the end of commercial whaling, but it was an increase in convenience rather than a breakthrough in terms of energy density. Oil doesn't run away like whales do.

                                                      That said, I was more bringing it up as a "non-artifact vehicle" reference point.
                                                      It's a good reference point, but I think it demonstrates just how huge the gulf between Autochthonian transportation technology and Creation's transportation technology is. The Haslanti have fragile dirigibles, the Autochthonians have armored, self-propelled airships.

                                                      Originally posted by NewK View Post
                                                      I would be reluctant to change Autocthonia to make it fit "Better" with creation. That, IMO, simply leaves fans of Autocthon and fans of creation-based games, equally unhappy. I'd prefer to just play up the fact that Autocthonia isn't mandated to appear in creation, leaving you free to play in a very different environment, while leaving some suggestions on how to handle contact, with the cavaet that it would be a big, big deal, and represent a change to creation easily as big as the fall of the Shogunate or the Usurpation.
                                                      I'm not trying to make it fit Creation "better" per se. I'm seeing a trend in the game line as a whole that I assume will naturally include Autocthonia, with characters possessing fewer artifacts, the number of artifacts in the setting being played down, while the impact of individual artifacts played up. A concentration of narrative weight, if you will. The game's mechanics already incentivize taking a single daiklave and buying several of its Evocations instead of buying the broader artifact panoply of 2nd Edition.

                                                      Maintaining the Autochhonian aesthetic while allowing this sort of change to the prevalence of artifacts requires the offloading of some of those elements from artifacts and onto something else. Luckily for us, Autochthonia has something that can easily shoulder that load in the form of patropoli and metropoli. That such an explanation also prevents one of the Eight Nations from becoming an instant world power beyond the capacity of Lookshy during a "Breach of the Seal of Eight Divinities" scenario is more a happy bonus than a primary goal.
