    What are the local consequences of suddenly creating large herds of cattle

    10-06-2020, 10:58 PM
    So let's say you're a terrestrial sorcerer, and you managed to learn a new spell, and it's pretty cool. It let's you make more animals. So you grab say, a cow bone, bury it in a plot of land, and out pops out a dozen or so smaller cows. It takes half a year for them to grow to maturity, by which means you got a dozen more cows. This applies to any kind of animal, like dogs, sheep, or goats.

    It's a ritual spell and you can do it, again and again, day after day. Which means that in the course of a month you can probably make a hundred or so extra cattle, ready to grow in less than a year.

    So what are the issues and societal implications of this?

    If it's too powerful, make it one mini cow a day.

          Among other things, cows have to have somewhere they can stand. If you don’t have the rights to the land that they stand on, this could result in some disputes.

          And cows take quite a bit of land. Land that other people would probably have use for.

          Even if you do have that much land and absolutely no one takes issue with you using it for a herd of cattle where they might have done something more productive, the land itself may not be ideal for grazing. And while it’s true that you could make some alterations, those might carry ecological impacts.

                                  Originally posted by Accelerator View Post

                                  Per animal? Much less.
                                  Per pound of meat? No, not really.

                                  Sheep eat ~3% of their body weight in dry matter (DM). Cows eat about 2%. But you can graze sheep on hills and crags, you don't have to call them in for milking and each sheep weighs SIGNIFICANTLY less than a cow.

                                  I'd suggest limiting the spell to only working under a full moon, and have it birth two, mundane calves (one male, one female) - no different to any other newborns of the species. So it takes a couple of years to grow beef to maturity, but each month nets you a breeding pair of whatever (or a side of beef and a heifer).

                                                      Thoughts

                                                      - Are these animals clones? Or random copies of random animals from around the world? If clones, then it makes selective breeding *interesting* (clone up Dolly the Bull and then use as breeding stock). Potentially it also means a shift to fewer cows and many gelded bulls if you're breeding for meat (since you don't have to worry about reproduction so much), but I think spell costs would typically be against that. May also lead to a bunch of inbreeding problems etc.
                                                      - could be useful for stealing particularly good breeds of horses and the like, just find or trade for a buried stud, then clone. Likewise long lost Solar improved breeds and the like (depending on caveats). Also easier to colonize islands etc (why bring cattle? Bring a tooth instead).
                                                      - if it affects relative productivity of pastoral v agricultural subsistence, useful for pastoral peoples
