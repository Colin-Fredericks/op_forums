    My reading was that the difference in opinion was literally her saying "the gods have abandoned us, so we need to make a new, better world" and him saying "no, we need to try and return things to the way they used to be", ie. his intention to summon the gods and her rejection of that was what made them split. I read his horrified reaction as being theocratic in nature (ie. he finally understood that she no longer cared about his gods and couldn't deal with it.)

    Both of them agree that they want a better world, they just disagree on what "better" looks like - Salem wants a world that was better than it was before, with the two of them becoming better leaders than the gods (not fucking hard), while Ozpin wants to try and force the world back to what it was when the gods were still around by "earning redemption" from them. In Exalted terms, she's a Solar Exalt and he's a loyalist Infernal.

    (Also I do recognize that some parts of what I'm saying are very obviously not what the authors wanted us to take from it. I think they're incompetent and are aping certain real-world stories - biblical floods, etc. - without understanding the mindset of absolute, unquestioning religious belief that makes them work, which clearly doesn't apply to their shiny glowing mass-murderers. We're supposed to accept that their big glowy demon-kings had the right to exterminate humanity Because Disobedience and Because Sin, and therefore we're supposed to be cheering on Ozpin's desire to grovel before the demons that genocided humanity once already. They haven't been subtle about giving Salem big obvious "THIS IS A VILLAIN" signals. And they're probably going to have her trying to inexplicably wipe out humanity because EVIL, and this is different from the God of Darkness because, uh, he calls himself a god and is really powerful I guess? And also she and a few hundred people she gathered insulted him, even though he was in no real danger, and therefore his murdering an entire planet was her fault? But I don't think it holds together at all. I do seriously mean it when I say I think that the gods are worse than Salem and that Ozpin is a misguided villain willing to eventually risk all of humanity for the sake of his demon-lords - I think the Loyalist Infernal comparison is very apt.)

    This also sort of ties into what I mentioned last season (where Yang went absolutely ballistic about Ozpin giving Qrow the ability to turn into a bird, when the really horrible thing he'd done was keep Salem a secret.) The writers recognize that Ozpin's actions are morally reprehensible and they clearly have "and then people get mad at Ozpin" written in the script, but they're either unable or unwilling to confront the worst parts of Ozpin's deceptions, so people constantly get mad at him for the wrong thing.

    I mean... Salem being immortal isn't really a big deal? Who cares? She's not omnipotent. She can be isolated or sealed or launched into space until she stops thinking or even just convinced to stop.

    The fact that the relics are capable of summoning demon-gods who have exterminated humanity and may do so again when summoned, and that Ozpin, not Salem, is the one who wants to use them this way, is... kind of a much bigger problem. Ozpin's motivation is a much bigger problem, too - basically 'make things the way they were a billion years ago when the god-monsters were around and judging us'. (Yes, he wants to improve the world. But he wants to improve it so he can summon the demon-gods so they can put the world back the way it was before. Again, that was his entire argument with Salem.)

    But they also want this part where people distrust Ozpin to be temporary. So they can't point out the core problems with his actions (since those are basically irreconcilable and turn him into a misguided villain when recognized.)

    I mean I'm not saying "Salem did nothing wrong" - she does want to rule over the world as a god, and they've been handing her constant Villain Ball stuff. I compared her to a Solar Exalt to Ozpin's loyalist Infernal above, and I think that's pretty accurate. But her problems are more with her methods, and... writing-wise, it feels forced? Why does she suddenly decide she wants to rule the world? Where did that come from? She was happy to just live in her hut doing nothing; Ozpin was the one who pulled her out of it. He's the one who had the idea to conquer everything, really.

    It feels like it'd be easier to talk Salem down than it would be to convince Ozpin not to summon his god-monsters. Like, they're both villains, and initially Ozpin is more sympathic because he's mislead and confused by bad theology, while Salem is doing horrible stuff with clearer vision; but Ozpin's motivations are fundamentally worse, and unless he can be convinced that his "gods" are horrific mass-murdering demons (which seems unlikely given that they literally murdered the entire world and he just sort of shrugs), it makes more sense to go to Salem and try to talk to her.

    (In fact, hold on, why are people determined to kill Salem? How, exactly, is she the Big Bad? Taking over the world was Ozpin's goal, not hers, even if she got caught up in the idea. The Grimm aren't her creations; she only controls them incidentally and, as far as we can tell, in a limited fashion, so killing her won't do anything to them. We don't know why she's collecting the artifacts, but based on everything we know, "ensure that Ozpin can never use them" is a very reasonable guess, ie. it's entirely possible she's trying to save the world and Ozpin is trying to destroy it. It feels like some keystone fell out of the plot at some point - how exactly is Salem the villain? She's been attacking academies, and has definitely done some really bad stuff in the service of that, but it was all ultimately only to get at the artifacts, and honestly based on what we know keeping the artifacts away from Ozpin feels like a very good idea - out of all the people on earth, he's the one person who can absolutely never be trusted with them ever, since he seems to at least be notionally willing to entertain the idea of eventually summoning his Evil Genocide Demon-Gods.)

                                                    From a strict plotting perspective, here's how I would have played it:

                                                    * * *
                                                    -The Gods come to be, and war forever in the time before time before coming to their entente cordial. There are two of them, the God of Creation and the God of Destruction, although each also contains within itself a small part of the nature of the other; within Destruction is the desire to watch the universe evolve and move forward, new growth springing from the ashes of the old, and within Creation is the need to ever-create new things and not just fill the world to the brim and then be done forever.

                                                    (If you want to be REALLY outre, you could do it so there are always two Gods, Creation and Destruction, but which one is which at any given time isn't sure. Sometimes the older, more level-headed one is the God of Destruction, and sometimes the younger, less secure, more chaotic one is the God of Creation. But that's not necessary, that's just me thinking out loud.)

                                                    -The First Age of the World, when man arises, the ultimate act of collaboration between the brothers, who grant them both powerful gifts. A Golden Age, not necessarily of peace, but of wonder, magic, and power. The God of Creation is worshiped and is beloved by many, who seek his gifts and favor and understanding. The God of Destruction is shunned, respected and honored but feared; people do not understand his gifts and his powers, and some view them as curses and afflictions.

                                                    -Ozma finds Salem in her tower. They adventure together until his death.

                                                    -Salem approaches the God of Creation. He sympathizes, but tells her that even if he wanted to bring back Ozma, he can't; the domain of death belongs to his brother. He could create something new, but not reach through death to bring back the old.

                                                    -Salem approaches the God of Destruction. Using her cunning, she determines not to simply straight-up ask him for a boon; rather, she presents herself to him as his acolyte, the first ever. The God of Creation has priests and priestesses in their hundreds and their thousands, but she is the first to offer to serve him. He accepts her, and begins to train her in his secret wisdom that she might go forth and teach the world how to better understand him.

                                                    -Salem doesn't give a shit about the God of Destruction or teaching people to understand him and his wisdom. Salem is here to invent necromancy, yo.

                                                    -Which she does.

                                                    -And uses.

                                                    -But both Gods find out, like, the very second she does, because Salem is one of those people who is so clever she shoots her own ass off.                                                     -Salem is cursed. Because the Gods are merciful, and also sort of dumb, for her insane transgressions against nature she's cursed with immortality, to wander the world until she learns her lesson.

One of them fucks up the moon in a fit of pique. The First Age ends; civilization collapses, both because it was dependent heavily on the gifts bestowed by the Gods, and because Salem so poisoned mankind against itself that it tears itself into pieces fighting over the scraps. The world is reduced, a remnant of what it once was.

                                                    -The Creatures of Grimm arise from the remnants of the God of Destruction's domain. The God of Destruction doesn't give a shit.

                                                    -The Faunus arise from the remnants of the God of Creation's domain. The God of Creation is elated; new life that he hadn't planned for has sprung forth! He turns his face to the world again.

                                                    -Salem seizes the God of Destruction's former earthly domain and begins to set herself up as a living god, offering "protection" from the Grimm that she, herself, largely (although not absolutely) has learned to control.

                                                    -The God of Creation, by this time, has forgiven mankind; his brother, however, has not; he's still really fucking pissed off that Salem betrayed him and that mankind then stabbed him in the face in her favor. And the two must act in concert. The God of Destruction has agreed to these terms; the God of Creation may dispatch a champion to Remnant to stand against Salem and to unite and rebuild mankind. Along with this champion, they will once again bestow blessings, in the form of the four Relics. Mankind may use the Relics whenever they wish to summon the Gods back to Remnant and stand before them; if judged worthy, the Gods will dwell in Remnant again and bestow their blessings and the world will be made whole again. If not, the Gods will abandon Remnant to its own devices forever. The nature of the Third Age of the world is thus up to mankind to decide.

                                                            It seems really hard to translate Yang Xiao-Long into a Dragon-Blood while keeping both her aesthetics and her tactics.

                                                            Fire Aspect (Athletics, Dodge, Melee, Presence, Socialize) who favors Brawl/Martial Arts and Resistance, with other Abilities being picked to taste. Integrity, Archery/Firearms, Ride/Drive, Craft...

                                                            Arguably the closest thing that a Dragon-Blood can get to Yang's "Super Mode" Semblance is the Fire Signature for Resistance, Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit. The Charm requires a Fire Aura, so any time that Yang's anima banner goes bonfire and turns any Aura she has into the Fire element, she can go Super Saiyan.

                                                            But the Charm's duration is Aura, and that leads to some complications.

                                                            Yang fights with her fists. Specifically, while wearing artifact fist weapons called Ember Celica, with great potential for Evocations. So naturally we'd want to choose the Ability that works best not only with Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit, but also with that weapon.

                                                            Falcon Style allows smashfists, but it remains to be seen how well it matches her character concept. If it does, as a non-elemental Martial Art, its Charms pose no threat to Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit's duration.

                                                            If Fire Dragon Style allowed the use of smashfists like her Ember Celica, it would be a perfect solution. But it doesn't, so it isn't. She can at least use the Style and its Charms for unarmed parries and kick attacks and maybe to potentially break limits with Falcon Style.

                                                            The Brawl Ability and Terrestrial Brawl Charms will work with Ember Celica and its Evocations, but this is the most complicated puzzle.

                                                            1) If she's using Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit, she needs to maintain her Fire Aura. As a striker who seldom grapples, she becomes limited to basically just Become the Hammer and Pounding Surf Style. She couldn't even use Blade-Deflecting Palm.

                                                            2) Even without using Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit, once Yang's anima reaches bonfire, any Aura she has immediately turns into the Fire element, and if she loses that Aura, she will be prevented from gaining any new Aura from non-Fire Charms. But basically none of the Brawl Charms are Fire elemental, so Yang is locked out of the Aura-boosted effects and Aura-dependent of every Brawl Charm except the Fire Signature. So outside of Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit, the Fire Aura is of limited usefulness to her, even after heavy investment into Athletics and Dodge.

                                                            3) Erupting Fury Barrage, the Fire Signature, only spends Fire Aura but not levels of anima banner. As a reminder, a bonfire anima will transform any Aura Yang already has into Fire, and will prevent her from generating any Aura but Fire. But Erupting Fury Barrage is also the only Brawl Charm which could let her generate a new Fire Aura, so if she mixes elements with the EFB attack, she won't recover her Fire Aura at the end of the turn. She probably can't maintain that Fire Aura long enough to build the Initiative for another EFB.

                                                            4) Ignoring the Wood Signature Charm, a Terrestrial's only way to avoid or escape bonfire anima through spending anima levels is a Currents Sweep to Sea, a grappling Charm which is useless if your opponent never leaves close range, or at least not when you need to spend an anima level. So once she's in bonfire, she's probably staying there and stuck with Fire Auras and not getting back to a Water Aura. (This is something Ember Celica can solve, with an Evocation that spends anima levels.)

                                                            5) As a striker, Yang might want to use Stone Fist Strike, but it would cost her a Fire Aura that could be sustaining Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit or enabling/empowering other Fire Charms. More often, it would cost her a Water Aura that could be empowering Blade-Deflecting Palm, Pounding Surf Style, and Hammering Wave Technique, and allowing Swift-Striking Tide and Fist-Spinning Maelstrom. Stone Fist Strike could grant her an Earth Aura that would allow her to access Rolling Boulder Blow or Firmament-Shattering Impact for a smashing attack with her smashfists... if she doesn't need to use a reflexive Charm of the wrong type before her next turn. The Earth elemental Charms don't seem promising for anyone but Earth Aspects, since they only need to juggle two different elements instead of three. (They always told me not to build a Magic: The Gathering deck with three different colors.)

                                                            6) Wood Brawl Charms really have nothing for Yang. She doesn't need more grappling Charms, and Oaken Thew Exertion isn't useful enough for punching things to justify juggling a fourth element.
