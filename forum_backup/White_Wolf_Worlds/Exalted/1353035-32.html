                            Personally, I will always dig my heels in to speak for it being a great concept to have the Great Curse as "Great heroes undone by a moment of extreme pride or rigidity (of moral principle, of belief, of behaviour) that they cannot undo and which has terrible consequences" and for this to affect PCs right from the point of starting play (as opposed to *just* creeping moral decline at some point in the future).

                            If you're making a game that is *about* heroism (and I think Exalted actually and rarely among rpgs aspires to be about heroism and virtue in a somewhat critically thinking and questioning way which helps its players think better about this topic, beyond just being "White Wolf's Heroic Fantasy Game", and this is really cool and unique among heroic fantasy rpgs), then it seems pretty good to have some element in that of thinking critically about the dangers of great power invested in heroes who ultimately all too human and flawed (that heroes are worthy of admiration, but never unthinking deference without concern for their potential for flaws). And dramatically it inspires high and noble feelings of tragedy in us and all this stuff.

                            (To add, it's even better to tie this into the perhaps-deservedly wrought death curse of Primordials, tying it into the cycle of heroism and violence in the world and getting us to think about those cycles of usurpation and revolution and the questionable legitimacy of each of those!)

                            But I think it is hard to get people to buy into it...! Exalted started as like 70% golden reborn heroes making the world better, and 30% great heroes with feet of clay, and is now as of 3e probably something like 90:10(?).

                            The Great Curse really hasn't been front-loaded in much of the Exalted material. Do we know when any Age of Sorrows NPC Solars have had Limit Break and how it has affected their story? As far as I can tell after 20 years, we actually really do not (either at all, or at least often). Do we really get any advice subtly wainscotted through the books giving STs Storytelling hooks for the Great Curse, advice how to use it, reminders that it should be integral to their storytelling? Is it really in any chapter fiction?

                            And in that situation, I absolutely am not surprised that people kind of see The Great Curse as a somewhat inexplicable facet or something to get to for Elder Solars.

                            If we really wanted it to work, it should be in a lot of places; hooks through the text, NPC biographies, Solar Caste Book subjective experiences, Storytelling advice. And I think the authors have generally somewhat shyed away from it, perhaps sensing it as a divisive element, which lends a bit of a feel of "Oh, you can have it, but you totally don't have to include any of this Epic Tragedy stuff if you don't want". And because it is not often mentioned, it functionally isn't present in a lot of people's Exalted as experienced, rather than woven integrally into stories, and the audience can't really blamed at this point for just going the remaining 10-30% into 100% golden reborn heroes (and in a sense maybe that's the more functional thing and I am being a hopeless romantic here). So if you had a 4th edition, maybe you should either front-load it a lot more in prominence, or take a different stance on it entirely...

                                                    I think the Sidereal book will give us a lot more information on the 3e incarnation of the Great Curse and the role it played for two reasons.

                                                    The first is that it's always been the primary reason the Sidereals launched the Usurpation, even if they didn't know about it at the time or ever. And a lot of their background information was written from a neutral perspective, providing information about the Great Curse to the players that the Sidereals did not have in setting.

                                                    Secondly, the Sidereals themselves have been said to have a different relationship with the Great Curse than the other Exalted. In 1e they didn't have a standard Curse mechanic, relying on roleplaying to get it across, and instead dealt with Pattern Spider Bite as a mechanical balance. While 2e, attempting to ease complaints about them not having a mechanical Curse, gave them a fairly weak one. And 3e... well while I've long been outside the discussion circles for Exalted altogether, last I knew the prevailing school of thought was that Pattern Spider bite should be done away with altogether, so that Sidereal players would no longer be deterred from using one of their splat powers because they periodically get punished for it. If so, then a mechanical Great Curse flaw would be called for to keep them 'in line' with the other splat mechanics (at least, those are my initial thoughts).
