    I think this topic is a little off.

    Not that it's wrong, but rather that it's missing what Hatewheel tried to do in 3E: in 3E's supplemental material, aka Hatewheel's myriad forum posts he was making while throwing out the book for the third time, he argued very strenuously that demon summoning was immoral slavery. To him, that was natural, and I remember him getting into arguments with old forums regulars that, yes, Demon Summoning was immoral and to use it was to be an immoral person. This is rather continued to be brought forward by the text of summoning a demon, which says it is summoned screaming in rage, sorrow or fear, and that if you fail to bind it it will most likely be furious with you. The implication is, of course, demons are summoned unwillingly, and that, most likely, summoning a demon 'willingly' is something that, if it's at all possible, probably is something done a very different way.

    In some ways, to me, it felt like a response to 2E's 'build a bear workshop' demons. In 2E fandom, you could summon an infinite amount of demons that would infinitely craft you artifacts forever who only ever wanted to craft artifacts, because 2E was a wasteland of idiot ideas pushed to absurd limits by people whose only focus was winning a hypothetical game they'd never actually play. Hatewheel's strong stance about demon summoning being slavery felt like a backlash to the fandom idea, that it couldn't be moral or right, which would only make sense, because you're conscripting the losers of a war into forced slavery to labor for you. While it may not be out of sorts for some of the bronze age inspiration of Exalted, Exalted has always been willing to go 'just because it's what everyone is doing, doesn't mean it's right, and you should be willing to force your mortality onto the world if it upsets you', after all, even in ancient times like those- or more recent times-, you can always find people who stand up against the 'conventional' wisdom and do what is morally right (Mr. John Brown is a big, and constantly more and more relevant, example.).

    However, I think there's two distinct issues with this that 3E did not address, or addressed in such a way that is very ineffective. I'll do my best to discuss both of them:

    The first is the question of 'what if you actually want to learn from the Demons?' and 'How do you treat demons?'. Forced labor is wrong, that much is obvious; chattel slavery, sharecropping, kidnapping, all of that, I will take the assumption that everyone who reads this post understands that such things are naturally disgusting and doing them makes you a worse person. Not that people won't do them, but that there is an immorality to doing them. But the question that then comes up is, how do you engage with demons in a way that is not part of those institutions? Demons are trapped in a hellish world that is also innately hostile to non-demons, unless that has changed. You can go to the Demon World, but it is not simple, and it is not easy. This means interactions with them where you wish to treat with them as equals are difficult; if you wish to learn something from a demon, or to ask them to make something for you, there is little way to effectively do that. It's a forced separation that makes it almost impossible to really engage with demons outside of chattel slavery, which is a shame because, just like humans- which, again is probably entirely intentional-, demons are fascinating characters, each with their own idiosyncrasies and quirks, but that, like Gods, could have information that you as a player want to obtain, and if you wish to do this fairly or without engaging in violent practices, the ST sort of just has to make up a way for you to do this, or you're going to be involved in a multiple sessions long excursion to Malfeas which easily has the chance of totally dragging the game off track. I think if you really wanted to present Demon Summoning as a horrific way to interact with demons, there needs to be other ways to interact with them that don't involve that. Ways where you give up the safety of sorcerously binding something to your will to never harm you or to never stop doing the task you set to them to instead interact with the demons as thinking individuals with their own opinions you have to respect.

    The second is Summon Elemental. Summon Elemental seems to be an attempt to be a more 'moral' demon summoning. Rather then forcing something into slavery, you create a being that is specifically designed for the task, one without personality, drive, or history, that completes it's task then disappears. That is clearly the attempt to be more moral and 'correct' about summoning, because it's not inducting a living being into slavery. But even then, part of this is how it casually and completely misses the mark of offering an actual choice. If the intent is to present the idea that 'Demon summoning is easier, and more effective, but it's morally unjustifiable', Summon Elemental doesn't do this; there is no reason to learn Demon of the First Circle, which is where most sorcerers would tap out anyway, because Summon Elemental is effectively the exact same spell but without the baggage. There is no real choice within the first tier; you would always want Summon Elemental, even if you are, yourself, an amoral bastard who wants the easiest way, because a failed summoning of the Elemental doesn't result in it breaking free. It just fails and doesn't exist. This makes it infinitely safer for literally everyone with absolutely no trade-off, and it's also morally superior! Win-win! Which is kind of awkward.

    To clarify, I don't think this is wrong or even incorrect. I think it could be a very powerful statement on the uselessness and negativity of chattel slavery, of enslaving the defeated, of forced labor and dehumanization. I think that showing that those things aren't actually advantageous or beneficial, that they really just get the same result as being a good person, or maybe even a worse result, but that they're still continued because of greed or stagnation, are good. But I think that 3E doesn't show, that I've seen, that Demon Summoning only remains popular because of that stagnation. It seems to be relying, at least in part, on 2E's assumptions that Demon Summoning was 'more effective' to build a 3E belief that it's Demon Summoning is more effective, that can then be turned on it's head by going 'actually Elemental Summoning is just as good and it's not as morally reprehensible'. Which is where I think it fails. I think there should be some sort of attempt being made to present *why* this got popular in the first place. What made Demon Summoning popular, why does it continue to be popular when it's the much less efficient way to get supernatural helpers for a project. Chattel Slavery continued almost entirely because of racism where the elites used readings of cultural holy books to explain away why owning people as property was actually the will of their God. What cultural forces make Demon Summoning the popular form of bringing in magical labor? If the game can explain that, and offer ways to interact with Demons in ways that don't completely absorb the focus of the game into the area around demons, it would go a long way towards making the idea something that can be interacted with as more then a binary. Because if you don't have the first, then it comes across as a paper institution powered entirely by metagame conversations on the previous edition's forums, and if you don't have the second, you make it so that the only way to interact with a fascinating, nuanced group of characters is by engaging in either chattel slavery or forcing the ST, and the entire game, to bend to your desires, and that the best way to do anything with demons is to just leave them alone and never interact with them as the only moral choice, which is problematic in it's own way.
