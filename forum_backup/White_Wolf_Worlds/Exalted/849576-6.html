    After SO MANY threads hashing out dice probabilities, got a couple of very mechanical ones:
    1. What if the "1-point Stunt" were no more? +1/+2 Successes for exceptional description, but no "unwritten" +2 dice to every action ever.
    2. What if a Specialty becomes +2 dice/+1 level of a static value? Of note, I tend to favor specialties which only come up sometimes (so, "Dueling" or "When outnumbered" for Melee, not "with my weapon of choice, which I never fight without").

    Off the top of my head, both are hard on Solar PCs (in that the former is hard on PCs in general, and the latter helps DBs out a smidge), but not overwhelmingly. Anything else?

            Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post
            After SO MANY threads hashing out dice probabilities, got a couple of very mechanical ones:
            1. What if the "1-point Excellency" were no more? +1/+2 Successes for exceptional description, but no "unwritten" +2 dice to every action ever.
            The big thing is that people will be much less likely to do the things stunting is meant to incentivize, and when they do go out of their way to engage with the stunt mechanics stunt they'll expect an "exceptional description" bonus.

            There are also various little things. You need higher dicepools to be viable -- stunting is partly designed to allow you to do impressive things with smaller pools. Dice tricks are generally less effective. The various zany system-pushing extended roll systems, like crafting and Wyld-shaping, run into issues since they're calibrated for stunts. I'd just resign yourself to giving the +2 to these rather than trying to recalibrate them without breaking anything else.

            Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post
            2. What if a Specialty becomes +2 dice/+1 success? Of note, I tend to favor specialties which only come up sometimes (so, "Dueling" or "When outnumbered" for Melee, not "with my weapon of choice, which I never fight without").

            Off the top of my head, both are hard on Solar PCs (in that the former is hard on PCs in general, and the latter helps DBs out a smidge), but not overwhelmingly. Anything else?
            Specialty optimization becomes much higher-stakes. That's the level of modifier that makes people sit up and take notice, so you can expect more people to go out of their way to game their specialties hard under this paradigm (you really want that bonus on your clashes, for instance). On the upside, you get rid of the thing where people take specialties to bump their static value pools from even to odd values to exploit sweet, sweet rounding.

            An autosuccess means you can't ever fail difficulty 1 tasks and can't ever botch a specialty roll; it makes charms that give automatic successes less valuable, since the first one is worth far more than the rest. This might be a bit much, given how high the bar is for difficulty 1 in Ex3. +2 dice benefits dice gimmicks like double 9s, obviously.

                                                    What breaks if: You can use a Move action after a successful Rush action?

                                                    Right now, the rules explicitly but specifically allow you to use a Rush action action after using a Move action in your turn. Which means you can Move into short range, and then Rush, or you can Move into close range, and then Rush.

                                                    But it also means that you cannot Rush while at short range and then Move into close range. Which is not a terribly huge deal, since you could Move while at short range and then Rush when you are in close range. But it does mean that you need to commit to advancing on an opponent before you know whether or not the Rush will succeed, whereas if you could try Rushing before you decide to Move, you could at least cut your losses and perhaps Move towards a different target or change to a tactic which requires you to stay put.


                                                    Possible answer to my own question:

                                                    The inability to Move after Rushing also means this: If someone successfully Disengaged from you and moved out to short range on their turn, you must first Move before you can attempt to Rush, and your Move action will trigger their reflexive Disengage movement. Whereas, if you could attempt Rushing first, your following Move action (into close range) would trigger their reflexive retreat (back into/maintaining short range), which would then trigger your reflexive pursuit (back into/maintaining close range).

                                                    Right now, you must choose to gamble on a Rush action directed at an enemy at close range, against the possibility of your opponent attempting to flee. If they do not flee, any attack you make that turn suffers flurry penalties for nothing. If they do flee and you were not prepared, they can escape from you even as you chase after them.

                                                    If you could use a Move action after a successful Rush action, the stakes of that gamble are fundamentally reduced. You can always concentrate on fighting an opponent at close range without fear that they can escape. You don't even need to flurry your attempts to chase them with an attack on the same turn, if you are patient -- in catching up to the target at close range, they once again need to beat you on an opposed roll with their Disengage action, or else you can spend all of your next turn concentrating on attacking them.

                                                    This is a systematic change to tactical possibilities beyond simply allowing an assaulting player the ability to wait and see if a Rush succeeds before taking a Move action.
