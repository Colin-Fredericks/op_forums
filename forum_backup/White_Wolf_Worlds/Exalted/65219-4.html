                            Godparents in Creation:
                            During the Time of Glory it was a tradition in many human communities for a couple to pray to a particular god or deva shortly after a child’s birth and make what sacrifices they could in the hope that the spirit took notice and agreed to protect the child.
                            Often these prayers were ignored or the spirit in question agreed but considered the child a very low priority but tales of a god being impressed by the prayer and sacrifice of mortals and taken up this duty have passed into legend.

                            The conventional role of godparents fell out of favour in the First Age and was later considered a heresy of the Immaculate Faith as the concept of literal godparents became somewhat demonised by tales of overzealous spirits that would remove any and all obstacles to its charge’s desires eliminating siblings and potential love rivals along with enemies. Though the honorary title of godparent is still given to a person in a position of authority who offers to ensure the child's well-being.

                            The resurgence of this custom in the Age of Sorrows was brought about mostly by the Fair Folk. A surprising number of Raksha have found the comparison to a god flattering and offered oaths to guarantee the infant’s safety (with certain loopholes of course, just to keep things interesting). Demons also tend to be receptive to such requests, the notion that mortals would request that denizens of Malfeas protect their children is often met with delight in the demon city and seen as a sign that Creation is lost without the Primordials.

                                Forgot one earlier: In my game, the breaking of the Lunar castes is a total lie. The "castes" are actually just tricks that any Lunar can learn to tap into, and the elders know it (the ones who aren't so crazy they've forgotten anyway). It's just that the dozen or so surviving elders don't have a full view of all the caste-type powers that Lunars could learn, so they broke it all down to three basic castes to simplify things for the young reincarnated Lunars they had to train by the dozen... and then kind of got high on their own secrecy and reserved certain kinds of knowledge for themselves. Lunars also don't need moonsilver tattoos to avoid Wyld taint--they're just an amplifier for a natural ability, and a means for the Silver Pact to keep a leash on their "pups."

                                Raksi retreated to her towers because she hated the hypocrisy shown by the other survivors of the Usurpation, and she turned into a horrible person after centuries of isolated brooding. She does eat babies occasionally, but mostly just as a means of terror to keep her ape-men in line and to scare off other Lunars from bothering her. Ma-Ha-Suchi isn't Wyld-tainted, per se; he's suffering severe PTSD and survivor's guilt, plus a whole head full of bad wiring brought on by decades of terror and anger. He used to be a dandy and courtier; having to become a murderer to survive made broke him in some fundamental way. In order to cope with the monstrosity he's had to inflict, he's turned himself into a real monster--he went from playing at the Big Bad Wolf to actually being the Big Bad Wolf.

                                Related to that, the heroic tale of Raksi and Ma-Ha-Suchi's escape from the Usurpation has entered into Lunar lore as a popular song and fable. Some mutated version of it crept out into mortal societies as the tale of Red Riding Hood and the Big Bad Wolf.
