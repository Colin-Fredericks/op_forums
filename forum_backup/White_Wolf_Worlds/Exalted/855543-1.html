                                                Pertinent quotes from the What We Know wiki:

                                                Q: Will Abyssals still be altered Solar Exaltations? (Mockery)
                                                A: Yes. (John Mørke)

                                                (on why Abyssals remain altered Solars)
                                                Throwing out the castes might as well be throwing out the book. "Dark Solars" continues to be a concept that fires my imagination, far more than "Exalt of the [Oblivion/Void/other generic concept]" ever will, and that the game has pushed them in that direction in 2e is one of the number one reasons why they went from arguably the most popular splat in 1e to the hands-down least popular splat in 2e. (John Mørke)

                                                No Mirror Keyword. You can't have a Charm set that is an expression of Ability skill and apexes in a display of peak skill or power and also have that Charm set contain a body of Charms that a Solar cannot have. The idea is not to challenge the paradigm of "Abyssals are Solars" but to embrace it, and then concentrate on what else they are so that they feel different. Being a Solar transformed into this thing means feeling like a Solar but also feeling like something else. Being subtle and substantial is the trick, and it took looking at what the design of the Solar Charm set is really pointing at to see how to point the Abyssal Charm set at something else. (John Mørke)

                                                It is stylistically accurate to depict the Abyssals as having deep connections to the Solars. EX3 Abyssals are radically different than they have been in previous editions. Having some identical Charms to Solars is a benefit, rather than a subtraction, because it emphasizes the fact that they were / are Solars, while also making them something else. (John Mørke)

                                                            I feel I could do with reading more dying Earth fiction myself, such as the actual Dying Earth series and the Book of the New Sun and the like, but what I can glean from it... when you've got Abyssals who are an effective combination of that kind of pulp hero wandering through a world on the brink of its end (which is also fairly evident in Vampire Hunter D, which is another thing people have often wanted as an Abyssal inspiration), and then combine those with some of the heroes of the gothic and macabre and Byronic, with their melancholy introspection and gloomy moods and aesthetics, I think you've both got a firm base for establishing the thesis of the Abyssal Exalted as internally coherent without relying on basic villainy and making them feel like an organic contrast with the Solar Exalted culture hero, including a basis for desiring a transition between one and the other that doesn't need to be about straightforward moral dichotomies.

                                                            I like the part where prospective Abyssals have to be offered and accept their particular mantle, almost purely for how it all feels very fitting as a mythic narrative, but have lost a lot of tolerance for it being used as a big judgemental cudgel to whack them over the head with (which, possibly unfairly, I currently attribute a lot to Nephilpal's hang ups showing through his work; see also akuma, his whole elaborate thesis on how throwing away your heroic agency means you're a contemptible loser who deserves to have your personality scooped out).

                                                            I think using straightforward surface terms such as "Redemption" serves about the same purpose as calling the Exalted in general "heroes"; that using the traditional terminology in the text while leaving the questions and implications in subtext provokes more introspection, with too much analysis and clarification to relieve postmodern anxieties being something that might make it kind of intolerable.
