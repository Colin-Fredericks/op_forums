    Have you done cool stuff with Medicine ?

    03-13-2020, 09:53 AM
    Everything's in the title. I'm a bit sad because I usually like Medicinal scenarios where the doctor is at the front, but I've never had an interesting scenario where being a Master Doctor was challenging and felt rewarding.

    Have you examples in your games where Medicine shine beyond "I do 1 roll and it's over let's go to the next part of the game" ?

    Fakeedit : Ok I've actually had one, and it was a character poisoning her leader during Limit Break to take over her place. Was pretty fun and interesting, as the other people were not formed in Medicine so no one understood how all the efforts of the Medic couldn't save the leader.

      Seamstress in my seafaring game was pretty fun. She was a Ghost-Blooded Daybreak Abyssal from Skullstone, and the adopted daughter of the Silver Prince.

      ​She first put her medicinal skills to the test when the crew she served with started coming down with sickness. Once she learned that a tainted water barrel was the source, she dumped out the bad cask and nursed the crew back to health on the first island they reached.

      ​Later in the game, a crewman got a hand crushed really badly by a belaying pin after a line was sabotaged by a ghost. Because that crewman had seen these kind of injuries before (and wasn't aware she was an Exalt), he was convinced that the only thing to do was have it amputated; she had to convince him that no, actually, she could restore it to full functionality.

      ​Even later, her captain, an Exalt, lost an arm to a combination of a duel with Octavian and taking a brass-banded stone ball (fired from a ballista!) to the elbow. Seamstress ended up cobbling together a necrotech replacement for him out of bits of metal and dead sharks, but because he was being an asshole, she dumped it into the ocean without ever mentioning it, and just used a hearthstone to regenerate his limb.

          I haven’t done it yet hit in my game this summer I have a doctor PC that I’m planning a lot for.

          One of the things to realize is that even Solar doctors can only treat a limited number of sick people in a day or at once. If a town is completely ravaged by plague that’s a LOT more than one roll. You need to prioritize the sickest people, maybe even make some hard choices about how much effort it will take to save some people. You need to institute quarantine to stop the spread of infection and stop people from being reinfected. That means stopping travel in and out of the place as well as sectioning off sick, potentially sick and healthy people.

          You may also be able to treat with a disease god if one is nearby and that disease is within their domain.

          Treating wounded after a battle is similar, you can use Solar medicine to knit bones super fast, but if there’s a ton of people who are risking infection you might want to spread yourself thin just staving off gangrene.

          In either case you’ll want/need assistants to help out, followers or retainers to help tend to the people when you’re off seeing to others. If there’s sabotage your doctor might need to check the medicine you are distributing to make sure it’s not laced with poison or something.

                                  Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                  I like as well the idea of investigating and finding secret clues about the ancient sickness that doomed a NPC but I got the feeling players usually went straight for "I do 10 success to heal him" and I quite didn't know where to go from here
                                  That...yeah that’s an issue. One person no matter how sick is pretty trivial for a Solar to fix, it’s only when like dozens of people are on the verge of death that they really have to flex.

                                  You could also introduce a magical disease that requires a very specific herb to cure maybe, but then getting that herb may not require medicine so much as survival and stuff.

                                  Originally posted by Chausse View Post

                                  I also noticed that "virulent disease" usually don't imply Medicine as much as it implies Bureaucracy and Followers, and I wasn't sure how to bring back the Medicine in this to make the medicinal guy shine
                                  Bureaucracy can be good for the organizing part, but ultimately somebody needs to do the administering of aid and for the worst cases that needs to be a legendary healer. Plague, for instance, has a morbidity of 5, which means even somebody with an outstanding 10 dice to the roll still fails 50% of the time to lower their symptoms. Stopping the spread of disease by organizing triage centres and getting people to a place where they can be treated is the job of the bureaucracy Supernal Eclipse, but actually healing them requires medicine. A town of 500 might be experiencing an outbreak of Plague, for example, and 30 people may be infected. You might have to calm the population down with social rolls, organize them to deal with containment with bureaucracy rolls, and then go about actually implementing that plan.

                                  The Solar Doctor will first have to diagnose anyone not obviously suffering from plague, but suspected to have it so they can separate out the actually infectious people. Flawless Diagnosis Technique is going to do serious work in that case. Next she has to go about actually curing the people, which for each one requires a medicine roll every day of at least 5 successes, but there are only so many motes to go around and only so much time. Each patient requiring an hour, unless you start using Instant Treatment Methodology, which you might have to do if somebody is right on the verge of death and doesn’t have even an hour left.

                                  Combine all of that together and the only people that have any hope of saving everyone is a truly master Solar doctor. Basically Solars have the power to fight Hell, so put them through Hell.

                                      Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                      Maybe I missed something but from my understanding trying to heal someone when you don't know what he has is "just" a -4 or something along those lines.
                                      The way I run things, you do have to declare what you're trying to treat with Medicine. Sometimes, that's obvious - if your Dawn has just taken a daiklave strike to the chest, and is now trying to breathe through her ribcage instead of her throat, well, soaking the penalty for not completely diagnosing all the injuries in favor of just getting on with fixing the immediate problem makes sense. But if you just go ahead and treat the person with the hacking cough and fever without diagnosing them, you'll clear up the bad cold they had, sure... but they'll still die of the lung cancer they had in a few months.

                                      As to the original question, I don't have cool Medicine stories for my own character (I ST, and I don't get to play much), but one of my players is currently running a Twilight Medicine Supernal. So far, Doctor Hargrave has stitched up more than his fair share of grievous wounds and staved off diseases, including treating a whole manse-full of people for tuberculosis. But the coolest moment for him was, in my opinion, when he found a copy of the Black Treatise on necromancy and, wanting to take the fastest route into initiating into it possible, followed the procedure the book outlined, which involved cutting off someone's arm and replacing it with a corpse arm - and did it all on himself, working with someone to hold the mirror for him.

                                                  My problem is rather that I have some difficulties handling the "multiple sick people" part.

                                                  The situation is usually the following : There are a group of sick people somewhere of a very potent disease. The Medicine guy wants to save them, so he will ask to roll for diagnosis/healing. I'll allow it, he will roll crazy results, which is fine, but then I don't know how to handle the two following facts :
                                                  - Many people are sick and need to be treated
                                                  - The Charms and mechanics are oriented towards healing 1 single person per use

                                                  Since asking for the same roll 10 times in a row is boring, I'll usually call for 1 single roll to see how well he treats them. The player gets crazy successes, and then heals everyone, which is a bit unsatisfying because I would like to develop the process but I don't know where to begin and I feel like the system doesn't entice you either.
