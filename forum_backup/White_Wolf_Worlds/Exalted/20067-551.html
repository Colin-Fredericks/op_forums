            The Hundred Kingdoms is a subset of the Scavenger Lands, located more in its eastern portion. Basically, once you're past Great Forks, you're heading off into the Hundred Kingdoms up until you start hitting the major forests.

            ​Jiara and Thorns are parts of the Scavenger Lands, but neither in the Hundred Kingdoms or the River Province (that particularly fertile area clustered around the main rivers as encompassed by Sijan, Lookshy and Great Forks, within which basically everybody is in a mutual defence pact against the Realm).

            ​The name "Hundred Kingdoms" is itself more of a colloquialism referring to the fact that the portions of the Scavenger Lands that are east of the River Province are home to a large variety of states of varying size and government, protected from the Realm by the River Confederation being in the way, and benefiting from the region's more accommodating environment and easier time supporting a large population than the North and South are. While it's also distinct from the Southeast by virtue of not being cut off by major geographic obstacles, not having a large body of water hooked directly to the Wyld to let weird magic traffic in, and still having a major (but largely non-imperialistic) military hegemony neighbouring them.

            ​In any case, when there are references to Lookshy engaging in mercenary activities, the Hundred Kingdoms is where a lot of that is going to be going on.

                                Current definition of 'creature of darkness' in 3e (a forthcoming sidebar alongside Golden Janissary Style in What Fire Has Wrought):

                                Creatures of darkness are foes of Creation who stalk through the night or dwell in the dark places of the world. This includes demons and undead by default, but the Storyteller is free to include or exclude beings from this category at her discretion. A benign ancestor ghost might not be a creature of darkness, while a subterranean monstrosity empowered by forbidden gods could be.
                                Speaking for myself, I don't see this definition automatically categorising a god of shadows as a creature of darkness.
