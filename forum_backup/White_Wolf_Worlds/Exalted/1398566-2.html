    Originally posted by Moss Reynholm View Post
    Something I really, really like about the Realm in 3e is House Peleps’ ambitions for establishing the Imperial Western Protectorate. Essentially, their response to the Realm Civil War is likely to be forsaking the Blessed Isle entirely, striking out on their own and establishing a new empire in the West.

    It just makes so much sense, in a world that saw Clans Burano and Ophris set a precedent with Prasad and given the fact that supporting the Imperial Navy is INSANELY costly, so much so the House is basically at a ‘go big or go home’ tipping point. It’s a daring move, one that might fail but one only they are in position to make (I’ve never quite understood why other Houses would be ok with Peleps having the manpower – marines especially – that the navy would require, but that’s by the by…) the type of high stakes gamble that screams story options and daring-do.

    The fact it’s an ambition that is largely being held in check by loyalty and patriotism at the highest level just adds another great wrinkle (this on Peleps Lai, head of the Admiralty Board, from page 58 of WFHW, sums it up nicely: She still believes the Empress will return, and won’t entertain the notion of another monarch until convinced otherwise — and while she’s not as spry as she once was, her martial skills remain sufficient to trounce the occasional upstart who’d defy her. She supports the notion of a Western Empire, though, and dreams of personally presenting this new conquest to the Empress.)

    The whole idea is further explored awesomely in Heirs.

    I could waffle on about the way 3e has enriched the other Great Houses. It's great stuff.
    That's fun, because in my first campaign I randomly chose House V'neef to establish themselves in the Dreaming Sea, instead of participating in the Realm Civil War.             One thing I always liked about the Realm is that the prestige of its highest aristocracy is enshrined, but not based on things that they can just sit on to continuously accumulate easy wealth. Even when they're not in direct service to the Realm (which relatively few of them are), they still need to continuously exert effort and engage in meaningful work to maintain their wealth and status.

            And that is reflected in the schooling. Nobody is given an easy ride through that system based on family background. I like how Third Edition codified the idea that not all Exalted Dynast children are up to the standards that the top secondary schools hold for entrants, and so they need recourse from the lesser schools.

They also have other more mysterious powers which are left up to Storytellers, such as battle transformations or other magical abilities.

                                    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                    Are you saying a young Exalted from a prestigious family can't &quot;buy his way&quot; into a prestigious school in Exalted 3e ?
                                    I think it’s more that the Realm has a culture that’s extremely demanding of the dynasty. Normally if you’re stupid rich you can basically just pay people to make you more money and be really lazy, as long as some people in your family are doing something you’ll never have to really worry about anything. You can afford to be someone who just gives up and nobody cares.

                                    In the Realm that’s rare, you get your ass to work. You can get your ass to work on your own personal projects or something, but there is enormous pressure to perform. So much so that you need to fail and fall several times before you get to just flop. If you didn’t get into the best school, go to the second best, or third. If you fail out of that go to your aunt’s estate and try to learn her business from her. If you mess that up you’re going to the military, or to become a monk. If somehow you can’t even do that right maybe a few other things until finally your elders give up on you and you’re pushed away someplace quiet where you can’t do any more damage. Once there you live on the barest dynastic stipend and get shunned by everyone. Which is pretty horrible. Your life is still comfortable, but with no real friends and a family that hates you, you could easily fall into some pretty serious depression.

                                                        Another Realm-relevant piece of setting info: the Immaculate Order explicitly exists to reinforce (and enforce) the proper social order. Monks collect intelligence, hoard academic knowledge, build community relationships, and hone their bodies into weapons so they can apply the proper pressure to fix any kind of breakdown in the social order. They may preach when that's effective, but as far as they're concerned, how layfolk act is more important than what they believe. This is why they don't generally tell layfolk that the Solar and Lunar Anathema are Exalted--it would only make the Wyld Hunt's inherent tragedy more apparent, when hunters need hard hearts. It may not be a Celestial Exalt's fault that their power will inevitably upset the proper order of things, but the end result is the same, and so they need to be dealt with quickly and efficiently.

                                                            Sorry, late to this and other folk have answered lots of it already. Only thing I’ll add is that the write-ups we currently have of the lesser secondary schools do a fine job of emphasising the bits of their curriculum they excel at/make them unique/interesting (‘cause they provides the best story hooks…) as Alpharius explains.

                                                            So the Balustrade of Glorious Harmony is geomancy nirvana, the Forge of Mother’s Fury (another sorcery school) is big on weapon-forging (and is ‘one of the few forges in the Realm licensed to work orichalcum’), while the House of the Whispering Squall is a Threshold academy that apparently educates most of (Cadet) House Ferem’s youngsters.

                                                            One further wrinkle that I really like and think got added to the lore in Heirs (although it may simply be I’ve not registered it before - any one know?) is that you can learn sorcery at any of the iconic academies. It’s detailed in two paragraphs on p.24 if anyone’s interested/missed it, my favourite part of which being the Immaculate sorcerer detail. Spoilered below:



                                                            The other three great academies, as well as Pasiap’s Stair, maintain limited sorcerous curriculums for those with the talent and inclination. Students at the House of Bells learn war-magic to decimate armies or turn storms against enemy fleets; the Spiral Academy teaches how to loose one’s shadow as a spy or mesmerize with a glance.

                                                            The Cloister of Wisdom hosts the most advanced curriculum, teaching sorcery holistically in accordance with doctrine and the Immaculate Dragons’ wisdom. Immaculate sorcerer-monks endure less of the Realm’s prejudices and fears regarding sorcerers, provided they adhere to the Philosophy in all things — particularly in their sorcery.



                                                            --

                                                            On Peleps, Heirs at the minute has them securing Wu-Jian and Black Gannet Rock, then Wavecrest, Zhaojun and onwards, targeting V’neef holdings wherever possible but also explaining how they could end up fighting against pretty much all of the Western powers if that suits your game.

                                                            Could say loads more (it’s a pretty big chapter) but suspect people will wanna read it all in their own time when it comes fully available. I really enjoyed it – big thumbs up from me.
