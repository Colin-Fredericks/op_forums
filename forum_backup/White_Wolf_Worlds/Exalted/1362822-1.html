    I didn't really wanted to keep the Cover bonus, but since you had an idea I wanted to integrate it into my solution. By the rules, the Fortification Stratagem makes what you said, but I think it's acceptable to invent another Stratagem that works with more of a cover bonus instead of something else, or just with a higher cap success.

    My idea is that it's really the other way around when you say "I think, I’m not 100% sure, but I think that stratagems are meant to be used in tandem with other battlefield considerations.". I think the battlefields consideration should be taken into account before the stratagem war roll to define bonuses to the roll of the general, and then you find yourself in the situation given by the result of this roll.

    For your last paragraph, yes that's how I thought at first, but I'm trying to think about how the battle groups should not be handled in a strategic manner, from the mechanic of the game viewpoint, because they are not here to allow for strategic gameplay but rather the opposite, they are here to simplify the "lots of people" gameplay and let the heroes shine instead. I'm not arguing like I'm 100% right here, I'm more trying to see "What if I decided to play like this ?", and I think there is a value to consider the things this way. I guess you have as well handled battle groups in a big fight, where there are archers and infantry and cavalerie and stuff and in the end you spend your managing boring group of NPCs (they are not boring on their own but when you have more of them it kinda becomes) because someone said "We should have infantry here and archers behind them", which is a fine tactic for description and narration, but I don't think it goes all the way the system actually want you to play the Battle Groups.

            Oh yeah I never thought about Charms that allows for Withering or Decisive attack. I don't know if it means that the attacker know it is not lethal, but it sure makes things harder to understand clearly. I think at this point the design of the Charm is more designed to talk to the player making strategic choices "Ok here I wish to do a withering attack so I will use the withering Charm" than the character deciding to use a non-lethal technique.
            I went through the core book and looked at all the withering-only attack charms outside of the antagonists section. I divided them into categories:

            [A] The attack has a specific effect on the opponent other than initiative damage - knocking them back/down, stunning them, knocking them out, or similar. In the fiction, it’s most likely that the character is attempting to achieve this effect and not directly trying to kill. You could also narrate it as an attempted killing blow that fails and has a lesser effect, but this seems like a more niche approach.
            4 charms (Force without Fire, Ox-Stunning Blow, Knockout Blow, Shockwave Technique).


            [B] The attack has no special rules. It's probably just a normal attack that happens to never kill anyone, though you could also flavor it as a technique the character knows to get into a better position.
            9 charms (Titan-Straightening Method, Circle of Bright Reaving, Precision of the Striking Raptor, Savage Wolf Attack, Striking Fury Claws, Seven Points of Weakness Strike, Empowering Justice Redirection, Azure Abacus Meditation, Double Attack Technique, Triple Attack Technique, Dervish Blade Frenzy)
            Striking Fury Claws is pretty ridiculous as the text talks about disemboweling people but it never does decisive damage. Guess the bowels aren’t that important…
            Azure Abacus Meditation also has a clear visual effect, which it’s weird thinking about the martial artist realizing that they never kill anyone when they get that. On the other hand some of them like Precision of the Striking Raptor are clearly not a separate technique the Solar is aware of but just a general increase in skill.

            [C] The attack is a withering attack but does decisive damage. In the fiction, this is surely an attempt to kill the opponent and stop their attack or achieve another goal simultaneously:
            2 charms (Heavens Crash Down, Flashing Edge of Dawn).


            [D] I can see arguments for both sides:
            Revolving Bow Discipline - maybe it's a series of arrow shots intended to make the target fight defensively (in rules terms, crash), like modern-day suppressive fire.
            Shower of Deadly Blades - in the mechanics is just a normal withering attack, but text says “normally used to scatter groups of enemies” so you could say the character knows this technique is about calling fear and disarray rather than death.

                            I must admit that I always kind of ignore this part about withering and decisive being invisible to the characters, because to me, it makes perfect sense in the fiction.

                            As DrLoveMonkey said withering attacks are clearly the "wiff" part of the lightsaber duel. The point is, that even though all these strikes would cause harm if not parried, it is clear to the combatant that they will be parried and thus are not intended as genuine attacks. At some point you see the opening created by your attacks and you now strike with the actual hope to hit, possibly leaving an opening yourself.

                            This kind of breaks down with ranged attacks, but then I'm not entirely sure that withering attacks model fiction well in their case and I think it would be an interesting direction to treat them similarly to combat sorcery.

                                        I feel like players of Exalted will have a lot more fun if they, knowing that they will get into a big battle, think about what in the game setting might help them win the battle, and what they can do to take advantage of it. I think the Storyteller will also have fun if the players engage with the world in this way. So of course the Storyteller should do everything possible to reward the players for this. This absolutely can include just saying "You gain the benefits of ____ Stratagem" or even bigger benefits. (If the players are in a fortified town, they should at least gain the benefits of heavy cover for everyone on the walls. If the players start recruiting the town's craftspeople to build additional fortifications outside the walls, or recruiting local water and earth spirits to make a temporary moat, or whatever, they should absolutely get additional fabulous benefits on top of that.) I don't think the Storyteller should let anything in the rulebook dissuade them from doing that, and I also don't think the strategic combat system is intended to rule out this stuff.

                                        On the other hand, I don't think it would be very fun, in a game like Exalted, to reason out the moment-to-moment position of their characters and how it affects the strategy. I don't think Storytellers should reward the players for drawing a diagram of their character's movement and the enemy's movement, and explaining where they go next and how it benefits them, so I'm happy that the game abstracts that stuff away. I'm also fine with games like 3rd and 4th edition D&D that expect some tactical movement.

                                        The "splitting up battlegroups" think is tricky because its mechanical benefits are so out of proportion to its narrative meaning. I think the best reason to split up battlegroups, or merge them, is probably to equalize spotlight time and effectiveness between different players. If a sorcerer character is not very effective in a big combat (unlikely but possible I guess), why not make their summoned beasties their own battlegroup, and let them roll that battlegroup's attacks? If someone has a social character who is feeling left out in a siege, why not let them inspire a specific subgroup of allies to take a special role in combat as a split-off battlegroup. On the other hand, if the War supernal is the player who feels most responsible for the soldiers and the archers, and also the player who's going to be having the most fun in a big strategic combat, maybe find a way to make the soldiers and archers one big battlegroup.

                                                            I agree with you on the descriptive level, but I feel something is off. I'll try to make an example to clarify what I think of it.

                                                            Suppose there is a faction of people that oppose the enemy armies that are coming for you, but your players never interacted with them. Surely, these guys would participate in a fight against the invader, but you wouldn't give your PC a bonus for the strategic warfare roll, because the advantage technically exists but it doesn't relate to your story (because the PC are not interacting with them). Now suppose your PC actually interacted with them, and successfully helped them join the cause, now you would give your players the strategic warfare bonus, because it feels good for them to have the bonus associated with something they spent time and energy for. But technically, this faction is the same as before, and will fight against the opposing armies in the same way than before. You could also add them as another battle group, but I think most of the time it will 1) Make the fight much more technical because you have more units to manage, 2) Make the fight much more unfair to the ennemies, because we all know the strength of divided battle groups.

                                                            I would also like to add that, since we are playing Exalted, this situation is rather common, and usually with even more factions involved : I've seen situations where the player assemble the elementals nearby, convince the shadowy faction of the town, and successfully plead the beast-god and his beastly armies to come to help. I think creating Battle Groups and stuff for everyone of them goes contrary to the idea of "Battle groups simplifying the battle" because you have too much of them, and on top of that we add the unfairness mentioned that the power of all these usually "little" allies make when incarnated as Battle Groups.

                                                            I think there are tools to make the battle more interesting or awarding for the players (the elemental give might to the soldiers because their weapon are imbued with elemental power, the terrain might be difficult terrain because it is swarming what dangerous beasts, warfare strategy bonus on the roll, etc ...) but I think straight taking the system and creating Battle Groups is counter productive, and usually unfair to the ST side as well.
