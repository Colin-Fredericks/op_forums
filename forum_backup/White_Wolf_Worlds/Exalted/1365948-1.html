    Originally posted by Jen View Post
    Is this because battling the villain through a long combat session is just what people expect supposed to happen at the end of the story? Is it possible to get some good drama when your PCs complete crushes FaFL when he tries to solo you like an Evil Overlord?
    For me, it's a mix.

    Firstly... it's the big boss. Boss. Why would he/she turn up to the battle by his/herself? There are lieutenants, bodyguards, etc. When the PCs fought the sorcerer Moku Star-Spear, he brought 30 raiders. On the opposite end of the scale, when Ahlat ambushed the PCs, he brought 100 minor war-gods.

    Admittedly, a bit like JohnDoe, I do prefer more mental villains, who probably have a couple of burly combat dudes to fight for them. Palpatine and Darth Vader.

    Coincidentally... this makes for much better fights, because unless you build specifically for it, it's very difficult for any one creature to fight 5 Solars, even if it's one Dawn, his combat-focused Night sidekick, a crafter with a crossbow, a Zenith who's tough but a mediocre swordsman, and the Eclipse with 2 kung-fu charms. There's still 5, and one's a Dawn. And when the PCs bring their own minions (demons, elementals, random dudes they've recruited, etc), it's even worse.

    I also find it makes for better stories, because there's more characters opposing the PCs. You can also have multiple boss fights of course, as they chop through the enemy; so even if the PCs do easily kill a villain, he's got his mates. Also, different PCs can face off against people in similar areas; the PC General can face the NPC General, the socialite can intrigue against the NPC politician, etc. And then at the end they can all have a big fight, where the Dawn faces the top combat dude on the enemy side, but the other PCs can fight their own nemeses.

    Because Solars are some of the world's greatest heroes, it can feel silly when there's one enemy who can do what every member of the circle can do, so splitting it up is better.

    I used to find that PCs would just kill any villains before they could really, well, develop any character, so I thought a fair bit about how to deal with that. Having a group of foes is one way. (See below for an example from my Solar game.)

    For example, in my e4 Solar game, they battled a rival band of Celestials, the Crimson Masters.
    First, they met the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine (Twilight), the Leper Prince (Liminal), and some Tiger Warriors. The Cardinal was torturing suspected demon cultists, and the Liminal had been controlling a ghost the PCs were friends with (he claimed to stop a demon cult controlling him). The PCs killed the Leper Prince and Tiger Warriors, captured the Cardinal, then let him go after interrogating him.
    Later, the Crimson Masters sent the Godslayer (their Eclipse) to meet the PCs (at a wedding, seemed safe) to negotiate some sort of peace deal, dividing up zones of influence. But Timid Silk Blossom (Casteless PC) attacked him, so they fought him and his Tiger Warriors, he fled, and the Full Moon hunted him down and killed him.
    So then the Crimson Masters turned their army around and marched it back towards the PCs.
    The PC Zenith went to negotiate with the Prophet of the Crimson Word (Zenith) and failed, the PC Full Moon attacked her army but had to flee their Tiger Warrior archers, and then the PC Solar sorcerer nuked their army with Death Ray, and they scattered.
    However, the other half of their army, led by their Night Caste leader Pride Khan and the Leper Prince, had gone through the Underworld to attack the city the PCs had seized to use as a buffer state. Their sorcerer, the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine, used Heart of Darkness to cover the city in arcane gloom. He and his Lunar friend the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine ambushed the PC Dawn and Twilight, but failed to kill them and had to flee.
    Meanwhile, the Solar sorcerer (having sped back), attacked and Death Ray'd Pride Khan and an unknown Lunar to dust, along with some of their soldiers, but the Leper Prince escaped into the Underworld with half the army.

    The Crimson Prophet was able to reform some of her army, and took them north to attack the PCs' closer buffer states (the PCs had a lot of buffer states). But the Dawn met her 1000 Tiger Warriors with his 70 horsemen and his ally, a mortal fish-man king with a few thousand peasant archers/spearmen.
    The Dawn smashed the Prophet's forces, and killed her in single combat.

    Meanwhile, the Leper Prince and Cardinal took their survivors through the Underworld, where they fought the local Viceroy of the First and Forsaken Lion, an Abyssal who's the father of one of the PCs. He killed them and smashed their army.

    So then the PCs went home to relax, but while relaxing in their ziggurat, the Cardinal and Leper Prince flew a giant owl onto the top of the ziggurat, and the Leper Prince threw them all into the Underworld, where they were attacked by Pride Khan, the Prophet and the Godslayer, and a bunch of Tiger Warrior. Obviously the Solars beat them because ghosts are pretty easy for Solars to beat.

    Anyway, later on the PC Zenith went to negotiate with their enemy the Leopard Empress, daughter of Ahlat and ruler of Harborhead, Varang and some other places (her sorcerer had kidnapped their friend ages before, so they killed him, so she didn't like them. Fairly straightforward). Turned out she had the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine, the Leper Prince, and the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine living with her as they'd taken refuge with the enemy of their enemy. She arrested the PC, and was going to execute him.

    The other PCs (except the Dawn who was lured away by Sidereal machinations) turned up to save Light. They had to battle the Leopard Empress, the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine, the Leper Prince, and the Cardinal of the Cathedral Encarmine. So there were 2 or 3 Solar PCs, 2 Lunar PCs, and their Second Circle Demon buddy (Mara). If they'd just been fighting the Leopard Empress, they'd have killed her anti-climactically. But because I'd combined her with another set of bad guys they'd half-beaten, they faced an e4 God-Blood, a Solar, a Lunar, a Liminal, some Brides of Ahlat, and a bunch of regular troops. The PCs still won (as I expected and wanted them to), but one lost an arm. I feel that's about right for the final big battle of a story arc.



    Anyway, in the campaign I'm running, the Big Bad is either the Elemental Dragon Mela, or the Bull of the North (who may be an ancient body-stealing parasite recently escaped from Hell). Obviously they both claim the other one is the Big Bad.

    But the PCs didn't just go straight to Mela. They battled Dragonblood multiple times, which built up to them reaching Mela's castle, where they beat some Dragonblood protecting it (who'd been injured by demons). Now they've reached her throne room. On the one side, is Yurgen Kaneko (not his real name, which is Teshub, just the one he's currently using), who has five other... Solars? Infernals? Solar Akuma? with him. On the other side is Mela, whose throne is surrounded by ashigaru, led by the Shogun (an Air Aspect) and some Dragonblood lieutenants.

    So the 6 PCs could battle a Dawn (or Slayer? Or demon possessing a human body?) and some other much-less combat-orientated Solars. That means the combat-optimised Full Moon can battle a Dawn, while the others fight a Zenith (Or Malefactor?) sorcerer, Twilight (or whatever) crafter, etc. (Though I suspect the arrogant but not-combat-orientated Aurochs-totem Full Moon will want to fight the Bull of the North to prove he's the best bull.)

    Alternatively, they can fight 3 or 4 Dragonblood and a battle group, and a massive essence 10 Dragon, which is a harder fight, but they'll have some Solars to help them. So they can probably choose the people they want to fight depending on their personality/bravery, while NPCs can fight the other NPCs.

    (Or, if they're really cunning, maybe they'll wait for the NPCs to fight each other, then mop up the survivors. Not sure. They're going to try negotiating first; very difficult, but I want the Changing Moon to have the opportunity, you never know.)

        As opposed to many posters on this forum, I strongly prefer single nemesis-type boss, so I would like to chime in. (Let me start with reiterating: mechanically, I think it's not as hard to have a singular powerful boss, as many people claim: just make them mechanically equivalent to many combatants, but narrate as a single entity. Carthage delenda est.)

        Now, I'd like to address two points The Wizard of Oz made. (I hide it that behind spoilers for better parsing.)

        1.
        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
        I also find it makes for better stories, because there's more characters opposing the PCs. You can also have multiple boss fights of course, as they chop through the enemy; so even if the PCs do easily kill a villain, he's got his mates. Also, different PCs can face off against people in similar areas; the PC General can face the NPC General, the socialite can intrigue against the NPC politician, etc. And then at the end they can all have a big fight, where the Dawn faces the top combat dude on the enemy side, but the other PCs can fight their own nemeses.

        It of course makes little sense in general to ask what is better: multiple enemies or a single nemesis. However, I personally think that there is certain specific and highy desirable balance, when there are two kinds of opposition:
        - rivals;
        - nemeses.

        Rivals are plural. They have rather intricate web of interactions. Some of them may be scary, some of them may be goofy. They may oppose one another. Some of them can have personal history with the characters. You can make allies of some of them. They don't have to be personally powerful, I think it's a great role to be played by a cunning mortal.

        Nemeses on the other hand are the focal point of the campaign or at least the story arc. In some kinds of anime-like stories which seem appropriate for the Exalted, Nemesis should be also personally competent enough as combatants to oppose the whole circle of protagonists. The point is: the nemesis is a focus. It's not a subplot to be ignored. It's all about them. They have they own music theme (I actually do that in my sessions, I play a specific piece whenever the Nemesis is mentioned). Rivals are scared of them.

        Of course, your Nemesis can bring back some allies. Making the final fight the final battle definitely has some specific feel to it. However, it's not neutral for the tone. Final battle is reminiscent more of bronze-age myths or of Tolkien. I think that more anime-like stories are better served, when the final fight takes place in isolation: protagonists vs the Nemesis. They could bring their mooks and lieutenants, but then that what the allies of protagonists are fighting outside the palace, when the real fight takes place already in the throne chamber.



        2.
        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
        Because Solars are some of the world's greatest heroes, it can feel silly when there's one enemy who can do what every member of the circle can do, so splitting it up is better.

        That's an interesting point that keeps being raised on this forum. I think that the key point here is maintaining certain illusion. In many sources, the protagonists are presented as over-the-top and Exalted explicitly introduces Solars as that. But I think it's not good to actually treat that statement too seriously.

        On many occasions, if you present the protagonists as the absolute peak of human skill or better, you try to illustrate that by showing how effortlessly that win with the regular opponents (and regular may be clearly competent within-the-setting). However, when it comes to your nemesis, it turns out they desperately surpass you. The key point is to build up to this confrontation. You need to constantly remind the audience that yes, your protagonists are still as over-the-top as they were last week, and no one could see this coming. I think that the real trap here is when Nemeses are too numerous or the trivial opposition too rare.

        Now, I think that the main trap in portraying this in rpg is as follows. In the narrative you want to keep the illusion that the protagonist is absolutely at the top of the food chain except for that unique foe. However, when righting a core book to an rpg you are forced to be more systematic. So you don't want to portray too many opponents as more powerful in order to avoid the impression that your protagonists are not so strong after all. For instance, you won;t portray Elder Sidereals as powerful enough to oppose a whole circle of Solars, because that would make Solars feel unimportant.

        I think that it is a trap and it should be resolved on the Storyteller's level: you should just pick your opposition very carefully so that the illusion is maintained that it's just that one foe that's out of the reach. Even if your players know well that this particular elder Sidereal is not more powerful then the rest of them, you should still try to make it look as if they were.

        By the way, someone could advise that stories with a singular Nemesis are better handled when playing Dragon-Blooded. Well, the point is: with Dragon-Blooded you wouldn't have this delight of playing the best warrior in the world. It is an important narrative feature. I just don't think it should be treated one hundred percent seriously when we actually start statting things out.

                Originally posted by Jen View Post
                Is this because battling the villain through a long combat session is just what people expect supposed to happen at the end of the story? Is it possible to get some good drama when your PCs complete crushes FaFL when he tries to solo you like an Evil Overlord?
                No.

                A curbstomp is pretty much never interesting to play through. If the expectation is that the enemy will get mowed down with little consequence, it's not worth even bothering to reach for the dice-- just throw out some narrative descriptions to set the scene and let the most thematically appropriate player describe their deathblow. Even the "defeated villain at bay, almost certainly doomed but with nothing to lose" scenario only works if they can actually threaten the players. Otherwise you get a sense of "wait, that's who we were scared of this whole time?"

                As you note in the original post, the action economy is a huge issue in any context. It's worse in tabletop games where everyone (rightly) expects to share the spotlight and at least contribute to major scenes. And it's worse yet in Exalted 3e, because the way the system links "health" and "offensive power"-- you can't have every attack against them miss, because that's no fun, but if they're getting hit with any sort of regularity, they're never going to build up enough Initiative to be a threat. Or worse, they'll build up too much initiative and one-shot a character-- also no fun. Add in onslaught penalties and the attrition element (run out of motes and you got splat pretty quickly) and pretty much the whole system is working against the outnumbered character. If you want to overcome that, you've got to bend things pretty damn hard. The thread JohnDoe244 linked earlier was my best attempt at dealing with that.

                    So, I don't believe in the one size fits all solution to boss fights and don't consider 5 on 1 leading to a relatively quick defeat inherently a problem.

                    To me it's an issue if it plays out exactly the same way. A couple of examples from my game to illustrate my point.

                    There was a nine on one fight (some NPCs were helping) I was pretty happy with because the opponent was a big monster with acid blood, due to everyone having to resist an environmental hazard it managed to wound two of the PCs via acid shower before it was overwhelmed and slain. The size of the group worked in my favour here because with 9 characters rolling at difficulty 5 the odds of someone rolling horribly were pretty good and Legendary Size proved to be a good obstacle to the fight ending too quick even against a Dawn, a Dusk, a Full Moon, a Zenith, a Night, a Twilight, a Fire Aspect a Water Aspect and (for ease of communication) an Exigent. The main misgiving I had was that once the Night Caste made her attack she was pretty much locked out of participating due to the creature's high soak and her only having a knife.

                    There was later a seven on one fight against a mad axe murderer Dawn Caste. He wounded the PC Dawn with his opening move and held out for four rounds due to hail shattering practice and a lucky win in a clash preventing the PCs from building much in the way of initiative also, notably he did 5 dice of lethal on his withering attacks so his own initiative wasn't relevant to the threat he posed. His method of denying initiative was shortcircuted by the Night who'd learnt from the previous encounter and used Stalking Wolf Attitude to build up initiative by evading detection instead of attacking, something she was better at and he was worse at. Appropriately, she was the one who turned the tide in round 3 when she stabbed him and took him down to his -4s.

                            Essentially, you are saying here two things. First, as of now, it's very hard to find a fair match for 5 Solars playing by the book. With that, I of course absolutely agree. That's why I generally propose houserules/broken charms which would enable Storyteller to keep up in this arms' race (provided that everyone is ok with the changes, but the groups I play with generally trust me).

                            Now, the second, more interesting part is that groups of Solars should be overwhelming. It's a widespread opinion with which I don't agree. If we really wanted Solars to be unbeatable, we could imagine a diceless game, say, like Chuubo's Wish-Granting Machine, in which the Solar warrior would simply have: I win button. I actually think that this could be a very interesting game, but by presenting this extreme,

                            However, it's rather common in some media in the broad inspiration circle of Exalted that protagonists are portrayed as extremely skilled or gifted. Really the best of the best. However, this doesn't prevent their Nemeses to be still better. Now, the subtle art, is to make the audience sure that this situation is completely unique and that generally the protagonists are as awesome as initially suggested.

                            Of course, if every second foe turns out to be a fair match to our protagonists, then the situation is getting out of control and the inflation of power becomes ridiculous. This is exaclty the same problem in rpg as in every other media. So it's a Storyteller job to make sure that protagonists are awesome.

                            In other words, I believe that the claim that as a matter of fact in the setting the protagonists absolutely dominate essentially every opponent, shouldn't lead us to think that in the story they will be able to beat every actual opponent.

                            For example, Geralt in the Witcher books and Benedict in the Amber saga, are both constantly and consistently portrayed as inhumanly skilled warriors who completely surpass everyone around. The authors of the books make great effort to remind us that they are exactly that good. However, like one-third of the time, when we see them fighting on-screen, they loose. Of course, it's important for the author to underline that this doesn't make them any less awesome.

                            So, in a nutshell, I just think that genre-emulation (or, emulation of some genres mixed in the Exalted), require that the best-of-the-best sometimes be matched with a powerful singular Nemesis... and then be reminded that they are still essentially the best. Note that this could not simply done by playing a Dragon-Blooded campaign, since those Dragon-Blooded are not narrated to be the best. They are by design outmatched by several different human-like kinds of beings, it's the narrative premise. So them meeting an overwhelming opposition would not be a story about the best being defeated unless they raise and fight again, but about hitting above your weightclass. An intereseting story, but still a different one.
