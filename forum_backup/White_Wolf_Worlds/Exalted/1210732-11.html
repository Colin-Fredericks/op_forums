                                            05-08-2018, 03:18 AM
                                            Lets talk character gen. DBs get more specialties, way more merit dots, and cool aspect abilities. Unfortunately they give up free excellencies and a supernal ability for it. Well, kind of, their excellencies are sort of free considering they're kind of like 2 charms in one.

                                            Aspect

                                            This is the most defining aspect of your character. It give you your aspect powers, but it more importantly prevents you from entering any other element's aura when your anima is at bonfire. Also at essence 5 it prevents you from buying a second signature charm other than your element, unless you already did that at essence 3. The aura thing is the biggest deal here. We'll get into this later.

                                            The other thing is that the effects of your anima are supposed to have a subtle influence on your personality. It's not like you get totally hijacked by an elemental stereotype, but it's just a little push in a certain direction that you might want to include in your backstory. This is actually mechanical, believe it or not, as doing so will make it easier for you to get your Dragon XP, which is helpful. If you don't want to make it a part of your character's everyday actions you might even want to have it where the emotions latent in her blood boil up every so often. Like your normally reserved and cautious fire aspect just flares up and does something impulsive every so often, something they strive to control.

                                            I think most of the aspect powers are pretty good and straight forward, there's really not much to say there. In context to different abilities and play styles they can really come into their own.

                                            Attributes

                                            So starting things off with attributes. Pretty much nothing changed here from the Solar set.

                                            lol, just kidding. As a Solar you need to put a lot more stock in your attributes versus when you're thinking about it for DBs. Solars base their dice caps off of their attributes, so if you want to be an amazing Solar brawler, but also want to be big and burly, taking 3 dex instead of 5 robs you of 4 dice when you go all out. God help you if you decide to have dex 2 and lose out on 6 dice overall.

                                            If you're doing up a DB though the choice has less of a deep impact. If you really feel like you want a fighter who's got 5 strength but need to sacrifice down to 3 dex for it, then don't sweat it. Your excellency combined with a 5 in your ability will keep you afloat. Especially since as a DB you'll be able to lean on the teamwork of your hearth more.

                                            Abilities/charms

                                            So abilities are something you really have to look at because there's some major interactions with your aspect. This is debatably a charms thing but you can start thinking about it here. Certain abilities just naturally fit to aspects, the ones that are set to them for your aspect abilities, but there's also some that kind of flow well even without being that aspect technically. For instance there's a little bit of a bias towards air in the archery tree, and fire for the firewand section, but no earth offshoot. That means there's really no charm benefit to being an earth aspected archer, it will just lock you out of every one of your aura abilities when you hit bonfire.

                                            You are going to have to play a bit of a game here. For your primary abilities, combat/social/other, picking something that's in line with your aspect can help a lot, but there are things you can do to mitigate the problem if you choose to make that earth aspected archer.

                                            That's because there's 3 sort of hidden tiers in the charm trees. Balanced, Aspected and Aura. Balanced charms being charms that don't affect your aura at all, except to help you get into it if you want. Aspect charms being charms that will knock you out of another aura, but otherwise don't require it. Aura charms are charms that require a specific aura up that your bonfire or Aspect charms might knock you out of.

                                            Your real bread and butter charms, your excellencies, your basic damage adders and stuff, those tend to be balanced. So if you're an earth aspect trying to keep air aura for the flashy air archery charms, you can go wild with full excellencies and stuff and keep up that aura. Your anima will be a swirling sandstorm for the whole combat. Then if you absolutely must go bonfire, don't worry, because that only locks you out of your Aura charms, not your Aspect charms. It sucks, I know, but you've got 2/3 of your charmset to work off of, be more careful next time.

                                            All that being said, if you actually go for a matched aspect, fire aspect swordsman, wood aspected archer, ect. it can be really freeing. Just go anima banner as fast as you can and really lean into the aura effects with full power.

                                            On the other hand an earth aspected archer gets bonus soak, hardness, and knocks people back a range band with her anima flux. So yeah that's pretty good too.


                                            Also one last thing on specialties, these are more important to DBs, they add to your dicecap. There's not too much other than that for them to interact with though, so don't worry that you'll need an applicable specialty to use a charm. In fact there's a lot of charms that give you a temporary specialty.

                                            Merits

                                            Merits for DBs are crazy. Getting up to 18 without even spending bonus points, this can be the real power of the Dragonblooded splat, setting power. It's no sweat at all to grab everything you need to be an established merchant lord with contacts, influence, backing, and heirloom artifact and resources. Then if you decide to spend bonus points on even more merits? Who even NEEDS charms, you're a seriously major player in your chosen field. I'm not even kidding a little bit there, you could basically get yourself into such a position of power that you wouldn't need to burn a drop of essence to contribute to almost any story.

                                            Of course you take that and, well, remember all those charms we talked about in the ability section where there's a presumption of authority and wealth? Now you've got that cash and clout in spades, and charms to help you leverage it further. You also have every justification you need to take any of these merits, your cultural heritage gives you your complete pick of whatever you want in whatever amount you wish to have.

                                            I can attest personally to this being a really fun character to play, and it stacks up with multiple people too. For example where a Solar circle might opt to calm the folks of a town facing annihilation by Raksha horde by letting their Zenith give a big public speech to calm, comfort and distract them, and then letting the Dawn and Twilight out to go fight it alone so nobody sees their animas flaring. The DB merit masters handle it a different way.

                                            First you deploy a navy battle group to the region from your home port on the south coast of the Blessed Isle. Two large troop transports carrying over 200 soldiers each, and an escort of a further two warships with 50 marines each on board. You show up and declare that Venala of the Righteous Steel is here to protect them, your famous name giving hope to the people that they may yet live. They look upon your army with a mixture of fear and relief, they know many people have been brought to heel by the soldiers of the Realm, but today they are the shield against greater horrors.

                                            Then you greet the Satrap like an old friend, because you are, and smoothly negotiate the temporary command of her forces under your leadership for a unified defense. The local mercenaries who were getting skittish about their odds have their nerves calmed with a good talking to and a talent of silver. Then finally, when the time comes for the defense, you charge into battle, surrounded by your elite bodyguards, bearing the Hearthstone of Courage, your great aunt's manse-stone that burns away fear in men's hearts, set into Summer's Pyre, the ancient storied daiklave that was used by Cathak Cainan himself to turn back far greater fairies than the ones you face today. Only now do you take more than a passing glance at your charm sheet.*

                                            Now I know Solars can do that too, technically, they just need to pump a lot more bonus points into it, almost all of them maybe. What they can't do though, or at least it's basically impossible to do, is have that kind of backing attached to a place like The Realm. You'd have to be a secret Solar, or have all of your own personal military and connections that aren't through the empire, and that will get noticed. When a DB gets noticed like that, it makes his status go way up in the Dynasty, when a Solar gets noticed like that, the Wyld Hunt perks their ears up as a possible indication of anathema. So you see even if they can do it it's harder and makes for a much different story where you want to keep your head down anyway.


                                            And that's where I'll leave it today, talking about charms is going to be a really big task since the essence thing changes the whole game and there's even more to say on elemental affinity and aura, and I want these to be in at least semi-readable chunks.


                                            *This is actually kind of a microcosm of how DBs and Solars can play differently as well. It's something I'll do a bit on after chargen and probably the last part of this thread. Solars can tend to power through things with their awesome might and heroic nature, whereas DBs tend to be encouraged, often to the point of necessity, to engage with the world on a deeper level to get traction going.

                                                    On to charms, and how you might pick them.

                                                    MadLetter's Awesome Dragon Blooded Charm Trees will be very helpful in this section

                                                    Essence

                                                    The biggest two things that are going to change if you've been making Solars is that you start at essence 2, and you don't have a supernal. When you're making a Solar, your character has the incredible power to ignore essence requirements for one ability. This is great, for Solars anyway, because it lets you expand your power to ridiculous heights and really grab hold of that Solar Supremacy. An Essence 1 athletics supernal for example could just snag every FoS charm and punch a fortress wall down in a single blow.

                                                    Dragonblooded don't have that, but the thing is a Solar is also restricted to essence 1 charms everywhere else. We already talked about how that makes a huge difference with evocations, but how about martial arts too, eh? Yeah! No non-MA-supernal Solar is going to get more than essence 1 charms in their MA, and for a lot of styles, all the Immaculate ones included, that means no form charm until they hit essence 2. That's not the only thing though, I mean just for Wood Dragon (the first style I checked) out of the first 6 charms 2 are essence 1 and the rest are essence 4.

                                                    Lets look at how that works out for Solars. You can't even start purchasing essence 2 charms until you're essence 2, which means that you need to have already spent 50xp, which is like 7 charm purchaces and 56xp if all you're doing is buying favoured charms, or 6 and 50xp if you buy one out of favour charm. So if you're being xp efficient and spending only favoured charms, and getting 5xp per session, you're going through 13 sessions before you get your very first essence 2 charm. And to get all 4 from wood dragon? You're 18 sessions, or 90xp into the game before you reach where the Wood Aspect was at from session 1.

                                                    So it works better for martial arts, but that also applies to the basic charms as well, and in this case the effect is twofold. Because you don't have a Supernal, there isn't anything sucking away your charm purchases. When you have the option to take an essence 1 resistance charm, or an essence 5 lore charm that lets you destroy a city, it can be pretty tempting just to ignore that resistance charm.

                                                    It frees you up to just go wide, you have complete access in equal part to every tree, but it also frees you up to go somewhat deep into specific areas of a tree. So lets say you're really into melee, but you don't care about the defense/counter attack portion of the tree. At essence 1 you'd be restricted to just two charms...bleh, not fun. At essence 2 you can grab 6, which is more than half of them.

                                                    There's also something else too, since your next essence up is at 150xp, you're not going to have to worry about much past that for quite a while. In fact 32 sessions before essence up, and by then you're going to have an extra 19 charms past your original 15+. So since you're looking to snag 35 non-MA non-spell charms before hitting essence 3 you can seriously broaden your character. Think about picking up a martial art, or sorcery, or even just grab charms from melee, archery, presence, resistance, ride, occult and bureaucracy at the same time. With all the dragon xp that you'll get, upping those stats if they're low shouldn't even be hard.

                                                    You also don't get free excellencies, so picking up 1-2 charms in an ability can get you those dice adders that will be so appreciated. You might even want to go really broad right from the start, pick 5 abilities and get 3 charms in each of them, give or take one or two. That way you'll have lots of room to grow in what you want to specialize in, and get a bunch of dice adders and useful charms. Also lots of ways to enter aura, speaking of...


                                                    Aspect

                                                    Okay so we've seen how your essence lets you be both less specialized but more generally competent* than essence 1 with a supernal. Now lets talk more about aspect and how that affects your character for charm purchases, because you're going to want to look a bit ahead here. Don't worry though if you didn't think things through you're usually only one or two charm purchaces away from fixing it.

                                                    I already talked about there are three somewhat kinda hidden tiers of charms, Balanced, Aspect and Aura. Balanced not affecting aura negatively, Aspect knocking you out of other auras, and aura requiring you to be in aura.

                                                    There's also a kind of a bonus side-tier which is charms that have extra affects from shedding or being in aura. For instance Presence's Eternally Argumentative Flame is a balanced fire charm that reduces the penalties for flurrying a social attack, but in fire aura negates them entirely. If you want to use this charm to full effect, you might think twice about taking Grinding Millstone Argument. You also might want to take Sweeten the Tap from socialize, which is a fire/wood charm that you use at the start of the scene, which will put you into fire aura already before you start talking.

                                                    There's lots of little interactions like this all over the charmset, but what it boils down to is this. When you're in a scene, lets say a combat one but they can be social or anything really, do you want to be in a particular aura? For example in Earth aura to use Rolling Boulder Blow. If you're an earth aspected brawler you might want to take only that, Stone Fist Strike, Crushing Avalanche Grasp, and the four balanced Brawl charms. That's a nice 7 charms there for your combat ability.

                                                    However if you're a fire aspect, still do it, but be careful. When your anima flares up you lose out on Rolling Boulder Blow and some bonus effects from Stone Fist Strike. For times like these I might suggest that you even pick up some water aspected charms, just no aura ones. That way when your anima flares up you have a secondary combat suite to fall back on where you don't take advantage of aura, but now you can feel free to use all those water charm combos so it's not really a bad thing it just changes how you fight. This would be much more helpful if you were a water aspect and could then just switch to using water aura charms, but that's why aspect still matters.

                                                    There's something else here too, and I mentioned it above, and that's getting yourself into aura when you need it. Since you only come into aura at the end of your turn, if you can somehow use a properly aspected charm before the combat starts, that can help.Like if you use an awareness charm to sense an ambush, or a fire charm in the social scene that turns into the combat. That earth aspected brawler might want to take some of these to help out.

                                                    So my advice here is that if you're kind of new to this exalted thing, or even just DBs, maybe stick with the element that your primary abilities seem to want to be in, or just take up to the balanced charms in the tree. Or at least try to find your aspect's charms in the tree you're in so you don't accidentally disable your powers.

                                                    And above all let none of this stop you from making a cool character. If you want to play a fire aspect brawler, or an earth aspect archer, or an water aspect occultist, in spite of there being no charms, apart from signatures, in those trees that match your aspect, go for it.



                                                    I think that's all there is really to know about picking charms, except what I mentioned previously in the abilities as to what to expect from each tree. Next time I post might be the last for this series as I finish up chargen and talk a bit about how the DB play experience is different from the Solar one, from both a ST and player perspective.


                                                    *Note here that I mean specialized/competent versus a DB setup that's more like the Solar one, Solar charms are powerful enough to make this statement debatable when comparing direct DB to Solar.
