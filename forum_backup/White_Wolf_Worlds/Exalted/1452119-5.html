                            So far one of the charms i most disagree with is Technique-From-Taste Approach. It sets a very bad precedent as a charm design.

                            "When using a mundane item or construct from this discipline, ask the Storyteller any of the following, spending one mote per question:
                            - How can this be repaired?
                            - How can this be improved?
                            - How can this be replicated?"

                            Asking questions to ST is the fundamental way we play the game. It should never be a charm. What happens when a dragon blooded, heck forget about DB, what happens when a mortal blacksmith character tries to ask these questions. Either ST will answer them strait up (that is who you play the game afterall) which will make the player that purchased this charm feels like he wasted this xp, or worse after reading this charm ST might answer to the player as "You have to purchase a charm to ask that question" or "You dont have anymore motes to ask questions".

                            Anohter stupid point, as ST you can still answer these questions as " you dont know, you dont understand the intricacies...etc", nowhere in the charm it states that the ST has to answer.

                            I understand the design intent of the charm but the wording is really bad. It should be " ...by spending one mote, you discover the best way to repair/improve/replicate the item/construct."

                                    Originally posted by Ncolic View Post
                                    So far one of the charms i most disagree with is Technique-From-Taste Approach. It sets a very bad precedent as a charm design.

                                    Asking questions to ST is the fundamental way we play the game. It should never be a charm.
                                    This is going to sound a little odd coming from me, but I think this may be a case where you should accept that you're not on the same page as the writers.

                                    EE is packed full of "ask the ST a question" abilities. Usually (like in the Sidereal "Auspicious [Caste] Insights" abilities), the text specifies that the answers must be useful, or "always reveal at least one clue," or similar wordings. But sometimes they don't, and even I have a hard time believing that any ST who wasn't going to screw you over anyway would blank all of those character options through asinine answers. Unwoven Coadjutor, Wickedness-Unveiling Method, Celestial Overseer Concordat, Of Truths Best Unspoken, Meditation on Rivalry, General of the All-Seeing Sun, and Strawmaiden's Calling all technically just allow a player to ask a question (sometimes, they don't even specify the ST - Imagine a game making you use a Charm just to ask another player their thoughts!), without ensuring any kind of answer. Heck, the "detective" Universal Charms ensure an honest answer, but don't specify "useful" as some other Charms do, so the ST can still follow your lead and say "you don't know" every time.

                                    Seeing it all laid out like this, there are even more ST question Charms than I had thought, so I wonder if cutting some of the duplicate wordings about honesty/usefulness would free up the wordcount to put a sidebar very early in the Universal Charms and call out the overall expectations here. It would also be really nice to have a few sentences in the ST section about making sure those powers are useful (even when the player doesn't ask the big, obvious question you planned for and focuses on something else), while also keeping some sense of mystery - but I know darn well they didn't have space for that.

                                                Originally posted by Ulthwithian View Post
                                                As a ST, this can help me because I will know if/whether the characters have these Charms (or are likely to gain them), and I can prepare answers for game-relevant uses... As a player, were I in a game where a ST stonewalled the use of these Charms, I would rapidly search for another game to join.
                                                In fairness to all the STs out there, I think there will be a reasonably tough period of adjusting to these effects, especially for someone coming from a background of lower-powered RPGs which don't expect the players to be able to simply bypass a mystery. For one thing, even if you do review all of the players' Charms and such before the first session (which is obviously desirable, but not always possible), it's easy to forget or overlook one or two. For another, answers which are helpful, but not total gimmies can be hard to improvise.

                                                To pick an example from somebody else's actual campaign, so that I'm not just deliberately imagining trouble*, in the Slack-based campaign of Vance's which people could read the logs of, the very first session had the PCs run into a very over-the-top sea captain. He was definitely something unusual, and the PCs were somewhat suspicious about his general deal. In actual play, they didn't have any good leads (and needed to scheme against the Satrap), so they parted ways and he didn't show up again for many sessions, at which point he turned out to be an exiled Raksha noble with a crew of loyal mortals which he (allegedly) only ever Banquet of Crumbs'd, seeking allies to overthrow his own usurper. (Technically, in the game itself, they never verified any of that, so maybe he was lying, but let's work with it.)

                                                But what if there had been a Chosen of Secrets in the mix! For the low cost of literally nothing, they would probably have turned to the ST and asked "what's the biggest crime this guy has committed recently?" In that position, I would probably struggle: even if feeding on willing humans is illegal (and they were in Realm territory, so it probably was), he does that out at sea on nobody's territory, making the matter hazy. I could improvise some normal Roguish Captain sort of thing (smuggling ten talents worth of opium away from House Cynis, say), but that feels like a total cheat when there is supernatural Shady Stuff going on. And I don't want to spend too long thinking about this, because that would both tip my hand that there's some big reveal here, and slow down play.

                                                Having typed all of that out, I think the best answer would be to list a not-soul-eating crime... but one which obviously requires supernatural means: "he stole a ten-ton stone idol which the Immaculate Order had slated for destruction, and it's in his ship's hold right now!" I took a minute or two to get there, which could be rough at the table, but suppose I took your advice and came up with it in advance. At that point, I'm groovy... unless the player instead asked "what's this guy's field of expertise," and I don't want to be as bland as "leadership," but also don't want to just blurt out "he's an expert at BEING A SOUL-EATING TRICKSTER FAIRY, ALRIGHT?!"

                                                It's worth repeating that for Sidereals, the first question is free. The game is functionally encouraging them to ping every named NPC in this way. That's a lot of preparation, even without getting into the weeds of whether you can ever "meet" the same person twice, and if so, how long you must be away.

                                                To be clear, I really like the "ask the ST" effects, and think they're good designs, especially for compacting down the wide range of information-gathering powers Exalts can have into a reasonable amount of space. But this whole rant is my long-winded way of saying: as a player, if you have a few questions whiff, try to be understanding. Maybe talk to your ST between sessions about how your expectations for those effects aren't matching up with theirs. Maybe they'll try to give answers more like you're looking for, or maybe they'll awkwardly tell you that they don't know if they can juggle that mental load along with running the game and ask you to make some other build choice, but either one is probably worth a try before you start seeking a new group.



                                                * Deliberately Imagining Trouble really feels like it should be a Charm name.
