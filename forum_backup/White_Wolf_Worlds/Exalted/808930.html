    Improving the Standard of Living without recreating the Industrial Revolution

    01-14-2016, 03:17 PM
    Hey, I have an idea of a Twilight Crafter that wants to improve the living conditions of Creation for mortals. Any ideas how I can do that without recreating the Industrial Revolution or the Ex2 First Age?

    Here are some areas of improvement areas I thought of:

    Clean Water
    Abundant Food
    Climate Control
    Health Care
    Safe Lighting
    Faster Travel
    Better Communication
    More Accessible Education
    Better Safety/Law Enforcement

    One idea I had was focusing on the community instead of the individual. For example, instead of everyone having a phone, you go to the phone office or something like that.

                  The key to economic growth and diversification is a surplus of food. If you can use a relatively small number of people to grow enough food for a relatively large number of people, that makes enough idle hands that skill specialization is the logical choice.

                  To that end, I would focus on projects that promote farm productivity and ease trade, such as dams, irrigation ditches/canals, roads, etc. Once you have a critical mass of "idle" labor in cities or larger townships, you have a solid foundation.

                  The next step is to develop your trades. You create policies that reward trade guilds and professionals. Make a point of recruiting excellent smiths, architects, doctors, etc, and give them land/titles, and incentivize them to teach, rather than produce. Being a Solar, you can afford to wait a few decades for this to pay off.

                  Once you have a really solid agricultural and artisan economy, then you leverage that wealth to bankroll an education system. I would start with a focused public education that does just the basics (math, writing), because that will feed back into your artisan class. Then create a "higher" education system, and recruit the best minds to do it (or train them yourself; you're a Twilight.).Make that also public and free, but competitive. This gets you the best minds from your citizens as your "white collar" bureaucrats, project managers, etc.

                  The best way to avoid the "Industrial" effect is to just not head in the direction of mass-production, and agree with your ST that's not what you want for the game. Your nation's culture emphasizes art, reliability, and craftsmanship over low prices and high volume.

                  Once you're at stage three, so to speak, you have a model that can easily be replicated elsewhere. Just transplant some of your young journeyman and "college" graduates to new territory, and repeat.

                          Clean water is pretty easy actually. If the water is dirty, summon up some water cleaner elementals to clean the water for you. In Nexus's case you may want several dozen. If the God of Pollution or small gods of dirty water show up, kill them.

                          Abundant Food = Survival charms. I remember reading homebrew charms a while back of Survival charms that greatly increased yield of crops. Survival needs a bone other than animal and environmental protection, so this seems fine.

                          Climate control = Workings or very lavish bribes to weather gods. If they start to get uppity, have a backup plan to make their lives hell.

                          Safe Lighting is from the glowstones of Gem.

                          Faster Travel is something that is problematic. Charms can help solve this, as does being a Sorcerer. I can't think of any other way.

                          Better communication is iffy as hell. There is already "God Prayer relay" system mentioned in 1e Cult of the Illuminated, which I actually enjoyed. Sorcerous magical mirrors or that one skull talking necromancy spell helps.

                          More Accessible Education can be done when basic need of survival are largely met. Even in the Nexus Philosopical schools one can audit courses by just attending them and listening. You won't be able to ask questions, but you can listen.

                                      This is a little bit of a side-topic, but I actually have a pet theory that a big part of the once-every-two-years Guild Councils in Nexus revolve around negotiating large scale standing contracts with directional travel gods guaranteeing safe, timely passage of Guild caravans along major trade routes. The Guild then licenses regional trade consortiums at a hefty fee, allowing them to benefit from those pacts and giving them an enormous advantage over unlicensed traders, who don't get the protections and assistance of the signatory spirit courts. The regional trade groups abide by set business practices dictated by the Council but are otherwise self-governing. The Guild itself acts sort of like a multinational corporate conglomerate with enormous resources and leverage because of its vast reach, but when you get right down to it the guys at the top don't actually run most stuff.                                                           Originally posted by Totentanz
                                                          If you can use a relatively small number of people to grow enough food for a relatively large number of people, that makes enough idle hands that skill specialization is the logical choice.
                                                          You can also get societies in which the relative ease of producing food and an abundance of available land means that everybody lives as a farmer, except with a really short working day, so there's a lot of leisure time. Even better is if you can add on some valuable livestock option on top of that, preferably one that breeds abundantly and requires very little upkeep. That's kind of what happened in England after the Black Death; reduced numbers of people meant large tracts of land and an emphasis on sheep farming (since it's really easy to raise sheep), and because of the high value of woll and good wages on account of a competitive labour pool, you had a lot of pretty affluent lower class people who didn't need to work very often. Reportedly, up to half the year could be spent in numerous festivities, often of that kind aimed at mocking the upper classes. Threatened aristocrats had to start bringing in sumptuary laws, because how are you supposed to tell class distinctions when everybody can afford at least one set of high-quality clothes?
