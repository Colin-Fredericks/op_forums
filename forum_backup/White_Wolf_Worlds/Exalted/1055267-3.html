        It was more getting away from the idea of Exaltations being these sort of separate "things" exist on their own. When unbound, they go and seek out and worthy host that fits it's criteria and then bonds with them (hence the Green Lantern Ring comparison). The Jade Prison simply held all of these "things" in a very literal sense. That was the presentation in 2e. Whatever the construct that holds back the power, my idea was to get away from Exaltations being distinct "things" and go towards it being a direct blessing from the Sun. His power represented in Creation. That's all.

                                            I think it may be that Michael Goodwin did a lot to ruin "evil is self-defeating and stupid" for me, at least with regards to Exalted.

                                            ​I'm all for the Deathlords having fallibility, but at this point my response to them being the incompetent architects of their own downfall is a bit cold; I'd prefer them to have a bit more credibility.

                                            ​On the other hand, the idea that there's not really much of a grand destiny behind the return of the Solar Exalted, and that it could amount to a fortuitous accident... at the moment, it doesn't appeal to me personally so much as their return being an expression of the narrative of the entombed folk hero coming again at the hour of greatest need, but there's a nihilism to it that I can certainly see the appeal of for people who would want to play up that aspect of the setting.

                                            ​It's at times like this that I hope the books would take a minimalistic approach to filling in certain aspects, to more readily accommodate alternative interpretations.

                                            ​EDIT: As for the Jade Prison itself, I don't know if it's specifically a reference to the 108 Stars of Destiny having been imprisoned since time immemorial beneath an ancient stele-bearing stone tortoise, but... it probably has a source in common.

                                            ​Honestly, even apart from the fact that I don't quite come down on "Exaltations are not literal things" as hard as some other people to do, for me a case like this is one in which no level of abstraction can get the same kind of visceral thrill as the idea of some kind of ominous vessel.

                                            ​A sarcophagus, a tomb, a chest, a Han dynasty burial suit, whichever; there can be more to it than being an object, but the tactile nature of it is very endearing.

                                                    The problem I have with this idea [snarkily described above as 'a literal Deathlord's actual master plan'] is twofold.

                                                    1) It implies a degree of complicity and malice to every Sidereal since the Usurpation I find nearly as bad as Lilun for Infernals. "We have participated in steadily grinding out the light of humanity since time immemorial 'for its' own good', but for its' own good, we mean so we don't get in trouble when they come back." In some several thousand years there have been no malcontent Sidereals acting against this Grand Doctrine enough to cause more than one or two rogue Solars? Plus it implies, like, "Check your humanity at the door", which I know is somehow really popular to assign to Sidereals but I find frankly ridiculous; the instant they get to Heaven they stop having personal biases and opinions on things? "At least my suffering to this point was part of some master plan..." "Yeah it was basically so we didn't have to take down the Solars again." "...What." "Yeah, outside of that it's pretty meaningless, totally not necessary for Fate to function, just made our jobs easier." ".......what."
                                                    It changes the context of the Sidereal stance, from "We ACCEPT that Creation will be lessened in exchange for its' salvation, but this is a cost we willingly bear the burden of on our shoulders" to "We MANDATE that Creation will be lessened in exchange for its' salvation, and we will be tasked forevermore with personally going around swinging wrenches at kneecaps, metaphorically speaking".

                                                    2) I know solar fanboys like to pretend Dragon-Blooded and Sidereals aren't REALLY Exalted, but even in 2e and I'm pretty sure even MORE so in 3e, there was still supposed to be the same sort of 'spark' of heroism, the moment that pushed them to rise up and ignite the dormant power in their blood or their stars......... which would be just as smothered by this grand master plan of ensuring noone rises up. Also, 3e is pushing away from "And then I rose up and punched out the Dragon Blooded Satrap screaming WHAT THE CHRIST ARE YOU TALKING ABOUT" as the default Solar Exaltation; Perfect Soul threw a hissy fit at a shrine of the Sun, Prince Diamond did a little legal wrangling and accepted punishment in his brother's place using an obscure law of his people, etc.

                                                    I mean it works fine if you consider the Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded and Lunars and Exigents not REALLY Exalted to the degree Solars are and the Sidereals to actually be the real villains of everything rather than misguided heroes who made a decision that was both unfortunate for Creation but the best option available to them, but I'm pretty sure neither of these tenets is accurate to the game as it stands.

                                                            I don't think that either 1 or 2 is necessarily true.

                                                            My thoughts on the heroic spark is that there is a different form for each Exalted type; it's pretty easy to draw that conclusion when you look at how each Exalt is 'chosen' in vastly different ways and how it's reflected in their abilities (Lunars have to try when survival is impossible, and they're the tough adaptable survivors. Solars have to attempt the impossible and succeed, and they get Supernals. etc etc). For Sidereals, I see them as destined heroes. Their entire shtick is meant to be that they have a part to play in Creation, and their 'spark' is that they accept this truth and push to fulfill that part to play, as best as they can.

                                                            While this doesn't necessarily imply the Sidereals are mindless, nor would I suggest that the Maidens have gone out of their way to choose people who would be complicit in this (though that's possible), it does imply trust in authority. A Sidereal's been brought up and made to believe that Fate has a grand plan, and that their role is to play their part to perfection. They run heaven and a bureaucracy, the latter of which reacts to change with hostility.

                                                            A Sidereal's place is not to question, nor to act against their masters; it is to interpret the will of fate and do everything in their power to bring about the best path. Think Jedi.

                                                            This doesn't mean a Sidereal is necessarily a bad person, or that they think the Prison is a good thing. But they've been taught to trust the will of the force- I mean, Fate - all their lives, by every teacher they've ever had. Putting trust in Fate's plan is what gives them their power, it's what moves them up in the bureaucracy. Seeing what they do of Fate probably allows them to see what happens when one tries to defy it, and I don't think it's pretty

                                                            And if they did rebel? I imagine they'd lose privileges and political power very quickly.

                                                            I'm not sure how I feel about a physical vs metaphorical prison persay, but I do think there should be a physical representation of it. (I've been picturing a floating orb that exploded when it was 'broken')
