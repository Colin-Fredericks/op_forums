            Products defined as antibotic are no more effective at sanitation than regular soap and warm water for handwashing.

            To make soap, all you need is lye (wood ash is a common source of this) and a fat to bind it in (animal fat or olive oil works). It's not that soap is difficult to make, it's that you need to educate people that they need to frequently wash their hands with soap and warm water.

            For toothpaste, if you have natron deposits, you can grind it into a powder. When people mix it with water, it becomes a sort of salty paste to brush their teeth with. You can also promote nice breath by getting people to chomp on fennel after a meal.

                                Really, while there's a lot of significance to the use of Intimacies in persuading people to act in ways they wouldn't otherwise be inclined to (and to not act in ways that they might; somebody can use that Defining Intimacy to resist attempts to persuade them to do something to harm you), one should also consider the fact that they're meant to represent what a person is actually interested in and inclined towards. You can rapidly give somebody a positive (or, rather, possessive) Defining Intimacy towards you and then simply let them act upon it of their own initiative. Plus, considering the fact that lust can be possessive rather than submissive, even if you are using it for persuasion, the characteristics of that interaction need not be coercive or withholding; you can be in a position to influence people from the role of their most beloved servant, prostrating yourself before them.

In legend, the plants were a gift from the gods, always to be grown together, eaten together, and celebrated together.
                                        Each of the sisters contributes something to the planting. Together, the sisters provide a balanced diet from a single planting.
                                        • As older sisters often do, the corn offers the beans needed support.
                                        • The beans, the giving sister, pull nitrogen from the air and bring it to the soil for the benefit of all three.
                                        • As the beans grow through the tangle of squash vines and wind their way up the cornstalks into the sunlight, they hold the sisters close together.
