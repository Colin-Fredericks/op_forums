        Well, a lot depends on the nature of the God-Blooded (just as Gethame depends on the nature of the Mountain Folk). Since 3e is trying to go back to the swords-and-sorcery focus of 1e (rather than the Final Fantasy focus of 2e), I just reprise everything from 1e and adjust to reflect the canon changes from 3e.

        The North in my games is popular among Mortal Sorcerers because of the limited influence of the DB outside of the Realm client states. I have Mela's Fangs being the loci of Mortal Sorcery in Creation, with seven hundred years of Terrestrial Circle Workings creating hundreds of small independent states that are allied together against supernatural threats. I also have the Mortal Sorcerers of Mela's Fangs resemble the Witches and Wizards of Discworld.

            Something new. The Lair of the Spider King alludes to the treasure-caravans of the Great Houses passing along the Black Shale Road in the foothills of the North that connect Whitewall, Pneuma and Gethamane to Fortitude and Grieve. It also mentions a place called Shale Strand.

            Very cool stuff! I wanted to get people's opinions on the region, or ask if anyone has developed anything. What is Shale Strand like, for instance?

            ***

            I also wanted to talk a bit about Whitewall, since my friend Praxiscat is running a game set in the North and my girlfriend is playing one of the members of an order of monster-hunters that work out of Whitewall that was mentioned briefly in the leak.

            Going back over Bastions of the North, it mentioned that the city has its civic government, Judges, Inspectors, Guardians, but it speaks about them in a sort of glowing fashion. One of the revisions that we've been playing with is that the Judges, Inspectors and Guardians are actually religious positions within the organized religion of worship for the Syndics in Whitewall.

            In that way, Whitewall's civic obedience and rules about etiquette and fear of outsiders is rooted in a religious order that legitimates the Syndics and carries out their will. I feel like this helps ground the Syndics a bit more and give more context for their control of the city, as well as giving the people of Whitewall more flavor to work with.

            In this framework, the government works like this:
            • Judges are elders, the high priests of the Order of Whitewall, who serve the Syndics and best discern the way to proceed according to their tribunal laws. They are leaders of the faith, administrators and judges in the legal sense who assign punishments like those described in Bastions of the North.
            • Inspectors are the main priesthood of the Order, and they serve the holy duty of instructing and leading Whitewallers in prayer, and acting as the eyes and ears of the Syndics to maintain their order. They inspect the integrity of buildings and perform other civil services like that in a ritualized fashion, and they also spy on the people like secret police for subtle violations of the tribunal law. They also help maintain the reputation of Whitewall's trade by uncovering criminals, counterfeiters and the like to bring them before the judges for punishment.
            • Guardians are the odd ones out. Technically members of the priesthood of the Order of Whitewall, their theological learning is more perfunctory than the others, though each Guardian is trained to hold strong loyalties to Whitewall itself. They are trained as the militia are (if they're not Whitewall natives) then trained further with intense, specialized training and education in knowledge of the creatures of the Wyld and the Dead. Then they use their knowledge and skills as monster hunters to protect Whitewall from the threat of monsters within its walls and to sell their services to Whitewall's allies in the Near North. The vast majority of them are exceptionally trained mortal men and women equipped with knowledge and special tools by members of the Guardians to combat the dead, like talismans and stuff, but they have some odd members too, God-Blooded, maybe a Terrestrial Exalt or two, possibly stranger allies. The Guardians are famous and eclectic.
            • The Militia aren't part of the government really, but like previously, all Whitewallers go through this training to defend the city. It's a good detail that totally fits the paranoia that seems to be a thing for them in 3e.
            A couple of questions for you guys. Does all of that sound cool or potentially interesting so far? I want to provide some religious/social foundations for the strong sense of identity of Whitewallers.

            Another one: Of the names, I find Inspectors to be nicely innocuous but also a bit... underwhelming. Should that name stay, or should it be replaced? Maybe each of these aspects of the Order of Whitewall has a longer name that gets shortened to Judge, Inspector and Guardian?

            What do you guys think of the Guardians in this version? I'm imagining they're about as close as you get to Witcher type characters without actually being superhuman mutants, and the unusual members of the Guardians (the God-Blooded and stuff) will provide some of the unique tools they have for fighting monsters and some of the institutional knowledge. It also might help to compare them to the BPRD in Hellboy. Does that sound cool to you guys?

            I have more ideas for Whitewall in 3e, but I'd love to hear your thoughts on these. I will also have all of this stuff written up in a more formal and complete style if there's interest.

            Oh, and one other thing! My girlfriend, who is playing one of the Guardians came up with a symbol for them that she called the Tri-Knot, figuring it's a symbol associated with the Syndics and a general motif in Whitewall stuff. Her character (and presumably others) frequently have them on necklaces.

                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                    Inspectors are accountants, so they should be boring, even if you make them into a religious fellowship. If you change their function, you should have something else become the accountants if you want to focus on the details of life in Whitewall.
                    They engaged in social policing in 1e, and I felt like it would be worthwhile to explore the implication of that. Plus the association of mundane activities like accounting or building inspection in the context of a religious order has a large precedent in the real world. They are priest-accountants, whose ledgers reinforce the Order of Whitewall and the will of the Syndics. I thought that was a way to help make them pop a bit more that felt real.

                    A more cynical reading might conclude that they're boring accountants with a trumped up sense of importance clad in religious aggrandizement who punish those who transgress against the social norms of the city (which were apparently codified in law in 1e) in order to make themselves feel powerful. That might certainly be true of some of them!

                        Another one! So, Eric Minton said this:

                        Originally posted by Eric Minton View Post
                        The peninsular North is not quite as inhospitable as previous editions, to allow some distinction between Near and Far North. The Near North encompasses a range of cold climates, though, and locales such as Gethamane remain as wintry and bleak as ever.

                        The Icewalkers mostly dwell east of the peninsular North, both north and south of the inland seas clasped between Mela's Fangs. "Icewalkers" does encompass a range of nomadic peoples, so it's not unlikely that a few Icewalker tribes can be found on the main peninsula.

                        We haven't pinned down the exact location of the Bull's empire, but what's left of it is looking to be centered east of Malice Bay.
                        Very cool environmental notes. I wrote up a location in the same style as the gazetteer write ups from the corebook for the coast.

                        The Location of the Black Cliffs
                        Originally posted by The Black Cliffs
                        East of the mountains and hills in which the Spider King’s servants dwell, steep cliffs of black shale rise from beaches armored in pebbles. These are the Black Cliffs, a region of the White Sea’s southern coast famous for its inhospitality. The cliffs are tall and jagged with few ways to easily traverse down to uncomfortable and unfriendly beaches that don’t offer much in the way of resources even during the summer months. Hardy fishing villages survive here, partially embedded in the cliffs, and they maintain their independence with isolation and violent suspicion. They are not wrong to be suspicious, either.

                        The Black Cliffs are home to more than just small fishing villages. The cliffs are riddled with small coves and grottos eroded by ice and time that form havens for pirates and other creatures. The inhabitants of the Black Cliffs must deal with these havens and are routinely extorted by criminals, capricious elementals and the Winter Folk, who sometimes ride in vessels of shining ice to pluck a tribute of bodies from those that live among the cliffs.

                        Among the most notable communities of the cliffs is Paradise Grotto, a pirate’s haven constructed in one of the largest coves in the region. Wooden docks and homes line the walls of the underground and paper lanterns burn in every corner, along with other more exotic smells. Trade and law here are honored by the nymph Lyrasu and her cultists, all devoted to obeying her every words. She is entertained by the drama of mortals, and brings disparate interests to the Grotto so that they might entertain her. All kinds of ships dock in the Grotto, even the frozen sails of the Winter Folk can be found here, though their approach always brings tension to Lyrasu’s paradise.
                        So that's something. Let me know what you guys think, or if you have any ideas to flavor the place. I'd love to make Paradise Grotto more creative for instance, to make it really feel like it belongs in Exalted.

                        On another note, the Bull's empire! What's left of it is, I believe, referring to how it has been downsized from 2e to 3e rather than to anything having happened to the place. So this is cool! I'm imagining that the Bull's Empire in 3e is probably gonna wind up somewhere between Icehome and Rubylak, or possible a little bit north/northeast of that area, since that means Tuskers can be a source of tension there too.

                        Any thoughts on any of this folks?

                                            ​That description of the geographic arrangement of the icewalkers makes for a similar dynamic to what the Asian steppe nomads had with China; mostly moving around and fighting over the habitable parts of their own territory, periodically launching raids into the more arable settlements, occasionally organising into big groups that conduct major raids on the scale of full warfare (although possibly still without territorial conquest)*, and with the capacity to form a major conquering army that can establish a sizable empire.
