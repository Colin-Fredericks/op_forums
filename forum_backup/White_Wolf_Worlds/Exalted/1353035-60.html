                        Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                        What had you hoped for? Sorry if you already said recently and I'm making you repeat yourself, but I glanced back and well... it's a big thread.
                        My hope with Exalted: Essence was for a game that removes a lot of the game's tedious math and fiddly bits (and some of its D&D heritage) in favor of a streamlined, narrative-focused experience. I was expecting Charm design to move away from math-adjustments and +dice or +successes and towards unique special powers and capabilities. I hoped that Essence would rework Abilities to function along a broader set of narrative permissions, but they seem to be largely truncated into a smaller list. I guess I just expected Essence to be a larger move away from the complexity of 3e, but it's mostly just a lateral move to a different set of complex mechanics.

                        Originally posted by Darkfoxdev
                        One time expenditures on excellencies, attacks separated from the initiative, turns are team-based, animas have much simpler scales(none/building/full), motes are scaled down into the single digits, charms/turn scaled way down, tiered foes and the universal charm set. I dunno this looks much more streamlined to me. Considering we're on the outside looking in and that was a WiP test game I'd say it was pretty comprehensible.

                        I will say it has some of storypath's unintuitiveness that's more do to newer mechanical philosophies than slow mechanics, it's pretty easy to catch onto. Currently, my only gripe is that your mote pool won't be high enough to reach bonfire outside combat, and might be bit small for games/sessions with less combat.
                        Breaking off attacks from initiative means more numbers to keep track of in combat. Anima has become a 0-10 scale as opposed to dim/glowing/burning/iconic. Charms have regained 2e's 'one per action' limitation in the form of '1 per attack step.' Some of these changes, like tiered foes and universal charms, are something of an improvement. Essence has also added a weird 7-part Virtue system which seems entirely superfluous, since it's keeping Intimacies as well. Also, in terms of unneccesary complexity, Essence has removed battlegroups and replaced them with individual mooks which must be defeated separately.

                        None of these changes seem to make combat any quicker to resolve. We've still got all the dice, all the fiddly Charms, and as far as I can tell all the numbers of Exalted 3e. Withering, Decisive, soak, hardness, etc.

                                                    Nobody mentioned a Stunt BAR, IIRC. You get dice from Stunting that can be banked or spent.

                                                    And the only mention I heard in the first session of a Power BAR was likening your Power that you build with Withering attacks to a Power Bar from a fighting game, in that you build it up.

                                                    So yeah, instead of building Initiative and having that coupled to when you attack, you just go in Whatever Order (no need to keep track of who goes when with it fluctuating with each slap) you just have to keep a measure of how much Power you have and whether that means you can launch a Decisive Attack. And it may cap at 10. So yes, it IS replacing Initiative as a Thing To Keep Track Of, but instead of "I need to keep track of this for my Decisive Damage Pool and when I react and oh right I gained another five from Crashing my opponent-" it's just "I gain this, I can launch a Decisive on my next turn". It's a Thing that you keep track of, yes, but you don't have to keep track of as much with it.
