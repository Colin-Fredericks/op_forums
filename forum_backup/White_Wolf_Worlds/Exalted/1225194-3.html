    Obsidian Butterflies works brilliantly on battlegroups. Brilliant Raptor, if you max your willpower, go for the flame initiation and take all the options, can be spammed several rounds to pretty good effect, allowing you to bypass initiative building and go straight for decisive, though I never actually tried that particular build. Now, Magma Kraken is awesome. That spell is simply devastating. I heartily recommend it!

    The 'problem' is of course that Obsidian Butterflies and Brilliant Raptor don't scale like a combat charm tree, but to be honest, for 8 xp you get a pretty decent package. Having said that, my Sorceress used demons, Invulnerable Skin of Bronze and her flame piece with Righteous Devil Tiger to much greater effect. In fact, after getting the form charm and the other Essence 2 charms in her martial art, I don't think I ever cast Brilliant Raptor anymore. Obsidian Butterflies on the other hand continued to be relatively useful against battlegroups.

                        I’ve found that most of the people I’ve played any version of Exalted with generally took Sorcery for its utility and not its combat effects. The game I play in now, 5 of us have Sorcery and multiple spells, but only two have combat spells, and only one of those is offensive. The other took ISoB to boost soak on a brawler. Sadly, this makes it hard to speak to this thread based on personal experience.

                        That being said, I’ve run some of the combat spells through the wringer to see if I wanted to buy them. At the Terrestrial/Emerald Circle, I’ve found that the AoE spells hold up better than the single target offense ones for combat. It’s hard to mimic the size of areas they hit with charms, and they hold up well for sweeping away battle groups even at higher essence levels. I haven’t played with Thunder Wolf’s Howl yet, but it looks promising.

                            Some people are seriously undervaluing Sculpted Seafoam Eidolon. It's an all-purpose, all-senses illusion spell that you have to get within Short range to even have a chance to see through without specialized magic. And many people's Perception + Awareness is going to be way worse than your Manipulation + Occult, so often they're going to fail even then. On top of this, it lasts an entire day. This means that you can cast it in advance and walk around with, for instance, an illusionary extra party member (or a duplicate of one of yours), sculpted to look huge and dangerous, and:

                            1. Any opponents who don't close to Short range with them are going to waste ranged attacks and other effects on them. This could lead to an ambusher wasting their opportunity against a target who isn't real.

                            2. Anyone assessing how dangerous you are from a distance is going to think you're more dangerous than you really are.

                            3. In combat, you can trick opponents into eg. sending their bruiser to fight your illusionary bruiser, putting them out of position. It's not hard to see how "oh, look, you sent your two toughest dudes to double-team the big hulking dangerous Dawn, but he wasn't there at all" could swing a fight - by the time they're close enough to realize they were tricked, it could be too late.

                            4. If they fail to see through the deception (by my reading, they only get one roll), they can actually waste attacks on it; they'll probably see through it if they touch it and will get suspicious very quickly when it fails to do anything meaningful itself, but even just making them waste one or two attacks can be valuable.

                            You can also pull all of these off in concert with other abilities. For instance against opponents who know your circle's composition, your Night caste disguises himself as someone or hides himself somewhere, and you create an illusionary copy of the Night caste to throw opponents off. It doesn't have to compete with Flawlessly Impenetrable Disguise - they can work in concert. Creating a fake copy of yourself to draw attention while you're disguised as your manservant or creeping around to set up an assassination or something is potentially a powerful strategy.

                            But that's only one of the things you can do with it. You can create a duplicate of yourself to act as a decoy and draw off pursuers. You can create artificial obstructions to hide behind. You can cover an existing pit, hole, or small gap with an illusionary floor or ledge.

                            You can summon artificial demons (Second or even, if you're daring enough, Third circles) to use as bluffs. Maybe that isn't Octavian. Maybe I'm not powerful enough to summon or bind him. Maybe I can, but just didn't have the time or don't want to take the risk. Maybe if you stepped up and examined him closely, you'd see through the deception. But given that his Equitable Resolution could blow your head clean off, the real question is... are you feeling lucky, punk?

                            This can also trick opponents into committing their strategic resources the wrong way in other ways - eg. if I roll up with an illusionary Octavian, my opponents are likely to start frantically putting together their anti-demon / anti-CoD / anti-spirit effects. By the time they realize he's a fake, they've wasted a bunch of their combat potential preparing for the wrong thing.

                            You have to remember that versatility is valuable in its own way (the same goes to a lesser extent for the weather-control spell.) It lets you fake exactly what's needed at any given time, and as long as nobody gets close to it or hits it with a powerful deception-piercing magic, it's guaranteed to work. That's pretty powerful for TCS.
