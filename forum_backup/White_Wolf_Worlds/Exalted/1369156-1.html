                                Yeah, probably it's more that nobles and commoners have different cultures with different social mores.

                                In Britain, certainly there's been periods where nobles could get away with stuff the plebs couldn't, but there's also been periods where the opposite is true. Nobles generally had much less control over who to marry (which lasted until our current Prince Charles, tbh), and had to marry much younger than commoners.

                                There's also a matter of what level of noble you are; the richest, most powerful nobles can probably define culture to whatever they like in a way that lower-ranking nobles can't. For example, defining what's "male" and "female" fashion. Some of our kings had long hair, make-up, high heels, because they made it the fashion for men.
                                Compare, for example, 3 generations of Kings (well, King George, his successor King William, his successor's husband Prince Albert).
                                https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikiped...er_1790-96.jpg
                                http://3.bp.blogspot.com/-ymSbxBw2RU...f+Clarence.jpg
                                http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_-HE1lR1mlb...lbert-1842.jpg
                                To me, the last of the three looks much more "masculine", but that's only because each one defined what "masculine" looked like. A man in 1800 would have thought the first much more fitting for a King.

                                On the other hand, their power may depend on cultural ideas.
                                Some English monarchs were able to define culture around what they liked; tons had mistresses and the Bishops just kind of... didn't talk about it. Some English kings had male lovers (in a homophobic society), and mostly got away with it, but not always. If the King was unpopular in other ways, it suddenly became a big problem (King Edward's boyfriend was flat-out murdered by his nobles, and Edward ended up being killed by his wife, and their homosexuality was used as a justification, but he probably wouldn't have had a problem if he was a more popular King. His boyfriend was extremely unpopular because the King was so biased towards him, and he took advantage of that. Whereas Edward's wife got away with adultery and murder because she was a much stronger ruler. And James I is suspected by many historians of being bisexual, but he was a very powerful and popular King, so no-one attacked him over it. But he didn't try to make society generally less homophobic, actually he made it worse, so that's just escaping cultural bonds and not shaping culture generally).

                                On the other hand, Elizabeth II managed to be a very successful female monarch (I'd argue the first successful female monarch of England, Matilda had to fight constant wars against her cousin, Jane Grey was a puppet who lasted 9 days, and Mary was dominated by her husband and father-in-law) by taking gender stereotypes and manipulating them to strengthen her, while avoiding the ones she didn't like. Turning herself into the virgin queen/wife of England itself. Presenting herself as a pure virgin girl that honourable Christian knights should defend, but then showing herself as having a "masculine" heart for war, like her father.

                                So nobles always have a balance between relying on and controlling the cultural mores of their society.

                                                            Couple of other games; I did a bit in my old game with gender roles vis-a-vis inheritance, where the monarchies in the area were matriarchal and women took a religious queenship role, while their husbands were warleaders and administrators. When a queen died, her husband lost power, because he lacked legitimacy. As a further complicating factor, the idea was that all the rulers were Dragonblood; yet because their bloodlines had declined, in reality they weren't. In a way, the system had developed to attract foreign (male) Dragonblood to marry in to the local power structures, or just to attract male Dragonblood from neighbouring kingdoms. This meant you had a powerful Dragonblood warrior king... but he couldn't rule without the support of his wife, because she actually knew the local power structures, and was considered a religious figure.

                                                            But then in each state, the way this worked practically was different, as people twisted the culture to fit their own political aims, and the lack of actual Dragonblood. Ashen Witch, a powerful Fire Aspect sorceress, had a stupid-but-handsome puppet husband, so paying lip service to cultural mores while she wielded both religious and secular power. The eastern of these four monarchies had a mortal king and queen, but the queen's father (Ashen Witch's cousin), an Earth Aspect, ran everything day-to-day due to experience (until PCs killed him). Another one had a charismatic fake water aspect (he was a pelagathrope) king, who was eager to work with the PCs, but his wife who was more connected to local power structures was not (she had a terrible obsidian butterfly-related accident after calling the Wyld Hunt on the PCs). The final one was ruled by Stormhand, a fake Air aspect (he was an elemental-blood), but as soon as he screwed up by trying to fight the PCs, his wife divorced him (as she held the religious legitimacy, she had control of divorce) and replaced him with someone less likely to start a war with Solars.

                                                            One confusing thing is that one of the PCs decided to create her own queendom and took some of the other Queen's tribes to do so. As a face-saving measure, Ashen Witch married her daughter to the PC. So the PC called herself Queen Enhenduanna. But this really irritated the local rulers, who were insistent that she was King Enhenduanna; yes, she was female, but her wife was of the true royal line, she had religious legitimacy, she was Queen, while Enhenduanna was a foreign sorcerer adventurer, so she was King. If Enhenduanna is King, married to the Queen, then she's accepted the local power structure and no one feels they have to declare war on a powerful sorceress with a ton of Dragons. But if she's Queen, then she's usurping the rights of the royal bloodlines to own the local lands, and that's a problem.
