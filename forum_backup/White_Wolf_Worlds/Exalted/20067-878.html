                    The Dragons are also kind of off-handedly mentioned in Games of Divinity calls them among the gods also. Basically them becoming Gaia's souls was I think an outgrowth of a couple things. First, in 1e it was mostly just fanon. Folks saw how Primordials were structured ala the Yozis, and kind fo grabbed onto the Dragons as her analogs as nothing else was said about her. Basically nothing else was said and folks took what was there and put it into the assumed box.

                    This kind of was cemented in the stuff in Exalted: the Autochthonians, which by then seems to have bled into a ocuple "after the chronicle" stuff and I think in setting descriptions of Autochthon. Again, his strucutre was templated on the Yozis in GoD, and her Dragons became by analogy the closest thing. Byt hen and into 2e, things that were "category: null" like Adorjan's children were kind of not thought on much, so they got reinforced as her souls. And come 2e's Roll of Glorious Divinity II, which went into detail on Yozis again in 2e, the Dragons were made for-sure her souls in that edition.

                    I in general tend to treat them as kind of just their own category of spritual being, whcih as far as the Celestial Bureaucracy is concerned are gods of the elements. So the Dragon of Fire is the god of like, fire and kind of like how the Sun embodies his purview, the Dragon of Fire does as well. Lesser dragons become more dragon-like becuase the Dragon of Fire is like, the Platonic Ideal of Being a Fire Creature. And the themes fo the Dragons probably were pretty important of how Creation was well, Created, but are like any god not their element exactly.

                    Also in this context, I go with the hinted bit of Gaia having twenty hidden souls, which are unrelated entirely with the Dragons and probably spit out all sorts of misc "Not god, demon, or elemental" spirits found thorughout the world.
