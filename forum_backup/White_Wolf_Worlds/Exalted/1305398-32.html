        I don't really know, necessarily, if that's something that should happen, even in a high essence DB game. For one thing even without managing the combat all on your own it would take hours, and hours, and slogging through everyone's massive soak and charms, and flipping through everything to see what you want to do. For another, even elder exalted often don't reach Essence 5. Mnemon is 5, Cathak Cainen is 5, Lilith is 6. They're all hundreds or even thousands of years old though, and excepting Usurpation survivors, Solars are in the 1-2 range being only a few years old.

        If you're running a high essence DB game, you'd have a way better time making the antagonists a circle of quick character Solars. Like the Wandering Deathknight in the corebook or Prince Resplendent in Adversaries of the Righteous. They can prove to be quite challenging without feeling almost impossible to run, and rather than having players getting frustrated flipping tables as all their attacks are being negated with Hail Shattering Practice and trying to whittle an enemy down through hundreds of motes the action will be a lot more dynamic.



        Something to keep in mind with all this is that any RPG system, and Exalted 3rd edition even more than most, is not a universal physics theory for the world the characters live in. It's not a system set in place where you set up all the pieces and hit the start button and everything falls into place as it should, logically as the setting presents it. The splatbook rules are designed to make player characters, who get special attention and focus in the story, not a glimpse at what those splats are actually like in Exalted.

        Some people don't like that, and to them I would say that the first thing they need to do then is start handing out only 1xp every 2-3 sessions to avoid the explosive and unrealistic growth of skill that their characters get over NPCs.

        Ideally the rules will help the game play out something like the stories are supposed to, while adding a fun tactical game element to it. You shouldn't be thinking "What's a realistic scenario given the rules of the game, if I assume that Solar antagonists have access to all these charms and abilities." What you should be thinking is "What's a fun way to portray a Solar that will not leave my players disappointed, and also not hate me and never want to play this game again."

        You could even make up charms that don't exist, and would be too powerful for normal Solars to have. Like a version of Circle of Bright Reaving that doesn't require you to incapacitate an enemy first, or cost initiative to use, but does cost a willpower. Normally, that wouldn't okay, but on your particular Solar who doesn't have 40 different charms to enhance that or chain into and out of it, that might be great.

            I wouldn't like to derail the thread, but let me just kindly disagree with you. If we plan to continue the discussion, maybe it could be placed in a separate thread?

            EDIT: That said, let me adress a couple of points. For disclaimer: I am not running a DB game, I am running a Solar game and idly musing about the system.

            I agree that fight at this scale would be difficult to run. However 90 percent of the difficulties would be already present in any other fight, since they have to do with players flipping through their charms. They either know it by heart or are already flipping (I have both species at my table). So the complexity doesn't seem really reduced. It's Essence 5 hearth versus any reliable opposition.

            Now, "replace one archnemesis with a circle of weaker ones" is not generically a good advice. This creates a completely different dynamics with completely different stories. I like archnemeses. I could imagine making a circle of enemies an archnemesis, but this would require a whole lot of story preparation. Each of them should be properly flashed out and have their own history with the players. That said, a single, nigh invincible archnemesis is a staple in the kind of stories that Exalted emulates. Moreover, I understand that in this edition Essence 5 means something different than in previous editions, but let's not exaggerate. It doesn't mean that we are not allowed to incorporate such beings in the stories we tell. I myself use some houserules that pumps things up slightly in the direction of second edition, but this scenario is reasonably canonical. Think of Mask of Winters if you like. He shouldn't be weaker than an Essence 5 Solar.

            So now, should the main villain be QC?

            I am personally not totally opposed to asymmetric game mechanics. However, I am not a great fan of QC as they are executed in Exalted. Replacing stats with dice pools doesn't really make preparations quicker (to me) and forces some arbitrary and barely consistent choices during the play. (E.g. what is exactly their armoured soak?) What is really time consuming is the choice of charms. And again, at this point I really prefer to simply pick some from the charmset, since I invest once in understanding it, than essentially make a whole new charmset each time I am coming up with the opposition.

            This has also a side advantage: when there are more participants to the combat, I let them be run by players whose characters are absent in the scene. Creating them by the book make them easier to run.

            What is more, I personally feel that whenever I prepare my NPCs like PCs I am forced to actually leave them with some weakness to exploit. For example, it turns out that they are very bad against stealth. When I'm making generic QC, I am somewhat lazy and all their capabilities end up rather generic.

            Now, I understand that some people like asymmetric mechanics, but I also don't like it on a personal level. It's ok in a very focused and light games, like PBTA, but in heavier more classical game, when I want to make the opposition stronger than individual players, it feels like cheating. Especially that my players also tend to ask whether some particular feat is something they can achieve and I know that at some point it would get frustrating if the answer is consistently "no."

            As I said, I understand that the fight I asked for is a whole lot of work, but I think it's doable. My players fought with regular characters and it was longish but in a pleasant way, It felt like a multi-episode fight in shonen anime, exactly as it should. They told me, they found it very satisfying.
