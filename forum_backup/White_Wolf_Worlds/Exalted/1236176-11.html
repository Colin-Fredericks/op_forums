                        This post reminds me a bit on what I said earlier, on how slavery doesn't really come about because of some ecnoomic benefits it has over other things or the need for it, as you said. It seems often to arise because some other itnerest or opportunity arises such that some folks find it profitable early on to use slavery and then it becomes self-perpetuating since well, stolen labor is pretty good marginswise.

                        And due to how generally autonomous many Solars in the First Age are depicted, slavery has a pretty high possibility at least of existing in a world with a lot of folks who if you wanted them tos top, could say "Make me" quite confidently.
                        Yeah, I think that's a good way to put it. And then once that thing happens, after an particular interest seizes that opportunity, systems can build around it that make it more difficult to disestablish, even for those who care to. Perhaps they end up with, built around an opportunistic structure begun by predecessors, not with magic that provides you with the ability to substitute for labour, but magic that allows you manage coercion much more securely than any mortal, and magic that complements simple, repetitive labour rather than replaces it, and military power that lets you mitigate rebellion, and then there's no clear way out of it that doesn't have a cost that they will willingly pay (without being "made" to).

                        (This is on top of accepting the conceit that Ex3 seems to run with, that magic is more art than science - you don't get what you want, necessarily, but what flows out of a process of exploration that is less than perfectly under rational control.)

                        (And I'd add we virtually know that at least some First Age Solars had coercion craft beyond what the Dragonblooded can easily bring to bear in the Age of Sorrows - the Perfect's staff is virtually the first artefact published for Exalted, and I'd be surprised if it was necessarily a hugely far outlier! Even it was somewhat..).

                            This feels like a good opportunity to make a point:
                            This thread has been running quite happily on First Age Conjecture for several pages now. Doesn't it make you the least bit sad, Third's whole "The First Age is Unknowable Mystery, and the Present Day is So Very Far From That Mystery that there's no meaningful connectivity between the Unknowable Mystery of the First Age and the Broke Rundown Iron-Medieval Present"? (question mark outside quote deliberate)

                            I get the idea of the Devs not wanting to spell things out so much, because they don't want What Was to default to Blueprint of What Should Be...Yet, I remain unconvinced that achieving that desired goal is worthwhile enough to abandon the elements of 2E that did work (2E Thaumaturgy allowing for meaningful mechanisms to advance the world, history and relics of the past having interesting and meaningful connectivity with goings-on in the present era) ....

                            To me, the decision to veil the First Age seems more like a desire to not write up the First Age in any meaningful way for Third, dressed up in high-sounding concept-talk about not wanting to create an Ideal Blueprint for PCs to follow. If Storytellers find they're having problems with their players drawing on meta-lore knowledge with PCs who have no way of drawing on such lore, they can simply have a talk with their players. For me, the irritation of the 1000 Dooms has been replaced by "EX3, now with More Ruined Ruins, ZERO Mortal Empowerment, and More Primitivism."

                            I really don't see the advantage game-wise in replacing a deep and game effect-meaningful Thaumaturgy-system like 2E's with EX3's Thaumaturgy-as-One-Trick-Miracles...Which itself has to strain to make exceptions, for stuff they conceptually wanted to keep, but that aligned with call-out's to 2E Thaumaturgy (Varangian Astrology, Sijan Funerists/Exorcists primarily).

                            On the historical level, I'm VERY pleased to see the map fill up, but the redaction of things like Opal, Tzatli etc etc ..again, don't see the upside.

                                    The desire to obscure the First Age is in part to just CTRL+ALT+DEL what came before so that they could tell new, probably more interesting in total, stories. Like, I think a big thing that's being begged here is the presentation fo the First Age in 2e was good. Even if we were going to get a more detailed First Age, it woudl be a very different one form the one we had before.

                                    I think the shifts on presentation of the eras before is a combination of not having the present defined by the past (which was a problem as many books in 2e more or less assumed you were a place of signifcance due to some relci fo the First Age), creates room for there to be more interesting things that just dont' fit a neat tidy timeline like DotFA had, and also banishing much of it the land of wind and ghosts allows for just more interesting storeis, a different take on what the First Age even was, and more ambiguity about the lens of history to work from.

                                    And as noted, there are hints of a lot of this. Arms of the Chosen isn't without First Age stuff, it's just a very different First Age than was in 2e.

                                    On thaumaturgy, the big thing there is that it had huge mission creep in 2e. A lot of it was stuff that either kind of warped the setting due to its mechanical assumptions (notably weather control and the spirit stuff), kind of made Occult this weird omni-Ability (alchemy again, geomancy, and some toher stuff stepped on toes of other things) and even wasn't clear why some things existed as they were (why is animal husbandry it?) It also had a side-effect of kind of being too common and taking away a bit of the idea of the setting being like Earth enough to make sense and also removed plot points like conmen or simply imisinformed people. When every village had a genuine shaman, it kind of made huxters not really have a place.

                                    There's alsoa gain, a lot of things that probably just didn't need the thaumaturgy subsystem to work. Like, most of animal husbandry is just....training and breeding animals. Alchemy works fine for Craft or Medicine. And things are moving towards more the sort of thing to make an interesting world PCs work in, not a speculative world-builiding thing to set novels in.

                                        Fundamentally, thaumaturgy in 2e was inconsistent with the setting presented in any version of Exalted. There were accordingly three approaches:
