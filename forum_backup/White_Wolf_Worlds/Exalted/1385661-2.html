                                                    As I've said, I consider Willpower more important than Motes, and wouldn't mind seeing Motes disappear.


                                                    So, the holistic theory I like so far:

                                                    Fear and hatred are Virtueless, and are closer to flaws than Intimacies.

                                                    Choosing a Virtue as your Major or Minor basically gives you that Virtue as a super-broad Intimacy, and your other Intimacies reflect specific areas you actually care about within the other five Virtues.

                                                    Someone who chooses Compassion as one of their Virtues is that all-loving sort of character who strives to save everyone, even their antagonists. Aang the Avatar, as an example, or the man that Uncle Iroh became before the show began. But someone who doesn't choose Compassion for their pair can only really be motivated or stirred if they care about someone in particular. Toph cared very little for people in general, except for the friends she made along the way.

                                                    Valor could represent confidence. Someone who chooses Valor as one of their Virtues is confident that they can meet and defeat any challenge, even in areas where that confidence might not be warranted. Azula is a good example of someone utterly convinced of her superiority and her ability to succeed; even if confronted in an area of weakness, she wouldn't balk but instead immediately look for a way to change the game in her favor. Meanwhile, Aang was confident in his ability to defend himself and escape from danger and in his ability to make friends, but he lacked the confidence to assert himself in his Earthbending training with Toph. Zuko never showed a lack of confidence in his ability to ninja-sneak his way into or out of any situation, but Azula knew all of the areas where he lacked confidence and struck him there. Katara didn't start as a very confident person in general, but over the course of time she developed confidence in her abilities as she mastered them.

                                                    Temperance could still represent self-control, with a Major or Minor acting much like the Virtue did before, give or take some adjustment. Someone who isn't Temperate in all things can choose specific Intimacies for the principles their character is most passionate about keeping: No Lying, No Intoxicants Ever, Vow of Chastity/Monogamy, Vow of Celibacy, Pay Back All Debts.

                                                    Conviction needs the most work, given its frequent interpretation as the "do what I want" Virtue.


                                                    My proposed Passion/Lust Virtue, taken as Major or Minor, would represent someone with a relentless curiosity and "joy of life". It's not that they must overindulge in anything, but they're always down to try anything at least once, and they live in a perpetual state of wanderlust. They can almost always see the bright side of almost anything, finding fun and humor in whatever they're doing... whether they have the compassion to use that to raise others' spirits, or whether they're a sadist who uses their joy to twist the knife on others' suffering.


                                                    If Ambition represents driving goals, it is easy to envision in terms of individual and specific goals, but as a whole Virtue, what would Ambition look like?

                                                    Would it represent a desire to achieve as much control over the world as possible, in general? Such a broad goal would definitely seem out of place as a single Intimacy for someone who didn't choose Ambition as Major or Minor.

                                                    But as a super-Intimacy of "Take Over The World", it doesn't provide nuance to lesser goals. I could persuade someone with a goal of "Reform The Scarlet Empire" to help me if I convince them doing so would further their own goal, but someone with Ambition as their Major Virtue may prefer the Scarlet Empire as it is... just with them in charge. Or they do want to change it after they take control. Either way, the distinction would seem to require a specific Ambition Intimacy despite having the all-encompassing super-Intimacy.

                                                    Alternatively, someone who wants to Rule The World derives their opinions on whether or not the Scarlet Empire needs reforming based on other Virtues. If they paired Compassion with Ambition, they would naturally want to reform the Scarlet Empire. But without sufficient Compassion, they probably don't care about anything but gaining and maintaining control.
