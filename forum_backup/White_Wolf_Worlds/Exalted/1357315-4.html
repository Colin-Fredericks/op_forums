                    Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                    Which is to say that a lighting ballista is not an artifact ballista, it’s first age artiface that makes it unique. Celestial Battle Armor or Dragon Armor or what have you should feel like that.
                    While I don't necessarily disagree that Dragon Armor shouldn't be first age artifice, I don't think it's its aesthetics that should make that distinction, because first age artifice doesn't necessarily have consistent aesthetics in 3rd edition. "Magitech" doesn't equal "First Age Artifice". The lightning ballista, clearly, does have that aesthetic, but I don't believe that's why it was defined that way. If you described an orichalcum artifact ballista as a tube filled with clockwork, that compressed springs that released the bolt, or even stated that it used a compressible forcefield as the "spring", but kept the stats the same, I don't think that would get classified as first age artifice. I'd even allow such a weapon to be self-loading (with an Evocation) and still not make it first age artifice. The lightning ballista, I believe, is first age artifice because of the effects of its evocations - it's enormously effective against armies. Basically, on a battlefield, if one side has a lightning ballista and the other doesn't have a way to counter it, the side in possession has won. I think it's that effect, with its huge impact on the setting, that justifies the first age artifice tag, rather than the look of the thing. If the lightning ballista happened to look like a medieval ballista, just one that shot bolts of lightning instead of giant arrows, but had the same game effects, it would still be first age artifice.

                    So, I think what you have to decide, when considering if Dragon Armor is first age artifice or not, is not whether it looks futuristic. Rather, you have to consider whether its powers being commonly available distort the setting, or if there's something unique about how its produced that does the same. As I already mentioned upthread, I think it's possible (though a bit weak) to argue that integrated weapons do that, and allowing sets to be produced as a single artifact creation project definitely do so. I could see an argument that the strength-boosting elements of any power armor also constitute a sufficiently setting-distorting element to justify calling Dragon Armor first age artifice. If the way to achieve great feats of strength is not to cultivate your Athletics ability, but rather to just get an appropriate suit of armor out of storage and use it briefly, that probably makes the Realm, at least, look rather different, I feel.
