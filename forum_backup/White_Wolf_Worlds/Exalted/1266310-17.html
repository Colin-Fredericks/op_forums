                                                            Originally posted by nalak42 View Post
                                                            Frankly I'm more curious about the hard light dust or whatever they said there were for the shields.
                                                            Why? We already saw it employed with the road-side barriers of the high-rise streets in Vale, the audience-protecting barriers of Amity Coliseum, and Velvet's Box.

                                                            Hard-light Dust creates temporary objects of mass and durability. These constructs are so temporary that machines have to keep spending energy or more hard-light Dust to maintain the existence of the constructs.

                                                            Stone walls and ice walls are not transparent, so hardlight walls cannot be used as visual cover to establish stealth, but it allows you to keep sight of your enemies in turn, and hardlight can probably be manipulated to create one-way barriers.

                                                            The temporary nature of hardlight constructs can be used to make barriers that block the passage or attacks of an enemy with a construct that disappears afterwards, allowing the passage or attacks of your allies. It can let you create a bridge or ladder for an ally that disappears before an enemy can use it to follow them.

                                                            Weiss has made her ice-constructs disappear when their continued existence would get in her way (as per the surrounding ice-spear wall in the Weiss Short), but that probably takes an effort of will and control. Her Earth wall, on the other hand, has NOT disappeared even after its purpose is fulfilled and the ice behind it would cover them just as well.

                                                            She probably made an Earth wall because she conjured more rock from the ground in front of her, so that it's all one solid piece, and thus harder to destroy or move.
