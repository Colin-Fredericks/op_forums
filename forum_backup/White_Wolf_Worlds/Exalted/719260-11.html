    Here's an initiation for all of you Clash-monkeys out there.

    Initiate of Strife

    Students of countless quarrels, the Initiates of Strife find enlightenment through conflict. Learned at the feet of countless War gods, Fae, Demons, or elder Chosen, this tradition stretches back to the Primordial Age, passed down through various dojos, gladiatorial academies, warrior fraternities, and the patronage of not a few Third Circle Demons. Though outwardly gruff and quick to anger, practitioners of this path place a deep reverence on honest conflict and the prospect of engaging a worthy opponent.

    Shaping Rituals
    The sting of battle is food and drink for an Initiate of Strife, the ebb and flow of conflict attuning him to the Essence of Creation. Whenever the Initiate would normally be subject to Wound or Onslaught penalties, regardless of any Charms he has active or Merits he would benefit from, he gains Sorcerous Motes equal to the sum of those penalties, to a maximum of 10 Sorcerous Motes. Additionally, whenever he gains or loses Initiative as the result of hitting or being hit by a Withering attack, he may Reflexively take a Shape Sorcery Action, provided he was not Crashed by the attack.

    Initiates are always perceptive of opportunity on the battlefield, turning a moment of advantage into fuel for their magics. Whenever the Initiate would succeed at performing a Gambit, he gains Sorcerous Motes equal to the Threshold Successes on the Initiative roll, to a maximum of 10 Sorcerous Motes.

    The ultimate expression of their style of magic comes in meeting might with might and parlaying that exchange into sorcerous power. Whenever the Initiate Clashes, regardless of who wins the Clash, he gains Sorcerous Motes equal to the Threshold Successes on the Clash roll, to a maximum of 10 Sorcerous Motes.

    Merits

    Ferocious Countermagic (Merit ••): The Initiate learns to confront enemy magics head-on, with harrowing effects. Whenever the Initiate attempts to counter a spell, he inflicts Withering Damage equal to the amount of Sorcerous Motes drained to the caster.

    Last-Ditch Counter (Merit •••): Initiates live by the maxim that the best defense is a stronger offense, and in the face of magic they can not counter, they fall back on the strength of their connection to Strife to overwhelm enemy spells. Whenever they are target by a spell that rolls to hit, an Initiate may spend Sorcerous Motes to Reflexively Clash that attack with an Intelligence + Occult roll. The Initiate must be within Close range of the casting Sorcerer, and must spend Sorcerous Motes based on the Circle of the Spell being Clashed: 5 motes for Terrestrial Spells, 10 for Celestial Spells, 20 for Solar Spells. If the Initiate wins the Clash, the casting Sorcerer takes Withering Damage equal to the Initiate’s Intelligence + Essence + Threshold Successes - soak.


    Any good?

                Divine Law

                By studying the laws of divinity, left behind by the Primordials, the sorcerer gains insight into the workings of the world. However, the laws are hidden. In one place, a mountain range depicts half of the law of waves while it's reflection in the lake below show the other half. In the far west, a whirlpool and a storm form in turns in the same place, declaring the law of seasons. The course of the sun and moon still occasionally divide the stars in such a manner that someone who knows what he's looking for can understand the basics of the law of time. Those rare souls who seek this knowledge are often consumed by the search for the laws and the study of them. Most are already initiated into sorcery, and Exalted. Indeed, this initiation is most common among Sidereals seeking initiation into the arts of Celestial Sorcery. Below are some examples.

                Shaping Rituals

                The Law of Seasons dictates the turning of the great wheel of seasons. By drawing on the symbols of an appropriate season in a stunt the sorcerer may gain (Stunt Rating) Sorcerous Motes once per spell. If the sorcerer casts a spell that draws on the symbology of the season that follows a spell that he has just cast - For example, casting Claws of the Wood Dragon and then immediately casting Flight of the Brilliant Raptor - The sorcerer may conserve any excess motes for the following spell. He may continue doing this so long as he follows the cycle of Air, Water, Wood, Fire, Earth and Calibration. A spell without clear seasonal associations may be cast at the end of the cycle. Otherwise, the sorcerer may cast any spell at that point without breaking the spell. This one I changed

                The Law of Time is the most esoteric of Laws. It allows a Sorcerer to take two Shape Sorcery Actions and then choose the better one as he glimpses into the future or draws on some power from the past. However, the use of such a power has a price; the Sorcerer suffers a cumulative -1 penalty to all perception rolls as past, present and future overly into constantly more confusing messes as he reaches further forward or back. And this one I like.

                Bonus General Ritual: By meditating on a Law related to his control spell, the sorcerer may cast his control spell ahead of time and disperse it into the law of Creation. At any later point, the sorcerer may call upon the spell, provided that the related Law affects his current area. The sorcerer may store 1 Celestial or 2 Terrestrial Spells, and no more, in such a manner.

                Merits

                Crowned King of the World(5-dot merit) The sorcerer may freely call upon all natural phenomena, summoning fire, ice or lightning, waves of water or causing the roots and branches of trees to grasp his foes. He may shake the earth or call boulders from the sky. He is crowned king of the earth, and granted a crown of 12 perfect diamonds which grow out of his skull. He may spend 1 mote to activate 3 of the gems for a scene. For each 3 gems so activated, he may upgrade the statistics of a weapon. With no gems active, he has merely the powers of a light mundane weapon, equivalent to a bow. With 3 gems active, this becomes a medium weapon. At 6, a heavy mundane weapon. At 9, it becomes a light artifact weapon. To activate the final 3 gems, however, he must also expend a willpower point, and takes one level of unstoppable lethal damage. However, he gains access to a medium artifact weapon. Mortals may fuel this merit with sorcerous motes gathered specifically for that purpose. The Exalted may develop Evocations for this crown, that function at any time, along the themes of Laws they have discovered and studied. Laws may be purchased as specific Lore or Occult specialties (E.g The Law of Time) but one may never truly understand more than (Essence x 2) such laws. Solars may teach Laws beyond such limits as they are the Givers of Law.

                            Note to self: Do not write without being actually in a coherent state of mind. I like the fluff, but I can't execute it properly it seems. So I'll drop the rituals(Except for the wave one, since that seems good... Just redivided) and replace it with something else.

                            The Art of Waves
                            Arising on the shores of the North-East where voices echo in valleys of ice, light is bent through age-old frozen waves and the great Western ocean slowly eats away even the greatest of mountains, those sorcerers who study the art of waves seek to understand and become part of this mesmerizing dance.

                            Shaping Rituals

                            The waves of the sea are chaotic, influenced by the wind and the movement of celestial bodies, as well as being repelled by the earth. However, they may erode mountains, given time. While away from the sea, the Sorcerer may perform this ritual by imitating the movement of waves in a short dance. This always grants 3 sorcerous motes. While at sea, the Sorcerer may instead add (Craft) or (Performance) dice to a shape sorcery roll as he moves with the waves or folds them into the creation of his spell.

                            The sorcerer recalls the Echoes of Power, and draws in sounds. While sounds can always be called into creation in even the stillest of caves, their widespread existence is confused, twisted by walls and drowned by the vastness of the sky. Thus they only grant limited amounts of power and may only be called on once a scene, granting (Performance) Sorcerous Motes.

                            Waves of light grant varying amounts of power as the sun and moon move across the sky. Artificial light or firelight can also be a source of power, but even the greatest inferno produces less power than the Moon. The Sun provides 5 Sorcerous motes. The Moon provides 4, while artificial light grants between 1-3 motes depending on the strength of the source. Light sources may not be drawn on more than once a scene, and colour spells appropriately: The sun will turns flames gold, for example, while the moon will turn them silver. This has no mechanical effect.

                            Merits

                            Call of the Waves(3-dot merit): As the Burning Name, save that the manipulation of light and sound waves also allows for a greater variety of stunts, and as such the cost of the merit has been adjusted.

                                        Divine Lotus Meditation

                                        Those individuals who have amassed worship find themselves empowered; sorcerers thus empowered can find the truly perfect form of meditation, allowing the prayers of their people to wash over them. Some few among these discover how to take these prayers and form them into raw sorcerous power. Those that do manifest a massive lotus flower of essence when they meditate.

                                        Shaping Rituals

                                        Rather than drawing willpower from his cult, the Sorcerer may enter a state of meditation for a few minutes in the morning, taking time to hear the prayers of their people and drawing in their worship. For the day, they may not draw on willpower from their Cult, nor may they do this if the amount of willpower they could draw from their cult per story is already exhausted. Instead, they gain (Cult Rating x 3) Sorcerous Motes for the day.

                                        Merits

                                        Lightning-Flame Sutra(3-dot Story Merit): This is an exercise in divine power, allowing the sorcerer to generate and manipulate flames, although flames so controlled become clearly unnatural. Most commonly the flame is streaked with small lightnings. This Merit works like the Burning Name, however it also allows the Sorcerer to parry fire-based attacks without a stunt and add the Flame tag to his attacks.

                                                Originally posted by raaabr View Post
                                                Just tossing an idea out there, since I have nowhere near enough experience with the system to actually put this into the game, but would Martial Arts as an initation work? Simply the perfect understanding of yourself, relative to the world around you, and the discipline granted by MA allowing you to do sorcery?


                                                On the one hand, I'm not sure if this even works lore-wise. On the other hand, I also have no idea if this is even balanced; depending on how you gain motes (Doing katas, I guess? Doing MA charms to generate Sorcery Motes seems like it'd be a recipe for disaster), it might be way too easy to gain them.
                                                You can have a martial arts flavor, no prob, but keep in mind that shaping sorcery is its own action - you'd definitely be pushing into potential game-breaker territory if you allow a character to gain sorcerous motes while taking other combat actions.

                                                The Soul-Perfecting Elixir initiation could be refluffed into using martial arts rather easily. For instance, the elemental Merits could stem from mastering an associated kata rather than harmonization of one's body. The key here would be creating a shaping ritual that fits the image you're looking for.

                                                I'm not all that experienced with writing these things either, but maybe it could be something like:

                                                The sorcerer draws power from embodying the principle of a style. This shaping ritual requires that the sorcerer activated a Form Charm earlier in the scene. If she performs an action that would allow her to reflexively activate the Form Charm she is currently using, she gains two additional sorcerous motes on her next shape sorcery action as long as that action is taken before the end of her next turn. If she has fully mastered the style associated with that Form Charm, she gains four additional sorcerous motes instead. She cannot draw more than ten sorcerous motes per scene with this ritual.
                                                This is loosely modeled after Mara's first shaping ritual. The idea is if you had, say, a sorcerer who knows Snake Style, she could gain +2sm on her shape sorcery action if she was already in Snake Form when she withered an enemy from Initiative higher than her own to lower than her own. If she had learned Essence Venom Strike, she'd gain +4sm instead. Those two values fall within the range provided by Mara's first ritual, and I copied the 10sm/scene limit as well.

                                                That being said, it's generally much easier to perform a combat action than instill exploitable Intimacies (unless you happen to also be a Black Claw stylist or something), and there's also the potential concern that some Form Charms have easier special activation conditions than others, so this ritual might not be balanced for all styles.

                                                I wrote the ritual with the intent of it coming into play once every other turn at most, i.e. by alternating between combat and shape sorcery actions; perhaps that should be a hard-coded limitation. In any case, I just thought I'd throw it out there as possible inspiration.
