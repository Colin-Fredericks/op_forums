        I guess I would put it that the Immaculate doesn't place a great emphasis so much on fixed nature and purpose for mortals, as much as that the social order ultimately flows harmoniously following the currents set by hierarchical superiors.

        It doesn't matter if the peasant farmer wants to use wood for a door, table or fence, as he sees it best to suit the warp of the wood, and as it reveals itself to him and seeks his approval, but it is ill if it were to rebel against its shaping and his needs. It doesn't matter so much that mortals do not stay in the same job, but it does matter that any changes in roles are at least ratified, if not directed, by superiors in the order of reincarnation.

        (This is a lot like social mobility in theory in a lot of real world societies, though the Realm likely has more competence and consistency in getting closer to realising the social ideal).

                    Oh also cool are the Breaths of the immaculate order. The breath of Sextes Jylis takes care of Human Resources, public works, and environmental management, like forest planning and the like.                                         Another thing not entirely from the Realm book, but also from 1e. The Imperial Treasury is located over several treasure manses, but centrally is a sprawling compound of buildings, some dating back before the founding of the Realm. In there are winding corridors with back rooms full of paperwork dating back centuries, and ungodly secure vaults, some so secure that nobody currently alive can even open them.

                                        There's a bunch of weird stuff there too, bound spirits and sorcerous enchantments.There's also a parliament of ghosts that convenes in one of the rooms sometimes on calibration.


                                        The organization itself is in trouble. People aren't keen to pay taxes to the government when they could skim it off the top for themselves in preparation for the coming war. So you have people rushing around trying to plug holes and figure out where all the damn money is going, fending off the depredations of the houses trying to gouge them out, and all set against a backdrop of a place so large, strange. ancient and labyrinthine that you could easily round the corner smack dab into a ghost flipping through the old ledgers from her past life, or have to walk past a vault door covered in jade eyes that follow you with unnerving precision.
