    I think a lot of your quasi-historical fantasy anime of the last few years can give a good sense for the structure of a story that could fit into a given part of Creation (or even a number of parts in sequence); a land in some kind of crisis that brings a diverse group together and sends them roving about trying to solve such crisis in the face of complex issues, varied personages and powerful institutions. Stuff like Heroic Legend of Arslan, Yona of the Dawn, Lord Marksman and Vanadis, Magi, those kinds of things. They might be particularly good for helping one imagine how to chain the locations of the sandbox together with common themes or recurring images. I think once one has a sense for how the things look at the surface level, it's not necessary to make them too intricate; for any given locale that the players visit, you can modify details in the appearance of the place and the names and face of the figures, and take advantage of Quick Character improvisation to throw up local soldiers and their masters, and the thing is kept from being too expansive by the sense of what problems people are facing regardless of where you go, and the idea that whatever the challenges the players overcome, they're breaking things down such that new things can prop up (probably things that might require a revisit at a few points).

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
    Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

                        The circle-ties idea can work I think, if you are clear that a positive tie can be basically any non-antagonistic emotion. Characters might be friends, co-workers, competitors, lovers, partners in crime or anything else. You can also encourage background that doesn't necessarily result in ties, like how Solar A never quite forgave Solar B for the losses they took fighting off the realm in Town C, or characters might have had romantic feelings at one point that have puttered out, and others.

                        A shared story at starting is a good idea, which may or may not require intimacies, but history is important for PCs.

                                    My group currently was not required to have a positive intimacy towards each other, but they are all close friends IRL, so most of them put it on their character sheets anyway. For prepping for me group I did a first age group prologue where they were invited to a dinner with some fellow solars that did not go so well, and they fought their way to Rathiss, where they all eventually died due to Sidereal backstabbing. I brought the group together by using their individual prologues for gold faction maneuvering, they all were basically instructed to meet in the same village, and each of them was promised something that would interest them. At this point I deviated from Exalted and had the group come up with Interludes from Savage Worlds. They told me how the meet, what mini adventure they had together, etc. It seemed to work well for my group, and gave me more RP fodder for down the road.

                                                I like having the characters being linked... but, honestly, I find that in practice it doesn't work that well. Things that happened before the game just don't have the same impact. And frequently characters quickly evolve into something slightly different from what they're supposed to.
                                                If it doesn't seem believable from the characters' interaction, intimacies don't tend to hold up.

                                                For example, I played a game where me and my friend's character were good friends in the past, and he saved my character's life. But then, when we played the game, that character constantly insulted my character (and everyone else) and her beliefs and culture (mostly out of ignorance), and crapped up a lot of our missions and goals. It made it a bit difficult to see why they were best friends.

                                                Similarly, when I first ran Exalted, I gave all the Lunars a Solar mate amongst the PCs. With some Lunars it worked well, with others it made literally no difference whatsoever. Just having an intimacy written on their sheet didn't make the player care about the other character.
