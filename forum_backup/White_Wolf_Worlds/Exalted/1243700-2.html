    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post

    I forget, how does it work and how do you see it factoring into this tactic?
    I'm not the person this is asking, but my analysis of the corebook sidereal excellency and clashes:

    Sidereal excellency is 1m per die or target number reduction, max of (essence) dice and 3 target number reduction. 2m per static number increase, max (essence).
    At >5 die pools, target number reduction is more efficient than dice adding, and for most possible essence/attribute/ability/other sources of dice the cap is higher than solars. (At e3+ the sidereal always has a higher cap for expected successes, at e1 or e2 a near max attribute+ability can beat the sidereal if there aren't many dice from stunt, speciality, accuracy, or similar coming in.)
    The static excellency, on the other hand, has the same efficiency as everyone else, and at low essence a pretty poor cap.

    This lets them do much better on rolls than other splats before charms, and often even after charms, especially on things like withering attacks with high-accuracy weapons. Clashes put a lot of focus on winning a single roll, can get a lot of non-charm dice from accuracy and dual wielding, and compensate for the sidereal's poor Defense cap.
    An e1, dex 5, ability 5, specialty sidereal dual wielding light artifact weapons in a clash with a 1-point stunt and max excellency averages 16.8 successes. And only spends 4m for this
    A solar under the same conditions averages 15, excellent strike brings that up to 16.556, for 10 or 11 motes.
    At higher essence this gets even more extreme - e5 gives 20 average successes for 8m.

    Because of this, a clash-specialized sidereal can win lots of clashes, even against other clash-specialized enemies, and won't need to spend as many motes.

    On another note, do people think Snake Form's penalty to attacks applies to clash attacks?

                Mantis Style allows no armor, and its only soak enhancement requires you to attempt parrying, so it doesn't have any precaution against losing a clash. The form and a pre-form Charm boost your parry defense, and the Style allows you to use a seven-section staff which has a medium-weight class Defense bonus of 1.

                This could actually incentivize the martial artist (when not clinching) to delay until after his opponent acts, without clashing. She lets her opponent’s attack crash against her Iron Arm Block and suffer an onslaught penalty that makes attacking him easier.

                (EDIT: A better idea is the Mantis stylist using a Full Defense to make sure Iron Arm Block works in the first place, and then attacking first in the next round before the attacker’s new onslaught penalty disappears. It’s the same gamble, but less risky and less expensive. Though delaying does have the advantage of letting the martial artist do anything else if her opponent decided to not attack at all.)

                However, all of the Style’s other weapons are light-weight, with no Defense bonus. With the exception of nunchaku, they’re all easily paired for a Clash bonus, and players probably have little difficulty getting most storytellers to let them pair nunchaku, thanks to TMNT’s Michelangelo and RWBY’s Sun Wukong.

                Clinching penalizes your opponent’s attack dice, and Mantis Style really wants to clinch. Robert Vance opines that savaging attacks don't need to be unarmed, as the rules as written dictate, so a Mantis grappler can clash pairs of her Style's weapons against the razor claws of a Tiger grappler.

                Crushing Claw Technique is a nice aid for winning a Clash (for which Tiger Style has no answer), but not with a withering attack. That’s fine, since Mantis doesn’t have a trick that depends on denying the opponent a chance to act between attacks.

                If your opponent is intimidated by your delay and successfully disengages rather than attack you, use Leaping Mantis Technique to chase them down. I don’t think you can delay the reflexive attack, and there’s generally no reason to delay with the alternative, unblockable attack.

                If they instead resort to a Full Defense to deny you a clash, Crushing Claw Technique can help you get around that with a normal attack, as can using a nunchaku or seven-section staff with its Flexible tag. Alternatively, you can use your action to Aim now, for your attack or clash in the next round.

                If your opponent is currently out of reach, there’s no reason to delay using Leaping Mantis Technique.

                Grasping Mantis Defense is great for when you’re the one at the Initiative disadvantage and you can’t trust your Iron Arm Block to protect you. Celestial Exalted who have the Initiative advantage also have the option of making an immediate withering attack to build up more Initiative, and then using GMD to clash when their slower opponent attacks. But that relies on him attacking instead of anything else. Though, again, Mantis Style has options for responding to a Full Defense or Disengage action.

                There’s no reason to delay using Joint-Locking Technique.

                You can Clash with Joint-Breaking Attack, if you really need to neutralize your opponent’s action and are confident you will win. If you’ve already broken one arm of someone who relies on a two-handed weapon, you should be pretty confident about clashing, whether you’re clinching or not.


                In conclusion, Mantis is much more flexible about parrying or clashing for defense than Tiger Style, having tools for both and leaving you to decide which is best to use at any given moment.

                It should be noted that even if you consistently clash your opponent rather than parrying, Mantis' parrying tricks are still extremely useful for protecting you from a second opponent who is also attacking you, especially while you are clinching the first opponent.

                                                            The serpent who holds herself in readiness to strike one opponent forfeits the benefits of her full evasiveness against the attack of a second opponent. In system terms, delaying your action to clash the slower of two opponents means that you cannot use the full power of Serpentine Evasion against the attack of the faster opponent. The Charm is, of course, completely useless in a clash.

                                                            As long as your Initiative remains higher than your target's despite the cost of delaying, Snake Form will penalize your enemy's clash dice by -3, and Striking Cobra Technique will reward you for succeeding at a clash attack. Striking Cobra Technique is more forgiving in terms of Initiative comparisons when used by Solars, or after any Exalt delivers the Essence Venom Strike (which will also ensure Snake Form inflicts the full penalty).

                                                            Snake seems incapable of grappling.

                                                            Snake's best weapons for clashing are the paired hook swords, which have the disarming tag (and taking away your opponent’s weapon is great for clashing), and also provide the +1 parry bonus of medium-weight weapons. The Style’s other weapon, the seven section staff, is similarly defensive. It also has the flexible tag for striking people who choose to Full Defense instead of clashing you.

                                                            Snake Style allows light armor, and Charms such as Snake Form Essence Fangs and Scales Technique, and Countless Coils Evasion provide further precaution against failing a clash.

                                                            Since Countless Coil Evasion doesn't care which form of defense you used, Snake is extremely flexible in regards to whether you want to dodge, parry, or clash, with improved soak to survive failing at any of them. However, Terrestrials are encouraged to dodge (and dodge successfully) if they want to use Crippling Pressure-Point Strike apart from Snake Strikes The Heel.

                                                            Snake Strikes The Heel allows the martial artist to defensively clash, when she is at the Initiative disadvantage. Snake Form helps with a -1 penalty against the opponent. If the martial artist delivered Essence Venom Strike to the opponent, then Snake Form inflicts the full -3 penalty (plus the -2 penalty from the venom itself), and the martial artist can use Striking Cobra Technique and (if a Solar) the Mastery bonus of Essence Fangs and Scales Technique.

                                                            Celestial Exalted can instead use Snake Strikes the Heel while at higher Initiative, to intercept an attack the opponent makes after their own.

                                                            Because Crippling Pressure-Point Strike is a gambit rather than a withering attack, it doesn't lend itself to the same "two action combo" as something like "Stillness-of-Stone Atemi and Avalanche Method", and Dragon-Blooded are greatly restricted in when they can even use it and cannot use Withering Paralysis Venom. But Celestial Exalted and Immaculates who deliver the Withering Venom Paralysis don't have to worry about their victims fully rising from prone even if they succeed at the action, so the window of opportunity is large.

                                                            Using Nerve-Deadening Venom Atemi against dual wielders and two-handed weapon users can really help your chances of winning clashes, especially with Snake Form.

                                                            Blinding Fang Strike creates the smallest window of opportunity, and I wouldn't allow its penalties to stack with those of Snake Form, since the opponent cannot be hypnotized by movements they cannot see.


                                                            Uncoiling Serpent Prana. You can use this to clash projectile attacks out to short range. Medium range, if you're a Celestial Exalt who has Essence Scales and Fangs Technique active.

                                                            Your attack isn't treated like a projectile, so your bare hands and feet still have a close range weapon's accuracy of 4. A short range, Thrown weapons have an accuracy of 3/4. Archery weapons match or exceed your hands at 4/5, but at medium range, your unarmed attack has better accuracy than artifact bows.

                                                            Using paired hook swords is also an option, like always. Artifact hook swords will have better clash attack dice pools than your bare hands, better damage, and potentially Evocations.
