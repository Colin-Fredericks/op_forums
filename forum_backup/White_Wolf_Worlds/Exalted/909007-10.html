                                        Something that I think might inform assessments of the morality of the Solar Exalted and their equivalents is...

                                        I think the folk hero is a major source of the "conventional" heroic narrative that I find the Solar imagery to be constructed from, and one interesting detail I've seen in descriptions of the folk hero is that they typically start out as ordinary people who are then made extraordinary by some kind of significant life event, often harmful such as a disaster or social injustice.

                                        I consider that against many origins writers have given to Solar Exalted, and it makes me think that a common element between them might be a certain scenario of desperation; something weighing down on them that some part would want to overcome. I think it's part of what can go into assumptions that people Chosen by the Unconquered Sun will pursue some form of renewal for the world; the comfortably powerful don't (often) get Chosen, regardless of their assets or ambitions.

                                        I would not say that everything people might do from a starting position of desperation is excused or justified, but I think it would be worth noting.

                                        I find it similar to what seems to be the origin for Abyssals, and what I'm positing as an origin for Infernals, and I find it to be something that would make the relationship with and contrast to Solars more meaningful than any inversion of powers or cosmetics would, and it's something I think skews whatever their "villainous" narratives might be.

                                        I think particularly for the idea of "the Yozis only have a few Essences, so they would be discerning enough to seek out only the most evil people", because I wonder what such people would be like. It makes me think of what Totentanz described, the privileged and the arrogant and the myopic; people who are perceived to be evil because they already hold and abuse power over others. I think if such people were given such power, they wouldn't really do anything interesting with it, because there's no urgency in their lives; I would see that as a scenario in which power is used mostly to secure what one already possesses, and then sit on it.

                                        I imagine Yozis scouring through the ranks of the desperate, the subaltern, the crushed, the vengeful, people for whom being bestowed the power of a god would be very catalysing, and I think the results would be a lot more interesting and to the purposes of the Yozis as I postulate them.

                                        From that position, with regards to the subject of things like their shintais...

                                        I've talked at length about how I dislike "power" being used as a metric for characters, because it's often vague and undefined. That's related to how I've often disliked stories that describe a character as having "powers" (especially for the plural), in which we don't necessarily see them do many things.

                                        One thing I like about Solar Charms is how a lot of them can be behind an action without being visual, in a manner that unifies a turn of phrase I'm a bit attached to with an application in which it makes sense.

                                        For Infernals, as another contrast with Solars and demonstration of their own thesis, I like the idea that their growth in power would be very visual and diverse. It's still not about the acquisition of power being the main thing they're driven by, but rather that as any given Infernal progresses along their narrative, they do become more powerful and those greater and more diverse powers further enable them to pursue what they want. So to me, the potential of something like shintais isn't that they're alien or make the Exalt alien, but that they're a very visual display of how far they've come and what new capabilities they have to go further.

                                        Hmm, I don't think I'm explaining that as well as I'd like, and this post is already running rather long...

                                        Maybe I'll come back to it.
