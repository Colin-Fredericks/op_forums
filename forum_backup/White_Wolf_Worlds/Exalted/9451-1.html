                Originally posted by The Despot View Post
                @ Aquillion:

                You can basiaclly play the game four ways. Combat/Sorcerer/Crafter and Social. We are the worst at sorcery of all Celestial Exalted and the worst of all Exalted at Crafting.
                There are so many things wrong with this I don't even know where to begin.

                First, that division is entirely undesirable. To the extent that it's true (and it isn't -- you completely neglected, for example, the entire Night caste, all non-combat applications of Shapeshifting, etc) -- it's a massive problem that needs to be aggressively fought in the game's design. If the game breaks up neatly into Combat / Sorcerer / Crafter / Social, then all powers and splats are going to become more dull by nature of becoming just yet-another-boring-implementation of the same basic things that that division describes. It also leads to "decker syndrome", where a character built into one of your rigid divisions becomes useless outside it -- the Combat Guy forced to sit and watch while the Craft Guy crafts; the Sorcerer forced to sit and watch while the Combat Guy fights, etc. These are not desirable things! Sometimes a degree of decker syndrome is inevitable -- it can be hard to fit crafting cleanly into the same paradigm as combat, sure -- but it's not desirable to have rigid divisions like that.

                In any case, it's important that the gameplay be built around thematics rather than forcing thematics to align to some ridiculous rule of gameplay. As a Lunar, you're a master of shapeshifting; your powers are what shapeshifting can do, as well as certain things derived from the thematics of your type of shapeshifting even if they're not the same thing, themselves. Then you find ways of playing the game that explore what this can do. You don't start by saying "well, these are the four ways to play the game, let's make the silver-tinted versions of them." You find the Lunar was of playing the game (which may not involve, for instance, any sort of crafting, and which may not quite match the Twilight approach to Sorcery), and you develop them into a full splat.

                (What kind of things do you develop, beyond combat and social powers? Well, that's sort of the idea of an entire book, but let's just start with "utility" and go from there. Utility is a lot of things, but the important part from your stiltifyingly rigid breakdown is that they're powers that, among other things, let you bypass combat without having to fight. Transportation and disguise powers are a big part of this, as are senses; some of it can move into more complicated magical effects, which Sorcery has but doesn't have a monopoly on. You blithely described Lunars as the worst Celestial Sorcerers, but the role of "sorcerer" -- to the extent that it's a role -- isn't just about knowing capital-S sorcery, it's the stupifying big role of "everything magical that isn't directly related to one of the other things"; and there's no reason Lunars can't have a lot

                Second, even if it were real, it's irrelevant. The vast majority of Solars, in fact, do not learn Crafting or Solar Circle Sorcery (stereotypically, they fall under one caste!) Obviously Lunars cover less thematic ground than Solars do -- Solars are the core splat, so they're designed to be unusually conceptually broad, to let people play more things without having to buy a splatbook. The individual splats tend to be more narrowly-focused in what you can do with them, catering to a specific taste (but exploring and developing that taste more thoroughly.) In other words, if your options for how you can build a Lunar really did only have the breadth of 4/5th of Solars -- I don't see how that's a problem? Your build is still just your build; just like most Solars never learn Crafting or Solar Circle Sorcery, I don't see how it's a problem to have a splat that never learns crafting or Solar Circle Sorcery. If you want those things, Lunars aren't for you, but -- obviously, there's a ton of players who are perfectly happy with the game without them.

                Saying they suck at crafting and sorcery...no shit - No Moons were trash in 2E. I'm saying its about damned time they got something to play with - so they can interact with the other half of the game that they have been locked out of. If it's ***wrong bad fun*** to play a Lunar Crafter - that's what custom charms are for. Also, its the main issue that plagues the Lunar community. The degree of ideas shot down and called out as wrong bad fun on Lunars far outweighs all that heaped upon the rest of the splats combined. If they play that badly with what people want to do and expectations - just brand them with npc and shuck them into the background.
                The reason why people get that reaction is because they usually look at Lunars, realize they suck, then say GIVE ME ALL THE SOLAR STUFF!, because the Solar stuff is well-developed and it's the only thing they can think of.

                This is a terrible approach to Lunars, because it would make both Lunars and crafting more boring. If your approach to 'fixing' Lunars is to describe the five Solar castes and their approach to the game, then demand that Lunars get all their stuff (roughly -- you completely ignored Nights for inexplicable reasons, combined the Zenith / Eclipse roles into 'social', and listed Twilights twice under Craft / Sorcery; but the basic idea was still 'take the Solar stuff that was elaborated on in the Core, and give it to Lunars), then yeah, your ideas aren't going to help. The trick of fixing Lunars is to find uniquely Lunar stuff to do, not to say "well, what's a Lunar way of approaching the stuff that exists already?" Because we've been there and it was 2e Lunars and it was terrible.

                    Originally posted by Kukla
                    And yet... Lunars make artifacts. They do this, sometimes - or at least they did in 2e.

                    However much Lunar artifacture occurs, the rules of the game should be such that it is not implausible that Lunars did it. If Lunars, for example, were canonically responsible for a great host of artifacts, yet their greatest wonderwrights had no more capability at creating artifacts than an exceptionally devoted mortal thaumaturge, that would be confusing and weird. Conversely, if Lunars had canonically never created and meaningful quantity of artifacts, yet they had a host of Charms devoted to the task which were exceptionally capable, that would also be confusing and weird.

                    The right hand should not shoot the left hand, that's all I want.
                    Very, very few, really. There's a handful of Lunar artifacts and that's it. Compared to the number of artifacts described as being made by Solars or Dragon-Blooded, the Lunar artifacts are almost nonexistent (which is very noticeable when you realize that Lunars have been around for 1500 years and Solars haven't.) Going by WotLA and O's Codex, the artifice of the Twilight caste defined the First Age; the artifice of the Dragon-Blooded defined the Shogunate. Lunar artificers have never defined anything, because (bluntly) Lunars as a group are not good enough or focused enough at making artifacts to ever define an era through artifice.

                    That said, as I said in my post -- Lunars are not incapable of making artifacts. They can make artifacts as well as any typical super-strong, super-smart, super-dextrous magical being with a vastly extended lifespan (and, therefore, extended of time periods in which to craft.) That's still pretty good! It's good enough that a Lunar with a bit of time to spare still has a reason to sometimes make something, since there's no sense in wasting that potential; it's good enough that there's no reason for Lunars to neglect the option to do so. If a Lunar wants to devote a lot of time to it, they can even make something pretty impressive.

                    It's just that, to them, it's a frivolity -- if you look at the described Lunar artifacts we have, they're almost all objects of sentimental worth, the kind of thing, say, that a swordsman who practices whittling as a hobby might make in his free time, or the kind of thing a deeply-devoted spouse might weave as a present for their lover. These things can still be marvelous to behold! But they're not... a big deal, in the grand scheme of things, and they've never been treated as that. There are Lunars who are capable of crafting if they choose to spend the time to do so, but there are no Lunars whose magic expresses itself, primarily, through their craft, because that's just not how Lunar magic works. There are no Lunars capable of competing with a dedicated Solar or Alchemical artificer, at all. Lunars can be unnaturally fast or strong, and they're magical beings (meaning that they can craft artifacts), and these things together mean that, yeah, some Lunar artifacts exist -- I'd even go far enough to say that a Lunar who lives long enough probably crafts a few things, just because hey, sometimes you need something and if you have the time, it's simpler to do it yourself than to ask someone else -- but...

                    None of that requires dedicated crafting charms! In fact, in mechanical terms, none of that requires more than Excellencies. So I don't think Lunars have much, beyond that, that can help with crafting. Maybe a few very general charms that help in an incidental fashion (like a generic speedup charm that enhances all kinds of things a bit, one of which is crafting), but none of what you're describing really requires that Lunars have dedicated crafting magic, and I don't particularly see why they would, so I'm inclined to say... they probably don't. There are craft-focused Solars or Alchemicals who can make magical artifacts staggeringly fast and with blinding ease; there aren't any Lunars who can do that. That doesn't mean that Lunars can't make artifacts -- it means that Lunars make artifacts using the baseline mechanics against which the setting's dedicated artificers look so impressive.

                    Which is the heart of the issue. Making anything a generic type of power or focus that every type of Exalted must have ends up stripping it of its meaning. If everyone who wants to make artifiacts can easily get their own version of the Awesome Legendary Artificer suite, then... it doesn't really mean anything to be an Awesome Legendary Artificer, because every single artifact in history will have been made by one. To me, the fact that there are Lunars who made artifacts without something like CNNT or the like is necessary for CNNT and its ilk to mean what they do.

                    That doesn't mean Lunars suck. Their talents are focused in other areas (and if you're a Lunar, you'll have spent your XP in other areas, because... there are no Lunar craft charms.) You'll have other capabilities instead.

                                            Originally posted by The Despot View Post
                                            Christ Aquillion, I've not said to take away from the Solars at all.
                                            What I mean is, you described (most of) the main areas of Solar expertise as if they were the areas of "general" mechanics which all Exalted get. None of them are unique to Solars, but if you make them all generic to the point where everyone gets all of them, you're making Solars entirely generic (as well as, well, almost everyone else), even if you don't realize it. I feel that making something like crafting into just a generic mechanic that everyone has their own implementation of takes away from its meaning.

                                            Which is what I was getting at when I compared giving everyone, without exception, access to Craft charms regardless of theme, to giving everyone, without exception, access to shapeshifting regardless of theme. There are non-Lunar shapeshifting powers where it's thematically appropriate, (Sidereals can turn into a raven; Infernals have all their wacky madness), but if you make shapeshifting into just something everyone does in their own way, then I feel you take away from the significance of Lunars being the legendary shapeshifting splat.

                                            Likewise, if everyone gets craft charms regardless of how well it fits into their themes, then it no longer means as much for Twilights to have forged the wonders of the First Age, or for Alchemicals to be the Chosen of the Great Maker. I don't feel that sacrificing these things is worth the dubious benefit of giving Lunars some shoehorned-in Craft charms, because I don't think that crafting is ever going to be particularly important to what Lunars are or to the overall experience of playing a Lunar.

                                            If you feel that this leaves a hole in No Moon competence, why not focus on other things to fill it in? I guess my real question is -- what do you want for Lunars, that you feel only crafting can provide? What are you trying to accomplish here that makes it necessary for them to have crafting charms? It feels like you can't really picture Lunar crafting charms either -- like you said, permanence and leaving a lasting mark on the world are not huge deals for Lunars, although they probably have ways to do it.

                                            I'm honestly not just trying to be obstinate -- I think that there are real, significant problems with giving Lunars anything that's clearly "craft-themed", at least if it's being done just for the sake of making sure they have Crafting Powers; and I particularly object to the idea of giving Lunar craft effects just because everyone should have craft effects. If your issue is that you feel that No Moons need some sort of special magic of their own, I think it would be cool, but the whole idea of making it something of their own would be to get away from the existing magics of Sorcery and Crafting. In general, I think most of the "uniquely Lunar" No Moon magic is therefore likely to involve making particular alterations to their body or making things out of parts of their body -- growing enchanted gemstones in their palms or forehead, etc, as a part of them or an extension of themselves rather than crafting separate artifacts. One of Monkey's tricks was to pull hairs from his tail and turn them into things; that, say, seems like a uniquely Lunar magic.

                                            But the underlying problem is that there isn't yet a coherent theme to Lunar magic that could illuminate ideas like those beyond the very basic, core shapeshifting stuff.
