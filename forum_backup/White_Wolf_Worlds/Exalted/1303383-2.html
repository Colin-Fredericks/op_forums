    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
    If a Raksha could create a domain of ice and cold in the deep South, should she be able to summon blasts of ice and cold outside of that domain, or within the domain, or is her domain just an environmental hazard to anyone who enters it?
    The domain of ice and cold is not real. this domain is 'where-ever the raksha is'. It's an illusory overlay across the world, like a backdrop flung up behind the stage to suggest ice and snow. There is no point in describing the Raksha as 'outside' this domain because they aren't. The domain is where they are. They carry their illusion with them.

    The domain is an environmental hazard insofar as it's the mechanism by which they attack people in their space. The domain is not a passive 'place' - it is a glamour-aura they forcefully exude and impose upon the reality around them.

            To me it seems like some of this swings back to earlier questions about how "mutable" Nobles should be.

            On a game level, partly this is going to be limited by the practicalities of player niche and role protection.

            On a narrative level, limits to how mutable characters create compelling stories (how much consistency of character do you need? how much does mutability serve the expression of character rather than negate it?).

            On a thematic level, the Nobles to me seem to have a dual role of wanting and fearing mutability; they are not quite the creatures of the Deep Wyld that they may have once been, and part of their themes *is* having shape thrust (oppressively) upon them by Creation. So how much does this justify that the lands they cross as they the enter the threshold into Creation become a defining immutable part of them?

            (This is probably getting off piste, but I do wonder how seriously you have to take, to some degree, in designing the Fair Folk, the internal mythology and bitterness of having hated shape thrust upon you, and striking out in bitterness against the shaped world. For players playing the Fair Folk, conventional Fair Folk as enemies of Creation, getting inside the headspace that Creation is a sort of violence on them could make them more compelling to play than simply shallow monsters? Those seem like they should be playable (a crusader of Balor as more than an empty manipulation and dramatic role, "told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, signifying nothing"). For all that I'd like Fair Folk as also unconventional allies of the Exalted to have a bit more fleshing out, along the lines I think hinted at in the early 1e material.)

            As another aside, having a quick look at Exalted: The Fair Folk, I noticed a little description of the Nobles as "a domineering class of overlords who use their command over magic and ability to shape reality to enforce their rule". I quite like the imagine that, within Fair Folk society, rather than being particularly beguiling as they are to mortals, or skillful in the use of power, they simply declare reality such that they are in power.

                                This just makes me think about how prior Editions underserved the concept of Wyld breakthroughs. That being said, I believe that there were Assumption Charms that could allow a shaped raksha to personify themselves in a settlement or geographic feature that would have Wyld properties.

                                But for how he spreads his own Wyld properties, I think it's important to disassociate him with old systems, because the important thing about Adeimantus is that the Wyld isn't coming from him. Not directly, at least; he gives people the insight and inspiration to achieve their dreams with greater efficiency and flamboyance, in return for which they build and sanctify a shrine to him, and that's why the Wyld happens. I think it's important to convey the idea that the Wyld also springs up from sets of behaviours or strange happenstances; look at how the cockatrice is born.

                                Going by Fangs of the Gate, I read Beimani-Ta as basically being his iconic panoply, in the manner of Bhadri's soul-shredding shrike, or the magical looking-glass of Maja of the Starling Song; he's deeply entwined with it, but it's ultimately just an expression of the power of his own legend of being a custodian of utopia.

                                                            But for the moment, I think it's important that Beimeni-Ta was a very spiritual, philosophical city that was appropriated by the Wyld, claimed by a Raksha who styled himself as a municipal authority and spreader of utopian ideals, and thus the city's qualities are the means by which he can spread that.

                                                            For breakthroughs, the essential quality that I would want is people enacting something that spreads mutation of a kind characteristic to the commanding noble, and makes the whole place Wyld-like. For the likes of Adeimantus, that's people with a connection to his city building something in emulation to it, which mutates the surrounding urban landscape into it, and mutates the people into its ideal citizens. For somebody more in the style of the Erlking, it could be inducing people to ritualistically hunt beasts and work their way up to other people, mutating themselves into monstrous hunters and their targets into prey that are alternately pitiful or suitably fearsome, and soon those mutations start spreading to the landscape and converting it into iconic primeval forest, changing those who enter by way of requiring them to find a role in the hunt.
