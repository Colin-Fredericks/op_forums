This is partly because of verisimilitude, which is important to me as a player and as a GM, and partly because aesthetically speaking I really hate the idea that you can sign onto Team Nihilism, get awesome death powers and pseudo-immortality, and there not be really, really powerful strings attached.

                                It may help to consider that the Deathlords and Neverborn* are coming at everything from a very skewed set of perspectives. The Deathlords were once the betrayed lords and masters of the First Age: they know full well what Solars are capable of doing and being. They've had centuries of reflection as ghosts in dead lands to tint those memories in a very cynical direction, to boot. This is going to impact their outlook and what they perceive as necessary. That, to my understanding, is where they get the sentiment in that John Mørke quote Isator dropped in earlier.

                                * I'd honestly not be surprised if the Neverborn were more-or-less not a part of the decision-making process. A search for particular quotes fails me, but the members of the EX3 Team haven't historically sounded too keen on the Neverborn as direct actors, to my recollection.

                                There's also the question of whether putting in a "control mechanism" was even in the cards. Well, aside from the control mechanism that was always there, "Choose Abyssal candidates with care." I gotta be honest, I don't think that one's to be neglected. I think there's a strong chance of everything cohering together for you, though I don't know your precise tastes.

                                You may find some interesting reading in the Abyssal section (and Deathlord section) of the EX3 What We Know Wiki. Here are some relevant bits.

                                Originally posted by John Mørke
                                Abyssals in 3e are deathknights. They are emissaries, heralds, and famulii, but not slaves. They have a Deathlord/apprentice relationship. They are not murdermachines or killbots; they do not have a Resonance which forces them to kill or kills for them. You will be able to strike out from the Deathlord's shadow. They intended you to do so, because they think that you will most certainly end up killing Creation even if you decide to be a hero. This is a legitimate claim when you consider that they were once Solars, and Solars in the late First Age were one fit of pique away from doing irreversible harm to Creation.
                                Originally posted by Holden
                                3e Resonance is a very different beast from either the 2e hardback version, or the Doom found in Shards. I'm pretty excited about it.
                                Originally posted by EX3 What We Know Wiki
                                Q: MiltonSlavemasta
                                Do all the Deathlords seek to end the world?

                                A: Holden
                                They're supposed to-- but they tend to have their own ideas about what to do with a deathless eternity stretching out before them.

                                ... Q: emeraldstreak
                                Still unsure what the Deathlords will consider a victory though. Dragging everyone in Oblivion? Avenging the Usurpation? Giving Creation the taste of torment they've suffered?

                                A: Holden
                                13 individuals, 13 different desires.
                                Originally posted by The Demented One
                                To re-rail this topic, I think I'll set that there are going to be many differences in how Deathlords are presented and conceptualized in Third Edition, and one of the effects of this is that they might be people you want to be the knights of, even if you aren't a sadistic killer or a true believer. For example, I've got a concept kicking around for an Abyssal loyal to the Dowager, but whose deeds in Creation earn them a heroic reputation, and rightly so. Instead of "serve the Deathlords" being one pole of your spectrum of play options, it's an entire spectrum unto itself (plus all the possibilities of being independent).

                                                            Originally posted by Eric Minton View Post
                                                            As I understand it, if you were a Neverborn, you would be thinking, "AAAAaaaaAAAApainAAAAaaaaAAAAagonyAAAARGH"
                                                            That's deeply and incredibly boring, if true. I'm okay with the Neverborn not just being a pack of death-inflected Primordials, and I'm even okay with (and it would be awesome) a whole thing where people are unsure about stuff like "how much of what I learned down in the Labyrinth is the true will of my dark patrons, and how much is my brain cracking in their presence and making up its own shit."

                                                            But they should still be action drivers, I think. They should have an agenda and be acting towards it, even if that agenda is clouded by pain, rage, and insanity. Perhaps that's just me; I've always liked the Neverborn, with their tomb-slumber and urge towards ending their suffering the only way they know how, to be a lot more interesting and engaging than the Deathlords, who have their upsides but who, as the thread said, have always been vaguely "Skeletors 1-13." I kept hoping word count would one day be spent on them. And I always tend to prefer beings that do over beings that are.

                                                            Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                                            I'll note that the concept of "gaming" Resonance sure isn't playing into the RAI of what Resonance is supposed to be, just like "gaming" Limit should really be a Thing since you're getting something positive from a state intended to be incredibly negative."
                                                            It's not, no, but both implementations have been sufficiently clunky that it's one of your only options to have fun with it.

                                                            The behavior mechanics should be fun. Now, this is of course one of the historically hot topics and some people are never, ever going to find behavior mechanics fun. I used to be of that opinion, before I learned to find the fun and excitement of really embracing a Limit Break and riding it all the way through to its wreckage-strewn end. But I haven't met a lot of people who find Resonance in its intended forms all that much fun to play around, and I've gamed with people who loved playing Prometheans.
