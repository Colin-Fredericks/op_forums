                Looking back at the document, there's a line concerning the acceptance of same-sex marriage that stands out to me:

                Paraphrasing, it says that the capacity to produce children that may or may not Exalt and who otherwise carry on the Dragon Blooded lineage is not considered to be something worth risking the possibility of losing the actual Exalted over. That is to say, if you're Exalted and insist upon a marriage that is unlikely to produce children, the House would generally rather keep you and your capabilities around then risk alienating you to the point that you flee to the Threshold.

                Particularly interesting considering how Epimetheus brought up, all the way back at the start of the thread, that gay Dynasts always had the option to elope into voluntary exile. The book outright admits that as a possibility, and says that Dragon Blooded families would consider that a greater loss than hypothetical children that they could have.

                I think it says something about the nuances in the position, in-character, that one of the highest duties of the Exalted is to continue their line, and complications in the values they place upon different potentials.

                Similarly, there was the matter of "joining the Immaculate Order is considered fine as a reason to not bear children, because they provide a valuable service; what does the non-monk, non-soldier Exalt provide that is so worthwhile", to which I'm now thinking that the most obvious answer is practically anything they can do as Exalted.

                Sure, it's not something that would work if it was the way that everybody thought, but it isn't, and is not portrayed in the setting as something that there is a risk of; the Scarlet Dynasty is secure and fecund, most Exalted enthusiastically follow the party line, so when there's the occasional outlier or holdout, well... they won't be free from pressure, but the amount of pressure that will be applied can be assumed to generally be less than the amount that would drive them away.

                Sorcery isn't entirely in the same boat, but I don't find it incredible that sorcery is distrusted and not wholly reliable enough that people don't look on it as an easy solution that can save everybody a lot of fuss. Using sorcery is not as bad as driving an Exalt away in the calculation of how valuable child-bearing is, but I find it believable that it's high enough to hover precariously around the bar.

                                So a thing that I don't think came up that often in this thread would probably how much the House stands to get from arranging the scenario.I mean that is the point of the arranged marriage, its not necessarily creating more DBs its making sure the Houses setting up the marriages are benefiting as much as possible. Now that said obviously the better odds of new DBs coming from the arrangement the better, but I do think that considering the number of ways to fulfill that particular obligation the Houses will be more open to other venue.

                                Now it stands to reason that most Dynastic houses will at least look into an a particular arrangement if requested to see if it works in the Houses interests. After-all a happy couple may be more amiable to fulfilling the interests of their birth house as opposed to just what they are obligated to do because of their duties. We already know that while it is not high on the priority list a Dynast's preferences are taken into account at the very least to avoid pairing up individuals that are very likely to end up killing each other. Say the one member was a general who headed a battle that got the other's Hearth slaughtered and the reason ultimately ended up as the general desiring more glory. Doesn't matter if said General would be a good benefit to the other's House that much bad blood will likely end poorly and the family won't want to get involved.

                                So I would think the question on an arranged marriage would be more what do the Houses get out of the arrangement. So like Isator brought up the scenario would a House would prefer a bad deal over losing the DB. In that case I would think there would be a lot of problems for the couple as the DB would be seen as being selfish and damaging their House's prospects for their own interests. I'm going to preface this with I don't totally have he Great House standings down aside from pretty much everyone hating Ragara because they almost all owe obscene amounts of cash to Ragara; Tepet isn't seen highly because they were basically gutted between Futile Blood and the Empress disappearing; and Iselsi officially no longer exists and hates all the other Great Houses. So let's say a Nellen scion had a confirmed mutual affection for a Tepet scion. Tepet would be interested as Nellen is a stronger house and could use such an arrangement to secure resources and supplies to help undo some of the recent damage it's sustained ergo they would probably be willing to approach the Nellen with the offer. Nellen is a stronger house and doesn't stand to gain a lot from Tepet off the bat, however the Tepet Scion is noted for having graduated from the House of Bells and amongst a few other notable military achievements. In which case Nellen would be more likely to approve of the arrangement because Nellens has been trying to acquire good commanders for the legions it recently acquired.
