                    This topic is concerned about Exalts with dependants, but I think there's also a potential and intertwined subject of interest for Exalts that are dependants, in a manner of speaking.

                    I was just reading about the grisettes of Paris; young, single working class urban women of the first half of the 19th century. Economic necessity and social restrictions require them to work in the textile factories and as seamstresses and hatmakers and shop assistants by day, but by night they would go out into the world of the bohemians. Frequent the haunts of the subculture of artists and poets of various means, become their casual girlfriends at a time when this was still an unusual domestic arrangement, serving as models verging on something close to aspiring professional muses. They were women in absolutely terrible conditions who still found a standard to assert themselves and be part of a more culturally and intellectually engaging life.

                    It reminded me of a character idea I had some time back, of a Zenith Caste Exalt who was something like one of the Great Beauties of China crossed with stories of the famed courtesans of Delhi by way of Helen of Troy. A woman of such beauty and character and insight and culture that so many who look upon her fall down in awe and want to hand her the world just for a passing touch of her favour. Who can look deep into people's souls and find what will give them comfort or the scorn that will shatter them, and they will become utterly dedicated for the privilege. She does not command, but it's not necessary to do so when the mighty gods and princes that surround her will bend all things as they suppose she wants in her direction.

                    That's perhaps on the more extreme end of how such a thing can go, but I think there's good dramatic and interpersonal potential for the Exalt as a figure who cultivates devotion in others to inspire them to proactively see to all of their wishes and needs.

                        And another thing. Apologies if it's already said.

                        I think there's potential for a story or exploration of the relationships when one of your children exalt. Like, put it like winning the lottery, or becoming the parent of a child star. One day, you're normal lady, waitress at the tri-forked inn. You work all day at the inn, serving meat buns and tea to passerbys. You live in a small town or city, where you and your child eke out a moderately comfortable life. Sometimes you get hungry, and the owner of the inn lets you take home some food for the day. You're frankly speaking, not all that wealthy, your husband having died a long time ago/ left you, and you owe it to your neighbours to watch over your son. You live in the poorer parts of town. Not that bad, but bad enough you feel worried while serving customers. Sometimes, the customers are rough, and you have to make sure they don't get too handsy. You don't get much traffic with the gods or the higher mortal authorities, and you pray that is all that happens. You pray to the gods, and you pay your taxes, and hope that you and your children pass the days uneventfully.

                        And then one day, the news comes. Your son has, in an altercation with a rather abusive/ violent guard, had exalted as an Earth aspect, standing his ground to the abusive mortal. News has come out already. Yes, you have never wanted to stand out and be up and running in the higher affairs of the elites. Too bad. You're the mother of a dragonblooded exalt in an area which venerates the immaculate faith. You don't have a choice now.

                                                            Originally posted by Accelerator View Post
                                                            I think there's potential for a story or exploration of the relationships when one of your children exalt. Like, put it like winning the lottery, or becoming the parent of a child star. One day, you're normal lady, waitress at the tri-forked inn. You work all day at the inn, serving meat buns and tea to passerbys. You live in a small town or city, where you and your child eke out a moderately comfortable life. Sometimes you get hungry, and the owner of the inn lets you take home some food for the day. You're frankly speaking, not all that wealthy, your husband having died a long time ago/ left you, and you owe it to your neighbours to watch over your son.
                                                            Well, keep in mind that in circumstances like what you're describing, in the economic realities of much of urban Creation, watching over your son means making sure that he's engaged in some form of work.

                                                            Best case scenario for that is apprenticeship in... really, any trade at all is going to be lucky, even if it's one that is unpleasant and lacks prestige. Below that is "unskilled" labour, especially the kind that is best suited to somebody small and quick: running messages, hauling cargo, cleaning gutters and chimneys, picking up animal dung from the streets, that sort of thing.

                                                            But that sort of thing has a tendency to be day labour, and will likely leave the kid with long stretches of free time. And that invariably means involvement in a gang, if only to get protection from the other gangs. That's a lot of time loitering and posturing, punctuated with short bursts of vandalism, petty theft and getting into brawls with the other gangs over turf, feuds or just to burn off some energy.

                                                            And keep in mind, of they're not in an apprenticeship or some form of schooling, involvement in some kind of gang is basically inevitable for urban kids of a certain age.

                                                            That kid Exalts? Your first concern is that his profile within the gang is going to rise, drawing the allegiance of admirers and sycophants even if he doesn't start acquiring them through violence. But violence is likely to come up if there's any ambition or pressure from anybody between the gang's still reigning leader (whom you can't possibly oust, you owe him everything) to the toadies to the new bed mate. Voices saying that you actually finally have the muscle to own this town. Beat all the other gangs into submission, take what you please from the smaller wholesalers and use the proceeds to live the high life with the businesses tough enough to not roll over for some lone Exalt. People here are Immaculates? Great, you can pass over throwing this weight around as your right for service in a past life.

                                                            You're his mother, maybe you feel some obligation to impose a moral standard on him, but next thing you know you're getting gifts of silk gowns, balsam perfumes and pearl necklaces (so long as you don't ask where they came from). From now on you're the one being waited on, and in more upscale joints than where you used to work. People who literally spat on you a year ago now start bowing their heads and scraping aside when your sedan chair passes by.

                                                            Are you sure you want to rein him in?
