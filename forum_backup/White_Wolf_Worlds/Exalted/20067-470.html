                                                            Originally posted by Raven79 View Post

                                                            That's not true. If ships were watertight they wouldn't have bilges, nor would they have to pump water out of the bilges. Not every ship is so perfect.
                                                            That's technically correct, but they still need to be at least watertight enough so that they fill with water slowly enough for a pump to effectively remove it, rather than instantly, because if they weren't then they would rapidly fill with water and become too dense to float.

                                                            The reason we tolerate extremely thin seams in a boat and not large gaping holes is still a matter of desperately not wanting them to fill with water, so they're as close to watertight as can be achieved.

                                                            ​Density is still the main thing that matters; the importance of the shape of a material is not that different shapes inherently float, but that certain shapes will more effectively contain air, meaning that the overall structure has a low density (i.e. low internal mass relative to its volume).

                                                            ​The problems with humans floating is that they are only slightly less dense than water. The addition of armour (or a decent covering of fabric) is a problem in that it adds mass without a proportional addition of volume. Hence more density, hence sinking. Hell, that's a ratio that can be disrupted by exhaling air, it's so sensitive. Adding another forty kilos with an addition of volume that can't be more than a couple of liters?
