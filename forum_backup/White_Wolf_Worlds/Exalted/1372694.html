    Mortal Martial Artists

    03-04-2020, 09:07 AM
    Do mortal martial artists play any significant role in your stories? I'm keen to have mortal dojos running around the setting. Say, that brothel is actually run by Dreaming Pearl Courtesan stylists who keep a library of secrets to build political power. This criminal gang is actually a school of corrupt Ebon Shadow Stylists that my ESS player can either defeat or take over and reform. Some wise Crane Stylists keep a tea shop and help people navigate personal crises, Black Claw stylists hang out being creepy and probably worshiping demons, and literally every other dojo is plagued by brash White Reaper Stylists challenging them to prove their worth.

    Different dojos of the same style could be loosely affiliated, but still distinct. Most of them could count on each other for shelter and support (White Reaper Stylists will probably fight each other, but they'll have a beer together afterwards.)

    The trouble I have is that there isn't a lot of mechanical difference between them. I can sort of dummy it up a bit by giving them relevant specialties (i.e. Ebon Shadow Stylists get a MA specialty in "surprise attacks," White Reaper Stylists get one in "while outnumbered") but I doubt player characters would even know the difference. Maybe I could make up some custom merits, or figure out some way to give them very limited access to charms? Do any of you have better ideas?

      "Do mortal martial artists play any significant role in your stories?"

      3E? No.

      There is, explicitly, a group of mortal immaculates who have helped out the PCs -- but it's a very minor, background role. Your ideas seem cool though -- maybe next arc?

      "The trouble I have is that there isn't a lot of mechanical difference between them."

      I think narrating it is the best way forward. Describe attacks using the description of the charms from that style (but don't use the mechanical effects).

      For example, check out the rules on wielding two weapons on page 586. You could totally have a mortal Dreaming Pearl Courtesan stab people in the face with tea cups, whilst mechically modeling it as an unarmed attack. Have the Crane Stylists all flurry Defend Other on each other along with Social Influence to stop the fight. Likewise you can have your Ebon Shadow Stylists drop smoke bombs and try to establish stealth in combat, and stunt their attacks as targetting the weak points in their foe's armor (no mechanical effect). Black Claws who start fights then loudly shout "I didn't start this fight, but I'm going to finish it!" or "Sure I started it, but I'm only trying to protect the one I love!"

      NSFW: White Reaper takes on four Earth Dragon Stylists. Is rescued by a Dreaming Pearl Courtesan master.

                                                  So, I’ve said something like this before about the Mortal Terrestrial Martial Artists of previous editions but for the most part, the charms themselves didn’t really do very much.

                                                  #1 Having charms to obtain shut down the part of people’s brain which would in any other situation be going “why are so many of these villagers (ability) 4? that’s so unrealistic!” and replaced it with “hey, it’s wuxia! I don’t have to explain shit!”
                                                  #2 Most TMA styles had charms which utilised underhand strategies such as ambush or poison. Using essence-based powers to do this made people more forgiving of these tactics than if say the inn keeper poisoned their character's tea.
                                                  #3 Before magic became involved 2e had no time at all for characters who fought without weapons or armour. A lot of TMAs were countering weaknesses created by their own equipment restrictions.

                                                  As such, I'm pretty partial to a solution like this for mortal martial artists when you don't want something like Mist the Eternal Revolutionary or Ku Nenaveya.

Even then, it doesn't match Martial Arts because there's only one set of Lore charms with a restriction on how you use them, which is the polar opposite of 10+ MAs with mechanical incentives to use different combat philosophies.

                                                          I don't get where this comparison was supposed to go. Moving on:

                                                          If the only practitioners of martial arts were the supernatural ones, the practice as a whole would constantly be in danger of dying out.
                                                          I mean, lost techniques from a bygone age of heroes that have to be rediscovered in the present is a staple of Wuxia. In Universe, Tiger Style and Ebon Shadow are Supernatural. They aren't just another type of kickboxing. Mortals, with perhaps a handful of rare exceptions, literally cannot do them. There could be 100 dojos teaching mundane Tiger Style and offshoots waiting for a prodigy to come and unlock the supernatural side of them (another common Wuxia trope), but there's no reason they would be dying out.
