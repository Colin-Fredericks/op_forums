                            It seems like most of you would rather forgo some god-given regeneration in favor of keeping Kasai entirely sane. Solid choice.

                            --------
                            A long moment passes, and you shake your head.

                            “< I am sorry, Corbitant, but no. I am not comfortable committing cannibalism for the sake of revenge, regardless of what power we could get from it. Your people are dead and gone, and desecrating our souls will not bring them back, no matter how much closure it will bring. The Anathema is dead, that is enough.>”

                            “< But we…. !>”

                            “< No. You may do as you wish, but I will not partake.>” Drawing in a deep breath, “< Instead, I will make sacrifice to the Horned Huntress, as is Her due as the patron of hunters. This was a hunt, and I am willing to bet Her help is the only reason we survived, much less won.>”

                            “< …. Not that I am complaining, but what do you mean?>”

                            “< During the fight, I did as you said, and ran for the altar and the sacrificed weapons. They struck with more violence than I ever could, and burned the Anathema at a touch. Two arrows were enough to bring her down to our level, and I finished her off with my spear. That …… wasn’t natural, and someone was interfering. I prefer to think that it was Luna.>”

                            Slowly, agonizingly, you make your way to your feet, using the grotto-wall and climbing as much as standing, and look around. “< Therefore, in thanks and deference, I plan on re-consecrating this sacred space and sacrificing both the heart and my spear, or try to at least. I am no priest.>”

                            ‘< What ritual are you thinking of using?>”

                            You shrug, before remembering that Corbitant can’t see. “< I dunno …… the ritual for a successful hunt, I guess? The one where you cut out the heart of the animal and place it in a pool of water? That is the only Hill-Tribe ritual I know for hunting, and the Immaculate Faith prohibits the veneration of the Incarnae……… The one thing I do know, however, is that I need to change into dry clothing, before this cold sets in any further.>”

                            He shrugs weakly, “< I’ve got a wool cloak, if you want it….>”

                            “< No, you wear it. I had one, it must have gotten torn off in the fight. It’ll be around somewhere, and I need to find my weapons anyway.>”

                            After stumping around for a few minutes, you find both your weapons and your cloak. Your weapons are unharmed, barring a few scratches and nicks, and your cloak, aside from a few small rips (thank the gods for Hill-Tribe craftsmanship!) is warm and dry. Before throwing it around your shoulders, you peel off the ragged remnants of your armor underpadding. Soaked with blood and water as they are, keeping them on will do more harm than good. Naked, shivering in the mountain cold, it is almost by accident that you stumble over a nest of sorts behind the altar.

                            Tucked behind and partially below the slate slab, is piled a truly-staggering amount of clothing. Most of it is bunched or rolled into a crude bedroll, but other pieces, along with what appears to be pieces of armor and weapons, are neatly folded underneath human skulls, gleaming dull-white in the moonlight. Several of the skulls are disconcertingly small, and you turn away in repugnance. Pulling at the piled clothes and muttering a quick apology to the assembled dead for disturbing them, you eventually find a jacket and trousers that will fit, as well as other strips of cloth to use as bandages and a sling for your arm.

                            Dressed in warm and dry clothes, you start to feel a little better (relatively speaking, of course.), and it comes time to actually get to work.

                            The ritual you are going to perform was one your grandfather taught you long ago when he took you hunting, and it is pretty basic, comparatively speaking. When the hunter is successful at bringing down their quarry, they must show proper thanks and reverence to the prey for giving up its life so that others may live. The hunter cuts off the head of the animal and suspends it in the crux of a tree, preferably oak. The heart, along with the killing-weapon or a facsimile, gets laid in a calm pool of water (preferably, a sacred well, but any pool the moon shines in is acceptable), and a prayer of thanks is given to the Horned Huntress. Pretty simple, and it only takes a few minutes. This is the one religious ritual you maintained with any regularity, performing it even when in your father’s house, although not when he was present (he was a comparatively-devout Immaculate, one of the few things the two of you couldn’t see eye-to-eye on)

                            It isn’t the ritual you are unsure about, but the “setting”. While the Hill-Tribes are largely not concerned about ritual cleanliness (they eat the raw hearts of animals, for godssakes!), the Immaculate Faith sure is (practitioners are expected to bathe and come to prayer-sessions in clean clothing, ritually rinse the hands and mouth with blessed water before entering a temple, and dead bodies are ritually unclean, for starters), and despite your irreverence to the philosophy …… they might have a point. While this might be a shrine to the Argent Madonna, this grotto reeks of blood and death, and you can almost feel the spirits of the dead crowding around you (something has to explain this miasma of fog clouding your thoughts!).

                            “< Corbitant, do you mind helping me move these bones to the sacred well? We need to dispose of them before we can start the ritual, and it isn’t like either of us can dig graves or build cairns.”>

                            He hitches back the folds of his cloak, “<… Let me do it. They are my people, I should be responsible for them. You should sit for a while, anyways, you are the badly-hurt one, not I! >”

                            Sinking to the ground, almost glad Corbitant can’t see how weak you are, you lean back up against the wall. You don’t remember closing your eyes, even for a moment, but you abruptly find yourself getting shaken awake, quite roughly in fact.

                            “.. Wazzat?”

                            “< Kasai, don’t go to sleep! With your wound, you might not wake up!>”

                            Groggily, you look around the chamber, seeing that the floor is cleared of all bones. That must have taken him a while, especially without the benefit of sight.

                            “< How…. How long was I out?>”

                            He shrugs, “< Long enough for me to clean the floor and say a few prayers. An hour, maybe? It’s hard to tell. I didn’t realize you were asleep until a few minutes ago.>”

                            Shaking your head in effort to clear the fog (and largely failing), you climb to your feet again. Your head throbs, and you are vaguely irritated. Just what were you doing again?

                            “<Are you ready to start, Kasai?>”

                            “<…. Yeah.>”. Passing a hand in front of your face, you stumble over to the corpse of the Lunar Anathema, sprawled over the floor. Kneeling down beside the body, trying to avoid most of the tacky blood, you make the mistake of looking at her face. In this state, she looks entirely human, eyes closed and fox-fangs hidden. That doesn’t stop you from remembering the sheer hatred on her face when you fought, nor the child-sized skulls behind the altar, and you suddenly shiver, at once fully cognizant of just how lucky you were to be able to kneel here, injuries nonwithstanding…….. you need to stop doing things like this, or at least think things through before jumping into them! You want to help people, but if you get eaten by an Anathema, what will you be able to do then?

                            In the middle of your glimpse into your own mortality, you suddenly realize that you won’t be able to complete the ritual as you are, not without help.

                            After all, in order to butcher game and remove the heart, you need to have functioning hands. Your right arm is bound up in a sling, and some of the fingers on your left hand are broken. You can’t hold a knife, much less crack the ribs open.

                            Turning your head (and wincing at the sudden lightning-bolt of pain that arcs around in your skull), you call out, “< Corbitant, can you come over here? I’ve got a problem.>”

                            Shuffling over, directed by your voice, “< What is the issue?>”

                            “< My hands are injured, and I can’t hold a knife. You are going to have to cut the heart out. Will your lack of sight prevent you from doing that?>”

                            You are surprised to hear him laugh, “< Boy, I have been butchering game for longer than you have been alive. I don’t need to see.>”, and he pulls out a sheath knife.

                            And he is correct. Despite his lack of vision, he quickly and cleanly dresses the Lunar, cutting open the chest cavity and after yanking aside broken ribs, pulls out the heart. Removing your spearhead from the spine takes a bit more effort, and he has to put all his body weight into tugging on the broken tang to work it free of the bone. At last, with a mighty heave, the steel breaks free with a snap, and he lays it down beside the heart.

                            Turning to you, wiping his hands on his trousers, “<Whenever you are ready.>”

                            Taking a deep breath, you shuffle over to the edge of the pool on hands and knees, pulling the sacrificial objects with you. Here, right by the water’s edge, the air is cold and your breath fogs, and the full moon reflects pristinely in the black water, almost to the edge of the pool. For a moment, the pool and reflected moon look like nothing less than a great eye emblazoned in the heavens, and you can’t look away, even if you wanted to. Something is going on, either a god is watching, or a severe head injury is acting up, and you have to take a moment to stop your hands and voice from trembling..

                            Lifting both the broken spearhead and the bloody heart in front of you, you chant, slowly at first, then more strongly as you gain confidence.

                            “ Great Luna, Horned Huntress, Argent Madonna, patron of hunters and the hunted, the wilderness and the Wyld, the lost and the forsaken, hear me!

                            My companion and I have slain a great and terrible monster, here in your sacred shrine, using weapons you yourself have touched! In thanks, both for our survival and for your assistance, to you I sacrifice the heart of the terror, and the weapon that dealt the killing blow.

                            The heart, the power and strength, is yours, as is only right and proper, and so that both the soul of the Anathema and the souls of the people it took might be guided safely to the underworld, by the glow of your majesty.

                            The spear ….. it was a parting gift from my father, and has served me well in battle for many years. It ended the reign of terror of the Anathema before you, and as such is well-suited for sacrifice under your purview. May you find it as useful as I did.”

                            Clasping your hands before you, ignoring the pain, “Great Goddess, I re-consecrate your shrine, cleaned of corpses and death, so that it may be used for its intended and rightful purpose.”

                            Reaching down beside you, you pick up the spearhead and hold it up to the moon overhead. Gently, you let it slip down into the water, sliding it beneath the surface without even a ripple. Doing the same with the heart, you watch as the entire pool is stained crimson, and when the dye reaches the edge of the pool, you press your hands to the floor and bring your forehead down upon them.

                            Your heart beats once, and again. You raise your eyes from the floor….

                            Nothing. Other than the moon reflecting in the pool, and the shuffling of Corbitant behind you, the grotto is silent and empty, and for a moment you are slightly surprised, and then feel vaguely foolish. Did you honestly expect anything to happen? Luna already helped you out once today, was She going to come down from the sky and thank you personally, maybe offer a lift back to town? Not bloody likely.

                            Grumbling to yourself, you clamber back to your feet, grabbing onto the longer section of your broken spearshaft for assistance.

                            Corbitant looks up, “<That was it?>”

                            “< That is about as much as I know>”, you admit through gritted teeth. “< If Luna doesn’t like it, She can write a letter or something. All I know is: we can’t stay here. Can you cut off the head?”

                            He grunts, “< I was just thinking the same thing. To me, it makes no difference whether I leave at night or during the day, I can’t see regardless. But you …. You can’t fall asleep, at least until we have a medicine-man look at you……>”

                            Sighing, you mutter almost to yourself, “< This is going to suck.>”

                            And it does. Even with walking and balancing-aids (Corbitant and the spearshaft), and with a full moon rising bright and brilliant over the mountains, your passage back to the Paskamansett village is slow, and the term “arduous” only touches at the difficulty.

                            The trail is rough, rougher than you remember, and you stumble constantly, threatening to spill both yourself and Corbitant to the stone-strewn earth. Your side, head and hip ache ever more with every step, driving you to tears at several points, and your vision fades in and out with the pain. At several points, you have to stop walking , to catch your breath and let the agony in your hip fade away. It only took a few hours to make you way to the Lunar shrine from the village when you were whole and hearty, but you are so slow-going that the sun has risen several inches over the horizon by the time you reach the outskirts of the village. Worst yet, the few tribespeople you see flee before you, throwing down their tools and sprinting away, chased by your curses. Left alone, you stumble along, until (finally!) you are intercepted by a group of chanting priests, waving burning incense of red-cedar in your general direction. The priests are backed up by skittish-looking hunters armed with various weapons, and the whole ensemble is so godsdamned funny that you can’t help but laugh, even though it hurts.

                            The chief-priest steps forwards, wafting red cedar smoke in front of him. “< What do you want, spirits-of-the-dead? If it is in our power, we will give it, so long as you leave us in peace.>”

                            Still giggling, “< Well, to be honest, hot food and a warm bed would be nice to start. Oh, and medical attention.>”

                            Set aback by your demands, “<….. what?>”

                            Suddenly enraged, “< We aren’t dead, old man! We got out and kill your monster for you, and this is the thanks we get?!>” You go to untie the head from your sash, only to fall over at the movement. The impact with the ground drives all air from your lungs, and your vision blackens, constricts, until all you can see is the dirt of the path. Slowly, even that fades out, and you pass into unconsciousness almost gratefully. At least things don’t hurt anymore.

                            It only feels like you are asleep for a few minutes before you gradually rise up out of unconsciousness. The first thing you are aware of is the smell of more burning cedar. Cracking open your eyes slowly, the inside of a Hill-Tribe longhouse is revealed. Spread all around and hanging from the ceiling are bunches of herbs, and in the corner, merrily working away with a mortar and pestle, is an elderly Hill-Tribesman, the same shaman you yelled at earlier (awkward). When he hears you shuffling around, he sets the pestle aside and hurries over.

                            “< Be careful, warrior! You were, and still are, gravely injured!>”

                            “< Yeah, tell me something I don’t know…. Ugh!>” grunting as you sit up on the bench. Patting yourself down, you come across numerous bandages, lathes and casts, all covering your various wounds.

                            “< How long was I asleep for? I must have been out like a rock to not notice this.>”

                            The priest shifts shrugs, “<About four days>”

                            You stare for a moment, and then laugh. Shaking your head, “< You know, I’m not even surprised anymore.>” Still exploring the bandages and wrappings, “< So, tell me. How badly did I cripple myself this time?>”

                            “< This time?>”

                            “< Yes, this is a habit for me. So, lay it on me; what is broken?>”

                            “<Ah…. Most of the ribs on your left side are cracked, though not severely. Your upper right arm is broken clean through, hence the cast. Several fingers on your left hand are broken, as well as your nose and several teeth. I yanked out the broken teeth and filled the holes with pine-pitch (which explains why your mouth tastes like you just sucked on a tree root). There are several cuts and scrapes over your body, which were sewn up without much issue.>”

                            He takes a deep breath, “< No, the most serious injuries were the cracked skull and the hip. The hip I can largely do little about, asides from bracing it for additional support, but the skull was treatable.>”

                            “< When we brought you in, I found the crack on the back of your head, and thought your brain might be swelling or bleeding. Luckily, I know how to trepan the skull, and when I drilled a hole above your ear, a rush of blood told me I was right. Lucky for you, there wasn’t that much, so the long-term effects should be minor.>”

                            “< You …… drilled a hole in my skull?>”

                            Nodding,”< Yes, a small one. Above and behind your left ear.>” He takes your hand and guides it to the spot, where you feel a small bumpy scar, about the size of a large arrow-shaft. “< Do you want the bone disc? Many people use it as a talisman or amulet.>”

                            Mildly amused, “< Sure, why not wear a piece of my own skull around my neck?>”

                            Before he passes over the amulet, “< Now, a few words of forewarning. Both the hip and the skull may very well cause long-term effects, minor as they are. Most of the deep-bruising around the hip has faded, but there will still be pain and stiffness, probably for the rest of your life, especially when it is cold and wet. As for the head, you will probably have headaches. There isn’t much you can do for either, asides from smoking the flowers-of-hemp or chewing on poppy seeds.>”

                            “<I’ll look into it. Can I walk? And where is my equipment?>”

                            “< You walked here, didn’t you? Most impressive, might I add. I would advise against running or jumping, at least until you get a better idea of how badly damaged the joint is, but you can certainly hobble around. And your equipment is stored in the central longhall, I believe.>”

                            After thanking the medicine man, and apologizing for losing your temper with him (as well as accepting a packet of poppy seeds), you head out to find your gear. Almost immediately, you notice a significant change in the atmosphere in the settlement. Windows are thrown open, the warding fetishes are gone, and people are happy (comparatively), hard at work and conversing with their neighbors. As you pass by, the tribespeople mutter thanks and bow their heads, making you feel vaguely uncomfortable.

                            The village is small, and it takes but a few minutes to reach the central longhall. Inside, you are greeted by the chief of the village, who welcomes you back to his table warmly (almost a complete 180 from just a week ago!), and passes over your equipment. While you are eating (painfully, even though it is just broth and cornbread), he talks about how the village has been breathing easier since the god-beast died, now that they don’t have to worry about a demon wearing the skins of loved ones, about what happened to Corbitant (he was brought back to the Elder Council to report what happened), and several other comparatively-inane topics you, quite frankly, don’t really care about. One topic does catch your attention, however.

                            “< Oh, and I heard from travelers that the warchief of the whole Confederation itself is coming here to congratulate and thank you, for killing the god-beast. Imagine that; someone so influential coming to our little ass-end-of-nowhere! Hah!>”

                            You freeze, spoon halfway to your mouth, “< Is it ….. confirmed, that he is coming?>”

                            “< Oh yes! Last I heard, he was just a few days away.>”

                            …. Well damn.

                            The next few days pass by in a blur, of walking around the village and sleeping (undoubtedly helped by your ample consumption of the poppy seeds), until, all of a sudden in his usual fashion (the man can be quite sneaky, in spite of being over 6 feet tall), you find yourself being woken up by your grandfather, looming over your sleeping bench. Silently, he turns to the shaman, who all-but scurries out the door. Turning back to you, you quickly see several conflicting emotions on his face, all before he folds you into a crushing embrace, letting go only when you grunt in pain.
                            He only lets go for a split-second before you find yourself lifted into the air, suspended by the front of your jacket. His lips pull back from his teeth in a snarl, and he growls, “Tell me, Kasai, were you knocked on the head one too many times training with your father, or were you always this stupid?!”

                            “….. What?”

                            “Challenging a Lunar to a fight at all, much less marching to it in its own lair and fighting it on its own turf, alone?”

                            “I wasn’t alone, I had Corbitant, an…”

                            He laughs, waving his hands in the air, “ Oh, excuse me, two people, two, against the wrath and rage of an insane demigod! I would have sent a full warparty to drive it to earth, and you just walked in there, dick swinging!”

                            “I didn’t know it was an Anathema until I got there! By that point, I couldn’t just walk away!”

                            “If you didn’t know what it was, why did you offer to go fight it?”

                            “….. You’ve got a point. By the way the Paskamansett described it, I didn’t think it was anything more than a Wyld-touched animal. Corbitant only described it in full when we got here. Besides, if you knew it was an Anathema, why didn’t you tell me?!”

                            He averts his eyes, “… I didn’t know either, not until after you left, and I asked the elders for more info. I tried to send someone after you, but they must have missed you. I couldn’t leave to come get you myself; until the Council is closed, voting representatives cannot leave.”

                            A moment of silence, then you add, “It doesn’t really matter anyways. The beast is dead, and..”

                            He growls, “No, you aren’t getting my point. Eventually, there won’t be a next time! You keep throwing yourself into trouble like this, and it will kill you.”

                            “My Oaths demand tha..”

                            “Your Oaths!” He sneers, “Your Oaths! Your father always prattled on about ‘my Oaths this’ and ‘my Oaths that”, what good did they ever do him, besides land him and his family in almost-exile in the backwoods?! Tell me, Kasai, are those pretty words and phrases worth your life?”

                            “Yes, unequivocally.”

                            “… Why? Please, explain it to me, make me understand, because there must be something I am missing.”

                            “ If I don’t do what I do, who will? If the people who would be heroes never stand up, who will help?”

                            Your grandfather is silent for a long time, and when you look up to him, his eyes are sparkling in barely-restrained amusement.

                            “That is …… amazingly naïve, Kasai. Where did you get such a worldview?”

                            Sniffing, “I prefer to think of it as optimism, myself. And I think like that almost-certainly because of the stories my father used to tell me as a kid. You know: Ryu Jiang and the Peach Grove, Honda of the Five-Dragon Fist, stuff like that.”

                            His face falls, filled with an expression of dawning horror. Pressing his face into his hands “… My grandson based his morality and worldview on fairy tales? ……. I’m never going to have great-grandchildren, am I?”

                            “Not for any lack of trying on my part!” you protest. “Besides, I haven’t exactly found the right girl yet.”

                                                That is the double-edged sword of having Marcher Lords. The borders of your realm will be well-defended, but it comes at a cost of vassal loyalty. If Lord Iysen could strip them of Marcher-Lord powers, she likely would. The clans have been vassals to Xia as long as Xia has been around, and been Marcher-Lords for almost as long. If she tried to strip them of power, they would certainly rebel.

                                                Stuff like this happened all the goddamn time, both in Europe and in Asia.

                                                As for stealing the men-at-arms from other domains..... it's possible, although it won't make Kasai many friends among the nobility. Peasants can leave and go wherever they want, while serfs need permission from their landlord (and they usually don't get it).

                                                If Kasai wants to increase the population of Jianghu, he will need to do one, or several, things:

                                                1) Make it economically beneficial to move to Jianghu. Lower taxes/rents, give out larger parcels of land, give "sign-on" bonuses for moving, that sort of thing. They are going to have to be significant (or aimed at a populace that lacks opportunity, like younger sons and daughters with no inheritance), because uprooting your life is expensive and potentially catastrophic, and most Men-at-Arms are already well-established.
                                                2) Make Jianghu a shining beacon of progress compared to other domains. If your landlord is a right asshole, who makes you pay heavy taxes, mistreats you, so on and so forth, and you hear about this new domain where people are treated nicely .... you might try to make a break for it.
                                                3) Discover some untapped natural resource and exploit it. Jianghu has large forests, deposits of stone and iron and coal, all untouched because of the remoteness of the area and the sheer ass-ton of bandits infesting the region. If Kasai cleans up the region (and he pretty much is going to have to. A great way to train troops and gain skill in commanding at the same time!), he can exploit these resources, drawing people in search of work to the region.

                                                So on and so forth.

                                                            As for recruiting Hill-Tribe warriors, that is entirely possible, but it will take a bit of work.

                                                            Unlike the Vanehani, who serve in war because their social class demands it (due of oaths of obligation), the Hill-Tribes follow warleaders due to the leaders being successful, generous (both in feasts and in weapons, jewelry, etc), and through personal charisma.

                                                            If a Hill-Tribe warleader does little other than rest in town, eating and drinking, they will find their support withering away, as warriors leave to support a leader who will lead them to glory. On the other hand, a warleader who leads their warriors to victory after victory, but doesn't give gifts or feasts to share their success, will have the same thing happen.

                                                            The younger, unproven warriors are more likely to join Kasai, but they won't respond all that positively to any attempts to install order or discipline. Hill-Tribe youth bands are akin to college frat parties: a bunch of young men (and women!) who are out looking for a good time, a way to boost their reputations and show off their bravery and skill, so on and so forth. Both events involve the ample consumption of alcohol to excess.

                                                            The older warriors, the Chosen, are less likely to join Kasai (they are likely already married, with spouses that are wealthy by Hill-Tribe standards, and don't need to show off how big their metaphorical dicks are), but they would be the easiest to train and integrate into Vanehani-style forces.

                                                            The two cultures are ..... different, enough to a degree that it would take generations of intermixing before the two approach homogeneous. There is already going to be a difficult amount of culture-shock as it is, and the populace of Jianghu are "used" to the Hill-Tribes already, having traded with (and intermarried with, sometimes) them for years. The Hill-Tribes, to put it bluntly, are not 'used' to peace, and almost every aspect of their culture is organized around endemic warfare (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_warfare). The constant, albeit relatively low-intensity, raids and skirmishes between the Tribes is one of the reasons their population is so low.

                                                            I don't want to dissuade you from the idea, but it will be very difficult to beat (maybe literally) the Hill-Tribes into organized forces.
