    What's the word on Exalted: Essence?

    08-14-2024, 05:25 PM
    My hardback copy arrived the other day, and some of the simplifications from regular Ex3 are kind of interesting. Has anyone run/played it? What did you think?

      I just wrapped up a long campaign that ran using the Essence rules, it started up about a month after the early backer PDF went on and ran weekly until last month, so I got quite a good look at it. The quick verdict is that I agree with the intent behind basically everything, but they way they corrected actually made things worse. That being said the system isn’t unusable, jus not great.


      For an example take the consolidation of attributes: The Attributes being condensed feels very weird, like to be smart you also have to be strong. If you were going to boil the 9 attributes (a system that I have never seen anyone have any trouble understanding) down to 3 then it would honestly have made more sense to go with Physical, social, Mental.


      For a second example lets look at combat: you have the same withering / decisive divide that 3e has, but your built power doesn’t effect turn order. In fact initiative is entirely irrelevant except for which individual got the highest roll, after that it literally does not matter what anyone rolled. Initiative aside, the fact that you don’t start combat with any power means that literally everyone’s first action was always inconsequential. Our ST started just giving everyone 5 power at the start of combat to speeding things up, otherwise there is just effectively a wasted combat turn for everyone as the first round.


      The pruning of the Charms desperately needed to happen, but it got to the point where it is VERY hard for two characters who use the same ability for combat to feel different from each other, there are now just too few charms for that to work.


      Martial Arts suffers horribly in this system, with many of them being just 3-4 charms, usually about the themes of the martial art, which is fine in flavor, but the player who took all of Tiger style was constantly frustrated that the entire tree DID NOT ACTUALLY MAKE HER BETTER AT FIGHTING. To be clear it isn’t that the charms don’t do anything but that they do less than the Excellency, which costs the same and cannot be used at the same time.


      The Venture system seems really nifty at first but then it actually bogs down and gets in the way of things, and relies on so much ST interpretation that it really should just have been replaced with a section on suggestions about how to handle these aspects in your game rather than trying to provide a mechanic.


      I could go on with examples for pages, after a couple of years I have a LOT of thoughts on a ton of details in Essence


      In the end I REALLY want to like Essence but I can’t. It needs a fair bit of re-working to be something I would ever run. Which is a shame because I LOVE the premise of a stripped-down system for Exalted.

      I should say there were some positives as well, the ability for some folks to effectively "cheerlead" in combat to build power for other folks to use through shenanigans was a really good fit for one of the players who normally can't get that outside of the Fate system.

                  Originally posted by satoshi View Post
                  For an example take the consolidation of attributes: The Attributes being condensed feels very weird, like to be smart you also have to be strong. If you were going to boil the 9 attributes (a system that I have never seen anyone have any trouble understanding) down to 3 then it would honestly have made more sense to go with Physical, social, Mental.
                  I actually disagree. Physical, Social, Mental would not work very well, at least not with the current list of Abilities. The issue is that most Abiliites would almost always be rolled with one Attribute (Athletics, Close Combat, Physique, Ranged Combat, and Stealth are Physical, Embassy, Performance, and Presence are Social, Awareness, Craft, and Sagacity are Mental, Integrity, Navgate, and War fit multiple). Characters that specialize in one Attribute would tend to specialize in the matching Abilities, and the possible diversity of characters would go down.

                  In an Attribute + Ability system you want to have either multiple possible Attributes for each Ability, or enough Abilities for each Attribute that different characters with the same Attributes will have interestingly different choices (this is how it works in D&D 3e and 5e for example), or enough Attributes that different characters will have interesting combinations.

                  Instead, with the current list of Abilities, I think it's better to get rid of Attributes entirely and remove the distinction between Attribute-based and Ability-based Exalts. This is almost how Essence works in practice, where you can use your best Attribute for almost every roll. Characters will still be recognizably strong in the Physical, Mental, and Social areas based on what Abilities they take.
