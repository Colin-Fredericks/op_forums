            Originally posted by MoroseMorgan View Post
            I had been revisiting the implications and strategies of when to go for Decisive Attacks recently as well, prompted by a comment in the other thread about a couple Buck-Ogres being more challenging if you are already at -2.

            At what point is the risk of suffering Initiative Shift worth the reward on completing a Decisive Attack against a crashed target? I mean, if they live, and hit you back for just 3i then they get to reroll join battle and attack you a second time. If their charms aren't perilous, they are just as dangerous crashed as not.

            Is the answer to Delay so that you can Clash or go after them and get a double turn?
            That's the game.

            Clashing is a fantastic idea, but it's your Decisive against their Wither. So...

            Delaying to go at the bottom of the round could work, but you've got to be prompt if you expect that to work -- remember they're going to reset to Base in three turns. And it's costing you two dice of damage -- more if they successfully Wither you.

            If they're not Crashed, you can hope they Crash you and you Shift (I've done this in play).

            Otherwise, you want to either put them out of the fight with your Decisive, or be able to stack enough Penalties (Wound or otherwise) to make them unable to effectively fight back.

            And, of course, your and their allies have options like Defend Other, launching their own attacks and so forth. And Charms and other weirdness.

            Chances are, they'll have some kind of Perilous effect, even if it's just Hardness. And do you want to bet that they don't? Do you really want to risk your Decisive whiffing on something like The Tower Still Stands? (See the Ahlat vs Octavian fight if you're unconvinced of the benefits of Crashing an opponent before you unleash.)

            Plus there's a load of Charms that work better on Crashed opponents. Not everyone is going to have them, and if you don't then this isn't a factor. But if you do, they're usually worth setting up.

                I think setting a hard cap on init is a bad way to do things.
                I think this because it doesn't change the dynamic. The players who want to build to super-init will still build to the cap and unleash from there to minimize the number of decisives they have to use. If the cap is high, 1HKs remain viable. If the cap is low, really lucky witherings are diminished.

                Instead, I'd propose eliminating the 1HK. For example, make Exalted Healing provide the side benefit that the first time in a fight a -2 or -4 health level is damaged, the rest of the damage from that attack is negated. That way, a 3HK is the absolute best you can accomplish against a meaningful foe, so players (and NPCs) are incentivized to get just enough init instead of giant piles.

                        There might be too many variables to say.

                        A lot of enemies retreat in their -2s, so putting them there might just see them off. If you have a friend nearby who's also in threatening position then if you flub they can cover you and finish them. Some berzerk creatures actually add their wound penalties to attacks, rather than subtract them, or don't suffer them to their defense at all. You might also just be opening yourself up to their allies, even if they're not likely to hit you at their -2s someone else might be.

                        In general getting shifted on is about the worst thing that can happen to you, it all but guarantees that they'll get three attacks in a row, as they get their full join battle, or better, and another attack on top which can be another withering to push them even further. Unlike the ST controlled characters just doing some damage and then dying also isn't really a great option. On the other hand you're a Solar and the game is kind of designed around the idea that a Solar using their full excellency will almost always succeed at whatever action they take, so if you mess up and they try to punish you for it just throttle up.

                                                            Originally posted by Marcob View Post

I don't agree with removing an option from the players. I don't presume to know your players, but I expect it's why you got such a kickback from your players on it. Nobody likes having an option taken away for being successful.

                                                            I was thinking on this subject more, about what to do when a player hits the hypothetical max initiative,or even before, to encourage using decisive attacks, and I hit upon the idea of rewarding them for making a decisive. Not just in the damage they get to deal, but to reward them for taking the risk of reverting to base. What rewards would be viable, I'm not sure about. 2-3m per damage dealt? Dealing 5 or more nets 1wp? Revert to (3+damage dealt)i?
                                                            I'll present things from my perspective, which is basically an analogy. You're totally free to disagree with the analogy itself or its content, it's really my point of view.

                                                            The Social System is a subsystem that helps represents the social dynamic in Exalted. It's presented as an abstraction of what the characters wants, how it can be used to make them act and conversely to protect themselves from influence. But the system is an abstraction, its purpose is to represent the dynamic of what happens in the game. There are of course strategies to accomplish your social goals through the mini-game that is the Social System, but at the end of the day, the mini game is supposed to serve the story and the dynamic of the play. For these reasons, let's suppose there is an optimal play which is to have no Intimacy whatsoever, this way no one can influence you to act against anything. You still can do things you care for, but they are not abstracted as intimacies on your character sheet so you actually have an optimal mechanical play. This of course would be terrible, even if I understand any player going for this play style when it is optimal, but that's not what the mini-game wants to emulate.

                                                            That is exactly the way I see a player with total initiative domination still withering during the fight. I understand the optimal logic that is behind, but to me it puts the focus on the abstraction mini-game when the mini-game is supposed to be here to actually help the dynamic of the scene through reversals and back-and-forth play style.

                                                            Also, I would not consider giving a reward to the player for engaging the rules, at least not reward of this "mechanical kind". If I wanted this kind of mechanic, I would prefer things that advances the plot like "When you decisive damage someone, you can discover one of its Ties through the heat of battle". I mean I wouldn't add this rule specifically because there are certainly Charms for that, but I would prefer the story be the reward that another abstraction pool increase be the reward.
