    <1>The Zanichka

    It is said that ten thousand tribes dwell in the great Eastern woodlands, and only Huntress Luna and far-traveled Mercury know them all. Jinru Rose-of-Dawn believed it. After 20-some years of gathering rare herbs and animals as a voyageur of the Guild, she knew that every few dozen miles brought a new tribe… human or otherwise. She had treated and traded with folk who dug their homes beneath tree roots and folk who lived in baskets hung from high branches; folk who trained bats to gather nuts and folk who fished for pythons; cat-folk and rat-folk and parrot-folk too, tribes cross-bred with animals or warped by the Wyld. Her husband and children were part of a tree-folk tribe, emerald-skinned and monkey-toed, more at home in the forest canopy than on the ground.

    Jinru also dealt with spirits. She opened her veins for wood spiders and offered nectar laced with opium to butterfly-gods, played the flute to charm vinerunners and buried piglets to appease trufflemasters. She once murdered one of the Fair Folk, a memory that made her smile; and she once destroyed a ghost, a memory that made her shudder. Jinru preferred humans to spirits. At least some humans were sane.

    As Jinru walked from branch to branch hundreds of feet above the ground, she wondered where her own wits had gone. She was good in the trees for someone city-born, but clumsy compared to her tree-folk companions, and getting old for such adventures. They indulged Jinru’s slow, careful pace because her husband was now second-chief and because she enriched the tribe with metal tools, glass beads and brightly colored cloth traded from the Guild. Other tribes wanted a piece of the action. So did younger voyageurs. Her trading network grew.

    A month ago, Jinru’s junior associate Sinan Greengage brought word of a new tribe. A huge new tribe. Moreover, these “Zanichka” people had abundant solar mistletoe, the best drug for treating consumption. They would not deal with Sinan, though — only with Jinru, the far-famed bringer of treasures. And that was why she and Sinan moved through the treetops 60 miles from Jinru’s home, with death if she missed her footing.

    Three hunters challenged Jinru’s group. Like her companions, they were tree folk armed with spears and darts. Unlike the tree folk she knew, the hunters wore breastplates of knotted cord and slices of some lustrous brown wood that Jinru did not recognize. The Wyld had touched them, too: Instead of hair, fine green vines grew on their heads. One hunter had small white flowers in his vine-hair. Another had tiny red berries. The third had a short, sparse beard; it was dark green but otherwise normal hair.

    Jinru offered glass beads and an iron knife to prove her identity. The hunters accepted the gifts and gravely invited her to their village. She had found the Zanichka.

    As she crossed to another tree, Jinru finally identified the wood of the hunters’ breastplates. She was in a miles-high chestnut tree. The wood came from chestnut hulls… but the nuts of /these/ trees were ten feet wide. Gathering them, she thought, must be a hell of a chore with only stone and bone tools. But what a food supply! The Zanichka had more than nuts, too: Jinru saw normal-sized grape vines and beanstalks growing in soil-filled cracks and hollows of the branches. She also saw a woman feeding pigeons kept in wicker cages.


    she came at last to the village: A long row of domed huts along a branch wide as a boulevard, each hut a single chestnut hull. At the end stood a larger hut made from nutshell sections laced together with vines. Her guides asked Jinru, Sinan and their tree folk guides to wait there while they gathered the elders.

    <3>Facts of Zanichka Life

    The Zanichka live in 11 gigantic chestnut trees. Each tree supports several dozen villages. Each village consists of three to eight septs, or extended families of a few dozen individuals. The tribe’s total population numbers in the tens of thousands, made possible by an abundant food supply.

    The Zanichka wear close-fitting harnesses of macramé with nutshell plates to protect vital areas. They make ornaments from feathers, fur, animal teeth and carved wood and bone. Zanichka obtain stone for knives and clay for hearths from the ground but must travel many miles to do so.

    Daring hunters travel 40 miles or so to reach the Bordermarches of the Wyld. They may find glittering treasures such as golden seedpods or glass mushrooms. Obtaining such treasures brings great prestige to a hunter. Suffering mutation, however, brings a sentence of death.

    Vine-hair is a hereditary mutation among the Zanichka. Once a year, though at different times for different people, the vines grow small, sweet red berries. Offering a berry is a deeply intimate gesture, usually done only between lovers.

        The Zanichka [Part 2]

        <2>Politics in the Palaver House

        Chilan-Yoben Ah Winituk pondered strategy as he listened to the arguments of his fellow elders. The elder woman from the distant tribe said she had many treasures to trade — best of all, metal, which Winituk had seen only on an Eastern hunt in his rash youth. He would vote for trade in a heartbeat, if it were only up to him and the good of the Yoben sept.

        But it wasn’t that simple. Chilan-Mohinder Ix Bolcla argued passionately for alliance with the bringer-of-treasures. Last week, Bolcla had broken her word to Winituk: He voted for increasing her sept’s hunting territory; but she then voted against his scheme for sharing out the healing mistletoe berries. Winituk could not let that betrayal pass lightly.

        On the other hand, the garden society elder Chac Ah Ramanchup argued against the alliance. He said that gathering herbs and animals for trade meant less time tending the gardens, and so less food. The children would suffer for it. Winituk knew the elder’s real reason: The hunting society would gain power by supplying the herbs, and the weaving society would gain by supplying talismans against mad-storms. The garden society would lose influence by comparison. Winituk liked Ramanchup, but he also owed a favor to Chac Ix Chamra for bringing Winituk’s niece into the weaving society. In another 20 years, his niece might know the powerful secrets of weaving talismans, to the benefit of the Yoben sept. Winituk would not lightly vote against the weaving society’s interests.

        Winituk also considered Nojoch-Nur Ix Palchuah, priestess of their tree’s dryad. Great Nur could protect her tree and everyone living in it from mad-storms, so the garden society did not care about talismans. In this vote, Palchuah and Ramanchup were natural allies against Chamra. Palchuah might also fear for lessening Nur’s influence compared to—

        A spasm of coughing interrupted Winituk’s strategizing. When he lowered his hand from his mouth he saw it flecked with bloody sputum. Oh great Nur, protect me! But the dryad had no power over disease. For that, Winituk must go to the healing society… and the new god, the god of the golden berries.

        He glanced around the palaver-house. His fellow elders inched away from him while still squatting on the floor, trying not to be obvious and failing. The stranger woman Jinru looked at him shrewdly. She pressed a hand to her back, groaned slightly, and asked if the esteemed elders could postpone further discussion. She was still fatigued from her journey. Politely, the elders accepted Jinru’s suggestion. Everyone rose. Family gossip replaced debate. Winituk knew the gossip mattered just as much as the arguments as a way to suggest alliance, opposition and reminders of favors given or owed. The stranger Jinru, he saw, still listened intently. Noticing his regard, she tilted her head and mouthed, Speak to me later.

        Jinru had listed the golden berries, which she called solar mistletoe, as one of the healing herbs that interested her. New disease, new god, new cure… new visitor who was also interested in the cure? Winituk did not know how it all fit together, but he felt sure that it did.

        <3>Societies, Septs and Secrets

        Male and female Zanichka use the same set of names. They show gender through a prefix, Ah for men and Ix for women. (People don’t use the prefixes in casual conversation.)

        Septs take their names from revered ancestors. The head of the extended family takes the title of Chilan, with the ancestral name: Thus, Chilan-Yoben Ah Winituk is Winituk, male elder of the Yoben sept. Every sept has its own tales, songs and family recipes that it keeps secret from other Zanichka. Marriages usually happen between septs, the husband joining his wife’s family but swearing never to reveal one sept’s secrets to the other. Such infidelity would disgrace him to the entire village.

        Four secret societies draw their members from all the septs. Many Zanichka belong to a society as well as their sept. The societies themselves aren’t secret, but they guard their own songs, legends, techniques and lore as jealously as the septs guard their ancestral tales. Society elders might know a few Procedures of Thaumaturgy and win the title of Chac as a result.

        • The hunting society practices that important art and defends the village from dangerous animals and foreign attackers. It teaches Procedures from the Art of Husbandry, the better to attract wild game and ward off predators. Hunters also conduct most of the expeditions in search of raw materials, as they have the most experience at traveling beyond the chestnut grove and are best able to defend themselves.

        • The healing society prepares drugs and medicines, some of them occult drugs from the Art of Alchemy. Its members also deal with social and spiritual ailments such as persistent grudges between villagers.

        • The weaving society makes baskets, nutshell armor, ceremonial robes and other accoutrements. Chacs craft a variety of warding talismans through the Art of Enchantment, particularly talismans meant to protect against the Wyld and warn of its incursions. Talismans all take the form of elaborate knots of plant fiber, animal hair and feathers, hung with animal claws and other ornaments.

        • The garden society tends the vines and other crops. Garden society members may learn the Art of Weather Working.

        The Chacs and Chilans form the village council along with the Nojochil, the priest or priestess of the tree’s dryad. Decisions are made by majority vote.

            The Zanichka [Part 3]

            <2>Divine Extortion

            Nojoch-Nur Ah Naginder climbed up the trunk of his home tree, fingers and toes easily grasping bark and vines. After a few hours and several rests he reached the high, sunlit cluster of branches that his goddess Nur favored. Priests and priestesses already waited. More arrived in the next hour. Nur had called a conclave of all her Nojochils.

            It wasn’t the only conclave in the great tree that week. The Chacs of each society held their own meetings to discuss the offer of the woman from afar. Naginder rarely wondered what the Chacs did at their conclaves — beyond arguing, of course. The societies were entitled to their secrets, just as were the septs and the priests.

            At last, the dryad herself appeared, her skin the hue of bark and her head crowned with chestnut leaves. The Nojochils knelt and gave the prayer of greeting. Then they got down to the arguing… very careful, gentle arguing when they argued with their god.

            Nur was angry. The Zanichka should pray to her alone! Did she not house them, feed them, protect them from the Wyld? That this upstart god Sundrop Breath-Of-Life demanded worship was an outrage!

            The priests assured the dryad of their love and loyalty. Three who were in fruit offered berries from their heads, which mollified Nur somewhat. But Nur could not cure the bloody cough that spread through the Zanichka. The new god could, through the golden berries. Sundrop Breath-of-Life said they could be free of the disease if his little bush grew in the bark of the chestnut trees; but to grow so many bushes at once he would need many prayers. What else could they do? The bloody cough killed slowly. Victims became a burden on their septs long before they died. The Nojochils pleaded for Nur not to withhold her gifts. Her faithful Zanichka could not be asked to choose between loyalty and life!

            A priestess rose to speak, introducing herself as Nojoch-Nur Ix Palchuah. Her village hosted the stranger, this Jinru Rose-of-Dawn whose skin was not green. The stranger wished to meet the great and generous Nur. She had sent gifts with Palchuah and hoped to present other gifts in person. It was possible, this Jinru said, that some arrangement could please everyone…

            <3>Zanichka Religion

            The Zanichka revere the ancestors of their septs, but they do not pray to them. They want nothing to do with ghosts. If a ghost appears to her descendants, the sept’s Chilan must persuade the honored ancestor to be honored in the Underworld, where the dead belong.

            Only the dryads of the giant chestnut trees receive regular worship in the form of chanted prayers and animal blood. As a group, the dryads call themselves the Kastanea. While they owe fealty to the region’s forest walker, the Kastanea are for all practical purposes a spirit court of their own: With so many devoted worshippers, the dryads can afford to blow off most other Terrestrial gods. Over the centuries, local gods and elementals made their own deals with the Kastanea to receive minimal prayer — one day a year, no more — in return for not harassing the Zanichka.

            Sundrop Breath-of-Life is not one of these local spirits. This god of solar mistletoe learned to create the disease his parasitic little shrub cures. He inflicts consumption on the Zanichka. Then he offers the berries as cures… well, auctions them for prayer. For enough prayer, he makes solar mistletoe grow in a convenient tree. Smaller tribes around the Zanichka already pray daily to Sundrop.

                The Zanichka [Part 4]

                <2>Departure

                After a month with the Zanichka, Jinru and Sinan began the long journey back to her tribe. She had a deal. Sundrop Breath-of-Life had his worshippers. The Kastanea were appeased, for now. Baskets of golden mistletoe would flow from the Zanichka to Jinru, and from her to the Guild. In return, the Zanichka would receive tools, cloth, beads and baubles a tenth the value of the medicinal berries. The Guild would soon have an outpost in the chestnut trees, from which voyageurs could explore even further and deeper — all the way into the Wyld. Sinan burbled in admiration of Jinru’s negotiating skill. But her persuasions and promises still tasted foul in Jinru’s mouth.

                What’s wrong with me? The Guild profits. I profit. Even the Zanichka profit. We are not using them. Not cheating them. We’re not!

                But Sundrop Breath-of-Life is.

                She’d heard of the racket before: Cause a disease, then sell the cure. Other gods did it. Uncomfortably, Jinru knew that some Guildsmen did, too. It was the way of the world. You couldn’t blame anyone for taking advantage. If you didn’t, someone else would.

                Not to my people, they don’t.

                When had the Tree Folk become her people? The Zanichka weren’t even her tribe. Her husband’s tribe.

                My children have green skin.

                Jinru’s lips tightened. The deal was good for the Guild, but it could be better for the Zanichka. Jinru’s sponsor in the Guild, the great Doctor Alethia, knew more about medicines than anyone else in the Hundred Kingdoms. Aye, and more about poisons, too. Jinru would send the Dragon-Blooded doctor a special request.

                I have killed the Fair Folk and a ghost. Let’s see if I can kill a god.
