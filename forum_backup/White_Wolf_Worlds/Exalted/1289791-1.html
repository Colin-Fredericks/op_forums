        I mean, in 3e you can also take a mutation to represent your tell, which is obvious (although the fact that it's a tell isn't) and persists between forms. That might be an issue when disguising yourself as a human. But there's also Moon's Hidden Face, which can make mutations subtle, and in this case I guess would make the tell switch to working like a normal one.

Which I think makes it more meaningful as a character concept. It gives Changing Moons a better purchase on their identity. They are not just "the other guys".

                                        Originally posted by Roadie View Post
                                        In my experience with previous editions, the Tell mechanic has always been at best kind of annoying. This one looks poised to repeat that: it's got the same fiddly thing going on where high enough rolls can see through it regardless of shape, until a minor investment then negates it entirely most of the time. In practice, this just makes the Charm to hide an obligatory tax for characters that ever plan to do social infiltration stuff, while others just won't care most of the time. On top of that, the GM will be stuck keeping track of who has or hasn't seen through it for individual PCs whenever recurring NPCs come up.

                                        Like, what's the reason to even include it? What do the game and its themes actually gain in play from forcing characters to have a distinctive quality that, broadly speaking, either they'll immediately suppress from character creation or they'll never care about enough to interact with mechanically?
                                        The base Tell so far is P+A difficulty 7, for nontrivial characters. Think about that for a moment. First of all, it walls off discovery from random npcs, so you know the only people who you're vulnerable to people who would have mattered to the story in the first place. Secondly, Difficulty 7 is about right, in my opinion, as it requires either four 10s, a success on all seven die, or most likely Awareness Charms to trigger. True, a lot of people seem to dump into Awareness anyway due to Join Battle rolls (unless you're a Stealth-Supremo or Tiger-Warrior-Resistance Solar (based on personal experience)), but seven dice still means your opponent is above average in either Perception or Awareness ++++ cresting the mountain of physical training and form in the other, so your opponent is a true rival worthy of having their heart eaten. Third, the fact that difficulty diminishes by 1 for people who know you means it's either a friend you put time and effort into cultivating, or an intense rival / foe you also put gametime and effort cultivating..... that sounds awesome. Finally, the bonfire flare making it obvious? C'mon. You're already visible for miles. Your Tell is the last thing you should be concerned about there. You also gain back 5 motes a round in combat, so you almost have to Bonfire On Purpose to trigger it (have had a Solar game where nobody's even bonfired once, 12+ sessions in.... and a Dragonblooded game where the players consistently bonfire On Purpose).

                                        This Tell mechanic seems reasonable.

                                            To generally answer the replies above...

                                            The scaling of my players' characters' rolls assumes:

                                            It's something they're good at. With 4 or 5 characters, depending on who shows up, there aren't a lot of Abilities that aren't covered by somebody. Which is definitely as-intended. The characters who'd be covering that Ability typically have Attributes to match, so a natural dice pool of 8 to 10 is not abnormal. Plus specialty.

                                            It's something they're interested in. I didn't separate out a perception roll in mentioning it, so that applies to fighting, crafting, socializing, shaping sorcery, and so on... any task they'd engage in and use Excellency/Charms/Stunts into. I don't typically call for perception rolls (in Exalted) if there's nothing to find. So, it's natural that they'd take an interest and follow through with their best.

                                            Each character is making their roll separately. Some tasks are only done by one character, and sometimes that's keeping watch. I do make sure characters who are doing something else aren't the ones making perception rolls for instance, but if the whole Circle is searching or otherwise ready and free to act, they'd all get a chance to spot. The odds of everybody in a Circle failing to roll a 7 start vanishing rapidly as the size of the Circle grows.

                                            [Edit to add here] A roll like a perception roll tends to stand for a Scene unless something changes. I don't make players roll over and over every time some random townie walks around the corner. So, I don't disincentivize characters spending some Essence on that roll.


                                            So, yes, 7 is a really easy target number if someone is good at that task, engages with that task, and has a cue that it's time to do that task.


                                            Worth mentioning as an aside - I do make use of nothing-there perception rolls in other game settings. D&D or WoD/CoD can both benefit from those, just to bring the creepy/creaky/crawly theme to the story. I also sometimes ask for declarations and make secret rolls in other settings. They don't seem to fit the themes I'd usually bring to Exalted, because I want the players to be in control of the excellency of their Chosen. Your motes may vary.

                                                            Looking at it from a wider perspective, I think it’s a good thing. Lunars, especially this edition, are not defined by their ability to shapeshift. You can easily be an ungodly powerful and versatile Lunar and never change shape at all. I think that’s been proven by the charms we’ve seen so far.

                                                            Imperfect shapeshifting is just like a bonus on top of that, like dragonblooded anima damage except waaaaaay better. It SHOULD require charms to make that shapeshifting into a perfect disguise or allow transformation into Tyrant Lizards. And, as pointed out, unless the Lunar is trying to fool PCs or sneak into the Mask of Winters personal study the 7 difficulty is well above anyone’s ability to get.

                                                            Like a normal DB who is quite good at spotting things with percep 3 and awareness 3, with a full excellency they’ve still got a really low chance of making it.
