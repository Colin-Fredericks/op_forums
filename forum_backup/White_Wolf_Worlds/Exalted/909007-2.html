    Originally posted by Rowanas View Post
    Are Solars the good guys?
    From their own perspective, most Solars are the good guys, sure. And, from the in-setting perspective of a savage Creation where human life is cheap and human rights don't exist, many of them could be. What's a little ultra-violence and general thuggery when you were Chosen by the Most High to guide Creation?

    From our 21st century, socially-conscious, being-vegan-makes-me-morally-superior outlook? Fuck no! Even most of the canon Circle, given to us as models of Solar-dom, are horrible people.

    I've played exactly one session of Exalted, although I'm more familiar with the setting and mechanics, because A: I'm the only player in my group able to read the goddamn book apparently, and B: Our ST is a giant Exalted fanboy. I created a character in the expectation of !Heroic Endeavour Style! and !Innocent Saving Prana!, but after our first session, I'm finding that my party are... not the good guys. Maybe that's just the people I'm playing with, but we've got one devious snake-tongued trust-betraying sidereal-in-solar's-clothing, one Lawful Evil Bureaucrat, one straight-up rape-and-murder pirate and one ex-mercenary bent on killing every dishonourable employer he's ever had (does this count as good?). In the middle of this party, I'm playing... basically Jesus. Am I doing it wrong? Are they doing it wrong? Are we -supposed- to be actual Anathema, or is there simply something I'm missing?
    This is a problem of mismatched expectations, not a problem of "Who is playing Exalted right?" The only version of Exalted that matters at your table is the one your group wants to play. With that in mind, it sounds like you arrived at the table believing one thing, and everyone else arrived believing another thing. I suggest asking for a brief OC discussion before the next game to figure out what people were expecting, and what they want. You might find the players with "evil" characters actually prefer more noble PCs, but thought they needed to fit into the setting.

    Also, I agree with Croakamancer that overall your PC groups sounds like hacky DnD Evil characters than Solar Exalts.

    More broadly, though, I think this is one place 2E communicated more clearly and effectively. The passage Croak posted above accomplishes the Ex3 design goal of leaving things open, but seems to fail at conveying the design goal of most Solars starting "good-ish," as Holden mentioned at one point. I'm using quotes for good and evil a fair bit because they are vague terms with an axiomatic connotation, and that irks me to no end. One man's sinner is another man's saint.

                        Solars are big, not physically (although many are this too), but they have big plans, big ideas, big impacts, they dream big, they think big, and they go big. This often makes them look like good guys because frankly Creation is in desperate need of such. Furthermore Solars have a lot of inherited enemies that are either attempting to destroy the world, cruel and oppressive tyrants, or mind bogglingly wrong in how they go about things. So it's very easy for them to become heroes by default, not because of any inherent virtue, but because a lot of people are worse.

                                    I take a slightly non-canon view of Exaltations.                                             I'd like to say no. I would really *like* to just say that, and leave it at that.

                                            However, disregarding my preference and, being totally honest, I think the original premise is probably (from way back First Edition) that they weren't the "Good Guys" so much as *some* of them, at least, were "Good" and crucially, that Solars that were "Good" could really make it stick and change the world. (While the "Good" men and women in other groups, like the Dragonblooded, probably couldn't, because the opposition was just too huge for their power to cope with).

                                            That seems more or less the same overtone in 3e, but with more overtones of their god, the Unconquered Sun, being a "Good Guy".

                                            So Solars can be "Good" and Solars can be "Bad". But if a Solar is "Good", he or she is the "Only Hope For Mankind".

                                            (While another premise of the game is probably more, if you're "Good" and not a Solar, what does that really amount to? Who really cares? Because only the Solars have the chance to actually effect real change in the world.)

                                            Does that make sense? (The above is probably a bit full on for a total new fan. But maybe not.)

                                                To get right to the heart of it: this whole topic is not really an in-fiction concern.                                                         Commanding Essence is a heady experience. It’s not like speaking an incantation, turning a key, or ﬂipping a card. To be aware of Essence is to feel—to always feel—a new part of yourself, a part that is alive and active and burning with power. The Essence of the Exalted is as much a part of them as their blood or thoughts or breath, and young Exalts ache to use it.
                                                        [...]
                                                        If greatness is in the nature of the Solar Exalted, then their Essence pushes them to seize that greatness now. It’s neither irresistible nor insurmountable, even for the very young— but the urge is there.
                                                        [...]
                                                        the voice that speaks out and urges him onward, to glory, to victory, to more.

                                                        To a Lawgiver, channeling Solar Essence brings a myriad of feelings and sensations. It is a kind of pure cosmic transcendence that is the order of the divine.                                                             So, I've said it before and I will say it again: Exalted is not Roleplaying 101. It's, like, College Senior Dissertation Roleplaying. It's rules-heavy and thematically DENSE. Is Achilles a "good guy" the way a children's character like He-Man or Batman is? Is Gilgamesh? I could go on a whole rant about sun heroes in myth and narrative, but the short answer is,

                                                            "The Solar Exalted are heroes who embody human excellence and achievement. They must be GRAND and GLORIOUS."

                                                            I've seen a lot of wonderful answers on this thread, but I feel like the real issue at the heart of the post is that the OP had a vision as Solars as The Avengers (which is a valid way to play them) but the rest of his troupe was feeling more "HBO series."

                                                            The question to bounce back is, "Can you still have fun playing Solar Jesus in this party of miscreants?" In a group of truly xcellent roleplayers, you definitely can, ASSUMING the rest of the group does indeed go along with your desire to "fix" them. If they don't, you'll likely get frustrated and feel impotent, as there is very little less enjoyable than being the boyscout everyone mocks. At this point, you can either have THEM influence YOU to become more morally flexible, or you'll want to just reroll anyway. Assuming excellent group alchemy, you'll all play off each other and the characters will grow as people and everyone will have fun times.

                                                            Clearly the majority of the OP's group wants grittier, feet-of-clay heroes, or outright scoundrels who are just excellent at being scoundrels. Only stick with a morally upright character if you're confident you can have fun and be effective playing him in such a group.
