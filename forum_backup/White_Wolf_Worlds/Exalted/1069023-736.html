        A few Dragon-Blooded Charms seem so corner-case to me that I fear I am missing something.

        Heart-Hardening Meditation reads like a trap. At 6m for +2 to a static value, it is straight-up worse than Granite Curtain of Serenity unless you have the Aura effect to make it Non-Charm. So, why not just give it the Aura Keyword and stop inexperienced players from wasting motes? Is there another use for it?

        Breath-Seizing Technique, meanwhile, technically works, but the price looks much bigger than the effect. Even without spending willpower OR landing an attack, spending 2i to roll a single die against someone else's Initiative (and you cannot gain anything!) would almost always be a terrible idea. A 60% chance of spending 2i for no effect cannot be the goal, so presumably you're expected to be using this with a jade chakram. That gets you up to 3 dice - enough to have a decent chance of breaking someone at exactly 1 or 2 Initiative. It's still an edge case: if they're any higher or lower you get no break bonus, 1 and 2 are fairly rare levels of Initiative in my experience because you reset to 3 and Withering attacks often do more than 2 damage, and of course if the attack either misses or incaps them then the Charm does nothing. Still, it's alright... until you read that it also gets capped by extra successes. In other words, you are spending 2i (weakening an attack which doesn't cause a Break) AND motes AND willpower to gamble that you will hit with a Decisive attack by 3+ successes against an opponent with 1-2 Initiative. What? If your opponent is low on Initiative and you're confident you can beat their Defense by 3, you have probably won the fight. Why spend WP on a Simple charm to win more? Compared to, say, Searing Edge Attack (lower Essence, more accurate, no willpower or initiative cost, Supplemental, doesn't need as many successes, works unarmed), this seems impossibly narrow.

        "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                                                What happens if a Dragon-Blooded archer tries to use Drawing Lightning Style with Horizon-Spanning Arc?

                                                HSA converts the bonus dice from aiming to non-Charm successes. DLS applies the bonus dice from aiming to damage instead of the attack roll. (Presumably, since you have to expend your Air Aura as a cost of using HSA, you can't expend it to get the improved effect of DLS.) So in this case, do you add non-Charm successes to your damage roll?

                                                This all seems logical enough, I just wanted to make sure that you can actually combine effects that interact differently with the aiming rules in this way.

                                                            Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
                                                            Dragon Blooded suggests Dynasts can take Influence within their Great House. I'm not quite sure what that would look like within a family structure. Fame? A positive reputation? Would that lead to any kind of privileges, or would that be more a backing thing?
                                                            Every family, whether real or fictional, has members that all the others know of. That'd be Influence. Influence 1, the whole family has heard of you, but that's about it. Influence 3, your have a positive reputation, probably the recipient of a Minor Tie from family members. Influence 5, you're be like Memnon in House Memnon; everyone knows you and obeys you.
