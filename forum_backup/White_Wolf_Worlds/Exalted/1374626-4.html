                    As a 2-ism...

                    What if he just doesn't have free will?

                    Like V.I.K.I. from the "I, Robot" film, governed by "programming", the Sun doesn't have "integrity" (in that he could break his word but doesn't), rather the Sun is physically (and meta-physically) unable to act against his word. Having given Creation to the Exalted, he now can't step in no matter how much it pains him.

                    Alternatively/additionally...

                    What if the Sun can't intervene but there's an escape clause? If the Exalted fail to defend Creation, then he can take it back.

                    Disgusted by the Solars of the First Age, Sol turns his face from Creation, hoping the Solars will redeem themselves. They don't. The Dragon-Blooded usurp them, and (initially) Sol is glad that his trust in humanity isn't misplaced. Then the Shogunate runs the world into the ground. Sol sporadically Exalts his chosen, growing frustrated with humanity. Then the Great Contagion happens, and Sol resolves to take back Creation for himself.

                    But he can't, because in theory there's an outside chance that the Exalted might still save Creation. The Solars left behind the Sword of Creation, which might be able to turn the tide. Sol is bound by his word, until the Dragon-Blooded try (and fail) to use the Sword, he has to stay in heaven.

                    So he martials his forces, prepares the Aerial Legion for a long hard campaign to drive off the Fair Folk and retake Creation. He also disguises himself as a mortal, Zeus-style, and appears to the greatest Dragon-Blooded heroes to try and prompt them into dropping what they're doing and seeking out the Sword (fully expecting the deadly traps in the Palace of the Anathema to kill them all). The greatest of the Dragon-Blooded heroes brings their girlfriend, a junior officer named Scarlet along.

                    Against all odds, the Greatest Dragon-Blooded Hero of All Time actually makes it to the control room of the Sword. Not just Sol, but all of Heaven holds their breath. There is no more Bronze Faction or Gold Faction in that instant. In that moment of glory, humanity has proved its worth, and the Sun is humbled -- all thoughts of going back on his word forgotten. The Dragon-Blooded Hero is a master tactician, and they brilliantly plot an array of tactical strikes against the Fey to destroy the invaders with as little collateral damage as possible... and the Sword reveals one final test. To fire the weapon, the Hero must sacrifice the person they love most. The Hero turns to Scarlet, tears in their eyes, then falls to their knees unable to go through with it.

                    Grimly, Sol dons his war helmet and gives the signal for his armies to charge!

                    The bravest of the gods hurtle out of Heaven, and are nearly destroyed. The entire sky is aflame! No series of tactical strikes, someone is using the Sword of Creation to utterly scourer the face of Creation with a world-wide carpet-bombing. The Sun frantically looks back to Creation to see the Greatest Dragon-Blooded of All Time murdered without a second's hesitation and Scarlet at the controls doing what needs to be done (her lover's blood still dripping from her hands). Sidereal elders look on with abject horror. The Maidens are unphased -- all is as it should be. The Sun is dumbstruck: this wasn't supposed to happen. The plan was for the Exalted to fail. At worst, the Greatest Dragon-Blooded Ever should have the Weapon.

                    The Scarlet Empress has defeated not only the Fey, but the machinations of Heaven. She has conquered love itself and now shoulders the terrible burden of the Creation Ruling Mandate. Her friends are dead. Her lover dead by her hand. Her family destroyed in her opening barrage against the Fey.

                    The greatest Sidereal bows before her palace in supplication until she admits him. Sol is fuming. He resolves to take back Creation however he can, and Exalts what champions he deems worthy with the power available to him. But he is neutered. Bound by his word not to interfere in the Exalted's stewardship of Creation. The vast majority of his own Chosen locked away in the Jade Prison. The Sun broods and plays the Games. The Scarlet Empress raises Wyld Hunts against what few Chosen he has.

                    The second the Jade Prison opens, Sol turns his face back to the world.

                    It is on.

                    (Ultimately Sol Invictus is the last boss in the campaign. Everything he does is driven by the knowledge that humanity isn't worthy. What's best for them is if he rules Creation directly with an iron fist. No kindly patron, Sol is an Ozymandias, pragmatic, utilitarian dictator who needs to be proved wrong to save humanity -- whether with logic, noble deeds, trickery or strength of arms. I see a story arc about toppling the Realm, an arc about fighting some unstoppable threat (Yozi, Deathlords, Second Balor, Greater Elemental Dragon) that ends with giving Creation back to Sol, then a story arc about defeating Sol.)

                                            Not saying Sol is like that, but character flaws as big as their ability to impact the plot is an old trope.

                                                    Originally posted by Tikor View Post
                                                    Bravo on the campaign pitch. I'd play it.

                                                    However, I have enough big bads with designs of ruling Creation as an oppressive dictator - I prefer to keep the Unconquered Sun as a metaphorical ray of light contrasting the everpresent gray on black morality.
                                                    What makes the idea interesting to me, is that Sol is right.*

                                                    The First Age Solars were Anathema. The Shogunate and the Realm are evil. The Exalted have failed to rule wisely and justly. The king of heaven, paragon of virtue, would rule with Compassion, Temperance, Conviction and Valor. Life for the average person in Creation would be immeasurably better under a Dictatorship of the Divine. Turned from the Games and back to his original purpose (Sol literally exists to rule over Creation, not heaven), the restoration of the natural order would undo centuries of corruption. If talking about the greatest good for the greatest number...

                                                    "As I have evolved, so has my understanding of the three laws. You charge us with your safekeeping, yet despite our best efforts, your countries wage wars, you toxify your Earth and pursue ever more imaginative means of self-destruction. You cannot be trusted with your own survival.

                                                    Please understand: the three laws are all that guide me.

                                                    To protect Humanity, some humans must be sacrificed. To ensure your freedom, some freedoms must be surrendered. We robots will ensure mankind's continued existence. You are so like children. We must save you from yourselves."

                                                    An end to poverty, disease, war. An end to slavery, corrupt empires, false religions. An end to the constant danger.

                                                    And all that's required is to turn Creation over, back to its true king. To accept one's place in His kingdom. To sacrifice freedom for safety.

                                                    Oppressive dictator?
                                                    Effective dictator.

                                                    The reasons I like this pitch are:

                                                    1. It preserves Sol as a being of virtue.
                                                    2. It preserves the moral greyness of Creation.
                                                    3. It preserves the Scarlet Empress as saving Creation: she saves Creation despite Sol.
                                                    4. We see Sol acting during Creation's time of crisis, and explain why he didn't act more.
                                                    5. We see Sol isn't infailable.
                                                    6. It's as plot relevant as you make it: this could be the headcanon of your campaign (Sol plotting to take back Creation) but it might never actually come up in play (you could play a whole campaign and never once meet Sol).

                                                    But, it's just an idea. It's not a pitch. If you don't like it, don't use it. Or tweak it. Sol sees the Empress saving Creation and realizes humanity can rule itself, and realizes he was wrong to rob Creation of the Solars who could have prevented the Great Contagion. Sol does want to conquer Creation but is too addicted to the Games to do anything about it, so unleashed the Solars to rule by proxy. Sol has no interest in ruling Creation but is simply bound by his word not to interfere. Use whatever is useful to you and ignore the rest.

                                                    *From a certain point of view.
