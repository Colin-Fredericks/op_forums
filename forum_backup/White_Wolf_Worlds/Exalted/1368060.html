    Hi, is this game 100% straight and fully heterosexual?

    02-15-2020, 01:20 PM

    So in my previous The Gay vs Cthulhu thread, some of the points raised have made me ponder about something.

    The premise when I made that thread is people should stop thinking about sexual orientation when using Celestial Bliss Trick since the people that you are going to seduce are not gay people, it's giant talking tornado, the transdimensional center of the observable universe, Sauron's armor, and Cthulhu obviously...etc, they don't have the human concept of sexuality or genders.

    But when I look at Exalted, I have to wonder if they do indeed possess such traits in Creation? Since many canon non-human NPCs are clearly written with obvious gender and sexuality traits that are very similar to human, from obvious things like male demons such as Florivet being described as a womanizer or the Ebon Dragon marrying a woman, to subtle things like most romantic and sexual text concerning non-human are usually between the opposite sex, take the Sun for example, no books ever mention anything about male NPCs having feeling for him, but it does for the Maiden, Luna, or Merela (and that chariot goddess in Glories: UCS iirc). So my question is whether most non-human in Exalted possesses the same sexual orientation and gender identities as humans?

    Another question that I'm thinking about that also appear in the Cthulhu thread is The Gay Bard. So basically gay people are a minority of the population, if we assume that this is also true in Exalted, and the answer to the above question about non-human is Yes, then would gay seducers constantly barking at the wrong tree since 80% of everything is straight?

      Huh. I don't think I've ever thought about this before.

      I believe that books such as Roll of Glorious Divinity describe a state in which romantic or sexual relationships between spirits wouldn't have the trends that humans have when talking broadly in the abstract, but it does seem that in practice hetero bias predominates significantly when there are any actual specific descriptions of them. Off hand I can remember two gods described as being in same-sex relationships with other gods, Wun Ja of the Shining Metropolis and Jagalza Satrap of the Realm (can't recall if it was with each other).

      And then there's Luna and Gaia, a subject I was introduced to in terms of them both being female entities, and I'd say still counts with the former being genderfluid. The illustration in Games of Divinity shows Gaia sitting alongside a masculine Luna, and while not inaccurate even then it kind of makes me wonder if it doesn't reinforce heteronormativity.

      I think there's... a Sidereal guy who has a relationship with a male-coded god? Off the top of my head, I can't recall concrete descriptions of queer relationships between gods and humans.

      And it's rather a missed opportunity, since there's a lot of capacity to use that side of gods to represent and explore matters of gender and sexual relationships to beings with no reproductive drives, or even necessity to stick to conventions (there's description of the potential for masculine gods to bear children).

      Yeah, this was a good thing to bring attention to.

              this is an interesting question because I think that, from what I've seen, actual games are often far more gay then the game's description. The game line itself often seems to suggest or want to present non cishet relationships; I think it was actively a design goal in 3E as well, but it can sometimes seem to fall flat. Lioness has also done a lot of work in cataloguing things like how the Realm isn't really a Matriarchy in prior editions, and I think it speaks to the idea that the Gameline often said these things, and didn't always do the legwork in presenting it, but the players often seem willing to grab onto those sayings and do the legwork themselves in presenting them.

              But then when you get into the spirits and gods of Exalted, I also think you're correct. They aren't often generally presented as alien or non-human, and instead just human people who are sometimes a little weird looking. It's more then just their relationships and presentation, though I think you're right that it's part of it in the same way the rest of the gameline kind of revolves around it doing a lot of telling and not being strong enough to show.

              At the same time, I think the idea of Gods being human like, with flaws, idiosyncacies that are innately human, etc, is also sometimes strong and a good part of them existing. Assigning all gods to a kind of blue and orange morality just because it spices it up could also be considered a bit awkward in my opinion, because it severely limits what you can do with them unless you're quite competent.

              For instance, The Hug Ninja portrays her Gods as being incredibly difficult to understand; a common theme is having to possess people in order to even talk to others because actually showing up could cause colossal damage and upheaval. She's done this for the gods of a character's culture and for the Elemental Dragon that rules Champoor. This is, I think, because she's a big fan of Conan and Eldric, where similar things occurred with their Gods. This makes them seem very distant and non-human, but it also does mean there's limited interaction with them as a whole. It's interesting, but a delicate balancing act that she has to carry out- from how I see it-, to maintain interest in something that your interaction with is intensely limited.

              Meanwhile, Bastet 's Sidereal game does more work presenting Gods as being very human in their interactions. They look weird or strange and sometimes they have a lot better memory then you do, but in the end they still are kind of working towards similar goals; they want money or power or to better the world, and interacting with them on that axis is generally the most favorable way to get them to help you.

              The presentations are very different but it's because they're aiming for two different tonal stories that the Gods and Elementals are manipulated to present those different stories in an effective way.

              So I don't know if Gods and Demons and Elementals should always be completely alien and difficult to understand, but I don't know if it should be impossible for them to be that either.

Hopefully I'm not mangling his opinion here, as this is an old conversation and I can't even give a link.

                  (My stance, as I remember it, as one of the folks in that thread was a bit more, to paraphrase, like "Obviously human sexes and genders are just a kind of legacy of us being sexually reproducing animals and it's dumb and limiting to sort of propose that these states are hard coded into existence as a fundamental spiritual property of reality, and to boot kind of normalizes sex and gender essentialism in our world and politics in ways that I don't like involving them having a spiritual dimension. Give me my post-gender gods who aren't men or women, please. So much more interesting than getting hung up on these kind of boring legacy states of human being from our reality." I don't know if I'd take that stance today, or not, or be more sympathetic to other stances!)

                  I figure on the same basis if you're probably going to extend the notion of divine beings having romantic drives, you're probably going to do so on the basis that again mirrors what gives most of the audience that same sense of connection to themselves and their own relationships, or it would be kind of inconsistent.

                              Originally posted by Jen View Post
                              So my question is whether most non-human in Exalted possesses the same sexual orientation and gender identities as humans?
                              I would say that many such creatures do so, yes, even if I wouldn't necessarily commit to saying "most". There's a couple reasons for that. In-setting, it seems like there's something metaphysically significant about a humanoid form. There are a lot of creatures that, despite not being humans, still look like humans, and they don't all post-date humanity's ascension to prominence, either. Many demons seem to have had basically humanoid forms before the War of the Gods, for example, and several primordial races (the Lintha, for example, and the weird alien Grey-style race that Autochthon ate) seem to have been humanoid, but produced before or at least simultaneously to humans. And having genders, with the spectrum with two main "poles" like humans do, seems to be quite common as well - the Dragon Kings have it, for instance. So, in short, in-setting, a roughly human-like gender distribution is possibly not just an accident of one species, but has some kind of cosmological significance.

                              The other reason, the out-of-game one, for many non-human creatures to nevertheless have something approaching human gender, sexual identity, and desire is that it gives human characters more opportunities to interact with them. If you can't seduce a god because they don't have a gender identity and would be equally likely to be sexually attracted by a shade of blue, that cuts off an avenue for some characters. Suddenly, the seducer character can't really participate in the scene, even in terms of suggesting to someone else what might make them attractive. The more those opportunities are reduced, the more the players will tend to default to "just punch it". And that's an outcome I prefer to avoid.

                              Originally posted by Jen
                              So basically gay people are a minority of the population, if we assume that this is also true in Exalted, and the answer to the above question about non-human is Yes, then would gay seducers constantly barking at the wrong tree since 80% of everything is straight?
                              I would say that the answer here is two-fold. First, I'd straight-up (pun not intended!) make homosexual, asexual, and other non-hetero orientations more common in Creation. Like, maybe 30% of humans, at least, are a full 6 on the Kinsey Scale? Second, I would say that the stigmatization of non-heterosexuality is much, much less common in Creation than it is on Earth. As a result, far more people who aren't full 6s on the Kinsey Scale, but who aren't full 0s either (that is, not exclusively hetero-sexually attracted) are willing to act on those desires. I believe research on Earth on real humans has suggested that the majority are some degree of bisexual, rather than having exclusive heterosexual desire, so this would essentially just be treating humans more "realistically" anyway. And fully homosexual people will also be more willing to openly announce that fact, presenting more opportunities for the gay seducer who wanted to focus on exclusively homosexual people.

                                  I can't say anything to developer intention or setting lore, but I explained to my players like this: In Creation, everyone is presumed Bi unless otherwise clarified- there are 100% straight and 100% gay and 100% Ace people, but generally speaking, the way you assume an IRL person is straight unless otherwise mentioned is the way Creation assumes people are bi. Each person is somewhere on the Kinsey scale and nobody gives a damn about who you like.

                                  I run gods and spirits as being ... alien, kind of. The River Mother favors women since she believes herself to be tied to the concept of motherhood, but she also loves men as potential fathers. Alasathasas the Tears of the Mountain is a god of song- "he" doesn't care about male/female, he's only attracted to how musically gifted someone is. One creepy god of kelp, drowning, and Wu-Jian's wells is attracted to how beautiful your neck is and how much pleasure she'd get out of drowning you with her hands around it.

                                  To say one thing about the Realm though, I told my players that there's a lot of unofficial support specifically for homosexuality, because marriages are arranged on dynastic terms and you will pop out babies, whether you like your spouse or not. Since its expected that Dynasts will have affairs and trysts as long as they are discreet and kept out of public view, its a huge boon to take a same-sex lover because there's no risk of surprise pregnancy (Which I don't need to tell would be a huge political shit show amongst the houses).

                                  So basically, it never comes up unless I decide that a certain character has a specific kink. My 2e Giant Athletics Night Caste Batman was gay, and only for huge muscle dudes. A standard sexy-kimono-courtesan tried to charm him and she was treated like a whiny child.
