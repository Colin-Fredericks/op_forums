    Originally posted by flumpet38 View Post
    As written, it seems like every single mote spent contributes to your Anima, compared with 3e where you needed to spend 5 peripheral motes in a single instant to bump your Anima up. This seems...way way faster and harder to avoid building Anima for characters that probably want to keep Anima down, and sets up a pretty strong incentive to not use charms in combat at all, especially if the combat's likely to last more than a few rounds.
    This is basically correct.

    I think that the desire to have more of a cushion before you flare out to the horizon is why there are now an otherwise-crazy eleven(!) levels of Anima to track. And remember that, so long as you don't go Above Glowing (up through Anima 4), "Storyteller characters don’t immediately notice unless they have a reason to."

    However, I think that it is deliberate that (other than the dedicated Stealth Castes), it's much harder to keep your Anima down during an intense fight than in 3E, where people are very rarely forced to flare against their wills.

    Alternatively (and understanding we don't have the full ruleset), does the system break in any big ways if I just....double (or 1.5x or whatever) the anima threshold values?
    I'm not sure whether you mean "it takes 2 motes at once to raise Anima" (which would essentially make a number of builds never do that) or "there are 20 levels of Anima" (which is somewhat more doable, though you'd obviously have to recalculate anything using Anima as a cost), but either way, the most obvious fallout of this rule is that it becomes much harder for characters to access their anima abilities when they want to.

    My off-the-cuff idea for a hack would be to simply change what Anima values mean, maybe nothing visible at Anima 0-4, a forehead mark and faint glow (which people are mostly too distracted to notice mid-battle, and you can make rolls to hide outside of it) at 5-7, an unmistakable battle aura at 8-9, then the standard miles-high beacon at 10. That would allow most of the same gameplay, but much less visibility.

    You could also try giving out a free Charm modeled after Guarded Mind Meditation, something like:
    "Commit 2 motes. The Exalt may hide her anima so long as she does not rise to Bonfire (7+) - or, if you really want to make hiding viable, you could only auto-break dimming at Iconic (10). An anima hidden this way never allows the Exalt the benefits of an Active anima banner."

    That version offers a tradeoff: if you want all your motes or all your superpowers, you need to "go loud," but you can operate at slightly-reduced power for a good long time.

    With any of these rules, you may want to consider throwing some extra bonus to Night/Day/Nadir Castes, since everyone gets a lesser version of their most famous schtick.

            I was discussing this with other players, and it seems that Infected Fate is both pretty powerful and easy to abuse.

            Say your circle is fighting Mask of Winters, and you are an essence 3 Getimian. For a down cost of 1 mote each:
            - Infected Fate: You circle's Dawn is now your bodyguard. While fighting MoW, he gains what amounts to three dice (or a free excellency), each turn. And then he can place his free excellency over it!
            - Infected Fate: Now Mask of Winters is your bodyguard as well! Now he loses 1 success for each action where he acts against it (that is, all of them!). There is no way for him to get rid of it.

            There is no limitation on Infected Fate for being used on other Exalted, and no limitations on it to be used in involuntary targets. This is way too good!

                            Maybe even eight!

                            Originally posted by Essence Edition
                            Every two points of Anima increases the level of display from Dim to Iconic. At Dim (1-2) the character’s Caste Mark becomes visible. At Glowing (3-4), the character is lit by a soft light. At Burning (5-6) the aura of their anima can light up a room. At Bonfire (7-9) it’s visible from bowshots away. At Iconic (10), it’s visible on the horizon, and it displays an image unique to the character and her personality. At the first three points, Storyteller characters don’t immediately notice unless they have a reason to. Once a character reaches 5 Anima, they can no longer hide their divine nature.
                            I read "at the first three points" as meaning Dim, Glowing, and the unnamed level of "Anima 0". But it is possible that they mean "at Anima 1-3, Storyteller characters don’t immediately notice... At Anima 5, hiding it is impossible. And by strong implication, at Anima 4, NPCs will notice it unless you successfully hide it."

                            The latter was actually my initial read, since the text does otherwise say "points" when talking about the numerical values and "levels" for the descriptive ones (also, that paragraph doesn't mention "Anima 0" as anything). I currently favor the former - not because I think the text does, but because it's probably wise to resolve ambiguities in a way which seems cleaner in play, and splits fewer Anima levels in half.

                            Of course, by that logic, my favored (definitely non-canon) version is probably more like:
                            Anima 0-2: Dim. There are no visible signs, and characters only get passive anima powers.
                            Anima 3-4: Glowing. The character is lit by a faint glow (which people will only notice if they're actively looking for it and succeed a somewhat tricky roll), active anima powers come online.
                            Anima 5-9: Burning. The character's battle aura is impossible to disguise, and visible from range bands away. Their Caste Mark is clearly visible.
                            Anima 10: Iconic, per usual.
                            (Also, if you wanna strut, Celestials below Anima 5 can reflexively turn their Caste Mark on/off for free at any time.)

                            Four tiers, like in past editions. Gameplay is only slightly affected - the biggest change is not getting active stuff until Anima 3, and if that bugs you, you could move Anima 2 up to Glowing (I kinda like that you need to get a little serious and use more than you recover per scene/round to get the benefit). Unlike the house rules I recommended for stealthy Celestials, this does preserve what I think is the game's intention regarding hiding anima banners, just without the inherent oddness that someone looking at your face could ever miss a glowing forehead sigil (maybe all PCs wear Naruto headbands?) and leaving the exact brightness of Burning more up to interpretation.

                            My desire for symmetry does make me want Glowing at 3-5 and Burning at 6-9 (3/3/4 just sits better than 3/2/5), but that is a further change for reasons of aesthetics rather than simplicity, so I'm more conflicted there.

                                                Writers are paid on a contract basis; they are contracted to write X words at Y cents* a word. As the Kickstarter does well, more stretch goals are unlocked, meaning more words need to be written, meaning more contracts need to go out. A successful Kickstarter gives the writers the chance to be paid more to do more work.

                                                This stretch goal is a bonus. Literally, a bonus. Additional payment, advice and beyond OPP’s contractual obligations. Especially considering the writers have probably already been paid; the manuscript is late enough in the process that writer’s work should done; it’s an editor and developer game now.

                                                I suppose the disconnect here is a misunderstanding of how writers get paid. It may make intuitive sense to you, but it isn’t how the industry works. Turns out mermaids have gills, I guess.

                                                *yes, it is cents. From what I remember, 10 cents a word is standard; some more technical writings can get up to 25 cents a word. I think Dave Brookshaw once got a dollar a word, for something that was due the next day. Writers don’t get paid that much, sadly, but that’s the industry.
