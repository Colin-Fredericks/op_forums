            You know, I've never really been a fan of the notion of Ragara as secretly badass, but damn it if I can't see something to work with in the idea of him drawing longevity from a daiklave. If nothing else, it could be an idea that getting it to up the juice to him so that he can fight at a higher level is something he would only do under the most desperate circumstances because it would rapidly eat away at the life he has left.

            ​I developed this image: Ragara sitting in a wheelchair, alone for the fact that his orderly has already gone out and fallen in his defence. He's sitting there, in a white robe yellowed with age, shrivelled and with lank, brittle hair hanging down from his pate, lifting himself slightly to look at the people confronting him. One ultimately moves forward to finally put him out of everybody's misery... and then a needle of blackened blood shoots from his left wrist, piercing the attacker in a vulnerable spot just enough to make them stagger back.

            ​Ragara grasps the needle and drags it across the length of his body, pulling away lines of blood that follow the traces of his veins and arteries, shredding through his robe as they go, before congealing into the shape of a black and gold daiklave, with red accents. His left hand drops to the ornate ceramic head of his arm rest, crushing it to reveal a hearthstone, that he places into the daiklave.

            ​Ragara stands, the remains of his robe dropping away to reveal a thin, wiry body covered in scar tissue, that rapidly blackens as though blood was congealing throughout his skin. He falls into a feral stance and lifts his weapon to rest upon his shoulder, while what hair he has straightens out and darkens a bit, and he raises his glance to look at those confronting him from beneath his brow.

            ​Well, it's good enough for me.

            ​(For a less overwrought version, and what originally came to my mind, there's something as simple as him keeping the sword concealed within his wheelchair, and if he draws it to fight, it confers enough vitality to effectively swing it around, but not quite sufficient to fully restore mobility)

                            So, Sesus. This is another one that, off the bat, makes a reversal of my previous perceptions of a House. I always saw Sesus as the crude, brutal military one (which… may or may not have actually been how they were written), and here they are now as the ones focused on information gathering and subtle warfare.

                            Sooo, basically the whole House is being run with the principles of Sesus Rafara’s childhood? Makes me wonder if she’ll really be part of the setting anymore. Still, it’s not a bad foundation to build on, and it gives them a neat shadowy cabal.

                            Ahhh, my previous perception of Sesus is now their public image. Clever. It’s also interesting to see them having a role for socialites that might give Nagezzar more context.

                            Hmm, I suppose if they want to maintain such a spy network, it makes sense for even members of the House to not know who their leadership is… presumably beyond the matriarch. I presume that there’s basic and reliable magic to mask their voices as well.

                            Let’s see, the House economics seem a bit more nebulous than many of the others, let me see if I can parse it… so they’ve got a combination of general financial opportunities in newly conquered territories, the benefit of commercial contacts and expertise brought in by close ties to Cynis, aaand subversion and sabotage of the conflicting activities of foreign powers, especially the Guild. Plus the basic idea of mercenary contracts.

                            So their current army is the second largest, but they’re not well-suited to direct confrontation. Seems to me that their best tactics are to use their agents to break down opposing forces beforehand, and then using their troops to mop up what’s left. It’s interesting that they’re so close to Cynis that they’re hoping for them to provide improved military expertise as well. Given the other House’s description, I can imagine their respective policies have a kind of co-terminous evolution.

                            So let’s see, Sesus is generally disliked, but has strong blood that keeps a stream of marriages. Cynis is closely aligned because they compound that blood strength, and the methods and proclivities of the respective Houses complement each other well. The Mnemons are similarly connected by blood and their romantic streak, but it’s counterbalanced by their piety and the matriarch’s personal concern that they would oppose her royal ambitions. They had a rivalry with Tepet and took measures to help in their downfall, and has shifted that attention to Cathak. And they have the general hatred of Nellens due to the historical element.

                            I wonder what it would take to get them to be more open about the existence of Iselsi. I’ll be interested to see all of the details of that House’s deal.

                            Heh, I like to imagine in-character that the Empress or Sesus would have deliberately chosen some harsher territories to be their homeland, just for the ambience and character-building. It also wouldn’t be a spy drama if it didn’t have a fancy and ornate mountainside spa.

                            Nothing to say about the satrapy besides it being an example of them undercutting Tepet in action, and what some of the long term political implications of their action.

                            Nothing much in Sesus, besides another interesting example of what the Houses do with their founders’ bodies. It’s interesting that the matriarch exists separately from the Masked Council, and is overall somebody who lives at odds with their methods. It is kind of weird that she’s married to her uncle, though.

                            I suppose that it makes sense with all of that super-powered espionage, there would be at least one person who was so secretive that even the other spies hardly know anything about them. I suppose that, with her having an actual name, it makes sense that there was a point at which she lived publically, and then just… vanished. I wonder if it would be too passe to ever have a scenario where it turned out that the actual Sesus Agelin was killed by an outcaste early on, who managed to assume her identity.

                            Ah, so Rafara is, and the childhood is standard but for the whole traumatic event of her mother brutally (and idiosyncratically) murdering everybody that she loved. So now her gimmick is focused primarily on being the spy who works for her own agenda and the benefit of the Realm as a whole.

                            And it finishes off with somebody in the vein of the familiar old jerks, even if he’s still got that side of spying and social popularity.

                                                    Okay, cliff notes version of reacting to Tepet:

                                                    Considering how much people have always disliked the Shogunate feeling like a non-entity, it's a good choice to give one of the extant Houses a foundation in Shogunate culture. Likewise further distinguishing Tepet from Cathak with the greater emphasis on personal heroism than on unit tactics, as well as how that informs their cultural regard for non-martial pursuits.

                                                    I like the idea that one of the military Houses based its wealth not on military adventures or mercenary work, but acting as consultants and instructors, especially with the novel manner in which this served the imperial agenda by creating stronger buffer states. It also creates a stronger basis for why the loss of the legions has damaged them so, and a particular irony for how a disproportionate number of survivors come from the mildly diversified House businesses.

                                                    The warrior code as rite of adulthood really helps to add to the House's mystique, and as demonstrated by Niruz, represents a unique avenue for characters to express and legitimize their identity. I've already said how I appreciate some clarity on what the Vermilion Legion is and how it relates to Tepet.

                                                    With most of the other Houses already seen, their relationships to Tepet have more significance to me, especially the rival military Houses. It's an interesting idea that Sesus saw their difficulties as an opportunity to expand their own prestige, while Cathak views its attempt to secure the House's final demise as a show of respect. There's also an interesting sense of contrast in how Tepet relates to more outlying Houses; to have assisted V'Neef in their early days, only to be snubbed when in need, and to have wealthy Nellens waiting in the wings to offer new opportunities. Same goes for how Iselsi sees Tepet as a possible avenue for their own vengeance.

                                                    Not much is really said about Lord's Crossing itself, since it's mostly a basis for talking about how the House is led in the absence of a matriarch. While it's not stated, I suppose that the relationship with the spirits of the Vale of Reverie is another instance of something from their Shogunate roots. Medo as the remaining secure satrapy presents a few plot hooks for Tepet's possible resurgence; I'd say secession, but think that departing from the Realm altogether wouldn't be their style, even if Peleps can manage it.

                                                    Can't think of much to say about the scions; Arada is about the same as he ever was, and Niruz has the benefit of being somebody who demonstrates the significance of warrior oaths. I feel as though Corino matters primarily because she's in a position where just about any story of rebuilding the legions would include her. Gadurin is probably the most interesting of the bunch, with that idea that applying Tepet ideals to his art has made him very popular, and he's now trying to capitalise on that by crafting narratives to draw more support to his House.

                                                        Okay, two Houses left.

                                                        It was already the kind of thing stated before the book was even written much, but it’s a great idea for the first thing to be said about House V’neef is that it consists of a large number of adopted Exalted (although even I wasn’t expecting them to number in the hundreds). It never really helped the House before to be super unclear on how large it actually was, especially when there were writers who seemed to run with the idea that it literally consisted solely of V’neef’s nuclear family, which was just… completely unbelievable as something that could make an impact in the Dynasty. I also think the notion that much of the family consists of rags to riches stories lends a strong sense of character to them, a basis why it would have a driving ideal even while very new, and more to set it apart from the other Houses. Plus a bit more significance to the much vaunted charisma of V’neef herself. And it’s fun to picture a bunch of elder Dragon Blooded regarding the youthful leader as their mother.

                                                        Plus how it has them on their own imperialist drive, and frequently thumbing their nose at Peleps.

                                                        I also like the tension between having militarily experienced daughters to give the House more power, and the matriarch being very cautious about actually sending them out.

                                                        So their economics are about what they always were, wine, horse-breeding and the Merchant Fleet (even if the significance of the Fleet has been altered). Ahh, but the Merchant Fleet is now tied to the reduced tribute, which is the background for why they’re focusing so much in the West (which is compounding their rivalry with Peleps). It’s all a rich tapestry.

                                                        So they’ve got a small number of legions, their sole military forces owing to their youth, rendered disproportionately strong by the number of experienced outcastes in their ranks. And they’ve retrofitted the Merchant Fleet into a naval force to compete with the Peleps Water Fleet. It’s interesting, the sense of maximizing limited resources.

                                                        So, V’neef’s ties to Tepet from their perspective: before the loss of the legions, they were seen as a firm ally and somebody to back for the throne, but since became a liability that they still can’t afford to cut loose entirely, for political and personal reasons. Hmm, suddenly I’m seeing a “breakup of the First Triumvirate” situation, where V’neef’s husband dying creates an impetus to fully sever ties.

                                                        Ragara doesn’t like their financial security, so there are a few paths of conflict there.

                                                        We’ve never really seen V’neef’s perspective on Mnemon, and it seems eminently practical to be deeply threatened by her. It’s an interesting loop there; part of why Mnemon hates V’neef is that others would see her on the Throne, creating a threat to V’neef (and those she’s taken responsibility for) that she feels she can only avert by taking the Throne.

                                                        Heh, I’m amused by the manner in which rivalry of Peleps can be a source of strength, by making them a draw for support from other Houses. Nellens is a rival on a different front, but could be a viable ally if it wasn’t for their reputation, and Cynis is only good for continuing to buy stuff.

                                                        Ahh, it’s very clever of V’neef to set up a haven for the magistracy. That’s the kind of move that, properly capitalised on, could have a grand payoff.

                                                        Hmm, considering how broken up the western quadrant of the Isle is, I wonder where exactly there is coast for Eagle’s Launch to go; it sounds like it’s taking up a position as a kind of Realm Casablanca.

                                                        Oh wait, I initially misread Nansha as being part of Wavecrest, but I see now that it’s just nearby. I like the way that it’s dealing with the social turmoil of being a relatively new satrapy, particularly the distinctly imperialist issue of messing around with the local power dynamics so that they turn on one another.

                                                        And finally some mention of Faxai, which should be expanded on interestingly in the Caul’s chapter in The Realm. That’s a nice mix of mystical and pragmatic needs for maintaining a presence in the city.

                                                        So let’s see, the partnership of V’neef and Tepet Igan is at the heart of the house… I wonder how S’thera became blind. I’d usually have concerns over the notion of a Dynast woman being under a pressure of having children rather than following her own aspirations, but it’s a singular instance, and the circumstances work.

                                                        I don’t think we’ve ever seen a reformed, pacifist type among the Dragon Blooded, and I wonder if a story in which Dancing Boar becomes obligated by V’neef to lead the troops that he’s trained into battle would be too cruel.

                                                        Oh Agayo, don’t compromise your House’s security from Ragara just for fancy demon ships.

                                                        So yeah, they’ve done a very good job of building upon the basic premise of V’neef, which was always one of the stronger and more distinct in the Dynasty, and giving it a lot more heft and some interesting nuances. The explicit note on the size of the House in particular is liable to give it a lot more attention; I can’t help but assume that, in the past, the implication that it was very small made it feel too proprietary to have any players come from there.

                                                            And finally, there’s the mysterious Iselsi.

                                                            That’s another one where the game used to be a bit unclear and even occasionally contradictory on the details of its status; was it dead, was it dying, was it on the ropes? If it’s going to be anything, it might as well be a House that has truly fallen, to help distinguish it from the likes of Tepet.

                                                            … Oh, the plot point of them being a shadow army remains, but… altered a bit. Like the Empress had a master plan, but the House still suffered indignities in the course of it being carried out…
                                                            Ah, I remember now; it used to be that the Empress was keeping them in secret, with the promise that in the future she would stage a big revival for them that would allow them to re-enter society in prestige, and her disappearance meant that the House was adrift with nobody to bring them in from the cold. Now it’s a case of the loss of the Empress taking the leash off of them, that they’re ready to capitalise on to take vengeance. Okay, I get it. Cool.

                                                            Hmm, that’s some neat imagery for initiation into the Vendetta. Real Masonic kind of deal there. I suppose that the pride of a Great House is not the kind of thing that can sit easily when it’s driven underground.

                                                            The details of them being given new identities around the Threshold and entering the Order en masse are familiar (although the former has the new twist of being granted training in techniques unfamiliar to the Dynasty), but I think the notion of them being widely attached around the other Houses waiting for a day of reckoning is new. That’s a fun take on that kind of long-haul conspiracy, deep cover agent story, where there are not only Iselsi who might be emotionally compromised, but those who fear that it will happen to them.

                                                            Heh, I love the idea that the House has built up a Keyser Soze kind of figure in its ranks as a false target for enemies to pursue, while actually being highly decentralized. It provides a lot of verisimilitude for them being able to sustain their organization. That and how they’ve only been doing it a relatively short while.

                                                            I imagine that the distribution of Iselsi’s resources, and the legal and administrative avenues for permits to it, is another kind of thing that Bureaucracy could work with, a game of obscuring and uncovering all of the details.

                                                            Well, coming at the end as it does, most of Iselsi’s relationships have already been expounded upon, but it does have the new detail of how any possible alliance with Tepet would be seen as a temporary measure before finishing the final House off. The last new thing is this idea that the infiltration of the Immaculate Order is used heavily as a basis for cultivating possible alliances with gods.

                                                            I wonder what it’s like to be an Iselsi living on the mattresses. The Last Little Sapling is a nice addition, as I do often like a good set of code exchanges in plain sight.

                                                            Now, what it is about the inhabitants of Ditola Village that makes them amenable to being trained like that, and taking part in that attack when it comes. Is it just hatred of House Mnemon?

                                                            So, if Iselsi was an economic expert, I wonder if that meant that the House was initially more deeply connected to the Thousand Scales, and drifted to intelligence and espionage after she died.
                                                            Awww, poor Dileko, going to have to eventually kill all of his friends. And jeez Ragara, how much of a dick do you have to be that even as an old man dining with a devout Immaculate, she thinks it would improve the Realm to murder you? Probably comes to the same result for the House anyway.

                                                            Oh dear, they’ve got an agent who went off training in the Southeast. That can’t end well, all the weird shit that they’ve got down there.

                                                            Ohh, and finally a mysterious old man of uncertain loyalties, who took part in distributing the military in the absence of the Scarlet Empress. That sounds like the kind of fellow that one would want to meet in a game.

                                                            Aaaaand it turns out that this isn’t quite the end; one last, short entry on Cadet Houses. I’ll get on to that promptly.
