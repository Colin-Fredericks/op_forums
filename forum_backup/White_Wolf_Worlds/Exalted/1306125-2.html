                    Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post

                    What sucks about it? I might have to come up with a house rule or two for my group.

                    Just to be more clear, I mean things like you found it hard to keep fire aura before your decisive, or there wasn’t a good opportunity to spend motes to flare your anima prior to the hit, or like maybe you felt really restricted while you were in fire aura because you couldn’t mix it with other aspect charms?
                    First, you have to carefully micromagane your Anima level and thus your mote expenditure. Not only do you have to avoid going bonfire too early, but since there aren't any Water Melee charms that you can activate on your turn (the only one being a parry charm) going bonfire is virtually the only realiable method you have of entering Water Aura. So you have to float at Anima level 2 and time it just right to flare iconic right before you drop the decisive. If anything messes with that gameplan (and EVERYTHING will mess it up: forced to full excellency parry to avoid going splat? lost your Aura. Whiffed the Decisive? lost your Aura. Landed the decisive but the target survives/there are other targets? lost your Aura) well you're stuck in Water Aura now so good luck fighting the rest of the scene with half your charms turned off.

                    The other half of the problem is that because my game-plan was based around eventually hardlocking into Water Aura that meant I was heavily de-incentivised from taking non-essential charms that were non-balanced or aura-keyworded. That meant no Flame Warden Stance, no Demon-Crushing Wolf Bite, etc. And it turns out all of the fun Fire stuff, the stuff that changes your gameplay and gives you unique strategies and options (reflexive clashes, non-resetting decisives, ranged attacks, flurries, status effects, etc.) ALL come at Essence 3.

                    Meaning that for several months of playing the game my combat flowchart was:
                    walk up to enemy => withering w/excellency => is it crash? if no step 2 again, if yes => full excellency decisive =>is it dead? if yes go back to step 1, if no cry for party members to save me cause I can't do shit.
                    If I got attacked I either parried with the excellency or face-tanked it with my artifact armor. Blazing Interception is almost never worth it so after a while I stopped using it altogether. Best defense against decisives that didn't fuck up my Aura was Wound-Denying Dragon Faith and a shit-ton of OX Bodies.

                    The build was quite effective (only really thwarted by soak-monsters due to a lack of withering damage enhancers or alternative ways of gaining initiative) but it was incredibly dull to play, with barely any meaningful decision-making.


                    Meanwhile I was looking at my Fire-Aspected Hearthmate who was far less invested in combat than I was, who went freely around tossing Melee (fire) charms, then when her initiative was deemed good enough she would reposition using Athletics (also fire) charms to get a good angle and then unleash TERRYFYING ROAR OF THE FIRE DRAGON (also a fire charm obviously) and win the fight. And her player never had to worry about anything: my aspect is fire, all of my charms are fire charms, my aura is always fire, I flare my anima whenever I fucking want, and my finishing move is the only charm I have that requires expending aura and I'm always in fire aura so it's always ready to go. Even my fucking social charms are fire charms. So yeah, you can tell why I was a bit jealous.

                    /end rant.

                            Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                            Alternatively, each element should have had a form of attack and a form of defense and they should have all been a single element.
                            Thrown (Air) and Archery (Wood) depend on distance for defense. Thrown also has defensive Air Charms, and Archery has defensive Wood Charms.

                            Stealth Charms offer another form of defense for Thrown specialists, and most of its Charms are also Air-aspected. Ride Charms offer another form of defense for Archery specialists, and most of its Charms are also Wood-aspected.

                            Brawl (Water) has Water Charms for parrying and for grappling (which hinders or outright prevents the target from attacking).

                            Elusive Crosswind Defense is an Air Dodge Charm that works best at distances, so someone using Thrown Charms to build up an Air Aura has the perfect Dodge Charm to use. Safety Among Enemies can work at any range, including against ranged attacks.

                            Swaying Grass Elusion works well at any range, but definitely helps when you're caught at close range with only a bow (and you might not have Wood Dragon Style for parrying), and certainly helps avoid ranged attacks.

                            Ebbing Tide Recedes is a nice complement to the Water Aspect anima power, and the Water-aspected movement-generating Charms in Brawl. Brawl also has Water-aspected Charms to attack from range. Flow With Strife works well with the Water-aspected Resistance Charms that help reduce the amount of damage you might suffer from getting hit.
