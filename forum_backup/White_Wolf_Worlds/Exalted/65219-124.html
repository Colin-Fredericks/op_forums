        I did a redo of the Lover in my games until the Abyssals book comes out, basically focusing on the idea of her as a master necromancer who has the best understanding of the metaphysics that drive ghosts, based on ideas I found in the discord and in Arms of the Chosen.

        The Lover Clad in Raiment Tears - Witch Queen of the Waste
        tl;dr: Elizabeth Bathoroy+The Evil Queen+ The Fairy Godmother = The Lover

        While indeed a being of beauty and lustful temptation, the idea that her power and aspirations are limited to lusts for appearance and pleasures of the flesh is the mark of woeful ignorance. The Witch Queen of the Waste is a being who knows the power that longing has over the underworld and has made those longings her tool.

        The Lover's true skill is that she is the first and foremost necromancer in all of creation and beyond. While the Dowager is closest to the Neverborn, even she must acknowledge the Lover's skill in the art and science of necromancy, and none match her understanding of the underworld's metaphysics; what it was, how it became as it is, and how it can be changed. While she cannot equal Eye's necrotech nor Mask's undead minions, her understanding extends far beyond command over ghosts and necromantic horrors, she has the most complete understanding of the passion plays of the dead and how these shape, change, and solidify the underworld.

        Those who call her temptress speak of her beauty because they cannot bear to speak of what she offers: dreams, appeasement of regret, an end of loss, a happily ever after that can never be. One half the Evil Queen with her mirror, one half the Fairy Godmother granting impossible desires, she lacks the insecurities and compassions that act as a weakness to both. She knows the price of every heart, every hidden desire, or abandoned dream that would tempt someone to leave their convictions behind. To the Lover, the longings of one's heart are the knives she uses to break the skin and let her poison into the bloodstream, for she will not be content until she has exposed every intimacy as the mercantile lie that it is.

                    In Autochthon, most cities are formed around a powerful Alchemical Exalt. We know female Alchemicals become Metropoli and male become Patropoli. Let's make it awkward:

                    Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                    Seeing as the current choices are Greek for &quot;Mother City&quot;, Greek for &quot;Father City&quot;, and the Greeks worshiped a non-binary god, &quot;Hermapolis&quot; probably seems right for a non-binary Alchemical -- with Metropolis being the cultural default and Patropolis/Hermapolis being types of Metropolis. (Like &quot;man&quot; meaning both human beings and the male gender; all Alchemical cities are &quot;Metropoli&quot;, but that's also the term for specifically female cities.)
                    Most folks in Autochthon just call their city a metropolis -- it's not meant mean-spiritedly, it's just the culture. Most Alchemicals don't mind -- they don't perceive it as misgendering, it's just a cultural norm. Those few who do perceive a prejudical issue, are generally not offended, but contemplate the best ways of reducing gender biases and promoting social harmony -- and that often starts with educating people on gendered language. The vast majority of the Alchemical Exalted shed their notions of binary gender when they become giant 15' warstriders (or earlier), and have no strong feelings one way or the other.

                    But there are a few who are DEEPLY sensitive about the issue. Referring to "the metropolitan area" instead of the "patropolitan area" causes the city to grind to a halt in protest until amnends are made with the offended Alchemical. Slightly more common are patropolitan rights activists, or patropolists, who campaign actively for gender equality between cities. The average citizen agrees that it's wrong to be prejudiced against a city on the grounds of gender, but is lukewarm at best on the specific issues brought to the table by patropolists.

                                                        During the First Age when there was a lot, lot more information hanging around, and there was an impromptu messaging boards, there were entire internet forums where people discussed sorcery. People threw around ideas for sorcerous workings, concepts for spells, links to tutorial videos laden with training charms, There was a monthly competition where people tried to think up new spell designs. There was also a forum where people talked about demons, what they could do, their various predilections, and also made new demon designs. The forums were backed up in an archive, somewhere within the Threshold.

                                                        The archive contains a snapshot into the thoughts and culture of the First age... and is also a priceless archive on the knowledge of sorcery. Anyone within it not only gains extensive knowledge of sorcerous, geomantic, and magitech principles of the first age, as well as extensive knowledge of demons, but they also gain many working demon and golem designs that can be created and used in the first age.

                                                            The nature of their jobs and Arcane Fate means many Sidereals have a particular approach to sex and intimacy.

                                                            An older co-worker or sifu might casually offer to sleep with a younger one who is clearly attracted to them to “get it out their system” behaviour that might seem shocking and an abuse of power but is intended as the reverse to try and avoid an office romance rooted in a serious power disparity.
