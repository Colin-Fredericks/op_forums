                                The Seven may or may not be color coded. (I'm fairly certain about the names and possibly the identities.)

                                Let's start with Mottom. She's the one currently speaking, and seems to have not aged very well. She shares Orange with the "Grand Dragon" (Who's probably Mammon) Then, Now.

                                Next there's Gog-Agog. The Green cloaked person. Then, Now. However, Gog-Agog and Jadis may be mixed in this case- while on one hand the color coding is correct, It seems pretty possible for Jadis to be Crazy Face and for Gog-Agog to be Shadow-Pillar-Person. That would fit their general.. theme? better, but would break the idea of color coding. So: Jadis, in Blue. Then, Now.

                                Solomon is obvious and Purple.

                                And then onto the no-shows.

                                The Big Red Idiot (Who is either Mammon or Jagganoth, I personally favor Jagganoth) Then.

                                Next is the "Grand Dragon" (Once again, either Mammon or Jagganoth) He(?) sent a representative (The paper pusher mentioned). Color is another shade of Orange, or Yellow perhaps. Then.

                                Finally, Incubus who has the color of Pinkish. He sent his First Consort. Then.


                                Or at least, this seems like the most likely way for things to go. Probably.

                                                    So, it's something from the forum game that eventually spawned the webcomic Kill Six Billion Demons, but it is Exalted's cosmology personified:

                                                    "God is Dead, you say, simply, savoring the dark fire of the meta-Brandy and the beautiful bloody slash of the infinite ahead of you. Already her destination is materializing around you, inch by inch.

                                                    I was wrong to stop there, you continue, first, because this is common knowledge now. God died at the moment of His creation. I have slept in his corpse.

                                                    You take another draft.

                                                    But the real question, you say, and this is the real ringer, is what happens to God's house after he dies? Do the mice and the rats and the pitiful insects take over? Who keeps all the clocks running? Who cleans all the windows and oils all the door hinges? Who makes the tea?

                                                    Who is living in our poor deceased God's house now, my young friend?"
