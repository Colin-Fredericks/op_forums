            I can see your point.

            I will say though that I think it's already somewhat improved over 2nd edition, because of this spell:

            Originally posted by Adversaries of the Righteous
            Summon the Shadow-Eaten (Ritual, 2wp; Instant):
            Summon an Essence 1-3 mortwight (Exalted, p.
            505) or other monstrous ghost in a night-long ritual,
            rolling (Intelligence + Occult) against its Resolve to
            bind it for a year and a night. If this fails, roll (Wits +
            Occult) against its Willpower to banish it.
            In 2nd ed, IIRC, with Iron Circle, you could either summon ordinary ghosts (which I would argue is a very useful and flexible power, though in quite a different way to Summon Demon or Summon Elemental; it's an excellent information-gathering power, for example, and ghosts are generally a bit easier to control than demons), or screw it up and be attacked by spectres.
            You couldn't really go "I want to summon up a combat-spirit to fight for me" the way you can do with Summon Demon, unless you actually know, say, the name of a dead great warrior who died and became a ghost and learned some combat powers.

            But this spell lets you do this, summoning a mortwright, doppleganger, hungry ghost, rantai, susurrus, etc (maybe ghostfisher? Not sure if you could summon one with this spell). (As an aside, I think the ghosts in HDNP are some of the more solid entries, though I don't know how people have found them in practice, I haven't actually used any of them.)

            And I assume the standard Summon Ghost still exists as well, so you can summon up a murder victim to interrogate, your dead mum because you're sad, a ghost to spy for you who's more reliable than a demon, a dead PC so you can stuff him in his own animated corpse and then go on a crusade to free his people and then establish him as their ghost-king, etc, etc.

            Also, one thing that necromancy is better at summoning than demons is armies. Summoning an army of demons is a giant hassle; you can only summon one per night, and you've got to keep an eye on them while you're building your army, lest they wander off and get into trouble (except Baidaks maybe. Baidaks are probably the best choice for a demon army).

            But with this spell:
            Originally posted by Adversaries of the Righteous
            Raise the Rotting Horde (Control) (15sm, 1wp;
            Indefinite): Animate corpses as zombies, up to a size
            2 battle group with poor Drill and Might 1. Zombies
            remain animated indefinitely unless exposed to sunlight
            for a full scene. As a control effect, the battle
            group has average Drill instead, or elite Drill by
            spending a Willpower while casting.
            You need a bunch of corpses of course, but this lets you summon up a battle group instantly. It'll only last a scene in daytime, but it'll last a whole night, and there are ways to keep them alive. The only way to get a battle group that fast with sorcery is having the second-circle demon games-box (I always forget her name, begins with S).

            Void Circle necromancy did have a spell to summon a Hekatonkhire, but it was quite generic compared to third-circle demons. Obviously, currently there are no rules for Third-Circle Demons anyway, but there will be, and there's plenty of fluff to get you started, whereas there's not for Hekatonkhires.

            STing Bronze Age Exalted

                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                            Out of interest, why do you think they're a mistake? Is it because they're too undemonic (they don't have any wackiness or alienness to them), or for a different reason?
                                            A core element of demon summoning in Exalted is that every demon's alien nature always offers potential complications; it isn't a Faustian bargain where they're trying to twist your intent, but you do need to work around their personalities and idiosyncrasies. Baidak don't have personalities and idiosyncrasies. The fact that so many discussions of what demons to summon eventually turns to "you should summon baidak because they do exactly what they're told" is a clear indication that they do indeed short-circuit this thematic element, and I consider this detrimental to the sorcerer play experience.
