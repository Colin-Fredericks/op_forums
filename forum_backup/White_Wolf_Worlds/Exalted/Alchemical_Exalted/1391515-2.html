        Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
        In previous Editions, sorcery only allowed for spells. Now it allows for sorcerous workings.

        Should Alchemicals be capable of sorcerous workings? Should Autochthonian mortals?

        Should sorcery somehow be impossible in Autochthonia?

        Should having your soul contained within a soulgem prevent one from being a sorceror?


        Celestial Compass: Autochthonia was well-loved for introducing so much color and variety to the Realm of Brass and Shadows.

        Common humanity was joined by six elementally-mutated subspecies that took on the roles of barbarians, dwarves, elves, and goblins. None of them have soulgems, so if sorcery is possible in Autochthonia but impossible for those with soulgems, that would be a cool way to make the mutants even more weird and terrifying to the people of the Eight Nations.

        One of the Eight Nations had an immortality-chasing cult of thaumaturges that unwittingly killed themselves and used their soulgems to possess their own corpses like techno-liches. Which could still be done with a very specific thaumaturgical miracle instead of a "procedure", but which could also be treated as a sorcerous working or even a Terrestrial Circle Spell.

            Kind of to expand on my "Weaving is only for Alchemicals and therefore kind of mostly useless", this is especially true in terms of support. We're gonna continue to get spells, example workings, artifacts, and stuff as the line evolves. Necromancy will get similar support in both the Abyssals and Liminals books, plus other books it might be handy. By being more shared systems, they're guranteed more support over the lifetime of th eline and not a lot needs to be done of rthat.

            Weaving short-circuits this for Alchemcials a bit. Because it's not sorcery, there's a lot that just doesn't flat-out work save what's called out as a cross-over (half-negating the point fo the sepration). Because they're to Autochthonia most of the time, it won't have nay real support out of that context. You will likely see nothing about it before an Alchemical book with Weaving, and similarly nothing afterwards save maybe the companion. And due to how it has been positioned to "fill in" for both sorcery and necromancy before, it means that Alchemicals usually got neither before, and there was an expectation to "fill-in" for them. It was something that how it was designed closed Alchemcials off to the generally more line-wide supported sorcery, and forced them to be using their onw special system that was often undersupported, needlessly complicated, and often diluted having ot use sorcery and necromancy anyways in order to fill in for these issues.

            Like, a big strength in the end is that it's something that's cool that if we asumed Weaving could get al lthe wordcount it needed. But often it eats up the section that'd of been sorcery in a book, but having to introduce its own mechanics and differences while at it. Kind of imagine if every sorcery-enhancing Charm, initation, bonus Merits, and spells were lumped together in one block fo text. Now think about how much you just stragiht-up lose having to explain Weaving. And also consider that it does not heavy-lifting for future wroks save teh Companion for that book.

            Like, Abyssals has to worry about this with necrmancy for instnace, sure. But it'll get more necromancy in Limnals. ANd who knows, Paths of Brigid or Towers of the Mighty might have some more that slides in there nicely. The Abyssal stuff will be load-bearing for future wroks. Weaving, by contrast, likely never gets that

            IN all this I just don't see a place for it. Especially since sorcery just kind of works anywhere: It's not about Creation's rules but reality in general. Autochthonia is just a different subset of Everything, even if a very isolated part of Evertyhing, and I think the idea that some Charms that Alchemicals can have installed let htem literally reality-hack, no matter hwere theya re, is fitting for sorcery just fine. ANd that they don't need extra 0.5 Circles to be cool with it.

                                                Not precisely.

                                                Solars can have up to level 3 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                                                Infernals can have up to level 3 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                                                Abyssals can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 3 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                                                Lunars can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                                                Sidereals can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 1 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                                                Dragonblood can have up to level 1 sorcery. In 2nd edition certain DBs could instead have level 1 necromancy, but not both. I'm not sure what'll happen with that in 3rd edition (the full necromancy rules aren't out yet, after all).
                                                A very small number of mortals (and god-bloods, etc) can learn level 1 sorcery. In 2nd edition, it was possible for them to get level 1 necromancy, but not both. I assume that'll be the same in 3rd edition, but we'll see.

                                                Liminals, Getimians and Alchemicals we don't know about yet. Presumably Liminals are better at necromancy than sorcery, and Getimians better at sorcery than necromancy, but we'll see. In previous editions Alchemicals had a completely different set of Sorcery-esque powers.
