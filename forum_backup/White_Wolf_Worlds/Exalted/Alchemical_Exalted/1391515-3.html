                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                        Not precisely.

                        Solars can have up to level 3 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                        Infernals can have up to level 3 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                        Abyssals can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 3 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                        Lunars can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 2 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                        Sidereals can have up to level 2 sorcery and level 1 necromancy. Doesn't make a difference if you've got both.
                        Dragonblood can have up to level 1 sorcery. In 2nd edition certain DBs could instead have level 1 necromancy, but not both. I'm not sure what'll happen with that in 3rd edition (the full necromancy rules aren't out yet, after all).
                        A very small number of mortals (and god-bloods, etc) can learn level 1 sorcery. In 2nd edition, it was possible for them to get level 1 necromancy, but not both. I assume that'll be the same in 3rd edition, but we'll see.

                        Liminals, Getimians and Alchemicals we don't know about yet. Presumably Liminals are better at necromancy than sorcery, and Getimians better at sorcery than necromancy, but we'll see. In previous editions Alchemicals had a completely different set of Sorcery-esque powers.

                        All of the above access to sorcery and necromancy is absolutely correct under 2e rules, with the exception of the Lunars' access to necromancy, which was level 1, not 2, per the Black Treatise. However, it looks like Fangs at the Gate p. 310 says Lunars can be the equal of Solars in necromancy, so 3e may be changing things up.

                        Also, in the 2e Alchemicals book it says that Alchemicals that have gone Gremlin and have a corrupted Man-Machine Protocol Weaving Engine CAN access regular sorcery and necromancy to the 1st level of each, and if they have a corrupted God-Machine Protocol Weaving Engine, then they get their choice of level 2 sorcery OR level 2 necromancy, but not both.

                            For me, the big difference isn't how magic works, it's the environment. You have elementals, but they are a different set. There are the deva subsouls, which presumably could be summoned if the higher up souls allow it. You cast magma krakens or summon rust monsters, what does that do to the Maker's body? You just punched a hole in some nutrient lines, or destroyed a sensor-nerve. That would probably get attention from white-blood-cell equivalent spirits, and maybe make the devas declare sanctions on your country, even if the body doesn't reflexivly spasm and squish you. Eyeless face, if it's even possible to summon them into the jouten's region of Elsewhere, would likely ping as Blight or gremlin. I could see the Weaving Protocol being designed to specifically lock out certain high-collateral-damage spells. Which is a restriction that could be lifted if the Alchemical was going into creation. Or, there are just spells that no one in there knows, so can't be taught.

                            As for necromancy, the Blight and such seems more "active", where Creations Underworld is "neverborn dreaming as everything gets slowly sucked into the Abyss". So I would have necromancy as... less reliable? You'll get roughly what you want, but each spell will be twisted to better serve the Blight.

                            The ultimate Working, of course, should be "jump-starting the Great Maker's brain". Biggest. Lightning bolt. Evah.

Dunno, I can imagine the concept but at my table I would have to rule "no Working can alter the Alchemical body" because aesthetic. Maybe a way to trade charm slots for artifact slots, or maybe not.
