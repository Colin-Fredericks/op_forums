    Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
    Wait you're saying that if your argument is shaky, the entire idea presented by the thread is shaky?
    No, that's not at all what I'm saying. However, if I did say that, I'd be right within the paradigm you presented - if my argument is shaky because it's based on 2e mechanics, then so is the OP's idea, which is based on such 2e mechanics as Alchemicals increasing in size past Essence 5, City-size Alchemicals being permanently saddled with Clarity, and Colossus- and City-sized Alchemicals being able to spawn a human-sized drone body. We don't know if any of those will be true in 3e either, do we? Hence, shaky idea based on 2e mechanics.

    Thus, ironically, you are defeated by your own strawman.

    The mechanics of 2e have never had strong influence on discussions involving 3e stuff. The most important thing is that whatever you add to 3e works in 3e. So stating that an idea is bad in 3e because of the mechanical implementation of things in 2e is an inherently flawed argument.
    The thing is, while 3e isn't shy to change things around from previous editions when necessary, it doesn't change things just for shits and giggles. Splats we've currently seen in 3e may differ in the minutiae of mechanical implementation, but they are fundamentally recognizable as the same entities - Solars are still top dogs whose powers go from peak human capability to superhuman, shining perfection, Dragon-Blooded are still individually weakest Exalted with flashy elemental powers, and Lunars are still Attribute-based shapeshifters.

    In this vein, Alchemicals have always been limited in how many Charms they can install at once, and this limitation has always been expressed through Charms having installation costs that can only be paid from their Personal Pool. This was true in both 1e and 2e (except in the case of Alchemicals, 1e doesn't really matter, as it's probably the only splat on which everyone agrees 2e version was straight up better). Arrays have likewise existed in both previous editions, and have even survived the culling of their conceptual progenitor, the Combo mechanic, in 2.5, because of how they factored into the aforementioned limitation. Given how important it is for Alchemical play experience, installation costs are extremely unlikely to disappear in 3e, especially since nothing about them clashes with any mechanical aspect of 3e that differentiates it from 2e. Admittedly, Arrays might just go away, as they really lost a lot of their value when Combos stopped being a thing, but their existence has barely any effect on the crux of my argument.

    In other words, I'm not pointing out issues with how the proposed change affects edition-specific mechanics (which would indeed be a flawed argument, I'll give you that). I'm pointing out issues with how the proposed change affects a core splat theme. And that's a perfectly valid argument.

    If you don't like Alchemicals starting as cities then just say that. Don't try to justify your dislike with 2e mechanics, and definitely don't think you're gonna make a discussion of hypotheticals go away just because someone calls you out on how shaky your argument is.
    The problem with discussing hypotheticals is that in vacuum, all ideas are equally valid. If we assume every potential issue with the OPs idea is simply not an issue without specifying why, then sure, the OPs idea works! Except, what are we discussing here? The most you can say about it under such assumptions is "I like it" or "I don't like it". To discuss anything more, we need some benchmark for the idea, some frame of reference. And since we're discussing a possible depiction of Alchemical Exalted, the most logical frame of reference we have are the Alchemical Exalted we already have, i.e. their 1e and 2e depictions (except, again, mostly 2e because it's better in pretty much every way). Hence, I have contributed to the thread by pointing out a major splat theme that's going to clash with the proposed changes. Now this is something the OP can think about and refine their idea.

    This is independent of my overall dislike of the idea, which BTW I have "just said that", right under pointing out the specific issue, in a separate paragraph so it's clearly marked as a separate thought. So I'm really not sure what you expected of me. But the next time you feel the need to lecture me, please make sure you have a case first.

        Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
        I like the idea of mummy but it unfortunately really only has one kind of game in group play. I don't think it really works.
        The first time I've read MtC, it seemed really prohibitive. Upon second reading, I've paid more attention to the mechanics involved rather than how it's described in narrative terms, and while you're still very much on a leash, it's a pretty long one, as a single Descent can easily last you a couple months or more before you get to shenanigans that could possibly prolong it even further. There's still the matter of "why would multiple undead demigods be awakened at the same time with compatible goals", but the adventure included in the corebook doesn't seem to make such a big deal out of it. MtC might be the only game I know where the stoytelling chapter actually tries to make things more complicated for you than they ought to be.

        This is all regarding MtC as its own game. As a potential inspiration for Alchemicals, I feel the themes (and mechanics meant to evoke said themes) are clashing beyond any hopes of reconciliation. Alchies are all about close cooperation between themselves and mortals, Mummies are about tense, distrustful cooperation between themselves and a complicated, highly formalized relations with mortals. Alchies work best when they come prepared for the particular problem, Mummies are all about powering through all obstacles on the go, because every delay costs you precious mojo. Alchies struggle to maintain hold on their humanity, Mummies have to regain it from zero. To change Alchemicals to more resemble how Mummies work would strip them of many things that make them Alchemicals in the first place.

        That's not to say I'd have a problem with a type of Exalted operating more like the Arisen. It's just I'd rather make them a separate type of Exalted rather than try to fit a cog-shaped peg into a sarcophagus-shaped hole
