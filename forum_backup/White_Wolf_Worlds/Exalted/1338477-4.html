    Originally posted by Flare View Post
    One thing I've been trying to test for a little while but have been having issues with due to how my.plauers have resolved encounters or discussed what they want out of my game is splitting combats.

    For instance, say your Circle of 5 Ecalts engages 2 Exalts; a Dusk, a Daybreak, the Daybreak's undead Rhino Centaur bodyguard, and a group of Zombies.

    Rather then a massive pile of Initiatives, I'd prefer to split up to where, for instance, the Dawn and the Night engage the Dusk, the Zenith fights the Rhino Bodyguard, and the Twilight and Eclipse fight the Daybreak and the Zombies.

    In this idea, each of these would in effect be a miniature fight in itself; you can't 'cross over' fights; the Dawn can't smack the much weaker Daybreak to farm Initiative to kill the Dusk, and similarly the Dusk can't do that to the Eclipse; they have to stay focused on their own opponents or they risk opening themselves up for an attack.

    However, in addition to this would be special Disengage Gambits that use the same rules as Disengaging but rather then leaving the range, they instead 'Tag-Out' without another ally, swapping places with them in their fight, possibly getting a bonus to their next attack for doing so as a 'surprise' action.

    So for instance, the Dusk might call the Zombies to distract the Dawn and get a sneak attack on the Eclipse, or the Twilight might leave the zombies to the Eclipse to go join the Zenith in dealing with the monster.

    As each battle ended you slowly fold the combatants into other fights, or they stay out of the others if they're too exhausted or injured.

    Hopefully I get to try this relatively soon and get some notes on it.
    Sounds interesting. Separating combatants into separate duels does feel like a good fit for Exalted's cinematic approach to combat. The only problem would be when a character's skills reward team play, such as someone with Archery or Charms that buff or defend others.

    Originally posted by Grod_the_giant View Post
    Wouldn't the judicious use of Range Bands accomplish much the same thing? If the Zenith/Rhino duel is at Short Range to the Dawn/Night/Dusk melee, that separates things nicely-- if you try to repeatedly Disengage and switch between opponents, you're losing initiative for sure, and quite possibly an entire action. Due to the way Disengage works, it also sort of makes the "stickiness" of being in a duel relative to the strength of your opponent, which I think makes sense.
    I think the main problem is when combatants have ranged attacks, which would allow them to "cheat". Also there may be other impacts, for instance Charms that require you to win Join Battle might be easier to pull off since you're not rolling against the enemies in the other duels.

        For the first part, this is why such duels wouldn't be 1 on 1. The Zenith battle singer in such an idea would probably have a bodyguard trying to protect them from an enemy who is trying to cut off the advantage.

        Depending on how it's set up, for buffing charms like that or the Empowering Shout, I'd lean towards the idea of that charm being able to cross the 'borders' as it were, since they're just theoretical ones anyway; if you're playing the buffer person you're probably not actively attacking at the same time, meaning you're not upending the Initiative order.

        It could create a sort of battle where someone has to protect the 'strategist' while they buff the other combatants to be able to handle other foes.

        As for Range Bands, many abilities have the ability to swap into new range bands either with attacks or powers of charms. Range bands themselves often suffer from being just constricting and vague enough that players can have trouble keeping track of them, since all of them are relative to each other; the game doesn't support the idea of a central 'feature' to base range bands off of, so a lot of my players often miss or forget they're too far away to attack someone, or get confused on how they work in certain aspects.

            I tested Flare's idea during my first campaign and it went very well I think, but there are few characteristics to be careful about :

            - It doesn't have to be 1v1 (just been said)

            - The actors of each fight need to have a special link between them that makes the fight more tense : My first fight was between a Twilight and his Lunar mate, the second was between the Night and an Abyssal that almost destroyed his soul in a prior scenario, and the last was the Dawn and Eclipse against the enemy Exigent and his troops, and the Solars had very strong empathic history between them (the Dawn ended killing the Eclipse after the fight because of a Limit Break, and they were the couple of characters with the most interesting dual link between them so it was very emotionnal). Due to this limitation, I don't advise using this method every time

            - Every fights needs to have clear objectives for the overall battle that are more than "Win or loss" : The Night was disabling a 2nd Circle Demon from the inside that made the enemy troops immortals; The Twilight and Lunar were fighting to end the Solar/lunar bond that tied them, and the Dawn and Eclipse were protecting the immaculate temple full of artifacts and emprisonned gods that could serve as weapons. If there are no "direct consequences" to the fight except hp loss it's not worth I think

            - I think it's best if it's setup and discussed with the players. Who do you want to fight ? At what objective ? Why ?

            Here were my 2 cents

                                I ran through the first half of a combat using my idea of split combats in the same area last night in order to attempt to cut down the complexity of a combat involving 2 battle groups, 4 PC combatants, and 5 non-PC combatants.

                                I split it into 2 PCs and an Ally NPC against 1 NPC and a Battle Group, and 2 PCs and a Battle group against 3 non-PC combatants.

                                They all agreed that the fight was much less complicated and we got through rounds a good deal faster because the split combat allowed them to essentially cut down on their possible targets for combat and not worry about having to be ready to defend against huge amounts of opponents all at once, while still allowing a fight to appear as a chaotic mess of multiple goals all pushing against each other.

                                Next week is going to be part two of the same fight, as the conditions have changed significantly, and we'll be testing a longer, more indepth fight instead of a 2 round brawl with the same concepts.

                                    Some people in the thread about whining because there is not enough Exalted asked me my home-brew "Quick NPC" so I'll share it here.

                                    https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1hPx-Q2jlDgAIZX_d0wQWmJkZMN51jas4QNp5meeQjgo/edit?usp=sharing


                                    I'll try to present it simply in 2 parts.

                                    First part : I think it can be useful for about everyone.

                                    This is essentially a sort of cheat sheet for scores of NPCs. I divided the power level of characters in basically 6, from mediocre to monstrous. Usually power level from 1 to 5 cover what a playable character can have, with level 6 being the strange NPC that have stats beyond what can be achieved with today's game mechanics. I do not claim it to be impartial or always perfectly representing the balance of the game, but usually NPC's from the books are within the appropriate descriptions of my home-brew. Each part is in 3 lines : The second line contains the topic covered and the associated score to each power level on the appropriate columns, the first line is examples of creatures that I see fit the scores given, and a black line separates the following topic. I'm pretty bad at designing cool beautiful files so don't judge me.

                                    I activated comments so if you want to make useful comments I can change the file. You can obviously just copy the file and change it at your own will.

                                    Second part : My personal addition to it

                                    Because I don't like handling essence 5 by 5 and having multiple different NPC's working under different core mechanics, I basically consider everything works like Solars because I ind their functioning simple, ie everyone can add dices to their pool in exchange for motes in a straightforward manner, and I divided essence pools by 5. Each "Essence power" one might use cost 1 Essence if the power is from mediocre to average, and 2 Essence if it's from Strong to Monstruous. This way, if on first round a very old Dragon blooded attacked with full Excellency (Average in Excellencies so cost 1 Essence), and used maximum Charms to add maximum damage because he is specialized in this (so +8 damage with essence, so cost 2 Essence), he used 3 Essence of his 14 pool of Essence. All Essence regenerate by 1 on each round. This basically handles all "numeral" Charms, that somehow gives you mostly dices bonus and small scenaristic power. You can of course use Charms that have more scenaristic effects, for 1 Essence for weaker Charms to 3 Essence to world-defining Charms.

                                    I believe the main weakness of this home-brew is that I didn't add enough examples to it, so I can use it quite fairly with my experience, but really new ST might be confused and use power level very far from the intended power level expected by the game, but it might get a little better if I get some comments to help me.

                                    Also there might be some French because I didn't check everything, but I think almost everything is English understandable. Do not hesitate to ask questions if you have some.

                                            Sure thanks for your questions I will try to explain.

                                            There are 2 different types of lines, but this is not noted anywhere except in my mind so I guess for other people it's not crystal clear.

                                            There are the lines that are "plain basic stats" : Compétence, Précision d'attaque, Malus, Dégats, Points de vie, Défense (Statique) and Soak/Hardness. They are very straightforward : Any character corresponding the description should have these stats. So indeed, an anti-Dawn Caste Abyssal should have Parry/Evade around 6, and a Huge elemental should have Soak around 12 (Note the 12, I will explain why not 16). I think these lines are useful for everyone because they mainly respect what's written in Core Books and are pretty accurate, everyone can use them and they don't have really any "houserule".

                                            Then there are the lines that use Essence or are affected by it : Pool d'essence, Excellencies, Double et reroll, and Bonus soak/damage. As I said previously, I basically took every effect and costs from the Core Books, divided them by 5, averaged them, and made the one that are purely "numeric" work like Solar Excellencies. So for example, huge Elementals will generally have a Soak of 12. Suppose the elemental has a little of spiritual power, so they have an essence pool of 14. When this elemental defends against an attack, he will first use an Excellency to defend himself, and raise his Parry of 4 (Line : Defense (statique), he's huge and used to fight, but maybe not very agile) to 6 (Line : Excellencies (succès), elementals are usually not potent to add lots of successes). For that, he used 1 Essence, passing him to 13. He get hit, so now he soaks with his 12 soak (Line : Soak/Hardness), and he can use 1 essence to raise his soak by 4 to 16 (Line : Bonus soak/damage d'essence, he is an elemental of battle), putting his Essence to 12. He might have a special elemental power that create a puddle of elemental power when damaged and put difficult terrain around him or environmental damage for ennemies that stay around. I'd rate it a pretty powerful power, so it certainly costs 2 Essence, putting him at 10 Essence. Next turn, he will regain 1 Essence making him begin the turn at 11 Essence. (I think Essence is gained at the beginning of the turn but I'm not sure I admit).

                                            Does that help ? The last line with scores "Bonus soak/damage d'essence" might not be clear to other people than me because of the formulation, but it means that on average a character with Charms will obtain the corresponding bonus through use of Essence.

                                            For the rest you described pretty well what I did. For example, the Abyssal Essence 3 has 12 Essence on my home-brew, which makes 12*5 = 70, which approximates his real 66 motes of Essence. For rolling dices, you take the appropriate pool and roll it. I took the habit of just rolling basic dices pool, and considering every excellency to be direct Charm success in order to spend less time counting dices (Line : Excellencies), but you can also just double the number given on this line and use them as Charmed dices to roll instead of Charm Successes. So for example, the Abyssal with a huge weapon will certainly be around 10 dices to attack, and spending 2 of his 12 Essence, he can gain 5 successes (or 10 bonus dices to roll) on his attack.

                                            I think that pretty much cover it. I'm still open for questions if things stay unclear. I think my home-brew should really just be considered a detailed and compulsed chart of NPCs and mechanics that are in the book.

                                                Over in another thread, I was discussing some of the ways to make non-supernatural foes in Exalted be plausible threats to Solar Exalted in combat. While I was writing that post, though, a couple of things occurred to me that I find a bit unsatisfying with battlegroups as they're currently handled. These house rules are attempts to address those issues.

                                                First, while battlegroups do get a bit of a bonus to hit, which can get quite nice if a commander gives them appropriate orders, it's all in the form of a bonus, and doesn't engage the existing way the system uses to handle being "swarmed" - namely, onslaught penalties. If you attack a Solar Melee specialist with five individual mortal soldiers, the last one will actually stand a chance of hitting her, or at the very least, make her spend more motes on things like Bulwark Stance. If you group the same five individuals into a battlegroup, however, they get just +1 to hit once, and the Invincible Sword Princess barely has to strain to improve her Parry. That seems off, to me - battlegroups are supposed to be a time-saver for the ST, not something that critically nerfs the opponents they want to throw, and the PC's charms shouldn't be rendered pointless quite so frequently.

                                                So, my proposed houserule: when a battlegroup attacks an individual, it automatically imposed an onslaught penalty on the target's Defense, equal to the battlegroup's Size. This penalty applies to the target's Defense against the battlegroup's attack, and then stays until the target's turn, as normal for onslaught penalties. Battlegroups can still attack multiple targets, but if they attack multiple targets who are all within Close range of each other, they must divide the onslaught penalty between the targets, rounding down. For instance, if a Size 4 battlegroup of hobgoblins attacks Volfer, Prince Diamond, and Novia Claro, and Volfer and Diamond are within Close range of each other, the onslaught penalty suffered by Volfer and Diamond would only be -2 each, while Novia would suffer the full -4. This is intended to encourage PCs to stay together in fights, and pull stunts like back-to-back fighting and stuff like that.

                                                The second, more minor issue that I'm not really satisfied with how the movement rules handle one character, or a group of them, trying to get past another character - the classic "one warrior bravely guarding the narrow way so that others can escape" scenario. As it stands, someone trying to pass by is doing a disengage, which is always (Dexterity+Dodge), and the opposing character must roll (Dexterity+Athletics). This devalues fighting skill in the "hold the line" scenario, which is, I would argue, an important element. When the Spartans hold the Persian army back at the battle of Thermopylae, it's not their raw strength or skill at athletic pursuit that matters, it's their skill with their weapons.

                                                Therefore, I'd introduce a new combat action, "Hold the Line". This costs 2 initiative, and it places the character who takes it in the path of anyone trying to move past them. Anyone who attempts to move past them through their Close range band must make an opposed roll against the fighter barring their way. The character holding the line rolls (Dexterity+[Brawl or Melee]), depending on how they're fighting, while the person trying to move past them rolls (Dexterity + [Brawl, Dodge, or Melee]). If the fighter holding the line wins the roll, they also inflict withering damage on the opponent equal to their margin of victory, and the opponent can't get past them. If the opponent wins, they move past. A character holding the line can't take other combat movement actions, including their reflexive movement, without abandoning the attempt to block passage, and they can't oppose disengage attempts by the opponent if the opponent isn't trying to disengage past the spot they are guarding. Battlegroups gain a bonus equal to their Size on all contested rolls involved in holding the line, whether attempting it or trying to pass. Additionally, poor Drill gives -2 to such rolls, while Elite Drill gives +2.
