    Luna's Guide to Destroying Creation

    04-19-2024, 05:34 PM
    With love to the original.



    Hello there, faeries and fair folk! I'm Luna! You might remember me from such successful divine domains as Ambassador to the Wyld, Being the Moon, and Exploiting Fair Use Laws to Use Others Artwork Without Asking. I see that you're considering becoming an antagonist to all Creation. An excellent decision, if I may say so myself. I think we can all be frank and acknowledge Creation was a mistake. It's full of suffering, death, diseases that cause crotch-rash. In my sober moment, I wondered if me and Sol have actually done a good job running it. Luckily, my sober moment was 3.5 seconds long and happened in the Second Age, and Luna on drugs loves this place! Now let's pit you against some exalted, I want to see blood!

    What? No. No, stop. Stop. What are you doing? You weren't seriously about to walk up to a group of exalts and start swinging a gossamer sword, were you?

    Yeah, I know I told you to. You don't have to do things just because a god tells you to. When I tell people to go get fucked, they don't immediately drop their pants. I mean, unless I'm using my social charms, but I only do that when it would be hilarious. Which is always. But you get the point. What's your plan to destroy Creation? How are we going to get this faerie invasion started?

    Because you throw sixteen dice for attacks? Okay, yeah, by dragonblooded standards I guess that's okay. But, look. Look. My chosen have an entire book dedicated to their ruleset. You know what you have? A page in the core rules. Just the one! Just the one page. This isn't second edition, and Graceful Wicked Masques didn't have great mechanics in the first place. You can't just walk in, flash your charms like Venus showing off her tits, and expect the Exalted to take you seriously. You need to put in some effort, maybe be more of a subtle, interesting antagonist.

    What? Yes, of course she does. She's the goddess of love and beauty and sex it's a good day when I can get her to keep her shirt on. Look, we're getting sidetracked. I can see you need some help. I'll walk you through the basics of being a faerie antagonist. First things first-







    Luna's Tip #1: You Are Powered By Horror

    Now I know what you're thinking! But Luna -- isn't this a setting where everyone is horrible? The Exalted practice slavery, the Yozi demand sacrifices, and the gods are way too busy doing magical coke to give a damn about their followers. And it's true! I love magical coke. But here's the thing. All those other groups do those things because they want to. Because they choose to. Raksha do not choose to ravish. If you don't ravish a mortal every so often, you calcify, which is a fancy faerie word for dying. You turn into a tree or a stone or some shit and you don't get better.

    That means when an exalt sees you hanging around in Creation, they know with 100% certainty you killed people to get there. Every time you use your powers, they know some mortal child gave up their dreams or their soul to make the essence and gossamer that fuels those abilities. You're like a gun that shoots innocent bystanders as ammunition. Every time you pull the trigger, it doesn't matter if you hit or miss, someone is dying.

    And if you want to be an effective antagonist to the Exalted? Embrace the horror. Lean in.

    Tip Corollary 1A: Manage Your Food Supply

    There are two ways to feed yourself in Creation. One is to be a raider -- you hit a village, eat all the tasty humans you want, and bounce before the Exalted or local gods show up. And if you want to be a not particularly deep antagonist, maybe a combat encounter or two, that's perfectly fine. Just remember my advice about the relative size of your "charms" to my chosen, and don't expect to last very long. But you've got another option.

    Responsible ravishing.

    You know why governments issue hunting licenses? Because if everyone could hunt as much as they wanted, every deer in the forest would be dead in a week. To ensure a stable supply of tasty venison, the hunting rate is capped at the population's natural growth rate. This logic can work for you! Rule over a village of mortals and eat just enough that the population stays pretty much the same. It limits how many faeries can be in the area at once, but you can be there forever. You can become a lasting presence, instead of a raider that does some war crimes and dips. And that lets you build up the core of a story.

    What's your relationship with these mortals? Do they know they're being cultivated? Do you steal your meals or do they offer up sacrifices? Do they worship you like a god, even though you don't particularly want or need their worship? And if they give you people, who do they give? The elderly? The weak? The homeless? Maybe the villagers see you as a blessing, ridding them of these vermin. Or maybe you trade with them. Maybe you offer gold, silver, beneficial mutations in return for sacrifices, and the people of Creation are willing participants in your balorian crusade.

    There's a lot of ways to build a fair folk up as a really interesting regional presence, all by knowing how they interact with the locals they feed upon.

    Tip Corollary 1B: Employ Helpers

    Every faerie in the freehold is another ravenous lamprey mouth to feed. Every faerie in the raiding party is another monster to split the spoils with. There are a lot of reasons why a group of fair folk might not want their numbers to grow, particularly is they're trying to maintain long-term relationships in Creation. So what is a faerie to do if their force is not adequate, but they don't want to bring more raksha into the story?

    Employ minions. Buck ogres don't need souls to survive. Silverwrights don't need souls to survive. Mutants and beastfolk don't need souls. They actually eat regular food if you can believe it! Which makes them very desirable company for fair folk, who don't need money, meat, grain, or any of those things. It's like two animals sharing a den, because one of them only eats cats and the other only eats insects, so they aren't competing with each other for food. And these creatures don't have to be extras either! Beastfolk can be heroic. They can have names, learn martial arts, become significant antagonists in their own right. They can ever exalt as exigents, although I get royalties every time they do because I have a copyright on animal exalts.

    Bottom line, when you finally encounter the exalted, they shouldn't meet you first. They should meet your goblins, your cultists, your right-hand beastfolk, your ogre enforcer, all these fun and interesting creatures that can space out the story and build up your legend. Until, when the players finally meet you, they know you're a mighty overlord who commands many lesser antagonists. You're a Princess of the Wyld, act like it!

    Tip Corollary 1C: Leave a Trail of Corpses and Sorrow

    When mortals are ravished, their bodies don't disintegrate. They often don't even die, but stand around drooling on themselves or screaming. The lights are on but nobody's home. Now you could quietly dispose of their bodies in a shallow grave to keep your image squeaky-clean, but that wouldn't be a very antagonist thing to do! If you did that, what would even start the Solars' revenge arc? No. You want to keep these things around.

    Remind the exalted of just what happens when faeries enter Creation. Maybe they find a mother who has been tending to her comatose son for years, hoping one day he'll "get better" when everyone knows his soul is gone. Or maybe you employ the empty husks as puppets, making them work as servants or backup dancers for your musical number. Or maybe you do actually clean them up. You carefully burn the bodies in the furnace behind your freehold, leaving an artisanal collection of childrens' skulls behind for the exalted to find.

    This advice holds if you're a "good" faerie -- "lol" as the kids say these days -- or a flagrantly evil faerie. You need to regularly murder humans to exist in Creation. The Exalted might accept that if they need you, or feel they can't stop you, but don't ever let them forget it. You aren't an elf, you aren't a cute little human with golden eyes and pointed ears, you are a monster from beyond reality, and when you decide you've had a bad day and want to "binge eating an entire tub of icecream", people scream and bodies hit the floor. The Hague has to get involved. It's bad.







    Luna's Tip #2: Make It All Worth It

    I get it. My first tip got pretty dark. Eating people? Artisanal skull collections? It's pretty grim. But here's the thing. You may eat souls? But you shit miracles.

    Creation is an awful place. Remember I said that in the beginning? Slavery, starvation, disease, blah blah blah. And yeah, the Exalted can sometimes fix some of that, but they're usually not around. And the gods can sometimes fix some of that, but ain't nothing in the world for free baby. You want me to shake my divine ass your way you better take me out for dinner and some burnt offerings first. But the fair folk? You can fix all of that. Because your whole thing is that you don't like how Creation is and you want reality to be another way, and you don't need prayers or sacrifice or burnt offerings or any of that.

    You're on a mission to change the world, and maybe the way you want to change it isn't so awful. Maybe when mortals fall in love with you, it isn't because you beguiled them. Maybe mortals love you because in your freehold, there's always enough food, and there's always clean water, and there are no slave masters to beat them, and no raiders to kill them. Maybe it's because in your world, nobody ever dies of old age, nobody ever shits themselves to death from cholera, and all the hard labor is done by goblins.

    Maybe mortals love you because you gave them that heaviest of burdens: hope.

    And trust me, when the Exalted see the beautiful world you've made, and know that it's powered by all the horrible stuff I talked about in the first tip? They might love you, they might hate you, but raksha they will never forget you.

    Tip Corollary 2A: Sell Your Vision

    I get it. You want to destroy Creation. It's a fair folk thing. But what do you want to replace it with? What's your plan for what everyone will be doing after Creation is gone? Now, you can say, "I plan for all of you to be dead," and like, that's fine. That's the basic fair folk raider answer, you storm into Creation, kill crush destroy. That has its place. But if you want to be a memorable antagonist, shoot for a more seductive answer.

    This is basically selling the Exalted on your Creation fanfiction. Like say, you're a talking winged horse who flies around Creation on a giant cloud. And in your presence, mortals gain the ability to fly -- wings sprout from their backs. Your goblins can fly too, it's a whole theme. You need to sell why that's a good thing. Like yeah, flying is cool, but what's so cool about it that it's worth all the people who had to die to make it happen? Maybe introduce a small child who used her wings to escape bandits, or a couple that can finally get married in the clouds away from their disapproving parents. Don't tell them it's good, show them it's good.

    And don't worry, your vision doesn't have to be all cutsy-wootsy. Let's say your vision is something more like, "THE REALM WILL BURN!" Okay, badass. Show me the humans who willingly follow you, the mortals, exalts, whatever, who fight for your crusade because the Realm sucks. Convince me that after you turn the Empress into the Former Empress that we'll all be happier for it.

    And just to be clear, you don't have to actually be a force for good. Faerie bullshit is a tradition! Just make the argument that you are interesting enough to stick in the Exalted's minds.

    Tip Corollary 2B: Make Excellent Wine

    Have you ever had faerie wine? Stories attribute all sorts of magical properties to it -- that it's addictive, that it beguiles those who drink it, that it's the blood of goblins! All nonsense. It's a gossamer item like any other, and no more or less likely to be enchanted than any other object a faerie has touched. But it is, it is very dangerous. And for one simple reason. It is the finest wine you will ever have.

    A sommelier, an epicurean, might say, this wine tastes like fruit, this wine tastes like oak, this wine tastes airy. But real wine does not taste like fruit, or oak, or a spring breeze. Wine tastes like fermented grapes, the rest is your imagination filling in the gaps. Ah, but faerie wine! That can taste like fruit, or oak, or a spring breeze, or the feeling of warm sun on your face. It can taste like peace and tranquility, like the promise of a heady buzz. It can have just a hint of sweetness, just a hint of acidity, somehow always exactly the way you like it. It can taste like warm childhood memories.

    Without any magical intervention, without beguilement, people who drink faerie wine too long start to think that regular wine -- real wine -- tastes like cat piss. And when that happens, the fair folk own their ass. There's no getting out of that. Those people will never drink real wine again.

    You can see the metaphor. Build beautiful, wonderful, graceful things -- that are entirely dependent on your reality-altering powers to exist. Build beautiful things that can't exist in normal Creation, so the Exalted need to keep you around to keep those beautiful things alive. If you do it right, the Exalted will fall in love with your graceful wicked lies -- and there's no getting out of that for them either.

    Tip Corollary 2C: Just Beguile People

    Okay, now that I've finished talking about how you don't have to beguile people to make them fall in love with you -- you should totally beguile people to make them fall in love with you.

    What? Don't give me that look. If a group of exalts run into a mortal who talks about how you're the best ever, how you saved his child from smallpox and all that shit, do you think they're actually going to check? No! Exalts are busy people. They will not go back to his house to verify the kid used to have smallpox until you healed them. Honestly that guy doesn't even have to have a kid. You can do this with incels. It's hilarious.

    Exalts will judge you by what people are saying about you, and you have mind control powers, so people should be saying what you want them to say. The more your vision for reality seems actually not-that-great for the people in it, the more mind control you use to fill the gaps. Your villagers aren't starving, they're thin! Ask them, they'll say so. Those people's souls didn't get eaten, they're just all taking naps. They said so, all at once, speaking in a uniform monotone. Depending on the sort of story you're going for, this can be a genuinely effective deception, or just build to the horror of finally meeting the fair folk themselves.

    And remember, the Exalted's social charms may be better than yours mechanically, but their charms have to make some sort of sense. A dragonblood can't slap someone and turn them into a sleeper agents who goes crazy ever time they hear the phrase "Ixle Piddlefub." You can. If mortals have been hanging around you too long, their minds should be shaped however you want them shaped. There are no limits -- be creative, be sickening, be uncanny.
