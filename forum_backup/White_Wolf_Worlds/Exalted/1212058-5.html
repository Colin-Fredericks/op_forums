        The "5 Races" Trope isn't suggesting that Krogans are reskinned Dwarves.

        Rather, it argues that if a setting has non-Human races - then you'll usually see 4 predictable extremes.

        Hyper-Masculine (Stouts or Savages - usually muscular, warlike, hirsute)
        Hyper-Feminine (Fairy or Eldritch - usually magical, aloof/alien, ranged)
        Older-Wiser-Powerful (High Men or Fallen - often counselors, leaders)
        Younger-Trickster (Cute or Crafty - smart, cunning, clever)

        These are all in contrast to the Average (Mundane or Humanoid).

        So for example, Krogans and Turians; Dwarves, Dragonborn and Half-Orcs; Orcs, Tauren, Worgen; Ogres and Giants -- all Stouts / Savages. They're all hyper-masculine, muscular or hirsute (or both), grim or dour.
        • Krogan females are a mere 1 in 2,000 of all births now because of the genophage.
        • Bearded-Dwarven ladies was such a long running joke it became a trope.
        • Warcraft Race/Gender demographics also reinforce this. The most masculine races are Dwarves (87.6%), Tauren (86.4%), Orc (81.1%) and Worgen (75.3%) - players simply don't look at those races neutrally (the way Humans, Pandaren, etc are).

        No one is saying "Krogan are Dwarves".

        They're saying "these are the 4 most common archetypes that splinter away from average/mundane/human".

        And then each of these archetypes has different cliches, tropes, perspectives, etc - by selecting one of these races, the player is deciding to engage in those tropes (whether to play along them or rail against them).
        • This is why I take issue with the idea that "muscular, bellicose Orcs" is a bad trope. The trope is what forms Orcs.
          • So when my friend plays a Half-Orc Fighter... he's declaring to the world (the DM & players).... "this is the trope I'm cleaving to - please don't expect me to engage in deep philosophy or social parties or similar frivolous things".

"this is the trope I want to play..... no, my character does not like the stereotypes.... but I, the player, want to engage in that conflict...... a Half-Orc Bard struggling to prove the greatness of his verse - or a Half-Orc Wizard striving to prove their worth against Elves & Co..... is a much more exciting and engaging motivation than being Elven Wizard #458,423.
