    Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
    That just makes things more awesome, though, does it not? In my mind (and in my games!) this is how conflicts within the Celestial Bureaucracy should play out, with a combination of cutthroat office politics in Yu-Shan and "controlling the facts on the ground" in Creation.
    It does! I just wish all the banal-seeming missions had suggestions of this. Because it comes across like every other Exalt splat is always Doing Important Stuff, and Sids vacillate between 50% Intern-level Bullshit and 50% Endings Special Ops Strike Team.


    Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
    This is something I've always struggled with.

    A circle of 3-5 Sidereals who are all in the same place, doing the same thing, is an enormous commitment of BoD resources. Four Bronze Faction Sidereals all in one place at one time is probably either literally every Bronze member in an entire directional Convention, or merely most Bronze members in an entire Conventional direction with some high-powered ringers diverted from other assignments. It's a little better if this is official business, not faction work, because that lowers the ratios, but that's still an enormous number of Sids. Even if all those people are starting characters, if four Sidereals are being thrown at a problem, that problem is probably a really big problem. Like, "you guys are four-fifths of the entire Convention on Deathlords, except for your boss, who is an Elder who basically camps out in Yu-Shan and serves on four other Conventions" big problem.

    That can make running group games tricky, because you whipsaw between "why is every problem we face a world-shattering one" and "it strains believability we're being allowed to idle this long." When of course the reason, out of game, is you have four people who all need to sync their schedules and whose PCs all need to remain more or less together.
    Yeah I think it's an inherent problem to having a flat 100 Sids. Sure that emphasizes that they're overworked, but it makes 5 characters always staying together rather implausible without having to jump through some severe narrative hoops. I hope this is addressed.

    Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
    Both editions haven't made this as clear as they could, but... the Sidereals have day jobs. All that stuff Chejop Kejak does with shadow puppeting the Realm and working hand-in-glove with the Empress and suchly? That, from the formal perspective of the Bureau of Destiny, is his hobby. Stuff he's expected to keep off the books and not allow to interfere with his work. And when he deploys official resources in pursuit of Bronze Faction interests, from another formal perspective, that's a willful act of corruption that really shouldn't be happening. He gets away with it because he's a huge political power player, but he has to do a lot of heavy lifting to make that work. When he dispatches Ahn-Aru to take care of Lilith, he can't just sign the death warrant; he has to cover all the bases. He needs to expend political capital to justify the hit as an official BoD op (which involves lying and then making sure Ayesha Ura can't poke through the lies and get the op cancelled) or he needs to do things like arrange for Ahn-Aru to be on furlough. He has to go work the Immaculate Order to shake loose some more prayers for Jorst, which, again, he has to do off-book, because that's not part of his day job. And he has to do all that while still doing said day job.

    People seemed to have a really hard time getting that.
    Yeah, I think people don't get it for the specific reason that you've outlined: we really have no details about e.g. Kejak's real day job. What does it entail, and rom it, what can we extrapolate about all Sids' day jobs?

    The "hobby," as you call it (and it's an apt assessment), is front and center and definitely described as more important and more time-consuming than the day job, whatever that may be.

    Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
    In my experience, the easiest way to make banal missions work is for them to be part of overall bureaucratic infighting. Sort of like how if you're playing Dragon-Blooded and your House sends you to do something seemingly mundane, it is probably part of the great game of jockeying for power and position, because you're a Dragon-Blooded and unless you're a willful fuckup, you are probably too valuable to waste sweeping floors.

    I once ran a game where my three players spent a month and a half's worth of sessions wondering why their assignments had suddenly gotten so disjointed and trivial. (They were having fun, they were just confused.) And then suddenly one of them went "Wait a minute. I recognize the co-signatory on all of these assignment orders. Didn't he work for that guy we got audited, censured, and demoted?"

    Why yes. Yes he did.

    Things stopped being banal rather fast after that.
    That's a great idea! Some of this issue I think comes from Arcane Fate, because Sids have a hard time carving out little shady areas of influence in Creation due to it (unlike how IRL bureaucrats work - for example, see the ongoing corruption pogroms in China...yes, some of it is probably pretextual, but a lot of it is Xi Jinxing actually addressing decades of built-up sludge).

    I guess you can do this when wearing a Resplendency, then the issue is always maintaining a continuity when your Respendency burns out to the next version.

    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
    Speaking for myself (as someone who likes the Sids and may not be the target audience) Sidereal missions should involve

    - A level of logical sense to the mission : "We want this kingdom here, because we've predicted this great person will arise in it if it does (and will not if it does not), which will mean..." more than "This guy must die. Because Fate... Why are you still in my office?".

    - A goal to change Creation beyond simply maintaining the status quo: "If we do this, new agricultural methods will be invented and used, the population of the region will grow, the city gods and the people will grow fat on the flow of money, Creation will move forward to a more glorious state", rather than "There are exactly 200 peasants in this hamlet; predictions show there we may be at risk of there being 500 peasants in this hamlet in 100 years. Rookie, you need to fix this situation. We do not want any change, ever, under any circumstances."

    This doesn't actually have to be altruistic - it can simply be "This will make the Bureau stronger." Whatever, maybe your mission is to capture new secret martial arts techniques. It just has to achieve something meaningful in changing the status quo, something new, rather than makework or just perpetuating the status quo ad infinitum. You're part of a functional bureau who achieve things and change the world. You build and create and enable and recover and restore. You're not just maintenance men.

    - A degree of latitude by the Sidereal in the field. The Sidereal gets a broad goal to achieve, and then uses his or her ability to accomplish the goal. The Sidereal does not get a series of micromanaged instructions that have no real input other than "How do I apply my weird Sidereal magic to these ends?". Comms with your bosses should be hard, and they should be busy, to create a feel where you're not on your cellphone to your mentor all the time and are working off your gut in a situation where intel and your briefings (which really resemble a meeting between the wizards of Middle Earth as much as an intel briefing) haven't prepared you enough for what you face and the actual issues in the field.

        Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post
        Were players ever really THAT bothered by PC infrastructure? It never really bugged me, as it was clear that that was all run by the ST, and my groups always had good relationships that way (even when it wasn't me!). The worst one for me was the Sidereals, but only because it was boring...
        The problem with Sidereals in particular is that the 2e presentation of their relationship to Yu-Shan at large was extremely one-note and hostile. It gave the implication that everyone outside the Bureau of Destiny was positively seething with absolute, all-consuming hatred of the Sidereals because of the Usurpation and would gladly execute them en masse if they could only find proof, which of course there was none because those dastardly Sidereals broke the Mask.

        From my own observations, this basically had the effect of making Sid players go into siege mentality. Instead of going out and interacting with Yu-Shan, they would hole up in the Bureau and only engage with Heaven as a whole when absolutely necessary. This basically wastes the entire setting of Yu-Shan for Sids, because who really wants to deal with that kind of shit?

        Interestingly, this was largely from the same book that had the "Dragon-Blooded aren't considered real Exalts" thing Mercushio mentioned, which makes me wonder a bit about the writers.

        Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
        - A degree of latitude by the Sidereal in the field. The Sidereal gets a broad goal to achieve, and then uses his or her ability to accomplish the goal.
        I feel like this should be encouraged whenever possible. Half the fun of playing a Sidereal is finding inventive solutions and ways to apply your magic that may not be immediatley obvious. I like to think of Sidereals as the Bullshit Exalted; their magic works works best when they're bullshitting the Universe/GM hard, fast and outragerously enough that the Universe believes it. I'm going to put the next bit in spoilers just because it's a character story. And also long.

        Okay, so the last game I played, I was a Chosen of Secrets operating out of the Lap. Eventually, the Solars in the group got tired of the Realm government and it's leader, the ever-popular and high approval Sesus Chenow. Up until that point they had been living secretly, but when the battle started by Auspicious Prospects for Secrets* went off and said that The Plan was that it was time the Solars' identities became known.

        I had an NPC Sidereal as a superior in the city, and she was heavily Bronze (I was indie). As such, she had been supporting the Realm's rule, and had take a Dragon-Blooded officer as a lover. He of course was taking place in the battle, and while a compotent fighter was no match for the Solars, the Dawn's army and the Eclipse's Lunar mate. Best he could really hope for was a messy death. The interesting bit is that they had just had a daughter together; she had apparently forgotten (or just never took) her Maiden Tea.

        The problem was, as I mentioned, I was Secrets, and not so good at any sort of talky thing. I could have just sent a message back to the Realm or something, but lacked legitimacy; as far as they were concerned, it would be a random rumor from the satrapies, at least until months later when they realized that the food shipments from the Lap had dried up. What I was good at was Stealth, to extreme munchkin levels even by Exalted standards. It took several rounds of whispering in his ear with Subordiante Inspriation Technique -- he had a family to think of now, there was nothing to be gained by dying pointlessly, he would serve the Realm far more by raising the alarm, there was no shame in fleeing from the overwhelming force of several Anathema -- but I got him to run far enough in advance of the PCs that they never realized he was there.

        So in one fell swoop I managed to fulfill the will of the GM Jupiter, ingraitate myself to my superior by protecting her family (I had previously hidden her daughter at the Immaculate Temple, which the other PCs decided not to destroy because civilians were fleeing there), and take over her position when she was reassigned for bungling the situation so badly (she had, among other things, summoned a Second Circle to try and deal with the PCs and had completely failed to bind it). And the latter ingraitated myself with the other PCs, because I was able to give them "official Sidereal support" of their administration.

        *One of our house rules was a fairly large overhaul to Auspicious Prospects, because of the fact that it didn't actually do anything. Aside from making it one Charm with four 1 XP repurchases instead of five seperate ones, we ruled that the Maiden whom you had the Prospects for could remotely activate it to give the Sidereal an optional goal for the scene, according to their (read: the GM's) Plan. If you failed nothing bad happened, but if you succeeded you dissipated a point of Paradox.
