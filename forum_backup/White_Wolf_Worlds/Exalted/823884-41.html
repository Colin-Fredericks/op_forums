    The Unburdened Blade (Moonsilver Reaper Daiklave, Artifact 4)

    This blade was forged by an ancient Lunar sorceress, a shapeshifting crone who lived as a hermit among snow-capped mountains as far from civilization as she could get in creation during the first age. It was crafted in her final years; a gift for her newly exalted solar mate meant to guide him in her absence and keep him from being snared by the world that had, in her mind, taken from her his previous incarnation.

    Fate was not on her side however, and the usurpation claimed the life of her young mate soon after her own death. Given the effect the blade has had on some of its less carefull wielders though, the intentions behind the gift might not have been entirely pure, or perhaps the execution a bit overzealous. Unlike most moonsilver artifacts, the weapon is shunned by most Lunars who know its history despite its undeniable power.

    ------

    While attuned this thin moonsilver reaper daiklave is astoundingly light and implores anyone that attunes it to forsake any other burdens that might weigh them down and live their life free as a bird. As long as the Solar or Lunar that wields it claims no possessions beyond what they could carry on their person (without the use of any strength-enhancing charms or magic), and claims rulership over no lands or men, they will find their blade always swift and ready to strike granting one non-charm success on all join-battle rolls.

    The wielder can summon or create beings with spells such as Demon of the First Circle/Summon Elemental or sorcerous workings and keep the bonus, but they must be called to fullfill a certain task or purpose rather than being bound to the wielder as slaves.

    (I've been back and forth on the attunement bonus. Obviously it's very good with Single Point, but I don't think in general as strong as the +1 Acc & post soak damage from Adorei and the requirements are a lot harsher. I made it a success over dice because 2 dice are stronger with rerolls and double nines and 1 die just seems too weak for all the trouble.)

    ------

    Pierce the Folly of Greed

    Cost -; Mins: Essence 1
    Type: Permanent
    Keywords: None
    Duration: Permanent
    Prerequisites: None

    The swords teaches its wielder to see through the folly of greed, in himself and others, for it makes you hunger for things that only tie you down. This evocation grants a permanent +2 bonus to Resolve vs. attempts to bribe the wielder, and a -1 penalty to the Guile of those motivated by lust for wealth and power over others against the wielders Read Intentions actions.

    ---

    Cut Through all Restraints

    Cost: 3m; Mins: Essence 1
    Type: Reflexive
    Keywords: Uniform
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisites: None

    Drawing strength from its wielders resolute desire to remain unfettered, the blade moves with impossible speed to strike away any attempt to restrain them.

    This charm applies a penalty equal to the wielders resolve on any attempt do restrain or grapple him that he attempts to parry or clash with the Unburdened Blade, as well as any attempt do disarm the weapon.

    Once the wielder has unlocked this evocation, any attempt to cut physical restraints such as chains or ropes with the blade recieve one non-charm success.

    Special Activation Rules: This charm can only be used when the wielder is in accord with the requirements to gain the blades attunement bonus.

    (I don't know if 3m is too much. The charm is obviously modeled on Doe Eyes Defense and in some ways much worse with not being usable against most attacks, not potentially giving you initiative, and there not being any passive Resolve adders like there's Guarded Thoughts Meditation for Guile. OTOH you can combine it with Hail Shattering Practice + Fervent Blow if someone is silly enough to try to grapple or disarm you.)

    ---

    Unburdened Blade

    Cost: -; Mins: Essence 2
    Type: Permanent
    Keywords: None
    Duration: Permanent
    Prerequisites: Pierce the Folly of Greed, Cut Through all Restraints

    Growing confident in a wielder that has learned to resist the bonds of material things, the blade feels the burden of its own worries lifting and it becomes light as a feather. When this Evocation is awakened, the blade gains a permanent +1 increase to its Accuracy.

    ---

    Sever all Ties

    Cost: 1 wp, special; Mins: Essence 3
    Type: Reflexive
    Keywords:
    Duration: One Scene
    Prerequisites: Unburdened Blade

    When those things you value most are used against you or prevent you from having the freedom the blade knows is best for you, it whispers in your mind to just let it all go, and with its guidance you can. Activating this evocation the wielders cares and worries for the world fade away, and in their place remains only self preservation and the purpose woven into the blade.

    All the wielders intimacies except those made involatile by charms such as Righteous Lion Defense or Ties to a Solar or Lunar mate are suppressed; for the duration of the charm the wielder acts as if they do not exist and no new intimacies can be gained. Instead the wielder takes on an involatile Defining Principle that his personal freedom is paramount.

    While this evocation is active the wielder also gains one automatic success on all combat actions, but only while fighting to protect his physical person, the blade itself (while this charm is active these are seen as the same), his mate, or his personal freedom.

    Giving in to the blade is never without cost, and frequent usage of this power comes at a terrible price.

    The first time in a year this evocation is used, the wielder gains its Principle at a Minor level unless he already has it at that or a higher level in which case it remains unchanged. Any subsequent acivations during the same year will increase the Principles intensity by one step. This intimacy cannot be decayed in a year in which this evocation has been used, and cannot be used in a year in which it has been decayed.

    At the end of any scene where this charm is used, the wielder will also lose one intimacy of the same intensity as this Principle, or two intimacies of a lower intensity if one of equal intensity does not exist. These intimacies are chosen by the storyteller but the blade goes for those intimacies that most strongly oppose its purpose first. Again only intimacies protected from decay by magic like Righteous Lion Defense or Ties to Lunar/Solar mates are safe.

    Special Activation Rules: Unlike most charms or evocations, the willpower cost of this charm is payed at the end of the charms duration rather than on activation. If the wielders temporary willpower at that point is 0, no willpower need be payed.

    (Social defense by going temporarily quite insane seemed pretty lunar/moonsilvery, but it's possible the effect of suppressing intimacies like this is just a bad idea. OTOH maybe the penalties are actually so harsh no one would ever pay 8 solar xp to have it when you can barely ever use it if you like your character not being insane or caring about things. The power seemed naratively fitting for the artifact but its probably deeply flawed in play.)

                        I've been contemplating an idea for an artifact (a white jade daiklave, named Gravedigger).

                        Tentatively, the first Evocation would allow the wielder to shove someone towards or away from another person on the battlefield. I was thinking this could just work as a special form of rush (Dexterity + Athletics, possibly resisted by Dexterity + Dodge instead since the aim is "don't get shoved by earth manipulation" rather than "keep your distance from the guy coming at you"). But I worry that it may be bad design space to let someone manipulate an opponent's position like that, especially if they don't even have to be at close range to shove them toward someone else (maybe they should?). As a side effect it might also be useful for large-scale earthmoving outside combat (although maybe that should be the bonus for attuning the thing instead?).

                        The second would be a close-range thing designed to make it difficult for a crashed opponent to escape from close range. I don't see why this would be inherently unreasonable, but I'm not sure what would make for an appropriately brutal increase in difficulty without being completely unfair. (This is kinda the blade's signature trick -- if the opponent is incapacitated with this in effect, it drags them all the way six feet under.)

                        If anyone has suggestions, I'm listening. This thing probably needs 1-2 more Evocations, even for an Artifact 3, but most of my ideas for crazy battlefield manipulation seem like really bad ideas, so maybe it should top off with a solid ranged attack instead?

I think that the drawback of being able to be disarmed is nasty enough without being easier to hit. You cannot use Melee parry while grappling either. Does this seem reasonable?
                            2. Is this a 4-dot Artifact? It seems like more of a 3.5. Do I need to add a little something more or does it seem on par with a 4?
                            3. Do you have any suggestions about how to clean up the independent movement timing? I struggled with clarity there.

                            (Edit)

                            Spinelash (White Jade Direlash, Artifact ••••)

                            The true origins of this weapon are lost to time. One legend claims that Spinelash is the fossilized remains of an actual species of serpent, dead ages past. Another says that it is the creation of a mad sorcerer whose dream of creating a living weapon cost him his life after it strangled him moments after its birth from wyld energies. A third describes Spinelash as an enslaved spirit gifted by a powerful demon to the Solar that mastered him at the height of the First Age. However it came to be, its ownership has been more remarkable and better documented. Spinelash is a weapon of assassins that passes from wielder to would-be victim. The white jade direlash can sense when a victim of its owner would be a more appropriate partner. It will attempt to turn on or abandon its owner if it discovers another to whom it wishes to be attuned. Spinelash has been wielded by a string of assassins after it was rediscovered during the Second Age in a deep cave by a clever bandit named Rufo that had gone to ground there. Rufo became a notorious power in the Eastern woodlands he plundered until Spinelash robbed Rufo of its power as he tried to kill a Dragon-blooded mercenary that had been dispatched to rid the countryside of him. That mercenary likewise fell to Spinelash’s treachery. And so on, and so on. It now rides the hip of a newly-Exalted Night caste merchant prince who has been exiled from his home in Nexus. Is this master the partner the weapon has been seeking or simply another that will be sacrificed to its disloyal whims?

                            Spinelash is comprised of white jade immaculately carved to resemble the vertebrae of a long constrictor. The jade would be nearly indistinguishable from actual bones of an impressively long snake, except for sharp serrations on each vertebrae and the unblemished, stark white color. The “bones” are connected with what looks at first glance to be black leather, but has the feel and tensile properties of sinew. The pommel is jade wrapped with the same connective material that does not easily slip in the hand. At the base of the pommel is the carved skull of a snake with two hearthstone sockets where the eyes would be. Spinelash has the unnerving tendency to appear to move when at the periphery of one’s vision. It doesn’t actually move under its own power though…right?

                            Spinelash has two hearthstone “eye” sockets in the carved snake skull at the base of the pommel.

                            Special Note: Spinelash may eventually abandon or turn on its owner. What form this takes varies: it may simply cease working in battle, or slither into the hand of the intended victim it's been ordered to kill, try to strangle its owner in their sleep, or silently slither away in the dark of night. If it does so (and the owner survives it), another Artifact will likely fall into the lap of the very, very lucky Exalt soon.

                            Evocations of Spinelash
                            A Solar or Dragon-Blooded who attunes to Spinelash may explicitly use Brawl charms through it that grapple, unhorse, or disarm. Attacks, grappling control rolls, and grappling combat actions (such as savaging, throwing, etc.) made with it always use the Melee ability, but Brawl charms such as Vicious Lunge (p.275) can be activated while using it. A grapple using Spinelash can be ended immediately by disarming the Exalt wielding it.

                            Spinelash has a jealous nature. It sees the work it does with its Exalted wielder as an exclusive partnership and brooks no competition. An Exalt that attempts to wield a weapon in the offhand while using Spinelash must spend a Willpower per round or lose attunement with the artifact (the committed motes are lost). As long as its partner abides by this mandate, however, they do not suffer the normal defense penalty while using Spinelash to grapple. The weapon cannot be used to grapple and to parry at the same time; Evasion or an unarmed parry must be employed instead.

                            Grip of the Constrictor
                            Cost: 2m Mins: Essence 1 Type: Reflexive Keywords: Decisive-only Duration: One grapple Prerequisite Charms: None

                            The Exalt feeds Essence into Spinelash after a successful Decisive grapple gambit, imploring it to add its strength to the endeavor. It shifts along its victim to find the most vulnerable places to squeeze and mercilessly begins to constrict them. Spinelash grants two automatic successes on the contested control roll. Also, during the grapple, the Exalt does not lose turns of control for being attacked, only for being damaged.

                            The weapon loathes giving up its prey and actively assists in holding them tight.                             However, Spinelash's reluctance to give up its victim can also be a hindrance. If the Exalt wishes to release a grapple without throwing or incapacitating the target first while using this evocation, the Exalt must drop the weapon or spend a point of Willpower to overcome its desire to keep constricting.

                            The Stirring Snake
                            Cost: 2m, 2i, special Mins: Essence 2 Type: Reflexive Keywords: Perilous Duration: One grapple Prerequisite Charms: Grip of the Constrictor

                            Whispering to the carved jade head on the pommel, the Exalt can begin to ask Spinelash to move on its own. When grappling an opponent under the effects of Grip of the Constrictor where there are at least two rounds of control remaining, the Exalt can use this evocation in lieu of a grappling combat action (even immediately after succeeding on the grapple gambit). By giving up two rounds of control in addition to the other costs, the Exalt may let go of Spinelash and attempt a disengage while the weapon continues to constrict the target via savaging attacks. If the grappled opponent is the only enemy in close range, the disengage automatically succeeds, as the target cannot move.

                            After the disengage (successful or not), Spinelash's attacks must be directed using attack actions, but they can be put into flurries at only a -1 penalty to attack. It cannot perform any action butsavagingattacks against the target already grappled on behalf of the Exalted. If the target becomes incapacitated, escapes the grapple, or Spinelash is successfully attacked (using the Exalt's Melee parry as a defense), it will drop to the ground and slither back to its partner at one range band per round, sliding back up into the wielder's hand when it enters close range. If Spinelash loses sight of its partner or falls beyond long range, it will follow until it catches up as long as it is attuned. Spinelash moves on the Exalt’s initiative, just before the Exalt.

                            Special activation rules: In order to activate this evocation, at least one of hearthstone “eye” sockets must have a functioning (non-inert) stone placed in it. Hearthstones cannot be removed while the evocation is active.

                            Choking Malice
                            Cost: —, (1wp) Mins: Essence 2 Type: Reflexive Keywords: Permanent Duration: Permanent Prerequisite Charms: Grip of the Constrictor

                            This evocation improves the effects of its prerequisite. Spinelash relishes every opportunity to crush or choke the air out of its victims. Subjects under the effects of Grip of the Constrictor cannot breath or speak and are subject to suffocation rules as if they were being forcibly drowned (p.232). Any living being that needs to breathe will be incapacitated after (Stamina x 2) rounds. If at any point the grapple is interrupted, the effect ends.

                            If a target being crushed enters initiative crash while being suffocated in this way, the Exalt may reflexively spend 1 point of willpower to cause them to expel most of their remaining air in a devastating rush. If they cannot escape or end the grapple by the end of their next turn, they are incapacitated.

                            The Slithering Lash
                            Cost: 10m, 1wp Mins: Essence 3 Type: Simple Keywords: Dual Duration: One scene Prerequisite Charms: The Stirring Snake

                            Once per day, the Exalt can awaken Spinelash entirely. The mouth at the end of the pommel opens with an otherworldly hiss as it slithers out of the Exalt’s hand. The weapon can move around independently for the duration of the scene. The Exalt can direct its movement and attacks at up to long range without taking combat actions to do so. It uses the Exalt's Melee attacks, lashing out as directed. It will perform a clash grapple attack with anyone who tries to pick it up.
                            On the turn The Slithering Lash is activated and any turn the charm is still in effect, if there are no enemies in close range, it may immediately rush an enemy in short range using the Exalt’s roll or move one range band if not in close or short range of any enemies.

                            An enemy can try to end the effect with a difficulty 5 gambit targeting the weapon, but Spinelash defends with the Exalt's Melee Parry. If successful, Spinelash gives up and slithers back to its partner at one range band per round, sliding back up into the wielder's hand when it enters close range. If Spinelash loses sight of its partner or falls beyond long range, it will follow until it catches up as long as it is attuned. Spinelash moves on the Exalt’s initiative, just after the Exalt.

                            If Spinelash incapacitates a target while moving independently, reset this evocation. Otherwise, reset it at midnight.

                            Special activation rules: In order to activate this evocation, both hearthstone “eye” sockets must have a functioning (non-inert) stone placed in it. Hearthstones cannot be removed while the evocation is active. The Exalt may reflexively activate this evocation at a 5m discount immediately after a victim of The Stirring Snake is incapacitated. Spinelash awakens easily after it takes down its prey.

                                Primodial Animus, (Orichalcum/Jade Daiklaive ****)

                                Concept and Concern- I wanted a daiklave that a battle sorcerer could use not only to give their melee attacks an elementally themed “magic” feel to them, but also augment their sorcery and enable them to cast effectively while remaining a melee threat. I started to design this with the intent that I could use it in tandem with the shaping Ritual “Talisman Of Ten Thousand Eyes”, swapping the talisman for the sword. Stunting with the sword during combat and sorcery felt very cool to me in theory, and of course the Merits would need some work if I actually managed to play a campaign with this sword. I was slightly inspired by the 2e Sorcery Capturing Chord, but understand that being able to instantly cast a sorcery seemed a bit much. Instead, I wanted to give the feeling of a swordsman understanding the flow of Essence and being able to repurpose Essence and use otherwise wasted energy to fuel sorcery. My concern is mechanical, mostly, and trying to figure out if the attunement bonuses are effective and appropriate for the cost and trying to balance the evocations between too much and too little. Bigger than that though is my concern about the lack of the capstone. For purpose of making no one element outshine the rest, I wanted to make my initial evocation both the bread and butter and the capstone, through higher levels of play. I’m less concerned about overall power of the evocations and more focused on providing a sense of versatility to the wielder. Thank you in advance for all feedback! And I apologize for the long winded nature of the artifact, I fleshed it out 100%, so background is included.

                                Description- Animus was crafted by a First Age Lunar for his solar mate, a First Age Twilight Sorcerer who mastered both body and mind, using specifically sorcery from afar and with lasting effects to wear his opponents down before sweeping in with his blade to finish off his enemies after they had been wracked by the masterfully cast sorcery.

                                The sword style of the original user prioritized quick and accurate strikes over powerful and overwhelming might, and as such the evocations of the blade prioritize accuracy and strengthening the weaker strikes of the blade using elemental force. The reason behind this is that the original owner understood that dividing their time between both scholarly pursuits and swordsmanship meant a gap in some department or another, so rather than try to match the physical might of a warrior, he relied on his wits and dexterity to outmaneuver such swordsmen. Wielders of this blade have had their fighting style compared to the swift and fluid grace of dancers.

                                The sword throughout history has been documented and seen very rarely, but has always been wielded by heroes that brought positive change to the world through their actions, whether it be the defense of innocents or the upheaval of dark forces. Especially skilled users were known to be able to use the sword as a focal point for their sorcery, using the blade as one would use a conductor's baton, commanding the flow of Essence intertwining to create masterfully orchestrated sorcery.

                                The blade of the sword is straight and composed of orichalcum, the handle and hilt of the blade is composed of a five jade composite and set with a small sphere of each color of jade that the hilt is composed of (Black, Blue, Red, White, Green). The Old Realm characters for “Wood” “Fire” “Water” Earth” and “Air” are written on the flat of the blade in jade lettering corresponding to the element channeled and they glow with ethereal light when the corresponding Essence type is channeled by the user. On the cross-guard of the weapon are two hearthstone settings, one on either side.

                                Evocations of Primordial Animus-
                                A Solar or Dragon Blooded sorcerer that attunes to Animus awakens the first Evocation at no cost. An Exalt attuned to Animus can spend an additional 3m to attune in order to gain deep insight into the blending of sorcery and swordplay. While channeling sorcery, if the user makes a successful attack with Animus, the sorcerer can roll (Occult), gaining the successes as sorcerous motes. This counts as gaining motes for the purpose of not interrupting casting. If the motes meet or exceed the cost of the sorcery, the sorcerer may expend 2i to cast the sorcery immediately.

                                Sorcerous Blade Channeling
                                Cost: 7m, 1wp (1i) Mins: Essence 1
                                Type: Simple
                                Keywords: None
                                Duration: One Scene
                                Prerequisites: None

                                The sorcerer uses the elemental energy to empower Animus, causing an elemental effect to persist in the sword attacks. Animus vibrates and a single Old Realm word on the flat of the blade lights up with Essence, gaining one of the following powers. The effects also enhance the Evocation of the corresponding element while active. Each element is available only after unlocking the ability to purchase the matching Evocation.

                                Air- A gust of wind emanating from Animus disorients nearby enemies and speeds the swordsman's movement. The user may move a single range band in the direction of their choice without making disengage action after a successful dodge or parry.                                     TaiMageBlade,

                                    I am here looking for assistance from more experienced Exalted players as well (see post directly above), so I cannot profess any specific expertise. However, I've waited in vain for feedback, so here is my 2 cents for what it is worth in case nobody else responds to you.

                                    First, I should note that I really like the idea behind the artifact, especially the concept that an entire chronicle could be built around a quest of discovery unlocking the powers. One concept I had when building characters was a Resistance-Supernal character that wanted to survive all the greatest elemental wonders in Creation to celebrate and advertise the return of the Solars; his goal was to jump into a volcano, surf an avalanche, dive to the bottom of the ocean, fly through a tornado, etc. So a journey to the edges of Creation to meditate upon the elemental poles seems really fun.

                                    I think the modular feel of the item plays very well into the concept: being able to learn the evocations in a different order is kind of integral, which makes the capstone part more difficult. However, if you were looking to shuffle things around a bit in order to fix that, here are some ideas to do it: First, can you imagine getting all this at Essence 2? Too much. Use an Essence 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 progression to the powers (or 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 for a shorter campaign), so that you can only learn Sorcerous Blade Channeling and any one other evocation at Essence 1. Then, instead of having them all grow at Essence 3+, each time you learn another power, give a bonus to the previous ones, representing the character's deeper knowledge of the elements. For example, when you learn the second elemental power, grant an extra die, a reduced cost, or more range, etc. to the previous powers. This way, the one you got first will grow in power as you master new ones. Or, you could reverse it and give the new benefit and all the previous benefits to the new power making the new element the most powerful.

                                    Sorcerous Blade Channeling has some pretty vague mechanical bits that ought to be cleaned up. For example, it says "on a successful attack" but is that for both decisive and withering? Some of the powers seem more suited to one or the other. Moving other characters is a complicated endeavor. Does the Air version move them away from you, wherever you like, or toward something else? Water will almost assuredly have an 8 difficulty at Essence 3 which seems really high; does this difficulty need to come down or does the roll need to go away and the effect have a per use cost? The Fire power seems more like a decisive damage power while the Water seems like a withering one. The Wood power is so sweet for sorcerous sword-wielders in that it keeps your spell from degrading. I'm not thrilled with the need to whack someone to do it though. How often are you going to want to pause casting to simply hit something with your sword rather than do something else that is so crucial that you are putting off the spell? I LOVE the Earth power, but kind of wish that the various pieces of the whole worked at different times like that.
                                    All this together makes me want to suggest something more like this:

                                    Open with a Simple action rather than reflexive, as powering up for the fight with access to so much versatility seems like it warrants an action. Ease back on the cost to swap though to 1i; I think this variant will be heavy on swapping. (Keep in mind that you could make Sorcerous Blade Channeling onerous and costly at the beginning and back off of costs and reduce actions, etc. as you gain elements.) You can open into whichever element you want.
                                    Fire: When you make a decisive attack, pay (cost) and get (x dice) extra damage if you hit.
                                    Water: When you make a withering attack, the target loses (x) extra initiative that you don't gain if you successfully hit (even if you deal no damage). If they are crashed with a Water withering attack, they will drown per the heavy exertion rules (see page 232) until they are no longer crashed.
                                    Air: When you successfully dodge, you may move one range band away from the attacker without making a disengage roll. If the attacker was rushing you, end the rush (any other enemies may follow per normal rushing rules).
                                    Earth: Steal (x) initiative on parry. Awesome ability. Super flavorful.
                                    Wood: In lieu of moving during your turn, you may root yourself to the ground and surround yourself with a defensive bramble. You have light cover while rooted, but moving or swapping elements ends the rooted condition. If you end your turn rooted, you do not lose sorcerous motes if you failed to acquire any that turn. If you begin your next turn still rooted, gain a sorcerous mote.

                                    Not sure on the values of some of this, but these are the directions I think of to solve some of the problems you've described.

                                    I had also considered what the weapon would look like if you could not access the yet-to-be-obtained elements with the core power until you acquire the greater power. Maybe you could activate the element once you have unlocked permission to buy the evocation, even if you cannot get it yet? If there is a severe power level concern, I could see going this route.

                                    Edit: This is probably not a Mute power. Anything where you are using your essence to light up your sword like a light show seems...whatever the opposite of Mute is.
