        While I enjoy responding to multiple people individually, for succinctness, I'll try to compress this.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
        God-Bloods, Elemental-Bloods, Fae-Bloods, Demon-Bloods and Ghost-Bloods have a lot to wrestle with.

        Half-Solars? Not really. Solars are super-humans with sun-themes. So you're half-human, half-super human. So a weaker super human.
        You have this backwards, friend!

        The various (X)-Blooded play to supernatural tropes - but most of their descriptions describe how irrelevant those supernatural heritages are to the world.

        You have Demon-Blooded who hunt demons, Ghost-blooded who exorcise ghosts, Fae-Blooded who can shrug off the Wyld..... the point is that none of their progenitors can influence the setting the way the Exalted can....and the half-humans often can side-step weaknesses explicit to their ancesotrs. And politically, the Immaculate Order might disapprove of liaisons - but they still recruit them.

        The mixed heritage children who are neither fully Exalted nor fully human are a completely different story. At no point are we led to believe one of them could viably usurp their parent. Instead...
        • Their write-up describes them being used as favored slaves and concubines. The Exalted were not necessarily kind.
          .
        • Their write-up describes the ensuing genocide (when the Solars were overthrown). Humanity & the Dragon-Blooded were not sympathetic.
          .
        • A Dragon-Touched isn't "a cool super human" - it's a failure as a Terrestrial Exalt, someone prone to attract all the same enemies the Terrestrials have - without the power to repel them. A Lunar won't care that you're "merely Dragon-Touched", so long as you have a useful face to infiltrate Dynasts. If the Tepet Legions massacred half a town, the local mortals won't be forgiving of you just because you're not a "full fledged" tyrant.
          .
        • A Moon-Born isn't "a cool super human" - not only the most populous of the batch, they're also kin to Beastmen, Lunars, Chimera, and all the horrors associated with them. The Wyld Hunt will kill you because you might just be a Lunar in disguise (or a spy for a Lunar). You will be dragged into Lunar politics the second anyone recognizes the kind of magic you wield.

        This has real world parallels which explains why the term for the mixed heritage children who are neither fully Exalted nor fully human was a deliberate (and good) choice.

        Calling them "Anathema Spawn" is too negative.

        Calling them "Grandchildren of the Gods" too positive.

        It's their official 2E term that sits on the fence, leaving the speaker and listener both "up in the air" as to how the other person intends it to be taken. Whether you view them as the "union of two peoples" (positive) or "bastards of an invading empire" (negative), those connotations define the.

        If you change the name, then you scrub out all that history and are left with "uh, boring super humans".

        Think of the Haitian Revolution - where mixed heritage individuals had an uncomfortable "in-between-ess". As the mobs massacred the French, mixed heritage individuals had no way of knowing which way they'd be treated. Would they be embraced as brothers-in-arms? Or demonized as conspirators? Some of them had supported slavery. Some of them were eager to see the French eliminated. Others went both ways, eager to see the French slaughtered - but then planning to step into their roles using their better education and resources.

        It's not a word that has negative/positive implications.

        It's the context.

        Racists Frenchmen and resentful Haitians would both refer to mixed heritage individuals negatively. And like with the Golden-Children, you could spend a life enslaved just to then be slaughtered when your masters are overthrown.

        But people who see mixed heritage individuals as a symbol of unity see it positively; Frenchmen who think mixed heritage individuals are lovelier than Europeans are using it positively; Haitians who see mixed heritage individuals as possessing education and resources, as well as mixed perspective - and who thus see them as a potential good for society - see it positively. And like with the Golden-Children, you could benefit from these stereotypes (for good or ill).

        It's much like playing a Half-Orc or Tiefling in D&D. They are not merely "fancy looking humans" - Orcs and Devils have specific roles in the setting - which affects how their mixed heritage offspring are treated. While in-character my characters can feign moral outrage - or be legitimately frustrated - by stereotypes.... out-of-character, I pick those "races" because I want to wrestle with those scenarios.

        This is what the mixed heritage children who are neither fully Exalted nor fully human are about.

                        So my thoughts on Golden Children and Starblessed respectively.

                        Golden Children are born to the perfection of the Unconquered Sun, at the low levels I see everyday superpowers like being born without birth defects and never taking sick days. Such passive perfection rounds them rather than sharpens them like a Solar Exaltation does. They lack their Solar parent's incredible aptitudes but they either never fail or recover from it very gracefully.

                        Starblessed have fate manipulating powers but without the administrative access to the Loom of Fate that makes many of their parent's powers work. Instead the only fate a Starblessed has to play around with is their own, this often means they've got insights into future ordained events and how to bring those about while some are acutely aware of their lifespan as it counts down.
