                    You could perhaps houserule that Hardness was the negative initiative threshold a battlegroup had to pull you down to before its attacks started doing lethal damage. So if you had Hardness 5, you'd have to be at -5 init before they started actually hurting you. If you did that, though, I'd suggest that it would be better to let them do the damage to you as soon as the attack went over your threshold - that is, if you have Hardness 5, if a battlegroup attacked and did enough to drop you to -6 initiative, you'd actually go to -5, and take two dice of lethal damage. The current system, where the battlegroup only does lethal when you start crashed, feels like it would be too hard to arrange under this "Hardness threshold" mechanic.

                                                        This makes sense for light Artifact armor, but not so much for things like Twilight Anima or Charm based Hardness. When your Hardness is there for scene or longer, OK, you're preserving resources. If you're facing a bunch of low damage decisive attacks and have to use lots of motes to shut them all down, or risk some damage from a few of them, you're just picking which of your resources to be low on when you face Ligier.

                                                        And none of this is really comparing your potential to have more strength for the big boss fight to other methods. Having a base or scene long DV that's high enough low level baddies can't hit you with an onslaught negating Charm as backup could easily have you better off that trying to rely on low level Hardness.                                                         If I'm worried about enemies using a lot of low initiative decisive attacks, Searing Quicksilver Flight is something a Solar can start with without going Dodge Supernal, and makes trying to spam those sorts of attacks exceedingly dangerous. An opponent that's going to try to hit me with a 3 initiative attack is a lot more dangerous if they hit, deal no damage, and reset to 3, than if they miss and crash themselves to -5.

                                                        I'm not saying low level Hardness is useless, but in the grand scheme of things, it feels like people are overselling how useful it is in context of everything else going on.
