                                I have to disagree with The Count on this one. An always on Terrestrial working for a spell seems Terrestrial 3. You are weakening storms, and storms would destroy property but be less damaging to people except maybe at finesse 5. A working seems like the right way to bless area. The reverse of enhancing a natural aspect, you are weakening it. The hurricanes would still destroy stuff, but less stuff. Context matters a lot on workings, as does intent. If you want to actual fix the weather so storms aren't constant and the rain isn't as harsh, Celestial 2, you are not blessing the area with better weather (weakening the storms) but improving the weather (shorter storm seasons or only soft rains.) Solar 1 would allow you to rewrite the weather to be soft rains and loving lapping waves, mostly sunny with 99% growing seasons. Or you can do a something like Banning clouds from passing over an area. (Perhaps creating cloudless hurricanes? Maybe just prevent hurricanes in the area. Depends on Finesse) as Celestial 1, but this would greatly reduce inland water and have other effects. You could then make ever full fresh water wells with Celestial 1, and another Celestial 1 to make an idol that protects the shores from both waves and warns of incoming unknown ships, keeping people safe regardless.

                                Creating land again, context and amount. Want a small chunk of land to build a house on? Terrestrial 1. On par with a new well or flattening a hill. Celestial 2-3 for major and profitable expansions. mutate shoreline trees to convert sand into soil, mutate coastal creatures to add sand to the shoreline as they die. Firmly Celestial 2. Creating a new animal that spawns in hills, and pushes dirt out to sea before beginning its life (Some kind of mole like early stage, to a aquatic secondary stage and the dirt moving something like it makes a dirt and rock shell that it loses as it reaches the seas edge, returns to lay eggs after some time, and dies to provide food for the young before they build armor and start the long journey again.) is only Terrestrial 3 but it takes a lot longer to let nature work in such a fashion. After some generations you could even do a second Terrestrial working to enhance your now mundane natural species to make a supernaturally large shell so it moves more dirt faster.

                                        Terrestrial Circle Workings: Workings of the Terrestrial Circle are generally rooted in transforming, enhancing, or weakening pre-existing elements of the natural world, rather than directly invoking supernatural forces.

                                        An Emerald Circle working might enchant all the fields of a village, but only with a minor blessing—something that would still be a marvel to the inhabitants of the village, but augments the natural properties of that area or protects it against a mundane threat or nuisance, rather than completely overwriting the nature of that region through magic.

                                        Ambition 1 (Goal Number 5): Ward a town or neighborhood-sized region against a particular type of mundane nuisance, such as forest fires, crop-eating pests, or rabid animals.

                                        ****

                                        Protecting an entire, Hati sized island, is probably Celestial 3.

                                        Protecting each settlement on that island individually is firmly Terrestrial 1.

                                            A sorcerer spends some time enchanting his home, a well-built brick building with iron-shod doors, to improve its security. His working has the following effects:
                                            - The walls (and floors, and ceilings, and doors) are all magically reinforced and stronger; a feat of demolition would require a Strength one "step" higher on the chart (corebook p. 231), and +5 difficulty. Thus, the doors require Strength 7+ and 14 successes to break down, and the walls (and floors, and ceilings) require Strength 10+ and 15 successes.
                                            - The building's structure is solid against immaterial beings, and just as tough for a dematerialized god (for example) to break through as it would be if the god were materialized.
                                            - The locks on the doors and windows are proof against any mundane intrusion. An interloper capable of challenging sorcerous locks, such as a Solar using Lock-Opening Touch (corebook p. 317), faces a difficulty of (the sorcerer's Intelligence+Essence).
                                            - As long as the building remains locked, it resists any attempt to teleport from the outside in via effects such as Travel Without Distance (corebook p. 480), unless the teleporter (or a willing person they're bringing along) is carrying a key to the building. Anyone attempting to gain entrance in this manner must roll a suitable dice pool (Wits+Occult is appropriate for a sorcerer) against the same difficulty as the doors' locks. This defense ceases to apply if doors are unlocked such that a mundane path exists from the teleporter's origin point to their destination without passing through any currently-locked doors.
                                            - The sorcerer has a basic awareness of any breaches. If one of the walls is seriously damaged, or one of the locks undone without the proper key being used, the sorcerer becomes aware of it as long as he is in the same realm of existence. This effect doesn't provide any information about the intruder(s) themselves, only which wall(s) or door(s) they've broken.
                                            - The enchantment persists as long as the building hasn't been totally destroyed. If one of the walls has been knocked down and needs to be replaced, the magical enhancement goes into effect again as soon as a new wall is built, without the sorcerer having to put any additional work into repairing the magic. (On the other hand, if the entire building is ruined via an effect such as Starfall Eschaton Hammer (Arms of the Chosen p. 147), the working is ended and the sorcerer gets his XP back.)

                                            I'm thinking Celestial 2 or 3. Per the book, warding a structure with magical barriers is Celestial 1 and enchanting fortifications to strengthen them is Celestial 2, but I may have gone a bit further with this list of effects. On one hand, I don't think this is anywhere near as fantastic as e.g. making a building fly or giving it the ability to rearrange its internal structure, but I think I may have pushed the effect from Celestial 2 to Celestial 3 when I started including things like resistance to teleportation and an instant warning if something gets damaged?

                                            If doing this to a good-sized residence is indeed Celestial 2, I'm guessing that doing it for a modest-sized fortress would be Celestial 3.

                                            Augmenting the defenses of one's own home is probably very common among sorcerers capable of performing Celestial workings reliably, so I'm interested in other ideas people have for sorcerous home defense.
