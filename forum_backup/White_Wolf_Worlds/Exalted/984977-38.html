    She urged King into a gallop, heading to intercept Seres.

    The youngblood noticed Ash coming her way and tacked in her direction. It was simple for King to run at Seres, for Ash to stretch out an arm, and for Seres to grab hold and swing into the saddle behind Ash.

    King immediately growled a complaint, but he kept running. Seres held onto Ash's shoulders as they rode away while the mob behind them howled in rage.

    "Thanks for the ride!" Seres said.

    "You're welcome. Hold on tight, now!" Ash replied. She cracked her reigns and King put on a burst of speed.

    They ran directly into the warren of cars and storage buildings that defined the outside of the depot. King veered around stacks of crates, sprinted down unoccupied tracks, and startled at least three yeddim. At one point, he climbed right up and over a flatcar covered in wooden piles, scattering railroad workers on both sides. Seres held on throughout, hard enough to bruise Ash underneath her coat.

    And then they were free and clear, with nothing but open land between them and the road out of town. King slowed to a walk.

    Seres finally relaxed her grip and twisted around to look behind them. "...I guess we lost 'em", she said, after a moment. "I expect those folks at the depot will remember us, though."

    Ash chuckled. "Let 'em! You know how legends become legends, Seres?"

    Seres turned back and stared into the back of Ash's head. "Doin' great deeds?"

    "Doin' memorable deeds," Ash responded. "If you go off into the wilderness to slay monsters, and don't bring back a trophy, then nobody'll know what you did and they don't believe you if you tell 'em. Take the time to put on a show for people, especially the ones who've never seen you before. That's how you build a reputation."

    "Is that why you didn't just go around the depot?"

    "That's half of it," Ash said with a shrug. "The other half was, I thought it would be fun."

    King growled again, louder this time.

    "Alright, alright. Seres, we best get off and walk from here."

    The sun had fully set by the time they reached the entrance to Rustbank. They took their time getting there, in part because Seres' legs had been rubbed raw by King (again) and in part because Ash wanted to talk. For starters, she wanted to know what had happened to Seres' horse. But the girl was reticent about recent events, and said she didn't want to discuss it until they found Lans again. So instead they chatted about the big names in southern folklore, and why what they did in particular merited memorials in story and song.

    They found Lans encamped by the side of the road near the mouth of the canyon. He was grooming his piebald mare, and the sight of it seemed to dampen Seres' spirits. Even so, they greeted each other warmly, and settled down around the fire to catch each other up.

    Ash could see Seres forcing back her discomfort as she insisted on going first:

    "I went straight to the camps, and pretended I was a migrant lookin' for work. First four people I talked to all said I should sell Huckleberry. One offered to buy him. "

    It took Ash a moment to remember Huckleberry was what Seres had decided to name her pony. She didn't talk to the animal much, so the name didn't stick in her mind.

    "But eventually, somebody asked if I could handle a spear or bow. I said I could, and they pointed me to a gang boss lookin' for people to guard a train.

    The boss was named Fat Alrec, and he needed a bunch of folks to stand around on a train goin' to Roca Roja - the 'three hunnerd ten'. He got cagey when I asked *what* we'd be guardin', but I figure it's got to be dust. Or silver - not much else needs that much guardin'.
    So I asked if he'd sign me up, and he told me to come to a particular part of the yard day after next, and bring my own weapons.

    I'd left Huckleberry outside when I was in Alrec's place, but as I was leaving, somebody hopped on him and rode off. I ran after 'em and..."

    Seres paused and her face twisted. She looked a little nauseous as she swallowed and continued on.

    "...I ran to this place in the camp where.... there were bones, everywhere. Nailed together to make tent poles, stuck into the ground to make little fences, strung up like chimes. Some of 'em still had meat on them, and the whole place stunk like nowhere I've been before.

    "I didn't see all that at first. I just knew I'd caught up to that horse-thief and I had a clear shot. So I threw a Bolt at 'em, knocked him down. Huckleberry spooked and bolted, but before I could chase 'em I was surrounded by these fat... people wearing bloody smocks."

    Seres paused again and took a breath. "I fought my way through them, threw another bolt or two. When they finally caught on I was a Dragon, they scattered. But then they came back, more'n twenty of 'em, and I decided to run."

    She bent forward and put her head in her hands. "I was lookin' for Huckleberry, but I didn't see him on my way out. And more people joined in the chase as we went along. So I escaped, and then you caught up with me, Ash."

    Ash nodded, grimacing. "Sounds like a fine mess. Run and leave your horse, or stay and die or kill a bunch of people..."

    Lans sighed. "A fine mess indeed. I think you chose well, Seres, but I don't expect that was easy for you."

    "...you did well, though," Ash added. She gave the younger woman a hesitant smile. "You found out what we needed to know. Better than that, you might have found your own way onto the train. That'll help a lot."

    "I know, I just..." Seres straightened up and glowered at the fire. "I wasn't expecting *that* kind of trouble. It'd be one thing if Huckleberry just got stolen, but this..."

    "...this is like something the Lost Souls would do," said Lans. "They do seem to have a certain... predilection for death."

    "Maybe, maybe," replied Ash in a noncommital tone. "They're not the only ones, though. Strange folks come to the frontier, sometimes. Some cult of knackers might've taken hold in the camp. But who knows?"

    They all silently mulled over that question for a while, until Ash spoke again.

    "Well, how about you, Lans? How was your day?"

    "Not nearly as eventful," he said, drily. "I went to the station master's office, waited until he left for the evening, then snuck in and peeked at his schedule. I took note of every train carrying freight from Red Gulch in the next week. And there's a big load going out on the three-hundred and ten, just as Seres said." He tugged the edge of his mustache. "Mind you, the schedule doesn't specify the contents - only the number of cars, volume, and weight."

    "Well, I can tell you for sure," said Ash, fishing around in her pockets. She pulled out the sheet she'd gotten from the Red Gulch clerk and tilted it toward the fire so she could read it. "Eyup, says it right here. Twenty tons of dust... gods, that's a lot... to be carried out tomorrow, and shipped the day after...." Ash trailed off and squinted at the sheet.

    "What's wrong?" asked Seres.

    "This isn't paper," said Ash, with growing dismay. She took off her right glove and felt along the sheet. "I had other things on my mind when I got this, so I didn't notice before. But... it feels like leather. No... skin."

    "Let me see it," Lans demanded with sudden seriousness. Ash got up and walked around the fire to hand it to him.

    Lans removed his own glove by shoving his hand into his armpit and waggling it free. Then he ran his fingers over the sheet. A look of profound disgust settled onto his face. "I've seen this before. It is skin. Human skin."

    The air seemed to grow colder around the fire.

    "What... what should we do?" said Seres.

    Ash set her jaw.
    • "We've got to stay focused. We've got a train to rob in two days. "
    • "Somethin's rotten here, and we'd be fools to ignore it. We'd better start digging deeper."
    • Something else?

                "Something's rotten here, and we'd be fools to ignore it. We'd better start digging deeper."

                Lans gave a resolute nod. Seres looked more skeptical. "I hear ya, but..." she paused, and a mix of emotions played over her face. "...Don't we have a train to catch? If we left right now, we'd have about one whole day to spare. That ain't a lot of time."

                "I know," said Ash, "but it don't feel right, leaving somethin' like this behind us. Can't take the chance that it might interfere. I reckon if we get an early start tomorrow, act quick, we can get the lay of the land and still have enough time to get our dust. We can decide what to do when we know what's what."

                They talked a while longer about what little they knew - there wasn't much. When the fire burned low, they set out their bedrolls and lay themselves down to sleep.


                Ash was awoke shortly before dawn with a start. Something hard was jabbing her in the shoulder. Without thinking, she rolled over and came up with Absolution in her hand.

                It took her a moment to realize she was pointing her firewand at Gaze-of-the-Sun. She stood just out of arm's reach, placidly holding up her staff and apparently unconcerned with the weapon aimed at her.

                "[It is good you are alert, child]," she said. "[Death has touched this place.]"

                "[I know this]", said Ash, lowering her firewand. She shook her head, sighed, and knelt down to pick through her belongings. She wasn't getting back to sleep now, might as well get ready.

                "[They keep no graves, here]," Gaze continued as if Ash had not spoken. "[Nor do they leave their dead to the sky or give them to the fire.]"

                "[What do they do with them?]" Ash asked, figuring the question was expected even if Gaze meant to tell her anyway.

                "[They are hidden beneath the earth, secreted away from the sun. I cannot see where they go, out of sight of the Burning Eye.]"

                Ash frowned. "[Do you mean... the firedust mines? Or... tunnels, beneath the depot?]"

                Gaze frowned back. "[There are pits, beneath those homes. Where they lead, I know not.]"

                "[Can you show me where?]" Ash pulled on her coat and glanced at her companions. Seres seemed to have woken to the noise. Lans was still lightly snoring.

                "[I can.]" Her grip on her staff tightened. "[I would see us cleanse this place with fire.]"

                Ash gave her a long look. There weren't many occasions where she could recall Gaze angry. "[Why, elder?]"

                "[It is heresy]", the old woman spat. "[Life eats life to live. That is survival. Life does not eat death, live in death, live as death. That is a dying that never ends.]"

                "What's she all riled up about?" asked Seres. She had risen from her bedroll and walked over to join them, barefoot.

                "She's found us a lead," said Ash, choosing to omit the rest. "Some... entrance to the underground where bodies go."

                "Oh..." Seres looked a bit disappointed. "I thought we'ld... well. Nevermind."

                "What's the matter, Seres? I thought we'd agreed to do this."

                Seres shook her head. ""s not that. I thought we'd go to back to the shantytown. Try to find where Huckleberry got to."

                "Well, that's... hmm."

                It dawned on Ash that whatever was off about Rustbank might be pervasive than she thought. There was a lot that merited investigation, and not a lot of time to look. And given how things had gone yesterday, it would be risky to split up again.
                • She decided it was best to go back to the Red Gulch records office. She doubted the little clerk would be any trouble, and before she did anything else she wanted to know how the mining company had gotten to printing on human skin.
                • She looked back at Gaze and made her decision. Whatever the old woman had found, it sounded like the root of the problem. Whatever the risks, the best answer would be go straight for it.
                • Her eyes met Seres', and she made up her mind. Bloody butchers attacking a girl in broad daylight? No point in looking for mysteries, with a real threat so plain to see.
                • Something else?

                                        She looked back at Gaze and made her decision. Whatever the old woman had found, it sounded like the root of the problem. Whatever the risks, the best answer would be go straight for it.

                                        "Gaze ain't steered us wrong yet," she said to Seres without turning to face her. "'Sides which, we ain't got a lot of time for searchin'. I'll take any shortcut I can find."

                                        Seres frowned, but she said nothing.

                                        Lans woke a short while after, they had a short breakfast of dry rations, and set out. They left their animals behind, trusting that Lans' horse was wise enough to flee from anyone but his master and King's teeth and claws would be enough to deter thieves. Gaze flew ahead, marking the place they should go with a circling cloud of black birds.

                                        Their path took them westward, circling around the depot and residential section. The daily commotion was starting to get loud as they approached a modest adobe hut, perfectly positioned to mark the west-southwest boundaries of the town.

                                        The first thing Ash noticed as they rode up was that the place was a shambles - part of a wall had crumbled, and the roof had slid off or fallen in. Weed-covered ground indicated that someone had put a food garden here at one point, but it had long grown over. In contrast to the noise of the town behind them, this house was silent. There was nobody around, nothing to indicate that anyone currently lived there - not even squatters.

                                        And yet, she could see places where the earth had been churned and flattened by boots, hooves, and cartwheels. People did come here, probably within the last few days. And they had come here many times before that.

                                        Gaze stood beside a ratty curtain that served as a door as they came up. She pushed the worn, heavy cloth aside with her staff, and gestured for them to enter.

                                        The interior consisted of a single large room. The floor was swept - or, at least, someone had taken pains to push all the rubbish to the sides, so that there was clear path from the door to the middle of the room. A large straw carpet, yellow-white in the sunlight coming through the ceiling, covered the center.

                                        Ash walked in and stepped to one side, allowing Lans, Seres, and Gaze to each enter in turn. "[Elder, what did you see here?]" she asked, as the old woman came through the doorway.

                                        Gaze moved past her and directly to the edge of the carpet. She flipped up a corner of it with her staff and folded the carpet over, exposing a narrow staircase leading down into a deep, dark hole. "[From above, I saw men carry a body here.]" She grimaced. "[It was in pieces. They took it below. This is not the only place I have seen such an act, but here is where the sun's eye sees clearly.]"

                                        "[When?]" asked Lans. He came up to the edge of the stairs and peered down into the black.

                                        "[Yesterday, at the edge of twilight.]"

                                        "Where's it go? Does she know?" Seres glanced at the hole, then started scanning the room.

                                        "Nope. We're goin' in blind," Ash answered with a frown. She pulled Judgement from her hip began going down the steps. "I'll go first."

                                        It was pitch black below, until Gaze conjured a small cluster of small, floating candle-flames that gave off no heat. They floated above Ash's head like a burning halo as she advanced into a long. low tunnel. Ash had to keep her head down to avoid bumping her head against the ceiling.

                                        With her head naturally inclined to look at the floor, Ash could see trails on the floor of the tunnel. Something had been dragged this way, not long ago. A faint smell of carrion was in the air. Once, she kicked a small white stone, only to realize an instant later it had actually been a been a chunk of bone.

                                        They walked through the tunnel in single file for a for some time. The tunnel curved slightly, and then Ash saw a junction ahead. Down one path, she could hear noises - someone whistling, and an irregular thumping. The arc of the tunnel made it impossible to see too far ahead.

                                        Ash's grip on Judgment tightened.

                                                    She waved at Gaze to extinguish the light, and slowly felt her way down the tunnel towards the sound.

                                                    They moved ahead in absolute darkness. Ash's feet were sure, but she soon lost her sense of how far she had gone or what direction she was headed. She kept her hand on the wall and followed where it led. The air grew cooler and the floor tilted, indicating she was going further underground.

                                                    She expected after a while that a light would appear in the tunnel, but it never happened. She knew she was getting closer only because the whistling got louder. Then the whistler turned to full on singing in flametongue, with a surprisingly melodic tenor that echoed through the tunnels. It was a variation on a traveling song that Ash had heard many times; the familiarity of it felt strange and unnerving in the present context.

                                                    "Pues voy a rayar mi nombre, en los huesos de la tierra... Clavare mis tacones, entre el suelo..."

                                                    Ash felt her hand slip off the wall, and she fumbled for a moment before she realized that she'd entered a chamber of some kind and the wall had sharply turned to the right.

                                                    There was an overpowering mix of smells in here - meat, salt, and vinegar. The thumping she had hear before was now distinct enough to recognize as the sound of a blade hacking through flesh and bone - the sound of butchery.

                                                    Ash had only just finished processing all of this when suddenly the noises stopped. There was silence, and then the the gentle clatter of steel being set down on stone.

                                                    "Hola?" said the tenor, uncertainly. "¿Quién está ahí?"

                                                    Ash held her breath. Could he really have heard her?

                                                    "Is it Renaldo?" Then there was a chuckle. "Renaldo, you can't fool me. The echoes, they change when you stand there like that. I'm blind, not deaf. There's no use in hiding..." the voice trailed off. The silence became uncomfortable. Then Ash heard the noise of steel scraping away from stone - presumably into a hand.

                                                    "¿Quién eres?" the voice demanded. "Speak!"
