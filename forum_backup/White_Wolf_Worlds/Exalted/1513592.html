    Infernals/Abyssals: Allow "Redemption" or instead not be related to Solars?

    06-01-2023, 09:59 PM
    I remember in 2E that there was some serious dislike shall we say to the idea that Abyssals could be turned back into Solars.

    Redemption was a dirty word apparently.

    Personally I found that funny as one of the first things about Abyssals that I remember was the Monstrance of Celestial Portion which basically converted Solars to Abyssals.

    Same thing with Infernals, they were basically Solar Exalts with Yozi Juice added.

    Apparently Solars -> Infernals or Abyssals was fine but the opposite was not.

    Now my view was always that if you could be converted one way, you could be converted back. If you did not want Abyssals/Infernals to be able to be converted to Solars then Solars should never be converted to Infernals / Abyssals.

    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    So my question:

    Would you rather Abyssals/Infernals be :-

    "Converted" Solars but have the option of being converted back to actual Solars (Note: Solars could be converted to Infernals/Abyssals in this scenario).

    OR

    Be actual Exalted of the Never-Born / Yozi's. They were never Solars in the first place.

    In other words 300 Solars wondering creation currently but who knows how many Infernals/Abyssals exist....

    EDIT: Keep in mind that I am not necessarily saying the individual exalt has to go on a 'redemption' arc but that the Exaltation SHARD can be converted back and forth. (The Exalt surviving the process or even wanting or being happy about it? That's an optional extra).

    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Side Note :-
    Reason for my question is that I am working on my own setting/background/lore changes and got to the Abyssals and began wondering about this question.

    This is pure setting/background fluff right now as we don't have mechanics for Abyssals/Infernals but I was interested in what people thought.

      I'm fine with Abyssals and Infernals being able to become Solars again. Thematically, it fits: the sun rises after a period of darkness; and it seems unreasonable to me that a Solar-level Exaltation should be capable of the kind of legendary impossibilities generally attributed to them but not be able to overcome external corruption inflicted on them. I know it's White Wolf, but the idea that you can only fall and never rise again feels off to me.

      So I'd rather they be corrupted Solar Exaltations than original creations.

          Note: Abyssals have always had the potential to become Solar Exalted again, although it was very much framed as "This can happen but it hasn't yet, you'll probably be the first, and this is a big enough deal to be the end of a chronicle." sort of thing. Infernals haven't been, although IIRC the motivation there was a Watsonian "The Infernal Exaltation isn't an alteration to the core functions of the Solar one, it's a Solar Exaltation encased in an entirely separate thing and powering the Yozi OS." and a Doylist "The Infernals haven't done things fundamentally wrong like the Abyssal did when they accepted their own Exaltation, and so "redemption" doesn't fit into their narrative." Likewise, Solar Exalted couldn't be converted to Infernals, at least not the Green Sun Princes; becoming akuma was different, but as far as I care making that decision is a Game Over and you don't get to be redeemed because that means you've completely surrendered narrative agency.

          Now, I haven't looked at Essence, for the most part it's not a product I'm interested in, so I don't know if the current creative team has voiced their plans on how the Exaltations work this time around. For all I know the narrative on the Abyssals has changed significantly.

          Anyway, this is a tricky subject because some folks, such as a former player of mine, will absolutely look at altered Solar Exaltations as a problem that absolutely needs to be solved: Abyssal and Infernal Exaltations alike not only need to be able to be restored to their original natures, but a necessary part of the win condition of the game is fixing them, making them the Solar Exalted they should have been all along.

          I disagree, FWIW--declaring that the only non-asshole way to be a splat is to pursue no longer being that thing is not a conceit I'm here for--but I can at least understand the perspective.

          I don't think I'm directly engaging with your question, though.

          If I were forced to decide, then I would probably cleave the Abyssals and the Infernals from the Solar Exalted. I don't think it's a good idea: if nothing else it severs both of those splats from some of their current hooks, and runs the risk of making Exaltation a little less impressive. However, if the alternative is a focus on the mutability of the Solar Exaltation then it may be a decision that needs to be made. It does them no favors for someone to look at every Deathknight and see primarily a Solar Exaltation that needs fixed.

          This may not be doing justice to the debate, but it's a little late, I'm tired, and I've kicked this question around myself before.

                                          I'm ok with Abyssals becoming Solars again, depending on how they changed from 2nd ed to 3rd. It wasn't pushed on them but the idea of the Monstrance of Celestial Portion converting Solars into Abyssals essentially necessitated a narrative counter point in the form of Redemption. Given how Abyssals in 2nd ed were tied to the barely conscious insane and distant Neverborn, only directed by the implicitly weaker Deathlords (if only because an experienced Abyssal could easily take on ghosts/nephwracks one on one). It made sense
                                          3rd edition has heavily implied that Abyssals will be more heavily tied to the Deathlords, rather than the Neverborn and the former are also implied to be more powerful. But a tighter leash only makes rebellion a more interesting plot hook. A lot of Abyssal play has been about breaking off from the Deathlords & the nihilistic goals of the Neverborn and finding your own way in Creation, a world that rejects the deathly Essence of your being. Redemption makes a lot of sense in that context.

                                          On the other hand, 3rd edition promised that there is more going on in the Underworld now, so if the new mode of play is down in the domain of the dead and more focused on the knight aspect, I can see the promise of Redemption losing it's shine. If death knights are not intended to being running around topside most of the time in their own stories, then the need to change who they are at the level of their own soul is...less appealing. if the Monstrance does not make a return, then there is less need to "flip the switch" back to light mode. After all, if you can't threan Sloars to go dark, then you don't need to let players have out from said dark.

                                          Infernals are different. Explicitly, you couldn't redeem them in any of the prior editions. What the Yozis did to their Exaltations was too drastic to undo and furthermore, it could only be done on an unattached exaltation. Additionally, they attracted a different kind of host. Solars were heroes, Abyssals were those who died in the process of their great deeds and Infernals were those who lost. They were the ones who choked, got caught cheating and who, by the their own metric failed. Then along comes the demonic host of the exaltation (and future Unwoven Coadjutor) to make a deal, to give them what can change the fate they have already sealed.
                                          The 3rd edition preview changed this somewhat, along with the Reclamation...but apparently even mentioning that demonic Creators of Creation might get something out of the literal Faustian Bargain they make with their champions resulted in that idea being dropped before the writing on Exalted Essence even began. Even when it essetially boiled down to "Go forth and be Awesome, as it will also make us look Awesome."
                                          Infernals were never meant be able to undergo Redemption, because they chose to do something irrevocable. Creation itself doesn't reject them, because it knows their makers are the same. They deleted the operating software that Solars & Abyssals run on and replaced it with spirit dna of primordial titans themselves. Instead, them could reach for ascention, becoming living world/titan themselves.
                                          And while 3rd ed Infernals are less "growing souls of demon gods" and do seem to run closer to the original software, I haven't seen any sign that they are intended to play that differently.

                                          Ultimately it comes down to this: there is play space for unwilling Abyssals right now (though maybe not in their new book) but there is no such thing a someone who exalted as an Infernal against their will. So Abyssals can be "saved" but Infernals chose damnation. Heck there was so much confusion on the matter that most people ignore the first two chapters the Infernal's Manual of Power book because of utterly irredeemable the actions of every Infernal are supposed to be. Absent of new information, which we don't have, Infernals have less of a shot at Redemption than Hearteaters.

                                          Lillun may be gone, but we don't have anything in the worldbuilding space where she was misused previously. We have that and the rejection of the idea that First Age God-Kings reborn to spread how great the demon god-worlds of Hell are. We don't have enough on 3rd ed Infernals to say if it is possible, only that it was off their old themes.

                                          So I vote:
                                          1. Yes, Abyssals should get the option of Redemption
                                          2. No, Infernals don't deserve it and would disservice it if they got it. Hard No
                                          And yeah, I might be a tad salty but how the idea the Reclamation existing as a concept got trashed because...what else did Infernals have that was theirs?

                                              Mm. This seems to me to be looking at it as a metaphysical thing - the Abyssal and Infernal Exaltations were created from Solar Exaltations, and therefore can be changed back. Making state-changes to the Exaltation.

                                              A redemption narrative is a moral one - the realisation you've done wrong and making up for it

                                              Considering the core 3e Infernal narrative is that of the downtrodden and oppressed being bestowed great power, to do with as they will, casting an Infernal->Solar change as redemption has... unfortunate implications. Is it possible an Infernal might want to change states? Certainly. But it would be a different story from a redemption - a transformation, more.

                                              For Abyssals, Vance's indicated that redemption in 3e isn't limited to becoming a Solar - it's still on the table, but it's not the only option for an Abyssal who feels they made the wrong decision, or done the wrong thing, and wants to make up for it. Put simply, you can pursue redemption while remaining an Abyssal; it's not necessary to become a Solar, though it's still an option for those interested in pursuing it.

                                              Plus, it'll be more than possible to be a heroic Abyssal without seeking redemption (we already have an example of that in Adversaries of the Righteous).

                                              Essentially, the Abyssal playspace is getting broadened beyond the binary of 'kill the world' and 'become a Solar'.
