                                            Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
                                            Tarnished bronze. Lesser, corrupted and in need of improvement, but safe.

                                            Buuuut I don't feel that 3E tackles those complaints. If anything dev commentary seems to suggest the intent is to lean further into the stuff I dislike: that trying to make an oppressive empire morally complex is the same thing as excusing Imperialism. *sigh* That's for a different thread, this is just me outlining my PoV on the edition changes since you asked. (I also kinda think WFHW leaned a little into Flanderization of the Great Houses... but just a little, and some of the new ideas I did like. Kinda evens on that front.)
                                            I guess my feeling on this is that I'm OK with the books being somewhat condemnatory of the Realm, so long as the substance is "smart".

                                            Explaining further: On the one hand, the Realm really is meant to be an empire with specifically a pretty awful ideology - Immaculate Philosophy preaching Dragonblooded moral supremacy and the Scarlet Empress's divide and rule controlled chaos and managed low intensity internal conflict - and empires themselves are a pretty imperfect form of political organisation. There's a limit to how defensible that is. So I'm OK with the books condemning it a fair bit.

                                            On the other, I want them to describe the Realm in a way that make economic sense, and doesn't give imply weird, wrong ideas about history. No "Material extraction is the only reason empires happen; no ideological, military or defensive reasons. If the Realm is somewhere, they're 'On The Rob'". No "In general, implausibly high, economically impossible levels of wealth are typically extracted under empires, keeping parts of empires that aren't the core at vastly different, vastly lower, wealth levels" or "In our real modern world, extracted wealth is why developed countries have high levels of income today!". Part of that's just the game world being a place I can take somewhat seriously.

                                            Also no weird asymmetries where it feels a bit like "When Dragonblooded do empire, it's all about material greed and in need of condemnation.... but if you want to rock out being empire-building Solar Alexander the Great, that's obviously legitimate and cool". If we're criticizing empires and empires as a form of state in general rather than the political basis of the Scarlet Dynasty in specifics, let's be consistent in our condemnation.

                                            Then finally, needs a bit of consideration of "Yes, if you get rid of the Realm, you are going to have lots of would be local powers crop up and try and fill their vacuum, rather than nice little kind of democratic 'free peoples' or whatever, and lots of those local powers are going to be less competent at fighting the Fair Folk and managing predatory gods and the like".

                                            Does 3e do a good job of all this? Undecided and I'd have to think about it some more.

                                            In edition terms, I kind of feel like First got away with all this much better than of the later commentary, though perhaps only because it mostly avoided a lot of these questions and offering specific answers, for better or worse. The flip side is that this can be less useful if you don't want to fill in the gaps.

                                            ...

                                            On thread in general, feels like a lot of the common theme of points raised pro and con Third haven't really shifted much since the edition first dropped; mainly mechanics bloat (lots of Charms, lots of resources to manage, lots of interactions) combined with lack of opponents / 'ST tools' in general.

                                            In a way that's good, as it's better than "The mechanics actually implode under use", but in a way that's a shame (for those who are experiencing Cons) because Third seems like it's on such a much longer cycle than previous editions, so you're stuck with what you've got.

                                            We're not moved on to a lot of "This looked like a problem before I started playing, but actually no problem", and really what looked like it would be difficult or useful at the start of the edition still is.

                                                        Things I've done to make running Ex 3 a smoother process (having had experience running both prior editions).
                                                        • I created my own Charm file, including Charm trees, for myself and the players. The Charms are all reduced to their mechanical basics, based on Irked's reworking. When a player uses a Charm, I can Ctrl-F to it quickly as needed. I can add or house rule Charms freely and excise those I dislike. The file also highlights a variety of rulings from the Devs and includes my own homebrew systems.
                                                        • Weaker starting Solars. I decreased the number of starting Charms by a handful, and no Artifacts were allowed. Next game, I'll probably only grant half the Excellencies, in order to make the early game more challenging. The power level out of the gate renders most published challenges useless.
                                                        • Created a combat tracker. I somehow acquired an old sheaf of (I think) accounting sheets. Initiatives at the top, crash to the side, and room in the middle for figures. Markers track initiative and are adjusted for having acted. All players can see it easily, and combat is far more manageable thereby.
                                                        • Ditched Solar xp mechanics. I still give Solar xp, but after playing a couple sessions by the RAW, I tossed that system aside.
                                                        • I started the game in a remote location with its own problems. Three nations are in a Vale geographically isolated from its neighbors. In between is an unnatural forest, Devil-Tree, from which horrors emerge. Constraining Devil-tree is part of Vale culture, a way of life. But of late it's grown more active... Two PC's Exalted in the Riverlands and fled the Wyld Hunt, arriving in the Vale. Two other PC's were from opposed Vale nations. Banded together, their war against Devil-Tree became an epic tale that didn't rely on the lack of other splats, with non-Solar Exalts making only memorable cameos, their natures not really understood by the PC's.
                                                        • I rely on a diversity of problems. Combat has remained a challenge, to some extent, because I've homebrewed most of their antagonists. But disease, poison, potential corruption from Devil-tree's minions, and multiple moral quandaries have kept things fresh and challenging.

                                                        The biggest con for me at this point is the eventual challenge of running a combat with multiple Exalted antagonists that takes advantage of their Charm sets. I don't want to simplify things too much. The lack of other quality opponents is likewise problematic. I don't have time to homebrew the entire Exalted world.

                                                        But my friends (3/5 of which were new to Exalted with this chronicle) and I do love this game.
