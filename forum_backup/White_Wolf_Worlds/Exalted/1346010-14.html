                            Should be noted that just like the Sun doesn't have Excellent Strike, Deathlords won't have some fo the Abyssal Charms and Infernals some of the Yozi Charms. There are some things that are pretty solidly "just Abyssal" or "just Infernal" that assuming their Charms all ape in some way their patrons misses. As noted, Lunars and Solars do a lot that is basically very much affects of the Exalt, than their patrons. The Deathlords and Yozis inspire their effects, not deterine them.

                            In addition, I think that it might help give some thematic strength to Infernal aesthetics. In 2e, I kind of called Infernals at times the Wastebin Exalts. (Deriving from the biology taxon term.) Since each Yozi was supposed to be its own character, it had to have a lot of redundent things to them. And since each was approached as if it were a complte Exalt, they had many jarring effects which sure, worked for neat combos, but had little underlying aeshtetic philosophy. And in this context, it also often had it where individual Yozis had Charms which were basically "Didn't fit a main Exalt, put it here" stuff, like shadowmancy, the evil genie stuff, or telekinesis. Things that just weren't going to show up in the main Exalts were tehre. Infernals likely will have things that help glue things together wiht more broadly Hellish and demonic themes, such as some of the self-realization in the Devil Tiger Charms being more easily acessable (Spirit of the Living World did literally nothing useful mechancially but folks wouldn't mind it), or general titanic stuff on world-creation, dreaming, music, and dance.

                            There are some thing about the Infernals having the exact Charms the Yozis did that were outright caustic to character ideas in my view. When you made a rally good Fiend, you kind of have really the main route plus four (later five) flavors of spcie to it. And the way 2e Infenrals operated, they said nothing about your character you are playing. They were an exercise of basically glorifying playing a powerful NPC in the setting with a character-shaped skinsuit. It's cute to have "No Infenral is ever a sailor because Yozis don't use boats" but it's also kind of stupid that the mechanics also basically say "No Infernal is a sailor since no Charms you ever buy will interact with Sail." It emphasized emulating NPCs rather than creating something to play your PC. This is cute in some context of exotic RPGs, but it's kind of weird in context of a game where you are encouraged to build your own character with their legend. Structuring their Charms around Abilities does a lot to give players things to hook their characters into, gives clearer ideas of giving more comprehensive Exalts (ie, suddenly we might have Archery Charms), and since each Yozi isn't bound to have to cover some suite of Caste Abilities or everything, more can actually be used where needed to give theamtic variety. The Ebon Dragon can have his Stealth Charms along with Adorjan-inspired ones. We can get some Szoreny Larceny and Socialize Charms without having to write-up a whole damned complete mini-Exalt for it.

                            And in all this, a lot of the customization/homebrew stuff that I feel Infernals came the dumping ground for (Again, Devil-Tigers were cool, they were also basically blank checks to make Infernals the Ex MIsicllenia Exalt) are kind of now better realized I think in Exigents. Not calling htem a dumping ground, but an Exalt sort defined by smaller packages that do a theme well, rather than some bloated multi-tentalced ting that had five fairly different aesthetics, added on kind of as needed assuming anyone can us eit, and also on top of that had a whole other brnach of "Just stop being htis Exalt sort entirely" was to me at leas,t aeshteically and coherently a mess in a way "Exalts defined by nicher aspect,s but doing that within the bounds of a character" that I think Exisgents don't get into.

                                    Let me preface this with saying, I love Ability based Exalted; it's a huge selling point of the game, that fundamentally a good part of why we all got into this was setting because we all thought that heroes doing chi-fueled feats of awesome and stylish superhuman ability is amazing and something which was very unique, certainly when attached to a setting with the mood and ideas and influences underlying Creation.

                                    And I think that's why it resonated with me when Holden and John made a great pitch for emphasizing up-selling *all* 3e Exalted as heroes with Ability based Charms as far as their design for Exigents, Infernals, etc went. This was coming after the weird, un-fun late 2e meta where being awesome at Abilities was sold as the sole province of the Solar Exalted, and was something that the *cool* and *successful* non-Solar Exalted didn't really do or try to do (in favour of "weird, subverting takes" on Abilities, having non-Ability based magic like 2e Infernals or Alchemicals and so forth) and was generally kind of a buzzkill for fans of Exalted who had Ability based Charms as part of their identity but which were not Solars.

                                    And yet I find myself these days not really caring if Infernals are actually Ability based on not, or instead follow purely esoteric Yozi based "patterns" or not. I think I'm less committed to the idea that an Exalt has to be successful and interesting in the game's full range of Abilities to be successful and matter. The dialogue around the Lunar Exalted in 3e probably helps with that. The Infernals could have "holes" where they really can't meaningfully, specifically support the expression of some Ability and it... wouldn't really matter to how successful they were.

                                    The only thing that seems genuinely non-negotiable is that Exalted player characters should not be aspiring to be beings of the singular power and nature of the Yozis as written by Jenna in Games of Divinity. Not even in a "This could happen but is mechanically going to be unsupported" sense. Has bad consequences (power inflation, messes with the goals of playing humanistic characters, etc.).
