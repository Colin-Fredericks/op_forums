    Solar Exaltation and Choice

    04-12-2015, 07:37 AM
    So I was considering how Solar Exaltations work in 2E, and I've never been entirely comfortable with the lack of choice in it. Yes, I know, the person has to choose to stand up to whatever they're facing or whatever, but other Solaroid Exaltations require you to choose to become an Abyssal or Infernal or whatever, and there's so much story potential in someone refusing a Solar Exaltation that it seems a bit of a waste not to have it. Plus, I don't really like the idea of a person becoming an Eclipse because they get zapped with an Eclipse Exaltation (again, yes, I know they get that Exaltation because it suits them, but more variability is always a good thing). So, I was considering a slightly different mechanic of Solar Exaltation.

    So, you've got Awesome Bob. He's pretty awesome. He is facing some terrible difficulty, and he makes the choice to stand up to it, rather than run away. ZAP, the Exaltation enters him and begins the soul-bonding-thing.

    Bob then finds himself facing some kind of test. The one that occurs to me is a courtyard that he must cross, which he somehow intuitively knows will grant him the power to overcome his trials. Behind him is darkness, at the other end is a doorway filled with golden light, and in the middle is a group of soldiers in a ready stance. How Bob proceeds determines what kind of Solar Exalt he becomes. If he raises his weapon and attacks, he becomes a Dawn Caste. If he makes an impassioned speech and persuades the soldiers to part - or join his cause - he becomes an Eclipse. If he avoids the soldiers, or causes them to fight each other by slipping in between them, then he becomes a Night Caste, and so forth. And if he goes back into the darkness, he is returned to his initial situation, not a Solar (which he may well not want to be, if say he's of the Immaculate Faith), but no worse off either, other than maybe a mite confused, and the caste-neutral Exaltation seeks another host.

    Obviously the test can and will vary somewhat, but what do people think of the basic idea? From what little I've picked up of how Exaltations will work in 3E, might gel well with that as well.

      Your choice of words has left me lukewarm to it.

      You don't choose to be blessed with the illimitable power of the Unconquered Sun; the King of Heaven makes that choice. You also don't choose how the Highest of Holies blesses you; this, too, is on the four shoulders of Ignis Divine. He's never going to say, "I want to Exalt this warrior, but all I have is a Night Exaltation." If he reaches from heaven to touch your soul and spark that divine fire, it will be something compatible with you… even if you don't initially feel it, even if you don't want it.

      After all, if denying Exaltation was a thing, would the Immaculate Philosophy not have a chapter in their scripture about "Just Say No to Conky?"

      Furthermore, Abyssals and Green Sun Princes are different. In some ways, fundamentally so. This is one of the reasons the devs aren't throwing around the word "Solaroid."

      That, and it sounds rather dumb.

                  I'm not a fan of this notion for a few reasons. Alucard has spelled some of them out pretty well.

                  There's also the fact that the "choice" component to GSP and Abyssal Exaltation exists to replicate the narrative of a Faustian bargain - "power at a price," sometimes a price you're not fully aware of when you take the power, sometimes a price you take in desperation and later regret, sometimes a price that shows you how much of a monster you were all along.

                  Solar Exaltation isn't intended to come with those kind of inherent strings attached. You can have Abyssals and GSPs in the First Age and they will likely carry that baggage, where Solars will not have the Anathema baggage attached to them.

                  Sure, one can argue that if you're playing in RY768 and Exalt Solar or Lunar, you have the "price" of becoming Anathema, or if Sidereal then your "price" is losing all the connections you made in your mortal life. But those are rather different, and they're not necessarily key elements of the story of those splats as "take this power and prevent your own death" or "take this power and succeed where you once failed" are to those who burn black and green.

                  I feel like Allan's concern is wholly an abstract one. No person in Creation is going to be Chosen and a Night and say "aww man, I wanted to be a Zenith!" Their reaction will run the gamut from "oh shit, I'm an Anathema, what's happening to me?" to "Yes! Finally I have the power to get my revenge on Count Blood and the iron grip his empire has put on the people of this land!" But they're not going to feel gypped on a meta-level the way you might be if you get a legendary drop in a WoW dungeon but it's no good for anyone in the raid group.

                  And no good ST is going to be like "well Dave, I know you wanted to be a Dawn, but all UCS has available right now, after Exalting your friends, are two Twilights and an Eclipse, and you did say you wanted to favor Investigation, Lore, and Occult regardless, so Twilight it is!" That kind of rude bullshit can't be fixed in any way by mechanics or suggest narratives.

                      Ok, you don't get magically transported to a dark hallway. But if a Barbarian Chief is going to attack your village and you choose to fight him, you become a Dawn; if you choose to negotiate, Eclipse; ask the spirits for help, Zenith; assassinate him, Night; find a legendary artifact to beat him, Twilight.

                      You might not be consciously aware of the choice (or its significance) but when you choose to stand up where others flee then that is you choosing to Exalt. When you choose how to stand up, that's you choosing your Caste.

                      (Hunter the Reckoning's narrative of people "Exalting" is actually better than Exalted's IMO. I borrow shamelessly from that when running Exaltation scenes. Exalted's "You decide to enter a library, for your great heroism you Exalt!" and "You watch a storm one night, you Exalt!" narratives leave me full of "meh".)

                      The Abyssals give you a "Do ye fear Death?" choice which isn't really comparable. Whereas Infernals let you make your choice, fail, then ask you if you stand by your choice and tantalize you with terrible power.

                                          I think about Panther, the gladiator and his moment of contemplation that led him to his understanding/the Exaltation, that moment of crisis in which he learned something of himself that before the singular moment he could not have understood. Even if Panther could have known he'd be chosen by the highest of gods, I imagine the choices he makes would put him on the path towards a Dawn Caste. However, his road to Damascus moment was bigger than all his choices up to that point.

                                          I think if anything is numinous in Exalted, the Exaltation is. This is not to say it's always good or just or fitting, but it that it is beyond the choice of the individual. It's bigger than what your character does for survival or their talents or whatever (though those are certainly, partly expressed by it) it's a reflection of who that individual is as far as the Unconquered Sun (or Luna, or the Maidens, etc) views the individual.

                                          And I think a Solar can fight against this, an eclipse can be a ravening murder-barbarian, a Dawn could be a dogmatic pacifist... but they would be playing against type, there may be a conversation and roleplay experience here.

                                          I don't think there's anything to be gained from making this an in character choice, and much lost.

                                          The Exaltation is a miracle, even at the height of silly 2nd edition meta-wankery that the Exalted hosts were just seen by the Incarnae as a means to kill the Primordials, the Exaltation is still a miracle. Your version sounds, meh, kinda cheap.
