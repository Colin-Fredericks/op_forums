        I also agree with John that the consequences of winning are not just a sometimes food. I’ve even have level 1-3 DnD characters run through those kinds of stories. Speaking of DnD there are some lessons to be learned from the Dungeon Masters Guide about having a challenging and fun experience. It mostly revolves around having a series of challenges where probably not a single one of them is actually hard, but as long as they leave the challenge with less resources than they started it slowly builds up the tension.

        This ends up being useful in a lot of ways, for one it gives your PCs a lot of more safe failure options. Sometimes it’s fun for the PCs to lose, but it’s almost never fun for the PCs to all die. The thing is if you’re ratcheting up challenge and tension by making your one fight harder and harder then retreat becomes less and less an option. Weighing staying in combat another round and maybe killing the guy who’s trying to kill you vs running and being chased down is usually a toss up, and with only one of those options ending in possible victory. On the other hand if your PC resource pool looks like this after the first 4-5 challenges, both combat and non-combat:

        PC1
        4 peripheral
        0 personal
        2 willpower

        PC2
        10 peripheral
        0 personal
        0 willpower

        PC3
        7 peripheral
        3 personal
        2 willpower

        PC4
        2 peripheral
        0 personal
        4 willpower

        Then they have to have a serious discussion about if they’re going to push on or retreat, and that’s a way easier discussion than “do we disengage and flee from this Abyssal or stay and fight since she’s on the ropes as well?” It also lets the players actually feel like the badasses they are. Again agreeing with John, if fights are all really hard and feel deadly the PC don’t feel mighty they kind of feel like chumps. It’s way better for them to smash a battlegroup of elemental guards like badasses, crack a thousand year old riddle door in five seconds, pick up the mortal guard captain and send him bowling through his mortal kingsguard, finally get to the king and convince him that his new “friend” is poison, and then one last battle between you and the newly revealed traitors, having your PCs collapse to the ground after, totally spent, rather than just one huge conflict where they get the crap kicked out of them and barely win. It also just helps make the world feel bigger. Diving into a first age tomb shouldn’t feel like a one room combat and then a treasure vault, it should feel almost like entering another world, like the catacombs of Paris or something, except full of even more wonders and dangers. Storming a Satrap’s palace shouldn’t feel like kicking down the door and throwing down the gauntlet, it should feel like an epic siege where you tear down walls, battle through hails of arrows, twist steel gates into a tangled messes, chase the satrap through his secret escape tunnel. Of course for story reasons it can be hard to justify doing that every day, so don’t. Don’t do it every session even, but do do it whenever you can.

        With Solars at higher essence it can be harder to do that, just because they have such powerful charms and their pools are so big. Although if you have your antagonists actually act smart, lay ambushes, and stunt and spend willpower like it’s their last day to live then you can squeeze some more potential out of them. You’re an Essence 1 elemental fighting a dawn cast to the death, what are you saving that willpower for? Even from an in game perspective, this is the greatest foe anyone has ever faced, the slayer of titans, if ever you were going to exert yourself it’s now.

        Even still eventually the scope might have to shift. Less so with Dragonblooded and more with Solars, but by the end to get those challenging days you might find it easier if the arc is set in Malfeas where third circle demons roam free, or deep in the Wyld. Solars are capable of fighting really cosmic threats, so let them do that sometimes.

        Finally, you can make things about degrees of victory, only the PCs are immortal gods, and they can’t be everywhere. If they don’t heed warnings of an impending doom because they think they’re invincible they might see half their kingdom crumble to dust before they win. From there they’ll need to deal with issues that charms and power don’t directly solve. There’s no charm or spell to instantly replace thousands of acres of burned crops, dead farmers, and water poisoned by demonic essence and the rotting bodies of your citizens. Those are the kind of large scale problems that can vex even powerful PCs, and can also lead to very personal human issues. If your PCs aren’t the types to make themselves kings that’s also okay, just as long as they in some way care about the people that live around them. In fact it might even be better as wandering heroes because then they can case down disasters over multiple nations.

                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                        Not to say it's impossible. But it's harder than DnD.
                                        First, that DnD story was great, thanks for that.

                                        Secondly it definitely is harder. The mote regen in combat means that anything that doesn’t take 5m or more per round to keep up with could just be a resource gain. There might be a couple of ways to get around that.

                                        1) Don’t roll out fights that are too easy. If there’s like 3 mortal conscripts just ask your players to describe how they smash them, or make it an ability check. Difficulty 3, dexterity + melee roll if they succeed the guards go down. It’s a problem that’s a little bit self mitigating as I wouldn’t expect super easy combats to last that many rounds, but it’s not exactly self correcting either. Even still the weakest fight I might go with is a size 2 battlegroup of elite soldiers lead by a grizzled 6-8 command dice vet and his 5-7 command dice lieutenant bowman for when he inevitably gets smoked turn 1.

                                        2) Don’t slip into the trap of thinking that Exalted is like medieval fiction when it’s mythic fantasy. I’m not saying go bonkerz crazy with it, but mortal sorcerers summoning and binding Blood Ape bodyguards, guards with alchemical firewands, highly elite, zealously dedicated and expertly lead soldiers are all commonplace. Well not common but common enough for people treating with gods and kings.

                                        3) Don’t worry about trying to cram 6-8 hard encounters into a day every day. Having some sessions that are more narrative focused or more chill helps cement the badass feeling and changes things up. It also lets you save up a lot of good ideas for when you unleash them.

                                        4)Have lots of challenges that are hard as nails but don’t involve combat time for the mote regen. They can even be really mundane things. Maybe there’s a 20 foot rocky unstable incline that they need to get up, quick shot, 2-3 motes spent into monkey leaps and graceful crane stances and they’re off. A solid stone door blocks the way, it’s lock mechanism long since eroded and fused, Increasing Strength Exersise, Ten Ox Meditation and a full excellency later and that door is torn down. Or maybe the door has a face and can only be opened by social influence convincing it to open. You can try punching through it anyway but at that point battle is joined with the door, and you’ve got really decent justification to make that fight deadly as all hell. Maybe it reflexively clashes all attacks launched against it or something. Or released a miasma of poison gas that’s either going to start peeling health levels off your characters, one of the most important resources of all, or drain more motes into a resistance roll. Or perhaps there’s two hundred doors, only one leads to the tomb of the Solar, and you need to do a big lore/investigation scene to figure out which one. Or spend days tearing each of them down, even Door-Evading Technique isn’t going to help you there. As long as your characters are making progress and not being stuck you’d be surprised at how much BS they’re willing and even excited to come up with.

                                        5) If the story calls for pressure, put on PRESSURE. They found one decent Dynast who’s actually willing to deal with them, and right now she’s dying of Purple Fever. I mean right now, it’d be a miracle if she lasts another day and she probably doesn’t have more than a handful of hours. If she dies she’s going to be replaced by a fanatical Wyld Hunt advocate. The only cure is a flower held deep within the sanctum of a wood elemental in the forest. The PCs trek their way there, weathering a jungle claw strider ambush leaping at them from the mist shrouded trees (-3 penalty to perception awareness rolls to spot the hidden Utahraptor-lizards) and thickets of razor sharp poisonous thorns dozens of feet high, but they’re getting a bit low, maybe half their motes gone so they rest go half a day before plunging into the sanctum to battle and/or treat with the elemental for the plant. Which they then do easily, and push back through the night in the now dark misty woods where they’re ambushed by the same assassins who poisoned their friend, again easily defeating them, but sadly return to find they’re too late. Now hopefully you really impressed on them how time sensitive this was, otherwise you’re being a dick, but if you did that’s totally fair. If they know you’re not going to lay out a safety net for them and always ensure they get what they want no matter how long it takes them, it should light a fire under their ass in the future since they don’t know how late is too late. That’ll help them act more like invested characters as well. Again not something that can be used all the time, not everything is a high pressure adventure, but when it is you can drag the PCs through hell. If the princess is about to be sacrificed to summon a second circle demon and you dawdle then she dies and the demon is born unto Creation.

                                        But I agree it is a lot harder, DnD makes it easy.

                                        Edit: it also works way better with less people. If your group is like 6-8 strong, that’s fucking hard. If you’ve only got 4...well seven silverwights all attacking at once with pack tactics might work. Also with less people you can run scenes faster and thus include more of them, and can use simpler foes who you barely have to think about because their only action is to attack.

                                                        I also have no real help here, Mahacara is the only thing I can think of that even comes close, and even then if you have a big party and/or multiple really combat focused characters... Also as much as I'm a fan of hammering the PC with challenges, a lot of the antagonists in the books just aren't able to do jack even at low essence. Although the next combat I run is going to be a pack of death moas and I plan to stunt and spend willpower like candy just to see if I can get them to do anything at all.

                                                        I find an almost worse problem to be breadth of abilities at higher essence, that whole "retrieve the antidote" plot I detailed above totally falls apart if even one character picked up an essence 2 medicine charm.
