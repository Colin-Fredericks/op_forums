                            To talk about the episode itself rather than the production for a moment...

                            Am I the only one mildly annoyed with the overall tone and moralizing in the whole White Fang arc? It feels like yet another extremely shallow "caricatured Malcolm X as the villain", which waaaay too many shows rely on to address issues of racism and prejudice. Like, ok, yes, violent extremism bad, but it's noticeable that the show has devoted almost no time to legitimate Faunus complaints (beyond one brief bullying scene in chapter one, plus vague handwaving indicating that worse stuff is going on), whereas it's devoted an entire massive plot arc with intensive focus to the flatly Dangerous Radical Chaotic Evil villain who goes Too Far. It doesn't even make it into a fair argument about methods - it explicitly states early on that Adam isn't actually interested in helping his people anymore. And while it talks in extremely vague terms about doing things another way, it never actually shows it (beyond the obligatory "fight on the side of the heroes in the big climactic confrontation", which does nothing to address the underlying structural issues.)

                            It's just so... shallow. The breaking point for me was Blake's tearful "stop looking for simple solutions" speech. Motherfuckers. They literally made the decision about what direction the Faunus should go in into a choice between the heroes who want to save the world, and a raving "dangerous radical" mass-murderer stalker who wants to hurt people for the sake of hurting them. Then they have the gall to lecture the viewers about seeing things too simply? Literally everyone in the White Fang (aside from the leader-lady who existed solely for Adam to stab to show how evil he is) is either a smirking, cynical manipulator with no actual convictions, or a naif who keeps making wrong choices and is just one friendship speech away from dropping everything they ever believed in and switching sides.

                            I am pissed. Like, yes, of course the White Fang and Adam as portrayed are scenery-chewing comic-opera villains with no depth, but it's annoying to have the show constantly present them as a parable for real-world issues of racism and prejudice and then lecture us about how we shouldn't go for simple solutions. How about we talk about those dust mines where the Faunus are being used as slaves? How about Ilia suggest that, you know, after we save Beacon academy, we march this army over to liberate some of the people there? Maybe, just maybe, in a world where Faunus are getting forcibly abducted and used as slave labor, there's an excluded middle ground between "use violence indiscriminately against everyone for sexual gratification" and "never use violence at all, just nicely ask Schnee Dust Company to stop enslaving us."

                            Like, I'm down for condemning Adam's violence, sure, but the show devotes huge amounts of time and focus to how horrible he is, and nothing beyond one or two bare mentions to the actual atrocities that have driven him to this point (in fact, it actually goes out of its way to say that he no longer cares about them, wtf.) Because, you know, if the Faunus bear collective guilt for Adam's actions unless they move to stop him, I am pretty sure you have to turn it around and ask what responsibility humanity has to address the stuff they did to the Faunus. And so far, the Faunus arc has been awfully light on that. Like... we're told Blake's father represents another path, but what, concretely, is he demanding?

                            (I am hoping we eventually get a more concrete scene where Weiss has to grapple with the crimes committed by the Schnee Dust Company and her own responsibility to do something about it in the same way - there have been some hints of that, at least. But right now I'm not terribly hopeful.)

                            EDIT: Also, the "Faunus are just as capable of hate as humans" bit was eye-rolling. Like... I'm pretty sure they know that, Blake? It's the specific things that humans are doing that have them pissed, not some abstract Faunus Supremacy. Even the White Fang in its full-on scenery-chewing villainy mode is still focused on that. So it feels like Blake (and the writers) are trying to make the situation simpler than it is by turning the White Fang's position (and, by extension, the real-world people it's trying to represent) into a caricature of itself and then make themselves look deep and wise by saying "look, everyone can be prejudiced!" Yes, sure, most people learned that when they were five, but I'm glad you've caught up, Blake. Now share your plans to close the literal slave-mines, which is the actual complicated part of this problem.

                                                            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                                            No, it wasn't.

                                                            He died before Volume 3 began airing, but he'd completed a number of Volume 3 things before he died. Such as expanding the concept of whatever Cinder was into "there's four of them, one for each season" and also parts of Penny's fight with Pyrrha, if not also parts of Yang's fight with Mercury.
                                                            By "production" I meant "animation production" as opposed to "writing and conceptual development." My understanding is that Monty died well before actual animation work on S3 began; they used a number of his assets (rigs, models, etc.) and concepts but that actual animation production did not begin in earnest until after his death.

                                                            But even if I'm technically wrong on 100%, Monty wasn't involved in the vast, VAST majority of Season 3 animation... and the fights still look amazing! They've got some of the best goddamn fights in the series in there, and they look and feel like a Monty Oum production. They actually to an extent feel BETTER than a Monty Oum production, because they have better assets and production quality backing them up than they'd ever had before. Actual backgrounds! Vivid colors! They managed to do that without Monty's presence.

                                                            And then they fell off a cliff on Season 4 and 5. The main change between seasons was they changed how they animate, moving to a completely different set of tools. Now, I've defended that move in the past; Monty was kind of an insane genius who got a lot out of Poser, but Poser isn't fit-for-purpose when it comes to "we're making a real show with a real production staff here."

                                                            But those defenses were predicated on them being able to get equivalent results out of Maya when it came to the giant, set-piece battles. And if they straight-up can't do that, even while throwing vastly more resources than Monty Oum ever had in his possession at the series, when they could do it with their previous tools... then that's a big problem.

                                                            I believe so, since I can't imagine that Rooster Teeth wants to make fight scenes LESS awesome than Weiss' character short, or that they're not aware of how most of the action in this Volume still isn't on Monty's level.
                                                            Rooster Teeth has marked tendency to sometimes not realize they aren't firing on all cylinders until it's way, way too late. That happened with more than one season of Red vs. Blue.

                                                            With RWBY, it's like... you can see what they WANT to do. When I take a look at the fights that have gone down this season, I can tell what they want the characters to be doing, but it's like they can't get there, like they threw up their hands at some point and went "fuck this shit, we're out." There's an almost palpable sense of frustration.

                                                            We know they can get there. The Ruby Volume 4 character short. The Weiss and Yang Volume 5 character shorts. To a certain extent the Geist fight way back when Volume 4 premiered. Those were all quality fights. They got REALLY close to being a Monty Oum Joint; close enough, I'd say. If everything were on that level, and it were occurring regularly, I'd have no complaints.

                                                            But then you get stuff like the Blake Volume 5 character short. Hell, just about everything Sun has been involved in; I don't know who this guy is, but outside of a very brief ten-second window he sure as hell ain't the Monkey King from the first three volumes, that's for sure.

                                                            I'm a bit baffled. Is it, like, a money thing? A time thing?
