            Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
            There's no tree that rewards you for focusing on any single element. [...] As Johndoe pointed out previously, Water melee isn't viable despite having a good chunk of the melee charmset.
            Melee rewards you INSANELY for focusing on Fire.

            All the charm trees reward you for going hard "in aspect". That's literally what the aura mechanic does.

            Water Melee, as DrLoveMonkey points out, is "fine". But if your character concept is defined as a swordfighter, then you're in for a bad time -- you will not remotely keep pace with a Fire Swordfighter using native DB melee charms. If you're playing a Gentleman Thief or a Naval Strategist, then Water Melee is more than sufficient to round out a little combat capability on the side.

            That's because Melee is a Fire Ability. If you want a unique and quirky character, that's not going to work fresh out the box. You're going to have to create custom charms to support your concept. That's a feature, not a bug.

            Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
            It's missing a number of core charms that let you take advantage of the later charms. That kind of design is just clunky and it doesn't particularly help a dragon blooded in a pure DB game let alone one where they are in a group with celestials who are capable of doing things x10 better. Even with an omni-aura, dragon blooded still distinctly under celestials. This is more for the sake of the dragon blooded having an easier time with their own build and concept. not having to take useless charms to get good ones.
            Again, this is a problem if you're theorycrafting.

            "What if I want to play an Archer? But I don't want to be a celestial, and I don't want to be a Wood or Air Aspect... I wonder if I can make an Earth Aspect Archer who can go kill Octavian? Wait... no I can't?!? THE GAME IS BROKEN!"

            Thing is... in a real game, if you wanted to play an archer... you probably would pick Wood or Air Aspect to support your concept. Just like you wouldn't make an Eclipse Archer and claim that Dawn Archers make your concept unviable because all the good Charms are locked behind Essence requirements and only Dawns get Supernal Archery. And even if you did make an Earth Aspect Archer, your ST would tailor encounters to fit the party -- they won't TPK you on Session 1 with a party of Five Octavians because you dared to bring an Earth Archer to the table.

            Exalted isn't a DPS Challenge.

            In a real game, you will have "good" charms on your sheet that you don't use (because you don't remember them, or you're out of motes, or whatever). And a good ST will note your XP expenditure and shoehorn in a challenge now and then so you can use your speedbump "useless" charms.

            No-one likes a chore. And if building DBs with the aura mechanic is not fun for you, then use that Golden Rule and fix it. I really, really, don't like "Duration: Aura" -- it's a real fun slayer in my opinion and I like to houserule it to "Keyword: Aura; Duration: Scene".

            But barring a couple of Charms that want Aura but provide no means of getting in Aura (can't remember what they are offhand), the Charmset isn't "broken", it just wants a bit if polish here and there. It might not be to your taste, but I like it.

            I like Aura as a peaking mechanic that let's Deebs spike high and punch above their weight. Omni-aura would very much dilute what I want from Dragon-Blooded. It's not a bad idea, but I don't think it intrinsically would help with running a Terrestial in a Celestial game. And I don't agree that giving a straight buff to a splat makes it easier to build the character you want. Having a Session 0 to discuss the expectations of the game and building characters as a party makes it easier to build the character you want. Not hitting some arbitrary DPS threshold.

            Hi, I'm JohnDoe244. My posts represent my opinions, not facts.

                            Wood aspects actually have good defense options in Dodge, at least close to what fire has. The only fire charms that help you defend are Flickering Candle Meditation, Bonfire Shadow Evasion, and Coiling Dragon Dance, and that last one is Essence 4. Meanwhile Wood has Swaying Grass Elusion, a cap bursting evasion booster that lets you reach Solar levels of defense boosting.

                            Is fire better at dodging? Yeah probably a bit, but Wood can dodge just fine, the wood anima is even a dodge penalty negator. Or if you wanted to you could just forget about getting wood aura and use the fire dodge charms anyway, or even just be careful about your bonfire anima and use the fire aura of the charms if you want to.

                            Resistance is pretty similar, Impervious Skin of Stone is balanced, and the only other direct soak boosting charm is water based. Meanwhile Wood, although less omni-applicable, lets your dodge based character wear medium armour, which is a flat +3 soak/hardness booster if you're not relying on parry. As for protection against decisive damage, water has a charm at Essence 1, Earth has the signature, but wood has a signature that heals decisive damage, Earth Bears Witness can be a wood, earth or water charm, and water has another two past that.

                            I will say that I think it was a mistake to put an air aura charm after two water aura charms in Resistance, and Revolving Hurricane Force should have just come off something else somewhere.
