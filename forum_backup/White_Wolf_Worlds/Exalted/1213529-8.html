                                            Hmmm... I agree with you in concept, that Exalted could use some nice adventures and things (I’m hoping the War in the West section will help out with that) but I do think that Exalted might have a couple of issues that make long Adventure Paths more difficult than they do in Pathfinder.

                                            Specifically, the Exalted in general and Solars in specific, have enough power and agency that it’s not hard for them to derail things if they want to. So, if I were constructing an Adventure Path, I’d try to make sure that the Players are really buying into the end goal of the thing and that the characters’ goals also matched up well. I’d also look for potential “failure points” in the adventure, places where I assume things will go in a particular direction and either make notes about what to do if it doesn’t happen that way or try to remove that particular choice from the Adventure.

                                            As for your specific example, it sounds pretty cool, but I think, now that you have all the bits worked out, you need to go back and try to make them fit together better so that it feels like one continuous story instead of a bunch of random vignettes connected together. Maybe take some of the early stuff and use it again later and pull some of the later stuff and insert foreshadowing for it early on.

                                            Also I think you need to cut Season 5. And the Airship.

                                            At the end of Season 4, you’re rushing off to go get into the final battle. And then Season 5 sends you off to wander in the Wyld for who knows how long, instead of going off to save the Sun from getting ganked. I could see it working for a session, maybe, to help build tension, but after that the players are just gonna be frustrated at how long this is taking instead of having a fun romp through the Wyld.

                                            Also, Season 5 starts with one of those “Failure Points” I mentioned before. Specifically, it assumes that a bunch of Solar Exalted are going to lose a battle. Which... Ok, I’m not gonna say I’ve never seen Solars run from combat before or lose a fight or anything, but... I would never bet on team of Solars to lose a fight, no matter how bad the odds. And, if the Solars don’t lose that fight, they’re gonna bypass the entire Season.

                                            Based on my experience with Exalted, it would actually be very, very difficult to force the Solars to lose a fight without risking a TPK in the process, especially if I was trying to murder an NPC at the same time.

                                            Thirdly, assuming their ship did crash, if you have a Sorcerer or a Craft focused Solar on the team, they could either take a couple days and summon some First Circle riding demons or just fix the First Age ship and be on their way, again short circuiting the adventure.

                                            So...I think you either need to cut it or move it earlier in the path when time isn’t such a big deal. And guard against those failure points I mentioned.

                                            As for the Airship, while it is cool...I feel like you should have the players capture it, recover it, dig it up, or something, instead of the Lunar saying “oh, yeah, I have the keys to the Five Metal Shrike in my pocket. Would that help?” Alternately, if the Lunar has that Legendary Size charm and can turn into a giant Firebird that everyone can ride, I think that’s probably just as awesome as a First Age Airship.

                                            Also, that undead Dragon King. You said one of the neverborn pulled a chunk of itself off and turned it into armor for the guy to wear? I think you can go bigger there.

                                            Two words

                                            Neverborn Warstrider.

                                                I like the idea of adventure paths. I've run several premade campaigns in other games, and in others have looted their premade stuff for some ideas even if I don't use it directly. However, Exalted has a major problem I feel in that the awesome powers available to a PC no matter what kind of Exalt they are makes it very hard to make any assumptions about how things will go.

                                                I'm going to compare this to another premade campaign I'm running right now, The Darkening of Mirkwood for The One Ring. The Darkening of Mirkwood takes place of 30 years of time in play with one provided adventure per year and suggestions for more if you want. After about six or seven years, things start showing up to the effect of 'if X NPC is dead/dishonored/you made them a foe, try to sub in a character who meets XYZ requirements'. By the end of the campaign there's precious few assumptions left and the adventures presented are mostly focused on the situations being created from the far side of the hero/villain line and the few guaranteed things left in the setting to help frame what you guys do. That isn't to say there's a lack of setting material or neat ideas, but basically the longer time goes on the less specific and more broad the strokes get. Though this going off the rails actually works out pretty well for the game, and the conclusion of it feels as epic as anything else LotR has ever offered and features ties between the Hobbit, the Silmarillion, and LotR.

                                                I mostly feel like an Exalted premade campaign will run into that problem of needing broad strokes even more strongly and basically immediately. Dragon-Blooded can do miracles that every possible GM can't prep for, let alone a Solar. Or forthcoming Lunars, Exigents, Sidereals, and more. It basically will become an exercise in planning for ways to encourage the party to stick to at least moderate rails which might be maddening for a writer, or an unfun railroad. I know this is a much maligned 2e product, but look at Return of the Scarlet Empress. There's so many contingencies and possible splits of story in there because a given circle of Exalted might lead to such different ends and arcs.

                                                    BrilliantRain I would like to respond to a couple of your points. First, practically all of the "Seasons" in our example AP were viewed from extreme distance. Each one would need to be developed into a very lengthy 100-page adventure book for it to have the kind of length and epicosity to truly qualify for an Exalted AP. As such, Season 5 would have way more to it than just simply a Wyld romp. That is merely is the central activity of that Season. In a similar vein, note how the broad overview of Season 1 mentions nothing about a fishing competition, and yet in the more detailed view, these kinds of neat events start showing up. I have no doubt that if we really dug into Season 5, we could have some very strong connections to Season 4 and Season 6 that would make that little side-jaunt seem very much in the right place. I felt much the same as you when Jim wrote that Season as the precursor to my War in Heaven ending, but I believe that with some work, it could end up being one of the best parts of the whole AP.

                                                    Second, I see everywhere (and hear it from some of the devs) that Exalted is just not the kind of game that could work well with a lengthy story (or just pre-written adventures in general) because the characters are too powerful, and the players will just not put up with being railroaded through some story, but I think that attitude is a bit dishonest. I could just as easily point out that D&D or Pathfinder or Starfinder characters are agents with free will that would never put up with being railroaded through the long stories written for them (the very popular and lucrative long stories, I might add), and it would be true. Our Iron Gods group could easily have walked away from the whole mystery and threat right at the beginning in the town of Torch and derailed the whole adventure...but then we wouldn't be playing Iron Gods - a story that we all decided would be really awesome and fun to play through. But that is exactly why the Pathfinder AP's all have the Campaign Traits that really tie characters into the setting and goings on anyway. You are essentially choosing a player option that links you into the story.

                                                    That being said, players will always derail a story a little bit, even when they WANT to do what they think they are 'supposed' to do to progress the story. I don't know if you've read a Pathfinder AP in a while, though. They're actually written very well, and I've almost never had trouble keeping players engaged in the fun. I pretty much defy the whole idea that such a thing can't be done for Exalted. Of course it can. But I would also say that even when you are preparing general situations for the players to face and leaving a lot of room open for player agency, players can still wreck the entire story, especially if they are misbehaving (maybe they're ticked at something, have indigestion, or whatever). This is actually the reason why Episode 2 of Wyld Shape is taking so long to be finished. We had to scrap the whole thing because of this very issue. But that could have easily happened in any Pathfinder AP with a player that just got it into his/her head to nuke the story.

                                                    Third, I think it would be very easy to engineer a fight that Solars will lose. The airship itself is not a Solar exalt. A sufficient number of enemies targeting vital systems could absolutely bring the craft down. There are actual rules for this game. I've not seen any Charms that say, "Pay 5m: win!" In fact, all-in-all I'd say the 3E Charms are much weaker on average than 2E. And I think it would be very easy to prevent your Solar crafter from just rolling to save the day - you'd be deep behind enemy lines for one thing.

                                                    I strongly believe that all role-playing games are a contract between gamemaster and players to tell a compelling story. Whether that story is massive and pre-written or completely homebrew with no prep beyond "What did Onyx Path release this week in the HDNP and AotR?" (looking at you, Chuck!) if the characters want to derail the story, they can. If they want to play the epic story you've devised, they can do that too. But in my mind, the absolute worst decision is just throwing up one's hands and saying, "Well, THIS can't be done, so let's not even try!"

                                                    I appreciate your comments, and the chance to try to 'defend' against them a little bit - even though I know that you are not 'attacking' with your words. The whole point of this particular episode was to challenge some of these very notions. In my anecdotal experience, the people I've encountered online have by and large played a couple of games of Exalted, but never really stuck with it for very long. Almost every single soul that I've ever met or interacted with who played Pathfinder, on the other hand, have told me all about all of the different APs they've played through, and which ones they've played all the way to completion. The difference is staggering, and if Exalted ever has a chance to really experience some success, I firmly believe it's going to need some really good stories for people to play.

                                                        Thanks for an interesting episode!

                                                        While I agree with many of @BrilliantRain's points, I'm mostly satisfied with your counterarguments. I agree with your argument that roleplay is an agreement between the GM and the Players about playing a story together - still, I think there's a huge difference between players refusing to follow the story and requiring them to ignore skills their characters have to advance it in a predetermined way. I mainly refer to the choice between fixing the airship and having a Sorcerer conjure a demon, Stormwind raider or (if they're already Celestial Tier) Travelling without Distance. The hypothetical Sorcerer player has invested a lot of resources so they'd be allowed to do cool stuff with spells. So it is unfair and bad design to skip a significant part of the adventure, if the PC wants to use a basic tool in their kit. (It's comparable to forcing a stealth encounter by saying the Fighter is suddenly not allowed to knife a guard.)
                                                        So I think your outline should at least provide a reason for the players to go to the Wyld even if they don't need the airship. (There are other smaller examples which I won't go into, unless you want.)

                                                        On another point, I have a vehement dislike for battles that the players are supposed to lose, so I frown that you already have two (airship battle and the first one with the slavers). If the players cannot influence the outcome, why have a battle? Why not begin the adventure with the PC's already captured or the ship already crashed? If there is to be a battle, its outcome should matter - even (or some would say, especially) if it's unlikely. Do the PC's have to get caught in the beginning? If they fight well, could they Exalt in combat?

                                                        The critique out of the way, I do think that your outline has a lot of interesting points: especially the ending, which I'm tempted to borrow parts for my own coming game. And I agree it shows that one can make adventure modules for Exalted.

                                                        I'm actually interested in what else you have to offer and how you would flesh out your outline further!

                                                        Anyhoo, I'd also like to see more open-ended adventures developed for Exalted. I think that the GUMSHOE method of adventure design could work even better for Exalted than the more rigid Pathfinder style. I think their flexible Scene-to-Scene style would suit a Circle, where the party might be hyperproficient in some areas, but lack even an Excellency in others. A critical difference between Exalted and Pathfinder is that Pathfinder characters can be assumed to be able to fight - and get better at it - at an even, measurable pace, while a Solar Exalted might not be able to fight at all and never get better at it. That's why a good Exalted adventure should have many solutions to any one problem. I could imagine a campaign style used in The Zalozhniy Quartet or Dracula Dossier easily adapted to the Exalted setting.
