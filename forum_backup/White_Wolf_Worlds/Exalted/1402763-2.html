            You don't need to Instill an Intimacy, IF someone thinks your Flex Tape is worth $20. If they don't think your Flex Tape is worth $20, your Bargain automatically fails. The full text of the Bargain action is in the OP, but here's a snippet:

            "you must offer a bribe, gift,
            or favor that the character you’re
            convincing believes is worth the dif-
            ficulty or danger of the task you’re
            asking him to perform."

            I don't see how you can read that and think you can just offer whatever you like. You have to offer something people think is worth the cost. That's the point of this thread -- who decides what's worth the cost.

            The rules aren't "you may offer anything you like if you roll really well". The rules are you must offer a worthwhile trade.

            That's why we have Charms for stuff like hiding a clause in a deal or selling your castle for a horse.

            They don't have to want Flex Tape. They have to think your Flex Tape is worth $20. If they don't think the tape is worth $20, it doesn't matter how many successes you roll -- your Bargain action fails.

            Funny you mention that Charm, because that middle ground is precisely what it finds (as opposed to Knowing the Soul’s Price which finds the offer they’d accept without a roll). All the examples of unacceptable bargains are egregious lowball offers like trying to bribe an aristocrat with a piece of fruit.
            Sure you can guess what a character considers worth the cost. But you have to offer something worth the cost. That's literally what the rules say. Sure, offering an apple to a noble isn't going to be as convincing as offering one to a beggar. But that doesn't mean you can offer anything you like to anyone for any task.

            My argument isn't "you should never make Bargain rolls". It's that the game is obviously skewed to assymetry between PCs and NPCs -- and PCs generally won't require Bargain rolls to accept deals where sufficient payment is being offered. Even if the system entitles them to apply Resolve against the deal, PCs (hungry for adventure, greedy for coin and eager for plot) will generally go along with what's offered. And if they don't, it's usually because you're trying to screw them by offering apples to nobles -- offer the characters something they want, or break out the Charms.

                        Apologies for the necro but I finally got around to reading through this thread.

                        Having read through the whole thing so far I'm generally more inclined to come down on JohnDoe244's side of things, mainly due to this particular extract:

                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post

                        Because I chose my Intimacies, or developed them naturally in play. I said to the world "my character cares about this". I decided that my Defining Intimacy defines what my character cares about. If you use something which I've told you I care about to make me act, then I don't have a problem.

                        My problem is if the ST says "you DON'T have a Defining Intimacy, but I'm going to make you do this anyway by paying you a talent of silver -- and no, you don't get to decide that this is worth more to you than money because I decide what your character cares about."

                        That's what I object to. No, the ST does not get to decide what a PC cares about. The player decides that.

                        The player decides what the character cares about. Cunning NPCs can manipulate the character based on what they care about. That's good gameplay.

                        Bargain doesn't care about what you care about. It cares about whether payment is sufficient.
                        This seems to me to me encompass the whole problem quite nicely: bargain supposedly works with reference to a character's desires, but for some reason ignores the system we already have in place (intimacies) and instead appeals to some nebulous set of motivations that exist beyond them. As pointed out above, though, the players have already declared what their characters care about by setting intimacies, so appealing to something outside those intimacies is meaningless because you're appealing to something explicitly outside what motivates their character.

                        To resolve this, I'd be inclined to do away with bargain all together and say that all dramatic conflict with respect to manipulating character motivations be carried out with persuade, instill and inspire actions. Bargain actions would then be a mechanically indistinguishable version of the persuade action, based in-universe on offering something to allow someone to satisfy an intimacy, as opposed to being based on rhetorical skill.or evidence.

                                                        Incidentally, intimacies do not cover things like this at all. Nobody has an intimacy for "I want to be fed, with shelter, and security". That's why intimacies represent "rerlationships, beliefs, ideals and goals" and not basic needs or simple human nature. Like again nobody really has an intimacy for "I really don't like to do pointless annoying tasks that take forever". Somebody could have an intimacy for that, but if they do it represents a very uncommon aversion to it. If somebody makes you till a field that isn't going to be planted one more time you will literally kill them, kind of thing.

                                                        But, like, why? Why aren't basic needs and stuff intimacies? Shouldn't it be harder to persuade somebody to do something if it endangers one of those needs? Well, yes, that's why you have to meet a bare minimum of Minor/Major/Defining intimacy to persuade somebody to do something that inconveniences them, risks harming them, or risks death respectively. If you're threatening somebody's most basic needs for life, and you don't have a Defining intimacy to key off of, you auto-fail.



                                                        Some more examples of where bargain would be best used:

                                                        A raiding party from a local dynasty-run kingdom has taken some of your friends as slaves, on orders from their matriarch. You bargain for them, offering 10 thoroughbred horses instead. He's going against the explicit orders of his matriarch, but surely 10 absolute unit horses is better than 10 unskilled and sickly slaves.

                                                        You need access to the imperial treasury, and you happen to know someone who works there who's a bit low on cash at the moment. You fix his jade problem, and he leads you into one of the archives without saying a word about it.

                                                        There's a small gaggle of street urchins in town, you know they wander the market picking pockets, and jingle a weighty little pouch of silver in front of them in exchange for any information about a woman with snow-white hair and a turtle-man bodyguard passing through the area. Might normally be an automatic thing, but this particular group was warned by their friends that anyone caught talking to people asking after that woman end up disappearing.

                                                        Offering to make a sizable donation to the local struggling immaculate temple, one large enough to completely redo the exterior and make it something that inspires awe and wonder in all who live near it, but in return the monk has to point you towards a fugitive he's been hiding.

Actually it's not, it an old Lunar friend of yours trapped in an artifact cage in the form of an extinct hummingbird, but the emperor doesn't need to know that.
