    So I'm the Dawn in DrLoveMonkey's game. We did fairly well, the fight wasn't a cakewalk by any means. Though the Tyrant Lizard was totally in there so the Deadly Predator Method(? The capstone survival charm if that's not it) Ox-Dragon could be used at some point in the game. Lots of motes were spent by most of the party, with myself and at least one other running off of just the 5 mote/turn drip by the end. I probably had the best setup for fighting him with Fire and Stones Strike and Iron Whirlwind/Invincible Fury of the Dawn. Having Octavian's mount there as a separate combatant definitely helped with killing him, but it also split up the party's attacks so I'm not sure if it would have gone better with everyone wailing on him or not.

    So we had a perfect circle (Dawn Melee Supernal, Zenith Survival Supernal, Twilight Craft Supernal w/ Sorcery, Night Stealth Supernal with Thrown, and Eclipse Occult Supernal with Archery), as well as a Dusk (NPC) that was the Zenith's wife and a Wood Aspect (NPC) that was with the Night. I fought Octavian, the Zenith Deadly Predator Methoded both his Dog and Ox-Dragon and sent the dog to fight Octavian and the Ox-Dragon to fight the Tyrant Lizard while he turned on Battle Anthem of the Solar Exalted. The Twilight summoned the terrestrial cloud spell and used that to take him and the eclipse up into the air while he charged up a flight of the brilliant raptor. The Night, Eclipse and Dragonblood were taking whatever opportunity shots they could against Octavian or his Agate. The Dusk was off fighting another NPC Abyssal off-screen.

    Myself and Octavian were reasonably matched in that our withering attacks didn't end up doing a whole heck of a lot to each other (Him having high soak, and me boosting my parry so he didn't hit me). It was once I had made my Invincible Fury of the Dawn attacks that dealt some decisive damage and applied some silly onslaught penalties to him that helped allow the Wood Aspect to get in a REAL good withering shot on him and allowed some more attacks to get through to stack on some more damage. The Ox-Dragon basically went off and fought the Tyrant Lizard, I didn't end up paying a huge amount of attention to the other combatants because I was trying to sort out what my next turn would be usually. I ended up having to attack the Agate twice using Peony Blossom to build up a bunch of initiative to then next turn Iron Whirlwind Octavian for lethal.

    There wasn't a huge amount of health level damage (though the Eclipse almost got one-shotted by his scream), but there was a LOT of back and forth in the fight which I think is just as important. There was a time where I honestly wasn't sure HOW we were going to beat Octavian, the high soak, high hardness, and ability to gain initiative from decisive damage that he's taken AND the ability to stem HL damage to his current wound penalty made him REAL nasty to fight. Having Battle Anthem up really helped our side as you always gained SOME initiative.

            He had like 17 Soak and 10 Hardness, so you weren't easily taking him down in initiative (For point of reference, before Fire and Stones Strike and overflow successes, his soak took away my entire damage pool, forcing me to use my min damage of my daiklave), and because he had a decent hardness it also meant that people couldn't just throw what initiative they had at him trying to inflict SOME damage. He also never got crashed, which would have changed things a fair bit. There was just no good way to knock him down in initiative without just straight up dogpiling him and hoping min damage chipped away enough. And then he'd either hit you back with a withering attack and gain initiative, or gain a bunch when he was dealt HL damage. It was not a quick fight by any means, he's a resilient opponent, but there are some holes in his defenses. Onslaught penalties in particular get to him.

            Him and the Nephwrack I think are fairly well made adversaries in that they have ways to stay relevant to the fight (being difficult to damage and gaining initiative from not normal means, and difficulty to attack as well as attack redirection, forcing the players to spend extra on raising a defense against the attack redirection as well as on their normal attack/defense respectively.)

                    The big thing with High soak bosses is that if you don't give the players SOME way of generating initiative it'll start getting frustrating for them. "I hit, roll min damage, oh look 2 successes. and the boss hit me back for like 6-7, and I'm lower then he started". Also having other combatants helps reduce the dog pile and onslaught penalties on the boss. I think the idea is more to have the big boss AND a competent underling there, someone that's scary enough that the party feels they should devote some manpower to tackling both targets without one being a pushover or mook.

                                        No problem KindledRadial, it was an end of campaign fight that was supposed to be difficult but enjoyable, and I think it faired quite well at that. As for the battlegroup thing I think it's less that beating up the mooks builds up combat steam and more like you're spending what combat steam you build up on fighting them, netting out to no real gains except when knocking off a magnitude track I think it was? Making them go down a size? Something like that.

                                        The thing in particular with gaining initiative in unconventional ways is that if you're outnumbered you just aren't going to win that initiative fight. if it's a 2 v 1 that one guy needs to deal with both onslaught penalties making him easier to hit AND two attacks sapping away his initiative. For each round he has to somehow put out twice the amount of withering damage then one opponent just to stay initiative neutral! But if he can passively sap some initiative from those two guys a turn, or generate some himself it becomes much easier to keep up with things.

                                                            Here's a general piece of storytelling advice: "one large fire is much easier to deal with than several small ones."

                                                            If you only have one bad guy, doing one bad thing, in one location, at one time, then the entire party will dog pile that one guy and in short order overcome him. Now if it's one bad guy and several henchmen they'll have to juggle targets in that combat. If those henchmen are across town pillaging the local citizenry then you might prompt a party split to deal with that and now your one big bad is dealing with much better odds while his flunkies take the fall.

                                                            Also a good idea in general: if you make an area/character/thing important, always have something planned for it. I almost lost two players last game because they stuck with the mortals while the rest of the party flew off to fight the big bad, so try and cover your bases.
