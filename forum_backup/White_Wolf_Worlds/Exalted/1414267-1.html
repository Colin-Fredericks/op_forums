                Originally posted by Scrollreader View Post
                Sure. And for a Supernal solar it's more a question of theme, than the one extra dice on top of your 10/20 dice roll with a bunch of charms. My Str 5, Ath 5 Ath Supernal doesn't have Athletics - Feats of Strength because I think I'll need it to tip over a wagon. He has it because he is the strongest one there is. And so he can lift one more plate than the other strongest guys. Or throw a Yeddim at someone. A stealth 5 dex 5 character doesn't want to sneak up on Ahlat, he wants to sneak up on Ahlat over a nightingale floor (-2) while injured (-1) when Ahlat knows he's coming (-2). Or hide behind Ahlat in Ahlat's shadow in his house in Yu-Shan for a day. Or do so on the move in combat without much cover (-5). You know, epic stuff for the epic stealth dude.
                With nothing more than an excellency though? If you’re sneaking up on a directional war god, while wounded, and over a nightingale floor that might be the time to combo Perfect Shadow Stillness with Sound and Scent Banishing Attitude. If you’re sneaking without cover in combat, use Stalking Wolf and Blurred Form Style to completely negate those penalties, and give you even more bonuses besides, like autosuccesses and free initiative. Those aren’t even like, epic Essence 5 Supernal Only charms, only one of them is even Essence 3.

                The actual Stealth guy, the guy who’s super into stealth? On top of his excellency he’s rerolling all non-10 dice, keeping the 10s, ignoring almost any penalty he has, treating her opponent’s 1s as her own 10s, reducing enemy successes by one for every 1 and 2 they roll, and that’s assuming she isn’t using any number of the automatically successful charms that are completely incontestable. If I can get a 99%+ chance to succeed on my stealth attempt against the greatest and most keen sentinels in the world with just my excellency, what’s even the point of all that other stuff?



                Originally posted by Scrollreader View Post
                My survival 3 - Gross but Effective dude is fine if his spec doesn't apply. He's not hanging his hat on surviving in hell, or turning a tyrant lizard into a pet. He just wants to be more or less able to make his way wherever, and survival 3 does that.
                Your survival 3 guy can definitely survive in Hell and tame a Tyrant Lizard.

                If I'm a dex 3 dodge* 3 Eclipse the difference between evasion 3 and 4 seems far different. And like you said, even for a maximum skill DB, a success or static adder is 50% more effective with a specialty.

                Originally posted by Scrollreader View Post
                Let's take a not-terribly combative dynast. Maybe he went to the Thousand Scales because men don't have enough self control to make good warriors. He's a fantastic bureaucrat, and getting better at politics. He's got dex 3, ma 3 and an Excellency (at least). That's pretty good (I mean, he's still a DB) If he has a spec, his parry is 4/6. If he doesn't, his parry is 3/4. One of these characters has a whole lot more to worry about from two or three Conscripts (literal peasants dragged from their fields) than the other does. That makes a specialty a big deal for him. (He's obviously going to win, in either case). But he's an elite martial artist (by the book's or a mortal's definitions, if not so among the Chosen), and the applicability of his specialty is likely to matter an awful lot more than either of the Solars above.
                It’s a bit different for DBs, as John pointed out especially for success added charms an applicable specialty can make a pretty big difference, and they also just have less “basically I just win” charms in their charmset. On the other hand DBs get more specialties than Solars or Lunars, and have charms that grant themselves temporary ones, which helps a bit. I still don’t necessarily think it’s critical to always have an available specialty, especially if you’re already at an even number of of ability dots. It might be a good idea to take multiple specialties in the same ability though.

                Originally posted by Scrollreader View Post

But that's an unrelated issue.
                I believe the reasoning here is that Dodge is used in more than just combat, it’s also used to resist environmental hazards and disengage, and also the dodge tree tends to be a lot more powerful defensively than brawl/ma or Melee. Although Solar Melee comes close. Also it can be used even while using a bow and against certain unblockable attacks. Although there are also yndogable ones.

                                                            I made a thread in ages past about this very topic as it relates to Melee (Brawl/Archery/MA/etc)
                                                            I hate Melee Specialties (and you should too) - Onyx Path Forums
                                                            http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/exalted/1222772-i-hate-melee-specialties-and-you-should-too
                                                            Provocative title out of the way, I hate the way Melee Specialties are boring flat bonuses. Lets posit a few statements first: 1- Only one specialty applies at a


                                                            Sorry i cant format it I'm mobile at the moment.

                                                            Basically, i find it incredibly bad design that the game says "your specialty shouldn't always apply" and "Melee (Swords) is fine". Many exalted take an artifact right out of the gate and if not they usually find one early enough. Call the Blade shuts down disarming (and honestly, how often does Disarming come up?) And TTRPGs just can't replicate the videogame feel of switching weapons for different combat scenarios.

                                                            So I told my players to think of an interesting specialty and that their weapons weren't allowed. Here's what they did:

                                                            Melee: Sword Tricks. This is functionally a specialty in Stunts but the player is new to rpgs and pretty hesitant to express himself. So his character used to do sword tricks on the street for money to finance his Occult studies in exorcism, and it really incentivizes him to RP more. As much as the conventional wisdom is "stunt every attack" it only comes up on the 2 pointers, so it works out.

                                                            Brawl: Grapple. Toes the line, but I allowed it. It doesnt help any of the punching or kicking and the player only uses Brawl as a side piece to Single Point, which leads to

                                                            Single Point: On a Boat. He's a pirate. This is exactly the kind of specialty i wanted to see because its cool, it reinforces the RP of the character, and its not always on. Its probably 50/50 in our campain so far.

                                                            Integrity: Heresy. Self explanatory and awesome.

                                                            Melee: Against Artifact Users. This is mine on my crafter. He is an artifact weapon and armor smith, so using an artifact against him gives him a ton of insight into your fighting style. Only comes up against other Exalt mostly but thats enough for a non primary combatant.

                                                            Ebon Shadow: Dynasts. An assassin with a specific type, hes studied DBs enough to know how they think and how their powers work. Also its implied that the specialty is 50-99% his raw hatred for them pushing him on.

                                                            Occult: Ghosts and Occult: Demons. Two different characters. Serviceable, a little boring but they belong to an Exorcist and a Demonologist respectively so it reinforces what they are good at. This and Lore are pretty much Carte Blanche to pick something boring but practical

                                                            Lore: Military, Lore: Great Houses, Lore: First Age. Pretty standard.

                                                            Stealth: (setting up an) Ambush. This one is interesting because it doesn't cover thievery and spying, but it does cover anything related to murder. Its powerful, sure, but it has a strong theme and doesn't cover the out of combat uses for stealth (which is a lot of the table time)

                                                            I'll stop there. One thing i wanted to show is that specialties in Emotional reasons can be quite interesting (i.e. Hate based specialties pushing you to succeed), and good specialties should reinforce your character's role play (the pirate at sea), and sometimes, just sometimes, boring is fine (Lore and Occult)

                                                            Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.
