    Thinking About 3e Lunars

    02-15-2017, 09:16 AM
    So I guess a disclaimer that must be said is that I'm not necessarily looking for an argument about what Lunars should or should not be, but rather an examination of existing information and conversation to try to best understand what Lunars look like in 3rd Edition. The goal is that in assembling some baseline of ideas, it might help with homebrew that occurs on a minor level (QCs and other significant NPCs) as well as major ones, like people who might be interested in trying to write up the splat in 3rd Edition.

    I was thinking of combing through the explanations offered by the Devs during the Q&A sessions that happened early on (with an understanding that some of these things might be outdated or might have had their details changed), looking at the QCs that are currently in the corebook, and hopefully pondering or investigating other posts where the devs have shared some insight or thoughts on Lunars.

    I would appreciate it if we avoid getting really negative about the direction of the Lunars if it's something that anyone expresses apprehension over. I think the most valuable thing to do in this situation is to try to understand the consistent idea of Lunars being offered in 3rd Edition, as I certainly feel from my own exploration of these conversations that there is a consistent idea being expressed, even if some of its details have evolved over time. Try to keep it positive!

    To give an example of what I'm looking for, I'd love to engage in a conversation about the Lunar QCs in the 3rd Edition corebook. We can start with looking at the basics.

    Lunar Excellencies
    The Lunar Excellency is pretty straightforward in how it can be powerful. Attribute is a solid pool that will usually be high for a Solar, nevermind a Lunar who might have slightly higher starting Attribute dots. That it doubles when stunted suggests two interesting things to me:
    • Stunts are important here. In his excellent discussion of barbarism (thank you for writing that, Holden!) Holden mentioned that the aesthetic of barbarism and the like was a rich one that Lunars can draw upon to help differentiate themselves, and I find that to be really excellently rendered in an Excellency which engages with stunts. From a mechanical perspective, it also intrigues because it could be suggestive of how stunts like those described could also play a part in enhancing the mechanics of other Charms in the Lunar repertoire.
    • A Lunar can take on a task where their Ability rating is very low, but possibly succeed at it through a canny stunt and raw dice power of their Excellency. This is cool, because if one doesn't fixate too much on the idea of Lunars being good generalists, the mechanics of the excellency are potentially suggestive of it. Similar to my mechanical note above, it also says, to me, that there's a great possibility of many Lunars being surprisingly capable of doing something which they might not have much natural training in.
    • A small part of me is anxious about the idea of how to make Ability ratings into meaningful choices for the Lunar Exalted. Perhaps I've had too many critical discussions about charm sets for other types of Exalted which felt completely disconnected from what a person could actually do.
    A sort of concluding thought is that, at its most basic, I know that Solars may reach for the greatest heights of power, but the Lunar Excellency lets them out on that playing field in a way that's really cool! I wonder how to describe their dice-caps though? As the Attribute in use x2? or would it simply be Attribute+Attribute used to stunt an action? A minor note that comes to mind is that it's probable that while they can throw around a lot of dice, they might hit the Solar dice-cap of 10 less often because of Attribute values being what they are and the degree to which it's feasible to always 'stack' your highest Attributes on an action.

    Shapeshifting
    Oh man. One of my favorite superpowers in fiction, the shapeshifting of the Lunar Exalted pulls me to the splat in a lot of ways. Here are more opportunities for noticing some mechanical baselines.

    One thing that I feel should be said is that I don't get the impression that shapeshifting is actually the root expression of Lunar Charms. A very consistent impression I've gotten from the discussions I've seen from John lead me to believe this. It does seem however that it isn't absent from the charmset either. It strikes me that if we look at shapeshifting as a distinct thing associated with the Lunar Exalted, that we might look at it in the same way that some of the less skill-oriented Solar Exalted powers seem to be powers that we'd associate with the Unconquered Sun. That isn't to say that I expect all of the Lunar shapeshifting charms to be the apex of the charmset, but rather that these effects will be flavor or seasoning that accompanies themes primal power, raw cunning, that sort of thing. Ramble aside...
    • Four motes to transform, just one short of what would lead to anima flare. A nice little detail. I wonder if Charms might potentially add to the cost of this while providing additional benefits? Could be good design space for Charms.
    • The transformation lasts indefinitely. Cool! If you want to spend all your time as a raven, you totally can. I love that part of shapeshifters, where they can just chill out in an alternate shape.
    • Transforming into your "true human form" is free and reflexive. That's an awesome touch. No mention of a spirit shape or anything here. A spirit shape is totally the kind of thing I would cut from a QC for the sake of word count, or because the team hasn't figured out how to tackle that issue again, but it will be interesting to see if that continues. I think it's a great way to give Lunars an additional shape at start and that the animal being connected to their personality is an awesome bit of aesthetic/informs a character in a really cool way, so it'll be very interesting to see how that's approached.
    • Animal form grants access to all physical dicepools. This makes perfect sense when you're using QCs, and is a great way to simplify it for NPCs, but it does beg a small clarifying question: Do you choose to use their dice pools over yours in that form, or are you obligated to do so? I think from what I recall of reading Manual of Exalted Power: Lunars there were charms in 2e that you had to learn to 'choose' which stats to use when shapeshifted into that form. My gut is suggesting to me it's a choice, but I'm not 100% sure. If it is a choice it might be a nice way to remove what some might call exp tax or one of those mermaid problem charms.
    • They get special attacks and merits of animals when using them. Awesome! Obviously these are the kinds of things which form the meat of having cool useful animal forms, but it's a great way for a Lunar to have some more diversity in his approach to his charms and abilities in combat. Plus, I don't recall that he can't use his Charms in conjunction with them.
    • Latent or Magical abilities are not granted by shapeshifting, but can be unlocked via training and charms. Training them might work like spending EXP to grab them, possibly the amount you might spend training a pet or familiar to unlock them? That gives a cool experience way for someone to 'trick out' one of the animal forms they like to take. Using Charms to do it too is pretty cool, it reminds me of that Solar Occult Charm that let's Solars learn thaumaturgy rituals by committing motes. I could see a cool one like meditating with a bunch of birds to 'learn' and spend the motes to unlock it rather than Experience.
    • Animals with Legendary Size are beyond the skill of most young Lunar Shapeshifters, but this limitation can be overcome. This brings us back to an age old conversation about the Tyrant Lizard Spirit Shape. Making it beyond the skill of most is intriguing, but it seems clear that if you really want to, you can overcome it. That does seem like a decent compromise. Sure, you might have the occasional Tyrant Lizard Lunar, but would it be good if they made it so that you couldn't have other big guys like the Mammoth or the Elephant? Or, god willing, the great and mighty Yeddim Lunar? I'm teasing around, but this seems fair enough.
    • The Tell is such a cool ideas, and I'm glad to see it here. It doesn't really need to state here if it's like some kind of psychic effect or not, because word count and QC, but I wonder if they'll spend many words on it in the Lunars book. That somebody who is looking for it might spot it is pretty awesome, and a default difficulty of 7 is no joke. The Tell is another great way to give a character a certain visual flair.
    One thing I note here is that you do not get the health levels of the animal you take the shape of. This makes good sense! Shifting HLs are already hard to deal with on their own. In addition to an Ox-Body, I can easily imagine a design space for gaining bonus temporary health levels when shapeshifted. It makes me wonder if broad criteria to activate powers like, "shapeshifting into a large animal" might be in there. It certainly feels appropriate with stunts being a big thing for their Excellency!

    It feels like a strong but basic set of guidelines for use. Great for the QCs, and leaving us with a number of intriguing questions.

    Going on from there, a look at a specific QC, one of the two we've got, the Barbarian Warlord!

    Barbarian Warlord
    So this is the first QC. I will try to make specific observations about mechanics.
    • Worshipped as a living god! So we see a Lunar among the barbarians in action. As Holden noted in his essay, it seems like even when he's fighting alongside a horde of barbarian people, he still exists as an other, a living god that they worship.
    • He's Essence 2! For an Exalt, that's big news. Tough guy who has clearly been at it for awhile at least. That makes it slightly harder to tell if his Charms are all Essence 1 or 2, though! Oh no!
    • The Shapeshifting section presents a Bear as his main animal form for combat. Could this then be a "Spirit Shape" or just the first one mentioned?
    • He has a variety of forms, animal and human. No mention of other things, but that's cool.
    • Deadly Beastman Transformation: Holy crap that's scary! But wait, "The Lunar may shapeshift into a ferocious battle form that is half-man, half-bear." That description of the Bear as his main form suddenly seems to lend credence to some kind of spirit or signature shape still existing... Which is cool. Clearly, it interacts with DBT, which I expect to be a Charm. Looking at the values, I'm intrigued... Glancing at his stats, I think that +4 to withering damage might be adding (Strength) to the withering attack damage. But maybe he has really high Dexterity? I dunno. The boost to Strength and qualifiers for Feats of Strength is definitely (Essence) which is really cool. Beware the Essence 5 Lunar! I suspect that, if DBT goes into the Charm tree, it might go into Strength. I considered Stamina, but suspect that is 3 rather than 4. I have a suspicion that this might be E2 DBT after getting a bunch of upgrades, namely halving Wound Penalties and healing damage conditionally. Maybe the (Essence) to Strength for feats of Strength could also be an upgrade charm, but because it's just for Feats of Strength, it might not be.
    • Spine-Breaking Technique this one is brutal. Really mess them up in a Grapple. I think this is an example of an effect that's a bit 'barbaric' or 'raw' but not necessarily rooted in the shapeshifting, and that Lunar Charms might look most often like this. I think this is a Strength Charm as well.
    • Terrifying Bestial Visage Charms which are conditionally activated on transformation! Awesome. He rolls 7 dice, which is likely a reference to his intimidation pool, and the successes are compared against Resolve and it acts as a command or rally action for the forces under the Lunar's control. So basically while shapeshifting they can get a social influence/command in at the same time. This is a neat charm, and a very powerful one! Since it has a specific condition for its activation, I think that might account for how this effect is used, because it's sort of limited by the amount of times you transform in a fight, and more notably, it's limited by what you transform into. This kind of conditional Charm usages seems like a great way to build a Lunar playstyle! I honestly have no idea what Attribute I'd peg this for. Charisma? Appearance? Intelligent? I suspect Charisma is the answer.
    • Impenetrable Beast Armor is this one literal or metaphorical? A straightforward and meaty defensive charm. Halving post-soak damage is a flat effect, but subtracting three dice from the raw damage? If we peek at his soak/hardness, I suspect the value here is (Stamina). I also would bet that this is a Stamina Charm. Whether one might call this a 'shapeshifting charm' is ambiguous to me, but I imagine it might be a bit metaphorical. It's very solid though! Lunar charms using Attribute values to boost things looks like it could be very scary, though.
    That covers the Barbarian Warlord, and I might try to do a close look at the Trickster later. Overall, I actually do think these charms do a really decent job of conveying a distinct tone of Lunar power, and of showing interesting mechanical conditions that Lunar charms could be activated in. This is definitely speaking to the combat side of things.

    I remember reading one criticism that DBT felt like it was an obligatory pick up for Lunars in 2nd Edition, and I wonder how they'll do that here. It strikes me that at a glance, I feel like the bonus to withering damage and the bonus to Feats of Strength are probably the core of the charm, and that its other benefits might be upgrades, but that thinking might be wrong-headed. It's also worth noting that if some charms have conditional activations which require shapeshifting that it might be having the trade-off between using your DBT and losing out on those conditional charms, which could be one way to do it.

    For some reading outside of the corebook that might spark some conversation: Holden on Barbarism

    That post by Holden is great not just for breaking down some complicated concepts that are gonna be engaged with by Lunars, but also gives a great little history of how they've been engaged with in previous editions. Knowing where they came from is definitely a part of trying to figure out how to not make the same mistakes, and it's generally a good read anyway.

    Eric Minton also did a great job collecting a number of bits from a conversation about Lunars on his blog. Lunar Exalted: What Are They To You? Summary

    This has some old posts by a lot of members of the team talking about things. Some of it is a mite bit confusing, and perhaps talking through it might help all of us understand better?

    I completely understand that views on some of these things might've shifted in the intervening years, but I do still think there's value to be gleaned from these things, if you guys have any thoughts on any of this or things you want to add, please do! Lunars are very intriguing to me as someone who has not played a ton of Exalted.

      I have a definite, strong image of them as dual-natured shapeshifting monstrous heroes based on how they've been portrayed in Ex3 so far and past discussions on their direction.                                                           After posting it, I realised that it may not apply to everyone.
                                                          When I first started playing Exalted, I thought stunting was really cool. And we used to stunt basically everything.

                                                          But having played tons and tons of Exalted, when in combat, we really just want it to go along at a quick pace, and not get slowed down by going back and forth by asking about stunt bonuses (2nd ed required constant 2-dice stunts, which got rather annoying).

                                                          And I myself basically don't give out stunt bonuses for flips, because... so... many... ****ing flips... it's just not exciting any more.

                                                          There's one guy who thinks of really cool stunts in combat, but everyone else doesn't care. It's something to do when you want to eke out another few dice on occasion, not something to do on every combat action.

                                                          In fact, the guy playing the Changing Moon essentially never, ever stunts (except when it's a social action, because saying "I use social stuff on them" rather than actually saying what you're saying, is pretty lame). He certainly never asks for stunt bonuses.

                                                          So that's the environment that we're using this rule.

                                                          If you have a group who are really keen on stunts and still love them, as we did once.



                                                          I'd also say, don't take this as me saying new Lunars are terrible. Other than that, I'm happy with pretty much everything we've seen so far, both mechanically and fluff-wise. The only other mechanical issues we've had is the general issues of using QC stats (ie there's some rolls there's no dice for, we weren't sure about how they interact with dice caps, etc), but that's likely to get cleared up in the book (Vance has already commented on the dice caps).
