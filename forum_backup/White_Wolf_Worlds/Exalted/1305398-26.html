                                                        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                        I also completely forgot engage actions exist, and they’re very much the battlegroups response to a single powerful character.
                                                        As a RWBY fan, I've recently been exploring how THIS is the thing that makes Grimm really dangerous.

                                                        That is: Almost any Creature of Grimm has at least as much mass as (and frequently more than) a human being, and Grimm usually try to tackle and pin targets to the ground.

                                                        This is why I've been asking about how to model that capability.


                                                        In the Red Trailer, Ruby Rose made exterminating battle-groups of Beowolves look easy because she avoided all but one attack, and that attack only knocked her back rather than down. Since she consistently avoided getting knocked prone or grappled, she always retained her complete freedom of movement.

                                                        Obviously, this was also helped by her ability to kill the closest Grimm in a single attack, and sometimes kill multiple Grimm with a single attack, thus denying them these individuals the ability to attack her at all. But that doesn't map very well to battle-groups.

                                                        But in the Weiss Short, Weiss Schnee gives a similar performance until she slips up and shows us how quickly Ruby could have died. Even ignoring the exact mistakes leading up to it, as soon as one Beowolf grappled Weiss and bore her down to the ground, she became unable to dodge or retreat, and she could not have easily parried or struck back with her sword even if she hadn't been disarmed. She was seconds away from being dogpiled and savaged to death by the other Beowolves when her sister banished them all.

                                                        I'm away from my book right now, so I can only vaguely recall that being "engaged" by a battle-group isn't as restrictive as being grappled by a single opponent.

                                                        Also, calling it "engaged" was a silly idea, since it's not what the Disengage action is supposed to counter.

                                                        It's as silly as not actually making more deliberate use of the "embattled" term that actually denotes the condition that the Disengage action is supposed to counter -- being caught at close-range by an opponent such that moving away is impossible without using a Disengage action. "Embattled" should have been called "Engaged" instead, and the term should actually be used in Charms designed to counter engagement.

                                                        A battle-group's ability to grapple could have called "embattle" instead, or something like "mobbing", "grasping", "pinning". Or just call it grappling.


                                                        But in any case, the publicly available preview for Volume 7 continues to treat the Grimm this way: As long as our heroes mange to strike them first, the Grimm die instantly and the danger seems minimal. Our heroes rarely give the Grimm a chance to test their Parry or Evasion ratings.

                                                        But repeatedly, other Grimm attack from a hero's blindside with a pounce intended to grapple and savage them. Twice, a hero is saved from this fate by an ally shooting the Grimm dead at the last second. Jaune counters being grappled by using his shield to bodily hold the Grimm at arm's length as he stabs it dead, but it takes so much of his effort that a second Grimm attacking from behind would have gotten him.
