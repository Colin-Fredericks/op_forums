    Also, what are the rules on using Thrown weapons – do you have to ready another one when the old one gets tossed away?
    One of the examples in the draw/ready weapon action is preparing a brace of knives for throwing, which indicates that you don't need to ready each individual thrown weapon.         Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
        How do you think Thrown compares to Archery?
        Archery feels more sustainable, and definitely safer - you can be an archer from range just fine. Archery has a lot of big reusable attacks, which Thrown is low on, and it can put a lot of sustained pressure on via magical flurries. It's also easier to guarantee you're never unarmed, if you're an archer - either by a summoned bow or by one you're keeping in Elsewhere - while Thrown seems almost designed to run out of ammo fast.

        Thrown feels light on sustained pressure. But Thrown is faster, and alpha-strikes hard; it can cripple you for the whole fight, or hit you at Defense 0 for double damage, or fire attacks without ever breaking stealth. I would probably want Archery for a sustained combat, but if I need guerrilla warfare, I'm going to go Thrown. I suspect a Thrown-based enemy could be a real pain in the neck to hunt down and pulverize.

                            Originally posted by tonberrian View Post
                            The way I've read riding your familiar: normally, your mount does not roll initiative, even if it is a familiar. You two, together, have one initiative attack, and withering attacks against either of you go against it and your mounts attacks use your actions. You can attack the mount decisively and kill it out from under the rider, but you're encouraged to use the dehorse gambit instead. If you check the respective rolling into battle charms in Ride and Survival, Seasoned Beast-Rider's approach allows you to roll your mount in and give it its own Initiative track and actions, while Ambush Predator Style enhances that roll, so no incompatibilities there.

                            So yes, your familiar is less competent if you leap onto it. It's part of the penalty for riding anything being a very significant advantage.
                            From Seasoned Beast-Rider:

                            "With this Charm, activated when the player rolls
                            Join Battle, the Exalt’s mount gains an Initiative track
                            with a starting value equal to her own, and may make
                            attacks on its own turn."

                            Which means Ambush Predator Style can't be used (since your mount never rolls join battle) and Force Building Predator Style doesn't work since it requires your familiar to have been rolled into battle with Ambush Predator Style.

                            OTOH it means you get double the payoff from using an awareness excellency for join battle since its generating your own and your familiars initiative.

                            Beast Rider tree and survival have great synergy in general, but some of the better ride charms require essence 2-3.

                            Once you get there though Wrathful Mount Invigoration (1 initiative per turn plus [your Essence] initiative for every successful movement action) + Crimson Talon Vigor (half the initiative from your withering attacks) lets your mount-familiar generate pretty decent amounts of initiative without even making its own withering attacks, which means with Deadly Predator Method it can just keep decisive attacking for ever larger amounts of damage unless people manage to do withering damage to it through its 30+ soak.

                                                    War

                                                    Number of Charms: 17
                                                    By Ability requirement: 1/3/3/5/5
                                                    By Essence requirement: 6/6/5/0/0
                                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Recurring 6s.

                                                    Themes: Train your troops to the peak of humanity. Outmaneuver your enemy. Protect your army. Produce lots of successes on a War roll.

                                                    One final tree! And if it’s not the smallest yet, it’s darn close. Let’s take a look at War.

                                                    Charms:
                                                    1. Your army has +1 Size and ignores the penalty for poor drill troops.
                                                    2. Train a crew to reroll nonsuccesses on an artillery weapon.
                                                    3. Your troops are immune to mundane demoralization (as per the maneuver), as well as penalties for weather, food, etc.
                                                    4. Make a reflexive rally action with bonus successes.
                                                    5. When rolling for a stratagem, ignore penalties for unfamiliarity with the enemy general or army.
                                                    6. Double-9s or double-8s on an Order.
                                                    7. Grant your unit improved stats and drill; spend XP to grant them bonuses against select enemies or perfect morale.
                                                    8. Recover points of Magnitude equal to the 1s and 2s on an enemy rally for numbers roll.
                                                    9. Finish a strategic maneuver with one less success.
                                                    10. Excellency discount on a Strategic Maneuver; with sufficient successes, pull several maneuvers at once.
                                                    11. Enact an uncounterable Strategic Maneuver with double-7s.
                                                    12. When your army empties the Magnitude of another army, you can roll Join Battle.
                                                    13. 1/scene, make an automatically successful rally action.
                                                    14. Make a reflexive rally for numbers action, recruiting animals or nature itself to your side.
                                                    15. Permanently, recurring 6s on a War roll.
                                                    16. When activating #12, you can perform a maneuver with successes no higher than half the Initiative gained.
                                                    17. Choose two maneuvers; if the enemy chooses one of them, you automatically win the maneuver roll and can enact a stratagem of equal or fewer successes.

                                                    The image of the lady in white burning through a pile of enemies with, presumably, the final Thrown Charm is pretty sweet.

                                                    Several of these War Charms – League of Iron Preparation (#3), Holistic Battle Understanding (#5) – are really stressing that Ex3 really expects you to regularly apply penalties to rolls.

                                                    Tiger-Warrior Training (#7) has gotten a major buff from 2e – a week and a month will get your soldiers up to about maximum human capacity, rather than having to track them dot by dot. I like it! (I also wish Lore’s training effects were a little more like this.)

                                                    General of the All-Seeing Sun (#10) starts out pretty weak, but I do like the ability to pop several maneuvers – that’s potentially a big effect. Does the discount from Redoubt-Raising Gesture (#9) apply to each of the several?

                                                    Immortal Warlord’s Tactic (#11) has an Initiative cost. Do you have Initiative at the step that you’re rolling Strategic Maneuvers, though? The Ambush Maneuver implies that you aren’t rolling Join Battle until afterwards – so where is this Initiative coming from? Hm - and Transcendent Warlord’s Genius (#16) implies you can pull maneuvers mid-fight. So how does this work?

                                                    On balance, most of the War Charms look solid. Even the dice-trick-y ones are potent dice tricks – recurring 6s isn’t much on its own, but as a permanent upgrade it’s pretty nice. But it feels like the whole thing runs out of steam a little too early, compared to all the other Abilities – I’d like to see a little more at the upper end.

                                                    Yes: Tiger Warrior Training Technique!
                                                    No: Nothing here quite gives me that Solar Admiral Thrawn feel. This is a good start, but gimme moar!
                                                    Just Plain Weird: Immortal Commander’s Presence (#2) is very artillery-centric. Does the book present artillery rules ore weapons at some later point? As of where I am, I’d be purely in houserules territory for how useful this is.

                                                    And that is, at long last, Charms. Up next… *sigh*… “Martial Arts and Sorcery.”

                                                    … more Charms.
