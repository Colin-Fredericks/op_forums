                I think it depends as well on where the game is set. If you're in, say, Jiara or Cherak or the Lap, there's a Realm garrison right there, and even if the locals aren't Immaculate believers they've heard a lot about how awful the Anathema are (plus older traditions from the Shogunate, plus the fact that everyone knows that barbarian armies led by face-eating shapeshifters do, indeed, sometimes attack satrapies).

                On the other hand, if you're in Skullstone, or Volivat, or Iscomay, attitudes may be quite different. Not "Hooray, a Solar, let us bow down before them!" But not "Aaargh a demon, we must kill it or die trying!"

                So the game I'm playing is set in Sukhmet, an Egypt-expy in the Summer Mountains. I am playing a Dragonblood who believes himself to be, like all Dragonblood in Sukhmet, a descendant of the River God, Sukhmet. Hence his plant-, earth- and water-based powers.

                Then there's Prince Meek, the Chosen of Sukhmet. Obviously, he must be an Exigent, because it's well known that he is Chosen by Sukhmet. Hence his great toughness, mighty strength, ability to heal the sick, and the fact that the sacred crocodiles like him (and didn't eat him). Sun aura? Well, he hasn't shown it much, but that's probably due to his connection with the sun god Ra (who is connected to the River God Sukhmet, as Gods are, they syncretise).

                There's also his foreign witch-wife, who may or may not shapechange. She's got in trouble several times with the government, because everyone suspects of being, you know, a foreign witch. She does, after all, have red hair. Some say she's the Chosen of Set. I've recently learned that she can, in fact, shapechange. So she is indeed a Set-witch. But Set, while tricksome, does sometimes work with the river Gods, using his knowledge of evil to help fight evil, and it makes sense that the Chosen of Sukhmet is married to the Chosen of Set. We just have to watch her to make sure she doesn't go too far.

                Then there's Akhenaten, a mysterious and stealthy noble... my old character thought he might be a shapeshifting witch. Very dodgy. Trying to cover up the Pharoah's kidnapping. Might be some kind of demon, like those Anathema I heard about on my travels... but that seems unlikely. They apparently have an evil mark that appears on their head, haven't seen that.

                Then we've got Penitent Gift, an Immaculate Monk who was in the Wyld Hunt, but got sick of the killing, had a crisis of faith, and has decided to actually hang out with some Solars, Lunars, etc, and see if they're as bad as he was always told. So far his conclusions have been... mixed.


                Anyway. The point is that this is an isolated part of the world, far from Prasad's Wyld Hunt (although the monks of the kingdom next door got very upset with my previous character and his anathema-ness, but what could they do?) so it works okay.
                Obviously it's quite different in Realm Satrapies.

You might want a lot more places that are like that if you're going to sell the setting as a place where groups can just freely mix whenever, and tone down the Dragonblooded Wyld Hunts fervor a bit.

                    In 3e as it is now the Wyld Hunt is a globally recognized Dragonblooded institution that supersedes the boundaries of politics and law. It's unites Dragonblooded of the Realm, Lookshy, and Outcaste groups worldwide, so that when a Hunt is called all nearby terrestrials put aside their differences and answer. Not literally 100% of the time, that's boring, but it's a pretty big deal either way.

                    It's also why the Silver Pact prioritizes going after newly Exalted Lunars so much. If the Wyld Hunt didn't have the reach it did most Lunars who Exalt would just be safe where they are.


                    It's not just one way either. From one side, if you're going to flatten the power curve, you should make the setting more agnostic towards what kind of Exalt you are, and from the flip side if you're going to make the setting more agnostic to what kind of Exalt you are you'd better flatten the power curve.

                    If you did that I would recharacterize the Realm/Lookshy/Outcastes in the setting. I'd put more of the focus on Threshold kingdoms that have nothing to do the Immaculate Faith whatsoever, and instead of the Realm being a global superpower casting its shadow long out into the edges of Creation, I'd have it be a more a smaller self-contained nation that has the Immaculate Order as one of its local setting quirks. Like have it be "This nation, that calls it self the Realm, has a state religion that demonizes all non-dragonblooded Exalted. Most Celestial Exalted avoid it for this reason. Now, onto Vaneha in the East..."
