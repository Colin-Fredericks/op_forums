              It probably wouldn't involve any Yozis, since the Major Arcana and the four/five suits are supposed to represent conceptual elements (at least, when they're used as fortune telling tools, rather than playing cards, as the Tarot were originally designed to be).

              One option would be to base the Major Arcana on the Constellations- sufficiently obscure and mystical to make good fortune telling cards, but not so obscure that people can't readily divine their meaning. However, the other option- similar to the real world Tarot- would be to base it on mythical archetypes and concepts that are important to Creation (and more specifically the Realm, since the Tarot seems like something that would appear in the post-Usurpation world as a pro-Bronze propaganda tool). So it might look something like this:

              The Five Elements: Fire, Water, Air, Earth, Wood. May be symbolized by things representing those elements, like Candles, Horns, Cups, Swords and Blossoms, or the Five Maidens, i.e. the Five of Battles, the Empress of Endings, and so on. And yes, the Empress would be the highest ranked card, at least in the Scarlet Dynasty.

              The Major Arcana:

              The Journeyman- Depicted as a young man in yellow clothes, walking down a plain, dusty road. Representing Mela and Mercury, the beginning of a new Journey. Can be Inverted to represent foolishness, pride or willful abandonment. Reigns over the Savant, the Monk, the Hunter and the Mourner.
              The Savant- Depicted as an older woman in scholarly robes, carrying a tome and holding a torch. Represents knowledge and understanding, particularly that of academic or secular matters, and indicates that success lies in research or investigation. Can be Inverted to represent ignorance or misguided judgment, or an unwitting falsehood laid bare.
              The Monk- Depicted as an older man in meditation, a lotus blossom situated in his lap. Represents enlightenment and spiritual understanding, or the need for patience and contemplation. When Inverted, represents false piety or hypocrisy, or the presence of a corrupting influence.
              The Hunter- Depicted as a man dressed in leather and fur, wielding a bow pointed towards the moon. Represents clarity and direction of purpose, and indicates a correct course of action. When Inverted, represents a literal or metaphorical straying from the path, or a lack of confidence in one's actions.
              The Mourner- Depicted as a woman in funerary garb, weeping over an empty, unmarked grave. Represents the need for a period of rest or mourning, or the shedding of old habits and practices. When Inverted, represents an inability to move on, or an unhealthy adherence to tradition.

              The Celebrant- Depicted as a beautiful, smiling woman in a regal blue dress, dancing through a field of poppy flowers. Representing Sextes Jylis and Venus, and predicts a joyful and prosperous future. Can be Inverted to represent self-inflicted pain, or a distraction from more pressing affairs. Reigns over the Ascetic, the Musician, the Star-Gazer and the Mortician.
              The Ascetic- Depicted as an elderly man in a dark cave, rail-thin but refusing to eat a plate of food in front of him. Represents moderation and temperance, and the need to abstain from worldly pleasures to fulfill a spiritual need. When Inverted, represents mindless self-indulgence, or the need to reconnect with one's physical needs.
              The Musician- Depicted as a woman in a plain outfit, stained with dust and playing a sanxian, with a blindfold over her eyes and a moon overhead. Represents a dedication to, and enjoyment of, art and music, as well as the struggle for self-improvement. When Inverted, represents delusion or a need to change one's career.
              The Star-Gazer- Depicted as a child staring at the night sky, with the stars plainly visible and shadows flickering around them. Represents finding hope in despair, and the possibility for success in the face of powerlessness. When Inverted, represents immaturity or the squandering of power and resources.
              The Mortician- Depicted as a young, pale man placing a white rose in the mouth of a corpse. Represents acceptance and beauty amidst ugliness, or the diamond in the rough. When Inverted, represents denial, or an unhealthy obsession.

              The Victor- Depicted as a battle-scarred woman in blood-red armor, standing atop a pile of treasure and skulls. Representing Hesiesh and Mars, and predicts victory through personal achievement. When Inverted, represents failure or incompetence, or a lack of preparation for a coming battle. Reigns over the Mercenary, the Prince, the Shaman and the Shogun.
              The Mercenary- Depicted as a middle-aged, balding man in rusted armor with a gilded sword in one hand and a ruby-encrusted goblet in the other. Represents a business opportunity or reward for hard work, as well as the need for good professional conduct. When Inverted, represents craven opportunism, or a betrayal for economic gain.
              The Prince- Depicted as a young man, with a crown too large for his head and an unwieldy scepter resting in his lap. Represents serendipity, or an unforeseen windfall, but can also represent the need to invest more time or resources in a project. When Inverted, represents greed or miserliness, as well as the impetus to hoard wealth or cut corners.
              The Shaman- Depicted as a wild woman in a feathered mask, wearing leather and pouring a poultice over an open wound. Represents the need for improvisation, or a sudden crucial realization at an inopportune time. When Inverted, represents fear of the unknown, or an unwillingness to experiment or try new things.
              The Shogun- Depicted as a woman in ornate armor with intense eyes, surrounded by a wreath of lilacs and with a map laid before her. Represents the need for strategy and planning, or getting a promotion to a position of leadership. When Inverted, represents incompetent leadership or a lack of planning, or the interference of micromanagement.

              The Sorcerer- Depicted as a man in green robes, with a mystical tone in his hand and standing in a mystical circle beneath the moon. Represents Danaa'd and Jupiter, and predicts the revelation of a secret or the discovery of valuable knowledge. When Inverted, represents a truth that should not be known, or a deceit by a close friend. Reigns over the Bard, the Wyld Dancer, the Vigilant, and the Spectre.
              The Bard- Depicted as a middle-aged woman with greying hair, a large satchel and a feather-adorned hat, with a bonfire back-lighting her. Represents an agent of hope or inspiration, a muse that will bring new insight to an old problem. When Inverted, represents a charlatan or crook, or an economic or spiritual harm that comes disguised as a savior.
              The Wyld Dancer- Depicted as a fey woman decorated by flowers, with a crown of thorns on her skull and crystals beneath her feet. Represents mystery and the joy of discovery, and the pursuit of knowledge in and of itself. When Inverted, represents forbidden pursuits and contracts with dark powers.
              The Vigilant- Depicted as a man in a guard uniform, standing outside a stone tower with a spear and torch in his hands. Represents the need for caution against a coming threat, or an inevitable disaster yet unseen. When Inverted, represents a cataclysm that will strike at the most inopportune time possible, or a crucial vulnerability in one's personal life.
              The Spectre- Depicted as a featureless being emerging from a door that has been left ajar. Represents ignorance and secrets that cannot be known, and the burden of knowing something that should be forgotten. When Inverted, represents a blockade against crucial information, or the influence of dark and otherworldly powers.

              The Gravedigger- Depicted as a woman with a shovel, placing dirt into a grave with a living man within it. Represents Saturn and Pasiap, and represents the need to cut one's losses and move on from a current pursuit. When Inverted, represents a thing enduring beyond when it should have ended. Rules over the Pariah, the Parade, the Pyre and the Elder.
              The Pariah- Depicted as a man on a horse traveling through a desert, sun against his back and his head bowed. Represents the loss of a home, or the premature end of a pursuit or life. When Inverted, represents exile or violent death.
              The Parade- Depicted as five hooded figures playing figures and dancing down a road of bones. Represents acceptance or the death of a tyrant. When Inverted, represents mob rule or mass chaos come to fruition.
              The Pyre- Depicted as a man on a stake burning to death. Represents a necessary sacrifice, or the surrender of a spiritual hindrance. When Inverted, represents a scapegoat, or a solution that will cause more harm that good.
              The Elder- Depicted as an elderly woman with a cane and one open eye. Represents wisdom garnered through experience, and the need for advice from a trusted friend. When Inverted, represents false wisdom, or advice that fail to provide insight or truth.

                      It all depends on how one looks at the possibilities - somewhat popular game that doubles as a easily acessible divination tool? Sounds more like the kind of social sabotage/"free wisdom for all" monkeywrench Lunars and some secret societies are prone to than a element of Bronze Faction manipulation/propaganda.

                      That said, the Tarot's uses could go far beyond "mere" divination, as a previous comparison to the archetype plates for the summoning of demons & elementals shows and could actually hide a number of codified layers of meaning, knowledge or esoteric enlightnement and in fact there is at least one popular, real life example of it being used in exactly such a way.

                      What starts as cards for divination to the initiate becomes a map to one's personal trials of esoteric enlightement of the journeyman and the fractal library of sorcerous permutations for spells in the pocket of the master.

                      If, for example, the card/plate images hid sorcerous knowledge, The Tower arcana could reference either The Princes of the Fallen Tower, The Violent Opening of Closed Portals spell or both of them even, much in the same way as The Chariot might bring its wise reader an awareness of Conjuring the Azure Chariot, Cloud Trapeze or Chariot of the Blazing Sun, among other sample associations/parallels.

                      If letters from dead languages can serve as wards or secret codes for occult wisdom, objects like cards have room for a exponentially greater variety of codified secrets to be hidden, combined or experimented with.

                      Not to mention one's books/card decks are as rich and informative as its writer/crafter/translator. Different decks may have subtle (or not so subtle differences) in symbolism depending on its crafter/publisher particular competences, knowledge or objectives, resulting in cards of varying quality, value and potential benefits - or risks.

                                  Different sets, different functions, so to speak, one set of cards serving as equipment/resources for divination rites, another as summoning plates focused in entreating the varied demonspawn of the many souls of a certain 3rd circle demon or the courts of certain lesser elemental dragons and a third set is only good for cheating at gambling and otherwise useless, except this same quality can make it into an ideal piece to get the attention of Plentimon, god of gambling, in the hands of an appropriately skilled magician-card shark, meanwhile in Harborhead or some other place in the South, a group of disgruntled priests crafts a fourth set of cards themed to beckoning and spreading the lore of Ahlat and its varied spirit servants across the land under the guise of a game.

                                  Meanwhile origins, meanings and functions are lost or usurped for new and different purposes, as the cards change hands between people of differing backgrounds, degrees of education, occult skill or interests.

                                  - An old soldier and gambler remakes one's incomplete set in what would later be a new popular game without seeing the invisible hand of the Salinan Working that partly guides the shape of these rules, unknowingly highlighting the mangled lore of the cards that remain through it.

                                  - A businessman makes a deal with a patrician scion of House Cynis for publication of a "erotic deck" inspired in the art of a foreign edition of a scandalous classic book, neither part aware of how that same art secretly codifies the demon-summoning ways of its long-dead salmalin artist.

                                  - A young immaculate monk with an interest in hidden codes in religious literature accidentally catches on to the esoteric patterns in the innocent pastime of villagers close to its monastery and starts a personal quest to collect a "perfect deck" and find out what mysterious lore of the Immaculate Dragons or Anathema do they hide, while distractedly leaving its notes and drawings open for a colleague to peruse, leading to a series of personal experiments in the disciple's part and the making of a set of geomancy-related cards, a pair of spirit-beckoning Heaven Thunder Leaves fans (Oadenol's codex, pg. 31) and peculiar terrestrial martial art based in predicting the adversary's attacks according to a pattern of move sets and combinations.

                                  - A scavenger lord finds a little package of cards in the pocket of a long-dead warrior in dragon armor and much to its surprise recognize a few, very few, images as familiar but soon finds out that the deck could never work with the rules it does know and so keeps it home as a little curio, until one's son decides to show off with his "lost deck" to some friends and gets knifed for this folly among the criminal, to awake in the following under the care of a priestess "Plentimon dislikes those who cheat at the table, so you may take this back." she says while returning the young man his parent's lost cards, along with a little scroll. "But neither are they yours. Return them to their owner and play them together, now that you are given their rules. Learn them well and pass the game along and Plentimon will be satisfied." and so the boy did, even though he would later learn there was no silver-haired priestess of Plentimon in his city.

                                  And so it goes... Not to mention how age, the passage of time and the rise & fall of societies secret or not and civilizations can add further complications to the terrain - the entertaining game of a period becomes the educational tool of master sorcerers obsessed with making the path of initiation acessible to all, this dual role serving to pass some of its rules or imagery, if not both, from parent to child, cardshark to gambler, court magician to apprentice and then to lover turned circus oracle and from there spreading to village wisemen and women everywhere.

                                  It's all in what you want to explore in your game and how.

                                          We have 25 astrological colleges and 23 Yozis in relation to 22 Arcana, either of those things should provide a decent enough tarot-surrogate. The colleges in particular, with their descriptions, associations and ascending & descending aspects, make great fodder for major arcana equivalents.

                                          On the matter of a "Yozi deck" i would suggest the Abyss/Oblivion instead of the Neverborn, as it is the doom beyond doom at least some of the monolithic corpse-titans strive for.

                                          Also, maybe it's just me, but i don't really see it going back to the age of Primordial rule - they were so distant of humanity and most mortal races it was half the point in making them the proxy of the gods in their rebellion, not to mention the ultimate "distant and scary thing bigger than us" of that savage age, making their representation in such leisurely pursuits even less probable imho. Other, greater, races might feel different from humans in those regards, but i don't really see the DKs with their "aztecs meets romans" refined savagery or ever workaholic jadeborn showing much interest in cardgames, though the Lintha are certainly a possibility.

                                          I find it easier to imagine an "Yozi tarot" taking form in the following centuries & millenia, in much the same way demon-worshipping cults would insert tools of their trade and tokens of their devotion in literary works of the First Age or Shogunate such as the Lover Clad in Blue.

                                          But then, going in a contrary path, what's there to say the "first tarot" wasn't a sort of game of chance and storytelling shaped in the long lost time of the Titans, as a tool of subversion and infiltration by the earliest among the reavers and invaders that spring of the 4 Graces, the Raksha and their manifold creations & spawn, a weapon of art, chance and unfolding narratives older than the Primordial War itself?
