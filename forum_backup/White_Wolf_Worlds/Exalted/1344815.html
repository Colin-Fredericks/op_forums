    Sandact's Magical Real Estate (Manses)

    10-30-2019, 11:24 PM
    You know what’s cool? Badass magical bases. Do you know what’s not cool? A delayed book on them.

    Fuck that.

    I’ll go ahead my own manses! With blackjack! And Hookers! In fact, forget the manses!

    What was I talking about? Oh right, manses.

    Let’s go ahead and use this topic as a collect of manses I’m typing out in my spare time. You have an idea for a manse? Go ahead and spill it out here. I’ll add it to my manse list and type it up.

    TABLE OF CONTENTS

    Lesser Manses

    Greater Manses

    FAQ

    Q: Why are there no hearthstones listed?
    A: I’m making manses, not hearthstones. Hearthstones will be added to manses if I feel there is a particular fit for them or some manses mentioning they do not possess a hearthstone at all. Unless a manse doesn’t have a hearthstone, feel free to put whatever you want there.

    Q: What process do you use to make these?
    New Answer: This.

    Old Answer: Remember my spaceship homebrew from Heaven’s Reach and the artifact ships I made for them? The manses are very similar to that. Mundane facilities are upgraded with magical enhancements to make them places that are truly impressive. You envy those who have them and covet them when you do posses them.

    That said I don’t have “mundane” facilities statted out in any place or form (Unless you want to use my mundane spaceship facilities as a guideline). In this case I’d use what would narratively make sense. The “Eye-In-The-Storm” Sorcerous Lab may have safety features built into it as a magical effect, but it’s mundane effect should be something akin to a well-stocked sorcerers lab that possesses most of the commonly used reagents for your type of sorcery and possibly a two die bonus for using exceptional equipment in matters related to sorcery.

    In terms of how many special traits I give each manse? Depends on the manse really. I'd say lesser ones would have around 3-4 special features while major manses have 4-6. These features are powerful, but unlike other artifacts manses cannot be taken with you (usually).

      Cloud’s Head Laboratory (Lesser Air Manse)

      After the Usurpation the Blessed Isle remained littered with the works of the Solar Anathema. Both powerful artifacts and sorcerous workings only comprehensible to their maddened souls laid bare and in the open. Natural disasters and abominations created by the Solars plagued the Blessed Isle and Threshold, while the fear that a single child could accidentally awaken a war machine of incredible power was an omnipresent fear that languished on the minds of the new Dragonblooded Shogunate.

      The Cloud’s Head Laboratory was formally issued for construction by the Third Shogun even when countless contested his rule. His logic was that if anyone, not just the Shogunate, would survive in this new age then they would have to rediscover the lost secrets of sorcery. It was constructed in an idyllic pasture in what is now known as modern-day Incas Prefecture. The manse was floated into the clouds itself, letting the endless fields of green and ice-capped mountains to ease the worries that arose from the burden of the researcher’s tasks or how any day a rival army being seen on the horizon. This floating manse also provided reassurance that any ‘mishaps’ would happen far away from any major population centres. If the worse were to happen then a massive fall and being buried under tons of rubble is an effective countermeasure to most things.

      House Ledaal is the current owner after centuries of changing hands and has made a considerable name for itself. Who could forget how it’s researchers discovered the Missing Forbidden note of the song to soothe the keystones in the Halcyon Valley or designing a new elemental that extracts salt from water to use when the forces of Thorns and House Mnemon first clashed. Students from the Heptagram dream of being assigned a term as a researcher in such a manse, only for them to be nearly crushed under the responsibility of finding safe solutions to incredibly complex problems that involve the safety of entire legions.

      The last head of the Laboratory, Ledaal Orleans, gave her life to smuggle out necromantic artifacts from Thorns. Orleans had only one surviving heir, a bookworm named Ledaal Joan who had panic attacks on what to have for breakfast much less dangerous sorcerous experiments. House Ledaal debated what to do with the manse as Joan was clearly not ready to be head of the laboratory, but the answer came to them in an outcaste called the Depths of Wisdom. Wisdom not only demonstrated an immense personal knowledge of the Undead but also provided them with many additional items of necromantic origin. House Ledaal desperately wanted to put his knowledge to use, so they quickly married him off to Joan and made him second in command.

      This has not been without significant backlash from the researchers. It is not alone they are unhappy for an outcaste to immediately leap ahead decades of hard work, oh no, but also how he runs the place. The current owner of the manse is Joan but merely serves as a rubber stamp for Wisdom. The fact that Joan remains holed up in her study reading trashy romance novels disgusts many, especially with how outgoing her mother was, but how she is a craven coward. She cannot even hold a beaker of explosive material without shaking like a leaf. Wisdom is well aware of the dislike for both himself and his wife, but he doesn’t care. Let them hate him, time bickering on politics is better spent on research.

      The Laboratory itself is a large blue pagoda that grows out of the clouds themselves with various smaller pagoda towers whose windmills are constantly churning. Each of these towers serves as research stations while the central hub houses the other areas such as Administration, housing, mess hall, and main archives. Below the manse itself lies the town “Foot Under the Clouds”, which serves to support the manse itself with food, supplies, and other necessities. In exchange for this arrangement, Ledaal maintains a vigilant guard in the town and surrounding territory.

      Facilities

      “Calming Winds” Residential Area: Subtle architecture from softer colours to special recreation spots such as the open-aired garden lessen the headaches one might incur attempting to decipher ancient mysteries. This feature of the manses lowers the effects of anger or stress by one stage. Attempting to use an Inspire action to inflict such emotions only has while any intimacies of anger are lowered by one stage. Minor intimacies related to anger/stress give +1 Resolve when used to boost resolve and reduce no resolve when using against someone.

      “Eye-In-The-Storm” Sorcerous Lab: In addition to a well-stocked sorcerous lab that contains many of the ingredient’s sorcerers find useful for their craft, but potent safety mechanisms. If any sorcerous spell or sorcerous artifact attempts to backfire within the manse the effect is harmless drained out by the walls of the manse itself and safely dispelled throughout the dragonlines. Any sorcerous workings that have dangerous or hazardous side effects are temporarily muted while inside the manse but will return upon leaving the manse.

      “Skyward Road” Transport: The Cloud’s Head Laboratory floats upon the clouds themselves, which while excellent for safety and secrecy is rather poor for moving people and supplies to it. Installed in the centre of the manse itself is an elevator system that works using the windmills stationed on the research towers. This elevator shaft is a wide platform capable of lifting an entire yeddium and its cargo without issue. The ropes have been sorcerously treated so that they will not sway in the wind.

      “Whispering Winds” Archives: The Whisper Winds library is a vast library that takes up multiple floors in the central pagoda. This library kept comfortably lit at all times by glass tubes that arc lightning through the ceiling, contains a wealth of information regarding all manners of sorcery. Anyone who uses the library is treated as having a specialty in sorcery for any ability they roll for, be it Lore for fact rolls or Medicine for treating injuries caused by sorcery. Studying for at least a full day allows the character to declare they have an appropriate specialty related to the task at hand so long as it relates to sorcery, once per season. Knowledge of rare heretical or Anathema magic is kept under a close eye, and only with permission from the Head Researcher is one allowed to take such books out of the library. Ledaal Joan is an ever-present sight in such a place, and if one can get past the several bookcases of romance novels she surrounds herself with, she is the perfect person to ask as Wisdom is usually too busy.

          The House of Many Faces (Lesser Lunar Manse)

          Back in the infant days of Nexus when Merchant Princes were flocking to the area and the rivers still clean, the Changing Moon known as Laughter in the Night was one of the first Lunars to take an interest in the area, seeing the potential for having a strong foothold in the area. He was regarded as a queer fellow by many other Lunars at the time, seeing the experienced Lunar making a manse when the threshold was still recovering after being shattered by two apocalyptic events and the fledgling Scarlet Empire. Why Laughter in the Night take such a gamble when there were much bigger events to tackle? But Laughter in the Night was willing to take a long term bet.

          The House of Many Faces was forged by weaving a lie throughout the entire city of Nexus. The rich in the Bastion district would know it as the house with sublime galas, while the gangs in the Firewander district knew it as the Cackling Ravens gang. Laughter aided several Raksha into the city itself, who returned the favor by using their magics instill the impossibility of all such rumors existing at once but also not at all.

          As Nexus slowly became the economic powerhouse it is today it was clear that Laughter’s own bet paid off. The manse continued to serve as one of the main safehouses for the Silver Pact in Nexus, allowing them the stealth and mobility to conduct any plan they pleased outside of the night-omniscient view of the Sidereals and away from the hostility of the Realm.

          After Laughter in the Night’s tragic passing, howling like a fool as he detonated a key Realm manse, had requested for The House of Many Face’s to be passed onto his successor, Nine Veiled Tales. The decision of a Shahan-Ya to actually enforce this has angered many of the other Lunars, especially ones who have served alongside Laughter in the Night for their entire lives. Many Lunars are eager to see if she lives to to her predecessor, just as many are plotting to take the manse for themselves.

          Facilities

          “Buried Secrets” Storage: Hidden compartments are such finicky things to use. With all the effort needed to maintain them, why not just simply say such things never existed in the first place? Whenever an item is placed inside of a closed space and is not being observed, such as in a cabinet or a room, the manse owner can make a command to store that individual item(s) in Elsewhere. While in Elsewhere the items cannot be interacted with normally and require special charms to do so. The house can store an amount of material equal to the volume of another house. It is possible to completely change the layout and contents of an entire room by opening and closing the door. Living and/or sentient beings cannot be placed in Elsewhere.

          “Perfect Fit” Co-Location: The house is capable of teleporting to several possible locations around Nexus, one in each district. The decor of the manse changes to match the type of house, but the interior always remains the same size and has the same contents. The hearthstone can control the manse location. Those who dwell within the manse automatically have their clothing and personal belongings changed to meet the new aesthetic of the manse while those who live around it believe it and the people inside have always been there. Moving the manse takes an entire night, shifting to its new location fully at sunup.

          “Silken Glove” Hospitality: The manse reads and casually adapts to the needs and whims of its guests. Either the Hearthstone owner or the manse can make Read Intentions action (the latter gaining 7+[Owner’s Essence] dice) to attempt to figure out the preferences of their guests, after which the manse can make small localized retrofits to suit the whims of their guests. A Guild Factor finds the manse carries their favorite wine while a snitch finds the manse staffed with guards of the mercenary unit he betrayed. This token hospitality or unease can create a minor positive intimacy related to the manse and it’s owner that’s either positive or negative.

          “Winding Roads” Remote Location: Uninvited guests who try and see the manse are forever doomed to wander the back streets of Nexus. Attempting to find the manse requires a difficulty [3 or Owner’s Essence, higher, maximum 5] Perception + Survival roll. Any hearthstone holders who are Resonant with Starmetal or Moonsilver subtract an amount of successes equal to the 1s in the opponent’s roll.

              Tenzan’s Opus (Greater Earth Manse, Banking manse)

              Uran Tenzan’s name is known throughout the Realm as one of the last great engineers of the Shogunate era. Born into a world filled with disease and death, Uran rose to the occasion multiple times by crafting incredible works of artifice, but is most well known for his incredible detail to attention on his manses. Lookshy proclaims his fortress manses hold off barbarian hordes just as well as the endless Raksha hordes centuries ago while those in Harbourhead say that it is only because of his manses that nourished their ancestors in times of drought. If one needed any sort of manse built, then regardless of the manse Uran would be on that list.

              Uran’s world came crashing down upon the appearance of the Empress, his Kingdom set in the sights of the fledgling Scarlet Dynasty. Uran watched as their power and influence grew, and it was only a matter of time when both the ambition of the Scarlet Dynasty finally sought to conquer the Blessed Isle. It was young Ragara himself who sought to convince Tenzan’s warlord to step down, and while he failed to make amends with the Warlord in the area he still considered it a victory when Uran Tenzan agreed to join the Realm. It was unknown what Ragara offered him, but many claimed that with Uran entering his twilight years he simply wanted to live in peace.

              Uran’s last work would take place in the port town of Riven Quay, a pyramid with stones as white as snow and shined with a radiance only befitting of a future great house founder. Inside lay potent defenses and security that ensured that not a single hard earned coin would be stolen but with enough space to handle the wealth of entire nations in the threshold. It was regarded as Uran Tenzan’s magnum opus, stubbornly refusing to die and only passing away days after it’s completion. It was in honor of the late builder that Ragara declared the structure be known as Tenzan’s Opus.

              Ragara himself used the manse as a foothold for his ambitions, only leaving it behind when Ragara was elevated to Great House status. Ragara himself left it in the hands of one of his most trusted children, Ragara Adra. Since then the Adra family has maintained their grasp upon the manse itself, where after centuries of family intrigue and assassinations, the current owner falls upon a Ragara Adra Ulner. Selling themselves as a friendly face, Ulner sells his family’s business upon the promise that one can get their jade immediately and up front rather than a promissory notes. The bank itself handles the expenses of clients from all levels, from the lowly dock worker to the regional house scion who wishes to keep all the treasures of his most recent campaign away from the prying eyes of family or collectors. Unless you’re Anathema, your belongings will always be safe.

              Facilities

              “Canyon’s Depths” Storage: One would have an easier time carrying boulders up a Canyon’s wall than to steal from Tenzan’s Opus. The inside of Tenzan’s Opus is composed of many vaults throughout the manse in which people can place their belongings. With but a word, the hearthstone bearer can shunt an entire vault into Elsewhere, making it nearly impossible to gain illicit access to. Attempting to open the vault without the hearthstone bearer requires either the client themselves opening the vault while speaking a unique passphrase. An Unintended setback A deliberate engineering choice allows those capable of picking magical or sorcerous locks can also open the safes, though the difficulty of doing so is (3 + Heathstone bearer’s Essence).

              “Mountain’s Peak” Barracks: The Adra family takes the security of it’s money very seriously, having an entire branch of the Adra family dedicated to safeguarding the manse and its contents. Anyone who spends at least one week is imbued with the Earth’s blessing, gaining an additional -1 and -2 health level and adding +2 dice to any awareness rolls. These supernatural benefits grant them might 1 when part of a battle group in combat. These benefits vanish when the soldiers spend (Stamina) days outside the manse barracks. The barracks can hold a number of guards equal to a size 3 battle group.

              “Pisap’s Scales” Reinforced Structure: The manse is as durable as Pisap himself. The manse is all but invulnerable to all but the most dedicated of attackers. The manse itself is immune to Terrestrial circle sorcery that seek to damage it, most miscellaneous weapon strikes that result in collateral damage, and the minimum feat of Strength requirement to even break open a hole by force is a STR 10 check requiring 15 successes.

              “The Unseen Spyglass” Scrying Network: A dragon whose treasure was stolen will always find the thief who did it. On the top floor office lies a large map of Riven Quay rendered in sand. The instant anyone attempts to steal something from Tenzan’s Opus will find themselves immediately being tracked by this map. No matter how clever the thief, stolen money will always appear. The range of this map’s feature is (Hearthstone Bearer’s Essence x500, minimum 1000) miles out, though the Adra family brags that virtually no one makes it out of the city. Attempting to hide from this scrying counts as an exotic sense that the thief needs to know about prior to activating stealth charms. Some critics are worried about being tracked by this manse’s feature after taking out their money, but the Adra family reassures people that it’s only tracks things that were stolen.

              This is course is a complete lie; the manse tracks all money of treasure that was held inside of it’s vaults for at least one week and disappears as soon the item is exchanged in a transaction. Only the top members of the Adra family and the head of House Ragara know about this feature. Uler himself uses this information to compile a private ledger book about personal spending in the area. A scion taking out a large shipment of jade and heading to a blacksmith’s guild makes it fairly obvious what the Scion intends to do. The Adra family are philanthropists in the area not out of kindness, but to make the area economically attractive enough that most people will want to do their business in Riven Quay, information which the Adra family can sell or use as blackmail.

              “Weight of Mount Meru” Security System: Those who attempt to steal from the manse will feel the weight of Mt. Meru itself upon their shoulders. Attempting to steal any treasures or belongings from the manse automatically places a mobility penalty upon the thief, with the penalty increasing proportional to the cost of the item. A hastily scribbled will of a peasant leaving his meager house has a -1 penalty, a deed to a seaworthy ship is -3, while a priceless Shogunate treasure would have a -5 penalty. When in doubt, use the item’s Resources value as the penalty. Attempting to carry objects that require a feat of strength score to lift increase their strength prerequisite by one category and increases the difficulty to lift by three. However, all of this only applies to physical theft. Using forged paperwork or obtaining the guards consent to obtain the items bypasses this security feature.

                  Grimfield Farm (Greater Green Jade/Soulsteel Manse)

                  The Underworld is a land tainted by death. From the very soil and water to the dimmed sun, only very unique souls would attempt to call such a place home. Life always seems to find a way no matter how challenging the conditions, and the town of Grimfield is certainly one of the exceptions. Located within a nondescript location in the Underworld Scavenger Lands, it was only recently discovered recently by a passing Merchant Prince, his shock only matching his intrigue upon its discovery.

                  Long ago the residents of Grimfield were one of the many who were fleeing the constant wars of the Shogunate. Desperate to escape their pursuers, the people of a fallen Kingdom escaped into a shadowland and into the Underworld. It was said that their ancestor spirits led them to the demense, praising them for their bravery. Story weavers no longer know whenever it was a Dragonblooded, Anathema, or some other mysterious entity lost to time. The residents of Grimfield are just happy to have a home to call their own, knowing how rare such a place is within the Underworld.

                  Spotting Grimfield in the Underworld is simple. When you see rows of apple trees whose leaves burn with a brilliant red and yellow leaves that give way to large fields of potatoes and house sized pumpkins, welcome to Grimdale. Traders in the area use this opportunity to rest and engage in trade ranging from food to grave goods. All visitors must register and the Central Hollows city hall, a large hollow apple tree in the center that is a temple to the ancestor spirits within the town. The citizens of Grimfield welcome all their living guests, inviting them into their pumpkin homes with warm smiles, good food, and infectious laughter. Just don’t go into the forest at night.

                  Storytellers are encountered to put the town of Grimfield wherever it suits the needs of their game. The hearthstone itself is typically given to the town’s leader, but the town may reward it to a champion who they deem as a worthy protector.
                  Hearthstone

                  “Harvest Season” (Greater Wood/Abyssal Hearthstone): Spotting the brilliant colours of the autumn season, this hearthstone carries the power of the Four Seasons Harvest power of it’s parent manse wherever the bearer may go. Planting this hearthstone in the ground allows an area up to (Bearer’s Essence, half that if not resonant with Soulsteel or Green Jade) miles. Anything planted will grow regardless of environmental conditions, but only for a single harvest season.

                  Facilities

                  “All Hallows Eve” Barracks: Those who serve in Grimfield are forever enshrined by their ancestors, their clothing placed on Scarecrows to forever watch over the fields of corn. But these scarecrows are not meant to deter birds, they’re meant to deter ghosts. When Grimfield is overrun by those that do them harm the residents can pray for their ancestors to protect them in their time of need. The Scarecrows scattered around the town themselves come to life, their breath hot in fury of those who dare harm their residents. These Scarecrows take the higher traits of both the War Ghost and mortal Champion. They are counted as a size 3 battlegroup with a soak of 10, Perfect Morale, gain an additional point of might against any ghost or other undead creature from the Underworld, and missing the Forge-Born Form charm. These creatures will continue to fight until only residents of the town remain. Using this charm is costly, it can only be used once every full moon in which residents need to perform a special festival of worship to give praise to the ancestors once again. Traditionally this has been going to someone’s house in a scary disguise, attempting to scare them. If they succeed then the home owner is supposed to give that person an item of food, usually sweets.

                  “Four Seasons Harvest” Garden: The garden of Grimfield infuses the dead land of the Underworld with life once more, the produce of the land growing large and lush. This manse feature grants resistance to the harsh Underworld environment. Multiple farmers can plant crops and grow them despite dim sunlight, poor soil, or brackish water year round. The size of the land and output of crops allows the area to comfortably fit a population around 1,000 people. Regardless of the season plants will grow with the colours of autumn. Lastly any ghost or necromantic effect (working or spell [I don’t know what necromancy will be like, so let’s use ‘effect’]) has the difficulty to curse or plague the field increase by a difficulty of 5, maximum 8. This power does not come for free, as one ghost per full moon is required to be ritually sacrifice within the Central Halls ancestor temple. It does not need to be an ancestor ghost, and usually it’s a hostile ghost. Living sacrifice are also accepted but not used as produce becomes trickier to use and sell, especially when human teeth replace seeds in pumpkins.

                  “Pumpkin House” Housing: The people of Grimfield normally live within giant pumpkins. As a result of being blessed by the Great Pumpkin of the Underworld, none may trespass within a Grimfielder’s home. Spirits always treat the walls of pumpkin houses as solid, regardless of material stance. Likewise the pumpkin houses automatically repair damage of anything less than being cut in twain. These pumpkin houses do not last forever, eventually rotting away. Therefore it is tradition within Grimfield to start a new harvest season by moving into a new pumpkin house.

                  “Twisted Black Ivy” Defense System: Vines of twisted black ivy can be spotted throughout the town of Grimfield, ever present but harmless. Spirits however find such vines painful to cross, and those with the ability to sense the dematerialized can see large black thorns on each of these vines. Such vines are always able to wrap around dematerialized foes. These vines force any would be attacker to treat the terrain in the town as difficult terrain. Spirits attempting to move across such barbed vines are exposed to an environmental hazard of 3i/turn (Lethal in crash or when dematerialized), trauma 3 (6 when dematerialized).
