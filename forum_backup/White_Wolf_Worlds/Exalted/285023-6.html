                            Well, I don't know what ya'll are talking about - in RPGs, I kill people who are only ambiguously evil all the time. I mess around with governments, too.

                            Who here remembers Loghain, from Dragon Age: Origins? The guy betrays you during the prologue of the game by leaving the Grey Wardens to die along with the King, leaving the nation of Ferelden unable to mount a good defense against the Blight because the nobles who followed the King are split on following Loghain. At first, it's easy to assume that Loghain is just a bastard - his allies are mostly jerks (especially if you're playing a Human Noble, in which case his chief lieutenant killed your family), but on inspection it becomes apparent that he did what he did because he genuinely thought that the Grey Wardens were not that important to stopping the Blight (incorrect, but it's a mistake anyone who wasn't a Warden could make), and because he thought the King would sell out Ferelden to the neighboring country of Orlais, which Loghain had led a rebellion against decades earlier.

                            Eventually, you can confront him, and kill him in a duel. I chose to kill him, not because I really believed he was a bad person and deserved to die for the good of everyone else, but because by the time I got to Loghain I had already killed a lot of people for much less serious crimes than Loghain's (Usually, for the crimes of attacking a Grey Warden, or banditry), and I wanted my ethics to be consistent.

                            Still made for a decent boss fight, and one that gave me something to reflect on afterward.

                            Then I set up a buddy of mine with Loghain's daughter/the Queen to the late King, so there would be a clear succession of leadership - somebody had to take charge, and it couldn't be me. My buddy wasn't much of a leader-type but he was likeable, and did have royal blood, while the Queen was a good administrator without enough popular support. I announced this spontaneously-arranged marriage without consulting either of them. Was that unambiguously good? No, probably not, but it was the best I could do at the time.

                            When my actions have consequences beyond "And then there was peace, in the absence of violence and tyranny", I tend to remember them better, and it's still fun for me.

                            On the frontier of the Wild South, there's only one woman with the grit to take on its most dangerous outlaws and bring them Back Alive, or Maybe Dead.

                                                    Originally posted by Ranger_Lord View Post

                                                    Okay, example;

                                                    Empires improve territory they conquer only so they can more efficiently extract resources from those areas. This is a common trope with a lot of historical background:

                                                    ...

                                                    The Realm, as portrayed by some writers, is actually as benevolent as it's propaganda would lead you to believe. This is presented as a clever, nuanced subversion of the evil empire trope, for sure, but it's unhelpful when your primary PC splat is considered Anathema to the Realm. It's not fun and is in fact quite frustrating if stopping the Empire that is ruthlessly hunting you down and obstructing the Mandate of Heaven means ruining everything for everyone when you finally win. The entire story of new Solar kingdoms rising, forming alliances and marching on the Blessed Isle to restore Celestial Law is ruined if the Dynasts are actually the good guys.
                                                    This is seeming to get into a couple weird issues. First, assuming that the Realm itself has to be good in order for people in it to be good (this is somewhat what the imperial apologists in other threads tend to do). Second, it assumes that it's bad that you as Solars don't inherently have some legitimacy to rulership by you not being the Realm. This second point kind of misses wha thas been talked about at length by devs in the idea that Solars are somewhat a subversion of the Destined Returned Hero. The White Rose coming back doens't mean shit if she can't acutally run things when she gets the job done.

                                                    Originally posted by Ranger_Lord View Post
                                                    This trope was subverted in parts 2e to show how "the truth defies simplicity" which is all well and good, but if it invalidates the major themes of Solar campaigns then that added complexity comes at the expense of playability.
                                                    This is an example of I think cherry-picking a bit. "The Realm is actually good" is often kind of argued as actually being in itself a simplification, as having a PC splat serve a full blown evil insitution would be bad times, afterall. So authors tried to rehabilitate the institution instead of let PCs have moral conflicts. You cite an example of attempts for nuance, and I think a lot of the detractors of the course see it as actualy being overly simplistic.

                                                    It also is presenting a false dichotomy that playability is contrasted with nuance. Nuance to me adds story options and interesting consequences. If it's a forgone conclusion that I'm in the right, than there's not really a challenge to be had. The Realm doesn't need to be Evil or Villainous, but it is. And it being either of those doesn't mean it isn't simple, it's just that authors in 2e thought complexity was brought by showing how good the Realm is inherently, rather than the actuality of empires having "good" as peripheral side effect to things.

                                                    The Life of Brian scene where "What have the Romans ever done for us?" is interesting in that despite all the things the Romans gave them, and there being many Jews at hte time who sided with it, there was still a big ass rebellion seventy years later, and just over a century and a half something so big the Romans went on full blown attempt ot genocide to make sure it didn't happen again. This is all while still building roads, aquaducts and police order. Similarly, as found in the novel Thints Fall Apart, murder over tribal grievances, letting twins to die via exposrue, or treating outcastes as people was something the British colonial government nad missionaries helped stop. At the price of destroying local customs, putting strain on religious beliefs in a region for centuries, and punishing people for crimes they did not understand or did not know could exist.

                                                    Defeating the Realm shouldn't be easy not becuase the Realm is actually good and that makes your PCs feel bad. it shouldn't be easy because destroying local power structures isn't easy. The 2e writers just kind of fucked-up on doing that as it wasn't seeking naunce, but simplicity of "Good versus bad". It wasn't thinking consequences, which is what I think good presentation of political upheaval should focus on.

                                                    Originally posted by Ranger_Lord View Post
                                                    We were talking about Lunar Elders before this thread got derailed, yes? I will continue my defense if my STing style and portrayal of Raksi in the campaign I ran years ago from the Forum's criticism if you wish (I can do this for days), but you can go back to talking about Lunars if you want; I won't think less of you or anything.
                                                    Lunar Elders in 2e generlaly sucked. The issue folks have is not that you depicted Raksi that way. That's for your game. The issue si that for a lot of how Exalted is vaunted, 2e's portrayal of her was lazy, a tad stupid, and to top it off a bit on the creeper side in parts (which bled into her depiction in the OP's depiction I would note).

                                                    Raksi in 1e, as a note, was a tad more subtle. She had influence on teh Ten Tribes for reasons we didn't know, and she seemed to be while a monsterous cannibal, much more in control of things than the 2e depiciton had. She wasn't some teen wtih daddy issues over millennia (for one she was never implied as to being a preteen when she Exalted). She was depicted giving a meeting for her troops and a patron to younger Lunars willing to put up with her shit. She's originally sort of a regional power, something you deal with to get power, and who if she died might actually cause issues, like that Thousand Fangs Army not having a head anymore.

                                                    It was 2e's depiction that reduced her to a insular brat in a city of apemen with no hinted political savvy and who had no real even reason to have the Ten Tribes invovlement. 2e took what was potentially a neat wide-ranging mentor and power player and made her into something out of Saturday morning cartoons.
