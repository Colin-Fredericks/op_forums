    All right, finally a break from mechanics, and with some truly new setting information. Bunch of Dominions, ruled over by shapeshifters no less; getting’ all Deep Space Nine up in here. Looking forward to this on several fronts.

    First off, love this running theme of depicting the Exalted in an abstracted manner with something inspired by Mesoamerican art.

    Okay, starts off with a little introduction to summarise the concept. The notion that a major thing driving support for the Lunars are people exploited by the satrapies for the sake of meeting their tribute obligations is something that I’ve been expecting for a while, and putting that right up front helps to root the conflict in the excesses of the Realm (especially for any younger Lunars less invested in the legacy of the Usurpation or the Shogunate). Hmm, as an Irish person, those references to the kinds of people who might range out from their own lands for the sake of conquest; it’s bad enough when that stuff is a matter of an official policy with some kind of supporting infrastructure, but when it’s about letting losers and opportunists go out planting flags, I doubt it’s never not been a human disaster, establishing a frontier culture of extreme hostility that future policy can only ever build upon and consolidate. The other stuff about the disease and the capital ventures, all makes sense as well, although it resonates less with me personally.

    That being said, I also think it’s good how there are references to the picture being more complicated than universally generating opposition, or even fear; that there would be bonds created in such orbiting societies of greed and faith, even admiration. After all, the Dynasts are still Exalted, and between a potential human tendency to become invested in such impressive figures and their own powers to capitalise on that, it would make sense that they could become connected like that. And it creates groundwork for making the work of the Lunars more complicated, adding a bit more context to why they need to undertake it so gradually. It also creates some interesting scenarios for those social Attribute Charms to play off of.

    Now, while I have no objection to the corebook depiction of Dominions, I would say it paints them as a little bit uniform, and maybe not painted in the best light for people that have reservations about this depiction of the Silver Pact. More to the point, there was often preliminary writer statement about part of why they’re able to fight the Realm so effectively is a diverse array of strategies, so it should really help people to point out several of the basic strategic formats that one might take. I’ve got to admit, much as I like the asymmetrical war of attrition aspect of their war, I’m most intrigued by that picture of some Dominions representing a military build-up from a distance to have armies in the future to engage in actual conquest against the Realm, perhaps partially because of the challenges such activity represents for Lunars.

    Last of all, a sidebar connecting the concept of Charm-created territories to the subject. Or… rather, summarise it a bit, with a point on how the dominion write-ups will reference territory qualities that they might include.

    So yeah, it’s a good introduction. I think it effectively lays out the overall background and stakes of the conflict, and gives it a lot of moving parts to play off of, suggesting variety in stories and scenarios. Once we have The Realm, it should be complemented very nicely by the descriptions of actual satrapies (possibly even including some of their perspective on neighbouring Dominions).

    Now that’s out of the way, let’s look at the first new one: the Mountain of the Spider King. I’d say that’s starting off on a high note, since that guy was one of the more interesting new things in the core book to me; hopefully now more people will talk about it.

    Interesting to start off with a reference to an important nearby piece of infrastructure that the Lunar is effectively denying, this Black Shale Road. I also personally like the reference to it being aesthetically pleasing, just for the sense that gives of remains of the First Age. Something I liked from the corebook that I’m glad to see repeated here is the idea that some Lunars exercise their power and harassment of the Realm and general offending institutions is by extracting taxes to profit off of their occupation of key arteries. I wonder what they might do with that silver; do they have contacts through which they can reinvest it back in their communities, or merely toss it away ala the alleged bandit in opposition to Alfred Pennyworth’s service to either a colonial apparatus or military dictatorship? Eh, either way is probably good.

    I particularly like the description of the Mountain itself, owing to it challenging some of even my own picture of what a Dominion might be; not a land as most societies might inhabit it, not even people living in settlements on the surface of the mountain (although I imagine some of those are present, clustered around some tunnel entrances). It overall creates a good picture of a society that might be effectively maintained in the fallen Age in a manner unique to a Lunar, as well as a sense of what might draw new recruits (Lunar and mortal) to seek refuge there. It also casts a nice image of a stronghold that might stand in defiance of the Realm, an ominous geographic feature in which a Lunar’s presence can be known, but does not present a ready avenue for legions or even Wyld Hunts to deal with (though I’ve got to imagine that the occasional daring group tries conducting raids into the tunnels, never to be heard from again, possibly never even meeting the Spider King).

    So, a proper introduction to Aum-Ashatra, the Spider King. I like how, in contrast to a number of other really old Lunars and even the connotations of his own spirit shape (although I’ve always been fond of spiders), he’s presented as a more personable and somewhat easier going, even if the conflict has still affected him. I also enjoy the image of a monarch or similar figure publicly conducting themselves with dignified impassiveness while others speak for them; the description of employing favoured granddaughters for that purpose reminds me of a dynamic I enjoyed from the show Orphan Black, in which there was a gangster who didn’t speak English, and had his niece as his interpreter (and was herself a bit nasty). It’s nicely complemented by the idea that he’s otherwise fairly playful.

    Heh, I’d never considered before that a Lunar who primarily conducts themselves in the hybrid form could go unnoticed in their own society simply by going in their original human shape, especially if it might look far younger than one might expect of somebody who might be around a thousand years old. There’s an interesting dynamic to it. And yeah, it’s explicitly laid out that he keeps his Mountain open as a refuge for the desperate and the exiled; it makes me want to go back and read some things about a few Cossack groups again. The description of taking in war orphans in particular makes me imagine something like a one-shot game of playing those kids journeying in pursuit of rumours of a rogue divinity who’ll take in people with nowhere else to go. And yeah, sounds like an all-around good situation, albeit not completely devoid of needing a bit of recompense; there is a war going on, after all. Speaking of, I meant to mention it before, I like the reference to how, for the most part, the tributes are scaled according to what a caravan can afford to pay.

    And a nice little description of his manner as a teacher and councillor within the Silver Pact. The idea that he specializes in espionage and intrigue does make me think that he actually would have contacts for the sake of spending his cash on supplies, although I have to imagine that a lot if also earmarked for bribes. And I think it’s good to suggest the elder’s mannerism and priorities when he meets up in council with other Northern Lunars; more than himself, I just like the reference to the idea that there might ever be any advocacies for minor or petty issues, that even with the conflict against the Realm they can sometimes get caught up in more personal vendettas.

    Huh, Aum-Ashatra is described as ancient, but his Dominion is surprisingly young. Mind, I guess that a lot of older Lunars might have gone through a few over their lives… and going by some of this description, it does seem that for all its security, even the Mountain might not be sustainable in the long run. The question of logistics has been on my mind through much of this reading, so I appreciate that the subject is actually addressed a bit here, and think it’s interesting how this can create a bit of tension in their society; how the mandate of hospitality and taking in newcomers can grate a bit against lean seasons, and how that might be expressed by people. It actually puts me in mind of a different dimension to what could cause the Dominion to collapse; it’s one thing to get to a point where the inhabitants can’t be safely supplied and will either risk mass starvation or need to disperse, but what about cases in which large numbers of the inhabitants themselves ask Aum-Ashatra’s leave to depart, and take their own chances? Hell, what if they start defying him en masse, either with things like larger and more frequent raids than he thinks is safe, or demanding that newcomers be turned away? He’s described as punishing crimes harshly, but he doesn’t seem to view the people as his subjects, so what might he do if there’s some major act of defiance that clearly has popular support? I wonder if that’s a possibility that ever haunts the Spider King’s mind. Speaking of the punishments, I do like how, for all of his generosity and personable nature, he’s still got a bit of Lunar edge to him in his responses to major crimes (although I have a sense that such a thing tends to more often fall upon newcomers who don’t properly respect the rules).

    Heh, and a cute little sidebar describing the Dominion as dark, well-defended and labyrinthine.

    Yes, interesting to have an idea that a very old Exalt would have repeatedly had spouses and be surrounded by descendants; it’s a notion I would have thought of as standard, and yet in the past has felt rather limited to me, with some vague lip service in Lands of Creation and old Lunars otherwise having almost felt asexual. I know some people would be of the opinion that “outliving your spouse is cripplingly depressing, so most would avoid mortal romance or children”, but, eh, dull. So anyway, Ashatra has often been attracted to the clever, determined and willful, fruitful enough to create four major families that hold sway in the Mountain’s society. Interesting idea to have elected tribal leaders who serve as intermediaries between the community and the ancient demigod (although my overall impression is that, while venerable, the Spider King is more of a local celebrity than distant god or monster). As for the families themselves, I think the premise of them each being specialized towards social functions in a manner reminiscent of the new Scarlet Dynasty in particular, although something about the more specialized distinctions makes me think a bit about the tribes of Wakanda as depicted in the film; plus, there’s something fun about the more financially powerful one being a tribe of mushroom farmers (I for one find the idea that anybody could get rich off of mushrooms hilarious, as though anybody would pay for them). And it creates a bit of structure for the process of refugees becoming part of their society, needing to actually be adopted into an existing family. And one final note that they don’t take slaves, even to the point of allowing war captives who have nothing useful to offer and probably aren’t too dangerous or offensive being allowed to flee (another thing that I could see possibly becoming a point of contention at some point in a chronicle). Mind, I will say that the basic social format of the Dominion being family of Aum-Ashatra makes the idea of a situation where they defy or reject him feel less suitable, although in a case where it did happen, it might create a stronger sense that he’d sooner bend than abandon them. Besides, the adoption process might filter out spies and Realm sympathisers, but there could still be room for charismatic individuals who question a lot of their ways and bring a large section of the society around (although they’d probably need the King’s attention to be elsewhere); given my thought on more vigorous raiding, I’m seeing the warrior Tehraihn family as forming a particular weak point.

    And a sidebar clarifying a bit on beastfolk, both for what the format and origins are, and the adjustments to relations with Lunars. Obviously the point on creating trials to bestow the mutations has already been provided in depth, but actual descent… I never had an issue with the zoophilic origins, and even like the mythic connotations, but must admit that the idea of it coming from just conceiving children while in the hybrid form removes a lot of the complications. I mean, the logistics of even having access to the Wyld, and the sense of needing to go out of your way to make children like that… it definitely works a lot less when they’re no longer supposed to have fled to the Wyld and lived there for centuries. Plus, one benefit it might have is, in removing a sense that special Charms are needed to have such children without jumping through all the hoops, maybe people won’t see pregnancy powers as a Lunar Thing anymore. Besides, it’s not as if the matter of mating between human and animal in the Wyld isn’t around anymore, it’s right there in black and white.

    Okay, layout of the Mountain… every family has its own sections of tunnels, the actual settlements are located… actually, it does seem to read as though their actual living spaces are on the surface, even if they’re high up and carved into the rock ala those Native American settlements named in a manner I cannot immediately recall. Oh I see, the tunnels are actually more of a fallback position. I like describing the manner in which they live and decorate their personal spaces, it recalls some of the best parts of Compass Autochthonia; need a lot of that in the books going forwards. Interesting that they sound like they’ve got a thriving little market economy, and I particularly like the breakdown of types and sources of goods, and the whole thing makes me feel as though there might be other mountain dwelling tribes that might come for the trade or luxuries. Lower reaches filled with enough traps that even the boss can’t remember all of them, probably filled with many Dragon Blooded skeletons, aaaand a sidebar about Ashatra having bred a variety of mildly giant spiders to serve as mousers.

    You know, I wonder if anybody who has anything to do with this write-up or the Lunar’s original concept was drawing anything from the Vampire’s Assistant book series, because the image of a society living inside a northern mountain with a spider association is really bringing to mind that depiction of vampires.

    Okay, a nice breakdown of the parameters for warring with the Realm around these parts. Also comes with a bit of history for how the Spider King came to take up residence, and… there seems to be an inconsistency in the timeline. Before it said he established the Dominion two hundred years ago, but now it’s when the Realm was planning for the event of war with Bagrash Kol and designated the Black Shale Road as a major strategic artery? Or has Kol’s reign been moved further along the timeline? Not sure what to make of that. Still, the history of their fight against Ashatra is more consistent: took time to trace him, have actually attempted siege in the past but never been able to overcome the defences or draw him out. Interesting that it’s more than just a base for raids and a strong fortification, that the society actively tries to lure wayward people into the caves and tunnels to their doom, perhaps for the sake of maintaining the formidable reputation of the place (and illustrating the Realm’s local impotence). Although perhaps I’m misreading it, and the people taken while seeking refuge from rain are incidental casualties, and the luring fires are meant to draw in people for their own safety. Maybe sometimes a mix of both. Rounded out a bit with a nice description of the favoured war tactics of feint, traps and false weakness, with the Lunar occasionally taking the field personally to counteract enemy Exalted or ensure troops remember the power behind the Mountain.

    We get a breakdown of some local luminaries who I am very glad to find are not solely more Exalted: we’ve got a fresh chieftain brought to authority by a good head for logistics (which in this environment is an obvious boon) but maybe a bit lacking in social intelligence and fully internalizing her patron-ancestor’s values, a beastman warrior chief who earned renown for killing a Dragon Blood but was cost severe wounds in the process (bringing to mind some of Michael Goodwin’s description of gunzosha warriors alongside the Exalted, which even I must admit was some compelling imagery) who seems to just be happy to be alive to train the next generation to take vengeance on his behalf, and a Lunar adherent who has become fairly prominent in the Mountain and tries to help out its people, but is also jealously protective of his esteem with the King in the face of Lunar students. Nice mix in there, good sense for some underlying disputes or agendas even while the society as a whole remains cohesive.

    Ah, and something I’ve been waiting for for so long; detailing the setting through brief descriptions of a focused location’s relations with neighbours, many of whom will nary be mentioned again. What have we got?

    Tantara, an old ornate coastal city sheltered in a bay not too far from the Mountain, long abandoned but starting to be revived by being adopted as an Imperial Naval base, particularly for the sake of safeguarding the sea routes that are increasingly necessary with the Spider’s disruption of overland transport. Starting to suffer depression and piracy again as military forces are being drawn back to the Blessed Isle. This one’s interesting on a few counts; it’s a neighbour allied to the Realm, but despite its proximity to a vengeful Lunar (whose attacks might look a bit less shiny on an otherwise sympathetic local), all of its problems are actually local and even come more from the Realm not providing the support that they’d like. Hell, it actually benefits from the manner in which the Lunar’s activities drove traffic their way; it makes me think that the local princes would actually want to engage in machinations to help Ashatra, or at least keep him around, because the prospects for their growing prosperity are dependent on his presence. I like this as something feeling an impact in a manner more varied than making everything about the nearby Exalt, as it makes the setting feel more alive, and gives a picture of more complicated relationships between mortal and Chosen (particularly the idea that the satrap and local authorities are in a conflict not easily resolved by who can punch harder).

    Spray is a large but old cliffside fishing hamlet, part of a confederation united by common worship of local seabird gods; nice picture of a community background there, as well as focusing divinity on a subject where it might often be overlooked. Ah, further resolution to my question about trading matters of the Mountain, this place serving as an outlet for that in exchange for protection (although apparently some occasionally chance their arm at selling out or playing both sides, and need to be punished for it, in addition to ones who sometimes suffer the wrath of the Realm; I guess even a Lunar’s protection can only go so far, without being sufficient to void the agreement). It’s not heavily detailed, but it covers one of my queries about how the society could function, and gives an interesting snapshot of life caught in the middle of Exalted conflict. Plus an interesting sense of nuance; they remain under a protection that can’t actually always save them, because what are they going to do, tell Ashatra they don’t want him around anymore? Even if they had the nerve, what would that accomplish? I would suppose he sometimes compensates them for his failures, but that doesn’t exactly bring back the burnt villages. This reminds me of why I so loved the first setting map I saw, dotted in shadowlands.

    And finally, a neighbouring mountain community called Skyborn, home to nomads who tame the really weird idea of large flying eels. Neat idea for a settlement consisting of descendants of a god, giving the God-Blooded a bit more to do than filling out some fighting ranks. Heh, I like the ruthlessness inherent to the idea that their isolation makes them vulnerable to economic exploitation from the actual Lunar, plus the idea that an offended god would turn to the Immaculate Order to seek redress for this. Turns a lot of assumptions about the power dynamics on their heads.

    It’s a good start this. I’m actually relieved that it’s interesting enough to have given me a lot to say without merely summarising what was already written (although I hope it’s not too obtuse to somebody that doesn’t have the manuscript).



    Now, for the first of the Artifacts. In my usual manner, I won’t break down the Evocations in as much detail as Exalted or Martial Arts Charms, although since I’m only doing one at a time, I’ll try to find a bit more to say than my usual summary.

    Oh good, starting out with a suit of armour, those are generally easier to process. I’ll actually say, I’m excited to see a variety of moonsilver Artifacts.

    Now, Eyebright: there are a lot of aspects to this backstory that I like, the aspect of a blind elderly person Exalting as a Lunar, a Celestial Exalt taking up service beneath a Dragon Blood and rejecting the prospect of succession on his passing. Ah, bringing back a reference to the Three Devil Princes, that’s nice; oh for the days when Masters of Jade gave us our first tenuous hints of this rendition of Lunars.

    You know, I swear to God, somewhere around my reading of earlier previews, my head came back to the image of a suit of armour for a Lunar covered in substitute eyes. It was… when I was reading the Dexterity Charms, the picture of high speed fighting made me think of Kill la Kill, the first battle against the Blade Regalia Goku Uniform. I’m quite delighted to see something like that presented here.

    Now, what can it do… basic function provides omnidirectional sight and removes the impediment to one’s natural eyes, although obscuring the sapphires functioning as armour eyes can still mess it up. Has one of those unique categories for focusing action which I like, this time Insight, based on degree of understanding of an opponent in the form of knowing Intimacies that might be driving the fight (probably synergizes nicely with Perception Charms).

    And from there… the general picture is of knowing an enemy well enough for a preternatural awareness of their offensive manoeuvres. It’s nice to see a power enhancing a Lunar Charm for once, with a nice method for using that observation to piggyback a read to get something to help Insight. Hmm, I think Essence 4 powers are unusual for level 3 Artifacts, but… consider that later. For now I like the capstone as a nice, vivid little method for keeping locked onto enemies no matter where they run or hide.

    It’s a nice Artifact to start on, although not the most evocative (not to its fault or anything). I’ll admit that I most want to see some interesting weapon, and would be really happy with something whose powers express a really compelling thesis ala Pyre of Legions.

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
    Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

                        Okay, so having covered the general framework for what a Dominion write-up appears to be, I expect that I’ll be a bit quicker on following ones, as I focus primarily on what is interesting about the specifics rather than general feelings on good priorities. With that, something called Skandhar-Bhal.


                        It’s curious that it would follow up with another Dominion focused on mountains in the North, but considering that I love icy mountain ranges and the image of travel around and life among them, I can’t complain. In particular to that are the things like carvings and flags that might be intended to help out such journeys, or at least colour them, and the idea of some recurring strange iconography actually gives me a certain horror movie monster vibe. That’s a setup that leaves me finding it leading towards it basically having finally given a nice Hidden Elf Village take on a Lunar domain a bit amusing to me.

                        The mythology behind the existence of the valley is nice, enough that I don’t particularly mind it attributing its unique and beneficial properties to something pre-existing rather than the efforts of whatever resident Lunar. I feel as though this kind of background, both in terms of a significant natural event and uncertain history behind it, is something whose absence felt very strong in much of the prior setting. And whatever the overall characteristics of the gods, I still have a soft spot for them occasionally being tasked with something more mystically significant and a bit abstract, and am glad to see it here. My initial take on the valley was that it was fairly small and its population concentrated, but at the point when it is describing resident monastic orders in the plural, I’m curious about what the numbers within this place are supposed to be like. Wait… no, I hadn’t been paying attention to the preceding paragraphs, basically everybody who lives there is monastic in some manner or another, that makes more sense. Actually, that’s now putting me in mind of… well, there are a few fictional representations of a Shangri-la style place, but I’m thinking particularly of one from the BPRD comics.

                        It’s nice to see a more spiritually focused Lunar. Let’s see about this Vanamaithri Mirror-Soul… huh, even more spiritual than I had thought, if there’s a whole element of having sought a relief from an innate drive to violence through it (also, person identifying with they/them pronouns). And here was me expecting a Lunar who had more co-opted the place or seized authority from its guardian spirits, not somebody who arrived as a student and rose in the resident esteem through a combination of their piety and actually being Chosen of Luna in a place defined by a moon relic. Heh, tortoise sensei; I wonder if they lean more Last Airbender or Kung Fu Panda. I take it this is one of those Dominions Eric talked about in which the Lunar’s concern for the Realm has only become enflamed with the onset of the Time of Tumult.

                        Let’s see, a nice looking breakdown of some of the resident monastic orders. I have an appreciation for the restraint employed here; we’re shown enough to get on with, and have a sense of the place, but it doesn’t go and name every resident god and describe their associated order. I also think it’s interesting that there’s room for specific dedication to other, lesser gods in that society. Of those, I like Jade Eagle’s one just because I’m fond on the whole to references to sky burial, and how the practices of Scarlet Doe’s suggest a form of low level magic, or perhaps at least a unique characteristic Merit.

                        Doing some more good with describing the layout and ornamentation of the place to help it feel more alive. I find the description of how the holy orders need to have lay people to cover a lot of infrastructural needs to be both logical and helpful for having a bit more to set games here. I like the descriptions of the ways in which even a relatively small and isolated community such as this can divide into cliques and possess meaningful conflict, even without much in the way of crime, endemic violence or a heavily stratified society. My comment about a Hidden Elf Village was a little bit ironic, but honestly, this kind of social texture is the kind of thing that I would have generally wanted in the past from descriptions of, say, actual Tolkien Elf societies. Or hidden villages.
                        For all that there’s a deeper and more genuine spirituality here, I also think it’s good that there are still some political wrangling underlying their belief systems. Honestly, that just helps it to feel more real; sometimes designating something as spiritually malign actually does come from disputes of authority. It’s also very interesting to see how that would create the circumstances for underground heresies even in a community this small, and there’s something especially great in a tidy little reference to temple stores and exchange between households to give a sense of commercial activity at this level; there aren’t shops and inns and the use of currency, there are the commons and families specialized to particular crafts, and it’s all driven by social capital.

                        Honestly, the picture being painted of this community is making me think of the potential for fascinating stories completely irrespective of the greater context of Exalted. It’s like… imagine
                        something between a soap opera and a prison drama, and add a sprinkle of period piece and supernatural elements to it, make sure to actually remove it from a lot of familiar modern conceits of commerce and government, and then run with it. I especially like the image of the one family who lives on the margins of the village, in the run-down and poorly furnished shack, because they’ve been known or suspected to consort with darker powers; they don’t really get invited to the festivals, and it’s uncommon for anything to be provided for them, while the head of the family keeps scheming to employ local politics and clever little curses to ultimately bring more of the village into their social debt (or in fear of retribution), and soon they’ll be the ones living in the big house in the square overlooking the nice statuary and getting the best of everything. Damn, I’d watch the shit out of that show… book… whichever.

                        Anyway; I’ll admit, after the descriptions given in the first paragraph, I’m a little bit uncertain about why there are longer ones for the orders back here. It’s not a problem or anything, it doesn’t mess up my point about not including more than are needed, I’m just curious is all; I suppose I could read those as an introduction to the concept, before they are provided with a bit of focus. Besides, there’s some good stuff in there. I like how the Order of the Moon-That-Fell regards Mirror Soul as a prophet of Luna not merely for being one of the Exalted, but because their own qualities align with what the Order sees and values in Luna, as well as the idea of martial arts training in this isolated context being useful primarily for its value in an avenue towards their process of self-actualisation, and in patrols through the more dangerous landscape to still help new people reach their home alive. What I liked about the Silent Acolytes in their intro isn’t quite present in their description here (although it can be inferred a bit from the description of humanity trying to become divine), but I do think it’s good to have a group with a stronger communal attitude. The School of the Pale Sky reiterates what I like with the sky burial, and the sense of communing with local ghosts helps root them as another facet of the overall social scene and local politics (especially the picture that sometimes a malevolent spectre needs to be rooted out and banished, to help keep life interesting). Plus maybe the general sense that the community of lay settlers might necessarily grow beyond what one might expect their orders to accommodate. Sketching out the relationships between the monastic and lay people; I like the note on how temple authorities tend to be people who’ve been in the orders their whole life, for the sense of how it has these powerful figures who might be a bit out of touch with the general population without disrupting the general dynamic. Ah, it took me a moment to understand that silver would be used for their prayer bells because of the whole Luna thing; not much else to say about their prayer activities, it’s all fairly solid. It’s also nice to have a sense of what a system of this size and isolation would do with its deviants, to round out the earlier image of how internal conflicts might arise.

                        You know, apart from feeling a sense of callback to just about any Hidden Elf Village in fiction, something about the spirituality and location of this place felt naggingly familiar, but I just couldn’t quite put my finger on it… but it’s from the description of how they shun they outside world and discourage their own inhabitants from leaving with descriptions of the issues awaiting out there. It makes sense that new pilgrims might want to take up residence here, albeit tentatively; this land is peaceful, its inhabitants kind, but perhaps they do not belong. The fact that sometimes their own inhabitants do leave for the sake of preaching to the masses gives me two pictures; on one side, the bit of public ceremony in which everybody turns out to make a big deal about them taking on those burdens, warning them of the dangers of the big city, and to never forget who they are; on the other, that they might arrive in the cities to find a lot of other prophets preaching that they’re emissaries from some hidden spiritual community, albeit ones who might be a bit more likely to be mistaken, deluded, or outright con artists.

Dancing Ceryneika sounds like an interesting type, on several fronts; that her divine blood extending her lifespan has allowed her to be somebody with youthful energy in being a kind of den mother for about a lifetime of the mortal inhabitants (I’m picturing some people the same age as her, mildly frustrated or bemused by the memory of her as a bossy pre-teen, and still seeing so much of that in her as they start to weary), the substance of some of her reprimands around the community, that picture of something like people asking for treatment for something of which she disapproves getting passive-aggressively turned down. She seems like a very dynamic figure to have in an environment such as this, and a testament to the values of soft power.

                        Ohh, a competing Exalt. You know, I was actually thinking that keeping Dragon Blooded out might be something of a priority, as their personalities and agendas might overturn the order of things, so it’s interesting to see the writers ahead of me on this one. And yeah, just their bad luck (or perhaps something fitting to the nature of a place blessed by Luna) that the one Terrestrial they get is so at odds with the usual orthodoxy, despite being an Earth Aspect of all things. It’s an interesting idea, that the pursuits and mannerisms of being Exalted might draw such respect, contrary to the usual strictures and standards, that could result in a severe schism if properly capitalised upon. Vanamaithri might be preparing to serenely lead her martial artists out for some kind of battle, but I wonder if Agallai Stone-Blessed, or anybody else, compromising the integrity of Skandhar-Bhal might just overcome her lessons and provoke her to Rage again (mind, the Great Curse might weight one of those options for her).

                        And it’s interesting to see that at least one of the gods, that sky burial Jade Eagle, assigned to watch over this sacred place chafes a bit at the duty, and would sooner by living a life that was less, well, spiritual. Like, I think this overall thing has been very good at depicting something genuinely religious surrounding some of the game’s gods, but it’s still fun to have a peek behind the curtain at at least one of them; they can provide a genuine inspiration and maybe even some truly meaningful guidance, but the fact that under it all they’re not actually numinous and transcendental beings has some drawbacks. I like the depiction of the social impacts this has, such as being a good viewed a bit more uneasily by the worshippers and a temple to help out the stifled a bit by letting them serve as people who’ll go looking for people not to help, but hinder. And to reinforce that element of Exalted being peers to gods, I think there’s something very neat in depicting Jade Eagle as having a complex personal relationship with Vanamaithri and concerns therein, that just aren’t mercenary; arising from a sincere concern tempered by a deep insight. Does inadvertently apply the wrong pronoun to Mirror Soul, though, probably a typo.

                        So I’d say that Skandthar-Bhal is the best example of its type of setting that I’ve ever seen, for all of the reasons I elucidated on throughout. It falls very firmly into that category I’ve discussed in the past; of not wanting to tell the book what I want, but for it to tell me what I want and then make a convincing argument for it. Lovely.



                        Hmm, getting a bit late… despite what I said before, I’m kind of hoping the next Artifact actually does turn out to be reasonably straightforward. Let’s see what Far-Ranging Eye can deliver.


                        Also, what, are all of these Artifacts going to be eye-themed?

                        Okay, forged by a usurped prince who had been a bit too naïve and trusting of those around him, became Exalted while stranded in the wilderness and discovering a hitherto untapped reserve of strength, made with the intent of being a personal vow to be more vigilant in future. Again I like the reference to deferring temporal authority.

                        I do like it when some Artifacts refer to a lineage of owners, just to fill out the history a bit more.

                        No special preamble to the Evocations… ah, so it’s going to be an Infinite Chakram focused on Investigation and, I’m guessing, understanding motives? Assisted by striking with the weapon?
                        Hmm, more like pursuit, focus and enhanced attacks against those the Lunar distrusts. I wonder if the weapon’s power has been perverted a bit from vigilance to paranoia. Being able to negate most forms of supernatural disguise or concealment is neat though. And the capstone… lets you hit multiple people, including being able to ignore their being hidden.



                        Well, looks like I got my wish. No real complaints, it’s still a solid Artifact, it just doesn’t quite pop.

Kind of asking Rabbit Krustofski if it’s right to by a Chrysler kind of thing. That and how their text covers a number of other topics, and being the basis of law imposed upon provinces, extends the authority of the khojas, so there’s a bit more implication and drive to the expansionism.

                                                I think the description of their architecture and ornamentation at least gives their cities a lot of visual flair; I can readily picture these places with their large buildings and all of the sculptures and stelae around, and I think they’d stand out a lot on those practices. And I do find some strong character in the descriptions of their fashions, both the idea of emphasizing their comfort with lighter fabrics than typical in this Direction, and their expansionism with incorporating heavy furs suggestive of the growing frontier. I think the references to incorporation of bears throughout has been just understated enough, while the ubiquity of it does give me a sense of what might distinguish an Animal Avatar as a patron deity for an urban culture, to what it would be for nomads or what might be found with alternative gods.

                                                Okay, been this far into the description of this Lunar Dominion before getting very much about the Lunar Exalt, so let’s see what this True Voice has on offer. Rrriiight okay, it’s getting interesting here. So you’ve got a Lunar who actually originated from the culture of the Dominion herself, and put together a whole regimen of insight into spirits and some other kinds of technical and organisation knowledge to help them advance. But it’s also not really done out of a personal sense of loyalty or obligation, since she’s more invested in the Silver Pact and wants to use her homeland as an eventual weapon against the Realm. Because of that, she leaves after not very long to take the fight more directly to them, leaving the growing empire as a reserve force, and has now come back to try and hatch her nest egg to turn on the vulnerable Realm, only to find that this Dominion has grown a bit beyond her capacity to control. The line about having put passages into the sacred text that conventional theological interpretation has appropriated to other purposes in the meantime is great.

                                                Yes, right, it’s coming together now. Boring would not have been the right term for it, but in its own right it’s… straightforwardly a polity that came upon an unusual degree of power, that has become a basis for dominating its neighbours. About what you’d expect of such a scenario far away from the Realm. The dynamic of it having been set in motion by a Lunar who is coming to collect, but it evolved in her absence into something of its own, it’s very, how do I put it… specifically in terms of a story related to Lunars, I think it’s a good strong idea for the format and challenges of a Dominion, particularly one intended to flourish at that distance. And it’s probably an unprecedented scenario, because there hasn’t exactly been another opportunity in which somebody might call a reserve force in to attack a vulnerable Realm.

                                                Right, so with that in mind, I can make my way through the rest of the features of this thing, in terms either or what True Voice has to navigate to try and redirect it, or just how it functions for its own games set in that theatre.

                                                High Sila sounds nice and lively, and I think the picture of it as a site for theological debate sounds like a particularly interesting feature. In the end, I guess the point on how not all visiting foreigners are there* by choice refers to the premise of how, having their lands conquered, they would end up drifting towards the new capital. I think it makes the place particularly inviting as a setting for play to have that element of being so cosmopolitan making it a hotbed for intrigue and revolutionary movement, and that among the subjects of concern for the congregating khojas is the manner in which such a consolidation threatens orthodoxy.

                                                Former capital Karaqa sounds intriguing with its kind of iconoclastic and debauched refuge status; if they can get in before any scheduled clearance of squatters and resettlement according to official policy, I imagine that a group of players could have a wild time set there. And I like Burdock as an example of the consequences of the empire’s ruthlessness that might just come back to bite them; considering how much of its power is predicated on the food surplus, I imagine a particular dramatic irony to vengeful ghosts who might come bearing weapons of famine. All they need is the right champion to lead them.

                                                Port cities such as Spur are always useful for their own kind of cosmopolitan character, both in travellers and hard goods. I often enjoy the image of rebels in the setting adopting outside patrons, so I’m glad for this idea of an ousted Raksha tyrant who finds themselves an object of some kind of reverence again when the new boss is perceived to be even worse. And Storm’s Breech sounds good for some frontier survival stories and working class unrest, and in the spirit of unlikely benefactors, I like the idea there of a Guild factor trying to convince the workers to help subvert control of the mines in exchange for better working conditions.

                                                Karaqa’s qualities as True Voice’s territory gives me a sense of it being a good reason you don’t abandon one for centuries (plus, a quality such as crime-ridden is just going to make that state self-perpetuating), and I can’t help feel that her decision to try claiming the new capital rather than attempt to clean up the old one and leverage it against the centre is the wrong choice.

                                                Right, there was a mention of the child emperor, named here as Ajurai Siladar II, before, and as I suspected it would seem his status is on account of having inherited a good strain of Moon-Touched power; I’d present that as some obscure law saying that somebody who inherits such is immediately given the throne. Not much to say about him otherwise, his status as being the focus for lots of other people’s influence and agendas is another fairly boilerplate element. His religious tutor Habja is in a similar boat, although I like the idea that she’s at the centre of tensions surrounding her agenda of trying to encourage a crackdown on heterodoxy, with opposition being partially on the grounds of it being something that just makes their new provinces harder to hold. Isipho Spear-Grace and Qismai are just okay as additional features of the palace intrigues. Might have been interesting to have a figure unrelated to the capital.

                                                And for the neighbours… I like the premise of one of them being a successful rebel state that is dealing with the attempt to reconstruct and constantly worried that they’re going to be reconquered, and think it’s a distinct dynamic for there to be debate in Inscomay on whether to bother, since doing so would really only be for show as Sulat isn’t really worth much anymore. Similarly the idea that the relatively young empire has taken a few too many liberties with some of the neighbouring nomads, whom they might end up driving into alliance with the liberated province.

                                                Dazul having a trapped demon as their ruler, with the aristocracy consisting of exorcists to both speak to it and keep it locked up, is delightfully weird. I think that’s nicely balanced by the more mundane concern of a major trade neighbour’s expansion having devalued their exports. Aaaand I like that them being shifted more to the east means they’re on the border of the giant weird forest, just for the geographic distinction (I find the idea that their folklore refers to scary bear-killing witches cute).


                                                All right so overall, Iscomay… it holds together, and it’s got a decent amount going on in and around it. It just shines a bit less to me in comparison to preceding write-ups. I do sincerely hope that somebody else with different tastes and priorities gets more out of it than I did (which, to be clear, is not nothing, it’s just less; it’s like the second Artifact to me).

                                                *Up to 100,000 words of review.



                                                Speaking of Artifacts, next we have… Nightbane, a powerbow. That should be nice.


                                                Okay, that’s a fairly dark backstory for it, with Luna in the plague victim shape and all of the murder and making the bow from somebody’s spin extracted and cleaned by partially eating a guy’s corpse. I’m looking forwards to seeing its balance of curative and corrupting powers. Given the impact of the Great Contagion on even Lunars, reiterated by things like Ma-Ha-Suchi’s trauma and the Lunar third generation, I’m glad to see it play into the backstory of an Artifact being lost to Lunars who keep trying to find it again. Particularly for the ironic element of it having been a healing Artifact that was likely mostly powerless against it.

                                                I wonder if you’d have to be doing anything with Nightbane to get those rerolls for diagnosis rolls and medicinal rolls. Like, carry it like a staff or something, or just enough to have it strung over your back. Probably doesn’t matter. What about the more important powers… heh, the whole time I’m reading Argent Panacea Arrow, I kept thinking about shooting the contagion out of somebody being a gambit would entail needing to get into a fight before curing somebody, and then it notes that doing it outside of combat doesn’t require rolling Initiative and I think “of course the gambit stuff is to give it a utility for curing people when poison or disease are being wielded as weapons against you”.

                                                Ah, makes sense that the concept would extend as far as being able to mess up creatures of darkness and fae, gives it a bit more to do in a fight. Actually… okay I get it, the whole thesis of the Artifact is about being curative, and it takes that to extremes in attacking people; creatures of darkness, individuals who, in being contrary to the wielder’s Principles, are viewed as metaphorical blights on the world, rolling in some of the murderous origins. It’s a solid premise so far, but it would really hook me if it did any particularly interesting things with it.

                                                Okay, being able to remove a certain class of Intimacy immediately and fully is a good start. Given the mechanics for succeeding with it, I’d almost wonder if it’s a bit too powerful for an Essence 2 power; I would suppose that would be kind of all right as something with a very narrow scope and only available from possession of the one Artifact, but it does mildly concern me with the precedent it sets. I do appreciate that the Resonant power for dealing with mental illness also can’t remove it completely.

                                                Hmm, keeps going with a power to remove curses and shaping effects… and finally a power by which a character can impose curses of their own against the kinds of people that some prior Evocations can injure, to permanently trap them in animal shapes. As an end point, I like how it ties in a more directly Lunar theme for the moonsilver weapon, as well as how it ties into their own power to impose transformations by potentially lowering the threshold for damage required to do that. It’s kind of… cruel, in a manner suggestive of a predatory quality even in a moonsilver item made for a more benign purpose.


                                                Ultimately, I find Nightbane to work effectively as a weapon that has a consistent line through its attacks, and is kind of weird. I’m getting a running sense that powerbows might be weapons for shooting things in a more abstract manner, and maybe some other utility tool stuff.

                                                    Yeah, I pointed out the north-west/north-east references, Eric said the north-west is a typo.

                                                    Back to the Toumans; one thing I liked about this chapter (of a number; it's my favourite fluff chapter) is that it presents a number of different models of "barbarians" that were common in real life, but previously not mentioned much in Exalted.

                                                    Skhandar-Bal and Iscomay are "barbarians" purely in the sense that the Realm has labelled them as such. One is a monastic community, the other a feudal empire built on a central fertile farming area. The Mountain of the Spider King is a raiding culture based in an area that isn't comfortable, but provides safety.
                                                    And then you have the Toumans, who are nomadic refugees pushed out of their homeland who've gone looking for another place to conquer. Like the goths and vandals.
