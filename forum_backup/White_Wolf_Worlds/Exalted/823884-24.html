    Here's my latest creation for the forum to critique.

    Windsong
    ●●● Artifact: Blue Jade Far Talon
    Light Artifact Thrown Weapon
    Tags: Lethal, Thrown (Medium), Cutting

    Appearance
    An elegantly designed dagger made of sky blue jade, with a several holes across its body so that it "sings" when thrown.

    Attunement
    Benefit Attunement Cost: 0
    - Vanishes after being thrown and reappears in the wielder’s hand unless evocation or charm says otherwise
    - While constructed for flight, Windsong can be used in hand to hand combat. Windsong is considered a Light Mundane Melee Weapon with the Lethal tag for hand-to-hand combat.
    Benefit Attunement Cost: +2m
    - Windsong is in tune to the flow of all that surrounds it, and is willing to share this insight. Convert 1 die on any awareness roll to a success and add +1 to parrying with Windsong.

    Evocations

    Following the Wind’s Lead
    Cost: 3m
    Mins: Essence 1
    Type: Reflexive.
    Keywords: Uniform
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisite Charms: None
    One Line Summary: Improve the attack of following range attacks from other ranged allies. Windsong’s call draws the notice of all archers and throwers to follow it as it hits its mark. After a successful attack on an opponent at short range or further, allied ranged attackers that are currently aiming (took an aim action on their latest turn and have yet to act) at other targets may spontaneously switch their target to the wielder’s opponent at no expense. Switching allies will maintain any bonus accrued by aiming if they were aiming for a second turn. They will all have the ability to hit target the new target if at Medium or Long range, limited by their weapon’s range. Also, all switched ranged attacks treat the target’s defense as one lower as long the ally fires on their next turn.


    Whistle Down the Wind
    Cost: 6m (2i+ depending on the distract gambit)
    Mins: Essence 2
    Type: Reflexive
    Keywords: Perilous
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisite Charms: Following the Wind’s Lead
    One Line Summary: Help an another’s disengage action and knock back the opposition When an ally within medium range and line of sight is attempting to disengage from any number of opponents, you may instantly aid them with a distract gambit. The gambit works as normal, with the disengager being the beneficiary and the difficulty the attack roll equalling the highest evasion of those contesting the disengage action. However, instead of transferring initiative upon success, the ally gains (gambit difficulty - 2) successes to add to the their disengage roll. Should the disengage succeed, all opponents involved are flung 1 range band away from the wielder and the ally, landing prone, by a sudden burst of air. Using this Charm allows the Exalt to act out of turn, but acts as her combat action for the turn.


    Out of the Blue
    Cost: 4m
    Mins: Essence 3
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: Decisive-only
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisite Charms: Whistle Down the Wind
    One Line Summary: Toss Windsong high into the air on one round, sneak attack a target within medium range on the next round

    The wind is more heard and felt, rarely seen. Under this truth, Windsong can strike unseen. On their turn, the wielder selects a target they can see within medium range, and then tosses Windsong high into the air, where it disappears from sight. On the next round on the wielder’s turn, like a bolt from the blue, Windsong dive-bombs the target. First, roll (Dexterity + Stealth) against the target’s (Perception + Awareness). If the wielder wins by 1 or more successes, the incoming attack is considered a surprise attack, if by 5 or more successes, it’s considered an ambush. Make a decisive attack, applying the results of the contested roll. This attack cannot benefit from any simple charms.

    While Windsong is airborne, the wielder can make separate simultaneous actions as long they do not include a decisive attack. Delaying one’s action also delays Windsong’s attack and withering attacks made by the wielder while Windsong is in flight do not add to the wielder’s initiative.

    At the end of the tick, Windsong will return to the wielder’s hand as long as one hand is free. Otherwise, it appears on the ground near the wielder’s body. At that point, the wielder’s initiative resets.

    Special Activation Rules: Can only be used if the wielder is not grappled and once per target per fight. This charm can be flurried with another action as long as it follows all other flurry rules.

        Originally posted by Binary Phantom View Post

        And critique I shall!

        Originally posted by Binary Phantom View Post
        Attunement
        Benefit Attunement Cost: +1m
        - Windsong is in tune to the flow of all that surrounds it, and is willing to share this insight. Convert 1 die on any awareness roll to a success and add +1 to parrying with Windsong.
        Converting 1 die to a success is, on average, the equivalent of adding +1 non-charm die to the roll (you turn 0.5 successes into 1). Increasing Parry by 1 is the equivalent of a 2m Excellency. That's a pretty hefty passive bonus, so I'd bump up the attunement cost to +2m.

        Originally posted by Binary Phantom View Post
        Following the Wind’s Lead
        Cost: 3m
        Mins: Essence 1
        Type: Reflexive.
        Keywords: Uniform
        Duration: Instant
        Prerequisite Charms: None
        One Line Summary: Improve the attack of following range attacks from other ranged allies.

        Windsong’s call draws the notice of all archers and throwers to follow it as it hits its mark. After a successful attack on an opponent at short range or further, allied ranged attackers that are currently aiming at other targets may spontaneously switch their target to the wielder’s opponent at no expense. Also, all ranged attacks treat the target’s defense as two lower until the wielder’s next turn.
        Conceptually, a good first Evocation, but the wording is unclear. Does "allied ranged attackers that are currently aiming at other targets" mean "allied ranged attackers who took the Aim action on their last turn"? If yes, does "may spontaneously switch their target to the wielder's opponent at no expense" include "retain bonus dice gained from aiming", or does it just allow them to make an attack out to medium+ range without aiming? Moreover, does the last effect last until the beginning, or the end of the wielder's next turn?

        In any case, effectively reducing the target's defense by 2 is an incredibly potent effect (+2 non-charm automatic successes on all incoming ranged attack rolls!), and a smart party can reap massive benefits from this if they're built around doing so. I'd either add a WP to the cost, or bump it up to 4 motes and give it a reset condition, with the assumption that you're modelling the opposition on the corebook's Antagonists session, most of whom have no convenient way to negate defense penalties. If your table regularly features opponents who can do so, the Evocation is fine as-is.

        Originally posted by Binary Phantom View Post
        Whistle Down the Wind
        Cost: 6m (2i+ depending on the distract gambit)
        Mins: Essence 2
        Type: Reflexive
        Keywords: Perilous
        Duration: Instant
        Prerequisite Charms: Following the Wind’s Lead

        One Line Summary: Help an another’s disengage action and knock back the opposition
        When an ally within medium range and line of sight is attempting to disengage from any number of opponents, you may instantly aid them with a distract gambit. The gambit works as normal, with the disengager being the beneficiary and the with the mean evasion of those contesting the disengage setting the difficulty the attack roll. However, instead of transferring initiative upon success, the ally gains (gambit difficulty - 2) successes to add to the their disengage roll. Should the disengage succeed, all opponents involved are flung 1 range band away from the wielder and the ally, landing prone, by a sudden burst of air. Using this Charm allows the Exalt to act out of turn, but acts as her combat action for the turn.
        I like how this expands on the native niche of Thrown as the tricky, gambit-oriented fighting style. On the other hand, I think using mean Evasion is quite unwieldy (are you rounding up or down? doesn't the math get annoying to deal with when there's 3+ opponents hanging around?). Just use the highest rating. Otherwise, it seems okay (cost is pretty hard to figure out - it lets you break initiative order, but that's basically Thunderclap Rush Attack, which costs 3 motes, but it also adds 1-3 non-charm successes to a disengage, but it's also tied to succeeding on an attack roll and has an Initiative cost, but it also moves people around and knocks them down, which is basically Mournful Crane's Cry, which costs 3m, but this is AoE, but MCC flings people away for 2 range bands - in any case, if you sum up the effects, 6 motes and the inherent costs of making a Distract gambit sound about right).

        Originally posted by Binary Phantom View Post
        Out of the Blue
        Cost: 7m
        Mins: Essence 3
        Type: Simple
        Keywords: Decisive-only
        Duration: Instant
        Prerequisite Charms: Whistle Down the Wind
        One Line Summary: Toss Windsong high into the air on one round, surprise attack a target within medium range on the next round

        The wind is more heard and felt, rarely seen. Under this truth, Windsong can strike unseen. On their turn, the wielder selects a target they can see within medium range, and then tosses Windsong high into the air, where it disappears from sight. On the next round on the wielder’s turn, like a bolt from the blue, Windsong dive-bombs the target. Make a decisive attack, with (wielder’s stealth - target’s awareness, minimum 1) automatic successes on the attack roll. Should the attack hit, ignore (1 + excess successes) hardness. If this were to reduce the hardness below 0, treat the hardness as 0 and convert up to (remaining excess successes) dice into successes on the damage roll. This attack cannot benefit from any simple charms.

        While Windsong is airborne, the wielder can make separate simultaneous actions as long they do not include a decisive attack. Delaying one’s action also delays Windsong’s attack and withering attacks made by the wielder while Windsong is in flight do not add to the wielder’s initiative.

        At the end of the tick, Windsong will return to the wielder’s hand as long as one hand is free. Otherwise, it appears on the ground near the wielder’s body. At that point, the wielder’s initiative resets.

        Special Activation Rules: Can only be used if the wielder is not grappled and once per target per fight. This charm can be flurried with another action as long as it follows all other flurry rules.
        Seems like a pretty fun effect. Mechanically, it's a bit all over the place (summary says it lets you surprise attack a target, but it isn't actually a surprise attack, and the way it interacts with hardness is just... bwuh?). If you assume artifact armor to be rare, the damage boost can get out of hand quite quickly, but then again, the cost is incredibly high (about 15 motes if you full Excellency it, assuming Awareness 4 on part of the enemy, which strikes me as reasonable for most combatants important enough to warrant using a capstone on). And, of course, it runs into the problem of artifact armor and a decent Awareness score taking quite a lot out of its teeth, at which point you're stuck with an incredibly pricey effect that's of little benefit above Initiative 10 (and why would you launch your big decisive doomcombo while below Initiative 10?).

        So, umm, I'd basically make it an attempt to re-establish surprise, forget about the Hardness interaction, and reduce the cost.

                                                    Here's what I've got for a fancier capstone for the Gravewright. I didn't want to go over the usual 5 evocations for an artifact, so this ended up being a dual function charm. I don't think that's really a problem. If it seems okay, I'll edit it into the original post and bump it up to artifact 4.

                                                    Overall, I'm not sure about the balance of this evocation and the various souls, but I still like the concept. I felt that emulating Generalized Ammunition Technique (with its adding damage based on the stunt) was worth doing. I was tempted to make the soul shots unblockable (being immaterial and all), but I didn't want it to do so many big things at once. I also thought of making the soul explode and hit an area, but this is supposed to work with Righteous Devil Style and I didn't want it to compete with Dancing Devil Trigger Finger.

                                                    Cloak of Lost Souls
                                                    Cost: 1m or 4m, 1wp; Mins: Essence 3; Type: Reflexive
                                                    Keywords: Decisive-Only
                                                    Duration: Instant
                                                    Prerequisites: Heel-Biting Plume

                                                    Not even death is an escape from the Gravewright. After slaying a target while Fear-the-Reaper Mien is active, the wielder may spend a single mote to rip their hun soul (or equivalent) from their body and bind it to Gravewright. For all intents and purposes, they are permanently slain, similar to Ghost-Eating Technique. Reducing the size of a battle group usually allows at least one soul each time. The souls may optionally become visible when the wielder's anima is at burning or above, a river of silent torment flowing from Gravewright.

                                                    At any time, the wielder may simply choose to release the soul, whereupon it either dissolves or returns to Lethe, as appropriate to its nature. They are also released like this upon the death of the wielder. What Gravewright does with the wielder's own soul is unknown.

                                                    Before a decisive attack roll, the wielder may activate this charm again for four motes and one temporary willpower to fire one of her captive souls, whirling and screaming as it burns into nothingness at last. The shot does not require ammunition, and one die of raw damage is converted into an automatic level of damage, plus one more die converted for each stunt level awarded to the attack (if any). It also has an additional effect according to the nature of the soul used. Some examples are as follows:

                                                    Chilling: The souls of the Abyssal Exalted and other deathly creatures (such as ghosts) chill instead of burn. If hit, for the rest of the scene the target takes a -2 penalty on all actions. They may take a miscellaneous action (which may be flurried) to warm themselves back up to remove the penalty, if they have some means to do so.

                                                    Corrosive: Some souls, along with the essence of demons, seethe with great vitriol, firing chemical lances instead of flame. If hit, the target's withering soak is reduced by 2 for the rest of the scene. This may be undone by a miscellaneous action to wipe themselves off, which may be flurried.

                                                    Holy: The souls of the Solar Exalted along with other holy (more or less) beings burn away the impure. The attack deals aggravated damage to creatures touched by darkness, such as natives of Malfeas or the Underworld.

                                                    Humble: The souls of many mortals burn only dimly, making the attack difficult to see. The target must roll (Wits + Awareness) at difficulty 4, or suffer a -2 penalty to their defense.

                                                    Shifting: The souls of the Lunar Exalted and other shapeshifters lend their protean nature to the shot, giving it a form that molds itself to fit around (or through) any cover less than full, negating its benefits.

                                                    This list is not exhaustive, nor is it required to use these exact effects for souls of the same type (a particularly bitter Solar could yield a corrosive soul, for example). Unique souls give unique effects when fired.
