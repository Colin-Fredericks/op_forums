                Thanks for directing my attention towards, Adeimantus as he is a nice piece of work - to say the obvious, hits some of the notes of The King In Yellow/Carcosa and Tlön, Moorcock's idea of Tanelorn and those themes of possession and invasion and being overtaken by a mirror of yourself which are part of the Fair Folk (noted explicitly upthread in reference to Coroline and other such works).

                Re; Levi' s statement on Fair Folk Nobles' natures as appropriative, I think it's an idea worth considering. It seems like it has some value - but I do think we should see Fair Folk as being capable of elaboration and extension and change in what they "take", and also of reproducing themselves an passing an "identity" on to "children" or "creations".

                I'd also kind of repeat (sorry) my earlier comment, I'd emphasize they also partake of being what is them thrust upon them (by Creation and the all-out-war of strong things in the Wyld), and then made their own. Playing a Fair Folk probably allows you to access personal and existential horror of having an identity thrust upon you (or being forced to pull it together from others scraps and leavings in order to 'fight back').

                Even from the perspective that you're not of Creation, I would argue they're not only there to powerful, self possessed agents of predation, greed, theft to be worked upon others. Part of them is the horror of things that Creation has forced upon beings of another alien nature, through its hubris and the inclusive, expansive ambitions of their domains and rule held by the Powers That Be through history (the Primordials, the Gods, man), coming back to roost. So I'd not really be inclined to have *too* much agency in being born only to self defining hunger and made by what they choose to take by will alone.

                (At least, that's the argument I'd make! This possibly depends on your taste for World Of Darkness-ish "You are the monster" personal horror type exploration things in Exalted? Whether those even work well at all, etc..).

                                        Sort of. The metaphor is that Creation is land, and the Wyld, the sea. The Marches are the continental shelf, and the Deep Wyld the deep sea beyond them.
