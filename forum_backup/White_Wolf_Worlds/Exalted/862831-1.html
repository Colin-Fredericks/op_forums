        I believe there are some misunderstandings between us working here.

        a) Whether this is trivial or not: I have no clue, really. I freely confess that I have not reviewed the relevant material before writing this, and that I have not been all that much "into" Exalted as of couple last years, and only started returning to it recently. Therefore, it possible that what I have written is in clear conflict with the text and the implications contained within books. In that case, the fault is on me for not doing the due research and just expanding on something that was not supposed to be expanded on.

        b) I do not feel like anything within my text suggests that Deled's reaction is typical; having a disagreement about a religious text is one thing, murdering others is another. Deled's reaction is extreme and probably unprecedented, but that does not mean it is something that does not have any sort of roots. Fundamentalist stances also come from somewhere, and I tried to give a genealogy of this particular one. His actions are not representative, not standard and certainly not treated nor accepted as any sort of a norm, even among the more radical-minded Immaculates.

        c) Finally, regarding the healthy dose of politics being mixed into this: I wrote this debate as being over a thousand years old, if not older. Therefore, whatever political contextes that were to it - dependant on the Shogunate politics of that time - are long gone, and as I have tried to imply, the Question of Origination is currently treated as something of an antique curio from the formative times of Immaculate Religion, often derided and ridiculed. That does not mean it is not political, it is just not political in a fresh way.

        While on that point, I would also like to note that while I am very far from denying that such theological disputes are often heavily involved in politics, I would be wary of rendering them to being primarily political or only political. It is a view that easily lapses into an anachronic lack of awareness of a fundamentally different - and more profound - role that theology, with all of its today amusing minutia could and probably have played in the lives of people of different times and cultures.

                                I get that in its original context, the whole "of vs from" things feels like a kind of punchline to the whole theological duel to the death thing, and that was probably the intent. Yet, when I read about the actual dispute between Western and Eastern Christianity concerning whether or not the term "and the Son" should be added to prayers addressing the emergence of the Holy Spirit, well, it can't help but feel sufficiently real to me to be afforded a measure of seriousness. I still don't think this needs to detract from Deled, since I don't think there's really any issue in the Order for which it would be considered acceptable to duel to the death (or at least not with such an unnecessarily brutal finishing move). I might even say that focusing too much on the idea of him doing it over trivialities and minutia is something that verges on caricature, at least retroactively (by which I mean, not bad at the time, but maybe refine it a bit).
