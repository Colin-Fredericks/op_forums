I'chan the tyrant-lizard-riding Djala archer/medicine-man was, while super cool, frankly one of the weaker characters in my 2nd edition group, hence his untimely death in a fight between the circle and some Sidereals (the only other PCs who ever died, died fighting alone against multiple NPCs. Or in one case killed himself).


                    In my DnD game, everyone chose their character based on something they thought would be fun and they'd enjoy, and no-one dislikes the idea of their character. But sometimes players (especially newer ones) get disgruntled when it seems their character isn't very good. Bruno was a bit disgruntled when he saw how much damage my lighnting bolt can do (8d6) compared to his sneak attack (4d6+4 I think? Maybe 5d6+4 once a day). Steffie was a bit disgruntled when she realised that Barbarians actually don't do that much damage, she thought being a giant Dragon-woman with a giant axe would be doing tons of damage (which I don't think is a stupid assumption), but they're really a tank with okay damage.
                    And these, frankly, are characters who are intended to be balanced.
                    If I was playing a Solar and Bruno and Steffie were playing Dragonblood, the answer wouldn't be "oh, I can do more damage but only twice a day, and I have AC12 and the smallest number of HP in the party, so while I can do the most damage in one turn we're actually balanced" the answer would be "yeah, my character is better than yours because I've played the game longer and knew to pick the best class, sucks to be you."

                    When new players pick something they think is interesting and cool, and after several sessions go "hey, why is my awesome fire-swordsman who fights ON FIRE and shoots fireballs and went to the world's greatest military school just not as good as Shiny Peasant McSolar the self-taught swordswoman who fights by stabbing people with the pointy end? I mean, I'm not demanding to be better than her, but it seems I'm a bit lame compared to her?"... well, I don't want to be the ST who has to say "Yeah, his character is just better than yours. You get to be cool, she gets to be good." Because players want characters who are cool and good.

                    Personally, I'd probably solve this by, during character creation, going "okay, you can play a classic superhuman demi-god like Hercules or something, or a were-beast-monster, or a secret agent of Heaven, or an undead-antihero, or a demonic anti-hero. Check out these pages and pictures.
                    Oh, and also you could play a frankenstein monster or elemental aristocrat or robo-golem if you want, but please be aware that those are weaker characters and so you would playing on Hard Mode. I don't recommend Hard Mode for your first character."

                    Although even the ST may not realise this (our DnD DM's never played a barbarian, so didn't know to make sure Steffie knew barbarians don't do loads of damage compared to arcane casters), so I think the book possibly needs to clearly divide Celestials and Terrestrials, and say "STs and players, be aware that Celestials are more powerful than Terrestrials."
