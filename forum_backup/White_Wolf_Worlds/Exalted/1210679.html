      So my players came together and in a collaborative effort, did a three-die stunt of Establishing A Fact about the Inn/Tavern in the Township of Tenyio that is their home base. its weird, creative, and so thoroughly the kind of rich mystical strangeness I feel embodies Exalted:


      Mysteries of the Fox & Hound Inn

      There was a consistent system used to bring in new Hounds to work for Subdued Summer at the Fox and Hound Inn. First, Autumn Tempest, the Houndmaster, would set her sights on a potential candidate. This individual must be both a loyal vault of secrets and a ready voice of reason. They must possess a keen nose for danger and the ability to fight whatever that danger may be. They must be a friend to Subdued Summer – who can not tolerate the notion of solitude. And, finally, they must show a healthy fear of the nearby presence of a door in the basement of every Fox and Hound Inn – one emblazoned with a cedar tree and humming with unfamiliar energy. This final requirement on Tempest’s list was tested at the conclusion of every interview. Any individual who could walk down the basement steps and not feel a chill down their spine was immediately disqualified without explanation.

      Radiant Koi had met and exceeded every requirement. Summer had been pleased, as there was a new inn opening shortly in Tenyio and she did not feel like the Fox without a Hound to assist her in day to day tasks. Summer and Koi became fast friends, and the pair developed the habit of having drinks together late into the evenings at least once a week.
      It was during one of those first long talks that Radiant Koi asked about the magic in the tavern. The question wasn’t an unexpected one – every Hound asks Summer about it eventually. Most take their time in finding the courage to ask; the words lost behind some combination of primal fear and the suspicion that the answer may be more uncomfortable than the mystery of the place. Koi, as expected, overcame his trepidation in record time.

      “So.. what’s the deal here? The basement creeps me out,” he said, taking a long draw of his pipe.

      Summer arched an eyebrow and sat a moment, considering. “Once I tell you, this becomes your home. Quitting or leaving are no longer options. Are you prepared for that?”

      Koi nodded then, and the confidence he had in his decision was palpable. “Yes. Tell me.” At that, Subdued Summer walked towards the door to the basement and waited expectantly for Koi to follow, which he did after swiftly knocking back the remainder of his glass.

      They stood in front of a large, solid door. To the casual observer it, like the inn it resided in, was unremarkable. It stood eight feet tall and was made of solid cedar which had been stained a deep red. A tree was burned into the wood at its center, the branches of which stretched across the surface, reaching towards the sky. Summer reached into one of her many pockets and removed what appeared to be a piece of leather cord. She handed it to Koi and took a step back. Another test.
      “You may not see it, but this door has a lock. I’m sure you can find it.” Inspecting the cord in his hand, Koi realized there was a pendant – a small, green leaf which seemed to be carved with some skill out of some type of wood. He sighed, a symptom of impatience and drink, and set to work finding the so-called lock. After what felt like hours he discovered it: one of the leaves of the tree had a subtly different texture than the others. On a whim, Koi pressed the leaf into the indentation in question and was rewarded.

      The cedar tree on the door seemed to come alive before his eyes. Subtle color manifested itself in the burned lines, green leaves seemed to move in some gentle summer breeze, and Koi was sure he could hear the branches rustling against one another for a moment. As quickly as it began, the illusion had ceased and the door split cleanly down the middle. The newly transformed entrance opened inward, revealing what could only be described as a lush grotto.

      At the center of the room Radiant Koi and Subdued Summer now stood in was a tree which perfectly mirrored the living illusion on the door. It stood impossibly tall, reaching a height that would, under normal circumstances, be causing its branches to breach the floor of the tavern itself. Spiraling outward from the trunk was an intricate root system, each major vein leading towards a pair of double doors which resembled perfectly those found at the entrance of every Fox and Hound Inn. A pale, ethereal lavender light bathed every surface, and the sounds of the tavern – easily heard just outside the threshold- were eerily absent. Summer waited, giving Koi time to absorb and come to terms with what he was seeing. Once he was ready, she began.

      “I grew up in Nexus, as you know. What you may not know, though, is that my father is Singed Wing of Dawn – yes, the very same – and my mother is a Haltan woman known as Oft Dreaming Lily. They loved each other once, I imagine, but soon came to quite the disagreement when it came to how I should be raised and educated. My mother wanted me to embrace my roots and help my people in their never ceasing war. My father wanted me to forge my own path. You can see what I tried to choose.

      As a child, I primarily stayed with my father in the Bastion district of Nexus. Occasionally, Lily would decide motherhood suited her once more, and I’d be whisked away to Halta for some gathering or training or other important milestone she’d decided to foist upon me. The last time I saw her was my fifteenth name day. We’d had an incredible fight over things i won’t get into now, and she gave me a satchel made of the softest silk I had ever felt. Lily said it was time to choose – that she’d be waiting for me on the day I decided to live with my ‘true’ family. And then she left, my half-sister in tow.

      The satchel was filled with seeds from what some Haltan tribes refer to as their home tree. Trees are as important as air in that country – they are the definition of safety, because any who fall beneath the shelter of the canopy are food for the fae. The home tree is the one in which you’re born and the one here, in this room, apparently grew from the seed of mine.

      Now, that’s not to imply all seeds of this nature result in sorcery like this. It’s true that animals and plants in Halta grow to exceed expectations, probably because of nefarious dealings made with the faeries, but something like this simply should not have happened under normal circumstances. And yet, here we are!

      Anyway. Lily gave me this sad satchel of seeds and went home. I threw the thing in a drawer and didn’t think of it for the next three years until I was moving out of Father’s estate. Keep them in mind, will you? They’re the first puzzle piece in this situation we find ourselves in.

      Another piece in this culmination of magic and timing is my father’s family. Singed Wing of Dawn is, as most know, a sorcerer. He posses an extra bit of magic, though, that he’s never been truly forthright about – something in the blood, you see? As far as I can ascertain, that bit of ‘extra’ is common in most generations of this family. Sometimes it’s impressive, sometimes it isn’t, and sometimes it doesn’t show up at all – but it isn’t always tied to sorcery.

      Father or, at least, his family own quite a bit of property in Nexus. One of those parcels of land eventually became the original Fox and Hound, but when I was growing up I just knew it as my great uncle Dove of Morning’s winter house. He’d show up every year just ahead of Calibration and stay there until spring – said he wanted to be near his favorite niece during the holidays, and that he had important work to do. I’m still not entirely sure what that work was – all I know is one morning he was there, and the next he was gone. His house was a burned out shell and no one would tell me why. I’m still searching for specifics to this day – we never found a body.

      I used to hide there, in the ruins, when I was sad or angry or needed to be alone. I still liked to be alone in those days. Fewer ghosts at my heels. Sometimes Autumn was with me – she’d been just like family even in those days. So, when I turned 18, Father gave me the land and a blank check to do what I wanted with it. I decided to honor the memories granted me by my late uncle and sought to create a home for strangers at the holidays. He used to call me Little Kit, and Autumn Tempest was, to him, a loyal hound who made friends with one she was meant to chase.

      The early days of planning weren’t easy. I struggled to balance the embarrassment I feel to this day with a yearning to be accepted by my mother’s family. And so, in designing the inn, I made some choices – irises, a saying from Father’s family written in forest tongue, that kind of thing. And one last touch: a single seed from satchel Lily had given me. That piece of home felt important and I’m not one to question my own intuition, so, I surreptitiously placed the seed in the exact center of the foundation of the inn. The spot held a vast amount of energy and my nerves were singing with it after I completed what I understand now was some sort of ritual.

      Once the Nexus Fox and Hound was completed, I did my final tour with Autumn, as one does, to make sure everything was as planned. And it was, except for one thing: this room. There was a door which was certainly not included in the plans, just like the one you unlocked for me today. We couldn’t get it open and believe me, we tried everything. Hammers, saws, fire – you name it. That is, until we built the second inn.

      I placed another seed in the foundation of the next one – and why not? It seemed like a safe enough tradition to that point. Autumn and I again did the walkthrough, as we always do, although this time we brought Dierdre along. There it was again – the same door, in the exact same spot. We tried again to open it and it seemed to react when the satchel of seeds hovered near it. On a whim, Dierdre took one and placed it into the odd leaf – just as you did today, although it’s different on every door, and the thing opened to this place.

      No, not a copy of this room – to this actual space. Each inn has a doorway which leads to this place. I can meet with other Hounds and conduct business within these walls – although if anyone besides myself spends too much time here, they come down with what can only be described as a short lived flu. The symptoms become worse if the person in question visits more than an hour or two a month – that seems to be the balance which needs to be struck.

      The other thing is the doors here. Each time I open a new inn, another set grows from the root system and a portal is created here. They all lead to different inns – you can tell which one by counting the irises in each cluster on the stained glass panels. It’s terribly convenient, until it isn’t. If you were to walk through that set there, you’d be in Nexus in what is, in reality, about five minutes.

      Time feels different, though, in the spaces between. It’s easy to get lost – especially if you don’t have that key with you to prove you are allowed to be travel there. When you first enter – and you will, all Hounds do eventually – you may be greeted by a spirit known as Rain’s Cadence. She’s friendly, for the most part, but she expects favors in exchange for her guidance and companionship. I think she may be a relative of mine, or at least friends of a relative, as she occasionally makes requests or asks questions revolving around my family. I have my suspicions she may be in contact with Oft Dreaming Lily – so whatever you do, be careful what you tell her. And don’t make her any promises you can’t or won’t keep – I made that mistake once and was sick in bed for weeks. If it wasn’t for Resplendent Jade Elixir, I may very well have died for my foolishness.

      Cadence likes unique stones and exotic, aromatic herbs and teas. I’d make a note of that, if I were you – often she’s happy enough with those offerings to forget about her other requests. I worry one day she’ll ask me for a great favor by way of one of you – so, please, use the portals sparingly and let me know when you do so.

      And don’t ever speak of the key or this room to anyone else. It’s one rule there is never an acceptable reason to break. Any questions?”

      Radiant Koi had several, and once he seemed satisfied, Summer stretched and stood. Cunning Bite echoed her movements at her feet. “Let’s get a drink, shall we?”

                              ::nods:: I have to say, as a Storyteller, I've always experienced a preponderance of "Most Creative Players = Most likely to be the players with PCs deeply involved with sorcery or necromancy." Storytelling arcs like "Yeah, let's steal a Demon" are a joy. You see where I described my first Storytelling of a PC summoning a Third Circle Demon, above.                               Logistically, do you find it difficult having more than one PC deeply into sorcery at once? I've had differing experiences, and I'm curious. Not so much talking about mechanics, so much as the sorcerers capabilities taking things in an unexpected direction surprisingly fast.

                                  No, mostly because each initiation is different and the needs and desires of each PC are different regarding what they want out of Sorcery. I am never caught off guard regarding solutions to problems because I build all my plots with Workings in mind when I construct them. My plots tend to be presented with a sense of urgency. This urgency often males the PCs feel that using a Working to solve the problem involves an element of risk they often do not want to take. So they find more direct solutions to accomplish their goals rather than always relying on the Arcane.
