    Blaque Yes, I know that there are some inter-edition inconsistencies with the setting piece. Some of it I feel is appropriate (like not getting bogged down with whether there are exactly 300 solar exaltations), and some of it might possibly merit an edit (like whether or not Gaia’s body forms the surface of the world). That all being said, if there are some particular facts that you think would be important to change, by all means please let me know.         For me the big ones were these, if it helps if you ever edit. Overall it's great, but I think it's worth highlighting I guess:
        - Primordial as a term in itself isn't actually used in 3e. It might someday, but right now isn't.

        - Gaia, as you note, didn't use her body for Creation. The Dragons aren't her souls or at least, not necessarily her souls in 3e either. They're her Chidlren and obviously important to Creation's well, creation, but we don't know much beyond that this time around.

        - The five Reclamation Yozi in 2e weren't the only Yozi then, but the way you phrased it in the episode kind of implied they did. This is just a slight editting bug. In 3e though, it's worth remembering that Infernals are not tied to speciifc Yozis but draw on Hellish themes and the Yozis more broadly.

        - There are actually about 300-400 Lunars this time around.

        - Autochthon's involvement in the creation of the Exalted is not so clear this time around. Note that the 3e coreobok more or less says the Sun started the get-together and doesn't mention him at all. I'd mostly jut de-emphasize the hows of Exaltation and such and focus on the whole "Gods got together and used humans as mistletoe" thing. I think here things would have been better to go like with the Deathlords. The specifics are less important than the broaders troke actions and events.

        - I'm guessing when we know more about Liminals and Exigents you'll put htem in but yeah, here it seems a bit a good idea to omit. Not relaly an issue, just something I observed.

        Hope this is helpful at all. I don't want to come accross as overly pedantic, but I like what you're doing and want it to be as good as it can be.

                                                So randomly listening through another episode, your one on the Caul was neat. Bit late on things, but some comments that came up:

                                                On Iconic Characters
                                                The term I use for characters like Chejob Kejak, Sha'a Oka, or Mnemon is less "metaplot" character and more just "canon character". I think the way to think of it is metaplot in the context of World of Darkness meant that there was an ongoing plot, book to book, whcih changed the setting over time. Things like that were how in Vampire: the Masquerade, the Assamites broke the Tremere cruse, Salubri antitribu and Harbingers of Skulls joined the Sabbat, Ravnos had the Week of Nightmares, and so on. And notably, events would happen in one book and the books from then on would incoreprate that into canon. Some of these happened in sourcebooks, but others did happen in novels or pieces of ficiton.

                                                Exalted has not really ever had this, save I would say the Scarelt Empress bredcrumb trail that ramped up through 2e. But most of the game is set in RY 768 and things are written form this "Day Zero" framework much of the time. Events in novels were not canon in 1e at least, and tehre was actually a lot of consternation when there were attempts to do so. But I think metaplot doesn't fit htese characters. They're assumed to exist in the world canonically though, which is why I use canon isntead of metaplot.

                                                Another term used is signature characters, which is something often I find ascribed to canonical characters in Exalted and other WW games. This is often used specifically for characters who are representitive of splats in teh way you note iconics. In 1e they were the ones you saw in splats, as well as the different Exalts having a "general" sig' to them a sa hwole (Lilith for Lunars, Tepet Ejava for Dragon-Blooded, Prince of Shadows for Abyssals, and Chejob Kejak for Sidereals). They featured in more fiction and a lot of cover art and some of the stock images. 2e used mostly teh same ones, but also did kind of try to draw a lot of previously-named characters from the Caste and Aspect books and other soruces into art, ficiton, and references hwere ti could. I think actually 2e suffered a bit from over-use fo them. In 3e, the sigs are the characters you see in teh splats for Solars and DBs, who are new this editon and are a bit more back to being sample characters.

                                                Kind of pedantic, but thought would have been kind of cool to add.

                                                Pronouns
                                                Something I did hear I wanted to note is that Exalted actually is kind of purposeful with the use of "she" for pronouns that other WW games aren't. Other WW games alternate between she and he, defaulting to she first. But Exalted actually does use it for mechanical purposes that you note. In Charms, the Charm's user/active character is always using the feminine pronouns. "The Exalted uses her power to..." or "She rolls her..." sort of thing. The target of a power's effects, by contrast, get the masculine ones. So Charms foten have "She uses her Charm against his defense" sort of format and does this pretty consistently in the Charms, Martial Arts, Sorcery, and Evocations and post-creobook books. The only spots it doesn't do it as consistently is sytems, but I think beyond that, it's always been she-as-default for generic mechanics.

                                                This is actually to me a perk. I have been finding that something of the flip-flopping in CofD books has kind of gotten on my nerves, as I find Exalted's being consisten there helps in readability when you know it's there.

                                                The Caul
                                                Generally cool stuff and observations on the way the Caul works. I loved the Fire Shrine imagery best of your ideas of what they looked like. As for adventures, I think that while the goal is weird for PCs, you note pretty well I think it'd be a great game just for the adventure. Glory in itself or securing a great marraige prospect I think would also be cool motivations for it.

                                                Anywho, fun stuff. Looking forward for the next one.
