                Melee

                Number of Charms: 38
                By Ability requirement: 2/2/6/7/21
                By Essence requirement: 11/13/11/1/2
                Nonstandard dice tricks: Enemy rolls -1 damage for each 1 rolled, bonus dice and successes for rolling a straight, attacker loses one success for each 1 or 2 rolled, free full Excellency

                Themes: Hit anything, and don’t get hit. Kill creatures of darkness – whatever those are – pretty dead.

                Charms:
                1. Gain a success and recurring 1s.
                2. Add post-soak damage or transfer extra attack successes to a Decisive damage roll.
                3. After reducing an enemy from Initiative above yours to Init below yours, make a Reflexive attack.
                4. Spend your anima to make an extra attack.
                5. Ignore Defense penalties; gain 1i for parrying.
                6. Ignore Defense penalties for one turn; opponent does reduced damage.
                7. Defend another reflexively.
                8. When Defending Other, grant extra Initiative to your charge with a Distract Gambit.
                9. Counterattack before damage is rolled against you.
                10. Call your weapon to hand with a successful roll.
                11. Store a weapon in Elsewhere.
                12. Add bonus successes if you roll a straight.
                13. Treat your Initiative as being three higher for the purposes of #3.
                14. Make a big magical flurry. Can chain off of #4.
                15. Ignore “certain” penalties to Defense.
                16. Parry the unparryable.
                17. Attacker loses 1 success for each 1 or 2 rolled.
                18. Gain 1i when parrying as part of a Defend Other.
                19. Attacks may not be applied to the character you’re defending.
                20. Counterattack every attack for the next round.
                21. Summon a magical glowing sword.
                22. Make a Melee attack at Medium range.
                23. Make a Decisive attack at Short range, ignoring hardness and cover.
                24. Roll Decisive damage dice against Creatures of Darkness on a hit, even if that hit was Withering(!).
                25. Gain 1m for every 10 rolled when using #12.
                26. Do extra damage to a Crashed opponent.
                27. Gain extra attacks with #14.
                28. Free full Excellency.
                29. Make a Withering counterattack, turning any stolen damage into Decisive dice.
                30. Reflexively Clash with an attack.
                31. Combine #29 and #30.
                32. Extra damage on all attacks for the rest of the scene.
                33. Raise defense against creatures of darkness; burn them if they touch you.
                34. Gain extra damage against creatures of darkness; treat people you hate as creatures of darkness.
                35. Shoot a bolt of energy at an enemy; works better against creatures of darkness.
                36. Decisively strike all opponents at close range.
                37. If you kill anyone with #36, make a withering strike against all opponents at close range. If you Crash anyone, you can pop #36 again.
                38. When you use #16, accumulate extra Initiative for later uses of #16.

                A lot of familiar faces here – many Charm names and general effects repeated from 2e. (I played a lot more Melee characters than I did most other Abilities, so this may be a general truth.)

                Solar Counterattack (#9) raises again the question, “What happens if you kill someone with your counterattack – do they roll damage against you, or not?”

                Call the Blade (#10) is scaled waaaaaaay back, presumably so Disarming doesn’t feel like a chump move.

                Rising Sun Slash (#12) is about my least favorite dice trick so far. Just… no, game. “Check for a straight” is too much complexity for something that’s probably almost identical to some double-Xs effect.

                Agile Dragonfly Blade (#13) is one of the most… limited… powers I’ve seen so far. I guess more opportunities to use OWTB is always good, but that’s an awfully narrow band of usefulness.

                So, Fivefold Bulwark Stance (#15) does one thing very right, and one thing very wrong. The thing it does right is give you a reason to be glad you still have Dipping Swallow: it makes that Charm into a more efficient Excellency + Initiative generator, even with 5BS running. What it does wrong is… well… see, the Charm itself only says it removes “certain” penalties. So, what’s a “certain” penalty? For that, we have to consult the sidebar on the next page, which informs us that it means wound, grappling, onslaught, and some crippling, but not blindness, stunning, or poison. Why on earth is the effect of the Charm in the sidebar, and not in the actual Charm? It’s not like the sidebar is applicable to anything else other than the Charm.

                Heavenly Guardian Defense (#16) is back! And isn’t actually a parry, weirdly – it’s more like a soak effect than anything else. It’s a bit confusingly worded, but the Charm seems to have three modes: spend Initiative to negate damage, spend motes to make the unblockable blockable, and block infinite damage.

                (The art for the past few pages has been much more dynamic and active than the rather stilted, posed art I complained about earlier. It’s also pretty anime, which is probably not unrelated to the first point.)

                Hail-Shattering Practice (#17) is potent. Dodge may get the most out of not being hit, but Melee has a better defensive suite – late-step success-stealing (or adding) is about as good as it gets, and Melee has that in spades. Expect to roll about 40% successes fewer than normal against a good Meleeist.

                It occurs to me that a meleeist Defending Other on a brawler would be all kinds of terrifying.

                Ready in Eight Directions Stance (#20) is an odd one – since it’s Simple, it consumes your turn just standing there and looking threatening. It combos well with War Lion’s Stance (#7), as has been pointed out to me, but by itself it’s a little lackluster.

                Ah ha ha, Glorious Solar Saber (#21) can do the “melt through a blast door” thing from Episode 1. I really like the ability to give your summoned weapons evocations, though I’m not sure that makes up for having to waste a Simple action summoning them. (Remember, your soul sword thing is just a logical extension of your skill at swording, and not something to be mistaken for a distinct magical technique.)

                Edge of Morning Sunlight (#24) seems to make a pretty fundamental rule of the game – that you know you’re safe against a Withering attack. It doesn’t feel implausible that you could, by E3 or so, Wither someone half to death with just this Charm. Of course, it only works on CoDs… until you grab Sharp Light of Judgment Stance (#34), which makes it work on anyone you hate (like, say, the villain of your campaign).

                Actually, speaking of SLOJS, it references the minimum damage increase from Corona of Radiance (#33)… which, er, doesn’t actually seem to be a thing; CoR doesn’t do anything to your minimum damage.

                I really love the idea of the cycle of repeatedly triggering Heaven Sword Flash (#36) into Bright Reaving Circle (#37) and back again, but I’m suspicious that it won’t actually get to cycle very often in play – how many separate people are going to be mobbing you at close range, and also likely to be Crashed/killed?

                Melee is, overall, a very strong tree. Not as much pure lethality as some others, but you will hit, and you’re pretty likely to avoid being hit in return. Very solid; lots of fun effects.

                Yes: Almost everything. One Weapon Two Blows is great as a finally useful double-attack.
                No: Agile Dragonfly Blade. The 5BS sidebar.
                Just Plain Weird: There are a lot of multiattacks here. Like, a lot. Wasn’t that something we were getting rid of, for the most part?

                                    Occult

                                    Number of Charms: 36
                                    By Ability requirement: 1/3/6/4/22
                                    By Essence requirement: 13/9/8/3/3
                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Free full Excellency

                                    Themes: Battle spirits; bind spirits; perform exorcisms; have a bunch of spirit minions; spirits spirits spirits spirits spirits.

                                    Charms:
                                    1. See spirits.
                                    2. A mental alarm goes off when there’s a spirit nearby.
                                    3. Permanent bonus dice on attempts to investigate or follow spirits.
                                    4. Strike a spirit.
                                    5. Do extra damage to spirits for the scene.
                                    6. Do aggravated damage to spirits, and steal motes from them. Permanently destroy spirits and, uh, imordials-Pray. (Shhh, don’t mention them by name!)
                                    7. An immaterial enemy becomes material when you strike him.
                                    8. Scenelong bonus dice to attacks vs. spirits. Very cheap Infinite Occult Mastery.
                                    9. An attack that would kill you doesn’t. You need to heal before using this again, though.
                                    10. Allow a spirit ally to materialize for 1m.
                                    11. Speak Old Realm and other spirit language. Bonus successes to shaping actions for the scene(!).
                                    12. Free full Occult Excellency.
                                    13. Detect shaping actions, sorcery, Evocations, and spirit sanctums for one instant.
                                    14. Temporarily absorb the powers of a spirit killed via #6.
                                    15. Force a spirit or spiritual disease(?) from its host.
                                    16. Steal motes equal to damage done to a spirit.
                                    17. Spirits at short range must materialize or retreat.
                                    18. Immaterial beings must pay Willpower to attack you.
                                    19. Trap and materialize a spirit for a few rounds.
                                    20. Materialize several spirit allies.
                                    21. Learn several thaumaturgical rituals. Er, what’s a thaumaturgical ritual?
                                    22. Notice disguises and shapeshifting with #13.
                                    23. Spiritually enter another’s body to do battle with possessing forces (or to inflict possessing forces on the target).
                                    24. #18 works on unshaped fair folk and similar beings.
                                    25. Fuel your Charms and spells with motes stolen from a spirit trapped via #19.
                                    26. Gain social influence bonuses against a spirit trapped via #19.
                                    27. Spirits to an enormous distance are forced to materialize and remain material.
                                    28. Commit motes to learn spirit Charms.
                                    29. When at full anima, #13 automatically activates.
                                    30. When using #23, detect signs of past spiritual predation; come to subject’s aid if such predation recurs.
                                    31. Reduce Essence of a target trapped via #19.
                                    32. Spend your anima to gather motes to spend on thinky-actions.
                                    33. Create a spirit.
                                    34. Terrestrial Circle Sorcery.
                                    35. Celestial Circle Sorcery.
                                    36. Solar Circle Sorcery.


                                    Spirit-Draining Stance (#5) would be a great Charm if it was Reflexive. It feels a little harder to justify spending a Simple action on 1W damage for the rest of the scene – you could probably land a Withering attack with your action and do more damage than this will give you.

                                    Ghost Eating Technique (#6) used to be an “every attack” kind of thing. Now, I’m not sure if you even want it on every Decisive – until you hit the -4 health levels, it’s a net negative, and Aggravated damage doesn’t matter most if you’re planning to kill the spirit.

                                    Man, Ancient Tongue understanding (#11) buries the lead. The “talk to spirits” thing is cool , but this is a pretty substantial Reflexive scenelong buff to sorcery – that seems like the bigger deal.

                                    All-Encompassing Sorcerer’s Sight (#13) has two enormous, substantial nerfs applied: (1) It’s Instant duration, and (2) it can’t see Exalted Charms. I somewhat understand (2) – that always took some of the guesswork out of fighting a new character – but I’m not sure I follow the motivation for (1).

                                    Material Exegesis Prana (#20) gives you all your demons if you roll 666. Thaaaat’s a little too cute for me.

                                    Soul Projection Method (#23) starts off strong, with a clear roll… which immediately switches to a dramatic action whose effects are Storyteller dependent and completely devoid of examples. I’m less worried about encountering this one as a player than I am as a Storyteller!

                                    Spirit-Drawing Oculus (#32) pretty clearly should be a Charm with a “3a” in its cost. Instead, the anima cost is described in the text of the Charm. It’s otherwise pretty cool – if nothing else, it’s a big extra source of motes for shaping sorcery. (Which… we don’t actually know how that works yet.) Again, though, this makes that one “Spend motes to purge your anima” charm look pretty thin – and the Medicine “spend HLs for motes” effect, for that matter.

                                    There is something wrong with the torso of the young lady climbing out of the pool in this picture, I think? It looks… oddly thick, to me. Like, she’s not particularly heavyset – her torso just looks disproportionately thick, front-to-back. I am definitely not an anatomy guy, though, so maybe that’s just me.

                                    The Mandala-binding Charms are just a little odd, to me. Like, I don’t know that they’re bad – I’m just not sure what you’re supposed to do with them. Hold a spirit down, while the rest of the party beats on it, I guess? The “use them as mote batteries” effects are a little bit peculiar, given the difficulty in actually pulling this off. These aren’t bad effects, to be clear – there are just a lot of Charms living in this conceptual space, and I don’t have an immediate vision of what to do with it.

                                    Sorcery is cool, though. So, sorcery is the first thing so far that isn’t subject to Supernal advantages – that is, you have to actually be E5 to throw Solar-circle spells. I think I can follow that motivation. On the other hand, sorcery is a pretty good buy now. Buying TCS gets you a free spell and a “shaping ritual,” whatever that is – further, that one spell you get becomes your “control spell,” which is presumably a good thing (apparently some Charms, though none actually in Occult, may reference it).

                                    Overall, Occult looks quite good. I don’t know what everything here is for, as I say above, but about the worst you can say of anything is, “Eh, don’t think I’d take that.” I suspect an Occult 3 sorcery dip is going to be very common, this edition.

                                    Yes: Sorcery looks very hopeful so far. We’ll see!
                                    No: Having a Charm whose effect is purely Storyteller determined, with no guidelines for what that should look like.
                                    Just Plain Weird: Uncanny Shroud Defense (#8). It’s a perfect defense… of sorts? Occult is an odd place for it, and the flavor doesn’t quite hold up (if I’m “stepping between worlds,” shouldn’t Spirit-Cutting Attack still kill me?) – but I do approve of adding more panic buttons and ways to stop your non-combat character from unexpectedly dying.

                                                        ... Thank you. That is all so very encouraging.

                                                        A bit of a reflection, as we are a little more than halfway through, here: I really, really wish these Charms were in a simpler, cleaner format. Like... I am inevitably Crunch Guy, in my groups. That is my schtick. I love some good, meaty rules.

                                                        And yet these Charms are a slog. That's not a sensation I got in 2e - probably in part because, if this was 2e, I'd be done with Charms by now. (Which had its own problems, obviously.) I don't know if the Charm fluff just isn't quite doing it for me, or if it's the way fluff and crunch intermingle and blend over a couple of paragraphs, or what. I do know that the vagueness in some of the rules isn't helping here - nor is the fact that some (though not as many as I had thought!) of the Charms are pure dice pool/success manipulators.

                                                        I can grant that there seems to be some role for that in 3e - but I really wish that such effects were short. And a clean, clear mechanical terminology would really help with that process. As-is, there's a lot of, "Wait, what did that actually say? Which part of that was actually the rules?" that really shouldn't be necessary.

                                                        That's probably not a universal reaction, but man. I want to have - as I think Holden once said - the "kid in the candy store" reaction to these Charms. And there's some really great candy here - but it's spaced out between things that feel more like vegetables. In general - and this applies at the system level as much as it does the Charm level - this is a very maximalist approach, with a very "Why use one widget when two will do?" feel to it, and that's just not quite working for me. (Combat is an easy example - it seems like land and naval combat should use similar rules, with perhaps some slightly different widgets, such as a boat's maneuverability or speed. Instead, we have two different resolution engines for the two subsystems.) That's a lot of conceptual overhead, and it's a little wearying.

                                                        So there are a lot of things that would help with this. Compressing that same - what, five lines? That same five line header at the start of each Charm down a bit would help - prereq Charms and Abilities don't really need to be separate entries. Reducing, or italicizing, or somehow distinguishing fluff from rules would help. Ditching the "Here's what the Charm actually does" sidebar or two would help. Better keywording, and better technical language in general, would help. Reducing redundant verbiage that boils down to "This has a cost of 3a" or "Supernal works as normal" would help. And, well - one of the things I had hoped, when I heard they were going to have to find some pages to cut, was that some of the dead wood in the Charms would get pruned out. It sounds like that didn't happen - and there are definitely some effects that seem like they should be upgrades, or fused effects, or something.

                                                        Again, personal taste, etc. - but two hundred pages is too much for this chapter.
