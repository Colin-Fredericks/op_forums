    What are your mortal campaign stories?

    03-05-2020, 05:49 PM
    I've heard from a few different people on this forum that Exalted 3E is actually a lot of fun with mortal characters. That sounds cool to me, but I don't really know how to picture it.

    Does anyone want to share, either some specific stories from their mortal campaigns, or some of the general premises of who the characters were, and what they did?

    Or, in a pinch, ideas you think would be cool to do in a mortal campaign?

    Trying to run a game based on this segment from the core book seemed fun to me, but probably not really possible until the Exigents book comes out:

    With the return of the Exigence, heroes come to Great Forks from every corner of Creation to vie for the Second Breath, while Exigents empowered elsewhere have followed to meet with their own kind. The city’s triumvirate has offered patronage to these heroes, and the services they perform in exchange gives the city new military and economic might—strength it will need to confront the threat of the Deathlord, Mask of Winters.

      I decided to start my first Ex3 campaign as a pure mortals one to ease my group into the system-- the party would exalt at the conclusion of the first arc, so that everyone could get a grip on basic system concepts like stunts and initiative. Even after Exalting, I only dolled out the free starting Charms slowly, to keep things under some semblance of control. At character creation, I had everyone pick a Supernal skill and distribute their Attribute and Ability dots according to normal rules, with two exceptions: Attributes were capped at 4 dots, and your Supernal skill could be bought up to 5 dots. Intimacies and Specialties were normal. They got the full 10 dots of Merits, but I banned overtly magical things like Artifact and instituted an in-game delay on anything that brought in extra NPCs. (I also added a 5-dot Merit to give you access to one Terrestrial circle spell)

      It went pretty well, all things considered. Stunts and Willpower became more important, but characters were stronger than I expected-- on the first night the party beat the crap out of two size 3 battle groups (albeit with poor drill and crappy base soldiers) and an elite trooper commanding them and an E1 Abyssal (with Evasion 4, Soak 8, 12 dice to attack, and a couple low-level Charms pulled from Black Claw and Dreaming Pearl Courtesan). At various other points they persuaded a dead Solar to ally with them, infiltrated a fortress disguised as bird people (and smuggled out a slave), and pulled some pretty well arranged strategic warfare.

      My biggest regret is probably allowing supernatural Merits-- almost everyone took something from the list, which created a rather more mutated party than I really like for Exalted. Otherwise it was a good time-- Ex3 flows a lot smoother when you don't have to constantly stop and factor in charms.

          AS one of the resident "mortal guys" here, I can safely say that mortals can pretty much do most things Exalted can do, they just have to be smarter about it. You can have kickass combats, moving social scenes, sorcery, politicking, and more.

          I have run several "mortal campaigns", from the Sengoku Jidai-ish Hundred Kingdoms, where one of my players turned a surprise victory in war into political success, earning a major fiefdom, then gradually using politics and diplomacy to get the rest of the Hundred Kingdoms under their sway, declaring themselves Emperor and planning on invading Vaneha, to not!Post-Roman Arthurian Britain in the North after the withdrawal of the Shogunate.

          It all depends on what you, and your players, want to do. So, in order to answer more fully, I need to know what you want to know.

                          I ran one mortal game that lasted 3-4 sessions. Everyone was a mortal from the Blessed Isle, I think one was an unexalted Dynast. They were sent on a mission to plant the flag of the Realm up north on Frosty Dorito Island and investigate the previous group who'd gone up there to do the same and never returned.

                          On their way to the north of the Blessed Isle they were attacked by a small army of bandits, suspiciously large numbers of them, a size 2-3 battlegroup. They'd somehow found out that this dynastic convoy didn't actually have any DBs on it and wanted to loot it, but the 4 PCs sent them all packing.

                          Then they sailed northward, and in the misty seas of the north encountered a school of fog-sharks, which terrified the crew and send them below decks while the PCs battled them above, including suplexing one into the forcastle.

                          Then once at the north base they investigated the former Realm colony up there and found it predictably abandoned, not even ghosts. Then they inflated an airship procured by a northern Satrapy and flew it further north, on the way finding that house Peleps had reason not to want this excursion to be successful, and sent an exalted assassin. She battled the mortal heroes and was killed unceremoniously. Not before damaging the gas-bag and forcing them to land to make repairs, where they had to deal with the local wildlife.

                          At last they reached the northern mountains and found the remains of a lost civilization of inhuman creatures. Through exploration and research their loremaster concluded that this must have been a civilization of sorcerers, one where rather than sorcery being a rare miracle, being without it was vanishingly rare. In disbelief they continued past the first mountains into the incredibly high ones behind, finding the other Realm expedition there. What was left of it anyway.

                          They found two people remaining, a sorcerer and his bodyguard, both of whom were a bit crazy in their own right. They said they found a crazed titan sealed away in the time-before the time-before the time-before by this sorcerous civilization, but it was on the cusp of getting out. The only way to stop it was to go further down and repair the runes there. Unfortunately everyone agreed this was insane and just decided to leave, the last scene of the game was the mountain cracking open and a great hand swatting their airship from the sky like a fly.
