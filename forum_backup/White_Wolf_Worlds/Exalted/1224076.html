    The Sea of Oblivion, and What It Reflects

    06-02-2018, 12:24 AM
    Oblivion is an infinite sea of black water at the bottom of the Underworld.

    The tombs of the never born stand above it on stone pillars. Every year those pillars lower by an inch. Every year, those waters recede by an inch.

    It is known that, in the underworld, Mount Meru is a great wound in the world, a staircase spiraling down towards that infinite sea. The sea that all things flow towards, which gives nothing back that falls into it, hidden from the world and underworld alike by a great and forbidding labyrinth.

    Those ghosts of the dead that lay eyes upon the waters of oblivion often slip into those waters and vanish forever, leaving no trace. Only the living can look upon the waters of oblivion without feeling a call to fall in to them.

    Within those black waters, there are 150 lights shining beneath the surface: the solar shards, broken from the jade prison, that were sacrificed to Oblivion, and as they were sacrificed and slept beneath those black waters, their dark reflections rose to take their place.

    The abyssal exaltations are dark mirrors to the solar exaltation. They are gifts from Oblivion. The price that was paid for them still remains within oblivion, in the dark waters, for any foolish enough to retrieve it.

    The abyssal exalted are the eyes and ears of oblivion. It’s sees through them. It knows the world through them. It hungers through them.

    Those who sold their death to oblivion in exchange for Exaltation will never know peace from it. Always the void sees through them, and when it sees life, the black waters stir with hunger, and grow turbulent.

    The secret of the abyssal exalted is this: their exultation bears no great curse because it was created long after the curse was laid down. No solar shard beats with in their heart. Something stranger, from a sea that has never known the sun, rides within them.

    What you must know of the primordial ones is this: we invented death and mortality and age and sickness to free ourselves from them, and only those who never knew the rule of the primordial ones question the wisdom of this choice.

    Only outside the garden of Eden could we find a weapon that could kill them, and we left without regrets.

                          To me, Oblivion is best as a state of being. Or rather, non-being.

                          When a spirit is permanently destroyed, it has been Obliviated.

                          When a human’s higher soul enters Lethe, their identity is Obliviated.

                          When a higher soul is permanently destroyed, the soul itself is Obliviated.

                          The Neverborn desperately seek Obliviation to free them from the pain of undeath.

                          The Well of Oblivion could be a hole that stretches down infinitely or actually lead to a point where anything entering it becomes Obliviated. No one knows, and if it isn’t, then the hole becomes a metaphor for the futility of the Neverborn’s efforts.

                          Deathlords would derive no power from Oblivion any more than someone could draw power from Elsewhere; their power comes from the Neverborn.

                          The Abyssal Exalted draw power from their own Exaltations, which draw power from nothing, but have been twisted to reflect the Neverborn and the Deathlords, and thus the nature of the Underworld and ghosts.

                          Oblivion is not a force. It is not a creature with any will or desire. It is just the state of “not-existing”. Total destruction. Annihilation.

                          That is true “nothingness”. “Nothing” has no power or motives. It is nothing.
