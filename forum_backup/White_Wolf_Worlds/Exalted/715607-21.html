                    I believe it goes like this:

                    --Person A performs an ambush on person B.
                    --A rolls stealth, B rolls awareness.
                    ----If B wins, no ambush is possible. Battle proceeds without stealth considerations after Join Battle.
                    ----If A wins, ambush is possible. Proceed to Join Battle.
                    --A and B roll Join Battle.
                    -----If A wins, they Ambush B (0 Defense for B)
                    -----If person B wins, then A's attack becomes a Surprise Attack (-2 Defense to B)

                        Gazellecarp – er, Dreaming Pearl Courtesan Style

                        Essence to Master: 3
                        Armor: None.
                        Weapons: War fans, whips, improvised weapons, unarmed.

                        Shtick: High-synergy style. Fight at short range with whatever is lying around your boudoir; your improvised weapons and bathrobe will be as good as real artifact tools, if not better. Mix social influence into your defenses; surprise your enemy with punishing damage, or by turning into an enormous flying deer-giraffe-gazelle-carp thing. Wait, what?

                        Charms:
                        1. +1 Evasion, or +1 success to Disengage. +2 if your Appearance is higher than their Resolve. +1i if you succeed.
                        2. Add weapon’s accuracy Withering damage, or treat a mundane object as a light weapon.
                        3. Your clothes are light armor. Spend motes for temporary bonus soak.
                        4. Form. #2 and #3 grant artifact weapons or armor. Make attacks from Short range.
                        5. Your weapon tags are more effective than normal.
                        6. Decisive damage roll gains anywhere from double-10s to double-7s, depending on how many triggers are satisfied. Activated after attack succeeds.
                        7. Bonus successes to entice your enemy. Steal 1i in combat, if you succeed. Triggers #6.
                        8. –(Appearance) to attempts to Rush, stop a Disengage, or control a grapple. Triggers #6.
                        9. Turn into a sort of deer-giraffe-gazelle-carp thing. Yes, really. Fly. Enhance all other Charms of this style in substantial ways. Fade from reality if you take any damage to your -4 level. Yes, this means you’re dead.

                        *snerks* This picture of the lady zapping the guy in armor does not have compression issues. This picture is a compression issue – it looks like it was blown up from a thumbnail.

                        Again, Demure Carp Feint (#1) is pretty unimpressive in its base form. Actually, I’ll go one better there – if you’re an Exalt, it seems like there is almost no reason to use the base form, here; it’s 3m for a bonus you could buy for 2m. (I suppose if you think the 1i is worth the extra mote and the Charm pick?) The high Appearance version is better, but I’m not instantly wowed.

                        At a glance, the only benefit of the “make a weapon” function of Elegant Weapon Repertoire (#2) is to gain access to various tags – your hands, after all, are already mundane light weapons, so you aren’t gaining much there. I can’t tell how useful the tags are, since we haven’t gotten to that part of the book yet. Again, though, not enormously impressed yet.

                        Pearlescent Filigree Defense (#3), on the other hand, is so much better than the Solar armor-donning Charms. 1m Reflexive? Yes, please.

                        Ahhh, and we get to the form (#4), and some things make sense – on-tap artifacts for a handful of motes are a much better use of the preceding Charms, and the focus on Disengaging makes better sense for a fighter who can hit from range. Okay, that’s at least a little bit mollifying.

                        Flurry of August Leaves (#5) says it “enhances” an attack – but the Charm is Simple; shouldn’t this be “provides an attack with enhanced…”? Either way, again, EWR looks better in hindsight – we have at least some sense of what to do with those tags. And some of them are pretty excellent – the Shield power, for instance, is basically a turnlong non-Charm +2 Defense. Unraveling Loose Threads is, again, strictly better than Snake’s armor bypass – cheaper, longer-lasting.

                        Vindictive Concubine’s Pillow-Book (#6) is a vicious little Charm; the late-step activation means you can, potentially, double your Decisive damage as a surprise. It’s not cheap, but, y’know, double damage after hitting. There’s a lot of overlap between these Charms, where they play better together than they do apart – I wasn’t sold immediately, but they’re growing on me.

                        All right, having finished the style, I have much more positive reactions than I did at the start. Everything about Dreaming Pearl works together, much like Black Claw – rather than separate powers, many of these effects are only useful in conjunction with each other. That still makes the first 30 XP or so pretty painful of an investment, but by the end, you’ve got a nasty package put together. Your only real defense is being at Short range, though; you have some tools to hold enemies away, but anyone who can close in despite that (or, for that matter, shoot you) is going to cause problems. The pinnacle effect (#9) makes all of the earlier stuff even better (and, you know, is hilarious – this is one of Dr. Moran’s, once upon a time, isn’t it?), but with a steep risk – I wouldn’t even consider going into this form off the “all my -2s are full” trigger unless I was sure I was dead anyway.


                        Steel Devil Style

                        Essence to Master: 3
                        Armor: Light, medium, and none.
                        Weapons: Dual-wielded swords only.

                        Shtick: Store Initiative in your second sword, using it to boost damage. Unleash massive area attacks from inside a superior defensive form, chaining into enormous overwhelm-the-target’s-Defense combos.

                        Okay, before we get started: Sidebar explains that you keep a stock of Initiative in your offhand sword; this is called your Charge. You can move (2 or Essence)i into your sword whenever you land a Withering hit, to a max of (Dex + Essence).

                        Charms:
                        1. +(2 or Essence) successes to a Join Battle roll. You can move Initiative to Charge. +1 Charge if you rolled highest.
                        2. Make a Withering attack. If you roll enough extra successes to hit the target again, add (Dex + Charge/2) to damage.
                        3. When using #2, if you roll enough successes to hit the target a third time, increase bonus damage to (Dex + Charge).
                        4. After using #2 or #3 to deal significant damage, make a reflexive Decisive with bonus successes, spending only the Initiative you collected by using #2 or #3.
                        5. Form. +2 to your Charge capacity. +1 to your Charge capacity per unique opponent you Crash. No penalties to your off-hand attack. #2 and #4 are cheaper.
                        6. +1 success on a Rush per point of Charge spent. If Rush succeeds, Charge is refunded.
                        7. After successfully using #6, if the opponent retreats, make a Decisive that hits all enemies in front out to Medium range, with damage equal to your Charge. Does not expend Charge.
                        8. Form. +2 Parry. Your Parry cannot drop below 2. If you do not attack on your turn, you may make Decisive counterattacks with a mix of Charge and initiative.
                        9. Spend Charge after an attack hits to add extra bonuses to your Parry.
                        10. After using #8 with at least 3 Charge left, reflexively Disarm your enemy. If successful, use #7.
                        11. As per #4, but spending Charge instead of Initiative.
                        12. On using #11, +1 Charge, and use #3 reflexively with bonus damage. If Solar, this activation of #3 allows a repeated use of #4 or #11.
                        13. Fuse #5 and #8.

                        I was initially going to complain that Double Attack Technique (#2) needs to explain how it works with Onslaught - but then it does! And opens new questions, because the target is apparently suffering from Onslaught penalties before we’ve even finished comparing attack successes to Defense. Does an attack benefit from its own Onslaught penalty? When do these things go into effect? And what if I use, say, Dipping Swallow Defense – do I need to reactivate it for the “second” attack, or does one application persist? Can I counterattack them individually?

                        I am fairly sure Steel Devil Form (#5) is the second place the rules mention a penalty to your off-hand attack; the only other place is the ambidextrous merit. In other words, the only places the rules acknowledge there is an offhand penalty is when they’re getting rid of it. Why would I even care that there’s such a penalty? 2e’s Rate is gone – there’s no impetus to attack with anything but my main hand.

                        Picture of the Steel Devil stylist looks pretty cool, but I’m more interested in that skull-thing behind him.

                        Sonic Slash (#7) is kind of ridiculous – it hits all enemies out to medium range, as long as they’re in front of you. Okay, first – in a line, in front of you? In a 180-degree arc? Second – maps, game. Third – wow, that’s potentially a very large, reusable Decisive that doesn’t expend your Initiative; as far as I can tell, it also doesn’t expend your Charge. True, your enemy has to retreat to trigger it, but if he does… good grief. Combine with those Athletics “multiple follow-up moves” Rush enhancers for extra pain – but honestly, a multi-target no-reset Decisive seems like it’s plenty of pain already.

                        Oh! Or we could use Iron Lotus Unfurling to (1) make it so we never have to pay for Excellencies, and (2) reflexively pop Sonic Slash with bonus dice at reduced cost. This is starting to feel a little bit ridiculous. The early Charms of this tree didn’t impress me; how often do you roll 3x your enemy’s Defense on a meaningful foe? But these later effects… late-step, practically-unlimited Parry boosting is huge; I really wish the rules were clearer on how this interacts with, say, Fivefold Bulwark Stance. This is a substantially better defensive power than Drifting Leaf Elusion, which caps at, effectively, +1 Evasion. That you can follow that up by Decisively striking every enemy out to Medium range at no Initiative cost just starts to get absurd. This style has both offense and defense comparable to the most offensive and defensive styles so far; Sonic Slash looks like it’s in the same playing field as Righteous Devil, and Iron Lotus Unfurling compares well with Crane or Snake.

                        And it’s still going! Steel Devil also gives two different Form Charms, which can then be fused into a single Form Charm that is, yes, strong offensively (more Charge) and really strong defensively (+2 Parry is a better Defense buff than literally any other style gets).

                        This just feels too good to me. Just… way, way too good. The pre-form stuff is not great in meaningful fights, but it’s cheap and can pile on Onslaught penalties; the later stuff is just kind of ridiculous. Steel Devil seems like it is far and away the strongest style in the game, especially against more than one opponent. Single Point had a really strong two-attacks thing going, but at least you could defend against the base form of that to shut it down. This… ugh. This thing has an infinite-as-long-as-you-have-motes combo that you can chain from inside your +2 Defense Form, backstopped by a late-step Parry booster that, hey look, also combos into a 3m Decisive against everyone out to Medium range at no Initiative cost. That seems completely out of line for the rest of these styles.

                        Okay, martial arts. Sorry to end on a down note there, but… good grief. Next time: sorcery!

                                                        Sorcery time!

                                                        I always wanted to like sorcery, and I never quite could – it was just too obviously a deathtrap, in 2e. Stand there with no Charms for 5-15 seconds? Pass. Plus there was the whole opening costs thing: buy a Charm that does nothing but enable you to buy more Charms – that’s just off-putting.

                                                        (Oh, hi, Martial Arts, fancy meeting you here.)

                                                        Anyway. But the sorcery write-up back in Occult looked really promising – it obviated a lot of the costs and made sorcery look like a good buy. So let’s see whether spells are actually worth taking.

                                                        Looks like Circles work more or less as they did before. There’s no mention of the Five Trials, just a more general “Sorcery is hard and will change your outlook”-type statement. I’m okay with that.

                                                        Hm – so spells don’t come out of your own motes anymore. Well, that’s easier. And there’s no fixed casting time – they take however long it takes you to come up with enough successes on an extended (Int + Occult) roll. Which can be backed by Charms – huzzah, Occult Excellencies are no longer useless! Successful casting also refunds the Willpower; combined, presumably this means a Terrestrial Circle Sorcerer can throw spells literally all day long. Finally, you get one spell as a control spell, which works better for you than anyone else.

                                                        Okay, I love that last idea. This seems to fit in the same category as supernals: it means that even two characters with similar traits (both Dawns, or both sorcerers) will have at least some tricks that are wildly different for them – and indeed, that the other one may be literally unable to emulate. That’s a nice degree of personalization, without a lot of overhead. Very very cool. In general, sorcery is winning the “most improved” award so far.

                                                        Countermagic is an automatic power for sorcerers! And… really inefficient; you’re only slowing your target by about half. Hm. But you can also try to distort a freestanding spell. That’s kind of cool; 2e had the problem that if a Lunar bumped into a freestanding Solar spell, there was just nothing he could ever do about it. Now, he has some chance to at least modify the spell, without just effortlessly destroying it. (Huh, or a spell can be negated altogether by a Working, whatever those are. So, okay, no more “Some Solar trapped this city in mists eternally, and that’s that.”)

                                                        Every sorcerer has one or more shaping rituals – again, a little bit of personalization for how you collect your sorcerous motes. Apparently Solars can get up to 3 through Charms, but can vaguely get others via roleplaying. That seems like something to prioritize pretty hard, if you’re a sorcerer, which is perhaps by design.

                                                        Mara’s shaping rituals! Mara is a “deer-footed creature of shadows,” yeah, and a Third-Circle Demon, (Edit: by which I clearly mean a Second Circle Demon), unless that’s been changed. This section is labeled “Shaping Rituals,” so I assume the three paragraphs for Mara are three different shaping rituals, and that you get one of them – but that’s not at all unambiguous. Some kind of subheading would be really useful here. Mara is about love and darkness: motes if people love you futilely, motes if you devour someone’s soul during sex, motes if you start the scene in heavy shadow. Mara-shaping apparently also unlocks some custom merits, which is a nice touch. Sorcerers get a lot of toys, man.

                                                        … I mean, actually, a lot of toys. Occult 3, Essence 1, and one Charm pick gets you: a spell (with a turbocharger attached!), countermagic, distortion, Workings (whatever those are), a shaping ritual, the potential to learn any number of other shaping rituals, and access to custom merits (some of which seem to be free). Why wouldn’t you take that?

                                                        Lots of other options for shaping rituals:
                                                        • You can make pact with a fire lord, draining motes from fire (okay, sorcerer-slash-Righteous Devil, anyone?), motes from burnt offerings, or motes from passion. This can also let you, say, resist fire or spit flames.
                                                        • You might have seen something terrible in the Wyld – one of my players is using a refluff of this. You can gain motes instead of willpower for sleeping, motes for holding to a strange taboo, or motes for devouring emotion. You might be immune to the Wyld, or shape better in the Wyld.
                                                        • You might brew a magical elixir, drawing power from potions or asceticism.
                                                        • You might construct a wondrous artifact, drawing motes when you incorporate it into your casting, or storing motes in it long-term.
                                                        … This section is really badly edited; I found several mistakes per page on a casual read-through of the preceding. That’s well below the quality of the last couple of chapters.

                                                        Okay, the Heptagram gets at least a mention as a shaping ritual, which is nice. I assume the DB book might expand on this more, but it’s good that scholar-sorcerer has some support.

                                                        And finally we reach the spells! In the Terrestrial Circle:
                                                        1. Cirrus Skiff is a pet flying cloud – if you’re making DBZ comparisons, yes, basically exactly like that. It’s slow, and it only carries you and a plus-one, but it’s still safe transport and a counter to flying enemies. As a control spell, you never have to recast it – your cloud follows you and comes when you call.
                                                        2. Corrupted Words makes the target unable to speak about a particular target, which has all kinds of utility. If it’s your control spell, you can even cast it with almost no visible sign, for instant suppression.
                                                        3. Death of Obsidian Butterflies attacks all enemies in a straight line out to medium range with a swarm of deadly glass butterflies. It deals (Essence + extra successes) damage to any enemy it hits – which is not all that great at Essence 1, honestly; 2+ rounds of charging probably only gets you 2-3 Decisive damage. Against battle groups, it’s more accurate and effective. As a control spell, it’s slightly more accurate. And… well, this is interesting. The spell also permanently changes you; your shadow grows little butterflies, and your fingernails might turn to black glass in a rage.
                                                        4. Demon of the First Circle lets you summon a demon (derp). Unlike other spells, this one can only be cast over a period of hours; it also appears to have no control effect. So if you pick this first, is your control slot wasted? A sidebar assures us that bound demons will obey “the letter and spirit” of your instructions, but might still misbehave due to being alien creatures. Eh? Didn’t an earlier chapter say they would try to tempt the sorcerer to wickedness?
                                                        5. Flight of the Brilliant Raptor consumes your Initiative for a long-range decisive attack, potentially starting a bonfire in the target range band. As a control spell, it stretches to extreme range.
                                                        6. Infallible Messenger lets you quickly contact anyone in the same realm of existence – one-way only, unfortunately. As a control spell, you can see through the Messenger’s eyes as it flies, which seems very potent if what you really need is to locate your missing husband (or find out why he’s missing) – but this also causes little cherubs to pop into existence around you randomly.
                                                        7. Invulnerable Skin of Bronze is daylong no-commitment bonus soak and Hardness. Score! I can’t tell whether this prevents you from wearing normal armor or not – it’s a natural soak bonus, which says no, but it also says you’re “considered wearing medium armor.” Can you put on armor if you’re already wearing armor? Actually, wait, is there any rule that says you can’t stack multiple sources of armor? P. 194 is silent on this issue. … Anyway, the armor also gives you a handful of basically-free Charms, which is pretty great. As a control spell, the armor adds (+Essence) bonus soak, whether or not you’re in armor, at the cost of having kind of bronze-y looking skin. Woe is me, my permanent tan now also turns aside arrows.
                                                        8. The Mists of Eventide are a three-round cloud of Initiative-draining poison. If a Crashed character fails to make 6 successes on his Resistance roll, he is immediately knocked out. That, uh, that seems kind of nuts. If it’s your control spell, you can force the sleepers to sleepwalk through a dream of your choice. You can also fire the spell reflexively – okay, no. No Charm should give “Reflexive action, no cost: Target Crashed character must roll a diff 6 (Stamina + Resistance check), or he is instantly knocked out” – much less “or you take control of him.”
                                                        9. Silent Words of Dreams and Nightmares lets you try to instill Intimacies in someone while they sleep. You need a lock of their hair or similar for the attempt, unless it’s your control spell.
                                                        10. Stormwind Rider will create a very fast magical tornado to carry you and the rest of the party around. As a control spell, you jump good, but your moods cause windstorms. On balance, this looks a lot better than Cirrus Skiff, but I guess it’s also quite a bit more obvious.
                                                        11. Summon Elemental does not, in fact, summon elementals – instead, it creates them from scratch. Hm. This seems to avoid the old 2e problem where a Terrestrial Circle spell could, in principle, bind a Greater Elemental Dragon to your service (or at least allow it to fast-travel to you).
                                                        12. With Wood Dragon’s Claws, your hands are indefinitely light artifact weapons, at no commitment cost. I mean, sure, I’ll take that. They can also add bonus damage to a grapple or gain any weapon tag you like. As your control spell, you can learn Evocations for them, which just sounds awesome. I like this more than Glorious Solar Saber on about every count.
                                                        Sorceress lady, maybe if you put some pants on, you would not have an arrow sticking out of you now.

                                                        Distortion is just vicious, on a couple of these effects. Invulnerable Skin of Bronze is great, but getting stuck with a -4 Mobility penalty (that you can’t turn off until the end of the day) is a lot less so.

                                                        Hey, how do you turn off Wood Dragon’s Claws? P. 466 implies that you, uh, can’t. Is there anything I’m overlooking that allows your own spells to be deactivated? (Edit: By dismissing it, as has been pointed out to me. Unfortunately, this raises new questions - is that Simple? Reflexive? Can it be flurried? Can any other spells be dismissed?)
