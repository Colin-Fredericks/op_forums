    [PEACH} Stealth Charms (mainly for Batmen)

    04-18-2020, 06:55 AM
    One of my players has Batman-like Stealth supernal character. I made some homebrew charms for him, since Stealth seemed to me to lack "flashy" high-end effects (whatever that means in the case Stealth).

    As some of you know, my tastes are rather for more high-powered games than intended for Exalted 3ed and I have much sentiment for the previous edition, so you may find many of the following charms heavily apocryphal, but my players liked it, so maybe someone else is also going to enjoy it


    STEALTH CHARMS

    Black Shroud of Tiger Warriors
    Cost: 6m
    Mins: Essence 2, Stealth 3
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: Mute
    Duration: One Scene
    Prerequisites: Guardian Fog Approach

    The Lawgiver avoids being noticed not by the tricks of sorcery, but by honing her skills beyond human limits. Deeply aware of the way a guardian listens to noises and stares at the shadows, she can lead a group of warriors unnoticeable.

    This charm allows the Lawgiver to use the prerequisite upon larger groups of people. All members of the group have to intentionally attempt stealth, be at most in Medium range of each other, and most importantly, strictly follow the orders of the Solar. The affected group’s maximal size is [(Solar’s War)×10, minimum 5].

    The Cloak of Impossible Silence
    Cost: 8m, 1wp
    Mins: Essence 4, Stealth 5
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: Mute
    Duration: One Scene
    Prerequisites: Black Shroud of Tiger Warriors, Mental Invisibility Technique

    Whether it is a pack of ninja warriors or a group of desperate survivors, the Lawgiver can safely lead them amidst vigilant guards and keen-nosed hounds.

    When this charm is active, the Lawgiver can extend the effects of Mental Invisibility Technique to a group of [Essence×3] people who must stay in Close range from the Solar.

    Fleeting Marks of Quill and Brush
    Cost: 7m
    Mins: Essence 4, Stealth 5
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: –
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisites: Vanishing from Mind’d Eye

    When the Lawgiver wishes not to be noticed, her presence is hidden even from gods. It would take utmost arrogance to think that mere paper and ink could track her. The written record turns out frustratingly incomplete and insufficient, the statements of witnesses seem contradictory and all leads end abruptly as if obscured on purpose. She seems to evade the attention of documents as gracefully as that of breathing people.

    When the Lawgiver is tracked down by an organisation or it tries to establish her identity, she can activate this charm and make an opposed roll of [Intelligence + Stealth] vs [Bureaucracy + an appropriate mental attribute]. If the Solar is successful, the written record and testimonies of witnesses are simply insufficient to undertake any reasonable actions. The same applies to individuals trying to track down the Solar based on previously gathered evidence, but not to an investigation led by an individual. This charm can be used retroactively, even if sufficient record seems to be already gathered.

    If the members of the opposing organisation are not aware of the existence of the Solar in the first place (for instance, when Vanishing from Mind’s Eye is in effect), the remaining written record is not complete enough to even link various events caused by the Lawgiver as the actions of one person. They rather seem as a series of unhappy coincidences, bad luck, or crimes of very different agents.

    With a 1wp surcharge, the Lawgiver can extend the effects of this charm to a handful of close companions, like circlemates.

    This charm can only be used once per story, unless reset by achieving a Legendary social goal by Stealth-based methods.

    Blurred Eye of the Crystal Mirror
    Cost: – (6m)
    Mins: Essence 5, Stealth 5
    Type: Permanent
    Keywords: –
    Duration: Indefinite
    Prerequisites: Fleeting Marks of Quill and Brush

    In the days of glorious past, the Solars of Night caste were spies among the foes of Creation. No wicked magic could overhear their words and see when they were swiftly advancing through cabinets. When someone tries to turn their astrology and distant-looking mirrors to see the Lawgiver, they see only faint shadows and clouded enigmas.

    Whenever someone attempts to magically spy on the Lawgiver or detect her actions, she can immediately sense that attempt and reflexively use this charm to prompt an opposed roll of [Wits + Stealth] vs a dice pool appropriate to the interrupted attempt. If the roll was successful, the opponent is simply unable to obtain any conclusive information about the identity or whereabouts of the Solar. In turn, she gains a vague glimpse of whoever made the attempt. If the surveillance attempt was general in nature, for instance trying to detect the presence of any Celestial Exalted, and the charm was used successfully, the presence of the Lawgiver is utterly obscured.

    Dancing Between Shadows
    Cost: –
    Mins: Essence 4, Stealth 5
    Type: Permanent
    Keywords: Perilous, Mute
    Duration: Indefinite
    Prerequisites: Shadow Replacement Technique, Vanishing from Mind’s Eye Method

    In the glorious days of past, the supreme spies of Deliberative where able to spend years in the courts of Hell, completely unnoticed, leaving a shadow of one wicked demon only to enter another one.

    This charm enables the Lawgiver hidden in the shadow of one character, to use Shadow Replacement Technique on another target. This requires a new grapple attempt, but if this attempt is successful, it cannot be witnessed without using powerful magic (like appropriate charms of entities of higher Essence or sorcery of at least Celestial tier). Moreover, in such a case neither of the victims remember her presence.

    When Shadow Replacement Technique is active, Solar can use Vanishing from Mind’s Eye Method on everyone. An attempt to recall the existence of the Lawgiver can be made only once a year, unless prompted by magic. Any time the Solar makes her host act against her intimacies, he may also make such an attempt to realise the presence of the Lawgiver. Once he realised that he is under Solar’s control, he may no longer be a subject to Vanishing from Mind’s Eye Method until this charm is terminated.

    Executioner’s Inevitable Visit
    Cost: 12m, 1wp
    Mins: Essence 5, Stealth 5, Larceny 4
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: Bridge, Mute
    Duration: Indefinite
    Prerequisites: Flashing Nocturne Prana, Door-Evading Technique

    The wicked can hope to run, but they cannot hide, when justice itself is after them, lingering in the shadows, to strike when they expect it the least. Behind the closed doors of chambers and thick walls of castles, it waits.

    When using this charm, Solar chooses one character who opposes an intimacy of at least Major intensity and who is located no further than several miles from the Solar. The Lawgiver reflexively and retroactively appears within a scene in which that character is alone or with a minimal possible group of personal retainers, as long as anyone could hide in their surroundings.
    The Solar can nominate specific circumstances in which she wants to appear, e.g., “when the treatise with the Realm is being signed.” She has to know the identity of the target or at least have encountered him consciously several times. It is not necessary however, that it is physically possible to reach the target, as long as he is in the same realm of existence. For instance, the target can be hidden in a deep underground cave to which one can only teleport himself. Neither is it necessary, that the Solar has reasonably much time to reach the target. When asked, how she got there, she shrugs.

    This charm can be only used once per story, unless reset by significantly advancing or protecting Defining Intimacy via a righteous execution.

    Everywhere and Nowhere Approach [Based on a charm from "Dreams of the First Age”]
    Cost: 9m
    Mins: Essence 5, Stealth 5
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: Perilous
    Duration: Indefinite
    Prerequisites: Executioner’s Inevitable Visit

    The Lawgivers were exercising stealth not to be assassins and thieves, but to haunt the night and fight the wicked. In the city she guards, no one can avoid her presence. Her steps are heard in most obscure alleys. Her eyes blink at the rooftops and behind the windows of palaces. You only see her shadow as she passes by you.

    When this charm is active, the Lawgiver specifies some actions or circumstances which she personally condemns. This in general should be represented by at least Major Intimacy, but can also be a matter of an implicit moral judgement upon Storyteller’s approval. The proscription becomes intuitively clear to her foes. It can be rather specific as “conspiring with the messengers of Delzhan” or as general as “committing violent crimes”. Every time someone intends to commit the prohibited deed in the city-sized region in which she currently resides, he clearly feels the presence of the Lawgiver which counts as a threaten attempt made with Solar’s regular dicepool. Moreover, as she feels the trembling Essence, she can supplement the threaten attempt with charms as usual and intuitively knows whether it was successful. Only one such ban can be in effect at the same time and the effect terminates if the Solar leaves nominated place for a significant amount of time.

    The steps of the Lawgiver in the dark alleys are not mere illusion, as quickly learn those who break the silent laws she sets. Once per story, she can reflexively and retroactively appear within a scene when the specified circumstances happen. She can also nominate some more specific trigger, for instance, “when the Scorpion Clan attacks the dojo of master Jin.” However, it still has to be a deed which the Lawgiver personally condemns. This can be reset by defending or significantly advancing a Defining Intimacy by an act of righteous violence.

    Dark Spirit of Vengeance
    Cost: 16m, 1wp
    Mins: Essence 5, Stealth 5
    Type: Simple, Perilous
    Keywords: Mute
    Duration: Indefinite
    Prerequisites: Everywhere and Nowhere Approach, Dancing Between Shadows

    Having reached the ultimate mastery of shadows, the Lawgiver becomes a shadow herself. Nightmare for the wicked, bleak hope for the oppressed. She blends into the darkness, only to return, when her name is called by a terrified victim.

    This charm allows to combine the effects of both prerequisites. When it is activated, she forbids some misdeed in a city-sized region as per the rules of Everywhere and Nowhere Approach and she hides into the shadows of the city itself. When the charm is active, her presence is felt by anyone who tries to act against her will. However, now she is not only vaguely aware when someone makes such an attempt, but actually witnesses it from shadows, as long as the attempt is made in a physically dark place. While the charm is active, she can attempt social actions which could reasonably done by a lurking silhouette, mainly threaten actions. At any time, she can return to a physical shape, reflexively trying to enter concealment. This releases the mote commitment.

    Generally, while this charm is active, Solar’s presence cannot be detected unless powerful magic is used (this encompasses appropriate charms used by beings of higher Essence rating or sorcery of at least Solar tier). While blended with the shadows of a city, she does not have to eat or sleep. She cannot be harmed either, unless the whole area is turned to ashes. If one year and one night passes without her turning back to the physical shape, she becomes stuck forever as the living shadow of dark streets.

    Once a region and circumstances were specified for the purposes of this charm, it cannot be changed until the end of story.

          So, first just a general note - the formatting on this post made it quite difficult to read (I eventually ended up copy-pasting it into another document so I could rescale it easier). Also, all the formatting kind of makes it a pain to respond to as well, as all the tags have to be stripped out. Judging from my own past experiences, I'm guessing you wrote these in some other document or text editor, and copy-pasted them into your post - that tends to create these weird messes of fonts and such. In the future, rather than doing that, I'd suggest copy-pasting your text first into something like Notepad that strips all the fancy stuff out, then copying that to the forum post, and then using the built-in stuff to add formatting. Much nicer.

          Anyway, on to the charms themselves. I'm more critiquing themes and the "internal" mechanics here, rather than essence costs - I haven't gone through the books and tried to compare essence costs or prereqs with anything else similar, so I can't usefully comment on those yet. Overall, I do like the concepts of most of these, and I think all of them fit somewhere into the Solar charmset. As to the specifics:

          Originally posted by Lanic View Post
          Black Shroud of Tiger Warriors

          This charm allows the Lawgiver to use the prerequisite upon larger groups of people. All members of the group have to intentionally attempt stealth, be at most in Medium range of each other, and most importantly, strictly follow the orders of the Solar. The affected group’s maximal size is [(Solar’s War)×10, minimum 5].
          I'd suggest that you use the mechanical system that already exists for determining how big a group is, namely battlegroup Size. If you don't, I'd expect you'll run into situations where someone has a Size 2 battlegroup ("several dozen combatants"), and you either can't tell them how many people that actually is, or you can, it's more than the limit, but it turns out that doesn't actually make a difference, because the numbers they can conceal are still a Size 2 battlegroup. Also, to me, having War be the only cap on a Stealth charm is a bit weird. I'd change this to protecting a Size of battlegroup equal to the Solar's (Essence), and noting that getting very large groups (Size 3 or more) to all fit within Medium range will usually require War rolls or charms.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          Fleeting Marks of Quill and Brush

          When the Lawgiver is tracked down by an organisation or it tries to establish her identity, she can activate this charm and make an opposed roll of [Intelligence + Stealth] vs [Bureaucracy + an appropriate mental attribute].           To me, this feels like it's kind of pushing the logical boundaries of Stealth. I can see it, but it's a bit of a stretch. This feels closer to Larceny's wheelhouse. It could be a notably lower-Essence and lower skill Larceny charm. Of course, if the character wants All Stealth, All the Time, then this isn't unreasonable. But if they've taken any Larceny (which I feel a Batman-clone really should have), you might want to build this in Larceny instead, and see if they take it there.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          Blurred Eye of the Crystal Mirror

          In turn, she gains a vague glimpse of whoever made the attempt.
          I like most of this, but this part doesn't seem Stealth to me. Realizing someone is watching you, fine. Making it so you know who it is, less so. I'd suggest changing this to say that the Solar does get a sense of someone watching them, but either a) they need to use appropriate Awareness charms to actually identify them, or b) they recognize the person's "feel", in a way that will allow them to realize if it's the same person trying to observe them another time, and if they ever see them in person, they'll recognize them.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          Executioner’s Inevitable Visit

          When using this charm, Solar chooses one character who opposes an intimacy of at least Major intensity and who is located no further than several miles from the Solar.
          Rather than only working on someone opposing an Intimacy of the Solar's, I'd suggest it work on anyone who the Solar has an Intimacy towards and who has one back at the Solar. In fact, I'd make it so that's the basic effect of the charm - retroactively declaring that he is in the Justice League meeting the whole time and has been listening to everything snide remark about him is very Batman, I'd say. Then, as an upgrade, allow it to apply to anyone opposing one of their Intimacies.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          This charm can be only used once per story, unless reset by significantly advancing or protecting Defining Intimacy via a righteous execution.
          If you change it as I suggested above, this usage restriction should only apply to the upgrade, not the basic effect.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          Everywhere and Nowhere Approach

          When this charm is active, the Lawgiver specifies some actions or circumstances which she personally condemns.           This one, I definitely don't feel fits into Stealth. This is much more an Integrity charm, I think. A good rule of thumb for Solar charm design is to ask yourself "would this effect be appropriate for any character who was extremely good at this Ability?" And I don't think this one passes that test, for Stealth. There are plenty of stealthy characters who don't have that "lurking moral watchdog" thing that Batman does. I wouldn't ascribe this kind of trick to an ancient, amoral mercenary ninja, for example. She doesn't care what you do in the shadows, as long as she's not being paid to do so.

          Originally posted by Lanic
          The steps of the Lawgiver in the dark alleys are not mere illusion, as quickly learn those who break the silent laws she sets.           If you put this in Integrity, I'd still allow the retroactive "I appear to castigate the people Doing Bad Stuff", but I'd just say that how you appear is up to you, and other Abilities can be used to enhance your arrival. So someone with great Athletics can use Mountain Crossing Leap to just jump into the scene, Larceny can let you reveal that you were a disguised bystander the whole time, Stealth can let you come out of the all-concealing shadows, and so on. I'd change the reset condition of this, no matter which Ability you end up putting it in, so that it can be reset by stuff other than violence, like achieving a legendary social goal (pacifist Batman should be able to reset this by terrifying a criminal so much he decides to change his life and become an honest man, for example).

          Dark Spirit of Vengeance

          If you move the prereq to Integrity, you could still keep this in Stealth. Having a cross-Ability prereq charm is somewhat unusual, but I don't think it's absolutely forbidden. However, you could also give the Integrity version of Everywhere and Nowhere Approach the proviso that someone triggering it also made them a valid target for Awareness charms to sense, allowing more precise identification, and then just allowing Executioner's Inevitable Visit to apply, since now Solar Batman does know the identity of the person doing the dirty deed.

          The effect from this of allowing you to talk and intimidate people from the shadows is good, but I think it should be broken out into its own charm, and have considerably lower Essence requirement. Something like this, perhaps:

          Unseen Speaker Stance
          Cost: 1m; Mins: Essence 1, Stealth 4
          Type: Reflexive
          Keywords: Mute
          Duration: One influence attempt
          Prerequisites: Easily-Overlooked Presence Method

          The Hidden Suns use stealth to conceal themselves, but not merely for deeds of inquity. True to her sobriquet of "Lawgiver", a Solar with this charm can speak to others to convince them to obey the laws and to castigate wrongdoing, while remaining concealed. Activating this charm allows the Solar to speak with anyone present in the scene, long enough to make a social influence attempt, without breaking stealth - she doesn't need to make a new Stealth attempt, and anyone hearing her does not get a new roll to penetrate her concealment. If she is under the effect of this charm's prerequisite, this social influence does not count as an overt action that would cause people to notice her.

          Some influence may be limited by stealth. Notably, if targets cannot see the Solar, she cannot use Appearance, either to apply a penalty if it exceeds their Resolve, or as part of the dice pool. The Storyteller may also choose to apply other limitations that make sense, such as forbidding attempts to inspire positive emotions or to instill intimacies with contexts of trust or security. Appropriate stunts can overcome this, however. Also, the Solar may choose to reveal enough of herself as part of this charm to allow her Appearance to apply. Doing so does end concealment attempts, but still keeps the Lawgiver's identity hidden - her face and any identifying marks remain in the shadows. Anyone who didn't pierce her previous concealment attempt can't identify her now, unless they manage to overcome her Stealth attempt if she retreats back into hiding.

          At Essence 2, this charm can be repurchased. If the Solar pays a 3m surcharge when activating it (which must be committed), the charm's duration becomes "one scene".

              A lot of good suggestions, thank you! Some of them, I immediately agree with. Obviously, the editing one, which I already changed. There is a number of suggestions which I find interesting, but which I still have to digest. A few remarks:

              1. I completely agree about linking the size of group in Black Shroud with the Size as a mechanical concept.

              2. I completely agree about "speaking from shadows" as a separate charm concept.

              3. The one I think I most disagree with, but kind of see you point is moving Everywhere and Nowhere Approach to Integrity. I agree with it, in that if someone would present the concept of the charm as "you immediately know when someone is doing wrong things nearby," that would absolutely be Integrity.

              However, my initial intent (and the intent of the original DoTFA charm) was: "you are so Stealthy that in a creepy way, you are Shroedinger-everywhere, so your foes are afraid to open a fridge". In DoTFA, this was simply an Illusion, which I really don't think fit into Solar themes (maybe that could be a Bureaucracy charm -- your image is everywhere). So I wanted to move it more into "I see what you're doing" territory, hence giving the threaten effect. However, then I wanted to put some more agency in hands of the player which suggested making the character aware of people being freaked out by them. Then again, I definitely, wanted to blend it with the "jumpscare" effect of the previous charm, to reinforce the idea that this is not a mere illusion.

              At the end of the day, I don't quite know how to modify this charm to avoid too much intersection with Integrity and I'm open to suggestions. I think that the base intent is firmly in the purview of Stealth.

              One last remark: I strongly disagree with the claim that: "A good rule of thumb for Solar charm design is to ask yourself 'would this effect be appropriate for any character who was extremely good at this Ability?" In some cases, it's true, but we see some charm trees which have very diverse themes. For instance, I could easily imagine Herakles as an Athletics supernal. However, running on walls, jumping on water, and flying, all see firmly outside his aesthetics. Wyld-Shaping Technique is in Lore, and it definitely isn't something, any person good at Lore would automatically know how to do. "Paladin" charms about fighting with darkness are in Melee and it doesn't seem something obligatory for Melee or even more suitable there as opposed to, say, Archery. It's even more apparent in Lunar and Bragon-Blooded charm design where effects are more loosely-tied to their trees (does being stealthy mean that you should be good at strangling people by taking away air from their chest?).

              Having said that, let me thank you once more for a lot of interesting suggestions!

                      3. The one I think I most disagree with, but kind of see you point is moving Everywhere and Nowhere Approach to Integrity. I agree with it, in that if someone would present the concept of the charm as "you immediately know when someone is doing wrong things nearby," that would absolutely be Integrity.

                      I think, if that's the point of the charm, it's okay in Stealth. I think it's getting a bit confused, though, by trying to be too direct a conversion of the DotFA version. Here's how I'd re-write it, incidentally making it more accessible at low Essence:

                      Everywhere and Nowhere Approach
                      Cost: 5m; Mins: Essence 2, Stealth 5
                      Type: Simple
                      Keywords: Mute
                      Duration: One scene
                      Prerequisites: Unseen Speaker Stance

                      It is well known that those who pursue acts of villainy are easily terrified and believe in supernatural terrors that await them. The Dagger of Heaven exploits these fears ruthlessly, for a terrified criminal is easier to capture. Activating this charm requires a brief dramatic action, in which the Solar reveals her presence in the scene to others in some way - appearing as a silhouette against a window, leaving an obvious symbol, or simply announcing her presence from the shadows. For the rest of the scene, she may make social influence attempts against anyone aware of her presence, either to inspire emotions such as fear or hyper-vigilance, or to instill appropriate intimacies such as "The Solar (terror)", rolling ([any social Attribute] + Stealth) to make such influence. She need not reveal herself again, or even be physically present near the people she is influencing - the knowledge of her possible presence is enough to terrify.

                      At Essence 3, this charm upgrades. Now, rather than an area defined by a scene, the Lawgiver's influence can cover a larger space - about a neighborhood in a city, or a single village - anywhere she could practically reach within about an hour (this is limited to what the people potentially affected know she could reach, so even if she knows Mountain Crossing Leap, if they don't know that, her influence cannot reach across 20 miles of rough terrain). The affected people must still be able to perceive some kind of sign of the Solar's presence in order to be affected by this charm. If she activated it before, and now wants to inflict her influence on a new group of people, however, she may make her presence known and have Everywhere and Nowhere Approach's effect apply to them without needing to activate the charm again.

                      At Essence 4, the charm upgrades again, now affecting anyone within one day's travel of the Solar. The charm's duration also extends to one day. The other considerations of the first upgrade also apply.

                      At Essence 5, Everywhere and Nowhere Approach upgrades once more, now covering an area that the Iron Wolf could reach in a week, and similarly extending the duration.


                      And then a follow-up charm:

                      Omnipresent Judgement Method
                      Cost: - (3m); Mins: Essence 3, Stealth 5
                      Type: Permanent
                      Keywords: Mute
                      Duration: Permanent
                      Prerequisites: Everywhere and Nowhere Approach

                      Once someone is aware of the Lawgiver's potential presence, his own guilty conscience or fear of the Solar's wrath is often enough to sway him from taking actions she would not approve of. This charm permanently upgrades its prerequisite. By paying a 3m committed surcharge when she activates Everywhere and Nowhere Approach, the Solar may declare, as part of the action of revealing her presence to those affected, some moral principle or code of behavior (such as "it is wrong to harm the innocent" or "remember the Code of the Guild"), or some reasonably specific type of action (e.g. "don't break the laws of Great Forks" or "don't betray me!"). To everyone to whom Everywhere and Nowhere Approach applies, the fear of the Solar's reaction to their transgressions is often deterrent enough, with the knowledge that she may be watching from the shadows, ready to punish them. Anyone who attempts any actions that contravene the Solar's dictum are subject to a social influence attempt, rolled with ([any social Attribute] + Stealth) with double 9s, either to persuade them to not take the action based on any appropriate Intimacies, or to threaten them not to take the action, based on the possible dire consequences.

                      If someone is subject to this influence, is not affected, and continues their activities, the Solar automatically treats the individual so doing as a valid target for Executioner's Inevitable Visit.

                      Originally posted by Lanic
                      One last remark: I strongly disagree with the claim that: "A good rule of thumb for Solar charm design is to ask yourself 'would this effect be appropriate for any character who was extremely good at this Ability?" In some cases, it's true, but we see some charm trees which have very diverse themes. For instance, I could easily imagine Herakles as an Athletics supernal. However, running on walls, jumping on water, and flying, all see firmly outside his aesthetics.
                      It's not a universal rule, it's a guideline. It's certainly a good idea to have multiple branches within each Ability, so that two characters can focus on the same Abilities and not look identical. All that said, in Exalted's logic of the Abilities, Hercules could fairly quickly pick up walking on water and dancing across bamboo stalks, yes, because he is good at Athletics and those are athletic things to do. Basically, the rule is supposed to make you consider, when you're designing a charm, "should someone good at this Ability be able to quickly develop this trick without having to go into other Abilities?"
