        Ah, the Sidereals. My boys!

        Anyway. Here's the thing. The non-Gold, non-Bronze Sidereal "factions", such as they might or might not still exist in 3e, basically boil down to "two or three guys with radical views who keep it to themselves." Those two or three guys might have a lot of subtle power in Creation, but until such time as they put together a big enough coup to sell to their brethren, they're less a faction than they are those weird guys in the office who are always muttering about this killer presentation they'll deliver one day but never quite get around to it.

        Everyone is talking about numbers, so I'll throw out my own. Strap in: I have thoughts on this. Everything that follows is simply how I do it, not necessarily, unless stated, how it is.

        I usually assume that at any given time, there are about 80 Sidereals active. Cutting their numbers by a fifth sounds harsh, but keep in mind I'm lumping every single inactive Sidereal into that; people who are between Exaltations, guys on leave, Ronin, in 3e Rakan Thulio, people who just got their arm bitten off by a Lunar and maybe need some convalescent time, guys detached for a special independent project they can't easily be recalled from, etc. 80 might even be generous, but it seems fair.

        The Directional Conventions (Capital, Fire, Wood, Water, Air) I figure have about 20 guys in Capital and 12 or so in the other directional Conventions at any given time. It's explicit that the Bronze Faction keeps Capital more heavily staffed in order to ensure that everything in the Realm, which is sort of a big deal to them, remains stable. The other 12 are either in Conventions that have time commitment needs that necessitate them not also being in a Directional Convention, or are full-time in Heaven for other reasons; regardless, they probably spend most of their time in Yu-Shan.

        I feel it important to note that these dilineations are considerably more amorphous than I've made them and people probably drift in and out. Your Convention Chair might assign you to be this years Yu-Shan liaison officer, meaning that you spend two-thirds of the year manning the desk there and working hard on behalf of your Conventions interests and shepherding through prayer requests and sitting in on meetings, and the other third shuttling back to Creation to update your superior and meet with your fellow Convention members. Even though that guy technically has a Creation posting, he is actually in Yu-Shan most of the time. He might do well enough there that the Bureau of Destiny transfers him to Yu-Shan full time to be their in-house liaison with one of the other Bureaus, or he might be cycled back to Creation and spend five years humping through the southern deserts hunting Fair Folk and never seeing Yu-Shan because Nazri thinks he could be the next great raksha troubleshooter and wants to give him some much-needed seasoning.

        Similarly, someone who supposedly has a full-time Heaven billet might actually spend most of their time in Creation; the senior Sidereal whose job is literally "be a Sifu" might spend all of his time visiting his students in Creation and training them while they go about their duties. That's his full-time job, passing down the techniques of the Five-Score Fellowship to the next generation. It would be nice to have them all in Yu-Shan at the dojo, but not often feasible.

        (This might also apply to, say, a Bronze Faction member who is given a ridiculously easy desk job in Yu-Shan to cover up the fact that he's really teaching sorcery at the Heptagram. Despite his billet in Heaven, that dude is basically living in Creation.)

        So them's the broad strokes. Let's talk Faction numbers.

        A lot of people like to give the Bronze Faction overwhelming numerical superiority. That's a way to go. It's an effective way to go, dramatic even, especially if you're running a game where you want to see the Gold Faction suddenly snowball out of control and get a horde of defections.

        Not how I go. The split I usually use, in order to maintain canon compliance with there being more Bronze than Gold, is 30/25/25, Bronze/Gold/Independent. I move that around somewhat but that's a good ballpark.

        This is because I prefer the Bronze Faction strength, and Gold Faction weakness, not to come via anything so gauche as weight of numbers. That's not very... Sidereal. Well, okay, it kind of is in some ways, "I thought I had the votes but it turns out I was wrong" is a very Sidereal problem to have, but... ehhh.

        No, I like to represent the Bronze Faction strength, and the struggles of the Gold, via bureaucratic control. The Bronze Faction only has five more dudes than the Golds, but the Bronze Faction has a disproportionate number of Sidereal Elders (I have never run a game with a living pre-Usurpation Sidereal who was not an ardent Bronze) and occupies a disproportionate number of important posts and has a disproportionate number of friends and favors among the gods and spirits.

        So they can do more with less. They can arrange assignments for their members that perfectly overlap with faction business, killing two birds with one stone. They can get their hands on awesome gear, magic when needed, divine intervention when necessary. The Gold, in turn, actually punch BELOW the weight class their numbers indicate, because they occupy a disproportionately smaller number of plum positions and so have to work twice as hard for half the result.

        That's how I model Bronze having the upper hand. It should not, in my opinion, be a matter of "there's just so many of us" but rather "we've been here forever and you, my young and idealistic friend, have not."

                    Actually, considering the aforementioned film, I'm suddenly struck by the idea that the incongruity a lot of people are getting is from thinking of them in terms of the factions of a country torn apart by civil war as opposed to within elected representatives of the same party. I don't really understand why, since the Sidereals basically have all of the features (the structure laid out for the Bronze Faction in both Sidereal books is almost exactly what you would expect of the kinds of old boys clubs that spring up in certain parties) and I think almost everybody here would come from countries in which factionalised party politics has at least been a historical thing, but never mind. Regardless, it's very easy to have at least two and maybe up to four or five divisions within even a relatively small (but still sizable) group that is tight-knit and politically oriented as a matter of course. Take several dozen people who are all about Deciding Things and have them able to split up and fill a couple of large rooms along lines of Different Decisions, and you've got some factions.

                                        One ameliorating factor here is that these are Sidereals, and belonging to one faction would be far too simple for them. Bronze faction member who has been in deep cover for a century? Definitely. Gold Faction member who is playing an Independent who is sympathetic to the Silver guys to gain their support? Oh yes. Double and triple and even quadruple agents? Hell yeah.

                                        When you take into account a political landscape that resembles Cold War-era espionage, things get a lot more interesting, and believable. Two large factions, with various minor and sub-factions attached, and almost everyone playing multiple sides of the game. So, sure the Independent's coffee breaks have twenty people show up, but who knows which ones are the actual Independents? Hell, one of the people might show up publicly as a Bronze Faction member to shoot the breeze, but they actually have been leaning Independent for a while now, and they want an Elder patron to cover their butts when they tell their Bronzie Sifu to screw off. Then sixty people show up to the Bronze planning meetings, but Kejak breaks them out into working groups anyway, and lets those group leaders worry about security. Even he can't keep full track of who is working with whom to betray whom anymore.

                                        At the end of the day, these people DO work together, so all this intrigue and insanity results in careers setback and ruined, audits and improper raises, and every once in a while, someone ends up dead. That's really rare, because even most of the hardcore Bronzies are not willing to straight up murder a "Gold Star" whose help they might need to save their ass on official business.

                                        So all of this crap goes nowhere...for centuries. Then the Solars come back, and shit gets real. That's the perfect moment for PC Sids to either pull a masterstroke of intrigue, OR to cut though all the BS and say "Hey, bros, we gotta set aside this Heptagram love triangle Yeddim shit and get stuff done."


                                        TL;DR Many Sid factions work when you realize many of them don't belong to just one faction, effectively.
