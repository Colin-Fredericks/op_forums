    So I'm noticing a pattern: Solar, Abyssal, and Infernal anima powers that mimic each other tend to be weaker or more restrictive in the hands of the latter two, for no good reason.
    I think most of these are due to certain developments and changes not being implemented properly, but it's a bad look.

    Let's go through them:

    Supernal Warrior/Death is Inevitable/Font of War: Dawns and Azimuths kill (Essence) extras per turn without a roll, while Dusk Castes can only kill one. Additionally, while Dusks gain a guaranteed success on decisive damage, Dawns/Azimuths gain +(Essence) damage dice against battle groups and a +(Essence) difficulty increase to their rout checks. However, Supernal Warrior applies this increase automatically, while Font of War has to build it over time for each individual battle group by successfully damaging them.

    Fearsome Visage/Terrifying Visage: The Azimuth power is just weaker, lacking the gambit cost reduction of Fearsome Visage; unlikely to be anything but an oversight. The Abyssal Fear Made Flesh is similar in purpose but works entirely differently, so I'm not going to try to compare it the Fearsome Visage.

    All Eyes Upon Me/Shake Off Your Chains: As above, Shake Off Your Chains is just All Eyes Upon Me with the secondary benefit (a situational dice bonus) filed off. The Ascendant power doesn't specify it has to be "reasonable," though. Also as above, the Abyssal equivalent is too different to compare.

    Purity of Purpose/Apocalyptic Voice/Laws of Hell: Zeniths get (Essence) automatic successes to Presence and Performance rolls, while the other two gain an equivalent bonus to social influence using any Ability, but each to a specific purpose. However, Zeniths also casually annihilate undead extras, and deal bonus damage to undead battle groups.

    Joyous Pursuit/Knowledge at All Costs: These are identical, but Twilights get a more significant difficulty reduction than Horizon Castes. Chalk this one up to a very understandable oversight.

    Indefatigable Genius/Underworldly Lore/Principles of Hierarchy: All three are identical, except that the Daybreak's Focus Will bonus only applies to necromancy where the other two get the (I assume) broader "spellcasting." And that is wack.

    No One Special/Walking in Daylight/Subtle Enemy: These are all worded differently, but are essentially the same. Subtle Enemy can technically dampen iconic animas, which is clearly a typo, while Walking in Daylight gets an additional air-breathing mermaid bonus (which, honestly, Day Castes do need).

    Cloak of Shadows/Grim Caul/Death’s Shadow: Grim Caul only provides its bonus to Stealth instead of both Stealth and Athletics. Meanwhile, Death's Shadow automatically hides from Extras, which the other two can't. Definitely typos/minor errors on both counts.

    Universal Scholar/God of the Ashes/Fiend to All Nations: All identical, except that the Penumbra Caste can't learn Charms from celestial gods. This is clearly deliberate for flavour reasons, though, so I won't complain.

    Esteemed Guest/Speaker for the Dead/Dealbreaker: The Eclipse and Moonshadow powers look like the same effect at different stages of development, with the former having a clear mechanical effect where the latter leaves it up to the ST. The Penumbra variant is also not grounded in the mechanics, but it has a unique social power to compensate.

    Now, having been negative for most of this post, here are some cool things I noticed while writing it:
    Towering Demon Emperor is rad (why ever play a non-Azimuth Infernal?). Corona of Fury pushes you to flagrant displays of your unlimited power, which is cool. Horizon Castes can steal the sorcery right out of your hands. Day Castes at iconic turn into cool ghosts. The Greater Sign of Mars is the best thing in the preview.

    I would have liked it if the Greater Sign of Saturn was more like the sign of Mung, but I'll take it.

                        I read them as expressions of resentment, as agents intended to overthrow the status quo regardless of whether that’s strategically viable. They’re Lawgivers in the sense that they have the power to make and enforce laws, but stripped of the Heavenly veneer that humans associate with justice and legitimacy. And because the Infernal Exaltation always chooses people who feel oppressed or betrayed, it will always be a phenomenon that challenges the status quo, not for the sake of chaos, but because there will always be injustice.

                        Put another way, the Solar Exaltation posits that justice is possible and should be sought. The Infernal Exaltation posits that injustice is real and must be fought.

                                                    You're assuming the Infernal knows that.

                                                    Hell, most demons probably don't know that, or at least, don't understand that.

                                                    As noted, out of universe, it's an irony. In-universe, it's not just a good recruitment tactic, it's a really dangerous and effective lie, because both the hearer wants to believe it, and the teller honestly thinks it's true, just leaving out the specifics for later. It's the kind of thing a cult recruiter does; find broken people and give them something to rebuild around. Just showing sympathy and rewards is a good way to get the Infernal eating out of the Yozis' hands.

                                                    This also has an inherent arc to it - the crushing realization that leaders they trusted were just as bad, and having to end up developing a healthier meaning on their own. This is not a bug. Hell, it reminds me of Megami Tensei 2 (no Shin), where the game ends up being a really good story because of how it gradually reveals your mentor is not trustworthy - and how your friend and teammate just shuts down rather than believe someone who gave him hope is this conniving treacherous ass.
