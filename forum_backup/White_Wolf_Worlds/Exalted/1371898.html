              Some Charms have ridiculously high minimums, yes.

              Does your character concept need those Charms? Because Excellent Strike and Dipping Swallow Defence on Melee 3/Dex 3 makes you pretty deadly. Yes, you can't shoot beams of golden light from your sword... but you don't need to if your Twilight Scholar is fighting Brides of Ahlat, Fog Sharks and Claw Striders. Intermediate combat skill is enough.

              Give that Twilight plenty of chance to use Lore and Medicine Charms -- start them on the Wyld Shaping Charm tree and Sorcerous Workings and they probably won't buy combat abilities if they already feel badass.

              Originally posted by Apromor View Post
              So, you're all deep into a discussion about how to encourage lower defenses for PC's. This was prompted by the observation that there are a great number of cool NPC's that can't be used in fights because they'd never be able to hit the PC's.

              Is there some particular reason why we're not discussing the hows and whys of adding one to four points to their attack values, or is it just the way that the conversation happened to develop?
              Because that's Dragon Ball Z. PCs and ST in an arms race. The players want to succeed, the ST doesn't let them, PCs get bigger numbers, ST gets bigger numbers until every random encounter is between war gods who can destroy planets with a gesture.

              If the PCs want to be good at something, arbitrarily upping the difficulty to under-cut them is bad form.

              Also, it's a case of using the wrong opponents. There's no need to add four dice to a Blood Ape when you can fight Octavian. There's no need to add four dice to Octavian when you can fight Ligier. There's no need to add four dice to Ligier when you can fight the Unconquered Sun.

              Sure, if you really like an iconic enemy but your PCs are overspecced to deal with them, you can just up their stats. But if you're doing that every encounter you're really going to wonk the power-curve.

                      Originally posted by JohnDoe244
                      Also, it's a case of using the wrong opponents. There's no need to add four dice to a Blood Ape when you can fight Octavian. There's no need to add four dice to Octavian when you can fight Ligier. There's no need to add four dice to Ligier when you can fight the Unconquered Sun.
                      This is a very good point.
                      A Blood Ape is a terrifying opponent for a mortal. Giant demon apes aren't there to terrify Exalted though. My Essence 2 Lunars fought a Battle Group of Blood Apes on a zeppelin; it was a cool fight, lots of cool stunts, lasted about 3 rounds. In the last round, several of the PCs literally did nothing so that the Ape-Totem PC could face off against the biggest demon Ape.

                      And that's fine. The battle wasn't there to make PCs badly injured. It was to a)show that the bad guys are putting in some effort to stopping people reaching the McGuffin before them, b)show that the bad guys use demons, and c)have a cool fight on a Zeppelin.

                      The following session, one single non-combat optimised PC fought a storm serpent, while the other PCs watched. Not just any storm serpent, but the biggest storm serpent around.
                      This combat lasted longer, like 7 rounds or so, and it wasn't extremely easy, but I don't think anyone was shocked when Krove the Tiger-Bull, Strongest In All The Land, proved himself stronger than a storm serpent and it ran off to cry to its friends.
                      Again, that's fine. The point wasn't to give Krove an extremely difficult fight, it was to a)show that the Elemental Pole of Air has lots of scary storm elementals nearby, b)have PCs work together to solve a problem using brains and brawn (the No Moon established a fact that if they did a special ritual, then defeated the biggest storm serpent around, the other storm serpents would leave them alone), and c)have a cool fight on a Zeppelin.

                      The fight after that was a few PCs attacking 100 assorted minor demons to save two Dragonblood. It lasted 2 rounds. The point was to a)show that there's a ton of demons invading the palace they were in, b)let the Full Moon viking and Changing Moon archer show off their combat skills, and c)have the PCs save some Dragonblood who are too injured to fight them, so they can talk to the Dragonblood and find out some stuff.

                      The fight after that... lasted 7 or 8 rounds, and by the end all but 2 of the PCs were incapacitated. That was a fight in a giant cold hazard against some Yama Kings; 6 ancient sun-demons who'd escaped from Hell having been trapped there for 1500 years by the Dragons, and were trying to kill the Elemental Dragon of Air (possibly in revenge, possibly because of the eternal winter that's gripping the world). That was the fight that was supposed to be difficult and challenging, because it was the finale fight of that story arc.

                      Similarly, in my old game, the PCs sometimes fought battle groups of humans, first-circle demons, elementals, etc... that's not supposed to be challenging. Second-circle demons and the mile-high, three-headed behemoth they accidentally released... that's challenging.


                      Saying this... I do, therefore, agree with a point DrLoveMonkey made earlier. If the above is the case, why do the writers keep releasing fully fleshed-out mechanically-written serious antagonists for mortals? Or 13 year-old Dragonblood?
                      I've got a folder full of wacky creatures with interesting ideas for powers, that are entirely inappropriate antagonists for a circle of Solars, except maybe in groups of 100 (where their interesting powers get turned into Might anyway).
                      I don't need a lot of stats for interesting flavour and scene-setting creatures. Just fluff description is fine, because I can pull "Yeah, they roll 7 dice and their defence is 4" out of my head.
                      The only Hundred Devils Night Parade entries I've used are animals (for Lunars), and a little bit the demons and elementals (if PCs summon them). I planned to use Mahicara, and there are a couple of other behemoths that'd be suitable. Other than that, I've got a lot of nice PDFs that I just don't really need for this game. Similarly, the book has all these rules for animals that... honestly, you could run with 5 dice pools (like 2nd ed did them). A lot of their cool abilities just don't come up that much, unless you're a Lunar.
                      What actually is useful as antagonists for a circle of PCs is stuff like behemoths and second-circle demons; not that they should be doing this every session, but that's what I need a complicated pdf with a bunch of stats and mechanically-interesting abilities for.

                      STing Bronze Age Exalted

                              JohnDoe244 I can't answer exactly Apromor exactly when you ask him "Why the Solar tools do not make fight interesting ?" and I think the answer is simple (and greatly explained by habitableexoplanet) : people want challenging fights that are fun and exciting.

                              To take a simple example : Take DD4. When you start DD4, the fights have very few options, so you feel your character is not necessarily "weak" but simple. The fights usually last 4-5 rounds against enemies who don't have very special abilities or comeback powers.
                              Then, 25 levels later, when you are on your way to godhood, you have multiple options for your character (I don't remember exactly but something along the lines of 2 at-will, 4 encounters, 4 dailies, multiple objects powers, special features of your class and parangonic path and epic path), you feel your character is usually strong and has some layers of complexity. And the fights are usually not down to Round 1 everyone unleashes everything and the fight is over, but will regularly lasts 4-5 rounds as well, or will have multiple phases against enemies like Lolth, where you have enough time and space to express the power of your character, and usually have multiple tactical choices to make.

                              I think the experience most people have with Solar tools is you one shot-people very dead, for a very long range of different opponent, and thus fights are not the interesting "back and forth" you expect them to be because that's what you think when you read the book but is more along the lines of : Roll 15 initiative, Attack first with TAP against any very impressive antagonist and some Charms, deal 20 damage, done. It goes very far from the expected experience of "the more power you have the more original and fun encounters you will make against your enemies".

                              I understand that some of these tools exist in the game (Octavian can't be oneshoted, Ahlat is certainly very strong but I've never read him in great details, etc ...) but they are not easy to master for the ST (it took me 2 years and a half to finally make a fight I was proud of against a Circle of 4 Solars with custom enemies because it was very tense and dramatic), and furthermore they do not exist in any reusable fashion like any DD monster generator for a specific challenge level you can't to play out. I can only understand people that didn't push the experience as far as me to try and tweak the enemies and read enough content to get their satisfaction the way they wanted it and I really can't blame them.
