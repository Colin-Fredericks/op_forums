        Join Battle

        Volfer spends 6m to Full Excellency his Join Battle (7/33m) for 7i
        Battle-Group spends rolls 6 dice for 4i.

        7i. Volfer attacks with Excellent Strike and a 4m Fire and Stones Strike (4/29m) rolling 10 dice with an automatic success, rerolling 1s against Defense 5. He rolls 8 successes, hitting (8i), then rolls (14+3-13) 4+4 dice of damage for 3 magnitude.

        4i. The BRTs will try to Engage with their Chopping swords. They spend Willpower and roll 12 dice. Volfer defends with a Full Excellency (0/25m) for Parry 9. BRT roll 4+1 successes, missing (1i).

        Battle-Ready Troops: 1i, Magnitude 8, Size 5, 3WP

Volfer spends 4m on Spirit Strengthens the Skin (0/9m). They roll (16-7-4) 4 dice of damage for 3i (1i/14i).

                                        Yeah, there are enough Outcastes to go around, whether as leaders, allies, or mercenaries. Toss one of those in a battlegroup, give them solid defenses and Roaring Dragon General, and you'll be on your way.

                                        But you don't even have to go that far. An unexalted general with a 9-12 dice Command pool still adds 3-5 dice to all a BG's attacks, which is a great force multiplier. You implied your PCs don't very optimized builds, so maybe you could protect that general with soak 10, parry 6-7, and a horse (for a little more defense in melee, and also a nice 11-15 die Disengage pool), but more likely you'll want to hide them off the field, and let them give orders using Intelligence - maybe there's an elaborate system of flags, or they're appearing like a hologram to address the troops with sorcery. These methods also allow some non-combat skills to shine, should the PCs try to disrupt their communication and/or hunt down the general directly.

                                        And, of course, there are non-DB options for a supernatural leader. They might be a sorcerer on a Cirrus Skiff at Extreme range above the battlefield as they are protected by Invulnerable Skin of Bronze and a Virtuous Guardian of Flame (and assisted by some other spell or working to get a few more Command dice, as needed). Or perhaps in desperation, this nation has accepted the aid of the Fair Folk - a Cataphract has good Command and is hard to kill quickly - establishing their Villain credentials by allowing such an "ally" to feed upon their own citizens in exchange for help. Or a summoned demon, local god, etc. The really important thing is that they add those dice, and don't just get sniped out immediately.

                                        (I would also advise 1-2 other named enemies, probably more based on mobility and/or ranged attacks, to both make the fight more memorable and give people a reason to struggle with moving around in a BG, but that's more personal taste. Again, you know your group to best determine whether than means Elite Soldiers on horses, demons similar to flying Blood Apes, or DB mercenaries.)

                                            I was wondering about two questions:

                                            (1) in regions of the world where nations and armies are not typically led by Dragonblooded (which I think is supposed to be most of the world that's not a Realm satrapy, Lookshy, or Prasad, although I'm not really sure), can an enemy nation be a threat to the PC's nation that demands more thought than "We'll just walk up to their army and fight it until it's routed"? If so, what kind of numbers do they need?
                                            Most states are ruled by mortals. But most states have access to something supernatural. Paragon is ruled by a mortal, but they have a Solar. Gem is ruled by a mortal, but they hire Solars. Nexus is ruled by mortals but guarded by a supernatural being who smacked down a 3CD and home to a Solar and his mercenary company. Harborhead is ruled by a mortal but blessed with a priesthood of warrior nuns directly empowered by the god of war. My own homebrew kingdom of Dovak is ruled by a mortal but protected by a warlike elemental.

                                            Then we add in Realm Satraps and Garison Commanders, Wyld Hunt and Immaculate Monks, Seventh Legion patrols, Outcasts mercenaries. There's no reason why a mortal kingdom can't get a little supernatural help.

                                            But none of that is necessary. It's just helpful.

                                            In a straight fight, you want to look for force multipliers. Good commanders. Abusing combined arms to exploit the action economy. Elite drill. An army the size of a Realm Legion is something like 5 Size 5 blocks of infantry, plus Auxiliary archers plus Cavalry. Plus over a thousand officers -- put just the ten Dragonlords in play as individual Elite Troops and the action economy really swings in your favor, but you could homebrew another Size 5 Battlegroup that can give commands to all the other battlegroups simultaneously on top of that to represent the junior officers. If the PCs just have one Size 3 unit of Tiger Warriors in their nation, that could be a pretty significant threat.

                                            But generally, you don't want a straight fight.

                                            Let the PCs destroy a Size 5 army of enemy mortal troops. Let them be bad-ass. Don't have the enemy's other two thousand troops just waiting to fight the PCs -- have them sack your PCs capital city and burn the fields whilst the PCs are busy fighting. Let them rout enemy armies, but they just run away and fight a guerrilla war -- welcome to the endless quagmire of military occupation. Let them slaughter enemy armies, but now there is a Shadowland. Let them conquer an enemy nation, but now the whole region fears them, has united against them, and is calling in The Realm to save them.

                                            Or give them a reason not to conquer enemy nations. Bonds of friendship and trade. Blessings of gods. Romeo and Juliet style live stories. A common enemy (The Realm, the Red Tide, The Bull of the North). What if the enemy has hostages?

                                            Or just say "fudge it" and give the mortals a Wandering Deathknight.

                                            (2) Wait, are any of these nations not led by Dragonblooded? Can an Outcaste DB just walk up to one of them and become the leader by asking nicely and then single-handedly fighting that nation's army if they decline?
                                            There are more city-states in Creation than there are Exalted. And in that Young Dynast fight, the Exalt was left totally drained and nearly dead -- assassin time.

                                            There are lots of states run by mortals.

                                            Then you have mortals with neat tricks, like a Raksha best friend, or a First Age artifact, or Sorcery. Mortals who just hire Outcastes to protect them.

                                            But... yeah... walking into a kingdom of mortals and just conquering it is totally in scope for a Dragon-Blooded. The Signature Fire Aspected Dragon-Blooded (Left Hand Chalima) literally did exactly that (and now struggles to manage the treasury, complete her paperwork, balance the cultural and religious concerns of her people, and has to hunt down the Anathema). It's a very appropriate Exalted thing to do.

                                            It seems like a nation that can field an army of multiple thousands is a threat to starting characters that is worthy of doing something interesting like finding allies, sowing dissension in the enemy ranks, or stealthily attacking supply lines to deal with, but no less, unless the Storyteller uses them in an indirect way.
                                            I think the big question is "what next".

                                            If an enemy nation has three thousand soldiers, they have them for a reason. No-one keeps the expense of a massive army if they don't need it.

                                            Your five starting Exalts might be able to kill them all on the field... But how do they now defend their conquered nation's boarders? And if they don't defend it, what are the consequences?

                                            Whereas, if they build up allies and raid supply lines and make the enemy soldiers switch sides, they might find themselves in a far better position to actually rule once the dust has settled.

                                            Then we've got stuff like PTSD. Shadowlands. Folks swearing revenge. Assassins. The Wyld Hunt. Sidereals.

                                            Just because the PCs can take an army head-on, doesn't mean they should.

                                                Orrrrrrrr...

                                                If your PCs seem like they'd enjoy soloing an army, maybe just focus on making that a cool and fun thing to do for their first outing. Then, afterwards, have NPCs they like point out how reckless that was, and all the things which could have gone wrong. And if they do it again, without taking appropriate precautions, hit them with all the cruel unintended consequences you can think of.

                                                In my experience, players respond better to that than to getting hit with the "how dare you fight people" hammer their first time out in an RPG with literal hundreds of combat powers.
