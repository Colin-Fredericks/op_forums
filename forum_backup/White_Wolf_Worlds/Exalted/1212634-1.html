                    Originally posted by Apromor View Post
                    I know, this isn't news. It's not like the Conan books haven't been referenced throughout the history of the line. Most relevantly there was the third edition statement that the sorcery rewrite was heavily influenced by them.
                    I've been re-reading some of my Karl Edward Wagner Kane books recently, and the treatment of sorcery in those strikes me as an even better match for Exalted 3e. In Darkness Weaves we see a sorceress using a quickly available spell against enemies (as well as the protagonist countering it at an opportune moment), summon a demonic familiar as a source of knowledge, and go through some explicitly described lengthy steps to prepare a sorcerous working that transmigrates her soul with another's. There are also surviving prehuman antiquities that make use of forgotten occult science beyond the understanding of people in the present day.

                            Right, I see my "political correctness" (I call it "ethics" and "basic decency") almost choked a couple dudebros. I'm honestly a little surprised, I remembered these forums as less of a shelter for Alt-Reich reactionaries, but we all have our disappointments, big and small.

                            What can I say? Conan doesn't exactly make my heart beat faster. It's mostly the story of a walking, white, cis, hetero, male power fantasy with almost no flaws, getting laid with any raven-tressed and ivory-skinned damsel in distress the author cares to lay, barely covered in jewelled lingerie and with the body of a goddess, at his feet. He's not without merit, as a character - he can be interesting, he can be cunning, he can be scared shitless (thank Mithras!), he's not completely invincible. I could do without his chivalrous attitudes and with more intelligent, capable, complex women (actually so, not just described as such).

                            Thoth-Amon is interesting as well, I would've liked to read more about him (and his sister, who was just fridged of course). Belit becomes useful - after she's dead. There are some rich and colorful descriptions in the books, and some decent fights - mostly involving weapons, which I'm admittedly a fan of (although if Howard deigned himself to study a little hoplology before writing about actual fights, maybe it would read a bit better), but also wits and will, and some sweet sorcerous imagery.

                            There's all the baggage you would expect from a guy who wrote in the '30s. He's not always racist, but Belit is a Shemite and as such dies for her greed (Jewish people and their attachment to money, natch!). Most people of non-white races tend to be treacherous, or foolish. Not always, and not only them, sure, but that's the trend. No homosexuals or similarly non-heteronormative characters in sight, just some homoerotic hints here and there and some evil almost-lesbians... no diversity in general...

                            I don't know, I'm not really a fan. Yes, sure, the 30's. Doesn't make it any more palatable.

                            Exalted? Exalted has a fuckton of complex female characters, of homosexual relationships, of actually different cultures whithout a clear superior one and "degenerate savages" blah blah blah. There are people with disabilities who can outfight or outsmart anyone in the neighbourhood, there are trans people, there are deeply flawed characters and beautiful plots and characterizations.

                            In all honesty I don't play Exalted to recreate crappy pulp from almost a century ago. But hey, you like Conan? Great news, have a blast! Peace out.
