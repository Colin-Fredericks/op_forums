        The other example, and I'm out on a bit of a limb here but I think I'm right, is Qrow vs. Winter.

        Team RWBY had always seemed fairly badass, and they ARE fairly badass. They fight high-end military equipment and a large terror cell by themselves. But I think the Qrow and Winter fight had a secondary purpose, which was to show the audience "Team RWBY are still just students; this is what truly elite Hunters look like when they throw down." Qrow and Winter moved, and fought, differently than Team RWBY did, with significantly more power, impact, and control. That whole thing conveyed the impression that if either Ruby or Weiss had actually decided to get involved, they'd have been stomped flat, because they simply don't play at that level yet.

        Volume 3 was far and away the best volume in so many ways. They did a lot of showing and not a lot of telling, and many of the scenes work on multiple levels.

                                    Thoughts on chapters 6, 7 and 8 of volume 5.

                                    Yeah, we sure lost a good bit of tempo here, didn't we? I'll agree with much of the criticism levied previously in the thread: there's a lot of wasted time. The thing I said about Blake being the low-energy front has gotten turned around, but not due to an increase in the pacing of Blake's story. This whole Ozpin training camp had some great potential, but it's essentially amounted to Ruby half-heartedly beating up a farmer boy. I'm not even sure Jaune, who was specifically called out by Ozpin, has done any onscreen training.

                                    RWBY is decidedly mediocre show when it comes to writing. Not only lines and dialogue, but in terms of event density. I'm currently reading Oathbringer by Brandon Sanderson, and I was recently struck by the vast difference in scene composition. Sanderson almost never lets a scene be just one thing; if he sets up a scene, it will serve the purposes of several different characters almost unfailingly. In RWBY, there are such scenes, but they are exceptions, as the show often favours dialogues between two sides about one thing.

                                    Don't get me wrong, though; some things have happened that are cool. Raven's and Yang's meeting and subsequent acquisition of Weiss was a good example of a multipurpose scene, and also a kind of hype moment that was cool to see. But it was also a very long scene, drawn out over two episodes (or, I guess, two separate scenes of which only the former was really multipurpose), and it's kind of emblematic of how RWBY goes. Hopefully we're getting out of the mid-season slump now when Cinder and team has shown up.


                                    Despite the criticism above, I will say that RWBY is still a pretty cool show. I do like watching it, even if it's decidedly been a "sure, why not" kind of deal for a long time (in fact, the short episodes and slow plot progression makes it very easy to follow with very low investment; not sure that's a compliment?). As such a "fan", I kind of feel I want to speak on some of the current topics in this thread.

                                    RWBY has changed. It's definitely become something quite different from what it started out as. I've heard there were plans laid for a large amount of seasons already before the premiere, but then we've also heard that Monty tended to come up with new shit he thought was cool now and then and just kind of put it in there. Therefore, it's impossible to say if the show had been more consistent throughout if he had gotten to remain with us, but I'm thinking odds are, it would have changed a fair bit none the less. We don't know how, exactly; perhaps more of the initial comedy and combat style had remained despite the shift in setting and plot lines.

                                    But how it might have been doesn't really matter now. Monty is gone, and I'm not sure if anyone thought that wouldn't impact the show? Monty was the primary combat choreographer and animator on the team, and when he was gone, there's really no way it wouldn't change. It'd have to, really. Now, it's fine to dislike that change; I certainly miss the tight combat animation, and the combat solutions they use now do have a decidedly different feel even if they're getting better by the volume. But I'm not sure I see the point of complaining about how things aren't the same. Yes, we know. Literally everyone wishes they were. There's a very tragic reason they aren't, and it's kind of impossible to do anything about.

                                    On another but related note, regarding fans criticising what they're fans of. Hopefully, unless it's your job to review stuff, people who don't like a show probably just won't watch it. Also, I think raising criticism about a show is a healthy sign of the ability to take a complex position on an issue, a quality direly lacking in certain aspects of society today. It might sometimes tip over on the negative, because it's easier to talk about on the internet, but I still think there's value in it.

                                    Dex Davican wrote: I can say without exaggeration or dishonesty that I am the most creative man ever to have lived
