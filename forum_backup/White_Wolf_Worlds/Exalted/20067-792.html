        Thanks!
        Not...exactly.

        There's interactions with movement which are one of the most complex parts of EX3, but mostly they work.


        So if you're starting at max range, say Long and your archer goes first. You can attack at Long range, but you need to aim first, so the archer uses an Aim action, and the melee user closes to Medium.

        Turn 2 starts, you can attack from Medium, but only if you aimed, which she did! So she fires a shot at medium and can move back if she wants. She retreats to Long range, then on his turn the melee fighter can close it back to Medium.

        Turn 3 starts. You can attack from Medium range, but only if you aim first, so the archer aims. Then on his turn the melee fighter closes to short and uses a Rush action. If he succeeds he is now rushing the archer.

        Turn 4 starts, the archer fires at the target in Short range, gaining +3 dice for aiming when within Short range, and tries to move back. If the melee fighter failed to rush, she can put him back to Medium range, if he succeeded, lets say he did, then as she moves back he gets to reflexively move with her. Then on his turn he closes to melee at last and attacks, lets say not dealing enough damage to swap the turn order.

        Turn 5 starts. Now the archer is in melee, but not trapped, she can use a Disengage action. What this does is lets her use her reflexive move to get out to Short range with her assailant. Let's say she succeeds. Now it goes to the melee fighter's turn, he moves after her, but because Disengage gives you a free reflexive movement to flee somebody chasing you like that he still doesn't get back into Close range. He can, if he wants, Rush her again instead.

        Turn 6 starts. The archer goes and attacks freely at the target at Short range, and retreats to Medium range. If the melee fighter didn't successfully rush her all he can do that turn is follow and try to Rush again. If he was successful, he's keeping pace with her and can close to Close distance again next turn.



        And it goes on and on like that, with the ranged fighter never being perfectly safe, but always having a chance to escape.
