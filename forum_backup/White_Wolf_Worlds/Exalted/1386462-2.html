                                                        I know that the corebook doesn't mention the word "Primordials" anymore, but everyone here knows what the word refers to.

                                                        We can agree that the likes of Cecelyne are not gods in the strict taxonomical sense. That's not my fault; I didn't choose to name Creation's gods "gods" instead of something slightly more nuanced. If gods were taxonomically called something other than gods, I would readily refer to more things in Exalted as gods.

                                                        "Yozi" can be viewed as more of a political term than a taxonomical one, if you want to include gods and elementals imprisoned in Malfeas under the umbrella of the word.

                                                        There's actually room to speculate that Primordials are particularly mighty elementals, since elementals predated the gods and many Primordials take the form of elemental forces.

                                                        There's no room to speculate that Primordials are ghosts, with the exception of the Neverborn.


                                                        I don't think anyone wants to go back to speculating that Primordials are just very large and powerful Unshaped Raksha Nobles, as some in the forum did during Second Edition.


                                                        Karvara was a behemoth from Zen-Mu. Last I checked, Zen-Mu was a world the Primordials created before Creation.

                                                        Karvara was not a creature born of the Wyld, but apparently belonged to the reality of Zen-Mu, and was an incarnation of one of its principles. Whatever that principle was, it was so thoroughly incompatible with Creation's reality that Karvara physically could not perceive Creation, and it destroyed anything of Creation it touched unless resisted by powerful magic. A Lunar Exalt managed to grapple with the beast, and his Dragon-Blooded followers managed to pierce and pin it with their artifact spears, and the Lunar's Solar Mate built a warstrider around Karvara to imprison and enslave it.

                                                        Karvara is plainly not a raksha. It is not of any species that serves the raksha as commoners.

                                                        Karvara was plainly not affected by iron, but it nevertheless might have been vulnerable to Earth-aspected Charms that hurt Raksha, for all that the Exalted discovered nothing that could actually kill the beast.

                                                        Or maybe Karvara's incarnated principle trumped Creation's reality so much that those anti-Raksha Earth-aspected Charms had no effect on Karvara at all, any more than they would against Primordials.


                                                        If the word "fae" refers to a creature alien to Creation's reality, and whose personal reality is weaker, then it is a useful term. Anything "fae" is vulnerable to effects that specifically target the Wyld and creatures like the Raksha.

                                                        If Karvara was completely immune to such effects, then trying to apply the word "fae" to it would rob the word of useful meaning.

                                                        For simply referring to anything not born of Creation, "alien" would be more useful.

                                                            You know what was actually a pretty cool Lunar thing in First Edition that I'd like to see return?

                                                            Songlines.

                                                            Songlines are a technique used by humans to combat the Wyld. As people pass through a land, they compose a song of the journey focusing on the natural features they observe along the way, and then they pass those songs down to future generations.

                                                            Songlines work like oral road-maps, allowing future generations to navigate the same path again in the future, which is useful enough in Creation, but it takes on deeper dimensions closer to the Wyld.

                                                            Songlines allow human societies at the world's edge to impose order upon lands where they currently do not dwell, by singing the songs of those lands to describe them exactly as as their ancestors observed them.

                                                            Songlines would become even more effective when sung as humans take the journey in person, as they harness the power of all previous generations singing the song and traveling the road before them, while putting their own present power into the song. Even if the land had become Wyld, the Wyld itself would be forced to conform to the story of the songline.


                                                            I think it would be interesting if Raksha couldn't fight the power of an old and faithfully-preserved songline. They would need to attack the people themselves, either killing them or tricking them into forsaking the tradition of singing the songlines, or getting them to change the song, or simply forget it in part or in whole.

                                                            formerly Tornado Wolf, formerly Inugami
