    Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
    There are plenty of ways you can ignore or downplay the Reclamation, or have it be less than total cosmic decimation even if it does happen.
    No. You really can't except in the sense of completely ignoring things, but then we're in the same territory as imperialism apologia for the Realm, portraying the Perfect of Paragon as the good kind of absolute dictator, or (less negatively) handwaving the genre tropes of clasic Silver Age Superheroes. Those are fine if that's the kind of thing you want, but it's not fine insofar as it's not really the default presentation of Exalted which is all about "fantastic powers in a brutally down to earth background." I.e., the old comments about an Exalted battlefield being a scene out of Saving Private Ryan, except every so often a couple of demigods touch the ground and everyone around them explodes.

    At best, you can lessen the idea of a Reclamation to something akin to what the original 3e Infernal preview material suggested, i.e. it's a very long term ploy that is not guaranteed to succeed. But the full on Return of the Scarlet Empress-style idea? The Yozis breaking out of Hell is the single most threatening existential crisis that can face Creation. Full stop. (At most it may seem like it isn't, if we're reducing the Yozis to 2e Infernals-style charm robots, but given the Primordial War was vastly destructive to Creation and the death of a Primordial has enough bad side effects imprisoning them seemed like a better idea...)

    The Primordials' role to be something incomprehensibly, inhumanly vast.... that can still be killed, with great effort. This is no different than a venomous spider being able to kill a human without that spider understanding the first thing about how how a television, atomic bombs, or bug spray works; the human is still capable of vastly more impactful actions on the world than that spider. If you handwave or downplay that, you're either not playing the 'epic titanomachy setting' of Infernals so many people want, or, frankly, you're doing a shitty job of portraying Primordials.

I just don't see why the characters would find any motivation to fight Reclamation, unless every book repeats saying that Reclamation is happening just right now and you have to kill this and this guy to prevent it. However, this clearly won't happen in any near future.

                Honestly, I am thinking of my current campaign and if some random person would tell PCs a story about Reclamation without any further hint of how this relates to their current situation, then I can't imagine this would immediately affect their actions. Of course, maybe the group savant would start reading through ancient lore, but I can hardly imagine how this could disrupt the natural flow of the events. They can't just chase every legend around the world, while the Syndicate of Three Spiders is flooding Jiro province with strange occult drug just right now. I mean, subtle hints that there are links to greater picture are fun. When the history escalates in the organic way, it's fun, and when at some point, late in the campaign, they have to travel on First-age spaceship where Gaia left in order to understand how to stop the Paradox which Broken the Seal, well this might be immensely fun, Obviously, if you don't wan't your story to escalate that much, it is ok. I don't always want my stories to do so.

                I mean, clearly reclamation could easily be a looming prophecy in-character even now. I don't understand how it makes any campaign more interesting to just state in boldface out-of-character in the book: "by the way, these stories are objectively wrong. Good night."

                                                Please, note how this discussion started. Some posters claimed that even the potential for Infernals to become new Primordials is destructive for the game precisely because it shifts it away from humanly concerns.

                                                At this point, we agree third edition clearly has got some more cosmic tropes and I am happy that I don't need to defend their presence or prove that they don't invalidate more grounded troubles.

                                                Now I claim that the potential for player characters to eventually move to that cosmic tier is also not detrimental to the core premises of the game, especially for Infernals where the roadmap is really fun and especiallly since such Infernals are a great source of reccurring and evolving antagonists.

                                                You are right that punching above the weightclass is one of main motifs. But note that Exalted also tends to subvert that topos. If this really was a classical sword and sorcery then there would be one obvious candidate for an enemy above the weightclass: Scarlet Empress. But here she is probably not personally more powerful than PCs, totally contrary to usual tropes. Nephracks, ghost sorcerers should be totally above PCs weightclass under more canonical take. So I don't think that moving this boundary further, especially in the climax of a campaign and moving capabilities to something conceptually different is really that out of place.

                                                It isn't really totally outside of the scope of Sword and Sorcery either. In Wolff's New Sun saga, Severian is basically an example of such character and how I imagine that kind of play.
