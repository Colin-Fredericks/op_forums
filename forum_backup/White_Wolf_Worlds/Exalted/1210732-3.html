                        Social Abilities

                        Right anyway before sleep tonight on to the actual proper social versus analysis that I promised to do. This should be much better thought out and easier to read than my last post.

                        I actually typed out a huge analysis of every ability, comparing and contrasting each, but it was repetitive. The real problem is that there IS no comparing the two, they competencies in each area barely overlap, amazingly. Where they do overlap however, there is a theme. Basically the Solar version of each ability is focused on doing what the mortal version does really really well. It's broad and it's powerful. They use dice tricks, charms that discount costs, and some other tricks to influence or read multiple people at once, or forcing situations where they can apply it.


                        Terrestrials do things very differently. In the areas where their charms overlap with the Solar competencies the Dragonblooded version tend to be both less powerful and more focused. The focus is usually on passions, inciting them, stifling them, seeking them out. It also focuses on loyalties, forming them quickly, and breaking them in others. In these places they can't be said to surpass Solars, not really, but they can do things Solars can't. Like instantly form a tie of loyalty to another DB and gain bonus diced based on that for the scene.

                        But here's the thing, what Dragonblooded CAN do with these abilities is just awesome. Like a presence charm which lets you breathe fire in quite a powerful attack honestly is pretty powerful. DB combat charms let you fight less efficiently than a Solar but in interesting and unique ways. DB social charms do that sometimes, like being able to instantly form or strengthen an intimacy for another terrestrial when they're in danger, but at other times they apply the idea of the ability outside the context of the system. Like the performance charm that lets you hide an ambush in a performance, and the integrity charm which lets you seek vengeance without rest, food, or drink, so long as you do nothing but seek retribution.

                        What this does that's so great is that, true, you're often relying on your excellency and maybe a few other tricks based on passionate intimacies to get by in the social system, your social DB has a much broader array of capabilities than a similarly focused Solar. For example if a super social focused Solar gets jumped in a diplomatic meeting, they're kind of relying on their bodyguards and few scant combat charms to get by. A DB who's similarly presence focused inhales deep and turns the would-be assassins to ash with a presence charm.


                        So yeah, basically that's it for a power comparison. The DB versions, after some careful consideration, are decidedly weaker, except in a few key areas, like it's very difficult to get a properpy focused terrestrial to betray their sworn kinship, for example. In those areas they might almost be a match for Solars, but they lag far far behind in other areas, often having only their excellency to work with. If you're going up against a Solar in the social game, you're going to need to leverage passions and loyalties to even have a shot, and even then prepare to have this escalate into a fight as they try and wrestle away your granite hard intimacies and you get frustrated trying to pierce their defenses. Lucky for you, your presence charm includes adding health levels and soak and his doesn't.



                        I might do a comparison on the rest of the non-combat stuff like Athletics and Craft if anyone wants to see it. Those systems are a lot more crunchy and leave less up to the ST so you can expect a bit more clear cut contrasts. I've got some stuff to say about Craft and Athletics for sure.

                                                    Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                    Take, for example Thunderbolt Attack Prana. An athletics charm actually! Which costs 4m 1wp, supplemental, can be comboed with any other ability, allows the user to jump a range band in any direction and DOUBLES damage after it is rolled. This alone means that a Solar can be deadly at as little as 8 initiative.

                                                    Now lets see what we can combo this with. I like the look of Invincible Fury of the Dawn there. For 5m 1wp that lets me make up to 5 attacks, distributed how I please, move a range band between each attack, and instantly gain initiative equal to the number of attacks I want to make. Also, apart from the excellency, and charm I use on one attack can be used for free on the others. It can also be activated reflexively after using Peony Blossom Technique.

                                                    So since all the stuff is free past the first I might as well load up, Excellent Strike (3m) rerolls 1s until they fail to appear, neutralizing a common DB trick to boost their defense in the process, and Hungry Tiger (3m 2i), which lets our overflow attack successes add to the damage.

                                                    Plus, of course, Rising Sun Slash, which activates on a straight 7,8,9,10 being rolled and gives me one bonus non-charm sux and (essence) non-charm dice on the attack roll.

                                                    OKAY so we're ready to make our attack. With as little as 10 initiative, lets see what we can do. We'll make 5 attacks, instantly giving us 5 initiative. That's 5 attacks with 3 base damage, then we attack. First we use Perfect Strike Discipline to give our first attack a full free excellency, between that and a level 2 stunt we're basically rocking 25 dice to attack, plus rerolling 1s, plus a big non-charm bonus with Rising Sun Slash, which is surprisingly common at those dice levels. Against a typical DB Defense of, say, 10, they've got no hope. Simplified calculations give us around 16 successes. That overflows by 6 so we get to deal 9 damage, which is then doubled thanks to TAP, giving us around 7.2 health levels, which is lethal against somebody without ox-bodies. If we drop our first target, great, if not, hit them again. When they drop we then reset Perfect Strike Discipline, for another free full excellency, and can jump a range band if we have to in order to strike our next target, and so on.

                                                    If we have just one target the onslaught penalty keeps racking up so that's good too.

                                                    And if we have a charm called Foe-Cleaving Focus we actually regenerate a mote for every 10 on an attack that triggers Rising Sun Slash. Which is good because that took like, at least 20-25 motes and 3-4 willpower.


                                                    People are going to point out that wearing artifact full plate would use hardness to defend decently against that, but this was just an example to show how Solar charms work, I don't want to proc a mechanics arms race to infinite xp.

                                                    A few things worth noting:

                                                    TAP doubles damage after it is rolled. This means it is very vulnerable to below-average roll results. "A Solar can be deadly at 8 initiative" is, therefore, a bit of a misleading statement - there is a more than 10% chance that the attack will deal 0 or 2 damage, and a more than 20% chance that it will only deal 4 damage (inflicting a mere -1 wound penalty on a character with even a single Ox-Body purchase). Over five attacks, your chance of getting no abnormally low result is roughly 0.7^5 - slightly less than 17%. And you're paying a point of Willpower for the privilege every time. Which you also need to fuel your Perfect Strike Discipline.

                                                    Moreover, Hungry Tiger Technique can only be used on crashed targets, which most characters you're alpha striking are definitely not going to be. (Granted, if you're wielding Stormcaller, this requirement can be waived... if you manage to get your storm's Intensity up to 6. Good luck doing that over the course of a single round.) This means you're going to have to use Fire and Stones Strike instead, for a much more reasonable (Essence or 3, which, let's not kid ourselves, is going to work out to 3 in the overwhelming majority of cases) extra damage per attack.

                                                    Out of curiosity, let's calculate the probability of missing a maxed attack against a fully specialized Dragon-Blooded defense! You've got 11 dice as a base, +2 stunt, +8 excellency to round it out, so you're rolling 21 dice plus an auto-success against a defense of 6 +3 excellency +1 stunt +1 willpower (after all, if he's going to die anyway, he may as well spend that point, not like he's gonna use it after). Anydice tells me that after taking Excellent Strike into account, this pool has a 74.91% chance to hit a defense of 11, which is far from guaranteed - but also ignores Rising Sun Slash. Sadly, while I can easily calculate the probability of successfully proccing RSS on a 21 dice roll, going through the hassle of determining the likelihood of successfully proccing it on a roll that would've missed otherwise, but managed to hit because of it is not really worth my time.


                                                    All things considered, no matter how awesomely kitted out a Dawn might be, I'd always put my money on a team of five E3 shikari in decent artifact gear and a reasonable number of Ox-Body purchases, just by virtue of the massive collective mote pool and action economy advantage. Even if the Dawn manages to doomcombo one or two right out of the gate, he'll tire himself out in the process, and the other side has +10 motes per turn to spend and three times the chance of landing a hit.
