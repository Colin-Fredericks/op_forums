    "Exalts and Excellencies"-- Playing Exalted with Mutants and Masterminds 3e Rules

    11-05-2021, 03:20 PM
    Exalted has an awesome setting of fantasy superheroes, but complicated mechanics that break down if pressed too far. Mutants and Masterminds has excellent mechanics for playing superheroes, but is oriented towards modern adventures. I tried to unite the two a long time ago, but it it didn't work particularly well. But I've learned a lot more about game design since then, and a new and superior edition of Exalted has been released and grown into a fairly mature game. So... time to try again, perhaps.

    I think my previous attempt ran into trouble because I was trying not to depart too far from the basic M&M rules1-- but at the same time, I was trying to import mote expenditure/anima rules with much more granularity than the system allowed. I'm not going to do that again. I'm keeping the core M&M rules for powers and action, but I'm taking a hatchet to the superficial structure of the game. If nothing else, I intend to replace the lingering D&D trappings with equally-insubstantial Exalted ones

    But anyway.
    Unless otherwise specified, all rules from the Hero's Handbook still apply normally.

    Core System
    Ranks and Measures
    Superhero capabilities scale faster and reach greater heights than even Celestial Exalts. Use the following Ranks and Measures table in place of the one presented in the Hero's Handbook. Because the values don't increase at the same consistent rates, you cannot calculate speed simply by adding Time and Distance ranks--you have to actually do the math. Or look at the "SPEED" column, where the numbers are already worked out and rounded off nicely for you.

    Anima
    As they tap into more and more of their power, an Exalt's anima grows increasingly powerful. It passes through the normal stages described in the Exalted rulebook-- Dim, Glowing, Burning, Bonfire/Iconic-- with the same effects on visibility. Unlike traditional Exalted, it is not an involuntary result of channeling too much magic too fast--instead, it represents how much power the Exalt is choosing to exercise. They are never forced to increase their anima level (except through Complications; see below). Instead, an Exalt can flare their anima to any level desired as a free action, even during another character's turn. They cannot choose to suppress it, but must wait 15 minutes for it to weaken to the next lower stage. Thus, full Bonfire/Iconic anima takes 45 minutes to completely fade.

    Without their anima, however, an Exalt's power is limited. At Dim anima, Exalts may not use any powers with a rank higher than one-half their power level, rounded down. If they have an effect with a higher rank, they may choose to use it at a rank low enough to fit this limit. For example, a PL 8 Dawn Caste with a charm providing Enhanced Strength 6 could only increase their strength by 4 when at Dim anima.

    Glowing anima is enough to use powers at full full strength. Increasing levels of anima continue to provide increasing benefits, varying by the type of Exalt.
    • Dragonblooded radiate destructive elemental power. This is a Damage effect with a rank equal to their current anima level-- 1 at Glowing anima, 2 at Burning, and 3 at Bonfire/Iconic-- and the Area (Burst) modifier. Other Dragonblooded are immune to this effect, but other allies are not.
    • Celestial Exalted receive a bonus to attacks, defenses, all effect ranks, and skill checks equal to their current anima level-- +1 at Glowing anima, +2 at Burning, and +3 at Bonfire/Iconic.

    Exalts can run low on magical power, but it's treated as a Complication when it happens (see below).

    Willpower
    Characters in Exalts and Excellencies do not receive hero points. Instead, they use Willpower, as in Exalted proper.

    A character's natural Willpower is equal to one-half their power level, rounded down. They begin play with full natural Willpower. If they every have less, they regain one point per night of rest until they reach their natural Willpower again.

    Characters may earn extra Willpower, though, up to a maximum equal to their full power level. Ways of earning willpower include:
    1wp as a bonus for a particularly cool stunt or bit of roleplaying.
    1-2 wp when following their Intimacies (see below) gets them into real trouble--1wp if it's the sort of thing that can be cleared up in a session or two, and 2wp if it's going to have long-lasting consequences.
    1-2 wp when the GM imposes a Complication, depending on just how bad it is.

    Once they've accumulated some Willpower, it can be used in many ways. The following uses cost 1 point of willpower:
    • Activate a Fortune advantage
    • Attempt to counter an effect used against you as a reaction--however, you lose your next standard action.
    • Edit Scene, as described in the Hero's Handbook
    • Gain the benefits of an Advantage you don't normally have for a scene
    • Increase an effect's rank, or your Strength or Speed, by 1 until the beginning of your next turn.
    • Power Stunt, as described in the Hero's Handbook
    • Resist an instance of social influence (see below)

    More powerful results can be achieved by spending 2wp:
    • Attempt to counter an effect used against you as a reaction without losing your next action.
    • If you fail to resist an Affliction or Damage effect, reduce your degree of failure by 1 step, to a minimum of zero.
    • Recover from being dazed, stunned, or fatigued, even if it's not your turn.
    • Reroll, adding 10 if the result on the die is 10 or lower, as described in the Hero's Handbook
    • Take an additional standard action.

    Complications
    Exalts don't need to list specific Complications if they don't want to--their Major and Defining Intimacies are more than enough to get them into trouble.

    Anima is another common source of Complication. If the GM feels that an Exalt is using too much power too obviously, they may force that character's anima to flare. The player is compensated with 1wp if they're forced to Glowing anima, and 2wp if forced to Burning or Bonfire/Iconic.

    Conversely, Exalts can also tire after prolonged use of power. The GM may give a player 1wp to declare that they're too tired to reach an anima level higher than Glowing for the remainder of the scene. For 2wp, all anima is off-limits for the scene--the Exalt cannot rise about Dim anima.

    Social Combat
    ...actually works almost exactly as it does in standard Exalted. Intimacies, Instill / Persuade / Threaten actions, are virtually unchanged. The only differences are the bonuses and penalties imposed by Intimacies-- 2 for a Minor Intimacy, 5 for a Major, and 10 for Defining-- and the calculation of Resolve and Guile scores. A character's Resolve is equal to 10 + their Integrity or Resolve bonus, whichever is higher; their Guile is equal to 10 + their Socialize bonus.

    Character Traits

    Abilities
    Are cut altogether. Characters simply do not have Dexterity, Stamina, and so on, and do not take penalties-- the normal effects of Absent abilities are instead a 10-point Quirk. When calculating melee damage, unmodified Toughness, and how much you can lift, use one-half your power level instead, rounded down.

    Skills
    The skill list in Exalts and Excellencies is changed to mirror that of Exalted:
    • Athletics
    • Awareness
    • Bureaucracy
    • Craft
    • Investigation
    • Larceny
    • Linguistics
    • Lore
    • Medicine
    • Occult
    • Performance
    • Presence
    • Resolve
    • Ride
    • Sail
    • Socialize
    • Stealth
    • Survival
    • War

    Excellencies
    The simplest exercise of power for an Exalt is the excellency, a direct boost to strength and competence. When using an excellency, you may roll 2d20 and take the better of the two results; characters can use excellencies a number of times per session equal to half their power level, rounded down.

    Supernal Skills
    Solars choose one skill as their particular area of mastery. They receive a free +10 bonus to their chosen skill, and if they roll an 8 or less on the d20 when attempting a check, their roll is treated as an 8 instead.

    Weapon Proficiencies
    Characters don't have to invest in Close Combat and Ranged Combat skills. Instead, for 1 pp they may select one of the following weapon groups. When making an attack with a weapon or power your are proficient in, your bonus to hit is (2*power level - effect rank)--exactly enough to satisfy the power level tradeoffs.

    The standard weapon groups are as follows. New groups can be created with the GM's permission.
    • Assassin's Tools-- Unarmed, dagger, needle, shuriken, blowgun, hand crossbow
    • Axes-- Axe, throwing axe, greataxe, scythe
    • Clubs-- Club, mace, sledge, staff
    • Bows-- Sling, sling staff, self bow, composite bow, longbow
    • Brawling-- Unarmed, cestus, improvised, dagger
    • Chains-- Whip, fighting chain, flail
    • Crossbows-- Hand crossbow, crossbow, ballista
    • Firewands-- Flame piece, firewand
    • Heavy Weapons-- Greataxe, greatsword, sledge
    • Light Weapons-- Dagger, dart, tiger claws, war fan, sap, sai
    • Magic-- Spells, non-weapon artifacts, anima blasts
    • Martial Arts-- Unarmed, sai, tiger claws, war fan, fighting chain, hook sword
    • Polearms-- Spears, staffs, lance, halberd
    • Swords-- Shortsword, sai, chopping/slashing/straight sword, greatsword, hook sword
    • Throwing Weapons-- Dagger, dart, javelin, thrown axe (throwing only), chakram, shruiken, boomerang

    Advantages
    Advantages work normally. If they involve a skill that no longer exists, choose the most appropriate alternative, as listed below.
    • Agile Feint-- Athletics or move speed
    • Artificer-- Uses Craft (Artifacts)
    • Attractive-- Applies to Presence and Socialize
    • Connected-- Presence
    • Contacts-- Investigation
    • Daze-- Presence or Socialize
    • Favored Foe-- Awareness, Presence, Socialize
    • Grabbing Finesse-- Athletics
    • Improvised Trip-- Athletics only
    • Improvised Weapon-- Replace with proficiency in the Brawling weapon group.
    • Inventor-- Craft (as appropriate)
    • Ritualist-- Uses Occult
    • Startle-- Presence
    • Taunt-- Socialize
    • Tracking-- Awareness
    • Well-Informed-- Investigation or Socialize
    In addition, the following tweaks are recommended for use in any Mutants and Masterminds games.
    • Favored Foe-- Replace the effect with "when fighting your favored foe, you may gain a +2 circumstance bonus to either attacks or defenses. The bonus only applies to attacks by and against a favored foe, and you may only use one type of bonus per round, as the Favored Environment advantage.
    • Improved Critical--Limit of 2 ranks
    • Takedown--The second rank is banned.
    Character Creation
    Caste
    An Exalt boasts certain abilities that are unique to their Caste.

    Solar Castes
    • Dawn: You can attempt to intimidate any creature, even those with an Immunity or absent ability. For 2wp, you may treat a successful attack as a critical hit.
    • Eclipse: You have diplomatic immunity with enemies of creation. For 2wp, you may sanctify an oath. Both abilities function as normal for Exalted.
    • Night: While your anima is at Bonfire/Iconic, your identity is concealed from all prying eyes short of the Unconquered Sun himself. For 2wp, you may receive a surge of power, allowing you to act as if you were at Bonfire/Iconic level anima until the beginning of your next turn, or the end of your next turn if you activate this ability on someone else's turn. While empowered, you can use powers at full strength and gain a +3 bonus to attacks, defenses, effect ranks, and skills.
    • Twilight: You can bind a friendly spirit to your anima. As an action, you may cause it to dematerialize and merge with your anima; as another action, you may manifest it in an adjacent space. For 2wp, you may use an action to dematerialize yourself. Until sunset, you effectively do not exist; when the sun goes down, you appear at a random significant location within 10 miles of your starting point.
    • Zenith: As an action, you may incinerate a body or lay a spirit to rest and possibly take on one of its intimacies, as normal for Exalted. For 2wp, you can use your action to order a dematerialized spirit into the material plane--for the remainder of the scene, it does not benefit from any form of Insubstantial.

    Charms
    An Exalt's powers are also known as charms. Each power be associated with a skill that you have a positive modifier with, and each ability can support one array with up to (half your power level, rounded down) total base and alternate powers.

    You can buy ranks of Enhanced Strength, even though the ability score does not normally exist. Ranks of Enhanced Strength stack with your natural melee damage and lifting ability. Immortality and Movement (Time Travel) are not allowed under any circumstances--though magical creatures such as Exalts are largely or totally immune to the ravages of time, everything can die.

    Artifacts
    Artifacts in Exalted are exactly equivalent to devices in Mutants and Masterminds. If an Exalt obtains a new artifact, they may spend 1wp to attune to it for a scene. While attuned, they can use it normally.

    Permanent attunement can be accomplished over the course of an hour. Doing so allows you to use the artifact indefinitely--but until you spend enough power points to buy it normally, you suffer an attunement penalty. This reduces the rank of the artifact's effects, with a severity based on how many power points you still must commit. Attunement penalty can't reduce an effect's rank to less than half of its starting value.

    To minimize the attunement penalty, an Exalt can permanently transfer some of their essence into the artifact. This allows them to redistribute the power points they have spent on charms to take advantage of the new artifact, potentially reducing the number of power points they still must commit.

    Attunement penalties based on remaining power points are:
    • Less than 5: -1
    • 5-10: -2
    • 11-15: -3
    • 16-20: -4
    • 21 or more: -5


    Power Level and Power Points
    As a standard guideline, Exalts begin at power level 8 with 100pp (anima abilities and cheaper weapon skills are judged to be roughly equal to the extra 20pp a level 8 character would normally start with).

    At the end of each session, Exalts receive 1-2 power points to improve their character. The improve to power level 9 when they have 125 total power points, power level 10 when they have 150 total power points, level 11 with 200 total points, power level 12 with 300 total points, and power level 13 with 500 total points.
    Terrestrial Exalted
    Dragonblooded instead start at PL 7 with 100pp and advance their power level at the same benchmarks as Celestial Exalts. Typically it's impossible for a Dragonblooded to exceed power level 9-- advancing beyond that typically requires access to unique magics or artifacts.
    Equipment and Wealth
    Games of Exalts and Excellencies should use the Wealth rules presented in the Gamemaster's Guide in place of the Equipment advantage. New characters enter the game with a Wealth of 5.

    Each rank of the Benefit (Resources) advantage increases starting Wealth by 5. At the end of each month that a character's Wealth is less than the bonus provided by their ranks of Benefit, their Wealth increases by 1 point per rank, to a maximum of said bonus. This represents profit earned from continuing investment in land, merchants, and so on.

    Intimacies
    At character creation, you must select four Intimacies. You must have one of each level, one positive, and one negative.
