            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
            I don’t ever want to read about another Lunar that decides to basically sleep away the better part of a millennium.
            That's fine; this is an extremely minor disagreement. All that I'm doing is answering the question of the kinds of things I would be interested in being in the book; I have my interests, you have yours. It's not a hill I have an interest in dying on.

            ​However, I do think that it's worth bringing up that Vance has stated that he likes the idea of Lilith having spent a long time as an owl, although I fully recognise that this in no way specifies it in terms of my preferences rather than yours.

            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold
            These people were Chosen by Luna and invested with the power to rock the world.
            ​On the one hand, I don't think that the intentions of one's patrons is the most important metric by which to measure the life of an Exalt. On the other, this just goes back to my point about the conceptual and in-character exhaustion of expecting the Solar and Lunar Exalted to always be closing, without break or relief, for thousands of years.

            ​The world can't always be rocking, because otherwise it falls over.

            ​That and my perspective, which may be way off the mark, that Lunars striving for freedom can entail them employing their powers mostly for their own leisures, as well as expanding the range of what those leisures are. I see that as something in them a bit more than in the Solars or Sidereals.

            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold
            Can you point to any other Exalt who spent as many as 50 years attempting to accomplish absolutely nothing?
            ​Setting aside the inherently loaded nature of the question with the phrasing "attempting to accomplish absolutely nothing" (which seems like a particularly pejorative way of viewing, say, somebody that has gone into retirement), I have an entire First Age in which to cheerfully make several of them up.

            ​I could also argue that Prince Aridhara eschewing the works of the mighty for the life of a travelling peddler, aiding the ordinary folk with small sorceries and subtle kindnesses, is closer to what I'm saying than what you are.

            Do you think she was a Lunar?

            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold
            Normal owls fear being eaten or failing to catch food. Somehow I doubt she would tolerate going hungry unless she refused to hunt for the sake of experiencing gnawing hunger, or getting injured except out of a masochistic desire to bleed.
            ​Hmm, I was initially inclined to dismiss this as pedantry that did not undermine the substance of my point, buuutt...

            ​Actually it's interesting, you do actually bring up a point that I've been wondering while watching the BBC documentary series The Hunt on Netflix in the last few days; if a Lunar opting to spend an extended period of time in the form of a predatory animal might undermine local ecosystems by virtue of the capacity to be a vastly more successful predator than any natural one, considering how part of the balancing systems of an ecology is the indelible fact that most hunts end in failure.

            ​If a Lunar is in the form of a blue whale, are they concerned with the sheer energy expenditure that it takes just to open their enormous mouths, and if not, is the blue whale Lunar willing to consume groups of krill that would not be dense enough to provide a mortal whale with sufficient calories? If so, does a Lunar going about in such a manner risk damaging the delicate balance of the ecosystem?

            ​Or if there's something instinctual to being a Lunar, especially while they're assuming the shape of an animal (which I'm inclined to view as something that basically every Lunar will frequently do for pure leisure reasons, especially seeing as their spirit shape is supposed to be a true form to them), it may be that a Lunar can naturally settle into a pattern of behaviour that means that they don't go around causing massive ecological devastation by virtue of the power of an Exalt every time they want to spend a week vacationing in the form of basically anything.

            ​So, while it is possible to regard the narrative of Lilith living an extended time as an owl in terms of her being a successful predator to the point that she quickly wipes out prey species within her flight radius, and either is constantly spreading her ecological devastation around, or is living as a kind of avian Shelob surrounded by a circle of quiet emptiness...

            ​If I'm taking my cues from the First Edition core chapter fiction that introduced her, in which her assumption of owl form seems to go beyond an act of costume play and into a retreat from humanity and consciousness, I would actually be seeing things in terms of her having been an owl that would be frequently hungry and occasionally get a bit scratched up by a larger bird of prey swooping down upon her, even if on the whole she manages to be a bird that is a slightly more successful predator than the norm, who generally managed to outfly other predators, and always struggled free any time they caught her.

            ​I regard that in terms of an uncanny and mystical (in the sense of actual mystics, "forsake all possessions and live in silence in the freaking desert" kinds of mystics) thing for a single, solitary Lunar to have been doing with her time. Partially because I'm simply enamoured with the fiction that introduced Lilith, partially because I like the idea of a famed ancient warrior Lunar having retreated into complete obscurity throughout the Second Age until such Time of Tumult as she is provoked back into action. And partially just in its own right, for the uncanniness and mysticism of a shapeshifter.

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                            I want to reiterate that what I meant there by "retirement" was the idea that, in the First Age, you might get some Exalted who need a break from the life of intrigue and adventure every few centuries, and wind up in a life situation in which they're doing something peaceful and mundane. They might occasionally use their powers to do something impressive in that context, such as hoist an entire tree trunk on their shoulders for firewood, but still just putting the call in their Essence aside for a while.

                            ​I'm compelled by the image that Lea has described in his conception of the relationship between Merela and the Unconquered Sun, in which they hook up every once in a while, except where for them hooking up entails living in a domestic co-habitation for decades and raise a few kids, because they've both got the time.

                            ​However, those are part of my being contrary to the basic principle of "the Exalted should be doing interesting things all their lives". The example of what I'm seeing with Lilith is specific and extreme both in what it entails for her personality, and in the idea of her intending it to be permanent.

                            That and how I would enjoy the narrative of her being an infamous warrior that is only just returning to the game if, for the intervening period, she was unobtrusive.

                            ​Leviathan's story, in which his retreat from society entails him becoming a great terror of the open oceans, that's a great story. I love that story. I don't want Lilith's story to be that one in a different biome.

                            ​I couldn't say much for Magnificent Jaguar beyond not particularly minding that he's a person that looked at the downfall of the Age and decided to sleep it out.

                            ​We've got some other First Age Lunars that are a bit more active, and maybe room for a few others.

                            ​Although on the other hand, I wouldn't exactly mind the idea that the surviving First Age Lunars are not necessarily the best suited to be driving the thrust of the Silver Pact. That the likes of your Sha'a Oka might look at Leviathan and feel as though his perspective and world view for having actually lived in the First Age is practically alien, as well as maybe the idea that having actually lived through what the loss of the Solars and downfall of the Old Realm has done to their Essence and needing to have their Castes remade might have given them some damage that they're perhaps better off being in the distance dealing with.

                                                    The past tradition of the Silver Pact achieving little since the Usurpation is a related but separate issue from the tradition of how none of the First Age survivors seems to be able to cope with depression or trauma even with several mortal life-times to rest, reflect, and recover.

                                                    But for this Edition, Ma-Ha-Suchi’s depressive sulk might be as much because of some relatively recent setback or defeat or loss, rather than one long, unbroken pity-party stretching back to the Usurpation.

                                                    Magnificent Jaguar might be given a different reason for his long sleep, or maybe a different time-frame.

                                                    Leviathan might be actively fighting the Realm rather than just hiding away, or bullying a sunken city of mortals and Dragon-Blooded. He might have better reasons to be with-holding powerful First Age weapons from other Lunars besides “I don’t feel like actually exerting myself yet”.

                                                    Lilith might have gotten over her abuse centuries ago and just decided she doesn’t give a shit about the Realm, choosing to remain a retired hermit out of preference, but still remaining connected enough to the world that she can hear about the return of the Solars, so maybe she might accept Lunar students who seek her out for training in return for favors.

                                                    In past Editions, the Lunar Exalted were victims. Even the Wyld victimized them.

                                                    This time, they are victors. They broke their castes to remake them on purpose. They endured the Wyld like no one else could. They are slowly winning the war for the Caul and against the Realm, at least for now.

                                                    I’d like to see them allowed to have the emotional strength to overcome their psychological challenges as well.
