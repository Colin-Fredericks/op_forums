They train in their powerset and bitch about their parents and get into trouble and spy on each other and occasionally save the world, just like their parents do." It gives me more "bodies" to work with; I can't expand the Five-Score Fellowship directly, after all.

    Best use I ever made of one was when I made her the PCs boss, because she was older than them and had longer chops with the Bureau. One of my PC's fucking hated that their Exalted could be bossed around by some Half-Caste purely because she was older, wiser, and really good at her job. (This guy wasn't a problem player by any means but he had a really huge sense of "Exalted rule, others obey" when it came to the setting.)                 Maybe be responsible enough to invest in some decent contraceptives. It's not really fitting to the intended image, so not really.
                See, I have to disagree. I think the idea of Sidereals in Resplendent Destinies conceiving children to be absolutely in-line with their image. When I think of the Sidereals, the group I use as personal inspiration is the Bene Gesserit of Dune. And for the Bene Gesserit, children created by their members were a major part of their political plans -- a child could be easily used as a pawn in their schemes, and even the untalented and extraneous children could be worked into some kind of position where they were useful. While the Bene Gesserit had a long-term goal different than the Sidereals, it was clear from Dune that in the short term, on a practical level, the Bene Gesserit were cultivating lines of aristocrat children to create an environment in which they could do their business easier, or to have leaders more sympathetic to their plans.

                I could see a Chosen of Serenity or Endings creating a Resplendent Destiny as a wife for a powerful tyrant, and giving birth to the tyrant's child in order to have somebody on the throne for stability's sake after the Sidereal poisons the child's father. That the child would also be tied to the Sidereal in a magical way would no doubt be a plus for her plans. A callous Sidereal would probably be apt to create children for the sake of an assignment and discard them afterwards like a coat to be taken off; but I think it would be interesting for a more compassionate Sidereal to feel a twinge of guilt about the children they needed to conceive and then abandon. I could definitely see a Sidereal like that keeping tabs on their mortal children's lives and maybe nudging fate every once in a while as an invisible gift for those children.

                Originally posted by Robert Vance
                Being able to see through your parent's Arcane Fate strikes me as a reasonable minor thing for a Sidereal's Half-Caste to have.

                Pre-Exaltation children probably do not have any special benefit, unless it's just really fucking depressing at the table, in which case sure, they can.
                See, that's what I was thinking. I imagine that nine times out of ten, the mortal children of Celestial Exalts aren't even as powerful (for multiple definitions of that word) as a God-Blooded child. But given that the Exaltation is such a magical, irrepressible force, it would seem out-of-place for a child being bathed in their parent or parents' Exalted essence during conception to not resonate with that essence in some way. The child of an Abyssal father might not be a cackling necromancer covered in Soulsteel, but maybe the child grows up with pale hair and an unquenchable desire to join the Immaculate Order as a monk specializing in exorcism. The child of a Solar mother might not be a glorious demigod who can perform the General of the All-Seeing Sun charm, but maybe the child finds it easy to become chieftain of their tribe of barbarians and becomes celebrated in local stories for their exploits in uniting the tribes.

                The child of two Sidereals might be able to see past Arcane Fate thanks to the overwhelming resonance of their parents' Exaltations in their soul (and the Bureau of Destiny prefers to keep them in Yu-Shan for the sake of keeping a close eye on them for that reason).

                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                            That works quite fine for that other fictional property. It's not what the Sidereals are.
                                            I don't think it's so absurd to draw parallels between one fictional universe and another, particularly when the Exalted sourcebook gives big lists of other works which can be cribbed for inspiration. I think that the Sidereals are the Bene Gesserit, some elements aside. I mean, both groups are uncanny masters of martial arts; both groups never touch thrones themselves, but act as viziers and kingmakers for those who do; both groups are invisible in their true form all but the most knowledgeable, and are tolerated only because they've made themselves indispensable to the aristocracy; and both groups' members craft identities that are so intricate that agents can run the risk of going native (read: Lady Jessica). Even the Missionaria Protectiva is pretty similar to the Immaculate faith in terms of being a religious manipulation done for the sake of the plots of the powers-that-be. I still think that the Bene Gesserit represent a great source of inspiration for the Sidereals.

                                            And nobody else can do that? The Bene Gesserit do their thing because their own genetics are pertinant to the people they're producing. If the only thing you need is to make sure a ruler has an heir, there are dozens of things that the Sidereal can do that are more sensible than carrying the child themselves.
                                            Even setting aside the Bene Gesserit, it's a fact in human history that children make for exceptional social and political tools. Acting as the regent of a child monarch means that they grow up with a will that is pliant to your wishes -- and even more so when that child is your own, as evinced by queen-mothers such as Catherine de' Medici. Impregnating or being impregnated by a person of higher status has been a method people have used for millennia to climb the social ranks. Having a child with a person creates ties of trust between the parents -- something that can, and likely has, been manipulated before by one party looking for some kind of gain. Creating a child creates a clear line of succession for monarchs and aristocrats. Having many children has historically been seen as a direct corollary of one's wealth, status, and prestige -- the "war of pregnancies" between Leah and Rachel in the Tanach, and between Kaushalya, Sumitra, and Kaikeyi in the Ramayana, were methods by which those aristocratic women jockeyed for status in their society.

                                            Considering that the Sidereals need to remain undercover when working in Creation, one has to imagine that they reserve their supernatural powers for situations that genuinely require them. And if having a child is the simplest or most effective natural way of insinuating into or manipulating a social structure, why would a Sidereal trust that task to someone else? Especially when their own child would prove so much easier a variable to control?
