            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
            Are those charms about aiming and shooting, or do they often rely on the gun having bullets?
            Well, although the charms are written explicitly around shell projectile weapons, they are supposedly intended to be used with futuristic energy blast weapons too.
            Originally posted by Queshire View Post
            If you're using the rules for the Drive / Firearms ability from Shards of the Exalted Dream, but you want your character to use a flame piece anyways, would you use Firearms or Archery for it? Firearms seems like the flat out obvious answer, but they were originally intended to be used with Archery in the vanilla game, so....
            The weapons were included to be used by Western pirates, Southern "flame"-slingers, and the like, without warping the game too far from the bronze-age aesthetic. They are basically musket and pistol stand-ins that use flamedust cartridges instead of gunpowder and lead balls. They were placed under Archery's purview simply so not to add another ability just for two exotic weapons.

            Archery Charms are mostly designed around Bows and may not work well or at all with Flame weapons, and only very few charms were added specificly for Flame weapons.

            So, if you are using the Shard's rules, and presumably you are allowing firearms to be common enough in the setting, then I'd use the Firearms ability/charms for firewands and flame pieces, but with an eye out for the appropriate.

            ( One thing to note: For Dragonblooded, Firearms is a Fire Aspect ability like Melee, rather than Wood like Archery. Introduction of firearms to the legions and navy, and development of charms, could have interesting affects on the balance of power among houses Cathak, Sesus, and V'neef, if that's the sort of campaign you're playing.)

                                    Not to be a rain on your parade but such a Raksha group wouldn't need to attack a major city for slaves (the most common reason such raids happen) unless something ulterior was happening.                                                 That would be the worst possible place for them to attack in the South!

                                                Well, firstly, Raksha don't need much in the way of resources, as noted, so a large group of Nobles, leading massive armies of goblins and thundering behemoths could easily decide to do this, literally just because it's a fun idea (this is what happened when the Fair Folk invaded the Scavenger Lands for conquest, prompting the formation of the Confederation of Rivers). There'd have to be a hell of a lot more than 40, tho...

                                                Chiaroscuro is probably the biggest city in the South, and certainly the most important in terms of trade and commerce. *Everybody's* got their fingers in that pie, and will react to maintain the status quo, from the Guild and the Realm, to the Court of the Orderly Flame and the Salmalin. The Tri-Khan would call on his Delzhan forces, both urban and nomadic, to defend the city, as well as draft any Exalted living in his city. Tammuz can call on Packs of Lunars, and Nazri has a Special hatred for Fair Folk, so he might well use his clout to pull some Divine Intervention down from Heaven, maybe even the Aerial Legion itself.

                                                Even if the Raksha could take the city, they could never hold it. And they'd never even be able to take Grandmother Bright's Plaza, in the first place!

                                                Smaller local kingdoms would probably also pitch in, to a limited extent, esp if they can wrangle a better relationship with the Delzhan out of it; and also because NO one wants a large nest of Raksha for neighbors. No, if they want to conquer someplace, they'd be better off trying someplace else...like Paragon!

                                                        I'm not sure if this is the right place for this, but I'm wondering if you guys could help me rate an artifact. I'm aiming for 4 but with all the stuff I'm adding to it I think it'll hit 5.

                                                        It's for a heroic Infernal and it's theme is supposed to be "Channeled Carnage," you know, unleashing all the cruelty and madness of Malfeas in a constructive manner. On the outside it looks normal and heroic, but underneath it's vicious and monstrous.

                                                        It's a vitriol-tainted Reaper Daiklave that comes in two parts, the blade and the sheathe.

                                                        By default the blade is stuck in the sheathe. In this form it works completely like a default Reaper Daiklave only it inflicts Bashing damage instead of lethal and to all senses it appears to be normal orichalcum, not the Infernal Vitriol-tainted version. It costs 2 motes to unsheathe it for the first time in a scene, though this cost is waived if the user's Anima is at the 11+ level.

                                                        When unsheathed the blade of the sword has the rusted appearance of tainted Orichalcum and it works as a Caustic Weapon version of a Reaper Daiklave. (drips acid, acidic fumes waft off of it, it halves hardness and adds +2 to damage)

                                                        However, the sheathe itself can be used as a weapon as well in this form. It works like a Reaper Daiklave only it deals bashing damage and it adds +2 on top of the defense rating of a standard reaper Daiklave. If you inspect it you can see the vitriol taint on the inside of the sheathe.

                                                        Both the Sheathe and Blade are meant to be used together. When wielded together they ignore the -1 penalty for using something in your offhand and they count as an identical pair for any martial art styles or charms or anything else that might count for that. On the downside if the owner loses one half of the pair for more than a day, whether through theft or being forced to give it up, then attunement automatically breaks and can't be re-established until the lost half is retrieved.

                                                        Between the two halves they have 1 hearthstone socket located near the tip of the sheathe and it costs 8 motes to attune.

                                                        What do you guys think?
