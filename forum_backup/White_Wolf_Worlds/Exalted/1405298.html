    I don't get Falcon Style

    07-30-2020, 07:16 AM
    What it says on the tin.
    Falcon is this weird mix of wanting you to attack from above, but it's first signature move, Raptor's Fatal Descent, leaves you prone, which prevents you from getting airborne again easily.
    A lot of it's bennies support grapples, but it kind of wants you to fly/jump up and swoop down again and it's not super great at dragging someone with you, so it apparently wants you to throw people a lot, I guess?

    Falcon also has a whopping 4 Simple Charms, which are incompatible with each other, well, because they're Simple type charms. I just don't get it.
    What's the synergy? It just seems like a bunch of random unconnected Charms that do neat things, but how is it a style?

    Since Centipede, Swaying Grass Dance, and Laughing Monster, all also from Lunars, make instant sense to me, I refuse to believe it's a badly written style. In fact, Lunars is an incredibly well-written book, so I assume I'm just not processing something right?

    Can anyone give me a short rundown on why, from a mechanics perspective, I would want to use Falcon? I am already sold on it's fluff, that is a very stylish... Style. But even if I were a winged Lunar wanting to throw people from up high, I'd probably be better off just using Mantis.

    What am I missing?

      Falcon Style very wants you to come on and slam, yes, which Grasping Raptor Clutch lets you do reflexively after jumping to vertical short range with someone. Assuming your fall back down from that jump isn’t supposed to happen until your next turn (which just feels correct to me in general and this style is direct evidence for because its best shenanigans don’t work otherwise), it’s Raptor’s Fatal Descent time.           Let's see...

          First a look into the Simples:

          a Simple for "dive-in decisive for adding up to 5 hit threshold to damage (may sometimes make a second move!). You are also prone but don't care too much about it"
          a Simple for "grapple++ that prones without a throw and steals init (usually enough to make up for the gambit cost)"
          a Simple for "far-throw with a secondary damage proc"
          a Simple for "post-throw decisive that adds foregone control to damage again. you may combo this with the first simple for a total of foregone control + hit threshold extra damage"
          The capstone is a post-throw extra decisive attack, with extra damage and may be used after a decisive for nearly as much damage as the previous one.

          From these the presumption is that you want to force an engage->grapple->throw loop. If you can't grapple, disengage to reengage.
          You also have access to Smashing weapons, which on its own allows this dynamic with non-grapple attacks (withering, especially) in exchange for losing a bit of init and defense, and not doing the grapple's comparatively high damages.
          So long as you can meet either expectation your throws and decisives are on steroids.


          The style doesn't have withering damage adders, in practice, but gives you an extremely reliable attack booster provided you are forcing your preferred engagement modes (2m to nearly double your hit chances against the 4-9 defense range, and your excellency will outpace a straight defense adder) and powerful Control rolls.

          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
          On your Enablers to play on that loop:

          You need a good engaging and a good disengaging tool. That's Falcon Takes Flight. FTF also trivializes Rising From Prone for when you use Fatal Descent.
          You also benefit from ways to easily reposition enemies without losing damage. That's Grasping Raptor Clutch and Smashing Weapons.

          You need elevation to get some of those bonuses and attacks. That's your Form.with its free monkey leaps. The form also gives you a boost to control rolls.

          Form + grasping raptor clutch also gives you a 1-round combo of "throw hard and force a short range fall damage" that only relies on beating the enemy's control roll by 2.

          Proning keeps you alive:
          Against a prone enemy, your attacks are easier to hit (-1/-2 defense) and their attacks are harder to land (-3 to attack). If the enemy tries to rise from prone he's still going to be hampered on attack because of the flurry...or give up attacking entirely(armor adds a penalty here, flurrying can be very very expensive). If the enemy is grappled he can't even do that (another -1 to -3 to attack, and can't rise until released)
          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
          Summing up:

          I expect the accomplished Falcon fighter will start with with either:
          1) engage + grapple to get an immediate big savage (either withering or decisive) and proning and maybe get the form up
          2) attack + disengage, go back to 1 and most likely get the form up (and after the form is up this disengage will always succeed, no roll necessary).

          Followed by a huge throw (withering or decisive, potentially dealing a good 50% more damage than normal) or some ground & pound if the grapple is broken.

          From the throw you can immediately follow up with a boosted decisive, and disengage/smash to restart. (Raking Talon Kick will frequently stack +5 to +10 damage here, so even at base initiative you can reliably aggravate wound penalties)
          From the ground & pound you may disengage or smash and restart.

          Doom Plummets Down not only gives you an extra attack (essentially doubling your decisive / making a withering-decisive in a single round), it also lets you double up on Raking Talon Kick, essentially tripling the gained damage from giving up control rounds on a throw: once on the throw, once on DPD and again on RTK (+Fatal Descent for good measure).



          Against an enemy they can't grapple the focus shifts to the stylist moving in and out of range all the time, preferably up, to exploit the reliable attack booster and Fatal Descent..

          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

          They are partially countered by fighters that can casually fight prone (tiger, swaying grass iirc?) or reflexively rise from prone.
          They are partially countered by not wanting to savage (the def penalty from grappling and the loss of control is a big risk against multiple enemies, so you want to throw immediately)
          They are almost completely countered by enemies they can't grapple or smash.


          Solars and Sids are much bouncier (fear the sid whose attack dice only fail on a 3), while lunars can grapple most enemies that would break this style.

              It seems fairly straightforward to me. Raptor’s Fatal Descent is a finishing move that costs a willpower, I don’t think you’re supposed to use it more than once per fight.
