            Originally posted by Chausse View Post
            Are there slaves of The Realm outside of the Blessed Isles ?
            I mean, Dynasts abroad will probably take their slaves with them, where practical and necessary. Don't forget that "the Realm outside of the Blessed Isle" consists of the loosely governed satrapies.

            Anyway, bullet points:

            * Slaves are almost always purchased or prisoners of war. Realm citizens can only be enslaved as punishment for some of the most severe crimes.
            * Only Dragon Blooded can own slaves, although they might delegate them to unExalted Dynasts or lease them to patricians.
            * Slaves have no rights guaranteed by the state, and are at the total mercy of their masters. Harming another Dynasts slaves is treated as property damage. They will typically be branded to identify their owners.
            * Slaves are allowed to possess their own property (although can only be permitted weapons when granted by their masters), but have no recourse to purchase their own freedom. That can only come with the decisions of their masters, but even Dynasts may only emancipate a single slave every year.
            * Current Realm law renders the children of slaves as automatically citizens of the Realm, rather than inheriting their parents slavery. This is promoted by House Cynis, to ensure that others require a steady stream of their product, as they hold the monopoly on that business; all buying and selling of slaves has to go through them.
            * Slaves can generally expect to be treated about as well as will allow them to perform their functions, at least as food and shelter go, but they'll invariably be subjected to brutal punishment if disobedient and the attitude of the Scarlet Dynasty typically entails some level of abuse for even the best-kept slave.

            Originally posted by Chausse
            In what kind of field do they work ?
            General domestic service in the sizable residences of the Exalted and additional work on their estates, whether that be labouring on the plantations or constructing the features. Slaves owned by the throne are the main workforce for the extension and maintenance of the national infrastructure. They're also used for the transportation of supplies in the Imperial Legions, those being among the better treated slaves to ensure that they don't disrupt logistics.

                        Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                        1) Aren't Blessed Isles (which I assume are Realm Citizens) supposed to become Dispossessed (and so legally it's impossible to enslave them) when punished for the most severe crimes ?
                        It appears not. Dispossession is generally imposed on entire communities rather than individuals, and tends to be particular to the crimes of rebellion and failure to pay taxes, or for the community in general displaying an overall disregard for the law. I presume that enslavement is a punishment that the Imperial judges have discretion to pronounce on heinous crimes such as rape or the most savage or insidious forms of murder. Maybe also certain forms of blasphemy.

                        But no, dispossession has always been presented as a particular thing to deal with communities that, in whole or in part, express contempt for the authority of the Realm. It should be kept in mind that it can be a case in which the actions of a few can harm the many. Villages pay taxes as a unit, but everybody needs to make their share, and if some fail to provide and the rest don't have a surplus that will make the difference, all will be punished. And a family can be punished for the seditious conspiracies of their neighbours, even if they knew nothing of it.

                        Originally posted by Chausse
                        3) and 4) I feel are in apparent contradiction. How can something with no right can be allowed to posses their own property ? Unless their own property is actually guaranteed by its owner I guess.
                        I would take it that denial of personal possessions is not undertaken as a matter of course, but a slave has no recourse should their master opt to just confiscate it at will.

                        Originally posted by Chausse
                        5) What happens of a child "citizen of The Realm" ? Does he become some sort of "child of the Empress" as could happen in some nations historically ? Or is it something totally different (they become child of the master that possess the slave) ?
                        It's not elaborated, and I suppose it varies. I would regard it as typical for them to fall under the custodianship of the Immaculate Order, as would likely be common with orphans, although many might find themselves adrift and unsupported in a manner that creates vagabonds and bandits. Still, that option might be seen as an embarrassment for Dynasts who are responsible for their existence. While adoption for mortal peasants is surely out of the question, a Dragon Blood might assign an agent to ensure that they are placed with accommodating peasant families, assuming there are not ones that proactively offer to take in such. And units of the Imperial Legions might periodically turn up looking for any that can be recruited.

                        Still, I also presume that in a lot of cases, Dynasts just opt to keep their slaves gender segregated to minimise the opportunities for them to become pregnant. I they don't get to keep the children, I expect that it saves them on a bit of lost productivity and any expenses for seeing them off their property. For the sake of not being too grim, I presume that the status of being Realm citizens affords such children just enough protection under law that to allow them to be abandoned to die of exposure would... not necessarily be actionable against the Dynast (unless a judge or magistrate wanted to prove that the Dynast was aware and did not intervene, if not outright complicit), but constitutes a loss of face.

                        Maybe some cases where the slaves set aside enough of what they have or receive to see the child to an age where they can begin to make their own way, at which point they shall have to. Or perhaps, even when not formally adopted, they might drift over into the portion of the household staff that is free. The child of a slave might end up becoming the servant of a servant to a Dynast; somebody to wait on or clean up after a clerk or guard or keeper of the winery or tutor.

                        It's one of those things where I would find it to make the most sense for it to vary significantly based on circumstances, because there's probably little interest in formally establishing a uniform support system, and so it depends on the conditions of where one ends up. The child of a slave in a rich eastern prefecture with plenty, stability and a large quantity of well-established Immaculate institutions probably fares better than one who pops up among miners or people laying roads through some of the more marginal territory.

                        Originally posted by Chausse
                        5) Does House Cynis pass through other slaves traders outside of The Realm ? Or do they operate alone in acquiring them ? (for example, could they buy slaves directly to nations that are beyond the Threshold ?)
                        Yeah sure. Purchase of slaves for transit to the Blessed Isle will be among the businesses that give them the best relationship with the Guild of the Scarlet Dynasty. Association with the Realm promotes slavery among the satrapies (in the cases of those run by House Cynis, the satrap probably often outright encourages the authorities in this, and maybe even incentivizes it by pointing it out as a good way to bring in Cynis revenue that can be turned back around to pay the tribute), and the knowledge of that will surely attract other slaver businesses.

                        I would say developing satrapies as markets to attract foreign vendors is a lot more common than a Cynis scion venturing out to purchase slaves held in a market beyond their control (for one thing, they absorb even more of the cost of transporting them back home), but maybe occasionally some member of the family struggling to find a niche elsewhere in the business might go that extra mile to try and get a leg up. I would say even that most typically requires places with well-established and well-stocked slave markets to shop around in, rather than meeting directly with vendors; Nexus would make sense in particular as a place that sees its fair share of Cynis.

                        But I can also think of other scenarios in which it does make sense to buy directly from a vendor, particularly if they were en route to a different market; intercepting a shipment before it reaches market and purchasing wholesale can not only be an underhanded way of getting some quickly and for relatively low price at a time when your stock might be low, it also might be done with the intention of undercutting a rival market or trader. Enough gets to Nexus that the family still goes there to buy, but there are several reasons to prefer that less stock goes to Nexus than does to your satrapies. That's a case where I imagine it's all running on elaborate calculations; if the Nexus market can be competitive enough (or Guild factors might arrange to buy up most of them before you can arrive and then sell it on with a huge mark up, because you're a Great House of the Scarlet Dynasty, surely you can afford it) that in some years that it may actually be cheaper to intercept a shipment ahead of time and redirect it home, paying for the food and the slave drivers all the way. Or there might be financial loss but political capital in acquiring slaves in a manner that does not put money into the economy of enemies; coin spent in Nexus can help fund the defiance of Lookshy and heresy of Great Forks. You'll lose money, but selling the slaves at the end still means that you lose less money, and that might be a case where what you're really spending it on is the goodwill of the Houses, the throne and the Order.

                        This distribution of interests is what you need hundreds of Exalted for. And members of House Cynis are in some level of competition with one another as well.

                        Originally posted by Chausse
                        6) Are there laws (explicit or social) that protect, to a minimal, slaves ? Like you can't outright kill them, for example ?
                        There's no reference to such, and it would seem contrary to the established character of the thing for there to be. The lives of slaves are generally protected by them being a source of revenue for their masters; the point at which inadequacy or resistance makes them wholly incapable of serving that function means that the last way for them to provide for that is to serve as an example to the other slaves, and many Dynasts probably want to be sure that leaves an impression. I can't see other authorities being interested in curbing that.

                        Killing slaves on a whim or for pleasure might not be proscribed, but I would regard it as the kind of thing that disturbs other Dynasts, which might reduce one's profile a lot but maybe has to be extremely severe to be outright shunned over it.

                        I personally once imagined a scenario in which rival Dynasts at a gala would engage in sharp-tongued banter with one another, and one makes a comment that elicits an involuntary but audible chuckle from an attending slave. It seemed to me that person would be doomed by such, as Dynast etiquette would demand that the person who made the humourous statement be the one to immediately see to their punishment, which is likely to be savage and motivated; I picture many a Dynast that would genuinely dislike the person they would mock while being deeply disgusted at a mortal of such low stature daring to openly laugh at them. You of course still pay for the slave.

                        (I can see a relationship between some masters and slaves that is cordial in a manner where the Dynast shares witticisms about their peers that the slave laughs at, but that stuff is best kept in private; if such slaves are part of your entourage in public, they'd be best not seen laughing along at any barbs)

                        Originally posted by Chausse
                        Physicians are usually very high regarded positions in societies, with very specific and high level training. Do slaves get treated well (like, very well) materially with a prestigious position or should I expect the prestigious ones (physicians, savants, architects, etc ...) to usually live in a granary with poor material condition ?
                        Physicians and similar slaves (I don't believe they'd ever be used as architects) are expensive and assigned to sensitive positions, so keeping them in good conditions is just practical (although they might be sent to the inferior quarters for a stint if there's a need to discipline them). Bad conditions are for slaves perceived as cheap and plentiful.

                        (Granaries are for grain, not people)

                                                        Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                                        I was pretty sure Greeko-romans slaves could buy their freedom with either money or years of service (or maybe they could do it in some states/periods and not in others ?).
                                                        In the Republican era, domestic and professional slaves, particularly those in an urban environment, were more likely to acquire means or good standing to receive freedom, although sometimes needing that to be granted in the wills of their deceased masters. There were enough of them to form a whole social class in Rome; it was not merely legal, it was part of the expectations of what would be done with such slaves (although popular sentiment as expressed in surviving writing still mostly held that a person currently a slave had a lessened humanity). Slaves who worked in fields and mines were much less likely to possess property or be treated well, and for mining in particular had very poor life expectancy. This was during an era when Roman legions were likely to be conducting active campaigns all across the frontiers that continuously brought in more slaves.

                                                        (Sometimes more slaves than could be fully handled; a lot of the people who rose up in the Servile Wars of Spartacus fame had been brought in by Roman success against a group of Germanic tribes that had been particularly threatening a few decades before. A large influx of people that had been proud and successful enemies.)

                                                        Starting with the imperial era, legal avenues for freeing slaves were significantly curtailed, and officials promoted the idea that it should be lessened. Expansion of the provinces was being slowed down dramatically, and that was going to greatly reduce the number of slaves being brought in at a time when they were still an integral part of the economy. That's a period where the position of slaves becomes a lot less dynamic.

                                                        I could see the value of slaves in the Realm as occupying a space somewhere in the middle of those, and with adjustments made in regards to some of the other important differences in class structure and prevailing religious attitudes.
