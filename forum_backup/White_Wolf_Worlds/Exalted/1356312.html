                          I liked how 1E tended to provide a lot of information and detailed a lot of things in the nations it covered, but only getting information on a single nation or two at a time, given the number of nations that existed, got rather expensive. While 2E's approach wasn't quite as detailed as 1E, it provided enough essential information and details on important locations to make it worthwhile... and balanced this with providing multiple significant locations within each of the cardinal directions (personally, I would have gone with eight volumes to provide more areas between the 4 main directions).

                          I am hoping that Across the Eight directions will still provide a good amount of information on popular locations from previous editions in addition to the new locations introdueced in 3E. Some information, such as significant Satraps of the Realm were given write-ups in the Realm book, so are unlikely to be repeated in this book... but An-Teng was not among them.

                          The main reason I am looking into this now (rather than wait for the new book) is because I am thinking of running a new Dragon-Blooded game in the jungles to the South of An-Teng (with this nation being the main staging area for the group). I think the furthest South they will be going will be the Fount of Mourning (the swamp that saw the death of an Infernal, Lunar and Solar back in the First Age and where the most likely location for the Autochthonian Incursion tends to happen... though the latter has yet to happen in this game). Just trying to get a basic framework for the campaign set up that I can modify when/if new information is provided.

                                                      Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                      In terms of the Realm, its geography has been changed to be more varied, but primarily it's been crammed full of Anathema tombs, forests of monsters, valleys of spirits, etc, to give you plots to do besides Dynastic intrigue, as honestly the 2nd ed Blessed Isle didn't present much to do.
                                                      Those are present and significant, but in a sense also a bit peripheral. Untamed wilderness has a stronger presence in text sure (I'm not sure it was strictly absent in the past, but at best mentioned uncommonly and never built upon), but the core elements that are the history, the society, the administrative organisation and government, these remain largely the same in the broad strokes and in many of the details.

                                                      Yet they're also written with a new kind of energy, with a focus on creating a dynamic sense of time and place, where off-hand details can imply plot hooks or ideas for how players might pursue some goal, and it gives the world a bit more sense of character.

                                                      That and examining some parts that had weaknesses and giving them an overhaul while retaining the core features. The All-Seeing Eye stands out in that regard, having gone from what felt like something between watered down magistrates and just being the official name of House Iselsi into something with a lot more of the flavour of stale beer and some of that good old rogue CIA in South America/Southeast Asia or former KGB in actual Russia prospects in their future to oppose, hijack or take part in.

                                                      Even among prefectures, you've got a lot of names and core concepts that have been carried forward, but upgraded from well-written but a bit dry and one note to more lively and vivid places. The Realm is more than the addition of tombs and forests to adventure in, it's the new version of Juche, a place that just off the top of my head brims with potential as a power base to safeguard and fund the ascension of House Nellens, an asset that their enemies can't afford to leave in their hands, and a core of the Realm's spirituality for Celestial Exalted to target for breaking down or appropriating for their own ends.

                                                      ...Oh dang, between the wealth drawn in from pilgrims and its significance to Immaculate beliefs, I've only just realised that seeing more of Juche puts a whole new spin on What Fire Has Wrought depicting House Ledaal as having designs on the assets of their neighbour.

                                                      And most of it written with a pretty decent economy of wording. I think I noted when reading Fangs at the Gate that Dominion write-ups managed to on average be shorter than places in Compass books, and that's without consideration for how the format in the finished product will make each of them take even fewer pages.

                                                      My point is that I think there's a firm basis for setting how our current writers and development could take something familiar and trim some of the gristle while spicing it up a bit, and still ending up with something that takes up less space than it has in previous books.

                                                      Give me a bit of time and I think I could identify some specific details that I think would benefit from that treatment.

                                                      (As to new players without the previous books, I don't imagine it makes much difference since it's all new, so why not do a new place anyway.)
                                                      Because it's neat and with sharing with new folks in a manner consistent with the format The Realm and What Fire Has Wrought has acclimated them to.
