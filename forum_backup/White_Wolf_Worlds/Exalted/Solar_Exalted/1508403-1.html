    The problem is that we're not talking about the same goals. Of course if you throw all the creation points/XP at every last thing that boosts disengaging, you'll be better at disengaging,and a bonus like double 9s gets better the more dice you have.

    1) DMW actually makes you worse at what it says it's supposed to do (ala TOM)
    2) DMW doesn't help disengage attempts in a meaningful way; I don't care if I can't get 12 successes at Dodge 2, I only want it for situations where 3-5 successes is an adequate average.

    Because we're talking about lowering the mins so that these sorts of Charms are more accessible not because it's optimal. Obviously it's better to have Dodge 4 than Dodge 2. It so that you can make a Dodge 2 or 3 character and still get more of a boost than an Excellency to push you a bit farther when you're willing to throw a lot behind something.

                        A rule I saw once, which seems to work pretty well, is to reduce Ability mins by 1, unless there’s a prerequisite Charm with a native Ability min of 5. So to use Dodge Charms as an example:
                        Reed in the Wind becomes Dodge 2, Searing Quicksilver Feint becomes Dodge 3, Force-Stealing Feint becomes Dodge 4, and Seven Shadow Evasion stays Dodge 5 (because FSF is natively Dodge 5).
                        Also, might I recommend weakening Supernal instead of getting rid of it entirely? Even just allowing it to give +1 effective Essence allows starting characters to have access to cool and flavorful effects, like Glorious Solar Saber or Martial Arts Forms.

                                    I"m not sure it's really a combat specific thing. Combat tends to heighten things because it's a more mechanically dense part of the game. Awareness starts at 2, and then jumps to 3+ too.

                                    Awareness has the advantage of having three distinct branches though, two of which have at least one Awareness 3 Charm (which Sight doesn't is... IDK). But if you just want your character to stick to Awareness 3 but have really good smell, you can have a pretty nice package of both strong utility stuff, and some stand out smell stuff. You can eve go for the Keen (Sense) trio over time if you feel like it. Awareness also has a pretty strong core trunk if you just want all around super-sense for people that are going to want to start with Awareness 5 where you could just bump up the Keen trio, the Unsurpassed trio, and then Awakening Eye and Eyes of the Unconquered Sun. You can get some of the neat tricks in the branches later, but if you just want to go all in on epic super-senses, you can to that too. The more combat-focused Abilities tend to be either really lopsided in what they offer (Dodge has tons of support of Evasion, but minimal for doing much with combat movement), or multiple trees that co-mingle in the branches; so there isn't that balance of a decent package for people that only want a small part of the whole and a core trunk of all-around super-competence (though Craft as the same problem).

                                                        Without retread the whole thread:

                                                        Nobody actually disagrees that the Ability minimums have a place to emphasize unique talent and high level effects going hand-in-hand. Heavenly Guardian's Defense should be a Melee 5 Charm.

                                                        The problem is that there's no consistency to this. For all the talking-past-each-other debates around the mechanical value of Dust Motes Whirling... why does double 9s on a single disengage roll (which isn't even a guarantee of doing better, just an odds booster) belong at the level of being one of the best in a whole Direction at escaping combat?

                                                        Dice tricks and other things that aren't obviously supernatural actions being behind the 4+ Ability wall detracts from those Charms that should be that highly rated being special. You should see an Ability 4 or 5 Charm and be able to trust you're getting something that you might not use all the time, but when you do it should be awesome, but instead it could be anything from an underwhelming dice-trick, to awe-inspiring display of divine excellence. So players aren't being trained to use Ability minimums are a guide to what sort of Charm to expect, just what sort of hoops the Charm is expecting you to jump through.

                                                        When >60% of the Charms are Ability 5 Charms, I'm pretty sure we're well beyond things having possibly gone too far. If the ratio of Charms was 1:2:3:4:5, so there's five Ability 5 Charms for each Ability 1 Charm, there would be ~57 Ability 1 Charms (which would still be only 2-3 Charms per Ability), not 23, and ~285 Ability 5 Charms, not 536. The weighting is intensely towards 5s in a fashion that's hard to excuse as good design.
