    Anyhow.
    Rewrite charms to be clearer. Especially Athletics.
    Also, there's quite a lot of charms that could be made immensely simpler with very small mechanical changes. Considering how much time my players spent reminding themselves what their own charms did, that'd be nice.
    Make the ability prereqs slightly lower.
    Move dice tricks out of charm trees (I don't like them, but some people do. So keep them available, just not as speed-bump prereqs).
    Redo the whole Craft tree to be more balanced and more interesting.

    No charmset is perfect, but I think that'd solve like 90% of the issues. Obviously there's always going to be some specific charms I don't like, but a lot of that is personal taste, so the charms I dislike might not be the ones you dislike.

            Since this is an unrealistic wishlist: Cut it by 2/3. Cap a given charm tree at 16-20 charms. Reduce starting number of charm picks. Increase XP cost of charms to 2x. Combine charms together into cooler "bulk" effects that feel meaningful. Thin out the number of charms that are pure dice manipulation and nothing else. Find another way to represent topping out other than an artificial dice cap, this is a fucking lazy way of limiting things that feels gamey as hell. Why has this gone unchallenged for 3 effing editions now? Excellencies are terrible, get rid of them please. Don't standardize dice tricks, I'm not trying to turn this into 4E DnD. Clearly define what the mechanical intent of each tree is if they are flavored (aka, brawl is primarily a 1v1 thing,etc). Offload a number of key charm effects into inherent caste powers so you stop getting people clearly trying to steal schticks from another caste's intended strengths and re-enforce caste roles. Shave the number of abilities down to 20. Make ability requirement the same as the Essence requirement of charms. Get rid of charm expansions that aren't automatic upgrades. Get rid of references to Essence 6 shit in the corebook, undermining your own stated goal at keeping the scale from 1-5 with 6+ representing personal powers.

            Incentive is not permission or justification.

                                                    Originally posted by Clownpiece View Post
                                                    Eliminating 1s is actually very powerful, as a lot of magic preys on them.
                                                    This, to me, isn't a justification for the mechanical effect, and really gets back to the really bad design issues with 2e. Charm effects that exist solely to counter Charm mechanics, just turn into "paranoia combat" style mote taxes. If I'm activating a Charm to eliminate 1s solely to stop "prey on 1s" Charms, again, I'd rather the Charm just say, "treat your roll as having no 1s in it," than have to go through the time of rerolling

                                                    My post was "standardize" not "eliminate." Charms should do cool and useful things, but there's no reason for overly complicated mechanics. Even Blaque 's case about giving Charm sets (both internally and between splats) a distinct feel doesn't really work for me. I don't need "roll a straight" as a strange new dice trick for Melee to feel different from Brawl. This also helps make understanding Charms easier. If the point of Excellent Strike is to help protect you from Charms that prey on 1s, it's a lot easier to know that when it says that, instead of it being an emergent property of rerolling 1s until you don't have any.

                                                    Originally posted by ParanoiaCombo View Post
                                                    Find another way to represent topping out other than an artificial dice cap, this is a fucking lazy way of limiting things that feels gamey as hell. Why has this gone unchallenged for 3 effing editions now?
                                                    Do you have any actual suggestion on this?

                                                    How do you represent topping out without a cap? How do you construct a cap that isn't artificial?

                                                    Why is this lazy, how is it "gamey" in a bad way (since this is still a game and all)?

                                                    I'd say it's gone unchallenged because most people recognize that letting someone boost their rolls without any limitation but how much they're willing to throw at a single roll isn't actually a good idea, and nobody has any idea that's a "better" limitation instead of a equal if different limitation.

                                                    Don't standardize dice tricks, I'm not trying to turn this into 4E DnD.
                                                    One doesn't have anything to do with the other. The point of standardizing dice tricks is to make the game easier to learn and thus more fun to play. Having every dice trick function under unique rules, even with the rest of your suggestion, still makes the game harder to understand and thus less fun for people. The "don't make this D&D 4e" complaint seems extreme odd considering one of D&D 4e's biggest issue is marrying mechanical standardization to niche protection... like:

                                                    Offload a number of key charm effects into inherent caste powers so you stop getting people clearly trying to steal schticks from another caste's intended strengths and re-enforce caste roles.
                                                    If you don't want D&D 4e, you don't want the niche protection in the Castes to be stronger, you want them to be weaker. Stronger niche protection means you're turning each Caste into something closer to the D&D 4e Classes with dedicate roles that other people intruding on ruins the synergy of the group.


                                                    Originally posted by danelsan View Post
                                                    I'd rather have the opposite, as I don't feel Solars need Castes at all, it is just WW/OP obsession with with every freaking splat needing Clans, Tribes or whatever.
                                                    This isn't really fair. Strong character archetypes backed up with mechanics isn't some "obsession" with some sort of vestigial design quirk. It's a regular tool in the designer kit that most RPGs use because it's a useful design element in lots of ways. I will admit that most of those benefits are aimed more at newer players, but that's fine by me. New people are targets for the books too, and they need more help getting into the game. Those of us that started playing Exalted with just the 1e Corebook have the buy-in and experience to help us house rule stuff if we want to remove elements like that.

                                                        My Basic Gripes with the Solar Charm Trees:
                                                        • Too many "repurchases", just grind that down into automatic upgrades at the relevant integers (Ability and Charm Requirements).
                                                        • Survival needs a focus other than pets, something that pulls on the powers of the environment they are in, yes that steps on dragonblooded a little, but they already step on sidereals and other splats.
                                                        • Occult needs more charms that deal with places of power, not just stomping spirits and enhancing sorcery.
                                                        • Archery needs more Firearms compatible charms, leaving firedust weapons to MA, is kind of a letdown.
                                                        • Craft and the whole damned system built around it needs a comprehensive rewrite (some may disagree).
                                                        • Stealth needs more cooperative charms, starting and expanding around Guardian Fog approach.
                                                        • War charms need expansions, I recommend drone warfare (summoning "light bending" drones ala Glorious Solar Saber and Glorious Solar Plate, and controlling them ala riggers from Shadowrun).
                                                        • The social charms need clarification of what sort of social action they are compatible with.
