                                Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                Religion, belief and worship in Exalted as presented in the book felt kinda weird to me, so if you guys don't mind me asking some question.......

                                1/ If a Solar is the roaming Creation freely type, then most of them will become less religious as they travel, aren't they ? Since meeting other spirits will makes them realize that their God is just merely a Terrestrial sky god and not some almighty creator who birth the world in seven day (this'll happen even early for Zenith).
                                Not necessarily. Remember that Exalted religions usually don't teach that gods are omnipotent, omniscient flawless beings in the first place. While familiarity might breed contempt to an extent, and it's going to be natural for a Zenith to think differently about the gods when they realize they can punch them in the face, a god is still generally closely connected to some aspect of the world and to their worshippers, and it's not really unthinkable for a Zenith to develop more respect and sympathy for this system, when it works - for concept of divinity and the idea of gods working with humanity to achieve a better world.

                                2/ Is "Cultural Christian" a thing if you are a non-religious Solar, as in you celebrate a festival not because you believe in its backstory about a guy coming back from the dead, but more because it's your city's traditions/cultural identity and you aren't gonna stop eating those delicious cake just because the stories aren't real. Kinda like an atheist Jew who still attend Jewish holidays.
                                Some Solars might participate in their faith's trappings purely out of cultural affiliation rather than genuine religious devotion, sure - this would be especially true of they lost personal respect for a particular god, of course.

                                But Confucianism (which is a big influence on Exalted, albeit more on the Sidereals and the Immaculate Faith than the Solars) tends to take the perspective that religion brings social benefits regardless of whether it is true or not. I could see many Exalted taking the same perspective (the Sidereals certainly do, and many DBs probably do as well) - even if you know that an individual god is corrupt, you might feel that there's value in retaining the traditions and structures around them, which can provide stability even if the god is personally weak or unworthy.

                                Of course, the Exalted might also decide to solve the problem by punching the god's face off and replacing them with someone more worthy. They wouldn't necessarily see this as irreverent, if their devotion is to the system and its trappings rather than to an individual deity. On the other hand, punching a god's face off too often tends to lead to instability, which leads to internal conflicts as Exalted weigh these options against each other.

                                3/ Just in my house alone are the altar for my deceased grandparent, near it is the altar for the Buddhism pantheon, scattering around the house are altars to god of kitchen, the ground, luck, wealth, longevity...etc. In the street are temples and shrines to river gods, wandering ghosts, deified people or even animal like tiger.

                                Is this suppose to be what most the religion landscape in Creation looks like i.e People follow a mixture of different beliefs and deities from various religions ? Since I heard that Creation is suppose to be animism and Shintoism or something, does that means place like Haborhead and Whitewall are oddballs since they only worship one supreme deity ?
                                Creation is huge! So there's a wide variety of stuff.

                                In places that see less travel and where culture is more monolithic, I would expect to see more dedicated reverence for a single deity or a discrete clearly-defined group of deities (or a philosophy in some places, although the fact that the gods are real and have genuine power makes it hard for that to make serious inroads without supernatural backing, like the Immaculate Faith has). In these places the importance of an individual deity or group of deities could be exaggerated, sometimes even to the point of making them chief deity and treating them like the ruler of heaven.

                                In areas with more cross-cultural contact, the kind of religious syncretism you described would be more common as different gods and faiths combine and form a common ground. But I suspect that while Creation has a lot of religious diversity, in most parts of the world it is somewhat constrained by the way certain religious facts are, well, true - there may be a few areas where an individually powerful god managed to establish monotheism (often dismissing or explaining lesser deities as eg. subservient spirits or angels or demons the way Christianity adapted many older deities into those roles), but that would be very rare. So a general understanding of "there are a bunch of gods, as well as our ancestors, plus some distant celestial gods who rule over them" is extremely common, with the main differences being over the exact Celestial Order, the attention and weight given to individual gods (with local ones often being exaggerated in importance), and the balance of attention paid to ancestors vs. gods - there would be some places that entirely deny parts or all of the basic understanding of Exalted's spiritual landscape, but they'd be isolated exceptions rather than the rule.

                                (The Immaculate Faith is in some respects the big exception, of course, but even it doesn't really deny most of the basic aspects of the setting beyond putting the Elemental Dragons over the Incarnae and positing a different way in which people ought to interact with the divine. And it's able to be a big exception because it has a lot of influence and supernatural firepower behind it. Also, in the First Age and in areas where the Immaculate Faith has little sway, the hero cults of the Exalted themselves would sometimes form a bit of a third focus for worship alongside gods and ancestors.)

                                                In fact, in said Gurus, the powerful often meet - you see many a head of state or powerful politician who meet under the auspices of gurus or weird churches whom they outrank and could destroy, in the real world.

                                                That says how easily it would be for Solars to worship Ahlat, and to meet in the worship of Ahlat and strike friendships based on their faith. The fact they could bring down his cult is no different than emperors being able to rout a cult with a word, and still deciding to follow all of its taboos. All it takes is the acceptance that to follow the precepts of that god leads to a healthier, more spiritually fulfilling life.

                                                ... and really, while Chosen sometimes have more raw power, it is important to note that chosen do not have the same kinds of powers, and are not the same kinds of entities. When you pray for a god, he can give you a blessing. Chosen normally cannot do it to each-other. If you are a devotee of Smiling Lover and believes she will give you luck in love... you know, that is something she can do. And blessings are invisible enough that you never know whether your luck in love comes from just following the precepts of her church, or from her actual blessings.
