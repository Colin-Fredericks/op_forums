            Originally posted by TSk View Post

            Real people can examine their behavior and ask what motivates them to do things. I agree with Hapless here.

            A character with Compassion 5 is someone who always spares the lives of his enemies and will go to great lengths to avoid doing lasting injury even to someone trying to kill him. When that same person goes into Limit Break and murders someone... yeah, he's going to ask what the heck's wrong with him. If he doesn't figure it out, he's going to keep asking.
            Let's concede that Limit Break is an actual in-game thing, Limit tracks were designed by Autochthon, and the Great Curse is a magical effect that makes the Exalted do things they wouldn't otherwise do and so on. I don't agree with those premises, but that is the view many take with the Curse.

            A character with Compassion 5 and Red Rage of Compassion is someone who cares deeply about all life and believes in the better natures of others. But when under the extreme stress of seeing the innocent suffer and being unable to prevent it she violates her own code and seeks justice through violence, or just destroys property to vent her frustration. And it feels cathartic to give in to urge; relinquishing partial control of the Limit Break gives you back all your Willpower points, after all. Perhaps the character feels guilt afterwards or perhaps they feel justified; "I did not mean to do that, but THEY just provoked me beyond reason!" or maybe "It was not right what they were doing to those peasants, perhaps the next slaver will think twice!". Either way they would still own the action because people rarely conceive of themselves as lacking self-determination, which should be doubly true of powerful demigods. The position of "I didn't do this, SOMEONE ELSE made me do this!" is the realm of the conspiracy theorist and persecutory delusion, after all.

            Of course, I don't believe Limit is an in-setting thing. I think the Exalted are responsible for their actions and Limit Break is just a clumsy social mechanic for generating emotional and irrational behavior that resonates with the source material the game draws on.

            Originally posted by Colapso View Post

            Because that is not how Player Characters have been played since forever. Because someone who goes sulk in his tent in the middle of a big battle is called a douchebag by the other people at the table. People on RPG.net once described the typical player character as an 'Hyperdetached Sociopath'. Exalted was clearly designed to get those people to act like... well, people, and the GC is a way to let that happen as part and parcel of the game being played, not a disruption of it.

            And of course it is the first thing everyone wants to excise.
            I would argue the Great Curse isn't a thing one should be interacting with in-setting; the parable of the Great Curse is hidden from the actors on Creation's stage because it exists to inform us, the players/storytellers/audience, of the essential tragedy of the Age of Sorrows. Later Exalted books added material that support the "alien transperfect UMI effect that makes you a jerk" read of the Curse, unfortunately, so I can see why there's a visceral reaction to it from the fandom where curing it becomes a priority. A lot of the bad fluff around the Great Curse needs to be stripped out to go back to its roots.

            To take that tangent back to the core topic, to me the Solar Exalted are the ultimate power fantasy in a game about the nature of power. It can be your chance to be the Chosen One of your own pulp fantasy epic, but it takes place in a world where not everything is going to turn out happily just because you shine gold and have a magical sword. But the Solars are passionate and virtuous and righteous so they cannot turn back from using their power. How power is used, and what legacy is left from the glorious heroics of the Solars, is up to the players.

                        Originally posted by maelstromseeker View Post
                        Having no Great Curse and instead just people going crazy because they have power would make the setting too hopeless. The return of the Solars at the end of the Second Age might doom the world to endless cycles of rebellion and corruption, but it might actually save Creation instead. Just now someone pointed out that the return of the Solars is meant to bring hope. If the Great Curse is not external, then there is no hope for Creation to have a better future.
                        I agree in certain respects and disagree in others.

                        ​My preferred take on the setting would be that there is no Curse. You picked 300 people with no other traits in common besides that they had ambition and were willing to kill the Primordials. You are not going to get the Justice League with that criteria. Some of them are going to be assholes. Then those 300 people got given the job of ruling the entire world however they wanted, a harem of shapeshifter consorts, and advisers who are practically defined by never wanting to rock the boat. Said consorts and advisers were war buddies and thus would be even less likely to speak up if they were doing anything immoral, because people make excuses for their friends all the time. The only people who they might view as superiors went off to play the Games of Divinity and were totally willing to let them do as they pleased. This is not a recipe for an age of peace and prosperity that will last forever and ever. It is a recipe for an age that produces a lot of great works, then a lot of atrocities, and then implodes violently. Not every First Age Solar was a bad egg, but the ones that weren't were much too willing to let the ones that were get away with it.

                        If you want to fix Creation, first, you have to come up with a better system than the Solar Deliberative was, one that doesn't give the Solars or anyone else such free reign. Then you have to win back the trust and loyalty of all the other Exalts after their bad experiences, while dealing with their bad eggs. Then you have to actually prove that you're not a bad egg yourself, and I hope you know how to do that when faced with choices that aren't pure black and white. It's your decision whether your character is the type of person to get drunk with power and repeat the mistakes of history, but you don't get to just say that, you have to prove it in every session with every decision you make. You can do it, but there's no Charm to help you make the right choices.

                        If that was how Solars were written, I would like them.

                            Originally posted by maelstromseeker View Post
                            Having no Great Curse and instead just people going crazy because they have power would make the setting too hopeless. Just now someone pointed out that the return of the Solars is meant to bring hope. If the Great Curse is not external, then there is no hope for Creation to have a better future.
                            The idea is to make heroes who aren't complete Mary Sues, and like most social mechanics it's primarily there to keep from all the characters becoming similarly maladjusted, as someone earlier mentioned happening with DnD's land of sociopaths. I like the idea of, but also have two major problems with, the great curse:

                            1) It's given an external cause but is deliberately built so the exalted can never discover it without a huge ass-pull. The closest thing to a logical reason I've ever heard to learn of the Great Curse's discovery that didn't involve rewriting part of the setting is that a Twilight caste began to notice patterns in how Exalted behaved under extreme stress - patterns that were too consistent to be coincidence. (but that is a little too meta for my taste) This makes the fall of whatever new golden age you build seem inevitable, since it's a curse specifically MADE to make you fail. It's the only thing in the entire setting that you can't do a single thing about. It's easier to destroy all of Creation in a single kick than to find out about the Great Curse.

                            2) The Flaws that it manifests in are defined within the book itself. It makes them too rigid. Virtues and virtue flaws define your character's personality - they are very personal. So why do a bunch of people who have nothing in common react to the suffering of the innocent in the same way? My Night Caste has heart of flint, but given the choice, I would have made his limit break that he utterly refused to admit defeat. (granted, I'm inexperienced with Exalted, so maybe there's more than the 2 or 3 per virtue I found)

                                            Originally posted by Nephtys View Post
                                            Yes, there is, because Limit Break is a binary state of mind. Either you have Limit 0-9 and couldn't be more happy with your life, or you reached 10 and completely break down.
                                            Adding to that that Solars can only reduce their Limit by 1 per story, every single one of them is going notice that sooner or later. It's an external force, more similar to mind control than your ideals and virtues. Someone got in your head, and you don't know who or even what. Sooner or later you're going to ask questions. In a realistically played 2E game it's probably sooner.
                                            Because Solars don't look at what they did and go on with their lives. Every single Solar is going to become a transhumanist in the long run, because they are not human. Making Solar special humans and nothing more is a notion owing its existence to the Infernals. Because Internals are transhumanists, aren't they? No they're not. They leave their humanity behind for power. That's not transhumanism. What Solars do is.
                                            I kinda feel like, if you interpret the Great Curse in light of its mechanical particulars rather than how the game describes it, you aren't really taking it seriously as a game element. Ignoring what the fluff says and attempting to extrapolate fluff from crunch alone results in making up your own game. You can do that, but it's not the game I'm talking about!

                                            Beyond that, though, I think you have an unrealistic notion of how people's minds work. Obviously we can't do this to check, but from what I've seen the evidence seems to be that even if you could inject wildly foreign thoughts into people's heads, most people would rationalize those thoughts as fitting organically into their personality and worldview so long as they were conscious of themselves while having them. The Great Curse is not foreign in that sense, because it comes from you. It is an expression of your own personality. If you have, say, Deliberate Cruelty as your Limit Break, the Great Curse is not that sometimes the curse of the Primordials makes you cruel, it is that in some respect you are naturally cruel, and sometimes the curse of the Primordials stokes that to a fever pitch.
