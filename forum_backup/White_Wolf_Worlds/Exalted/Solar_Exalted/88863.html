    "Twitch Plays Pokemon" and other Solar Circles

    03-11-2014, 11:48 AM
    One thing I've often been challenged by on Exalted's basic premise is thinking of examples of circles, rather than just individual Exalted. A lot of our references- Hercules, Arjuna, etc. tend to be fairly independent.

    Then I realized that you could probably pitch the Israelite leaders in Exodus as a party of Solars; Moses is the Zenith, Aaron is the Eclipse, Joshua is the Dawn- you can even work Miriam as a Night caste.

    From this it also occurred to me that you could distribute Solar Exaltations to Mohammed and his followers; to Justinian and Belisarius.

    I'd be lying if I didn't say the cognitive spark of looking for groups where the key players display a mixture of religious authority, charisma, and willingness to use force (with the weight of each trait varying from individual to individual) did not come from this poster, no doubt distributed by the local chapter of the Wyld Hunt: https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Bib9mWICYAA1b76.jpg

    Edit: Can you think of any other examples that have been useful for you in conceptualizing stories where the Circle works as a team, rather than 2-5 powerful people that all know each other?

              Originally posted by Mahasamatman View Post

              Yeah sorry, I guess I should have been a bit more clear on what I was going for here-I've edited the OP accordingly. Really I'm curious about exactly what Dracsin mentions: examples of stories where the protagonists are plausible inspirations for Solars, and actually work together on a regular basis towards a common goal, rather than occasionally meeting up to stop some existential threat that overwhelms whatever personal narrative they have. Basically, teams where they're all on-screen most of the time, rather than just the final boss-fight.

              For example, I don't really understand the Avengers or the Justice League, at least from my limited experience of them, as being useful examples of the sort of stories you'd tell about a Solar Circle. Most of the time, Ironman, the Hulk, and Thor are all pursuing their own very different adventures; it's only when some alien super-fleet shows up that they all converge onto one battlefield.

              The party from Twitch Plays Pokemon is a Solar Circle because their individual legends within the community are thoroughly intertwined, and are all thought of as working together to serve Lord Helix/beat the game/whatever. Each gets their turn to shine, but they're all traveling together all the time, and working towards the same goal.

              The party from Exodus is a Solar Circle because, again, they work together towards the same goal. They spend a considerable portion of their time on-screen together, either discussing their next step in leading the other Israelites or directly suppporting one another, as Moses channels divine inspiration into Aaron, who gives a rousing speech to the fighters that Joshua leads into battle.

                                  Originally posted by BvBPL View Post
                                  I don't think that the fact that those guys have individual adventures impeaches the teamwork they display when brought together.                                   Let me clarify further: I don't mean to denigrate the Avengers, etc. as not having real teamwork. What I'm saying is that I don't find them very useful as a guide for how to run an Exalted campaign where you have players who have to sit through any scenes where their character isn't present. I find it hard enough to get people to commit for 3 hours of game without then telling them they only get to play their character for a small portion of that time.

                                  Exalted is competing with D&D, yeah? In D&D, the default narrative is one of a bunch of hired thugs sent to loot and pillage some monster-infested ruin. Say what you will about the weakness of that narrative, at least all 4 thugs pursue their interest in killing green people and taking their gold together. They work together as an adventuring company. The fighter and the rogue may have their own subplots which occasionally come up, but most of the time the rogue is picking locks so the fighter can run through the door and stab things.

                                  I'm looking for examples of Solar Circles that operate chiefly as a team in other media as an exercise in coming up with campaigns that are about heroes of arete empowered by the gods who also happen to work as a team most of the time. The Avengers are limited in this instance because their plots, at least as I have experienced them, are "there is some bad guy so bad that we need to drop our own storylines and team up to fight her/him/it/them together!" Sure, they work together to deal with this, but I'm not sure I can make every session of Exalted an existential boss fight.

                                  So far, the best examples I've found are religious/revolutionary groups. This way the Dawn can lead the troops, the Zenith can inspire the faithful, the Night can guard against internal threats, the Twilight can build cool stuff/heal the injured, and the Eclipse can handle any negotiations. I've yet to run this, but it seems like game-play would consist of the PCs spending a lot of time in the command tent, meeting with external rulers, handling complaints brought by their lieutenants, and discussing how to deal with particular problems.

                                  The way the Solars have generally been presented previously is as a bunch of exceptional individuals who Exalt and then happen to run into each other, and occasionally find reason to work together. This makes for great chapter fiction, but if you want to run a campaign about 4 of them, it would seem that you need to make their stories overlap a lot more than that.

                                  As another example, I suspect you could have the Solars all be in the ruling cabinet of a kingdom. You could possibly get away with them being Robert Baratheon (Dawn), Eddard Stark (Zenith), Varys (Night), Petyr Baelish (Eclipse), and Grand Maester Pycelle (Twilight).

                                                      Okay, it looks like y'all need some ENSEMBLE CAST input. So. Here we go.

                                                      The Party of Two:
                                                      If you don't know it, check out Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser: They star in a number of sword-and-sorcery stories from back in the good old days when fantasy was pulpy and sword and sorcery was less Tolkien and more Burroughs and Moorcock. Fafhrd was the mighty Dawn caste warrior, and the Gray Mouser was a Night caste of incredible sophistaction. They were bros before people started using the word bros, and got each other into and out of all manner of difficulties, sometimes involving treasure and sometimes involving romance and often involving a bit of comedy. Two players is the smallest I'm willing to go when I run a game, because parties of one are boring, so I'll start with these guys.

                                                      For additional party-of-two dynamic ideas, try Sherlock and Watson (badass Watson, not incompetent Watson or sexy Watson or gay Watson-there are many Watsons) for a Twilight and a Dawn caste doctor (now that's a great character concept right there!). You can also go Gilgamesh and Enkidu, and if your players are comfortable with it you can add a little romance to the mix you can get all manner of delightful interpersonal complications. If you're looking for a reason for a party of two to stick together even though they have severely different life goals, make 'em family: siblings, parent-and-kid, lovers, or even a married couple. This trick also works to keep sections of a larger party together.

                                                      The Party of Three:
                                                      Here, you'll want to look no farther than Buffy, because if there's anything Joss Whedon does well, it's ensemble casts. Buffy is a great dawn, and her party members start out as mortals (though Giles the Sidereal helps get them all on the path to hero town) but Willow turns into a badass Twilight. Xander stays a heroic mortal the whole time, showcasing many of the really fun and interesting party dynamics you can have if your players are willing to play at different power levels: his desire to be useful to the team and his insecurity about his abilities to do so drive a lot of interest plot for the whole party. Other players join the group occasionally and showcase all manner of useful dynamics: interparty romance, interparty betrayals of trust or confidence, interventions over addictive behavior, etc. Lots of social dynamic ideas here. Notable Exalted inspiration in the show includes Faith and Angel and Spike as Abyssals (and hell, Drusilla too), a fourth-season casting of Unity of the Closed Fist, everything the Watchers do as Sidereal fodder, and the emotional and interpersonal consequences of power and regularly fighting off apocalypses.

                                                      Also, Kirk and Spock and McCoy are the best trio. Just...the best.

                                                      For additional party of three ideas, take a look at the Power Trio page over on TV Tropes for a number of common party dynamics. In fact, check out the Four Man Band, Five Man Band, and Battle Couple and Adventure Couple pages as well. If you're looking to create a stable party with interesting dynamics, those pages are a gold mine.

                                                      Party of Four: Here you'll want to take a look at Avatar: The Last Airbender, in particular the second and third seasons. The main characters are all Exalted whose raw power level makes them Solars in comparison to the world around them and their ability to change it by sheer force, so don't get hung up on their elemental affinities. Sokka's actually a Sidereal Half-Caste, by the way. You'll note that once it becomes a party of five, they start splitting into smaller groups to do things on their own. This is because four is most you can count up to instinctively (a lot of magic tricks and misdirection rely on that fact, by the way): once there are five people it starts getting harder for the audience to track them.

                                                      Party of Five: This is the largest number of PCs I generally like to run. It means each PC only has to keep track of four other PCs, which is generally the largest number of PCs they can be relied upon to become emotionally invested in: larger groups tend to sort themselves into smaller clumps or cliques, and the point at which it gets harder to give everyone screen time to develop their own personal plots. Generally once the party is this large you have to focus them on a unified goal or they start breaking up. You can find good examples of parties of Five or higher in more Whedon-works (Firefly is great Exalted inspiration particularly if you're running Heaven's Reach: the party has a Zenith, his Full Moon mate, a Dawn, a Twilight, an Air Aspect, a Chosen of Serenity, an Eclipse, a Chosen of Secrets, and an Abyssal). It's hard to keep a party this big stable, and the best way to do it is by having each player have some manner of unbreakable bond to at least two other players.

                                                      Now, I know you're looking for "party of Solars" here, rather than mixed parties. But here's the thing: ANY character, from ANY of these pieces of source material, could be a Solar. Solars are humans who can change the world due to unsurpassed skill. Kirk doesn't need to be able to glow or summon demons or shoot fire out his ass: he is a leader, and he's brilliant Zenith or Dawn, or hell even Eclipse inspiration for your characters. Look not to their powerset, for they're in a different universe: look to their role in the story. Are they an explorer, a negotiator, a leader, a scholar? These archetypes are what castes are built on, and anyone badass enough to be an impressive Main Character is probably worthy Solar inspiration.

                                                          Mostly what Wise Old Guru said, but some additional thoughts:

                                                          If you want to keep the cast together most of the time, it'll be hard to find inspiration if there are more than three or so characters. But then, as far as my own gaming experience goes... you will never succeed in keeping the party together all the time. It just won't happen. But, if you're satisfied with most of the time (which is really all you'll generally get), there are a bunch of examples - basically anything with an ensemble cast. Wise Old Guru threw out Firefly, which is a good example, but some others do come to mind. So here, I'll just toss out a bunch of examples at various party sizes (mostly anime, because it's the medium I happen to have substantial lists of at my disposal, and I've been watching a bunch lately):

                                                          Two to Three
                                                          Castle
                                                          Elementary
                                                          Sherlock
                                                          El Cazador de la Bruja
                                                          Precure Series (numerous - Futari wa, Max Heart, Splash Star, Suite, a lot of Heartcatch and Fresh)
                                                          Magical Girl Lyrical Nanoha, Magical Girl Lyrical Nanoha A's
                                                          Senki Zesshou Symphogear
                                                          Mai-HiME
                                                          Ga-Rei-Zero (depending on how you slice it)
                                                          Buddy Complex
                                                          Log Horizon (at least what I've seen)
                                                          Supernatural
                                                          Sabriel (all three, really)
                                                          Powers
                                                          Lord of the Rings, post-Breaking (really, post-Fellowship the story is three of these - if you have eight players who want to play, this would be a hell of a way to kick off several separate games)
                                                          Original Shannara trilogy


                                                          Four or more
                                                          Avengers: Earth's Mightiest Heroes (while there are individual flashback episodes, there is a very solid core cast that shows up most of the time, even if it fluctuates a bit)
                                                          Wakfu
                                                          Young Justice
                                                          Lord of the Rings, pre-Breaking
                                                          Every Power Rangers, ever
                                                          Magical Girl Lyrical Nanoha StrikerS (which set of four are the PCs is open to interpretation)
                                                          Precure Series (Splash Star, Smile, Yes!, parts of Fresh and Heartcatch)
                                                          Puella Magi Madoka Magica (albeit with a group of players with very different interests than a party of Exalted players usually will)
                                                          Genei wo Kakeru Taiyou
                                                          David Eddings' Belgariad, Mallorean, Elenium, and Tamuli, as well as the Redemption of Althalus (kind of)
                                                          Gundam 00


                                                          Note that all of these examples fit into at least one of three categories:
                                                          Bound together by holding the same job or calling
                                                          Bound together by a quest
                                                          Bound together by circumstances beyond their control

                                                          It is possible to do other combined plot types with a coherent group, but it's hard. Since the third plot type doesn't work extraordinarily well with Exalted, and the first constrains stories pretty thoroughly in the first, you tend to default to quests (whether explicit, standard "destroy the dark lord", more implicit "keep this town safe", or more variable "well this one character's plot just came to the front" sort of deals).
