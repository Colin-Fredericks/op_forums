    Solar sorcery

    02-21-2020, 10:41 AM
    I've been trying to plan new custom solar sorcery. I have the idea on what I want it to do, but would welcome all suggestions in how to balance it reasonably.

    Catchy Name Here, ideas welcome
    ---
    Cost: Ritual, 4wp OR 50sm, 2wp
    Keywords:
    Duration: Instant

    fluff and effects

    Solar sorcery spell to store anything temporarily to any surface as image.
    - Have farm/castle/town in wrong place?
    - Need to move army stealthily?
    - Want to steal something big?
    - Need to store something away (huge dangerous creation threatening beast maybe) frozen in time for long periods of time?
    - You should have the idea I'm after

    - Seal (1 km/100 m/? per essence?) sized area inside some object that now holds image of what is sealed inside

    - Time is frozen to everything inside
    - OR should time be only partially be frozen? Everything inside stay unchanged but they can interact inside seal with their environment. But with everything, them self's included staying as unchanging while inside. For them it would all be greatly muted dreamlike stage where time and action loses meaning.
    - If time is only partially frozen, control effect could be for caster be able to enter/exit image and affect whats inside to some degree...

    - Breaking the item or image on the item will restore sealed content to area facing broken image. If area would prove to be immediately dangerous for sealed content, it is instead then restored to closest safe spot to original spot. Usually exact same spot it originated from if it has not changed.
    - For example trying to unseal land bound army over water would not work but unsealing city in middle of forest clearing would work even if there would be forest fire, as long it would not be immediately dangerous.

    What would be good sized area to seal in?

    How fast it should be doable? Spell with sorcerous motes or some ritual taking x time?

    Should creatures inside of area have some way to resist and be left outside seal when used? Example int+occult vs resolve, control effect to have +essence to throw?

    Maybe limit how many one person have active at time? One or half essence (rounded down) as control effect?

    ---

    I'm still trying to form details down, especially considering how to balance it. All comments, discussions and suggestions are welcome.

      So, first, a note: there's actually already a thread for this kind of discussion. I realize it's not been posted to in a while, and dropped off the front page. Nevertheless, for future questions like this, I'd suggest posting them in there. There's a couple reasons for that. First, I suspect you'll get more responses, or at least quicker ones - people interested in this topic may have threadmarked it, or simply be more willing to check it when it comes up. Second, it's more helpful for other people who want to find things later. Someone else might be interested in this idea, but not remember who posted it or exact keywords to search for, and having all the details in one go-to place is easier to find than having to search through all the threads on the forum. Anyway, I'll repost this suggestion in there, so no need to ask for this to be moved or anything. Just a suggestion for the future. Note, I'm not a mod, this is not a directive on how to post, I'm just trying to provide advice I hope will be helpful. :-)

      That aside, here's my suggestion of how I would do something like this:

      The Fidelity of Images
      Cost:       50sm, 3wp OR Ritual, 4wp OR Ritual, 5wp
      Keywords:None
      Duration: Indefinite
      The sorcerer speaks the Mudra of Referent-Symbol Equivalence, the proscribed dictum uttered by one of the lost titans, that states that an object and that which describes it are the same. A blank canvas of shining white appears before her, and she begins to paint, colors streaming from her fingers or simply appearing on the blank sheet as she gazes at it. What she beholds in front of her is faithfully reproduced on the canvas, and when she is done, there is no difference between the two - only the painting remains.

      This spell can be used in three different ways. The first version, usable in combat, targets a single character or an object no larger than 5 yards in its longest dimension. Once the sorcerous motes listed above have been gathered, the sorcerer makes a special gambit. She rolls (Intelligence + Occult) against the target's Resolve to hit, and this attack is considered undodgeable and unblockable (an object on someone's person use the owner's Resolve, while unattended objects have an effective Resolve of 1). If she hits, the difficulty of the gambit is equal to the target's current temporary Willpower, or 3, whichever is higher. If the initiative roll for the gambit succeeds, the target vanishes, and is effectively stored in the painting.

      The second version is cast ritually, over the course of a day, starting at dawn and ending at dusk, costs 4wp, and can effect an area with a diameter of (EssenceX100) yards. During the ritual, various signs and portents appear within the area to be affected that indicate that something strange is going on (rolls of ([Intelligence or Wits] + Occult), difficulty 3, can identify the specific spell), and the sorcerer must paint in a location that allows her to see the majority of the area, and thus must be visible to the majority of the inhabitants as well. She may not perform any other activity, and any significant disruption or distraction (especially being forced to roll Join Battle) will end the ritual without effect. Once the ritual is complete, the sorcerer rolls (Intelligence + Occult) once, and compares her result to the Resolve of all who remain in the area at that time. Anyone whose Resolve she beats are taken into the painting, as are all inanimate objects and the terrain within the area.

      The third version works much like the second, except that it costs 5wp, can effect an area of up to (Essence) miles, and the ritual takes 7 days, during which the sorcerer must wander the area to be affected, sufficient to see a majority of it. She may pause the ritual to sleep and eat, but otherwise must continue it uninterrupted, as per the second version. At the end of the ritual, again like the second version, she must roll (Intelligence + Occult) against the Resolve of everyone who remains in the area, and all who she overcomes, and all the objects and terrain in the area, are taken into the painting.

      In all cases, people, objects, and terrain taken into a painting are effectively held in stasis - no time passes for them, they need no food or water, and will never age or decay. If terrain or large objects are taken up by this spell, what remains is "generic" terrain suitable for the region of Creation it was in: a grassy field in the East, empty sand in the Burning Waste of the South, a sandy islet or open sea in the West, and so on.

      The painting itself is vulnerable to damage, though it is far tougher than a mundane canvas, requiring effort and magic similar to that required to damage artifacts in order to harm it.

      The process of conversion to a painted image can be reversed. The sorcerer casting the spell must have the painting in hand (though it need not be a painting they themselves created). This always requires a casting of the second version of the spell, regardless of how many people or how large an area were originally contained. The people or objects take shape in the area before the sorcerer during the course of the ritual, finally jerking into motion and life again at its completion. If the painting contained terrain, it is "mixed" with the existing terrain, without significantly displacing existing features or overlapping anything. If this can't be done, the spell automatically fails. For example, if a painting of a forest scene were to be overlaid with an existing forest, the result would be a much-denser wood, with approximately twice as many trees packed together. However, the same forest scene could not be overlaid on an existing fortress or city, because the trees could not fit in with the buildings without displacing or overlapping with them.

      A sorcerer who chooses The Fidelity of Images as her control spell can interact with the paintings she creates much more comprehensively. While holding or regarding a painting, she may spend a point of temporary willpower to enter it. Doing so puts her in a pocket dimension of sorts, where the terrain and people in the painting exist. While the sorcerer is in the painting, time passes normally there, and she can interact with anything there just as she could in the real world.

      Outside of the scene contained in the painting, the pocket world is a flat white expanse stretching into infinity, the same color as the blank canvas. A sorcerer in the painting can expand it by casting the spell, using the second version with the day-long ritual. Treat each such casting as one phase of Wyld-Shaping Technique, except that she rolls (Intelligence + Occult) in place of (Intelligence + Lore), there is no encounter roll, and she may never do more than 7 phases of creation (which requires that she cast the spell 7 times in sequence).

      Terrain, objects, and people created in the pocket dimension of the painting can also be expelled into the real world. To do so, the sorcerer must spend 3wp while holding the painting to change its viewpoint to the new area she wants to make real. If some but not all of the pocket dimension is taken into the real world this way, the missing patches will appear in the painting's pocket dimension as blank, empty stretches of canvas.

      Distortion (Goal number 30): Distorting The Fidelity of Images requires that the distorting sorcerer target one of the paintings created by the spell, not the casting sorcerer. Successfully distorting the painting allows the distorting caster to change one major detail of the painting. A person could change appearance, a forest could transform into a different type of tree, a fortress could shift its design, and so on. The changes are limited to what a skilled painter could do to an existing artwork without completely destroying it, and transformations to people are always cosmetic - a woman from the South could be given the blue hair and brown skin of a native of the West, but when released from the painting, her memories, Intimacies, and other features of her mind would remain the same.
      A caster may distort their own casting of The Fidelity of Images, if she wants to transform something caught in the painting.
