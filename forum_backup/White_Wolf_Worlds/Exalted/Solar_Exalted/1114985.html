          1. Honestly I think most people in Creation, from the lowest peasant to the highest king, have a practical knowledge of their gods. They worship the gods as the giver of good things if mollified and bringers of dooms if they are unhappy. And those gods they do worship tend to be ones that have a distinct impact on their lives, like a field god for a hamlet of farmers or a directional goddess of trade for a Guildsman. Anything beyond that, like the very distant Incarna or gods in general, are simply included in generic acts of reverence to make sure none of them draw offense or withdraw their blessings. In short its not an act of faith as a Christian might understand it but a divine business proposition. This is of course a generalization. Creation has plenty of dedicated cults and sects of various gods.

          2. Sure. Stemming from above many people are more interested in the feast at the festivals than than the meaning behind them. I don't think the gods would even see it as a problem as long as the worship at the appropriate times and place keeps coming and a basic respect is maintained. Of course some gods are more temperamental and demanding than others so its not always a safe assumption.

          3. Yes. At least in the Threshold you would see roadside shrines to a dozen gods right next to the grand temples to the Incarna, with people attending the various shrines and temples as life's needs and duties drove them. In Immaculate-held areas the worship of the gods falls more directly into the province of its monks than the common people but even there little private shrines pop up all over the place. Even Harborhead only recognizes its bull god as its patron deity but that doesn't stop worship of various other types of god as long as the populace remembers who is the patron god.

                  Originally posted by Jen View Post

                  I would say that it depends on the nature of the Solar's initial religion. If it was based on a significant deception on the part of the god involved, particularly in the form of elevating their own significance, I would say that a lot of Solars would become disillusioned by likely experiences. If it's somebody more like Ten Sheaves, important to the community and promulgating a decent message that the Solar can still live by as one of the Exalted, then I would say one can still maintain a lot of respect for the god (in a manner that can include worship), even while being elevated to the status of a peer.

                  ​It may sometimes go further and become a case of the religious element of the gods still being held up as important while the Solar inserts themselves into it. Not necessarily as a deity, but at least a case of "these are still figures to honour and principles to live by, and I'll lead you through the structure of them". That kind of what Perfect Soul is doing, although in her case her own regard for the gods is lower.

I'd say it's most evident in cities such as Nexus, where gatherings of large numbers of people will create a synthesis of many cultural systems and practices, and knowledge of a wide variety of spirits, and Great Forks is kind of built around it.

                  ​I think that in a lot of smaller areas, especially rural, it's easier for certain gods to monopolize the people's attention, either because they control a very significant landscape feature, or have strong-armed their way into it. If they're organized into a court, promoting a bit of secondary worship for a subordinate is likely a goal that many compete for influence over, or might receive as a reward for some service.

                          If somebody put "just fucking wish for world peace, you scum sifu" on a t-shirt, I might buy it.

                          1. If you're already accustomed to polytheism, incorporating other people's religions into your own isn't too much of a mental hurdle. Even if you're a committed monotheist, it doesn't take much in the way of mental gymnastics to accept the idea that people in other places have their own beliefs. You may think those beliefs are, to some extent, wrong (only you know the true will of God!), but you accept them anyway.

                          Something important to remember is that the books do not have the First Commandment ("Thou shalt have no other gods before me") written in them anywhere, for any deity. Unless you decide otherwise, there's no rule saying that you must refuse to acknowledge other gods even if you're committed to only worshiping one.

                          2. I believe people who practice a religion, but are not spiritual, absolutely do exist in Creation. In fact, they might be more common in Creation than in the real world - in Creation, you can personally meet your gods on occasion, and your interactions with them might be more like a series of business transactions than true worship. That, I think, would lead more people to develop pragmatic, earthly-minded approaches to the practice of religion.

                          3. Different parts of Creation have different practices. I suspect your house's collection of shrines makes a pretty good template for most of the world. Maybe in areas where the Immaculates hold sway, nobody is allowed to have personal shrines and access to public shrines is limited to certain days and times. Maybe in Sijan, monuments for the ancestors outnumber the temples 100-1. Maybe there are Malfean cults that eschew the worship of all gods in favor of demons. Whatever you think is appropriate!

                                      1.) I think that this really depends on the character being played, especially in a setting like creation where faith and belief get a bit muddled when you can actually see the physical manifestations of gods and demons in the world around you which proves their existence. I think in creation religious practice would have a lot more to do with who you believe is going to benefit you the most, and in most cases that is not going to be the sun considering he has turned his attention away from creation, whereas of course you're going to give worship to the local field guardian, or you could starve. however, it would be very interesting to see a character whose motivation is to earn the suns respect again and to turn them back towards creation, in which case a manifestation of their religion would be their faith that they can make the sun god care again, for this character no amount of seeing other spirits is going to change their opinion on worshipping the sun, for that matter no amount of seeing other spirits is likely to change anyones already preconceived notions about faith, especially if they are an ardent believer, given that seeing spirits in creation should not be all that uncommon, although most peoples of creation imo would shift their worship as appropriate as they move from place to place recognizing the greater power of one god over that particular region than the god from the previous region.
