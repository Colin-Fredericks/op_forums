    Solar Transhumanism?

    04-15-2014, 07:53 PM
    There's an old quote somewhere on the old forums about a Solar who goes searching for cosmic transcendence and eventually figures out that he already has it by virtue of being a Solar. This has always struck me as a cop-out when compared to "real" transhumanists who have mechanical support for their transformations into "something else" like the Infernals and Alchemicals. Could someone explain to me where this position comes from? I have difficulty seeing how a Solar can be called transhuman any more than Batman or Son Goku can be called such.

          Yes, it's sort of a cop-out, in the sense of "achieving immortality through the memories of others." But that goal is going to be kind of like chasing the pot of gold at the end of the rainbow, so I can't really fault anyone who decides at some point, "The real pot of gold was in me all along! What a relief, I'm gonna go home and take a shower."

          I sort of think of the "weird, busted" Exalted -- the Abyssals and Infernals -- as the ones you go to for transhuman themes, by virtue of the fact that they're kind of alien and "unstable" and evoke themes otherwise at odds with the nature of Exaltation. Alchemicals sort of apply, I guess -- mentally I tended to exclude them because the transhumanist narrative tends to be strongly individualist and Alchemicals start out fairly un-individualist and become less so as they mature, but it fits.

          I don't think it's ever going to be anything but at odds with Solar motifs, though. Solars are the most human Exalted, and transhumanism comes from a place of deep dissatisfaction and disenchantment with humanity and humanism. A Solar is never going to be able to step away from their basic humanity the way an Abyssal can -- they might become weird or terrible, but not in a transhuman way. This is so integral to what "being a Solar" is that I would probably say that any decisive success at overcoming it would involve either shedding your Exaltation or becoming a different sort of Exalted.

              I'm not tremendously well informed on transhumanist culture, but from what I can see, at least some of their objectives can be achieved by means of Solar Charms, in the sense of transcending the limitations of their human bodies and minds.

              Jumping over mountains, running faster than a car, seeing things that are invisible to normal humans, manipulating the fundamental forces of the universe to create stable reality from unreal chaos...there is a lot of stuff in the Solar charmset that fits within the desires of few transhumanists with which I have talked about it or whose desires I've read about. You know, the guys who go "I wish I could see the full electromagnetic spectrum instead of seeing the universe with these limited human eyes". Essence Sight is a decent analogue to that in Exalted.

              We look at genetic manipulation or trading our bodies for machines and things like that as a way to leave aside many of the worries and limitations of humanity, but when you have Solar magic on your side, you don't necessarily have to look for something weird in order to achieve that.

              Perhaps I have misunderstood something, though. When some people talk about it, it seems they want to be fundamentally different from humans, while other simply seem to want to be able to do things humans cannot, cases in which makes it hard for me to find the distinction between transhuman and superhuman.

              I mean, ok, a Solar probably doesn't do the equivalent of uploading his personality into a network of computers to expand their mind beyond the human limit, but he could probably achieve any of the end results (amazing amounts of knowledge, calculating things at incredible speeds) by just "being really freaking knowledgeable and enlightened". He won't turn his eyes into cyber multi-spectrum-binoculars/microscopes, but he can see great distances or very small things or in the dark or the energy the makes up all things all the same, and so on. What is it that constitutes transhumanism, beyond the transcending of normal human capacities and limitations?

                                  Humanism also includes assertions of an ideal, exclusive and universal model for what humans either are, or should be.

                                  Which... actually kind of encompasses the ideas of self-realization through reason and that they all have dignity and worth.

                                  This is not me saying that humans can't or shouldn't self-realize through reason, mind, just that self-realization is not an ideal goal for every human, nor that it is only or ideally acquired through reason*.

                                  Likewise, if I view not every human to have dignity or (to the extent that such a subjective value can even be meaningfully ascribed in the first place) "worth", that is not me saying that it's okay to mistreat or otherwise degrade people who might be viewed as lacking those qualities.

                                  Antihumanism does not mean being anti human, it means being anti the philosophy of humanism.

                                  I reject humanism because I find the impulse to find a universal standard of what humans are or should be to be somewhere between misguided and logically inconsistent (I look out upon the rich varied tapestry of what human beings are and see neither worth nor possibility of success in attempting to box it all up in uniformity), and because, at worst, I view it as a preface to defining models of humanity specifically designed around marginalizing people who do not and cannot fit into them under a pretext of rationality.

                                  It's why I reject reference to even the most terrible people as "inhuman" in all but the most poetic terms (especially as regards the concept of "if you act 'inhumanly' you waive your entitlement to human rights and dignity", one of the more odious arguments for varying kinds of brutalization of convicted criminals).

                                  Humans are anything they have been, anything they are, and anything they can be.

                                  You're no less human for having had an ancestor who was a snake, or having better physical hardiness or restoration, or having been an active part of a culture that thought throwing a bag of cats into a bonfire was a good larf.

                                  I find no credibility in transhumanism, because I find no credibility to the idea that any qualities that such modifications might provide are exclusive from the condition of being human.

                                  Hence, a Solar is no transhuman for anything provided by their Charms, by their Sorcery, by any internal or external artifacts they might possess.

                                  Some of the terminology John has used in the past actually lines in neatly with this perspective I'm developing; I'm growing to prefer to articulate Solars as being less obliquely magical than (in a setting where the lack of oblique magic was initial a severe disadvantage of humanity) over Solars as being "most human" of the Exalted, since I shouldn't really view the latter term as making much sense, and even kind of detracting from the other Exalted. Likewise with the idea that there's a necessary core of humanity to Terrestrials and all other Exalted.

                                  * In the context of Exalted, I feel as though the Infernals and possibly Lunars might be fairly explicit in form of self-realization that are achieved through... means other than reason.

                                          Transhumanism is kind of this big lump of a term that a lot of different ideas get tossed into.

                                          If you want to talk about transhumanism in the sense of you, personally, surpassing the limits of humanity by becoming "human plus", then that's literally every Exaltation. All of them come with a centuries-long lifespan minimum, superhuman physical abilities, and in most cases superhuman mental abilities as well. You don't even need to buy any Charms to be transhuman under that definition.

                                          If you want to talk about transhumanism in the sense of becoming something that is distinctly inhuman, then that's Lunar chimerism or Infernals. This concept of transhumanism really doesn't have much to do with the others, though.

                                          If you want to talk about transhumanism in the sense of improving the entirety of the human condition through technology, then no Exaltation does that. Exaltation is generally a very self-centered thing. Some sort of artifact construction or sorcerous working could deal with this type of transhumanism in theory, but it's not at all material covered by any of the Exalts. The only exception would be if the Dragon-Blooded had a way of guaranteeing Exaltation in a way that they could over time replace all humans everywhere with Dragon-Blooded. That would generally fit that model of transhumanism, but again it's something that would probably be accomplished through creating an artifact system to do so or performing some sort of great sorcery, rather than something within the Exaltation.

                                                          Interesting that this came up. Last night, I posted to my art blog a picture of my custom Deathlord who accidentally killed himself a few decades before the Usurpation, trying to make a robot body, but in the process trapped his now-dormant Exaltation in that robot body, and provided the first practical demonstration for the Sidereals that something like the Jade Prison was even possible. He was mostly intended as a cautionary tale about throwing out the baby (Solar Exaltation) with the bathwater (not being a gross assembly of meat and hair that won't stop secreting, excreting, bleeding, shedding, and exhaling all over things).

                                                          I think transhumanism is a thing Solars can explore, because it's a thing Humans can explore. Therefore, a Solar who gets it into his head to start shedding the traits of mere mortals is going to do a pretty boss job of it. To a certain extent, the story of jaded immortals becoming distant from their roots will automatically be similar. But it's not a default path, or one that would come across as particularly enlightened. If a Solar says "I want to be a Robot", he can do that, and probably make it work. If a Solar says "I want to become some kind of weird space octopus", there's probably a path available. If a Solar says "I want to become free of need and want, tranquil and centered, and sit on a mountain for a thousand years without eating or sleeping or breathing or blinking", that's a thing that he can do. It's good for everybody to have a hobby, is what I'm trying to say. The same Solar could say "I want to become SO GOOD at playing Poker, holy crap!" and dumps a thousand years of downtime EXP into custom card shark charms, if they wanted to. Sometimes people get crazy focused on one weird goal, and lose sight of everything else. That's absolutely a place Old Solars can and should go, if they like.
