            Originally posted by Odd_Canuck View Post
            IMO 3e is probably not going address it directly.

            Because having a solid answer doesn't add anything to the game, and leaving it undefined grants ST the ability to make up whatever best suits their own story.

            "Oh but the deathlords would have to know and they'd share that information with the Abyssals!" Why? Unless the deathlords are cartoon villians revealing plots to captured heroes or deliberately telling the potentially vastly more powerful Abyssals things they don't need to know, there's nothing to be gained from it.

            "How did you get my exaltation then?!? Oh Eye and Seven Black Rabbits That Peer Into The Abyss to Forsake The Chains that Bind.... *deep breath* to the Eternal Stone That Bears No Death?" Again, simply honest answers work well. "I traded a bunch of land to Bob for it. Canny ghost that Bob. No idea how he got it." or "As part of a convoluted arrangement with forces so dark the living can not bear to hear even the names of the forces uttered!"

            (IMO again) having it defined just gives players(and the STs) a reason to go "Ah-Ha! my super-clever Abyssal clearly KNOWS they were a Solar before, and is therefore doing X!" or "Well, everyone knows Abyssals are inverted Solars, of course X, Y, Z are all true."
            I agree with this.

            Much like with the role of the Incarna in the past editions, we should have enough information to extrapolate and create theories - enough points to support the framework of a theory, but not enough that it is spelled out to us. It might be useful to know if the Neverborn somehow came by then, and passed them to the Deathlords, if the Neverborn are less of a force and the Deathlords just got them themselves, somehow, if they got them from an unnamed-or-named third party, etc. But not the exact mechanics of how.

                        Originally posted by Elkovash View Post
                        I'm my current game I'm running with a slightly more philosophical rendition of the Jade prison.

                        Essentially, after the usurpation, the devastation wrought left the world's populace largely in despair - the new world order imposed by the Sidereals is the calculated balance of *just* enough turmoil to satisfy the power players and *just* enough insurmountable social structure to keep the lower classes in line. Balancing the world on this fulcrum of despair and hopelessness while rewarding the more avid and aggressive citizens meant that no one was worthy of Sol's love, adoration and - by extension - Exaltation.

                        There were always the fringe cases of someone hoping against hope but they were so few and far between in this new world order that a marginally organized Wyld hunt could track them down easily enough and dispatch them in so much pomp and rituals that it would further dissuade the populace from dreaming the impossible dream.

                        The rise of the Scarlet Empire was exactly what the Sidereals needed after the devastation wrought by the crusade and contagion; a new state ordered religion that actually prohibited greatness on a spiritual level, the final nail in the coffin to any potential Solar Exalted. All they had to do was let the empires influence expand, and clean up the last remnants of those few dreamers that had slipped through the web of mundanity. Everything was going great for almost eight centuries and then... the unthinkable happened.

                        Suddenly the Realm's influence turns inwards, those Sidereals tasked with maintaining the balance in the threshold areas loose the looming threat of a world-spanning empire and the people start to think that maybe things could be better, and the light of hope begins to return to the world.


                        I really like this idea! It plays with the indian vedic themes that are already present in the core (like with the shinma and the Raksha). It feels like what you are describing is from Mayahana Buddhism (I think).The term Maya has been translated as 'illusion,' but then it does not concern normal illusion. 'Illusion' does not mean that the world is not real and simply a figment of the human imagination. Maya means that the world is not as it seems; the world that one experiences is misleading as far as its true nature is concerned. Māyā not only deceives people about the things they think they know; more basically, it limits their knowledge. That is what you are describing.

                        or for a more pop culture reference- it is the matrix. Which is basically Immanuel Kant critique of Reason....

                        Or- another philosophical bent- Plato! Plato has Socrates describe a group of people who have lived chained to the wall of a cave all of their lives, facing a blank wall. The people watch shadows projected on the wall from objects passing in front of a fire behind them, and give names to these shadows. The shadows are the prisoners' reality. Socrates explains how the philosopher is like a prisoner who is freed from the cave and comes to understand that the shadows on the wall are not reality at all, for he can perceive the true form of reality rather than the manufactured reality that is the shadows seen by the prisoners. The inmates of this place do not even desire to leave their prison; for they know no better life.

                        The Jade prison is Plato's Cave.

                        thumbs up dude. I like this metaphorical prison much better than a physical one. Kudos.

                                                I'd just as soon see it being something totally different that broke the Solars free, regardless of it's narrative impact on the Deathlords and Yozi's getting their hands on some of them.

                                                I'd love to see the Bronze Sids do it by accident when checking in on it.
                                                I'd love to see the Gold Sids do it intentionally because enough is enough.
                                                I'd love to see the Scarlet Empress do it intentionally when she foresaw a massive threat in the near future.
                                                I'd love to see the Lunars do it by accident, when they think they're assaulting the Scarlet Empress' secret source of power.
                                                I'd love to see a minor battle between the Realm and Lookshy accidentally crack it open.
                                                I'd love to see a mortal adventurer think they're looking an artifact that will make them rich, but instead release the Solars and exalt as the first of their number.
                                                I'd love to see a freak Wyld storm/incursion break it open by accident.
                                                I'd love to see an intentional Wyld incursion break it open because only the Solars ever brought the Fair Folk lord the challenge they've sought.

                                                I'd love to see all of these be the reason. But more than any of that, I'd love for it to never be mentioned again in a book, so that any of these could be the reason. Because every one of these creates interesting story hooks that haven't been published before. The Deathlord/Yozi thing has been done. Twice.

                                                    For something completely different what if we put a focus on 3e's approach to exaltation as a "blessing from the Sun" instead of a "green lantern ring"? What if they're not a bunch of shards that the Sidereals locked up in a box?

                                                    I propose a new headcanon that the Sidereals did a great Working that locked away the Sun's power from Creation after convincing him to turn away from his Chosen. Perhaps they spend decades influencing the Sun, drawing attention in heaven toward the Solar's atrocities and acts of selfishness and hedonism, so that the Sun turned his face away from Creation. With his attention gone, the Sidereals Worked a way to block his power from continuing through Creation. Then in the modern age, the Sun turns his face back upon Creation for the first time and sees heroes, who just need the power to save the world. His attention breaks the Working of the Sidereals, returning his power back unto Creation, blessing those heroes he was so impressed by.

                                                    There's a ton of handwavy in here, because I just thought this up during lunch. But it helps shift the focus away from "shards of exaltation" to "the influence of The Unconquered Sun" at least somewhat. Just a headcanon, but it might be my new default in my games.
