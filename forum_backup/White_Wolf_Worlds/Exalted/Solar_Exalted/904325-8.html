    Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post
    The "running" Athletics Charms talk a lot about "Tests of Speed," which seem to have been some sort of Extended Action, but are not otherwise attested in the rules.
    It's "test of speed" not "Tests of Speed." It's just a phrase, not a rules term. The first instance of the phrase in the Athletics section refers you back to the page on extended rolls (because that's what tests of speed will most often be), which even gives an example of a foot race. The phrase "test of speed" is used a lot in the Athletics Charms because a lot of Athletics Charms benefit you when you're trying to go quick, and "test of speed" is a relatively succinct phrase that envelops any kind of movement (climbing, swimming, jumping between rocks) for which you're trying to do something faster than someone or something else..

    I realize how it happened, but I never took that phrase to mean anything more than it is, and until I heard other people talking about it how you do, CK, I never would've realized there was this idea it used to be some special rule or something. A totally weird reading to my eyes.

    I always suspected, and Sanct has a link to prove it, that there aren't Athletics Charms to benefit opposing Disengages because the devs wanted to push combat to be more dynamic, not have everyone get stuck around the one guy who can hold them all in place with big charm-powered rolls.

    That said, I think there's space for limited use Charms that benefit opposing disengage. Like a once-per-scene reroll or something as you trip someone up.

                    Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                    You're right, you're not being gaslighted. You're just not getting what I'm trying to say.

                    Maybe it's my fault. I'll try to make it clearer:

                    The specific reason you suggested for cap-boosters to not exist is not a plausible reason for cap-boosters to not exist.
                    Expanding on this a little: let's do a very coarse-grained division of all of our possible opponents into two groups, which we will call "Mortal die pools" and "Above mortal die pools."

                    If you are a Solar who has invested in his Resolve at all - like, even just raised the mundane stats - you are mostly okay against people with mortal die pools. This is because (a) most of the people with mortal die pools are mortals, who mostly throw, like, 5 dice, (b) even for people throwing 10ish dice, Intimacies tend to favor the defender (because you more rarely need to be convinced to do something that's in line with your character concept anyway), (c) if someone's throwing mortal die pools at you, they're probably also using mundane influence that you can disregard for a Willpower, and (d) if you're really worried, you can always spend a 4-6m on the Excellency to cut their odds down to low-single digits; thanks to the Retrying Social Influence rules on p. 222, they can't spam persuasion attempts, and so this is probably not a repeated expense.

                    The "Above mortal die pool" group is much scarier. A social-specced Exalt might be able to average successes on the order of 2x your base Resolve - Lunars, Sidereals, and Dragon-Blooded, at least, can all manage this trick, and E4+ spirits seem to have comparable Excellency-equivalents. And that's if you're also Resolve-focused; if you're rocking 3-4 in the relevant stats, you're in considerably more trouble, and your Excellency cap is lower to boot. Added to that, magic is more likely to be able to ignore your Intimacies, it's often more expensive to resist, and if you don't resist, it can very plausibly wreck your day.

                    So it's this second group that's the major concern. And for the second group, the Excellency alone is not likely to suffice; if you have an attacker and a defender with equivalent pools and equivalent Excellencies, odds favor the attacker. That's a big deal! Now, the defender might gain mitigation techniques - ways to recover from being mind-whammied with social magic - but in the Solar combat trees, he also has access to prevention magic that kicks his natural defenses higher than he can get them with Excellencies alone. In Integrity, he doesn't.

                    So "But cheaper Excellencies!" - and the Excellency conversation in general - are important for the "Mortal die pool" crowd, but that's not really as major of a concern. By contrast, they're not really a lot of assurance against the really dangerous social characters, and it seems, at least on the face of it, a bit odd that Integrity doesn't get more toys to use to protect yourself.

                    (Sanctaphrax, is that roughly fair to your position?)

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                        Holden has repeatedly said that playing combat as everybody dividing one on one is a fool's game, and I see several areas in the system around that.
                                        So... Random page 2 necromancy!: My take on this is with both to a degree. Of course, with Isator's point, group dynamics matter for all, because it's a team game.

                                        However, it means something for an Exalt to have Castes, with a combat Caste, or alternatively to have Aspects, with no combat Aspect. If you have a combat Caste, then in combat the theme is that they take point and everyone else follows and supports. With Aspects, a small group of diverse individuals who express part of what it is to be a warrior to the fullest combine district skills. While this can be modified by situation (e.g. the Dawn is inexperienced vs an experienced rest of part, multiple Dawns in a team, different degrees of specialisation into combat by different Dragonblooded), the designs *do* point to different dynamics. Solars Castes do push more to "Buffy + guys who are helping Buffy" and not "Team of equals" so much. Especially in 3e with Supernal Abilities and revamped Caste Animas.

                                        (Sidereals seem a bit are intermediate here, and properly, so are Alchemicals and Infernals. With Lunars you probably tend to have more than one Full Moon on a team, if you're playing a game of five, so...).

                                        I guess I could say this fits with Solar vs Terrestrial mythology - the sun is singular and above, each phase stands alone and dominant in its time, passing over authority to next in their time, the elements combine to be greater than their parts.

                                        There's a different play experience for each. The Caste social structures of the Solars represent only one possible model and perspective of a heroic team.
