                Being an orphan doesn't preclude having friends. In fact, threatening friends is generally a much better motivator than sigh, your mom is in danger again, because if you've made friends with NPCs you've actually put the time in to get to know them a bit. (Note, this goes out the window if the GM actually lets you spend time with your character's mom!)

                Having parents is a premade "I am attached to this NPC" tag, but if the GM doesn't take the time to give it any emotional weight, it's just tiresome!

                It's sort of like threatening Earth in a sci-fi game. Video games especially. It's like yes, I know I'm supposed to care whether or not the Earth is destroyed, but maybe actually let me visit and spend time on Earth one of these days instead of just saving it all the time? (No, running away from it as fast as my legs will carry me does not count!)

                                    A general comment to players: Don't let poor experiences from one ST punish another ST by tying their hands with regards to doing interesting things with your character because you have bad ST PTSD. Or PBSTSD. Post Bad Storyteller Stress Disorder. That includes "defending" a character by making their background sparse or made with a siege mentality. A game takes a group effort, and that includes investment and a reasonable expectation that you trust the ST.

                                    One of the things that helps discourage this kind of chargen behavior is a strong session 0 and a "Justify your dots" mentality as a Storyteller. Having players explaining character dots in a satisfactory in-universe manner often forces people to imply they had training or relationships with others to acquire skills or abilities. I am open to PM's telling me about how I'm a terrible ST for doing this.

                                    Incentive is not permission or justification.
