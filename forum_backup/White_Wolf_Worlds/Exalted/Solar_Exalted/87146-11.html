    I don't think all solars should be heroes.

    As a general rule, any generalization reduces the depth of the characters, the richness of the setting and the expierence of gameplay.

    If every solar is a hero, the options a player have at character creation are reduced, and you automatically know that every solar you meet is one of the good guys.

    Anything that allow you to automatically distinguish good from evil makes a setting more bland and uninteresting.

        Really Solars are heroes like the characters from Watchmen are Heroes.

        That is to say, they explore the various different elements and ideas of what Heroes are. The Hero as the Ubermensch (Ozymandias), the Hero as the God (Dr Manhattan), the Hero as the Everyman (Nite Owl), the Hero as the Legacy (Silk Spectre) and so on. Solars explore what those different various versions of heroism just like Moore's work does. Your Solar does not have to be so archetypical as those characters, but they probably do explore what it means to be Heroic in some way.

                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

                            Ok.... you know that applies to virtually anyone in position of power. Almost no one outside of complete psychopaths thinks of themselves as a villain. If your definition of "Good" includes Stalin and Hitler - than fine, all Solars are generally "good". The word is now completely meaningless.
                            This comes up over and over again. You and almost everyone who disagrees with my stance are under this impression that either "good" means "morally palatable to 21st century mainstream Western sensibilities" or it means nothing at all. In almost every post I've made in this thread, including the one you quoted, I've tried to explain that I see "good" as pertains to this discussion as a really broad, flexible concept.

                            Saying that good is subjective doesn't make it meaningless, because a great and skilled person can be a person who doesn't subscribe to any notion of good at all. You don't have to think of yourself as a villain to be looking out first and foremost for yourself. Think of every person you know. How many of those people are just trying to get by, or succeed, or leave a legacy for their family? How many are motivated by a desire to change the world, and show that motivation through their actions? In my experience, the latter group is rare indeed.

                            Yes, my conception of the Unconquered Sun and the "goodness" he sees in people is that he would totally have made Stalin and Hitler Solars because of their "goodness". He's unable to weigh good against its opportunity cost, so when he looks into their heads and sees that they want to lead their people to world prominence, he cannot fathom that this means they are going to kill millions of people in the process. He just sees a reflection of his own all-encompassing good and goes "yep, there's a worthy Solar".

                            If Solars are myth-heroes, then the interesting thing about their stories is the roll of magical monstrosities that they face and overcome. The story of Odysseus would have been pretty boring if Poseidon hadn't been toying with him for 10 years. Beowulf has no character traits besides the fact that he kills monsters. If your character needs to be subjected to adversity in order to be interesting, is your character actually interesting? I think that kind of character is just a foil.

                            Steve Irwin knew this. He made himself a myth-hero in order to show people the wonders of the natural world, because it's the essence of a myth-hero to be the proxy by which an audience learns about mythos. His shows were about nature, not about him, just the same as the Odyssey was about mythical creatures, rather than about Odysseus. It is the job of a myth-hero to triumph over the supernatural dangers they face, but this doesn't make them any more dramatically interesting unto themselves than an unplugged television. And if there's one constant theme you see in White Wolf games, one setting element that overrides anything else you can say about their games, it's that your character is supposed to be dramatically interesting. That's why I think Solars Are Good: because introducing that thematic constant makes Solars interesting, where otherwise they would be the most boring beings in the setting. Myth-heroes have stories where stuff happens to them. Good guys have stories where they make stuff happen.
