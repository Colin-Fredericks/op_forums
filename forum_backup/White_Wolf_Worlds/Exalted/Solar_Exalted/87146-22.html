    Personally I see Solars as righteous beings, rather than good. Because if Warhammer 40k has taught me anything, it's that 'righteous' does not automatically mean 'good'. More specifically, I think a Solar should embody at least ONE of the virtues, even if not in a particularly good or wholesome manner. Exalt a warrior who drinks and fucks everything she sees because she'll fight to her last breath (and then swing once more for good measure), an assassin with perfect self-control and a love for the thrill of the kill, etc etc. If Sol judged people by his own standards of good, after all, it's doubtful he'd ever exalt anyone.

    That said, Evil McBadguy exalting as a solar should be the exception rather than the rule. Much like the Lunar who lives in the city, or the Abyssal who loves animals, or the Sidereal who thinks fate is bullcrap.

                                        Every Solar you will ever meet is either brave, honorable, caring, or stubborn as a mule.

                                        Some Solars with be two or three of those things. Some rare Solars will be all four. Few of them are what we might call "good," though they might see themselves as heroes.

                                        The Sun will honor your bravery if you are a child who stands up to an armed man to defend an innocent slave, and he will honor your bravery if you ride out alone with a firewand and a whip to singlehandedly put down a slave rebellion. He does not care if you're good, for some modern definition of good. He cares if you are brave.

                                        The Sun will recognize your honor if you fast on a mountaintop and eschew life's temptations in search of enlightenment, only coming down to heal the sick and always refusing their attempts to repay you. The sun will recognize your honor if you give your word to your dark ninja master that you will slay an innocent man, and then you keep your word despite the obstacles in your way, because you have given your oath and it binds you. The Sun does not care if you are good, only that you have mastery over yourself.

                                        The Sun will grant you power if you are caring. When you spare a defeated enemy out of mercy, the Sun is with you. When you surrender instead of fighting, because you have fallen in love with the invading general, and you doom hundreds of your own people to suffering and oppression because you refuse to harm one who you love, the Sun is with you. He cares not the object of your compassion, but he sees his own love for the unworthy reflected in you and recognizes it for a strength, and he will give you the power to fight for it.

                                        The Sun will honor your conviction if you refuse to offer mercy to a murderer or a heretic or a blasphemer, even though they are your own child. The Sun will honor your conviction if you steadfastly refuse to betray your heretic child to the authorities even when starved and tortured. The Sun doesn't care if you're an absolute fanatic or a an unswerving juggernaut of justice: he only cares that you will move the world rather than give an inch.

                                        The Sun chooses great men and women: people who will reforge the world in their own image, people in whom he sees reflected back at him his own image. The Sun's perfection unopposed would scorch the earth and wipe out all life (that's the reason Night was invented), and the wise observer will note that the unimpeded rule of the Solars at their height was not dissimilar. The Solars are glorious and mighty and strong, and yes, some of them are even good. It is not a requirement.

                                            In order to get to the core of what heroic is meant to mean in Exalted's context, let's go back to the basics of what inspired Grabowski:

                                            Dinosaurs Rule The Earth!
                                            / \
                                            / Eastern Epic +
                                            Western Epic Chinese Novels
                                            / \ | \
                                            / Classical | \
                                            Lord Dunsany Histories | Floating World
                                            \ / | Prints
                                            \ / Wuxia |
                                            Pulp Fantasy \ Anime
                                            / \ \ /
                                            Riverworld* Pulp Revival CRPGs
                                            \ | /
                                            \ v /
                                            +-----> Exalted <-----------------+


                                            So, yes, anything from classical Greek heroism to Shonen protagonists are valid. Also remember you can play Exalted in different ways going from 'Grand Theft Yeddim' to more serious styles. This means any individual Exalted campaign is going to be highlighting different themes and might even be in a different genre altogether.

                                            Now, what does this mean? It just means that the characters in your group have to be able to play together in the story created by the ST. There might, or there might not be room for paladins and/or villains in the party.

                                            That said, to focus more on classical Greek heroism vs modern heroism: the sacking of Troy came down to rape and enslave the women, kill the men, salt the earth. This was standard practice. These kind of heroes are obviously not for every table or campaign. Many players are not able to identify with such characters, even when they try. From what I have seen in games, completely abandoning the modern definition of heroism is pretty hard.
                                            In my experience, aiming for the middle ground instead of extremes, namely good guys with flaws or bad guys with redeeming features, seems to work best in the long term.

Exalted and really many RPGs are best when they have a strong thesis and view of what kind of games they are and worlds they emulate. If Exalted wanted to be a catch-all fanatsy game that does the spectrum from gritty shithole 40K to an the old GI Joe cartoon morality, that's one thing. But the game is written an dwill be written with a fairly solid idea of what it wants to be and emulate. Strong vision isntead "Everyone's opinion is valid!" leads to a better written game.

                                                            So in short, no, I won't support the "Exalted can do anything and that's fine!" thing, becuase that' smore or less to me the, "Everyone's opinion is valid and special." thing. I would rather have an ability to call out an idea as patently bad than be on self-appointed feel good high horse.
