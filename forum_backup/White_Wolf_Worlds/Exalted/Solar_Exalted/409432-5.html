        * You only want to kill Wyld creatures, and other outsiders that threaten the Creation you're comfortable with.

                                    I do think the Path of Enlightenment should totally be a supported thing for the Abyssals, separate from the Path of Redemption. I can see where some Abyssal players would want to play the Enlightenment thing without "But Your Existence Inevitably Pushes Creation Into Oblivion's Maw!" hanging over them. Can Abyssals then be Exalted of death and the Underworld, instead of Exalted of Oblivion? If Abyssals and Ghosts weren't inimical to Creation by their very existence that would actually reduce the grimdark, Creation Is Doomed feeling that I always got from 2E -- a possibility I hadn't thought about before.

                                    Originally posted by Couch View Post
                                    As has been noted, it would help enormously if going from Abyssal to Solar wasn't a straight mechanical upgrade. Like, shedding your cursed power is less poignant if it's to become a god-king of everything with all the same powers you had before only better.

                                    If shedding your Abyssal status caused you to become a mortal, but the displays of heroism required to do such a thing were so grand that such a former Abyssal was exceedingly likely to then become a Solar the next time that they were in a situation where they might potentially be chosen, that would be much more resonant with these sorts of character arcs.
                                    If I were a ST, I would make Redemption a matter of reconciliation with Sol Invictus for sinning/blaspheming against him (accepting a stolen Exaltation, doing things he considers morally wrong, or that the character considers morally wrong, helping Oblivion) and wanting to reject death in favor of being an Exalt of Creation and life. That doesn't mean that their actions were morally wrong according to the player or ST's beliefs, let alone any objective measurement. It would be the choice of a character who does not want to be Abyssal, who decides being one was either wrong or just not what they want to be anymore, and tries to convince Sol, personally, to forgive them for the things that he sees as wrong in Abyssals, and adopt them as his Chosen.

                                    And if that Abyssal does become a Solar, yeah they lose whatever (hopefully less punitive) mechanic replaces Resonance, and they no longer have the Great Curse, which means their story is also a story of potentially redeeming the Solar Exalted.

                                    But the Sun also demands sacrifice, and the nature of turning their exaltation inside out forces sacrifice. Depending on the character arc and the circumstances leading up to the change, they might die, or lose a limb, or lose all their artifacts. At the very least, they are now almost a newly Exalted Solar. Their connection to the Underworld is washed away -- Essence back town to 2. Their powers of death are erased -- they lose all charms except up to 10 excellencies (or just 10 starting Solar charms if excellencies aren't a thing in 3E). Maybe they keep sorcerous initiations, but can't use them until they build their Solar Essence up to the pre-requisites. They lose all attunements to soulsteel artifacts and Abyssal manses. Their Abyssal heartstones shatter. Some of their Backgrounds are taken away. They aren't quite starting over, because they keep their Attributes, Abilities, Virtues, and some types of merits and artifacts. But they still lost a lot.
