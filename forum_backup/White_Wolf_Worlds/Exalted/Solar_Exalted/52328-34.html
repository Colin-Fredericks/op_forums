            Originally posted by Dayen View Post
            I'll shrug mightily at that. You're talking about men who had enough support to gainsay the Temple in Jerusalem and get away with it (with some hiccups for the ones who resisted the duty to prophesy) and continue live well, even if someone else wore the crown. Who are we to say they're not kings?
            That's because they usually didn't. 'Prophet' was a job that one could be trained in, and usually one 'qualified' for the training in the same way that one qualified for most jobs in those days: by being the son of someone with that job. There were exceptions to this; Amos, for example, explicitly stated that he was not the son of a prophet, but that God had chosen him to deliver the message, when a priest called him out for prophesying against Beit-El.

            Usually, though, they worked more or less alongside the priests, as the other half of the line of communication with God. The priests managed the Temple and the sacrifices, sending the people's prayers to God, so to speak, while the prophets told the people what God wanted them to do.


            Also, unpopular prophets were sometimes jailed or kicked out of cities for unpopular prophecies, and there is a story in the Book of Kings where Obadiah saved a group of one hundred prophets (out of a much larger number) from being killed, so they often weren't able to defy the crown.


            My point is, prophets had a designated place in that society, and often weren't standing outside speaking inward. That said, the times when that did happen are the times that are better remembered, and these are probably the one that Zeniths are meant to reflect. Elijah, for example.

            If you want to play Thomas Edison, you'd better be up to the task of carrying all of civilization through the Renaissance and Industrial Revolution, while the Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded are shooting at you.

                                Putting emphasis on the punching is also misleading: Zenith do have social Charms. The point is that when they intercede with the gods, they don't do so as supplicants. The relationship between the mortal priest and the god is inherently unequal because the god is always more personally powerful than the mortal, even if we're talking about a situation where the mortal has enough political power to get concessions out of the god. A Zenith, however, is mostly going to be equal or superior to any gods he intercedes with on behalf of mortals. Even if he uses diplomacy, the fact that he COULD punch the god changes the relationship.
                                And did you read the passages earlier in the thread that comment that all gods, from lowly Terrestrial terrain features all the way to Celestial powerhouses like Ahlat, should consider the help of a Zenith a boon because they are very good at leading the people in prayer and inspiring them to worship?

                                Nobody is saying that the Zenith "core concept" is to punch or, at best, bully every god he comes across. You are totally free to be nice to all gods ever and not punch them. Nothing in my previous posts is meant to say that Zeniths cannot speak with the gods on the behalf of the people. It IS meant to say that a Zenith has an ability to sway the masses to the god or away from the god that no mortal has. When it is positive, the relation between the god and the Zenith will tend to be that of allies, with the understanding that both can feasibly harm or help the other, rather than boss and subordinate.

                                I would also note that single-handedly building a nation and then offering its praises to Ahlat, while it is priestly as you yourself comment, is definitely NOT what a normal mortal priest from Creation does. Heck, when a mortal achieves that, then bring on the Priest-King title. They're still not going to have the same sway with gods as a Zenith, but the dude is badass. They earned it.
