        Hm.
        So, I like making religion a thing in my games, that seperates or unites culture groups etc. Almost more than politics. So a few thoughts.
        Zeniths at the end bc they make things a tad complicated.

        1) Most gods worshipped probably aren't creator gods. Cosmogny is important in world religions but...Well, Zeus didn't make the world. Amaterasu to my understanding didn't either. Neither did Thor (Okay, Odin was chief god there, but 'made the world out of a dead dude' isn't high on his deeds), etc. Most gods worshiped are for either their daily involvement and actions. More common might be gods who established social orders and customs among people. Lawgivers, if you will.
        This is why Hearth gods are weirdly important, even if they do less asskicking compared to the big names. They protect and govern the home, which often reflects the cosmos. Hestia doesn't do much, but she doesn't need to. She's in charge of domestic affairs.
        And as others have said, most faiths, regardless of size, easily adapt to incorporate other gods into them in some form. Even 'monotheists' have the demon/saint label they can place on things. The bigger trick with Monotheistic Creator God is the whole Almighty part. That gets tricky when you get beaten up. But again, that's not exactly a common god type.

        2)Cutlural Christian is probably a thing. in the Ancient times it was as well. People knew ritual had value on it's own, regardless of reality. A bigger point however is that there isn't a religion so widespread as Christianity or Islam except the Immaculate Order. So Religion and political/ethnic group will probably get blended to some degree.
        Not to say national variations on those religions aren't a thing, but not typically as blatant and tend to get invovled in that Saints buisness.

        3)Yes? I mean, Creations landscape of religion probably varies greatly, and might not be as apparently clean cut as sometimes real world religions are. Gods migth move, but then their context will probably change as well. Indra's role in Hinduism, from what I've read, varies a bit depending on the time and text. A more concrete example might be how the Roman and Greeks in popular conception share deities but change the roles to better reflect certain values of their culture (you could argue this about all Indo-European affairs, but my knowledge gets muddy).

        Zeniths throw a giant wrench into this because they see the King of the Gods. And he talks to them. ...Which I like.
        What a Zenith takes away from that experience probably depends on their standing and beliefs. After all, my shaman saw the UCS as the elephant god Hathi he had worshiped for years (the UCS just had elephant heads). The message UCS gave also will shape that, since unless it invalidates existing beliefs no reason to bother. It gets tricky if your original god claimed to be supreme...but then most gods who claimed to be supreme will also probably try to claim the vision was from them (it's short and you don't get regular contact after all).

                    Well, my view on the matter is this: most people, influenced by the Cult of the Immaculate Dragons, know about Anathema, i.e. people possessed by evil spirits. They've always identified them primarily with Lunars, but now that the Solars are back of course they're Anathema too according to the Realm, and the Realm can be persuasive and culturally very wide-reaching. So people will often know to run away and seek an Immaculate Monk or Dragon-Blood as soon as they meet anyone starting to give off silver or golden light or displaying some strange mark on their brow. Of course the fact that the Abyssals have visible animas and caste marks as well doesn't help the Solars and Lunars, because the Abyssals actually are usually hostile to Creation.

                    So people are very confused. But they mostly fear the Celestial Exalted. Not many know that the Solars are the Chosen of the Unconquered Sun. It doesn't help that the Unconquered Sun is not doing his job - he's completely absorbed in the Games of Divinity and only a substitutive bureau of minor Celestial gods is charged with answering prayers directed to him. They try to, but these "letters to Santa" are legion, and sometimes they give off a random miracle without reading them, or smite the worshippers to discourage further praying (and further headaches for them).

                    The Unconquered Sun is rarely worshipped, as a result, and his temples are mostly ruins (when they're not imperishable manses). Common people are more likely to pray to the god of the nearby river, to the gods of the fields they work in, to the god of the road they use most, to the god of the village/town they reside in. Sometimes they hold sacred hunts in Luna's honor (she's not as completely absent as Sol, but then again, she's awesome like that), but they mostly worship the small gods ruling in the area they live in, and some celestial gods presiding over matters currently important to them (a Southern cattle raider or soldier prays to Alaht, a slaver to Rabszolga, and so on). Then there are the little gods you wish to avoid, like the sirens, who try to have sex with you and/or eat you. The "sex" part is not usually the problem, you see.

                    That's it more or less regarding the gods. Also, people know they can be morally bankrupt and favorably disposed towards bribes, but they're gods, so the best course of action is to acquiesce to their demands and perhaps hope a hero passes by and teaches them a lesson. If that hero is an Anathema, well, they helped, so they can't be all that bad, right?

                                                    1). Well the big thing to keep in mind is that the closest thing to the relationship the people of creation have with their gods that we see IRL is the shinto-model where they're closer to local lords than ultimate authorities on anything. One of the defining religions of Creation boils down to 'Don't worship the gods too much, it gives them ideas above their station.'

                                                    The vast majority of gods are gods of things like the local river or such, and rare is the spirit willing to claim power on the level of the Incarnae even as a bluff or a prank. Most exalted with a bit of occult study are more than capable of dealing with uppity gods.

                                                    In fact look at the ability list, there's no knowledge religion like in some other games, because gods aren't special, they fall under occult like ghosts, elementals, and other spirits. They have desires, follies, and the vast majority aren't all that powerful, especially outside their specific domains.

                                                    So it's not even a solar thing really, most people aren't going to go praying to a god for world peace, because that's outside the wheel house of all but the top tier divinities who haven't been really active recently.

                                                    Conversely Atheism is the wrong word. Atheism implies a disbelief in divinities, which in creation is probably a lot like not believing in Skitter if you live in brockton bay.

                                                    All that said exaltation, especially once you pick up the ability to find and affect dematerialized spirits, alters the power balance between a person and the gods, it's not that the person believes in gods any less, it's that they're now the more powerful party and the gods can't pull that disappearing trick they do to hide from people against you. Furthermore the exalt is empowered with their own divine powers and are thus able to perform feats of their own, in many cases an exalt can quickly grow to be better at whatever service the local gods provide, and alter the relationship between the humans and the local divinities.

                                                    So it's not that Solars become atheistic, it's that in a world with a fairly mercenary take on the relationship between mortals and gods, Solars are the nations with well trained citizen armies.

                                                    2.) Probably, in fact you'll probably find most people are more than willing to celebrate whatever local festival is happening.
