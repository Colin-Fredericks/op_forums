Since the Scarlett Empress disappeared it seems like the suicide squad got abandoned.


        The GM has a very cruel side sometimes. Same happened to the family of a different character and player. He left because he exalted and wanted to protect them by not being there. They got murdered and tormented from an outcast of the realm.
        The whole village of my last character fell victim to the army of Mask Of Winters while my character was out far in the west adventuring in the name of Fakharu.
        The next character was kicked out of his tribe because of his Exaltation, because I didn't want to deal with this again.
        My first Vampire character got his new life only through the fall and slaughtering of his whole noble house that got eradicated through the intrigues of a different family. He was the last one left alive. (All of it was planned from my former GM)


        So I can understand why people don't want to have families ingame. Either they are cruel and antagonists that nobody really wants to have, or they get cruely murdered anyway without the chance of doing anything to prevent it. Revenge in one form or the other is pretty much the only story I ever experienced being told with the family in the center.
        That pretty much sums up my experience with families in RPGs.


        Personally I don't like this at all. I feel like it is a hassel. Important play time that is not wasted but that could be spent better on so many other things.
        The group I'm GMing in would never have to deal with stuff like this except when the players ask for it. Even when they fuck up ingame I don't just let the family get slaughtered. Even if they are antagonists they are so while being torn apart on the inside or having other reasons than hate and madness. Also family does not need to be rescued every freaking time. They are actually allowed to not die and be awesome themself! It's not like some people wouldn't be motivated to be a better version of themself if a Solar pops up within their bloodline.

                    I've been gaming for 25ish years. I've had more family & friend based plots than I care to count and they are all based on the same thing. An essentially random NPC gets railroaded into a dangerous situation and I have to save or avenge them. The problem is, unless this is an NPC that has been brought to life by the GM through serious roleplaying, my character cares but I don't anymore. If the GM brought him/her to life, its no longer an NPC from my backstory and indistinguishable from any other major NPC. The only seperator is that I wrote a few lines of rough description at some point, the rest is the GM.

                    Frankly, I don't get what the big deal is. Background ties are a very hand-wavey thing. Without the GM investing a serious amount of work into it, they are basically a McGuffin. Either the GM invests into shaping the rough outline of the player or he shapes his own NPC. I don't see much of a difference here.

                    Personally, I think the time spend shoehorning in family against the player's wishes could be spend developing "normal" NPC ties with the added bonus of allowing the player a choice to care.

                    Originally posted by ParanoiaCombo View Post
                    A general comment to players: Don't let poor experiences from one ST punish another ST by tying their hands with regards to doing interesting things with your character because you have bad ST PTSD. Or PBSTSD. Post Bad Storyteller Stress Disorder. That includes "defending" a character by making their background sparse or made with a siege mentality. A game takes a group effort, and that includes investment and a reasonable expectation that you trust the ST.
                    Statements like these make me angry to be honest. Player's don't "punish" the new GM. They try to avoid a situation in which they are uncomfortably emotionally involved during their free time. They want their fun to be fun, not distressing.

                    Trust is a fragile thing that needs to be earned. Dealing with emotionally distressing situations in RPGs is not a given. Its something that players and GM need to be okay with. A player that tries to "defend" himself, as you put it, against that is someone whose trust you need to earn by showing him that you won't abuse his emotional ties in horrible ways. Asking a scarred player to just forget what made him not have fun and making himself vulnerable all over again, with nothing but your word to go on, is a bad way to do that.

                    Players open up to well crafted NPCs. It's a natural process. If they don't, its because they don't want to. Personally, I think the question why is much more interessting than blanket blaming the player.

                                As someone who has had dick GMs before, and also as someone who has replaced a dick GM, the road to recovery isn't as long as it sounds.

                                Step 1. As the new GM, you talk to the players about what it is about past games that you feel were lacking, overly antagonistic, or otherwise bad for the experience, and promises to avoid these things.

                                Step 2. Ask players to inform you of how they envision their character's story progressing, and work with them to integrate that. Specifically mention that if there's any aspect that they'd like to pursue, but didn't feel comfortable in previous games, for whatever reason, such as family ties, you would honor that and integrate it POSITIVELY into the plot as much as you're able. Rescue and/or revenge plots are viable, but only if specifically requested by the relevant player. Possibly offer additional character resources, like Solar XP, for receipt of the idea. Not when you get around to addressing it in-game, because that means the currently featured character is getting both game focus and character sheet rewards that the other characters currently aren't.

                                Step 3. Take what you're offered. Do what you promised.

                                Step 4. Watch as players respond to positive reinforcement.

                                            Kidnapping was a bad example. Here are some other examples:

                                            > PC comes home and finds her family harassed by bandits/mob/etc. PC takes care of it.
                                            > PC comes home and finds that her family business is failing. Her mother is getting old an senile. PC works with estranged sibling to make peace between mother and sibling.
                                            > PC hears about father's death. Comes home to participate in funeral rituals. Deals with family drama and may find some problem in his home town to fix.
                                            > PC's sister made a deal with some bad people. PC has to help her get out of it.
                                            > PC's brother is trying to start up a new business. PC helps out in getting things off the ground.

                                            Two things I should note:

                                            1) I don't force players to make characters with family. Not all role players want to deal with family drama. I do like my characters to at least think about their childhood and have some connection grounding them to the setting, but that can be beliefs/organizations/etc.

                                            2) I always talk such plot over with the player before hand. I'm not going to drop family drama on the player out of the blue. I have GMs drop family plot on me out of the blue and that is not fun ("Wow, thanks for creating a rapist father for my lost cub werewolf character without consulting me"). I'm going to be "okay, you are visiting your character's home town. What would you like to do there?" "I want my character to deal with his father's funeral" "Okay, I can work with that" I also am not going to kill off family members unless the player agrees to it. Anyways killing off intimacies destroys the chance for future story lines.

                                            I like including slice of life scenes in Exalted to keep these god like character grounded and human. Yet at the same time, not all role players want to deal with slice of life scenes, which is perfectly fine too.

                                            My old Exalted game like them. We used to do funny training montages even though we didn't need to do them. Our Night Caste was doing resistance training and his teacher poisoned him. The Dusk and I made fun of him as he was puking in the bucket.
