    The D&D contrast holds for people, but alignment is both descriptive amd proscriptive, as far as actions go. There are races of creature that are "born evil" (things like demons, many types of undead, etc.), and a particular individual *not* being evil is on the order of an Exalted demon not being a creature of darkness.

    So I guess what I mean is that the difference between the two concepts is that a non-naturally evil character can "opt in" to being evil with their actions, but a non-naturally CoD can't. A naturally CoD character definitely needs magical assistance to "opt out"; there are gradations of difficulty for the naturally-evil character doing the same, but it seems like as often as not such outliers are the result of freak mutation at their creation rather than any change the character precipitated by their own actions, which interestingly with CoDs happens the other direction (e.g. Five Days Darkness being born a CoD even though he's a god).

            Off topic, but I like to point out the absurdity of D+D style alignment with the following thought experiment :

            Two people are in a room with you. You must choose one to be pushed into an incinerator.

            You are not given the option of not choosing. Your Gods are watching and judging you. This is the most important event in your reality. Armageddon.

            One of these two people is the most virtuous and noble individual who has ever existed. The other is basically Hitler and Satan's baby.

            You do not get to know which is which... who do you choose?

            After this choice, how is your alignment changed?

            Now, the thing to understand about why D+D alignment is absurd is... every single situation in the real world is more or less like that. 'Good' and 'Evil' are not at all clearcut, often matters of interpretation and perspective, and hopelessly intermingled in an endless web of context that's sorta impossible to see more than a few layers into.

                                        A large amount of word count goes into what Good and Evil mean in the D&D cosmology precisely because they do not entirely, match with our concepts of good and evil. For example, we consider the killing of intelligent beings to be no better than non-evil, and that only in very restricted circumstances, but in D&D, it's clear that killing evil-aligned beings is not an evil act. How can we possibly know that? We can know it because paladins exist. If killing intelligent beings, regardless of their alignment, were inherently evil, paladins could not exist. But they do, so clearly it not an evil act to kill evil intelligent beings. That's the part a lot of people refuse to accept: in the default D&D cosmology, Good and Evil do have objective meanings. They're not perspective based. A man who detonates the equivalent of a suicide bomb in the middle of a good-aligned village is not a holy warrior striking a brave blow against the evil enemies of his righteous faith. He's a mass murderer who has committed an act of evil. Period. End of sentence. The people who conceive the plan are evil, the people who assist him in executing it are evil. Period. End of sentence. At the best, they are of Neutral alignment and sliding fast toward Evil. A good-aligned person would not do such thing. Good and Evil in D&D mean things. A person who does good things is a Good person, or at least heading that way, and contrariwise for evil.
