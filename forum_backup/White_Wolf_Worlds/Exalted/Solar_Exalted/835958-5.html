    Originally posted by danelsan View Post
    I confess I never understood this "give mortals less dots to distribute, but more bonus points" that has existed since way back in the storyteller games.
    I think the idea is to encourage anime tropes with the mechanics for Exalts. In anime, characters often have that "one thing" they do better than anyone alive while being laughably incompetent at everything else, re: the super tech genius who can't cook, the martial artist who gets lost for days in cities, the stealth master who never speaks because they can't carry on a conversation, etc. The rules for generating exalts encourage these tropes by heavily favoring min-maxing, but maybe they want mortals to seem more "real" by making all skills and attributes about equally difficult to level. It still benefits you to max your stats out at char gen, but at two BP an ability dot players are more willing to experiment with stuff like this as well.

                                So we had to wrap up before finishing the combat tonight, but here is what I pitched at my Dawn and here is what happened:

                                I built a Full Moon Lunar soak-health beast hoping to create something that would at least last a couple rounds with him. He has better soak than Octavian: 11 from artifact heavy armor, 5 from Stamina, and +4 for his war form, for a total soak of 20 which is what my Dawn has. He has 28 Health levels, 14 of which are -4s. He wields a Dire Lance (Heavy Artifact, Piercing, Reaching) and rolls eleven dice to hit, with +23 raw damage (another +4 for its war form). His Parry is five. He has all Physical attributes and melee/brawl at five. He rides a stat-boosted Hellboar, which is also his totem animal, and gains all of its creature powers in war form. I gave him several of Octavian's powers for good measure but halved their mote cost to account for his smaller mote pool.

                                Here is what happened:

                                My Dawn got 22 on his join battle roll to my Lunar's 18. My Dawn rides an upgraded Simhatta (son of the Simhatta god) and they both rushed each other and clashed on round one. Both are using the charm Seasoned Beast-Rider's Approach and roll their mounts into combat on their tick. My Lunar gets 18 to hit on the clash but my Dawn rolls 20+, then does 14 withering damage. The Simhatta also wins the clash and does substantial withering damage, but does not crash the Hellboar. The Lunar and Hellboar continue charging past the Dawn, aiming for the nearby town of soft targets. So the Simhatta gives chase and uses a willpower to reflexively attack again with decisive damage, killing the Hellboar in a single strike. My Dawn then activated Peony Blossom Attack, striking at the Lunar for the second time that turn. The Lunar attempted to react with Invincible Iron Bastion and a full excellency, but Parry 13 did not defend it from an attack with 21 successes. The Dawn did a total of 14 decisive damage, putting the Lunar just barely into its -4s in round one. However, my Lunar activated The Tower Still Stands, putting him back at 16 initiative. Both fighters are now unmounted, as the Solar lept off his mount to deliver that blow (SBRA allows you to do this).

                                And that's where we ended. Here are my most important take aways:

                                1: Never underestimate the benefits of a reflexive decisive attack. With a good Join Battle Roll and a good Withering hit you can get all the Initiative you need to kill most things in this game, and being able to follow that with a Decisive attack on the same turn before losing any of it to withering ripostes is a big deal.

                                2: Defense alone will not save you from charm-users. I had a really big boost going to my Lunar's Parry, more than any excellency could give, and it was still not nearly enough to combat an attack with a full excellency and Excellent Strike. Honestly I feel like the motes I spent boosting his Parry were just wasted. 7+ Defense might save you from most things but don't expect it to hold up against a combat specialist.

                                3: Those extra levels from Ox-Body are so worth it. Obviously this is an extreme example, but if I'm able to turn this fight around at all next game it will only be because my Lunar survived a hit that should have killed them and now has enough initiative to counter attack a Solar who has only seven health levels, which brings me to my last point:

                                4: Having another way to build initiative in a fight is indeed a must. It was suggested earlier here and I will testify, if not for The Tower Still Stands my Lunar would still have less initiative than my Dawn and surviving that first hit would just mean dragging this fight out for another round. Hopefully I can crash my Dawn next game, and then we'll see how effectively a well-made fighter can come back from crash.

                                There are many things this Lunar has not used yet that I am looking forward to testing in their fight in two weeks. Not least of which is the fact that he actually functions better at his -4 health levels, but no more on that here on a thread my players might be reading.

                                                Are you playing on Roll20? Or using any other online die rolling API like an IRC bot? If so, I think your party dawn is rolling like this: Xd10<7; instead of this, which is how he should be rolling: Xd10>7. The die rolls you're claiming are too incredible to be a coincidence, and an inverted greater than sign easily accounts for it, especially if you're counting 10s manually on top of that. The sorts of success totals that you're claiming on pretty much every roll are tremendously unlikely when playing normally. I've only seen numbers like that very rarely over the course of multiple games of Exalted 3e.
