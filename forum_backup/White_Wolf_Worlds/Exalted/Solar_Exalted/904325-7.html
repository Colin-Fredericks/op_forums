        All right, so having looked back over the Dodge Charms myself and seeing that the one being used as an example of a cap-booster is the very first Charm, I see how the term is being applied, and I believe I can refine my point.

        ​So, I see three points of comparison between a cap-booster for Evasion and one for Resolve.

        1) Evasion is used in combat, meaning that matters of efficiency in spending even more motes are partially offset by the constant mote drip; combat in general can be easier on Essence concerns, since it's a high stakes scenario that encourages big investments for big returns.

        2) The effects of being hit with something that you would Evade can be a lot more granular and dramatic than social influence or Psyche effects, which tend to be mechanically binary and easier to recover from. You'll more likely have a scenario in which an opposing dice pool is high enough, which can create a more severe effect upon success, that increasing the difficulty of that dice roll will feel a lot more warranted.

        3) While Dodge Charms help add a lot more nuance to the gameplay of evading in combat, the actual act of dodging itself is not very narratively invested.

        That last one is something I see as particularly pertinent, looking at the Integrity Charms we got, and indeed, the Psyche Charms we got, because I find that the thing they were often going for there was to make the process of preserving your will or shaking of mind control more a series of actions or relationships, rather than always boiling down to just spending something to unilaterally shut something down.

        So even if you have no Integrity Charms, something like Memory-Reweaving Discipline has an in-built story process for somebody shaking off having their memories altered, and if they're using the Integrity Charms designed for shaking off such effects, those also portray an action or process to doing so based on things like who the character is or what they have seen, rather than it just being switched off.

        That and how Psyche effects are less common than their prior Editions' counterparts and are, if not narrow, then at least intricate in their procedures, so having your mind affected can rarely be character destroying. Even if one was going to present a power that did control more thoroughly, I would say that the precedents given show that they should be designed to include interesting plot hooks around their methods of resistance.

        I mean, that was all something that I didn't like in Second Edition; you come up with all kinds of interesting powers to affect the mind, and the only necessary response to them is to spend one to three Willpower. It was worse then cheap perfect defences, because even if those were terrible for cutting the teeth from your cool attacks, at least the only things attacks could do was kill you; mind powers create entire new courses of plot, except they don't, because the method of stopping them is boring and people aren't likely to let them play out for its relative cheapness.

        This is also why I like how they made responses to things like deleterious transformations and curses into potentially useful new traits or ongoing plot hooks.

        And it's all packaged in the context of how social actions or Psyche effects are most likely taking place in low-stress, low-stakes environments, which not only shapes concerns over their success, but also the frequency with which they might be deployed and concerns over how draining it is to resist them. Even if, on occasions where you absolutely, positively don't want a social action or Psyche effect to so much as succeed, your natural Resolve cap will probably be good enough anyway.

        Really, to expand on the point about the narrative investment of Dodging, that's another thing that shows how the Abilities are operating in different contexts that suggest different Charms; the narrative process of surviving through a fight consists of all of the actions you take during the fight, and all of the ways that opposing actions constrain and move you even when they don't succeed. No attack will hit you if you use Seven Shadow Evasion, but the conditions of that Charm will then shape the next several rounds of the fight. Things that are not fights are not so intricate, so other avenues have to be taken to make action and reaction into an interesting story.

                    Originally posted by Darkuwa View Post
                    Uhm... no. A cap breaker is something that allows you to increase your dice by more than the maximum of ability + attribute you are normally allowed. Non-charm dice yes, double X no.
                    I use "cap-breaker" to refer to doubles, rerolls, subtracting enemy successes, and everything else which gives you a better chance of victory than a full Excellency.

                    This is because I am a dreary person who sees dice as probability.

                    I know it's a bit controversial, and so I've hesitated to mention it, but the game does conspiciously lack Medicine Charms for helping more than just the patient in front of you. No Charms for devising cures, recommending an efficacious program of health and exercise, making the area around you especially hostile to diseases, etc. Yes, this is deliberate. But coming from a splat full of Old Testament references who can nourish followers with their ideals alone, lead prayers which keep the raw tides of Chaos at bay, and and train crowds of laypeople into world-class experts in weeks, it seems really odd to be so stymied by germs*. Helping others and applying personal efforts across implausible scales are core Solar themes, right? Certainly, it seems odd to me that the best Solar Charm for fighting a plague is Favor-Conferring Prana (to set up multiple doctors each capable of providing treatments, since the Ultimate Solar Doctor can still only treat twenty people per day, tops).
