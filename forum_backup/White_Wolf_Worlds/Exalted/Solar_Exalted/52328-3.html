        So, here's what I want Performance to be able to do. I understand not all of this will necessarily be reasonable or doable; I don't know which of these things might already have been allocated to something else. But these are the various things I might want.

        1. Damage. I want to be able to play the lap harp to cut people up like a scene out of Kung Fu Hustle. I want to be able to shout so hard and loud that I can parry attacks with it. I want to be able to dance a crater into the ground. I want to be able to issue a prayer to the Unconquered Sun to mess up creatures of darkness in my presence, like a priest stopping a vampire in its tracks with a rosary and a word to the Holy Ghost.

        Time and time again in fiction, you see - admittedly over-the-top - things like this. Dance-warriors, musical artillery, and prayers as weapons. There are already examples of this in 2e - Vibrating Strings Defense and Three-String Sword Prana for Dragonblooded, for example, or Withering Phantasmagoria for Abyssals. I would just really like if, more than just a fringe option, it was a solid, viable choice for cinematic action in play.

        2. Buffs and debuffs. 1e/2e already did this decently, I just want to make sure this is still a thing. If 'supernaturally perfect music that invigorates and improves allies' is something D&D does better with a bard than Exalted does with a Zenith, I'll be wildly disappointed. I'm not expecting to be disappointed on this one, I just want to hedge my bets by saying how important it is to me.

        3. Large scale social actions. 2e always struck me as odd in that on the mundane side great effort was gone to in order to explain that Presence was mostly for direct, one-on-one interaction and Performance for social area attacks, oratory, and group persuasion, and then many of Presence's charms affected groups, to the point that going with Performance over Presence largely ended up a matter of fluff and an extra long tick per action. I want Presence and Performance to each have their own extremely distinct niches - Presence is for when you're talking to the king, and Performance for when you're orating to the king's court. I feel like if that distinction isn't protected, one or both lose their special mechanical and intellectual space in actual play.

        If you give me 3/3 I'll be ecstatic. If you give me 2/3, I'll be pleased, my expectations and desires well-met. If you give me 1/3, I'll be disappointed. If I don't get any of the above, I'll be, to put it lightly, shocked, and more than a little confused. If you want any clarification, details, or anything else, feel free to ask.

            I'd like to throw my voice into the chorus of "Please make it feel different from other overlapping abilities". This is something I feel 2E Performance failed to do meaningfully.

            In 2E the skill felt it should be called "Arts (but not speeches, that's Presence, and not writing, that's Linguistics, and not disguise, that's Larceny, and not making priceless works of art, that's Craft).. I have no idea where the conceptual space begins and ends, and while some overlap is OK (I mean, we have Melee, and Martial Arts with mundane form weapons, so it's not like this isn't unprecedented), right now it's just confusing.

            I really feel like in order to hash out the charm design, you need to know what the ability means, ESPECIALLY for Solars, who's Charm Paradigm is in doing things that are the pinnacle of human endeavor breached through into the supernatural realm... so in order to know what the, say, Athletics equivalent of "Jump so much it's almost like flying (but you aren't flying because that thematic space isn't Solar-ish) is for Performance, you have to define what it is much more strongly. I understand Legacy Issues on ability names is a thing, but this isn't as much a naming issue s "really, what does this do?".

            I apologize if I've overstated my point, but this one is a big deal and has been for me ever since I've started playing Exalted.
