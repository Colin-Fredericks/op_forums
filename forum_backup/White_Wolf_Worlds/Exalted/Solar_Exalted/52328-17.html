                    Originally posted by John Mørke View Post

                    A Zenith who wants to be a gardener is also going to be really good at it, but there aren't any Charms specifically for that either. There's a big difference between having a Charm designed directly into the set for a certain, stated, specific task, and having one that supplements it indirectly. A specific Charm colors the character of the entire splat, where an indirect Charm allows for players to individualize their characters and set them apart from everyone else. Traditionally, you can use your Performance Charms to pray, but there aren't any specifically for prayer.
                    That's a little bit disappointing to me, and sort of make me wonders what the theme of the Zenith caste is supposed to be in this new edition. In 2e, one of the central schicks of the Zenith was that, as an official priest of everything, all prayer rolls they made were at -1 difficulty. That's not a huge bonus or anything, but it's something apparently central enough to the concept to be included as an anima effect for their entire caste. It means that Zeniths are supposed to be doing specifically that - praying or, when they have a congregation, leading prayer - aside from their other assorted responsibilities, like corpse disposal and messing up Creatures of Darkness.

                    2e failed to add charms that tied in to that apparently-central-to-the-theme role. They made their Zeniths priests with an actual mechanical benefit when it came to prayer - whose whole roll is to get a god to pay attention and mete out some kind of boon - and then didn't really give anything to help that along other than an excellency. They could have given charms that give a god a bonus when they fulfill that prayer, or a penalty when they fail to, or otherwise help convince a god to help, reward it when it does, punish it when it doesn't, and facilitate its effects coming into existence. As the priests of the Solar Exalts, it seems like authoritative management of deities would totally be their thing, and I feel like 2e not including charms for that is a failing, not a success.

                    So, I suppose my question is, what are the Zeniths' role in 3e if they weren't the priest-kings of previous editions? If they can't use prayer to get gods to pay attention and hand out goodies, can't pray to turn aside creatures of darkness, are you going to be completely restructuring the themes and anima effects of Zeniths to make them musicians and statesmen instead of priests? Because otherwise, it seems like there's going to be that empty design space again, despite Zeniths being primed for it already.

                    Even if it's just a single short branch of prayer charms on the Performance tree, it seems like there's everything to gain for including it, and like there would definitely be a sense of something missing for a lack of it.

                    Unless, of course, prayer is just included in the social influence system that's been hinted at. If prayer is playing on intimacies, if prayer is affected by all the other performance charms that convince someone of something, if performance already includes systems for attracting immediate attention, rewarding those who follow your requests and orders, and penalizing those who refuse. If, in 3e, prayer is just an avenue of otherwise-normal social influencing - like sending a letter or calling someone on the phone, social influence but at a range - then I could see prayer just using the charms that Performance already has to get gods to do what you want.

                    But if that's not the case, then I really do wonder how Zeniths are supposed to fill their primary role as priests. They were better musicians than priests in 2e, and I find that, to put it lightly, odd. So, I'm hoping either prayer will be better facilitated in 3e or for the central theme of the Zenith to be changed to something else.

                    That's all, really.

                                                        Originally posted by Scutarii View Post


                                                        Question then - for the uninitiated in the ways of Exalted - what does 'being a priest' mean in terms of actions performed day to day, in times of war, in times of celebration? What are these important things that priests do? How are these distinct from the actions of, for example, a revolutionary leader riding on charisma and rhetoric skill?

                                                        What makes the Zenith approach different to the approach of a charismatic Dawn?

                                                        Or, reversed, why would a charismatic community leader exat as a Zenith rather than a charismatic member of any other caste?
                                                        The gods want priests who will organize continuous mass-worship and exorbitant and repeated sacrifice. Performance helps with this, because being able to dance, sing, play instruments or orate helps you to identify yourself with a higher power. Such behavior inspires awe, and creates a sense of worship. Effective priests not only lead prayer through worship and ritual, but are also part of the temple bureaucracy and are relatively wealthy, based on the size of the temple, size of the cult, dominance of the religion, etc. A god won't tolerate an ineffective priest. He wants someone that will inspire the people to worship and will manage funds and use resources to perform acts in his name, to keep the people worshiping and sacrificing to him. Having high Performance is a great bonus, but if the god is particularly powerful, entrenched or prominent, such as Ahlat, or if people are particularly afraid (such as in outlands regions where shaman-exorcists make a living), Performance is less necessary, and Bureaucracy and Occult are more effective talents for a priest. Siakal's priests may or may not have Performance, but every last one of them has War and Sail.

                                                        At best, an effective priest organizes sacrifice and worship and uses this to bargain with the god more successfully. At worst, the priest is a lackey whose temporal power allows him to sign off on public works, military action, etc. in the name of the god, and who is supported by the god so long as he keeps the sacrifices coming.

                                                        When Buddha Elvis rolls into town with his glorious Solar sanxian, every respectable god is either going to be terrified and looking to kill him, or wanting to make nice, because he's got every quality of an S-rank priest but can also steal all their worshipers, reform or destroy their cult, or even put the gods themselves to the question.
