                Originally posted by Foxcalibur View Post
                So, I have a slight boggle. I have a full Circle of Solars. I'd like to add another player, but I really dislike redundancy.

                Is there currently anything I can give him to play wherein he can hang with a Solar Circle and have enough stuff to buy and do to not be bored? I'll accept homebrews, whatever. Has anyone experimented with playable Gods?

                If there aren't, I'll ask my players if anyone of them is willing to share their Caste, but I'd like to avoid that if I can.

                Thoughts?

                And I don't mean any flavor of mortal. Has to be someone who can survive and excel in shit like fights with Deathlords and First Age Lunars. Even God-Blooded feel a bit weak for what I'm doing.
                I think everyone has already offered the most complete fan projects. I have a few other suggestions.

                My experience with Ex3 so far is that while Castes are more sharply demarcated, role and concept are fuzzier. Let me unpack that a bit.

                For Solars, Supernal is limited to Caste, and Supernal is intended to "define the legend of that Exalt." So, characters defined as "Crafter" or "Doctor," are going to be Twilight, for example.
                However, what I have found is that many of the Abilities just don't lend themselves to being Supernal. War is a good example of this. It has few charms overall, and no charms at E4 or 5.

                The net result for Solars is some characters are defined by Supernal, but most are some degree of generalist or hybrid specialist. A Dawn Caste who builds around Melee, Thrown, and War is going to feel very, very different from one who builds around Brawl/MA Supernal. So different, in fact, that I doubt they would conceptually step on each other much (other than they both fight things, and don't most Exalts fight to one extent or another?).

                This brings me to another point. Some "concepts" are just really big. Martial Arts and Sorcery are conceptually and mechanically broad enough that they can sustain a character concept all on their own. MA has enough neat tricks and combinations that a character who invests heavily will always have something to bring to the party. Sorcery, assuming you are willing to update spells to 3rd Edition, is also pretty broad, because a Sorcerer can do Workings to enhance the party, make minions, alter terrain, etc.

                So, the new Caste Ability spreads for 3rd didn't just give a little wiggle room to the Castes themselves. They unlocked completely different ways of approaching the Caste in question.

                Example Twilight concepts (for funsies):
                • Pokemaster (Twilight Anima power+Survival charms and Sorcery for Summoning/Workings)
                • Crafter (you can spend every point of applicable XP from E1 to E5 and STILL have charms to buy)
                • Lore Master (or as a friend put it, a Burning Crusade-era Shadow Priest. Lore also has a crap-ton of charms.)
                • Martial Sorcerer (MA+Sorcery for ISoB and WDC)
                • Cat Burglar Twilight (Stealth, Larceny, and the Twilight anima to pop out after infiltrating and acquiring loot)
                • THE Sorcerer (Summon Demons, pour XP into Workings to augment the party and self, and wreck BGs with DoOB)
                • Sherlock Motherfuckin' Holmes (Investigation+Bureaucracy+Presence+Socialize)
                All of those characters can/ought to be Twilight, but they are different enough that I don't think they will step on each other at all.

                                                        The Wizard of Oz

                                                        Righteousness doesn't really mean good. It just means the Zenith believes what they are doing is right, and right is often subjective.

                                                        So, a Zenith can righteously invade some nice land, slaughter its inhabitants, and settle his people on it. His people benefit, so he's remembered as a great hero.

                                                        And priest can mean many things, and doesn't have to include the baggage of Abrahamic Monotheist-type holy people. Maybe the Zenith's faith is intensely personal, and he wants to work his will on the world, and make it work in accordance with his perception of UCS's desires, rather than just convert people. He doesn't preach about Sol; he speechifies about freedom.

                                                        The nice thing about 3rd Edition and the gods is the character can say just about anything is "what Sol wants," and have it be true, at least true enough.
