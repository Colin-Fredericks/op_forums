    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
    That's a bit silly; that logic leads to "Solars shouldn't have soak charms because they're so perfect that they never get hit in the first place."

    ...

    saying "Solars shouldn't have charms designed to let them recover from failure, because the idea behind being a Solar is to be so perfect that you never fail" leads to a gameplay where Solars are either ridiculously boring -- in that they have to solve everything in the first go no matter what -- or extremely brittle, which is neither fun nor suitable for their thematics.
    Gee Zuz.

    This is nothing like what I said, or what the Charms I referenced and proposed imply. Look at Arete-Driven Marathon Stride. Its entire context is an extended contest in which fortunes may ebb and flow. It performs best when your opponent is doing best. It never actually gives you an advantage, only denies your opponent an advantage they thought they were getting. So your back-and-forth, your dramatic reversals and your moments of epiphany and brilliance are all totally possible (and likely to happen naturally, on the turn of the dice) with these Charms. We are by no means talking about an Accuracy Without Distance, or Judge's Ear Technique, or Heavenly Guardian Defense. It is very much the Shonen anime hero style of charm, only instead of driving their opponent to their last life point only to find the accursed hero has drawn exactly the card they need to win ...

    (Uggh, forgive me)

    ... The bad guys find themselves at the pivotal moment of the race, experience a moment of strength or luck that should seal their lead, only to find the Solar whispering 'Thanks for the Arete' into their ear as they steal the lead.

    The same is true of the half- (maybe even one-quarter-) baked Charms I suggested above. Wisdom, in that example, still needs to come up with thirty-five sucesses. Now, yes, I'm assuming that that's going to be possible for him somehow, but it's a hell of a long way to calling that always succeeding.

    Misunderstandings about all that aside, there are some thematic and structural reasons that I don't think this is a good space to explore for Solars.

    The first is that Solars succeed the most. They have the best Charms, the highest dice caps and the most potent orichalcum artifacts. It follows that they fail the the least, especially when they put forward their magic. It also follows that Charms you design specifically to kick in when failure happens will have much less opportunity to come into play and feel like worthwhile, enjoyable purchases despite having cost just as much XP.

    The second is that trying again is not a lost and secret art known only to the magical. If you fail at a static task there have always been rules for trying again. If you're in a contest like a combat or a foot race there may come a pivotal moment where success or failure is determined, but you've come there by a series of successes and failures, tactics and manoeuvres any one of which could have turned the tide of battle. The point being that there is almost always the opportunity to just go around again and when Solars do so they can apply their full magnificence to the task again next time, as opposed to exercising a charm in limited circumstances for a marginal benefit.

    The third is that this doesn't particularly speak to Solar themes. Solars are mighty and glorious, not infallible. They defy Gods and cast down mountains, but they're not unbeatable. They changed the length and breadth of the world, but how many look back on them and think 'reliable'? In short, opening the scope for success at the top end of the scale is always more in theme for SOlars than closing the scope for failure at the bottom end. Charms like Arete-Driven Marathon stride, which work to make sure that the Solar is always in the contest, no matter how strong their opponent is, are even more appropriate. That is the stated intent, after all. These are Charms that make sure a Solar is always in with a chance.

    Fourth is that themes are both rare and expensive. They're rare in the sense that Exalted has a crowded world with a lot of different character types who need some thematic and mechanical distinctiveness. We can re-use themes, everyone's going to get dice-adders of some description one imagines, but the more overlap you allow the less distinction you have because everyone is more the same. They're expensive because each group can only hold so many themes. The more you put into them the less cohesive a vision they represent as a whole, and you loose distinctiveness because a group that can do everything represents nothing in particular.

    So. The question is whether this is a good theme to explore for Solars. Given that (4) we shouldn't assign themes willy-nilly, (3) the mechanic doesn't represent Solar themes terrible well, (1) the Charms hae a limited window of usefulness which all other Solar Charms work effectively at diminishing and that (2) Solars would often we well advised just to try again rather than settling for a consolation prize ... no. I don't think Solars are the best place for this to be.

    ​It is interesting theme-space for further down the ladder of three circles. 'I am defeated ... but I got what I wanted. All goes according to plan,' and 'you may beat me, but I overcome all hardships' are perfect Sidereal and Lunar answers to (3), neither of them have the large dice caps and cheap bonuses that make (1) so problematic and they each have a reputation for oblique approaches that sits well with (2).

    Frankly 'Solars should always succeed' has nothing to do with it ...

                        Originally posted by SuddenThunder View Post

                        Gee Zuz.

                        This is nothing like what I said
                        It is exactly what you said. You even went on to say it a second time in this post! Dumping a massive number of words on it doesn't change that. When you say "In short, opening the scope for success at the top end of the scale is always more in theme for Solars than closing the scope for failure at the bottom end", you're saying that you don't think Solar charms should be able to handle failure gracefully, or that "I failed this time, but it will give me the tools to succeed next time" is within the Solar theme. That's a completely wrongheaded statement to make.

                        (As I think you ultimately recognize, since you immediately denied making it, then put it in much more convoluted and opaque terms when you said it a second time.)

                        Solar themes embody heroic excellence in the fields covered by the 25 Abilities (and, more generally, human endeavor.) Recovery from a failure, and then going on to succeed, is a core element of many of the heroic narratives that Solars draw on, and a core element of many stories about peerless mastery; again, someone like House or Sherlock Holmes sometimes fails at first, then goes on to succeed based by building on that failure. Thomas Edison's "I haven't failed, I've just found 10000 ways that won't work" is absolutely a valid description for the process of playing a Twilight, and if you don't feel that Twilights should have charms that allow them to build on failures, then you're saying that huge portions of the literature of heroic investigation and heroic thought are outside of their themes.

                        More generally, taking something that is clearly and unambiguously within the thematics of heroic talent at an Ability and saying "let's take this away from Solars to reserve it for other Exalted" is a terrible way to design splats. Solars are good at Solar things, which includes talent at investigation (and therefore includes an investigation charm that allows you to recover from a failure by building on it to enable later success); players should not feel that the conceptual space available for them shrinks with every new splat that's introduced. There is room for overlap, but if you want a charm that is definitively and completely non-Solar, you need to move away from the core of what a Solar is, and explore charmspace that is completely non-human or utterly abstract. That is the charmspace that was reserved for everyone else when the core concept of the Solars was established. If you make a charm that purely expresses straightforward human competence within the realm of an Ability, it will always overlap with Solar thematics to an extent.

                        Again, thematic overlap is not necessarily a bad thing. But if I saw that charm with no reference to splat names anywhere in it, I would say "that's a cool Solar charm!" It is completely defined by the Solar thematic of pure excellence, and shows no hint of any of the unique thematics that define the other Exalted. A version of it that fit into the themes of another type of Exalted would probably operate at least slightly differently.

                            So, I made this thread over a year ago.

                            Then SuddenThunder and Aquillion got into an argument. They said a lot of words at each other, backed by much thought and deep energy.

                            I could not find the spirit within me to navigate their arguments and synthesize my own understanding. Intimidated, confused, and dismayed, I let the thread die.


                            Tonight, simply because I wanted to put off going to bed that much, I finally forced myself to truly read their arguments and try to understand.


                            I can finally say that I think Aquillion speaks the truth. Perhaps his points were too exaggerated for my sensibilities, such that it felt to me like he was strawmanning both SuddenThunder and myself, but ultimately I have to agree with the idea that is silly to suggest that "recover from failure and come back stronger in the second round" would dilute Solar themes of excellence. Excellence on the first try and excellence on the rebound are both excellence.

                            Which is not to say that the Solar Exalted wouldn't have a significantly different balance between those concepts than my hypothetical Exaltation whose narrative focuses more on learning from mistakes and growing stronger than on being strong in the first place. Or that the hypothetic Host couldn't be comparatively better at second chances than Solars while being generally worse on the balance (ie: they learn better, but that only makes them as smart as the Solar was at the start).

                            As a separate example, the Terrestrial Exalted ought to have a stronger inclination towards "stronger in numbers" than Solars, but Solars still have Charms that let them cooperate with others; defending them, inspiring them, teaching them, leading them, and healing them. Giving Solars such Charms doesn't dilute their theme of personal magnificence, but rather works as a facet thereof.



                            Solars absolutely should have options for "closing the scope of failure at the bottom end".

                            I mean, there should be a limit to how strongly they should be able to come back after a defeat, and they shouldn't do so nearly as well (comparatively) as the hypothetical "second time's the Charm" Exalted, but that's because the Solars are already so powerful and the hypothetical second-wind Exalted have much farther to climb in order to be so powerful.

                            Solars face limits in all things, such as their dice-pools. But those limits still place them at the top of the heap, and exist for the purpose of not making that position too assured.
