                          Originally posted by magius View Post

                          Solars can theoretically learn SMA, sure, but it's a bit... silly for them, in 2e. 12 xp a charm, and they pay more to use them than Sidereals because they lack sutras. The only reason it's ever worthwhile for a Solar to learn SMA in 2e is because some of the styles are flatly broken.

                          (There is a huge laundry list of attendant problems and related issues and various arguments inherent in the above paragraph, but that's just a quick summary of how it is. A bigger problem, for the topic at hand, is that the generally mechanically-broken nature of most SMA limits its usefulness to Sidereals in a typical game, as do its outrageously high Essence minimums.)

                          2.5e is a quick patch to a broken system focused mainly on making Solars playable, though -- they didn't want to spend more time on that because they ultimately felt the game needed an entirely new system to fix its underlying issues; a fix for every problem in 2e would basically read something like "replace all text in every book with the following:"

                          Lunars and Sidereals have underlying issues that would've basically required a total rewrite of their splatbook to fix. Infernals have issues that make them nearly impossible to adapt to 2.5e without writing insane amounts of material. (You can still play them, but they're currently somewhat limited in the builds available to them -- absent extensive homebrew, Malfeas is not optional if you want to be really good at combat, because they have no other way to get decent magical soak.)

                          Alchemicals are usable, I think, but remember that they're intended to be the weakest Celestials. Honestly, 2.5e probably indirectly buffed them a bit, since their big weakness was their terribly overpriced perfect defenses, and those are now no longer as central to combat.

                          But yeah, 2.5e is not well-balanced. The best bet is to just wait for 3e.

                              The Solars benefited late on with a certain amount of Charm bloat. They are, after all, the most popular splat in terms of sales (helps that they're in the Core book!) and they were the focus of things like Dreams of the First Age and Glories of the Most High: Unconquered Sun. The net result is that they have way more Charms at way higher levels than just about anyone else, including their "equals," the Abyssals and Infernals.

                              That said, the remedy to this is simply to invent new Charms for the splats. Sadly, not something that's gonna happen in 2.5, but we can help with home-brewing the Charms.

                              Now, as for Martial Arts... no, Sidereals can't make you forget them. That was a joke. And technically Sidereals have the best Martial Arts, and technically Solars can learn them, but... have you ever heard the term "opportunity costs?" You spend XP on one thing, you don't get to spend it on another. So you can't just pick up Sidereal Martial Arts at a whim- you need to make a pretty significant investment of experience and time before you can learn even up to the Form Charms, much less the entire Style. And once you put all that together, Sidereals still have Sutras to make wielding SMAs more practical than it would be for Solars or Abyssals.

                              Infernals can't learn Sidereal Martial Arts. Long story there, but basically, if you want high-level form Charms as an Infernal, take a shintai. That's what they're there for.

                                                  I came to this thread expecting to say "unbalanced compared to what", but the 3 M's above me seem to have covered that.

                                                  The Solar Exalted are meant to be more powerful than everyone else.
                                                  The Solar Exalted are meant to "break the setting" (that was the reason they were created in the first place).
                                                  They are meant to be able to change the world and defeat any opponent you can throw at them.
                                                  They are even meant to be better (on the whole) than the Abyssals (who are Solar power turned to a niche use) and Infernals (who are Solar power corrupted).

                                                  That said:

                                                  They are not meant to be better than other Exalts "in their niche" (they're not meant to be better than Abyssal's at necromancy for example - which is not to say that a Solar can't be good at necromancy or an Abyssal can't be bad at it).
                                                  They are not meant to be so much better than everyone else that nothing is a challenge.
                                                  They are not meant to be better due to twinky min-maxing the character generation mini-game.
                                                  They are meant to break the world, not break the story.
                                                  They are not meant to make the game unfun for everyone else (which is not to say that people have a right to play non-Solars and expect not to suck compared to the Solars, but it is meant as an indication that one Solar build can easily overshadow the other PCs (whether they are Solars or not) and this quickly leads to people losing interest and feeling frustrated).

                                                  So in answer to your complaint: yes, Solar charms are better than Abyssal Charms and Infernal Charms. They are meant to be. Players are meant to play Solars. You can play any splat you like, but you need to understand these splats are weaker than Solars who are intentionally more powerful than everyone else (because they are the faction you are supposed to play in a game that's supposed to change the world).

                                                  Or to use a D&D analogy, you seem to be complaining that Player Class Levels are more powerful than NPC Classes/Racial Levels: this is an intentional, and fundamental, part of the game. If you are actually complaining that the Wizard Class is more powerful than the Monk Class, then I apologise: you are right and I hope that this is fixed next edition.
