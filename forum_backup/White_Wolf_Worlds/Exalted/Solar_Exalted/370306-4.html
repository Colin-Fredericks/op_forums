        Personally, I think there's a degree of 'bend the rules so you think it'll make a good story'.

        That said, if you're going to hold an Exalted prisoner, you probably need to completely immobilise them (or at the very least, leave them no wriggle room with their limbs) AND cut off their ability to use Essence. A cocoon-like restraint might have... more luck that you might otherwise have, although I imagine you'd want it to be an artifact binding to be safe... and yeah, you need something Manacles of Night-like.

        .And still, you might have to worry about whether they're actually caught or just waiting for the right moment/enjoying the chance to relax/into that sort of stuff- honestly, if the Dragon-Blooded had found a long-term method of imprisonment for the Solars that didn't get caught up in the Jade Prison, they would have used it in an instant, since that would lock them down nicely. You only really hold an Exalted prisoner if they're more valuable to you alive.

                    It should be possible for a Solaroid, barring certain highly specific skill sets, to be imprisoned by a group of Terrestrial-power level beings for the short to medium term (say up to ten years) with only a moderate level of ruthlessness and suitable thoroughness. They simply should not be able to do so indefinitely, because that turns exalted into a perpetually diminishing game of capture and imprison that isn't any fun.

                    The precise mechanical and fluff reasons aren't especially important and could easily vary from table to table. An easy excuse is simply to say that the Powers That Be (flavor to taste) notice if an exaltation has been idle over a sufficient period of time and send someone to investigate and ultimately intervene if warranted, which they couldn't do wit the jade Prison because they didn't know where it was/how it worked.

                        The fool had no idea he was in chains--bronze chains, to be precise, out of respect for his captor. The thought of "respecting" such a creature left a foul taste in Lysander's mouth, but who was a little lost egg like he to argue with his superiors? He might have been an admiral, if he had chosen any other master in all the world, but now he was a dog of the Realm, sent to guard monsters guarding monsters. The Raksha holding the leash had the temerity to name himself "Goodfellow" (as if such as he could ever be a person at all, much less a virtuous one!) Regardless, he was useful, and he had trapped the Anathema in his pleasant little dream for months now. Such was the will of the Foresaken that illusions could not contain him against his will, but the poor sap would have to want to break free, or even know to try.

                        "My dear little dragon," said the mocking voice of the Raksha he thought he was watching carefully, from over his shoulder, "you look troubled!" He turned, not bothering to watch the false image of Goodfellow in front of him collapse into quickly vanishing gossamer.

                        "And not because you can apparently watch my expression from behind my back," was Lysander's answer.

                        "Is it the nature of our arrangement? If so, consider it not a matter of men and souls, but a machine. You feed it a lifetime worth of dreams, and our friend there slumbers for another night. Input. Output. A fair exchange, says I, considering he would slaughter ten thousand mortals on the open field."

                        "No," was his answer to that, but he couldn't keep the disgust from reaching his face. "Unsavory as it is, criminals are criminals. How do I care how they die?"

                        The foul thing in front of him laughed. It was beautiful laughter, too, the infectious sort that reached Lysander's eyes even as he growled through the temptation to join.

                        The laughter died with an exclamation of, "How angry! Well, then, my dear little dragon, why so troubled?"

                        "You keep him trapped, not even knowing that he should want for freedom, by showing him what he wants to see."

                        "Yes! Battle and glory, loss and sacrifice!"

                        "And this arrangement of ours, gallivanting around the threshold from manse to manse so his allies never find him. It's working like a charm."

                        "Yes! Swift as a magpie with treasure in beak!"

                        "In fact, horrible as it may seem, with you growing fat on not-so-innocent souls, this is precisely what I want to see."

                        "Yes! Oh... why, yes."

                        "Then how do I know that HE'S really the one in chains?"

                        His only answer was more of that damned laughter. The captain didn't say a word in argument when Lysander stormed off from his watch. It had happened often enough by now. Everyone was tired of Goodfellow. Someone else would be assigned to guard duty, as useless and irritating as the post was. And as it turned out, the ... supplies were running low. Lysander would be easily reassigned to the nearest town, to collect "tribute to the Realm."

                                I'd tend to say that when dealing with a Hero(tm), there is no completely-reliable way to lock them up with absolute certainty that they will not escape. This doesn't mean it's not worth trying! Implausible escapes are just that, implausible. But the narratives Solars draw on tend to have a huge number of stories where the main character is locked up in a completely inescapable! situation of some variety in which escape would be absolutely, totally, and in all other ways inconceivable and still manages to free themselves.

                                I would generally say that when you're trying to imprison supernatural beings, you should have to use complicated and interesting things. Manacles of Night and other "your mojo doesn't work at all" stuff is usually pretty boring to play with -- it's fine to say that you're just using it on NPCs, but even then, it's just not as exciting an answer as pinning someone under a mountain or trapping them in a painting.

                                (And this is generally backed up by the setting -- if it were possible to imprison Solars or Lunars reliably, then the Sidereals would be doing so. You can imprison them, but never without an element of risk.)


                                Laurence Yep's Dragon series (which is a good inspiration for Exalted in general) has the hero repeatedly escape from impossible supernatural prisons like that, say.

                                        Yes! This is absolutely true.

                                        Now, for those who would like to imprison extremely dangerous things, let me recommend the following method:

                                        1. Seal them inside of an indestructable artifact mirror.
                                        2. Place the mirror inside a temporal disruption which causes time within to flow at 1/xth (where x is an extremely large number) of our own!
                                        3. Build a great egg of ice around the temporal disruption. The ice should be from the wyld North- hard as steel, and quick-growing to repair itself.
                                        4. Surround the ice egg with mighty statues of shinmaic principles, such that as long as they are in alignment, the central ice egg is crystalized with the principles of Advaita iraivan and does not truly exist.
                                        5. Encase the entire chamber in solid stone and sink it beneath the ground, then fill over the hole with terrain precisely matching the surroundings and create an uninteresting area of forest land.
                                        6. Build a very interesting manse which people will struggle over and focus on about five miles to the west.
                                        7. Weave a powerful destiny which will prevent anyone from ever finding that cavern.
                                        8. Craft and ancient and supernal guardian to watch over the chamber evermore.
                                        9. Also include alarms which will let you know if anyone somehow DOES break in.

                                        This was very expensive but it worked for nearly three hundred years the last time I tried it.
