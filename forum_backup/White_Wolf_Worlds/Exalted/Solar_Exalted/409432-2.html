                        Originally posted by Ravraxes
                        And what "acting like a Solar" is supposed to be?
                        I would have phrased it as "acting how the Unconquered Sun would prefer Solars to act, upholding his Virtues and preforming to a standard of heroism that he endorses." Your Exaltation shard presumably loves it when you do that, since that is what it is was made to do. I'd argue that acting like that has a real high chance of eventually making your shard start acting as it was originally conceived to do so.

                        Originally posted by SachKreiosLucy4 View Post
                        Abyssal champions the dead, spreads ancestor cults -- even finds a way to harmoniously stabilize them, ensuring that the ancestors educate and civilize and guide their barbarian descendants
                        "Spreading ancestor cults" is inconsistent with "harmoniously stabilizes them", because harmoniously stable ghosts stop being ghosts. They enter Lethe and are reborn. Being a ghost is a twisted, messed-up existence even for the best-adjusted among them, and every single ghost contributes to Oblivion merely by existing. The whole reason we have Oblivion is because there are a bunch of ghosts out there that can't ever enter Lethe.

                        I keep seeing people either act like being a ghost is just like being a person. It is not. And I would argue it shouldn't be. If you can be a ghost without being psychologically damaged in some way that fetters you to a never-intended and very uncomfortable existence, then where is the downside to dying? You just became functionally immortal! Sure, you have to go live in the Underworld, but that's a small price to pay for continued existence that's just as comfortable as your previous one was. Very few people would want to enter Lethe.

                        When the Yozis conspire to bring Creation under, such that it would desecrate the memory of the long departed? The Abyssal slays the fallen Primordial, sending it to join the Neverborn, and giving a clear signal to the rest of hell's prisoners.
                        That's not a very good move if there were any other options, as she just made the most destructive things in the setting that much stronger and more powerful. There might be unintended side effects. The Labyrinth might suddenly grow immensely overnight and swallow half of Stygia.

                        And then she begins glowing gold one day.

                        And hates it.
                        Well, under 2e rules becoming a Solar would be a manifest upgrade, as suddenly she wouldn't have Resonance fucking up her life any more; limit is a lot more manageable. The only downside would be needing to start carrying Hearthstones to respire in the Underworld.

                        She might not want to be a Solar, but, well, she signed up to murder the world and then went backsies on it. There are consequences for that sometimes.

                        I have to admit that I am consistently baffled as to what precisely people who don't currently like them want from Abyssals. Their underlying design has been pretty consistent for two editions: the Neverborn made them to fulfill their aeons-long goal of ending everything. They can either embrace that, or rebel against it in some way, and how they make that choice and deal withthe consequences that come with it are the basis of how games involving Abyssal protagonists are supposed to evolve. If you aren't down with that, that's fine, but you have to make a case that that structure is just kind of inherently bad and wrong from a playability standpoint.

                        It seems like a very big part of the underlying structure of their design is that there are big, big consequences for going back on your deal with the Neverborn. (I keep trying to type Malfeans, because I like the word Malfeans, even though I know we don't call them that anymore.) If you take that away, and take away anything at all resembling Resonance, aren't you basically just left with Solars that glow black? Solars as a splat have a kind of absolute freedom, because the Unconquered Sun doesn't compel them to do jack shit and they don't have day jobs (Sidereals) or existing societies imposing their own expectations on them (Lunars, Dragon-Blooded) and so can be whatever they want to be with no downside other than the fact that the world, in general, may not like them for it. Other splats don't get that, and that's okay. Some splats might wrap your character in heavy chains, because the point of them is interacting with those chains, and that is also okay.

                        I mean, don't get me wrong, there's conceptual space for some kind of weird elegiac death-inflected Exalt who is suffused with the Underworld without necessarily being tied up in the whole Oblivion thing... but aren't Liminals going to be filling that niche?

                        "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."

                            I think my big problem with the redemption arc is you don't redeem types of people, you redeem your actions in the view of the one your seeking redemption from.

                            In the angle of Abyssals to Solars I guess this makes sense, if you get the UCS approval he can turn you back into what is fitting for you to be in his eyes.

                            But if you're an Abyssal who likes what they are, I can see you thinking that transformation is pretty terrible. Even if Abyssals can become Solars, there's nothing saying that makes them objectively moral superior in a numinous sense (partially because such a state is unknowable).

                            Turning from Abyssal to Solar is only a statement about morality if it is impossible to act in a subjectively moral way as an Abyssal.

                            And if the answer to that question is "they can, but they still poison and doom the world by their very existence" or even just "they can, but only by murder" then I feel like I've lost something that I really looked forward to playing in Third edition.

                            I mean, I would be sad If the fact that you need to be able to play Vampire Hunter D (an idea I support) means that no one can play Abyssal who can create a lasting common good.

                            Notice, please, that I am using "create a good" in the political and not in the moral sense. I mean an Abyssal Barrow Lord who genuinely cares for his buried principalities and whose subjects feel that their afterlives (heh) are enriched by his actions.

                            Summed up, I'd like to feel that playing an Abyssal is not an exercise in futility if I'm not trying to turn into a solar or kill all-the-everything.
