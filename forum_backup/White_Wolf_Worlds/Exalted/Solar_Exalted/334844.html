    How do you exalt a Solar Circle?

    12-09-2014, 02:06 AM
    Beginning as mortals in a Solar game is fun as you get that rush of power when you exalt, but I have a hard time figuring out how to exalt these characters at the same time. I guess one possibility is to throw them into a challenging situation beyond the limits of mortals, like a fae or beastmen attack and have them all exalt in a glorious fight, but it sometimes feels difficult to justify the social or intellectual characters earning the exaltation in these situations. Another possibility is to have the characters naturally earn their exaltations one or two at a time as appropriate situations arise, but there can be problems when the first person to exalt becomes a sponge for every epic deed, and the mortals get left behind.

    Personally I like the idea that the UCS or even the Exaltations themselves upon seeing a moment where a individual deserves to exalt, might decide to exalt the people around him who have the potential to be great Solars, even if it isn't their exact moment to earn the exaltation through action. So a young backwater priest might stand up to an immaculate monk and exalt Zenith, but this would instigate the retired soldier, scholarly herbalist, local hunter, and visiting merchant to exalt Dawn, Twilight, Night, and Eclipse as well. They now get to contribute to this momentous moment even if they didn't directly act to start it.

    How have you handled this process in your games?

      Well something to remember is that while the more epic deeds are more easily accomplished by an exalt they aren't honestly as impressive when done by an exalt. I mean don't get me wrong a I think you need extenuating circumstances to make holding your own in a duel against a rhaksha lord not an impressive feat. Just that not having charms, the soak benefit, or potential boost of skill from a past life would make the same thing more impressive for a mortal.

      To quote Superman, "It's easier to do when you're bulletproof."

      As for exalting the entire group I'd say a major military battle could do well since it would allow for a variety of situations for a pc to have their own moment of awesome to justify the exaltation. It also helps since different moments make it feel more personal than say the whole lot exalting in the middle of a brawl.

              This is the main thing that always put me off running a mortals-who-become Exalts game, even though I've actually played two mortals who became Exalts in-game and absolutely loved doing so.*
              I like the idea of a military battle as giving each person an opportunity to do something Exalt-worthy, because being Exalted because you were standing near someone doing something awesome is going to feel pretty lame to the other players.
              If you want to be hardcore (and this only works with some players, so you'll have to decide based on your playgroup), have them in a battle, and try to give them opportunities for heroics. If they avoid the heroics... then they just die like chumps in the battle. Most will try I imagine, but it depends. Then they can make a new Solar character who can join the band next session, since he/she heard that some people had Exalted in this battle.
              Another possibility, if you're happy to allow Infernals and Abyssals, is that you give them the opportunity for heroics. If they try to be heroic and don't fluff it, they become Solars (Lunars also works fine of course if that's what they'd prefer). If they try, but botch their first few rolls or something, so it feels ridiculous for them to be Solars, then they die and become Abyssals by a rogue Black Exaltation. If they fail to be heroic... a few days later, while they're moping around with their now Solar buddies, a demon turns up and offers them an Exaltation as well. Of course, since they'll then be hanging around with Solars, I imagine when some other demons turn up from Hell and go "hey, come back with us" then the Solars are likely to stab them in the face and the group can happily continue.
              I recognise of course, that this is a pretty radical approach and won't work for a lot of players.

              *The first time was as a mortal in a short-term mortal game. We were a Realm-aligned special operations squad trying to drive the Bull out of Linowan. I exalted after hopping from canoe to canoe across a river during a battle and clubbing a water-aspected Icewalker Dragonblood in a warstrider, in the face (well, warstrider helmet). I didn't quite knock him out, but I came close (I did 6 bashing to him, so close!). So when I exalted as a Lunar, it felt awesome, even though I then had to flee before my own side killed me. But that was one guy, the rest of the party carried on as mortals, because you're not going to get that kind of thing happening for 5 people all in the same scene.
              The second time, I was playing a mortal in a Celestial game. Anyway, we were trying to rescue my character's girlfriend from an evil Twilight and his psychotic AI buddy. We got into the underground manse, found the chamber where he was keeping her chained up, fought him, and defeated him. And then I exalted as a Lunar. Now, obviously I had just gone toe-to-toe with a Solar with MA 5 who was trying to rip my head off with Wood Dragon Claw, to save the Raksha I loved... but actually, I think I did like one bashing damage to him, if that. It was the Celestials with me who actually knocked him out. So, while it was obviously the most fitting moment for my character to Exalt, I didn't actually feel like my character really deserved it.

                                                  Our storyteller had us tie each of our characters to a particular emotion that our character identified with. We didn't exalt together, but we didn't really care to. Starting as a heroic mortal gave us each enough power to play with until we got there. Each of us eventually came to an awesome scene where we embodied our emotion and underwent a cathartic release where exaltation was the only path to survival or success. I think it worked well - certainly encouraged role playing since gaining Essence was tied with character psychology.

                                                          In the recent game I've been storytelling I've done it by degree's as my players demonstrated awesome creativity during gaming, as follows...

                                                          1) Our Night Caste, 1st to Exalt (and a generally awesome player by the way) Exalted when he used White Veil Style to Poison a Raksha Noble that I had no intention of forcing the players to actually fight.He drew the creature in, gained its trust, drank a vial of handy poison manufactured by a PC alchemist, internalized the poison through a White Veil Technique (yeah, my Creation is a little different) and then stabbed her in the back.

                                                          2) Our Eclipse Caste, at the tail end of that same session, Exalted after negotiating safe passage, the release of captives, and then seducing a Heart Chakra Argent Blossom (again, my Creation varies in some respects). How did he do this? Dancing. Dancing, and some very impressive stunts.

                                                          3) Twilight number 1 Exalted two sessions later when he faced down a caravan of Reavers after trapping them between a large cliff and a bridge over a narrow river. When they refused to surrender he cast Death of Obsidian Butterflies.

                                                          4) Twilight number 2 (the alchemist) Exalted while the Circle was clearing out a labor camp run by Yozi worshipers. She got caught between escape and leaving prisoners to die at the claws of of a Blood Ape. Since she bought up Past Lives to 5 I have her temporary access to one charm of any essence level <6. The player chose a homebrew E5 larceny charm that allowed her to steal the blood ape's sorcerously bound loyalty, and then direct it against the cultists.

                                                          5) Our Dawn Caste Ronin Kenshin-alike (and brother to the Night) and last to Exalt did so when facing down a minor river god who blamed him for killing his mortal lover, a local petty lord that was partially responsible for bringing the characters together. While the party was in full-spring running the hell away mode, the Dawn-to-be compelled an earlier instance in which he had heard a Demon Harpist Play Time and allowed him to break the river gods essence-induced fear effect.
