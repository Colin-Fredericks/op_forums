                Solars draw from the human hero, the Dragonslayer, which goes, 1. The Dragonslayer will kill the Dragon, and 2. That is impressive because he is just a man, while the Dragon is an enormous engine of destruction able to melt metal and bring down armies. When you see a Solar fighting a DB, the Solar tends to appear weaker, most of the time. There is one guy shooting fire from his hands, becoming an elemental bonfire of destruction, raising the earth or bringing down lightning! OK, now that is just plain unfair.' Solars will likely win, but they cannot turn into a T-Rex, summon elemental dragons, rise all the dead on both sides as vengeful wraiths, or any other sort of power that really, half exists to just be that damn _impressive_.

                If the other Exalted do not come off as incredibly impressive and puissant, Exalted has failed. If the Solars cannot overcome them, then Exalted has failed, as they are the weaponized narrative of the human hero, who conquers the strange, the natural, the feral, and death itself.

                    Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                    Yeah. Solars got a big buff in Ex3.
                    Disagree. Ex3 gave them a few new pop-off-the-page charms, but note that their workhorse superpowers were often nerfed. EotUCS, for instance, is now an instant charm rather than scenelong; and of course the big searchlight-eyes thing, while it looks powerful, is a lot weaker than 2e's "you automatically see through everything for the rest of the scene fullstop." In 2e, you could just put up EotUCS and be totally immune to an entire category of strategies for the entire scene; if someone's build was focused entirely on combat illusions, their options were to run away or to die. 3e EotUCS is more like a super-move -- it'd be devastating to the illusion-guy in the one moment you use it, but it's not shutting him down for the entire scene.

                    Other things are similar; there's a lot of powerful-looking charms, but fewer scenelong "I win automatically for free" powers. In particular, while the removal of IAM affects everyone, it hits Solar(oids) the hardest, because they have the largest dice pools -- in 2e, a Solar fighting DBs or Mortals could just max out their dice cap with IAM, at which point they had no reasonable chance of being hit without the enemy using charms and would hit nearly automatically with every attack. In 3e, a Solar has to actually spend motes to press their full advantage over a DB (or even a mortal!) And that's without getting into the removal of 2e-style perfects and paranoia combat, which, again, favored Solars due to them having the best perfects by far.

                    A lot of the new "pop-off-the-page powerful" charms, meanwhile, are things that will maybe come up once a story, if that; God-King's Shrike or Soul Reprisal or Dual Magnus Prana are hardly going to come up every session the way the old scenelong buffs and perfects sometimes did.

                    (Of course, Solars still generally have the best workhorse charms in many areas, but the structure of the game is no longer one where the default implementation of that kind of power is "I activate Persistent Invulnerability Suite, I win.")
