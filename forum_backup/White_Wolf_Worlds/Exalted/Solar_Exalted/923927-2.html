                This is where player intent and game design come into conflict.

                I believe many people feel that if they want to focus on a given Ability roll, then they need to get as many points in it as a they can. So if someone wants to be investigator because they like murder mysteries and such, then they need to get 5 dots in Investigation, 5 dots in Perception and grab a bunch of Investigation Charms. But, in reality, when you do that, it doesn't make murder mysteries more interesting - instead it trivializes murder mysteries.

                When the player is faced with a mystery, instead of actually spending multiple scenes and time investigating, putting clues together and coming up with a theory and then confronting the suspect after an intense and prolonged investigation (which is probably what the player who just got done reading a bunch of Agatha Christie stories wants) they just spend some Essence, activate some Charms, point at the target and, boom, the mystery is solved.

                The same thing is true for any other field - if a player wants to focus heavily on his character being a performer so he makes out Performance and Charisma and backs it up with Performance Charms, then his character is already the greatest performer in Creation and almost any "challenge" that the ST comes up with is going to be trivial.

                In other words, there's an inherent tension in the system - the more heavily you specialize in a given field, the less time you will need to spend dealing with that field in the game, since any challenges are more likely to be trivial. It seems rather counter-intuitive but it's better to think that specializing in a field is not something you want to do because you enjoy that field and want to spend a lot of time dealing with that field, but rather the opposite - that you want to skip that field and deal with it less in the game than you otherwise would.

                If you don't want to deal with Investigations in game, then make a super Investigator so you can just steamroll those mysteries. If you don't like combat, then make a super combat character so you can steamroll those enemies and get the combat scene over quicker

                This is something I think a lot of people don't understand. So a player wants combat to be a focus because they like making fighty characters and they enjoy the combat system. So they design a badass warrior, max out their stats for combat, grab a bunch of combat Charms... and then grumble when combat is over in two rounds because the enemies died so quickly. This in turn pressures the ST to create challenges for that super-specialized player (because he's understandably not having any fun), but at the same time the players who aren't specialized don't feel like they are really contributing.

                The ST comes up with a super-duper mystery to challenge the player with maxed Investigation stats and all of the Investigation Charms and then the other players, none of whom are invested in Investigation, are kind of stuck on the side because there isn't any chance that a character who isn't super-duper invested into Investigation would have a chance to solve the mystery in the first place.



                tl;dr I think you are absolutely right Paranoia, it's just that most players and ST's don't really understand this because it's so counter-intuitive - it just seems obvious you would invest in something you like doing and want to spend a lot of time doing, rather than invest in something you don't like doing and don't want to deal with. But essentially, that's exactly what ends up happening a lot of the time in Exalted.
