    Solar Exaltation
    The moment when a character gains her first Mythic Tier is called her Exaltation. This is similar to Mythic Ascension for a character with a Mythic Path, and indeed Mythic Paths, Exaltation and Mythic Ranks are mutually exclusive. Exalted characters, for all rules purposes, are considered Mythic characters.
    A Solar’s Exaltation (which can refer both to the moment she received her power and the resilient spark of divine power that rests within her) is granted to her directly by the Unconquered Sun, the highest amongst the Celestial Incarnae and King of the Gods. The Unconquered Sun has a finite number of these Exaltations; a mere 150 Solars to defend all of Creation, and as such is extremely selective about those who receive His power. Mortals can receive Solar Exaltation only if they are at least 3rd level and levels in NPC classes do not count toward this minimum.

    The following are universal abilities all Solars gain as they increase in Tier. They gain these abilities in addition to any abilities from their specific Caste. Unless otherwise stated, they work exactly like their base Mythic Ability equivalents, see Mythic Adventures page 12.

    Ability Score: Upon reaching the 2nd Mythic Tier, an ability score of your choice permanently increases by 2. At 4th, 6th, 8th, and 10th Tiers, another ability score of your choice permanently increases by 2; this can be an ability score you've already increased or a different ability score.

    Mythic Feat: Select one Mythic feat or non-Mythic feat as a bonus feat. You must qualify for this feat normally. You gain another mythic feat at 3rd Tier, and again every 2 Tiers thereafter.

    Personal Essence Pool (Su): Exalted characters can draw upon a wellspring of power to accomplish amazing deeds and cheat fate. This power is used by a number of different abilities. Each day, you can expend “motes” of Personal Essence equal to 3 + double your Mythic Tier (5/day at 1st Tier, 7/day at 2nd, etc.). This amount is your maximum amount of Personal Essence. If an ability allows you to regain motes of Personal Essence, you can never have more than this amount.

    Peripheral Essence Pool (Su): In addition to your Personal Essence pool, you also gain a second pool of Essence called your Peripheral Essence pool. This pool functions much the same as the Personal Essence pool except as follows. Each day, you can expend motes of Peripheral Essence equal to 6 + six times your Tier (12/day at 1st Tier, 18/day at 2nd, etc.). This is your maximum amount of Peripheral Essence. If an ability allows you to regain motes Peripheral Essence, you can never have more than this amount. When an effect causes you to regain a certain number of motes, they are not allocated to your Peripheral Essence pool until your Personal Essence pool is full.

    Sidebar: Mythic Power vs Motes: Uses of Mythic Power and motes of Essence are, essentially, the same resource measured in a different metric. If an Exalt gains an ability (such as a Mythic Feat) or an item that requires her to spend Mythic Power, she can instead spend triple that amount in motes for the same effect. If an effect would normally increase an Exalt’s maximum amount of Mythic Power, that Exalt instead increases her maximum personal Essence pool by 1 and her maximum peripheral Essence pool by 2 for every use of mythic power the effect would normally grant.
    Likewise, a Mythic creature may gain an ability that requires that creature to spend motes of Essence. This means the creature may, at some point, have one or more thirds of a use of mythic power. It may be easier to keep track of that character’s mythic power as motes from that point on, even if the creature doesn’t actually use motes. Alternatively, you could keep track of fractional Mythic power gained in this way as a repeating decimal, fraction, or mixed number.

    Anima Banner (Su): When you spend uses of Peripheral Essence a bit of the power leaks into your Anima; an invisible aura possessed by all living creatures. This leak creates a visible phenomenon called an Anima Banner. This banner grows more intense as you spend more Essence, but this intensity decreases by one step for every minute your character doesn’t spend any Peripheral Essence.

    Hard to Kill (Su): Whenever you're below 0 hit points, you automatically stabilize without needing to attempt a Constitution check. If you have an ability that allows you to act while below 0 hit points, you still lose hit points for taking actions, as specified by that ability. Bleed damage still causes you to lose hit points when below 0 hit points. In addition, you don't die until your total number of negative hit points is equal to or greater than double your Constitution score.

    Surge (Su): You can call upon your Essence to overcome difficult challenges. You can expend three motes to increase any d20 roll you just made by rolling 1d6 and adding it to the result. Using this ability is an immediate action taken after the result of the original roll is revealed. This can change the outcome of the roll. The bonus die gained by using this ability increases to 1d8 at 4th Tier, 1d10 at 7th Tier, and 1d12 at 10th Tier.

    Limit (Su): Whenever you successfully save against a compulsion effect or are forced to act egregiously out of alignment, you gain a point of Limit, to a maximum of 10. Once you reach 10 Limit, you lose all your Limit points and suffer the effects of a Limit Break from the list of Solar Limit Breaks. You get a Will save to establish partial control of yourself (DC 15 plus your Tier), as detailed in that Limit Break’s description.

    Amazing Initiative (Su): At 2nd Tier, you gain a bonus on initiative checks equal to your Mythic Tier. In addition, as a free action on your turn, you can expend three motes to take an additional standard action during that turn. This additional standard action can't be used to cast a spell. You can't gain an extra action in this way more than once per round.

    Recuperation (Su): At 3rd Tier, you are restored to full hit points after 8 hours of rest so long as you aren't dead. In addition, by Expending three motes and resting for 1 hour, you regain a number of hit points equal to half your full hit points (up to a maximum of your full hit points) and regain the use of any class features that are limited to a certain number of uses per day (such as barbarian rage, bardic performance, spells per day, and so on). This rest is treated as 8 hours of sleep for such abilities. This rest doesn't let you regain motes or any Exalted or Mythic abilities that are limited to a number of times per day.

    Mythic Saving Throws (Su): At 5th Tier, whenever you succeed at a saving throw against a spell or special ability, you suffer no effects as long as that ability didn't come from a mythic source (such as a creature with a mythic Tier, mythic ranks or an Exalt). If you fail a saving throw that results from a mythic source, you take the full effects as normal.

    Force of Will (Su): At 7th Tier, you can exert your will to force events to unfold as you would like. As an immediate action, you can expend three motes to reroll a d20 roll you just made, or force any non-mythic creature to reroll a d20 roll it just made. You can use this ability after the results are revealed. Whoever rerolls a roll must take the result of the second roll, even if it is lower.

    Unstoppable (Su): At 8th Tier, you can Expend three motes as a free action to immediately end any one of the following conditions currently affecting you: bleed, blind, confused, cowering, dazed, dazzled, deafened, entangled, exhausted, fascinated, fatigued, frightened, nauseated, panicked, paralyzed, shaken, sickened, staggered, or stunned. All other conditions and effects remain, even those resulting from the same spell or effect that caused the selected condition. You can use this ability at the start of your turn even if a condition would prevent you from acting.

    Legendary Hero (Su): At 10th Tier, you have reached the height of Exalted power. You regain motes at a rate of 3 per hour, in addition to regaining your full pool after resting.


    Gaining Tiers

    A character's knowledge and growth as an Exalt is classified by Tier, with a 1st-Tier Exalt already being significantly more powerful than an un-Exalted character of the same level and a 10th-Tier Exalt having power equal to that of the greatest of the gods. Mythic Tiers are similar to levels in a class or prestige class in that the powers gained at each Tier are added to all those that came before, but Tiers are gained in a different manner from levels. A character gains a new Mythic Tier by completing a number of trials that occur during play. A trial is a difficult task that adds to the legend and story of your character. Achieving a new Mythic Tier occurs independently of Experience point progression (though you will also gain Experience points for the various encounters you complete as you progress through your trials).

    Trials are moments in the story when you must rise above the deeds of lesser heroes. These moments become critical junctures in your legend. The GM decides what qualifies as a trial, and it's up to you to complete it as you would other adventures. You might not even know you are attempting a trial until it is completed and the GM informs you to note it on your character sheet, though you'll likely get an inkling when you find yourself facing a particularly challenging foe or attempting something that most would find impossible.

    The number of trials required to attain each new Tier appears on Table: Trials per Tier. For Example, suppose a 5th-level fighter receives Exaltation and becomes a 1st-Tier Dawn Caste. Over a number of sessions, she earns enough Experience points to gain her 6th level of fighter. During this time, she doesn't complete a trial, so she doesn't advance to the next Dawn Caste Tier. During the next session, however, she engages in a climactic battle against a Fair Folk warlord that has been ruthlessly pillaging the countryside for slaves with a hobgoblin army; by defeating the Fae, she completes her trial, allowing her to become a 2nd-Tier Dawn Caste. To reach 3rd Tier, she will need to accomplish two more trials. A Solar can't gain more than 10 Tiers.

    Note that the number of trials needed to achieve the next Tier might vary from the number listed on Table: Trials per Tier.

    The GM can reduce or increase this number as needed to suit the campaign—refer to the Mythic Trials section of Mythic Adventures for more guidance.

    Solar Limit Breaks

    Note that some Limit Breaks can be dispelled early by magic such as calm emotions or remove curse, as detailed in their descriptions. Using magic to end a Limit Break early requires a DC 35 caster level check, and failure means the spell has no effect on the Exalt whatsoever. Anyone who attempts to magically end a Limit Break early is contesting Neverborn-level sorcery, after all.
    Limit Breaks and the Great Curse are not known qualities of Solar Exaltation, so any use of these spells on Solars in Limit Break needs to be spontaneous to avoid metagaming. For example, a spellcaster might think to cast calm emotions on an enraged Solar just as he might any suddenly, inexplicably furious ally.
    Mage’s disjunction and remove curse are always valid choices for this method, even if the Limit Break description in question does not specify any magic that can dispel it. Additionally, mage’s disjunction has a 5% chance to work in spite of a failed caster level check.
    Trying to avoid dispelling a Limit Break directly by simply assuming magical control over the Exalt in question (such as with dominate monster or geas) still requires the caster level check above, and does not end the Limit Break early, even if you are able to force them not to comply with its conditions for the duration. If allowed a save, a Solar in Limit Break will always choose to do so against such magic, and rolls any such saves with a +10 profane bonus.
    The simplest magical means to deal with a Solar in Limit Break may be to just incapacitate them. Solars under Limit Break do not gain any special defenses against spells like deep slumber, hold person or a good old fashion horrid wilting.

    Compassionate Martyrdom:
    For one hour after reaching limit break, you are wholly directed by a need to alleviate the suffering of others in the most direct and dramatic manner possible. Upon seeing an overseer beating slaves, for example, you will stand before the whip before allowing someone other than yourself to suffer.
    Partial control: On a successful Will save, you must still directly and immediately aid those who are suffering, but can choose to do so more effectively. In the above example, you could simply kill the overseer and set the slaves free.

    Heart of Tears:
    For one hour after reaching limit break, the grief and suffering you have witnessed in the world overwhelms you, and you can do nothing but weep pitiably. You cannot function for the remaining hour, and only attempt to flee if attacked or endangered. If you are unable to flee, you can deal with the problem directly, but with a -2 penalty to all attack rolls, saving throws, ability checks and skill checks, as well as a -4 penalty to your initiative.
    Partial control: If you pass your wills save you can defend yourself against danger normally but still take the penalties to rolls as listed above. You cannot be proactive or seek out danger or conflict, only react when it confronts you or your close allies in the vicinity directly.
    Dispel: calm emotions

    Red Rage of Compassion:
    You are enraged by the suffering you’ve seen for half an hour after Limit Break, and immediately attack the closest obvious cause. You enter a rage, as per the barbarian class feature, with an effective barbarian level equal to twice your Tier. Your rage overcomes sense or reason. You are unlikely to even perform simple actions such as putting down what you are holding or drawing a weapon before simply attacking unarmed or with whatever you happen to be holding. If there is no great cause of suffering in the immediate area, you instead move around erratically, smashing objects with no regard to their value and attacking anyone foolish enough to interfere. A rage granted by this ability lasts the entire duration, but you are fatigued as normal afterwards.
    Partial control: On a successful will save, you must still immediately choose to attack a source of suffering, but can choose to do so effectively. You can choose whether or not to enter a rage like a barbarian, and may draw a weapon, or drop, say, the corpse of the unjustly slain peasant you were cradling in your arms a moment ago, if either action allows you to destroy the source of your rage more effectively.
    Dispel: calm emotions

    Deliberate Cruelty:
    You become unspeakably cruel in pursuit of your goals for one hour after Limit Break. You are incapable of empathy for the duration. If you perceive any other creature’s weakness (real or imagined) interfering with your goals, you can only react with contempt. You will casually use torture, mutilation, and any other means at your disposal to instill fear and obedience. You suffer a -5 penalty to any Charisma-based check not related to fear or intimidation.
    Partial control: On a successful Will save, you can moderate your cruelty if necessary. You can show restraint and indifference when cruelty would obviously be less effective, and your penalty on social rolls decreases from -5 to -2 for the duration.

    Heart of Flint:
    You lose emotional capability all together for three hours after Limit Break. You don’t quite become an automaton: you only consider efficiency when considering your next course of action. As a result, your speech becomes tactless, and you take a -5 penalty on Charisma-based checks for the duration.
    Partial control: As above, but you suffer a -2 penalty instead of a -5 penalty on Charisma-based checks for the duration. Your emotions are simply suppressed rather than lost. Your primary motivation is still efficiency, but you can avoid doing things you have a strong emotional aversion toward.
    Dispel: heroism

    Ascetic Drive:
    For one hour after Limit Break, you must seek simplicity or penance. You shave your head, clad yourself in rags, or otherwise dress simply. You begin removing items that increase your AC or improve your ability scores and leave them where they fall. You give all currency on your person to the closest individual that looks like they need it (though you can be persuaded to give it to an ally through roleplay), and retreat somewhere away from worldly concerns. You will not take any action to increase your level of physical comfort, or make small talk on trivial matters.
    Partial control: On a successful Will save, you act as above, but do not need to flee from the world or remove magic items You still cannot accept any luxuries such as sleeping indoors (unless it’s very dangerous to do otherwise) or wearing anything but the most practical of clothes, such as armor.

    Contempt of the Virtuous:
    For one hour after limit break, any character flaws in other people disgust you, and you will do all within your power (including magic) to put them on the path toward righteousness. You are disdainful of anyone you’ve previously witnessed overindulging or acting without honor. If you see anything even remotely self-indulgent or dishonorable, you attempt to intervene, physically if necessary. You self-righteously (and loudly) urge everyone around you to turn away from sin or weakness and adopt a more honorable lifestyle.
    Partial control: You must prevent your immediate companions and subordinates from committing unrighteous or dishonorable acts, but not others. You disdain strangers or superiors you see as sinful, but only lecture them if you believe they are trying to tempt you, or if they ask for your opinion.

    Overindulgence:
    For one hour after limit break, you lose all sense of self control. You take to sex, drugs, alcohol and gluttony with equal vigor, and will not listen to reason or wait for a more appropriate moment for your debauchery. You indulge in all the vices available to you, regardless of expense or consequence. If you are unable to pay for these vices, you will attempt to borrow or steal until you can afford them.
    Partial control: You can be persuaded into performing the bare minimum of what duty and obligation require of you; such as commanding an army or pursuing an enemy. With any time you can spare, however, you will indulge in any vices that are available to you in that instant. You can avoid drinking on duty, but not being exceptionally hung over. You will always say yes when directly offered a vice for free.
    Dispel: Any spell that grants a direct bonus to Will saving throws.

    Berserk Anger:
    For half an hour after Limit Break, you enter a rage, using twice your Tier as your effective barbarian level. Furthermore, if something moves, you will try to kill it. You start with obviously hostile creatures in your vicinity, but after they are dead, you move onto bystanders, and if you finish them off, your own companions. If you run out of creatures to attack, your surroundings draw your ire; you attempt to sunder or break any objects within reach as you look for more blood to spill. A rage granted by this Limit Break always lasts for the duration, and you are fatigued afterwards as normal.
    Partial control: On a successful will save, you can avoid attacking allies (as long as they don’t get in your way) and those you are committed or sworn to protect. Anyone else, however, remains a target. You also needn’t attack inanimate objects and, in lieu of other targets, you can choose to move to an area where your anger will be productive.
    Dispel: calm emotions

    Foolhardy Contempt:
    You become senselessly courageous for two hours after Limit Break. You gain a +15 profane bonus on saves against fear. You always place yourself in the front lines of battle, challenge the most powerful-seeming enemy you can see to single combat, accept any and all dangerous missions, and will never retreat under any circumstances.
    Partial control: On a successful Will save, you are still without fear, but not without sense. You can retreat or avoid battle if tactically inadvisable. You still do not take personal risk into consideration when forming a plan, however. Your profane bonus on saving throws above is reduced from a +15 to a +5.
    Dispel: Any magical fear effect, though you must fail your save, if applicable.

    Universal Dominion Abilities:
    A Solar may select any one of these Dominion Abilities in lieu of gaining an out-of-Caste Dominion Ability.

    Extra Mythic Feat (Ex): You gain an extra mythic feat. You can take this ability a number of times equal to half your mythic tier (minimum 1). Each time you do, you gain another mythic feat.

    Legendary Item (Ex): You gain a legendary item. This item grants a number of abilities equal to your tier (maximum 3). At 3rd tier, you can select this ability again, increasing the maximum to six abilities and causing the item to become a lesser artifact. At 6th tier, you can select this ability again, increasing the maximum to 10 abilities; the item then becomes a greater artifact.

    Terrifying Visage (Ex): When your Anima banner is at the 3rd intensity or higher, you gain a bonus on your intimidate checks equal to your Tier. You may spend 3 motes as a swift action to make an intimidate check against all hostile creatures that can see you.

    Solar Dominion Ability: Every Tier, you learn one Solar Dominion Ability. These Dominion Abilities can be from your own Caste or any other Solar Caste. Starting at the 1st Tier and every three Tiers thereafter (4th, 7th, and 10th) this Dominion Ability must be a Dawn Dominion Ability, or a mythic feat with a style feat as a prerequisite. Note that some Dominion Abilities are spell-like abilities, unless stated otherwise, these Dominion Abilities function at a Caster Level equal to twice your Tier. The Dawn Caste Dominions are Archery, War, Martial Arts, Melee, and Thrown.


    Archery Dominion Abilities

    There Is No Wind (Su): Prerequisites: Far Shot (feat)
    As a standard action and by spending 4 motes, you make a single flawless ranged attack. You take no penalties from range to this attack roll (though you must still fire within your weapon’s maximum range) gain darkvision up to your weapon’s maximum range before selecting your target, and ignore concealment. You also ignore unfavorable winds and other kinds of natural weather that might negatively impact your shot. By spending two additional motes, you may ignore even supernatural wind and weather such as a wind wall spell. At 4th Tier, you may spend two additional motes on this Dominion Ability and fire this shot at any target you can see, even one beyond your weapon’s maximum range.

    Accuracy Without Distance (Sp): Prerequisites: There Is No Wind, Base Attack +2.
    As a free action, you may spend three motes and gain the benefits of the true strike spell on your next ranged attack.

    Forceful Arrow (Sp):Prerequisites: There Is No Wind
    By spending 2 motes as a standard action you, make an attack with a ranged weapon and imbue your ammunition with a powerful force effect. If you hit a creature’s touch AC they are immediately subject to a bull rush using your CMB. If you hit a creature’s normal AC they are subject to the bull rush plus the normal damage from the ranged attack. At 6th Tier you may select this ability again. If you do, you can use this ability as a ranged attack action instead of a standard action.

    Trance of Unhesitating Speed (Su):Prerequisites: Base attack +2
    When you make a full-attack action, you can make up to half your Tier (minimum 1) in additional ranged attacks by spending 2 motes per attack. Each attack you make in this way has a cumulative -1 penalty to the attack roll (-1 for the first attack, -2 for the second, and so on).

    Arrow Storm Technique (Su):
    Prerequisites: Trance of Unhesitating Speed, Base Attack +5, Tier 3
    As a full round action and by spending 6 motes, you may make a ranged attack against a number of creatures or objects within range and line-of-sight equal to 4 + your Tier. Make one attack roll and compare it to the AC of all your targets, this attack hits every target with an AC equal to or lower than your attack roll. You must have separate ammunition equal to your number of targets (though you may use Phantom Arrow Technique, as normal). If this attack roll threatens a critical, it only threatens a critical on one target (your choice).

    Essence Arrow Attack (Su): Whenever you make an attack that uses ammunition, you can spend 2 motes to give your ammunition an enhancement bonus equal to your Tier, up to a maximum bonus of +5 (not including weapon qualities). You can apply any of the following weapon qualities to ammunition you enhance this way, holy, flaming, axiomatic,flaming burst, shocking, shocking burst, demon bane, devil bane, daemon bane, fey bane, magical beast bane, aberration bane, or undead bane. Adding these properties consumes an amount of the enhancement bonus equal to the property’s base price modifier (see Table 15-9 on page 469 of the Core Rulebook). Unlike the normal rules for adding weapon qualities to ammunition, ammunition you enhance in this way does not need a minimum enhancement bonus of +1 before you start adding weapon qualities.

    Phantom Arrow Technique (Sp):Prerequisites: Essence Arrow Attack
    Whenever you make an attack that uses ammunition, you can spend one mote per piece of ammunition you would normally need to fire a sparkling phantom version of your normal ammunition as a spell-like ability. This Dominion Ability can be used to create non-magical, mechanically-simple, thrown ammunition you are proficient with, even if you do not have any of that ammunition on hand. This Dominion Ability is a conjuration (creation) effect. Ammunition created by this Dominion Ability fades from existence after inflicting its damage.

    Summoning the Loyal Weapon (Sp): Prerequisites: Phantom Arrow Technique or Call the Blade
    As a move action, you can banish a weapon you are carrying and are proficient with to a special plane called Elsewhere for one mote. You can summon any weapon you’ve banished in this way by spending one as a move action (or as part of a move action if you have a Base Attack Bonus of +1 or higher, or as a free action if you have the Quick Draw feat). Both of these powers are conjuration (summoning) effects. This is an Archery Dominion Ability if you use Phantom Arrow Technique as the prerequisite and a Melee Dominion Ability if you use Call the Blade as the prerequisite.

    Immaculate Golden Bow (Sp):Prerequisites: Summoning the Loyal Weapon, Phantom Arrow Technique.
    By spending 3 motes as a move action, you conjure a golden composite bow of pure Essence with an appearance based on your personality or preference. Each time you use this Dominion Ability you choose either a short composite bow, or a long composite bow. This weapon has hit points equal to 4 + your Tier, hardness 10 + your Tier, and an enhancement bonus equal to half your Tier (minimum 1) and the adaptiveUC weapon quality. This weapon fades from existence after a number of hours equal to your caster level. This Dominion Ability is a conjuration (creation) effect.

    Rain of Feathered Death (Sp): Prerequisites: Manyshot, Phantom Arrow Technique, Arrow Storm Technique, 6th Tier.
    By spending 4 motes you increase the number of arrows you can fire with Manyshot on your next attack by 2 at sixth Tier and 3 at 9th Tier.


    Melee Dominion Abilities

    Hungry Tiger Technique (Ex): Prerequisites: Tier 1,
    You know by how many degrees of 5 you exceeded your opponents AC by whenever you do so with a melee attack. Additionally, when you hit with a melee attack, you can spend two motes per five on your attack roll that exceeded your target’s AC. For every 2 motes spent in this way, your attack does an extra 1d6 points of precision damage, which stacks with other sources of precision damage (such as Sneak Attack), up to a maximum of 1d6/Tier (plus all precision damage from other sources). Like other sources of precision damage, this damage is not multiplied on a critical hit.

    Fire and Stones Strike(Ex):Prerequisites: Hungry Tiger Technique or Falling Icicle Strike
    Regardless of the alien nature of your foe, you can always find a way to hit something important. You can choose to spend 3 motes whenever you successfully hit with an attack. If you do, that attack ignores Damage Reduction and affects creatures immune to precision damage as if they weren’t immune to precision damage.

    Peony Blossom Attack (Ex):Prerequisites: Base attack +2
    When you make a full-attack action, you can make an additional number of melee attacks, up to half your Tier (minimum 1) in additional attacks by spending 2 motes per attack. Each additional attack you make in this way has a stacking -1 penalty to the attack roll (-1 for the 1st attack, -2 for the 2nd, and so on).

    Iron Whirlwind Attack (Ex):Prerequisites: Improved Two-Weapon Fighting (feat) or Whirlwind Attack (feat); 3rd Tier
    You gain Fleet Warrior, as per the Champion Path Ability:
