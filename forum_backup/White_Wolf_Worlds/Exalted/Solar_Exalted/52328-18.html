When a core component of the Zenith was that all prayers were at -1 difficulty and they were called the priests of the Solars, when the (pre-errata) Malefactor anima was all about consecrating an area for easier prayer to the Yozi via personal prayer, it seemed bizarre to have there be no charms that helped it along shy of a basic excellency and, in the case of Malefactors, additional consecration. So, when asked what I would want Performance to be able to do in a new edition, right near the top of my list is definitely 'that thing the castes of Performance were supposed to be specifically specialized in would be good, thanks.'

                So, I suppose my questions are, will rolling Performance for prayer still be a thing, or will it be moved to a different Ability or removed as a roll entirely? Will Zeniths still count as automatic priests, along with the benefits regarding prayer, as they've been in past editions, or are you shifting much more to them being the core figures of religions in their own right? If it's the latter rather than the former, will you include charms that allow Zeniths to lead prayers to themselves to, say, increase their Cult rewards for the scene, or gain other benefits? Are there any other details about what you've already decided Performance and Zeniths will involve or feature that we might need to be clued in to in order to make more educated suggestions, or does that just about cover it?

                Oh, and thanks for the time spent collecting feedback and clarifying points for the community. I always appreciate developers doing this kind of thing while the product is still in development.

                        Please note that if you dropped Michael Jackson into the outskirts of Creation and had him dance, the effect would not be "wow cool, he can dance really well." It would shake the foundations of belief. They would think he was a divine madman, surely touched by the gods. His dancing would provoke mania, fear, rage, awe. The people of Creation have never seen such movements before. They would be provoked by them. They would conclude that he was possessed by demons or by the spirits of the air and sky. If he claimed "I am the representative of X god," they would be far more inclined to believe him, because he dances as if possessed.

                        Dance, oration, singing—the heart of Performance—invokes the divine madman. It leads people to believe the person speaks / acts / embodies Heaven. Gods want priests who have high Performance because people think these things about people who can dance, speak passionately before large crowds, etc. This idea makes them want to listen to that person, it makes them want to believe what that person says. And if the god truly backs such a one, that person can stand in for him, run a bureaucracy and do all his temporal work while he soaks up the prayer.

                            Originally posted by John Mørke View Post

                            This is not a change, tbh. Zeniths are still priest-kings because every god would want one leading his religion. But calling a Zenith a priest is like calling the Terminator a bodyguard or an assassin and leaving it there. It is a bit of a simplification. You note yourself that in 2e, prayer was not supported and doesn't really do anything concrete. That's not an oversight. It was that way in 1e as well. Meanwhile the Zenith Charms point to the Zenith being the beneficiary of divine ardor, might, primacy, and worship.

                            Think of it this way. If a person claims he is a police officer, you ask to see his badge. In Creation, claiming "I am a priest" requires similar verification. You say "I'm a priest," I ask "Where's your khakkhara?" Or maybe "Where's your robes? Where's your beads?" etc. But John the Baptist, naked and smeared with dirt and honey, with the legs of a locust caught in his beard, claims no such title, and is backed by no organization, and yet he invokes the divine. You may question his authority, but you don't question his position.
                            I understand entirely where you're coming from, but the simple fact that Zeniths got a decrease in all prayer roll difficulties - the equivalent of one free automatic success - suggests to me that they were supposed to be able to pray, get heard, and get a response accordingly, and that the interaction was then largely forgotten about for more cinematic uses of charms because people find receiving power from an external source underwhelming. There was clear space in their direction of design and inherent bonuses to interact with that system, it was just never explored; it was design space squandered.

                            I find it encouraging that it seems that won't be a thing at all for them in 3e, because it means there won't be any issue of, 'Well, here's a prayer system, and Zeniths' anima gives them a bonus for it the same way a Dawn's anima gives them a bonus for combat and an Eclipse's for spiritual diplomacy, but the prayer interaction isn't even remotely as explored as normal physical or social combat.' It's encouraging because instead of it being a tantalizing teaser of a system that the caste could or even should be using, but lacks any real means or incentive, it's just going to be largely avoided for a different role entirely. The Zeniths will be central figures of a faith, and the recipients of those prayers, rather than guiding prayers as they were enabled to do in 2e.

                            At any rate, I'm hoping that means there will be charms that do what I said above. Charms that boost their benefits from their cult, or charms that let them turn the force of prayer from a cult led by them against a creature of darkness, warding their holy temples consecrated in their name and image from intrusion by unholy spirits. Charms that make them Nazarene and Buddha, rather than just the Pied Piper. So, I still want prayer charms, just of a very different bent. Even if they're changing from being the givers of prayer to the recipients, that still means prayer should play a role for them.

                            And if that's just a suite of charms that gives them benefits for having an audience? That's fine too. Whether they're getting an influx of essence motes from giving the sermon on the mount or performing a ballet for a filled house, they can both be built on the same charms. Whether you're chanting a prayer with your warrior-priests to steel them before a battle or raising your sword for a battle cry and for the war drums to start, giving your viking troops bonuses before a battle, they can both be built on the same charms.

                            But whether they're prophets or priests, prayer should play some role, have some meaningful mechanics and interactions and display. If being a prophet is as central to their concept as being a priest supposedly was - and by all appearances, by the universal bonuses they got to praying, it really was supposed to be much more central, at least at some point - then there should still be some reason to court faith, worship, and prayer - for yourself and others - aside from just regular maintenance on the Cult background, or 3e's equivalent.

                            It just seems, I don't know, strange that a caste characterized as holy men have more reason to be bards and statesmen than high priests or living gods. Strange to say the least.
