    Solars are Heroes.

    03-08-2014, 09:52 PM
    In the sense that they are good people performing good deeds in order to make the world a better place. Solarness is not about beating down everyone else with your glorious, golden dick.

    Some Solars will not be heroes, but those are exceptions.

    Heroes can have flaws. Heroes can live in complex worlds and have to grapple with difficult decisions. Heroes can have adversaries who are black-hearted villains or who are sympathetic, noble opponents with contrary goals.

    It's fine to look at the ways heroes fail or the ways people with power aren't heroic. I am not saying that playing a Solar who is a hero is better than playing one who is not. The problem is when we start talking about the edge as though it's the center. Exalted is not a game about how humanity is horrible, heroism is a lie, and the PC is just another asshole clawing is way to the top. Rather, Creation is a setting where humans (and non-humans) can be horrible, heroism can be false, and there are many assholes clawing their way to the top.

    That's why it needs Solars. Bringing light to the darkness is only meaningful if it's dark.

                                      If I understand correctly, 3e is taking us away from the notion of an Exaltation as an ineffable, autonomous construct that Exalts people for reasons nobody can quite nail down. Instead, each Exalt is literally Chosen by an Incarna. What this implies, if true, is that people who become Solars almost certainly have demonstrated some intent or drive that makes it reasonable to expect they will become a force for good in the world.

                                      In any case, that's certainly a requirement of player characters in my games. I am not interested in running a game about shameless assholes with world-shaking power. I am interested in a game where the Age of Sorrows is confronted by divinely empowered people who want to do good. Any argument predicated on Solars being generally evil (or even generally assholes) is not useful to any Exalted game I will ever run.

                                      The archaic definition of "hero" which UltraFennec notes applies to all Exalted. Literally every person who Exalts has at least one Virtue rated high enough to compel some kind of heroic mode of action. Solars are a class above that. Where Dragon-Blooded don't do anything to earn their Exaltations, Sidereals are defined from birth by their Exaltations, and Lunars are offered Exaltation simply for the will to survive, Solars are supposed to be people who are not only great, but good. The Unconquered Sun doesn't Choose people for being selfish assassins, no matter how many Ability dots they have and how high their Conviction is. He doesn't make merchant-princes living the unexamined life into Eclipses, no matter how high their Socialize and Bureaucracy are. He looks into the souls of the great, and once in a while, he says "yes, you are a person who will use My power to make the world a better place". That's who Solars are.

                                      And I'd go one further than that. I'd say that characterisation of Solars is what Exalted is for. At least, it's the thing about Exalted that makes it most attractive to me. Because Exalted sets up a world full of existential threats, terrible injustices, and corruption in every court, and then it knocks that world down by saying "except the greatest power in the world is reserved for the good guys". Exalted is a statement that hope and idealism can triumph. Exalted is a statement that things can get better, even if only through concerted and extraordinary effort and dedication.

                                      The nature of Exaltation as an irrevocable and unassailable gift means that any Exalt can start out as a good guy and then turn into something else. Exalts are human. But whatever happens to a Solar after their Exaltation, I agree strongly with hippokrene: the default state of a Solar is Person Who Sets Things Right.

                                                  I think there's a widespread meme that Good Is Boring, and that's why people want Solars to be nigh-inescapably disposed toward being shitheads. And I don't agree with that notion.

                                                  The thing about the Unconquered Sun is that he embodies every aspect of good, despite many of those aspects conflicting with each other when applied to the real world. Different Solars will very frequently have different ideas about what it means to be good, and view each other's actions in different moral frameworks. The Eclipse who accumulates power in order to ultimately free the Threshold from the grip of the Guild is good; the Twilight who binds elementals into dangerous servitude to fight the Fair Folk in her quest to protect Creation from dissolution is good; the Zenith who starts a bloody revolution to build a free and equal society is good. All three of these people could think the other two are wasting their time. All three could accuse the other two of doing something outright immoral! Good comes into conflict with good all the time, and that's why it's more interesting to me that Solars are inherently good than that they subvert the bland-good-guy stereotype by actually being inevitably terrible.

                                                          I don't want them to be indisposed toward being shitheads, but neither do I think that the default condition for them should be 'chosen for being good people who want to do good deeds.' That's just as boring as the default condition being 'chosen for being bad people who want to do bad deeds.'

                                                          I don't think the setting is very interesting if the default condition for the Exalted, Solars, Lunars, whichever, is based on the idea that they must have a drive to make the world a better place. Part of what draws me to the game is that not only do I, as a player, have the option to play an anti-hero in truth or even a villainous person given 'righteous' power, but that the setting backs that up as well.

                                                          Default statements ARE important, and at least to me it's very important that that default statement NOT be slanted toward any particular angle except one: the need to be a driven individual willing to use the power they're given. Whether that becomes good, evil (and what are those but defined by people?), or anything in between, whether it's world-shaking or completely self-absorbed, should be up to the people at the table, not the book.
