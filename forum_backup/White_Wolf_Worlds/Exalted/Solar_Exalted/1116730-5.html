    Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
    Great Cthulhu: Yeah, but "DBs/Sidereals are actively watching the tomb to know when it gets successfully looted, and call the Wyld Hunt themselves" is a very different story from the one posited, "people notice the tomb has been looted, local leaders get interested, and from there, the Wyld Hunt may get called."
    Both stories exist to provoke drama and action in a player character's future. They are equally valid means of doing so. But the point is that information of the tomb robbery can get out, either quickly or slowly, through active surveillance or chance and rumors. Solar tombs and their contents are important enough that people are paying attention to the events and rumors surrounding them. Short of a tomb where all knowledge has been lost tomb robbing them will always be risky and attention getting if done wrong.

    (Also, it still doesn't answer how the observers would know it got looted, unless they are conveniently there to see with their eyes a guy walk in and then back out a day later, glowing, arms heaped with orichalcum. The tombs tend to have very potent defenses, after all, so unless the act of robbing it changes the landscape, or sets free an angry spirit, or whatever, how's the observer able to tell someone made off with the daiklave at the center?)
    I provided three examples in my previous post but here goes.

    1. Patrols by people with the skill and interest to be competent at their jobs. They might not swing by for a while but presumably they will know enough about the tombs to do their job, look for signs of intruders (campsites, footprints, residual magical effects, witnesses), deploy Charms as needed to support their task, etc. Sid agents and DB shikari patrols were both mentioned as having the motivation, the knowledge, and the skill and magical support to be effective at their job. You can easily expand this list if needed.

    2. Passive magical wards and spells that will alert the appropriate people about potential robbers snooping around the tombs. These don't even have to be part of the original structure. Perhaps they were added late to the surroundings to save on manpower issues or to provide an immediate alarm and response. It need not be Exalted or whatever doing this either. Mortals could place fetishes doing the same thing since many would have an interest in the loot themselves.

    3. Local spies. Minor gods, local peasants or tribesmen, animals, etc would be in a good position to note any unusual activity and report to their paymasters.

    I'm sure you can come up with other ways besides these three examples.

    And I have to point out that none of these have to immediately show the tomb has been robbed nor do you absolutely need to see Glowing Solar Joe walking out of a busted tomb door shining with orichalcum hardware in order to suspect that a robbery has occurred. Strangers showing unusual interest, a broken tomb door, signs of heavy excavation, residual magics, disturbed spirits, old campsites where nobody goes, unusual artifacts ending up on the local market, and more are all valid evidence of the threat to the tomb or the aftermath of a tomb robbery. And I'll emphasize that Investigation Charms are an excellent way of putting two and two together once you know where to look.
