            Humanity only at its best, with what it can do at its worst shown as an "exception", "aberration" or mistake to me shows a fairly naive and simplistic view of things. It also simply shows humanity as caricature, rather than humanity as the diverse, nuanced and complicated thing that it relaly is. People aren't altruistic saints. They aren't malicious assholes.             Note folks don't advocate Solars need to be evil bastards. I'm rejecting Solars are inherently comic book good guys. Solars are people. And you judge people on the basis of what a person they chose to be and act. The thing that makes them glow a lot and lets them act on things most people don't get to shoulnd't have a criterion on how it can be used or by whom. It just needs to be such that it is able to be used and that the person who has it will use it. Otherwise the game's tagline (What legends will they tell of your deeds?) in part is meaningless if we know a priori it is "Probably good guy shit."

            Exalted isn't a game where you are going ot have Supermans and Darkseids. It's a game where you are going to have Conan, Jubei, David and Sinbad. People who are great in the deeds they might do to help some people and awful in what they do that destroy people or themselves.

                                            Originally posted by Quimeradc View Post
                                            Would it be safe to say all solars are heroes to someone, either willingly or unwillingly and most solar tend to be good people?
                                            I would say almost all Solars are heroes to someone, and most Solars tend to exemplify at least one virtue, as has always been the case. In 3e, there will be a little more freedom to explore various virtues, as there will no longer be the overly reductive 4-virtue model; Courage/Valor is obviously going to be a common one from the classical assortment, as is Temperance. It's also good not to ignore Indignation: I can see a Solar being Exalted who gets destructively, violently angry, but usually does it when it is appropriate. Generosity/Magnanimity is also worth thinking about.

                                            Are Solars "good" people? Well, what is the "good?" That is a question thousands of years old at this point, and it is still debated today. Most Solars do have at least one character trait that is considered good, and exemplify it, often to the maximum. Mostly, I disagree if this is used to deduce they tend to run around doing good deeds most of the time; I think it is better if that question is left more open-ended.

                                            Whether a deed is "good" or not is difficult to determine, and trying to make it easy, I think, would take away a lot of nuance. It wouldn't be surprising for a group of Solars to kill or exile a tyrannical Southern Dynastic Satrap. But what about everyone earning income through trading with the Realm? What happens when Wong Bongerok's goons join forces with the Old Gods to set up a new hierarchy over the people? What happens when the Wyld Hunt is gone and strange Exalts begin moving in and carving out their own fiefdoms? Acts which are good and justified in isolation can have consequences that are far worse than acts of petty evil, and Exalted, with its emphasis on sociology, economics, and anthropology, is positively begging people to consider the ramifications of "good" actions.

                                            Solars will, in most cases, fight the power structures that perpetuate evil in the setting and lead the re-creation of a new status quo. That is their principle role. However, the question of whether things can get better or whether there is a constant struggle to keep things from getting even worse seems to be an important and open-ended one. To say that the Solars can beat up the bad guys and make everything better is both superficially true and dubious on a deeper level.
