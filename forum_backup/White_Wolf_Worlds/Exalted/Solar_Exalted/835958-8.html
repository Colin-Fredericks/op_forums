    Ryoki, I think you need to stop challenging your Dawn with fair fights. He's a powerhouse, so any NPC who wants to take him down will not fight fair. Focus on range attacks and play keep away if possible. Your Dawn can't hit them if he can't find/reach him. Make the opponent another Solar if you have to get access to the Athletics charms. Solars don't have to be good guys. Abyssals had a really annoying short ranged teleport charm in Ex2, so if you are up to Homebrew you can roll up an Abyssal instead.

    Or be honest with your Dawn. "Dude, this is a new system and the rest of the splat books aren't out yet. I can barely make challenging opponents at your current power level much less when you get more combat charms."

        Originally posted by Grindavikur View Post
        While your specific charm example matters a lot less than your overall point (Which I agree with), my players proposed an easy set of solutions for that problem, ranging from just giving minions incomplete/false knowledge, to "Hold on, IQT says they answer truthfully and to their fullest knowledge, but doesn't specify how. They could answer in riddles or ciphers, code, or even languages the character doesn't speak/slang they have no knowledge of".
        This is a great suggestion. In the Vorkosigan series by Lois McMaster Bujold, the main character Miles is not immune to that universe version of truth serum, usually spies are made allergic to it so they die if it is used on them instead of spilling their secrets. Miles did not go through that treatment, but because of the circumstances of his life, his body responds to the serum differently.
        He still has to tell truth, but he has total control about how he tells it. So he starts telling the truth, then strays off talking about related topics, that brings memories of some songs so he starts singing, etc.

        On a more practical note how to beat the IQT, how about a chamber with a working on it, that will erase all your memories about your plans once you leave it and when you come back, it will restore them? You can make notes for yourself about what you want to do when you leave the chamber, but IQT will only get those details, without ruining the whole plan.
        Why you can even make fake notes for yourself to throw people with IQT off.

        The schema using unrelated crimes for distractions is another great idea. It works even better if your player has Defining/Major intimacy towards solving puzzles and mysteries like that. Even if he knows what his opponent is doing, it should trigger limit rolls.
        (I have recently read Mistborn: The Bands of Mourning by Brandon Sanderson where the main villain used exactly this strategy for years to distract the main character.)

        EDIT: Use Defend Other on the important NPCs by BG is a good strategy how to help them avoid the grapple. Combine it with ranged attacks by another BG/NPCs and it should become challenging fight for the Dawn.
        Sometimes enemies could try to use poison to weaken him. Either on blade or in food/drinks.

        To solve the power discrepancies, you will have to split the group a bit during the combat, giving them different objectives. The Dawn could take care of the main villain and his elites, while the rest of the group takes care of the guards to free the prisoners.

        You will also have to diversify your story if you want to accommodate the social as well as the combat oriented players. The Dawn will feel pretty useless in the Twilights forge or during some treaty negotiation.

            I tried with battlegroups... Though in one encounter that was supposed to be a challenge the big bad got destroyed by the Dawn before the battlegroup could engage him or his team (the group got the jump so the BG needed 2 rounds to close distance). Mobility Charms are strong.

            Also if I use the encounter you proposed it would result in 2 deaths. Everybody but the Dawn would be screaming bloody murder at me. The problem is not challenging the Dawn. The problem is challenging the solar without killing the other two that are nowhere near as strong in a fight.
            I find that it helps to communicate to my players out of character that there are some fights better left to the specialist, and then making it very very obvious what fights those are. For example, in my last game a bunch of things were attacking this dynast town all at once, but the Terrestrials were holding their own and keeping their cool until they heard the Full Moon Lunar approaching, at which point they simultaneously lost it and started evacuating. My Dawn picked up on that loud and clear, and so did the rest of the party who had plenty of other things to do (never leave someone with nothing to do, they will get bored and try to help) who let him go duel the big bad without much discussion.

            It's only cheating a little bit. Characters will eventually notice a power discrepancy this big, and letting the PCs know about it means they don't have to learn this lesson the hard way.

            As for the Dawn, if you are having trouble competing with him in the one thing he wins at throw other challenges at him. Can he lie or detect lies? Pair him off with a trickster who fools him into beating up his rivals. Make good use of his intimacies. Put him in situations he could punch his way out of but has good reason not to, like having the bad guy be a tyrannical dictator protected by legions of drafted citizen guards who just want to go home and feed their families. Sometimes the best stories in Exalted don't involve dice, just hard character decisions.

How to both let the PC be the brilliant detective he is while still giving him a nemesis he can't unravel with trivial ease? Beyond that, it threatens to make a major part of the character moot in the future, too - what's the point of playing a brilliant detective when you can just make anybody confess right away? The Maltese Falcon would be a much shorter novel if Sam Spade knew Investigation Charms.
                It's a Terrestrial Working to grant a mutation - have the demon grant himself a very fantastical version of multiple personality disorder. The dominant personality is the one the player knows, but the second, subversive personality bubbles up when he's not around, and the dominant personality goes to sleep, leaving blank spots in its recollections but permitting it to answer questions like, "Did you call up a nephwrack to eat my children?" with "No, of course not!"

                Of course, when the PC inevitably demands, "Have you done anything to sabotage me," the proper response is a belly laugh and, "Everything I can think of!"

                        I wouldn't allow the demon to defeat the spirit of Irresistible Questioning Technique, I think. If the player wanted that story and wanted to use Irresitible Questioning Technique on the demon, it seems like the best way to go is just to have the demon be sincere when called for, like "Okay, okay, I admit it: I'm never going to stop attempting to corrupt you and I intend to either make you useful to me or destroy you in the process. Fair cop. On the other hand, you're the one with all the power in this relationship, I can't make you do anything you don't want to, and there are many things you need that I can provide for you, so it sounds like a pretty good deal, right?" In other words, you need to rely on a form of corruption that doesn't fail when it's exposed.

                        This is a bit atypical, and it's harder to pull off, but it seems like the only way to be true to the nature of the story and the character -- to make it about the lure of corruption even to someone whose eyes are open, and the dangers of hubris in assuming you're safe. Hannibal didn't actually lie to people very much, and they knew he was a son of a bitch. His power was in insight. So give the demon a bunch of Read Intentions kit, Charisma to go with their Manipulation, and make them good at finding the things that could make becoming a pawn of the Yozis or adopting the demon's worldview appealing to the Solar, and good at pitching those things at opportune moments. In some respects, I think it'd be similar to playing a Shadow in Wraith?

                                                            I should clarify: this is only a problem in Ex3 when you have a HUGE power gap between enemies, like the power gap many are seeing between their focused combat Dawns and everyone else. So my advice here is really only applicable if you have someone head and shoulders above the other antagonists like Ahlat fighting alongside individual Brides. But since I strongly recommend making something that powerful in order to entertain your focused combatant, this shouldnt be all that uncommon an occurance.

                                                            Another consideration is whether or not your combatant has an onslaught negator. Traditionally these are cheap and solars can easily afford to keep one active every turn, but without one pack tactics are still quite effective in Ex3. Basically, I would say that either an onslaught negator or 20+ soak, or both, are what will make the difference between a fight that your whole party can help with and a fight your non-combatants should really let the Dawn handle.
