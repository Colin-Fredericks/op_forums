If you'd spent those two motes on an Excellency, you probably could have made a real Read Intentions action, learning either more nuanced information about what they're thinking ("what is their reaction to looking at the host of the party?"), or that they're an exceptionally-skilled liar (when you can easily get 4+ successes, failure can be more revealing than success... assuming you don't auto-succeed). And it gets even less useful when you learn what an unseen cook was feeling hours ago. Even in a hospitality situation, it won't tell you whether overwhelming joy from the farmers taking you in means you've got kind people, or Misery Graves.

                I like the idea of the Charm! But I'd struggle to justify it as anything other than cool fluff if it were free, Permanent, and explicitly called out that it identifies all ingredients (for both showing off and resolving the poison thing). As-is, I am - as ever! - glad to hear that people are having fun in their games, but it looks super narrow to me. Hum. Maybe an upgrade, to let it pick up on a random Intimacy? That moves its usefulness into the realm of GM fiat (since a "random" intimacy could always be something dull and trivial, even from a personal rival), but makes it a flavorful way to hand off information, at least.

                                        From a certain point of view, the answer to the question of wanting Solars to stake out the best design space is... kind of yes.

                                        In the past, it was for the case of establishing the credibility of Solars as the mightiest of the Exalted, and would be the kind of thing that would diminish over time as developing new Exalted caused the Charm technology to evolve without them. I understand that one of the big complaints about Sidereals was that they had a lot of social effects that the core system had not really accounted for, so it made Solars look weaker both in the sense that their own social options were crude or limited, and they lacked an effective way to preserve themselves in the face of it, neither of which really seem like they would fit the Solar image.

                                        So in terms of mechanics, one of the things they were going for is trying to draft out how others can work ahead of time so that Solars could be preconceived with ways to interact with them.

                                            I'm going to ignore the loud assertions that I am wrong about things I never said because...yeah.

                                            Instead, let us consider the possibility that Solar essence expresses itself in particular ways that can be unsurpassed without being all-encompassing. Brawl was mentioned and is a good example. The Solar Brawler is nigh-unapproachable in expressing raw physical violence. By such a measure he is unequivocally the best - even if there are certain smaller component aspects of combat that do not find expression in the Charm set! Does this mean that no other character type is allowed to explore that space in their Brawl tree? No, of course not. And in fact this is a good thing, because it allows Brawl to be explored in distinctive ways by other Exalts without threatening the supremacy of the Solar as king of the ring. He can beat up anybody, even if he doesn't know every conceivable move. He is the best but can still be bounded by the ways in which his essence naturally expresses itself.

                                            Now consider a hypothetical case: War. I have seen more than a few complaints that War offers great options at the strategic level but few (or no) options at the small-group or Circle level. Leave aside the fact that Battle Group rules kick in at three Followers for the moment and let's simply take this observation at face value for the sake of argument. It is typically presented as somehow evidence of a shortcoming in the tree, i.e. "I can command an army but not an elite team of dudes, wtf devs?!". But...is it an oversight? Or is it perhaps instead an implicit statement to the effect that the essence of the greatest God of War in Heaven, when wielded by his Chosen, simply doesn't do the small stuff. After all, Solars were raised up to be generals, not sergeants. What if Solars don't have small-unit tactics Charms because they're not supposed to. They are supposed to lead armies, not individual units, and their essence swells and expands to find expression at the grander scale rather than the lesser. This in no way makes them weaker - it reinforces the massive scale that such world-shaking heroes are supposed to operate at. If a Solar wants to beat the crap out of his enemies, he can either bring a mighty host against them or he can personally kick their asses straight to hell. The fact that his essence doesn't lend itself to small-scale teamwork may be simply...irrelevant. I can think of other Exalted who were intended to operate well at such scales, however, and I bet you can too.

                                            The Solars can be the best and the brightest and the strongest without necessarily being kitchen-sink repositories of every conceivable take on a given Ability. And I would argue that the game is better if this is the case. Not everyone will agree, I know. At some places and times the game has interpreted the nature of Solar essence as "unlimited" to apply not just to puissance but also to comprehensive breadth of potential, and some folks will be very invested in retaining this. I think it's worth considering the possibility that 3E might not be making that assumption. The Unconquered Sun is, after all, unsurpassed without being infinitely plastic in his power. Why would his Chosen be otherwise?

                                            I don't know if this is definitively the case, and I don't claim to. But perhaps Solars don't have to be capable of anything to be the best at everything.
