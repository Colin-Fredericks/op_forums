                                    Originally posted by Murcushio View Post

                                    Well, hmm.

                                    From my perspective, and this is me trying to do tea-leaf reading so it may be wrong, there are a lot of people who...

                                    1) Would like to play an Abyssal, but...

                                    2) Don't want them to turn into a Solar, for a variety of reasons including but not limited to rejecting the moral authority of the Unconqured Sun to declare that they're not evil and are in fact heroes,

                                    3) Don't want to pursue the annihilation of Creation, and

                                    4) Don't want to labor under the Abyssal Curse of everything they touch redounding to the ultimate benefit of the Neverborn.

                                    This has always kind of baffled me. The whole point of playing an Abyssal, it has always seemed to me, is that you agreed to the agenda of Oblivion, for whatever reason, and now you have to decide between either serving that agenda with your whole heart, or rejecting it and starting the long slow climb towards redemption and returning the Black Exaltation you bear to its true state, free of the taint of the Neverborn. There's always been a potential third path, where you decide to usurp the Deathlords and then just not pursue Oblivion, but the Underworld by its very nature serves Oblivion, so... yeah.
                                    From my perspective, and this may sound shallow, the point is to be a goth superhuman in Exalted's genre, who can invoke the power of death in support of any skill or disciple.

                                    The stuff about dooming the world and causing suffering to random innocent bystanders is not so interesting - "Woe, woe, must the taint of my power corrupt all that is good!".

                                    The real appeal of Abyssals to me do not seem really about that at its core, in terms of their primary draw, any more than being the appeal of being a fire aspect is that you doom the world to eventually be consumed by a fiery wasteland, or being a water aspect is about bringing the great flood or even how being a Fair Folk necessarily means being bound to inevitably dissolve Creation in the Wyld, rather than being a clever, powerful prince of chaos.

                                    Still, given redemption as a theme, for Abyssals, I do think redemption would be more satisfying if there was something in the Abyssals that specifically tied them to the service of the Deathlords and Neverborn - I don't like this setup where the Abyssal can divorce himself from the service of the Deathlords and then resonance occurs and random powers reach out into the world. I think it's that feel where resonance feels randomly "rubber banded" on to the character of the Abyssals, a glue on that it is easy to imagine an Abyssal without, that damages the idea of redemption. It feels like an arbitrary thing put in the world to force heroism to focus on Solars, rather than a natural consequence of the Abyssal power.

                                    It just has that feel of arbitrary magic rather than a logical connection - there's nothing in the character's personality or his social relationships which is acting. You can contrast to the Star Wars "Dark Side of the Force" that is trying to be emulated, where the dark power the person is wielding reaches out through the to corrupt their actions or force them into social relationships with people bent on destruction. Even being haunted by a specific spirit or deathbrining specific spirits connected to the Abyssal's actions might be better than the way resonance works.
