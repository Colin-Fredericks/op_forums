        Medicine is one of the few places where I'd like to see a definite break from the "Bronze/Iron Age" setting. While certainly beyond the capability of most mortal doctors, I do want things like organ transplants to exist in setting, just being something that only Exalted doctors can successfully perform with any real frequency. Someone has cancer? They don't get chemotherapy, but they do go to their doctor for acupuncture along certain chakra nodes every week. Generally speaking, if it's a medical technique that we can perform in our modern world, I want to see some sort of equivalent in the Exalted setting.

        *EDIT* To note, a lot of these techniques would require Exalted to perform and be will out of reach of the "common man." Still, if a Dragon Blooded or wealthy merchant prince had cancer, or needed a transplant or a skin graft or whatever, he could find treatment. For most people though, your average "doctor" wouldn't be able to do much more than set bones, perform some basic acupuncture or advise people on a diet to try and balance their chakras.*EDIT*

        So, that said. The number one thing I want to see revolving around Medicine charms. I want the game to make a decision - should there be "instant" healing, or should healing take awhile? And then I want it to stick with that decision. In 1st and 2nd edition, healing was intended to take time; there wasn't supposed to be a Lay on Hands or Cure Wounds spell. But, after the core book, other splats got Lay on Hands charms and eventually so did Solars quickly ruining the "No Instant Healing" rule that was supposed to be a central statement the game made. So if you guys go with a similar statement, make sure not to break it.

        Now for specific charms,.

        As for charms themselves I definitely want a "Surgeon Needs No Tools" type ability - manga like Blackjack or Franken Fran are often my go-to inspirations for Solar medicine capabilities and in many situations they quickly perform amazing medical surgeries with extremely limited tools.

        The idea of a "vaccination" effect that can be used to provide a person with bonuses vs disease or poison is pretty cool.

        Certainly the idea of "offensive" Medicine charms is cool. We've seen a few before such as in the Abyssal book or the Dragon Blooded book.

        I'm sure I'll think of some more ideas, but these are some of the bigger ones I had off hand.

                Count me in on the "Lay on Hands is actually kind of cool" side. Although, more exotic treatment methods wouldn't go amiss.

                Other than that, though?

                There's really only two things I really want out of Solar Medicine with all else being extra goodies.

                I want the ability to treat a large number of people without eating up my entire mote pool. After a big battle, a Solar Doctor strides through the battlefield and the field hospitals going from one patient to the next and treats them all with supernatural skill, or a wandering Twilight comes upon a village beset by plague and treats every last villager from the seniors down the to children and does so in a single night.

                My other, and personally more major, desire is also simple. What I want is that the only concern of a Solar Doctor is "Is my patient alive?" That's the only thing they need out of them. For them to be alive. Other that? They can be burned, they can be crippled, they can be poisoned, they can be sick, they can be insane, they can be twisted and corrupted by the Wyld or by foreign magicks, or any other form of aliment imaginable or all at once. But so long as they're alive, a Solar can heal them and make them as healthy as they've ever been, if not more so.

                As long as there's life, there's hope. And hope is all a Solar needs to heal someone.

                                                First, let me present my bias regarding the field of medicine. I don't like modern medicine and its primarily because of my experience with surgery(or lack thereof as the case may be). Doctors have told me several times that I need to remove vestigial organs because I don't need them and they are making me ill. I always rejected this approach because I had Chinese practitioners in my family (guess that reveals my origins a bit) that would cure me through some other method, whether that be holistic concoctions or acupressure (a slightly less acute method than acupuncture). My problem is that all my experiences with modern medicine have failed me (unless they resorted to "just cut it off or out"), whereas the other approaches I mentioned earlier seemed to work just fine.

                                                When I heard about the rejection of common applications of science to explain the setting and the reduction of artifice and other scientific based wonders, I made the assumption that medicine would also be mythic (for lack of a better term). To me, medicine is just a term used to describe that which transforms health. For example, breathing in certain ways is a "medicine" to improving overall vigor in the body. With that in mind, here is what I would like to see and not see.

                                                I don't want to see a scientific or advanced approach to medicine because I feel that would conflict with other elements of the game that you have already described.
                                                I don't want medicine to be restricted to the specific area of just removing afflictions, diseases, and other debilitating effects.
                                                I do like some of the ideas already presented regarding concoctions and the creation of external medicines, hence I also think medicine charms should cover internal ways of improving health.
                                                In keeping with medicine as being a tool, it can be used for good or ill. It might also be nice if it could scale to the point where a solar could unleash a plague or create a space in which illness and the like can never exist.

                                                I look forward to how you will shape the philosophy of medicine for mortals, exalted, spirits, demons, and stranger things still.

                                                How can I know if what I claim I know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I might not know? How can I know if what I claim I don't know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I do know?
                                                -Zhuangzi
