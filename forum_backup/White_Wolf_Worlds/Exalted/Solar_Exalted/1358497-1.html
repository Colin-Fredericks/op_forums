    Originally posted by Lioness View Post
    The main thing that's been ditched is that essence 6+ automatically entitles characters to Attribute and Ability ratings in excess of 5 because that led to a lot of elders being unbeatable in roll-offs at stuff that wasn't even relevant to them. I don't know if this has changed between developers but Morke didn't seem averse to elders having traits higher than 5 just that this kind of permanent advantage had to say something about the character, rather than elder superiority because.
    I truly don't understand or agree with this decision. The idea that Exalts have a soft cap towards their physical development at an arbitrary but manifestly finite degree is strange to be, especially considering there's nothing to stop the elders from artifacts or sorceries, and leads to a strange situation where a Supernal Brawl Dawn may be just as physically strong as a hypothetical E10 Dawn before the consideration of charms. For another, it seems odd you would not pile on unfair amounts of advantages such as artifacts and sorceries of your own, summoned demons and preferably taking the fight to an advantageous location such as an allied god's domain if you were a junior Exalt challenging a senior Exalt instead of standing there and trying to roll-off with them.

    Honestly, it just seems sad that you could be a Solar luchadore with the motivation of being the strongest brawler in the world but for no particular reason you are unable to raise your natural strength above some arbitrary qualifier that still leaves you unable to physically left a tyrant lizard without using charms.

                        GL, first point I'd venture is for me, it doesn't seem so arbitrary that an E6+ cannot buy higher dots*, any more than its arbitrary that an E4 can't get higher dots than an E2?

                        You mention a lot of other stuff elders could have, and it's true that they have it but those are advantages which are A) not Essence score gated (and so available to young and ambitious as well) and B) where they are Essence gated, careful thought should have gone into what they are and how big an advantage they are and so if they should be in the game.

                        Generally the idea is that when elder accessible only powers are there, they should be there with a mechanical eye to challenge level and not so much because someone arbitrarily decided in 1e that it was cool for max stats to go up beyond E5 with Essence and the fanbase have just been resistant to change ever since.

                        Part of why that matters for 3e, as I understand it, is because the designers felt strongly that the unique selling point of Exalted is being powerful enough to fight elders and "shake the Heavens" pretty soon after character creation. Rather than the late 1e-2e thing where player characters post E5 go into a journey of being increasingly unopposable heroes for young Exalts, and hard to describe and play out satisfactorily in the system (and give rise to issues where Elder Lunars and Sidereals make starting Solars less relevant, Elder Solars make the setting irrelevant and the Usurpation impossible unless reducing it to an intra-Solar conflict etc). There is an attempt to define better the normal upper limits of power to avoid them undermining this core theme.


                        *without a lot of justification and consideration of mechanical weight at least!

                            Lioness: I've never thought of charms as spells, so I don't really understand what difference that makes. Considering there were several distinct situations in which beings of a similar gulf in power were outwitted by their lessers (e.g. the Heron and the Syndics) it strikes me as more plausible depending on their charm loadout. I agree the old edition did make things rather disproportionate for characters not at a certain scale, I just feel that completely throwing out the things that made Elder Exalts come across as impressive isn't the right answer rather than going back and trying to rework how dependant abilities/attributes were on Charm rolloffs.

                            vwlls trnt prncss: As someone who has, I would say that broadly speaking many Charm abilities normally found at elder essence range were rolled downwards to be more immediately accessible, but not as a 1:1 equivalency. For example Sky-Breaker Throw enabled you as a default to perform feats such as throwing a Deathlord into the Maw of Oblivion, throwing Ligier down Isidoros' throat or hurling a man in creation into Malfeas. There isn't really an equivalent effect in Brawl; even Nine Aeons Thew merely lets you attempt any feat of strength with no guarantee of it's success, and comparing the 2e version of Titan-Straghtening Method shows it to be a great deal more loaded with benefits than the 3e one while also dealing extra damage to landscape-scale targets.

                            I admit PC experience gain has always been disproportionate but much content in 2e such as Lords of Creation was dedicated to affirming the magnitude of high Essence, which I don't feel is totally reflected from the earlier editions in the current one. Burning Sky Apocalypse Strike for example makes no mention of damage directed at the environment compared to the 2e Cast Down Stars Condemnation.

                            Ghosthead: I don't disagree with the first paragraph. For the second, my contention is there is a balance between mechanical challenge level and the feeling of actual accomplishment which I do not feel has been adequately met, and for that matter I don't see why change should be accepted for it's own sake. There are plenty of changes I like about 3e Infernals such as abandoning the Reclaimation entirely for example, but plenty I dislike as well. And well, speaking about the goal to "define better" the upper scales of power: The fact that the devs will not be writing out charm trees for elder essence feels like it hinders rather than helps that, because it seems as though STs and players will largely have to make up statblocks for the likes of Lilith or Raksi rather than have a tangible challenge. The end result being that it as though the value of high essence doesn't seem like it's there at all if an even younger Exalt could theoretically come along and kill you both.

                            It just feels as though 3e is sending the very strange message that your accomplishments in personal attribute improvement don't actually matter in Exalt-on-Exalt conflict no matter your seniority.

                                        Originally posted by Guitar Longcat
                                        The end result being that it as though the value of high essence doesn't seem like it's there at all if an even younger Exalt could theoretically come along and kill you both.

                                        It seems something that needs playtesting and weighing up value. If ratings past 5 mean that elders are almost autowin or "literally can't take any other, more flavoursome, powers" then that judges against. I'm really just with the chorus here that they're a huge and not particularly flavoursome advantage that shouldn't become a shibboleth that compromises good challenge balance for elders. If your personal experience is that they add very little power and there's room for elder Exalted to have access to these and other Charms without becoming too big to fight and beat for young Exalts, that's something else and a different data point!

                                        On challenge balance for elders more generally seems a bit like it depends on what we want out of elder Exalted. When it comes to "elders", there's a mode like Vampire(?) where you have ancient creatures that have lost their humanity and far surpass the limitations of young supernaturals (and there are other examples in other genres, so I'm not suggesting this is tied to urban gothic fantasy). Or one more like a kung fu movies where being a kung-fu old master is a different experience but provides more in the way of wisdom or secrets and connections and reputation (and if anything may be physically weaker, offset by superior technique). Rather than making it so the old master who killed the hero's father and then adopted his techniques, using them only for destruction, and whose time has finally come is not actually likely to lose in the Final Battle. Both of these could have value as play experiences. Though I'd underline that the vast value of elders is gonna be as NPCs who serve the stories of young Exalt PCs and that's got to be their design priority? Exalted's setting probably drinks from both bottles, but seems like it fits better to place more emphasis on the latter (at least if we agree with the "Shake the pillars of Creation, from the start of play!" thing?).
