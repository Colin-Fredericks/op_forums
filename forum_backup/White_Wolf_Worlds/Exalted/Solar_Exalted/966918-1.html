                Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                They're generally fuzzy enough to not cause any real problems. A solar might somehow just know how to find a hidden manse or some secret cache of weapons. They might dream of lost glories and epic wars, or they might see someone and get the unshakeable sense that they know them from somewhere.

                Ultimately it's meant to be source of plot hooks rather than any real drama. There's already plenty of that in Creation as it is.
                I think in general starting characters have exalted far enough in the past that they are used to the memories but as far as being effected by them, I tend to agree with Elfive. On the other hand if considering the freshly exalted, I would think it would start as vivid dreams as you are more susceptible to a roaming mind while asleep. After a while, you would start having effects as suggested by Elfive. You would experience a lot of deja vu but unless you had some serious flaw, it would seem normal and come gradually enough to handle.

                As far as starting charms, I think of it along the lines of one of the Spiderman movies (all of them failed to portray Spiderman correctly except Civil War) where he wakes up from a fever and suddenly tears open the door by accident, leaps up on the wall while startled, shoots webbing without thinking about it, etc. That would be more tramatic than having a dream/vision/deja vu moment in my opinion. Especially depending on how religious you are and how indoctrinated in the evils of Anathema.

                                Originally posted by Erinys View Post

                                Because it is nonsense. You don't spend xp then time; you spend xp and time.

                                The time captaining at sea absolutely counts as training Sail. It is "a significant portion of the quoted time working on the skill in question," because "practical experience also counts as a significant amount of time training." You would, however, have to establish that you also spent some of that time studying your charts and almanacs, listening to advice from experienced hands, and such as "neither training nor experience alone is sufficient for mastery." [EX3 pg 178]
