                  The thing to remember about Resistance is that, while Soak can be immensely strong, it's not set up to be equally good as parry or dodge. There's no charm that will turn you into Superman and let you just parry every attack with your face, forever. This is because Soak is a second tier defense, used to mitigate the results of you failing to block or dodge.

                  As such, ironically, the first thing you should look into is Dodge or Parry. At the very least you want an excellency and maybe something to negate penalties.

                  After that, it's time to look at Resistance. Starting with Ox-Body, it's important to note that every HL you have means your opponent needs to build up 2 to 3 more initiative before they can kill you. Which means that, as long as your Stamina is above 3, each time you take Ox-Body, your opponent needs about 9 more init to kill you. And, past a certain point, it can be hard to build up to that. Also, the Pain Tolerance Merit will turn all of your -2 HLs into -1s, which will be really handy.

                  Something else to consider is whether you want to wear armor or not. Several of the best soak boosting charms in Resistance are specifically incompatible with armor. You can grab Artifact armor and the armor boosting charms or go for charms that simply boost your soak. I'm not sure which is better off hand, but I did work on a character that specifically didn't wear armor once, so I'm gonna give advice in that direction.

                  The thing to remember about being Soak focused is that, in 3e, it means 1) you're going to get hit and 2) it's going to hurt. Resistance charms are there to help you manage how hurt you get, not to prevent you from getting hurt. Durability of Oak Meditation is a good example. Like other defense charms, it needs to be invoked before the attack roll, so, it's not there to save you after you get hit. It's there to help you boost your soak a bit so you only take min damage if they hit you or to help blunt the damage a bit when your opponent unleashes their mega damage combo. Also, because of the damage reduction, it gives you an effective six hardness against Decisive Attacks. As some builds use charms to increase their base init rating then spam 4-5 damage Decisive attacks, this shuts down all of those tactics cold.

                  As for specific charm advice, many Resistance charms are a bit on the expensive side, so Essence-Gathering Temper is a good buy. Ox-Body *2 and Body Mending Meditation are a must. If you're going unarmored, then Diamond Body Prana and it's prereqs are essential. If you really want to be "Dude who can shrug off a Goremaul to the face," then I'd advise grabbing Adamant Skin Technique. Alternately, Tiger Warrior's Endurance will give you free healing whenever you recover from being crashed, so, if you think that might happen fairly often, that might be something to look at. If you have charms left, look at Ruin-Abasing Shrug and Wound-Knitting Exercise.

                  If you want to be a berserker, Battle Fury Focus and Blood Thirst Sword-Dancer Spirit will give you some pretty impressive combat buffs, at the cost of you being unable to stop fighting willingly.
