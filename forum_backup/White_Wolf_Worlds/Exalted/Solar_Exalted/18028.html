    Binary Thinking, Solars and YOU!

    11-25-2013, 07:50 PM
    Originally posted by John Mørke View Post

    You seem to think a fuzzed definition is tantamount to having no means at all of discerning an Exalt from something else. Stop thinking in binary. I opened the definition to some non-standard ideas, not to "just anything" arbitrarily defined.
    I’ve been meaning to make a post about the utility of binary thinking for a while, but I’ve never quite reached the right balance between motivation and boredom until now. It’s come up a few times, most recently in the post I quoted above, and I think the number one problem with binary thinking around here isn’t that people are thinking in binaries, but that they are not constructing the proper binaries to understand how Exalted is constructed as a setting and a set of mechanics.

    All thinking is fundamentally binary – a thing is itself and not any other thing. The trick is defining what the thing is and what it isn’t and determining how a thing is marked as itself and not any other thing. In other words, how do you define the binary distinction?

    As an example, I’ll use Solars.

    Distinction 1: Exalted/UnExalted

    The fundamental distinction, the distinction that the game is named after, is that of the Exalted and the UnExalted. All other distinctions – between splats, castes and individual Exalts – are based on this distinction. This also means that the distinction between the Exalted and the UnExalted has nothing to do with any characteristics of any of the splats. A third soul? Chosen of the gods? The craftsmanship of Autochthon? All of that is completely irrelevant to the distinction between the Exalted and the UnExalted.

    So how do you establish the distinction between the Exalted and the UnExalted? The books call them Exalted. That’s it. You will never, ever find any other definition of the Exalted (unless John and Holden want to rupture the concept of Exaltation and cause most of the splats to collapse as the distinctions that define them are removed so that all splats can be defined simply as Exalted based on common criteria that differentiate them from the UnExalted, but I’m pretty sure that’s not going to happen).

    But that’s all out-of-character information. In-character, Exaltation is self-produced, self-recognized and self-declared. The Exalted are Exalted because they consider themselves to be so and announce their Exaltation to the world. The state of Exaltation is intrinsic to the character and cannot be attributed to external forces, or else Exaltation would no longer be self-produced.

    (That some Exalted are chosen or constructed by a seemingly external force is not necessarily problematic to this definition. It simply means that the process of selection is intrinsic to the Exalted herself. I might make another post about this once I’ve spent more time thinking it through.)

    This self-declaration stems from the Exalted’s observations of the UnExalted and of the differences between themselves and the UnExalted. There are points of similarity between the Exalted and the UnExalted, but they are irrelevant. Only the differences matter, namely, that the Exalted are Exalted and that the UnExalted are not. The tautology is quite intentional and unavoidable.

    So that’s the distinction that defines the Exalted and the UnExalted. Next up:

    Distinction 2: Solar/Exalted

    This distinction was a theoretical nightmare for me, because it’s impossible for the Exalted to be distinguished from both the UnExalted and the Solars. It would no longer be a binary if it were.

    Which is why Solars (along with all the other splats) and the Exalted are two independent concepts.

    Exaltation as a concept defines itself in relation to the UnExalted. This means that it is incapable of defining itself in relation to Solars. In fact, because the Solars are Exalted, Exaltation is incapable of recognizing them as Solars. It would only ever refer to them as Exalted because the concept of Solars is unnecessary to the concept of the Exalted.

    However, the concept of the Exalted, which is inclusive of all splats, is necessary to the concept of Solars, as it allows Solars to define themselves and differentiate themselves from all other Exalted splats by using a single point of reference – a binary distinction. All Exalted are Exalted, but only Solars represent 1) the pursuit of human excellence 2) through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun 3) in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    None of these three points are definitive of the Exalted as a broad concept (only the fact that they are Exalted is), so they create a distinction between Solars and the Exalted.

    “The pursuit of human excellence,” which is generally taken to mean ability-based powers, differentiates Solars from splats that don’t use ability-based powers.

    “Through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun” differentiates Solars from other splats that use ability-based charms, but that are based on different aesthetics (Abyssals, Infernals, Sidereals, Dragon-Blooded, etc.).

    “In a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power” isn’t really necessary as far as defining the splats go, but it does further differentiates Solars from everyone but Abyssals and Infernals (assuming they also operate at the upper boundary of Exalted power).

    So if there was some other splat that wasn’t called Solars, but that represented the pursuit of human excellence through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power, you would have to call them Solars because they would be functionally identical to Solars in every way (unless you added a fourth criteria of distinction which I can’t think of right now).

    (As an aside, this is why superficial comparisons between Liminals and Abyssals or Getimians and Sidereals are pointless. Liminals are not defined in relation to Abyssals, nor are Getimians defined in relation to Sidereals. In any case, similarities are never relevant because, by their very nature, they can’t be used to define anything. Only differences matter.)

    Which leads to:

    Distinction 3: Caste/Solar

    Actually five distinctions: Dawn/Solar, Zenith/Solar, Twilight/Solar, Night/Solar and Eclipse/Solar.

    Much of what I said about Solars and their relation to the Exalted also applies here. The concept of Solars includes all five Castes, so it cannot recognize the five Castes as discrete concepts. The Castes only exist because they differentiate themselves from Solars and, in doing so, from all the other existing Castes.

    Solars represent the pursuit of human excellence through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    The Dawn Caste represents the pursuit of human excellence in combat through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    The Zenith Caste represents the pursuit of human excellence in messianism through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    The Twilight Caste represents the pursuit of human excellence in scholarship through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    The Night Caste represents the pursuit of human excellence in furtiveness through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    The Eclipse Caste represents the pursuit of human excellence in diplomacy through the aesthetics of the Unconquered Sun in a manner that defines the upper boundary of Exalted power.

    (Yeah, most of those specializations suck, but I can’t think of any better one-word summations of what each Caste does.)

    The purpose of the Castes is to provide a level of complexity that can be used as the foundation for a concrete a character concept. They’re more precise and useful than the concept of Solars when writing up a character. They’re also useful to introduce the concept of intra-splat divisions as a core piece of the setting.

    This leads to the final distinction:

    Distinction 4: Character/Caste

    These are the infinite distinctions that differentiate individual Solar characters from their Caste. As with Solars and the Exalted and Castes and Solars, the Castes include all characters of that Caste, but characters are not their Caste. While Solars are too broad a concept to base a character on, Castes are too narrow, so individual characters can use favoured abilities, intimacies and character background to differentiate themselves from their Caste.

    And that’s how you use binaries to think Solars through.

A binary is a duality, where it is either one thing or the other, and cannot possibly be something else: a light switch is either on or off, it is either day or night, a man is either alive or dead, and so on.

                                              The only case Tiresias presents that is actually a duality is Exalted/Non-Exalted. Beyond that, he's just talking about distinctions among sub-groups: if Solars/Exalted was actually a binary, then one could only be a Solar or some generic Exalted, rather than being one of the other many, many types of Exalted. The same goes for the castes, unless there are suddenly only two Castes in the entire Solar splat, and I'm not even getting into the character variations that are possible within a caste.
                                              There's a gap between abstract conceptualization and practical application of game concepts that I haven't been able to resolve to my satisfaction, probably because I can't find the correct words.

                                              It's like... if there were two Castes, you could get a distinction of Dawn/Zenith for the combat and the social Caste and that would work. However, because there are 5 Castes, it's impossible to set that up as a binary, so you need to set up a system where 1) all of the Castes are independent of each other and 2) all the Castes differentiate themselves from the same thing because, fundamentally, they're all Solar Castes and must have something in common.

                                              So you get Dawn/Solar, Zenith/Solar, Twilight/Solar, Night/Solar and Eclipse/Solar and those work as binaries because they're all independent of each other, including the concept of Solars.

                                              In the distinction of Solar/Exalted, the concept of "Exalted" has no inherent ontology. It's constructed by the concept of "Solar" and exists for the sole purpose of defining the concept of "Solar" and the criteria that are and aren't part of "Solars." The concept of "Solar" constitutes itself in contrast to the Exalted in general, defining itself through the characteristics of "Solar" that are not part of the Exalted in general.

                                              In the distinction of, say, Dawn/Solar, it is the concept of "Solar" that has no ontology and that exists only so that the Dawn Caste can define itself. The distinctions of Solar/Exalted, Dawn/Solar, Zenith/Solar, Twilight/Solar, Night/Solar and Eclipse/Solar all use concepts of "Solar" that are theoretically unrelated to each other, despite being identical in practice.

                                              I thought about using a distinction of Caste/Other Castes but it just didn't seem right. I think it just makes more sense for the Castes to be defined in relation to Solars and I don't think the concept of "Other Castes" really works as a common point, but I can't quite figure out why.

                                              In any case, the utility of binary thinking here is that, when you make a Solar character, you only need to make five comparisons: Dawn/Solar, Zenith/Solar, Twilight/Solar, Night/Solar and Eclipse/Solar. Once you know how each Caste is different from Solars, you're good to go. If you want to tell the Castes apart from each other, you need to make 10 comparisons to get all the permutations. Binary thinking is twice as efficient.
