                My take has always been that Primordials are simultaneously physical and spiritual - just like Autochtonia (which is how they explain that you can't just dematerialize and walk through walls in Autochtonia).

                As 3rd Circle Souls, Deva must also be simultaneously physical and spiritual - Ligier is a Demon and a Green Sun (and the source of all light); Munaxes is a Demon and a Ravine (and a bottomless pit); Amalion is a Demon and a Manse (and the forebearer of many and all her manses); Ipithymia, the Street of Gold Lanterns, etc, etc, etc.

                *

                Thus the idea of geomantically combating or "pruning" the Yozis is much like what we saw in Autochtonia - sure, you can kill Autochton's primary souls but the First and Forsaken Lion also achieves victory by sabotaging and wrecking and misaligning the world-body. In this view, Debok Moom is simultaneously Autochton's belligerent inspiration - his immune system - the temple-factory-complex that produces war-machines - and in itself, the same factory-complex is whatever organ is regulating his immune response. Likewise, Malfeas isn't a lifeless crust - if I bring destruction to the Street of Gold Lanterns, to the Manses of Amalion, if I shoot the Green Sun out of the sky with my bow - then I'm also waging violence against demons and Malfeas.

                There's no artificial distinction between all these things.

                They're not gods - who merely shepherd the cities and domains they watch. They are the domains.

                Originally posted by Erinys
                I assume that at the very least, you can't kill a Primordial until after you kill and ghost-eat all its souls.
                I agree with this.

                But one problem I have with the setting..... is that if you kill the Fetich, everyone else allegedly just vanishes. Oh, Malfeas has 23+ top tier demons that are awesome? Murder Ligier and the others implode.

                Which can be fun (for sheer ripple-effect destruction) but makes me wonder whether we can have any sense of continuity --- what if some of those other demons were kind of awesome, how can I best help them "splinter off" (Lunar or Sidereal charms?). Wyld exposure? What if we wanted to kill Ligier - but give the "crown" to another demon. Shouldn't a yet-living 3rd Circle serve as a better "inheritor" and "seed" than a completely splattered Fetich? This may be some Scion inspiration (where it sounds like the Primordial is Terra, it's Fetich is Gaia, and other 3rd-Circle "Avatars" variously ally or want to usurp her position). Stuff.


                *

                What I'm saying is that "as written", it doesn't seem like you need to kill them all. Just kill the Fetich with ghost-eating technique. The remaining demons probably either get dragged to death with the Primordial or splinter off as new behemoths and hecatonshire.

                And "as written", an equally possible interpretation is that you kill the Fetich - the Primordial needs to reform - and all the other demons die too, to be recycled and reshaped. Killing them "in the womb" is a lot easier via this interpretation too.

                *

                And then, if 3rd Circles could replace the Fetich - what if you kill the Fetich and 2 or 3 of the 3rd Circles can't agree on the new leadership? Could they splinter off and form 2 or 3 smaller Primordials? Will they automatically be weaker than the original? What if they begin recruiting more souls to flesh out their ranks?

                Etc, etc, etc.

                                                        There's nothing in a Solar's powers that makes everything they do turn out "Good" or moral. But the Abyssal curse guarantees everything they do will end in ash and sorrow. To an Abyssal suffering this curse, the motive/creative/holy power of Solar Exaltation represents not a guarantee of a state of moral good, but the chance to do moral good as he deems it, without bringing ruin and death to everyone and everything he loves as a guaranteed promise of his power. An Abyssal who wants to break the curse would rise to the lofty heights of sainthood in pursuing this goal, despite the awful price in suffering he must pay. It has everything to do with the Abyssal's idea of morality and how the Unconquered Sun recognizes a hero's virtue, and that Solars have the choice of good or evil. Redemption does not mean the Abyssal who has become a Solar will be free from temptation and unable to make mistakes or act immorally, although it is highly implied that they will stay a moral actor by virtue of what they have suffered.

                                                        And yes, this all requires the ST to deem actions moral, which some may reject outright. Those kinds of Storytellers won't run Redemption stories, and that's AOK, but other Storytellers doing so won't hurt their games.
