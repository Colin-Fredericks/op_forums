            I think the question of how different Exalted work out in play needs to acknowledge certain points about how player groups should organise. I mean, if you're playing a Dragon Blood and feel that you're being overshadowed by another player who is a Solar... why does one group have two people who are trying to do such similar things in a manner other than playing out cooperation? Is that something that would be better if they were both Solars? Wouldn't that just change it from "I'm being overshadowed" to "I have to fight for limited access to every gameplay opportunity for our group's niche"?

            If it's a question of "I'm playing this Dragon Blood, but can't help feel that this concept would be done better if it was the exact same thing as some theoretical Solar who isn't actually present in the game", well, some of that does depend on giving other Exalted distinct gameplay mechanisms that create unique and fun forms of play, but some of it requires one to buy into the setting details provided around the different Exalted types (of which Dragon Blooded have one of the most distinct), and some of it is just something that somebody is going to have to get over. It's like, if you're looking for an optimized play experience or group role, then no, you're not getting that while playing other Exalted; the game is never going to act as though it's waiting beyond the core for all of the basic tools for a complete play experience.

            It all comes back from the really early design conceit of a pulp fantasy game set in a world close to the end in which ancient heroes whose absence is the major factor in why things went down inexplicably return, and Grabowski's realization of "hey, if we set this thing up as important and then wait to reveal it, it will never really live up to the build up, so it would be more functional to start by defining the top and then working out from there". I, personally, will always appreciate that, since I've had too many experiences with things in which something was continuously built up to be the greatest ultimate thing and then floundered in the execution.

            Heh, of all the things to first think of for this, it reminds me of how in Frasier they initially didn't want to make another version of the unseen wife character from Cheers, but eventually had so much fun coming up with outlandish descriptions of Maris Crane that they realised that no human actor could actually portray her, so they just stuck with jokes for the outlandish descriptions. That's a case in which you turn the limitations of the reveal into a strength, but obviously you can't really do that so effectively for significant actors in a roleplaying game.

            Works better for the Yozis, though.

                This is one of those topics that can really only be addressed in the context of the previous two versions. Since the only thing out for 3rd edition at this time is the PDF file for backers and even that is having changes made to it before final release of the core rule book.

                Historically speaking this is how the Exalted have always broken down.
                Solars are champions, heroes and personify perfection in all things. But they are not the masters of everything.
                Lunars have always been bdf's. You really don't play a Lunar if you are looking for an intellectual. They start as better fighters than Solars, but get bogged down by higher experience costs.
                Sidereals are just jackholes. They mess with people. Sure they have the best and most broken martial arts, but primarily they change the odds. Things get easier for them and harder for their opponents. A Sidereal doesn't need to hit you to make you wish that you hadn't gotten out of bed in the morning. Worst day ever.
                Dragon Blooded are suppose to be about numbers and cooperation. Personally, I've always thought that the Sidereals have had the better caste abilities for working as a team. What Dragon Blooded really have is very essence efficient charms. I.E. in 2nd ed. their 1st excellency gave 2 dice per mote of essence spent. Which kind of makes up for the smaller essence pool. This same thing happened with Paladins in WoW. They became the best healers because they had smaller mana pools that they could regenerate faster and their healing charm costs were reduced to reflect the smaller pools.

                                                I think the root problem is more of an acceptance that multi-splat games are the norm, and not a weird outlier that you need to have proper planning and exceptions for. Once you internalize that multi-splat games are the norm, you begin tacitly thinking the game ought to consider them in every written word and that the game's base balance and themes revolve around them. They don't. So, while you may not like the solution, accepting that multi-splat games are typically your table's problem and not the line's problem is a better approach and leads to resolved cases and (continues) to make the people going on about this at great length for no reason look like howler monkeys.

                                                Incentive is not permission or justification.
