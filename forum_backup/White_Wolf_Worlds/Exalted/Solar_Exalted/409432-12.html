        You know what?

        I think I'm finally completely sold on the idea that Abyssals should just never have been written to inevitably destroy Creation even against their wills. It's as crappy as rewriting Infernals to inevitably cause the Reclamation to suceed just by existing even when they try to rebel. I'll take Dusk Shine's explanations about why this could be possible.

        I love the idea that Abyssals could actually discover ways to put the Neverborn to rest without destroying Creation. A high-Compassion, high-Whispers Abyssal who wants to protect the ancestor cults' interests (thus not destroy Creation) but feels absolutely driven to relieve the Neverborn's suffering could totally go for that. And it would be very interesting/profound if the narrative arc of an Abyssal was the one that found a solution to Creation's problems other than "kill thine enemy some more."

        Mind, I still think that it should be hard for Abyssals to avoid being agents of destruction and blight and death, and to escape the logic of the organizations that train and equip them that killing stuff is the answer to whatever problems the Deathlords and/or Neverborn face tonight. But it just shouldn't be impossible.


        Edit: If Exalted were a novel or Abyssals had never been intended as PCs, I would still find the inevitability more plausible given the Neverborn's goals, but it's a crappy thing for a PC splat.

            Originally posted by Gallus View Post
            Or they want to kill creation so that they enter the wild and maybe come back to life. As much as dead things can want and plan anyway.
            Yes, it was mentioned somewhere as a possible explanation for FAFL iniciating the Fair Folk invasion (which, apparently, is the reason the Great Contagion became curable, thus one Apocalypse (The Great Contagion) became neutralised by another, simpler one (Balor's Crusade) which was solvable - and solved - by brute force):
            He always claimed the Neverborn told him to do that. Turns out, they might have: A possible reason given was that they wanted creation to be swallowed by the Wyld (not destroyed) so in the realm of infinite possibility, they may break Exalted's big golden "No Backsies"-rule.

            That being said, Yes to both "The Neverborn just want to stop being in eternal pain (Creation is just collateral)" and "The Neverborn broke Reincarnation".
            It's been stated that before them, there was no Underworld - their death broke the circle of reincarnation. Whatever mystical mechanisms (Lethe) they put in place to process the dead couldn't handle them because their own (proper, non-fetich) death was inconcievable to them (I like to say "the drain broke because something to big got stuffed in, now it leaks").


            That being said, it's also been stated that the "orders" the Neverborn give are... often cryptic. It's generaly accepted that they really do seek Creation's destruction (and it has been compared to a Fetter), but a never canonically mentioned alternate character interpretation (read: A random idea I just had) might just be that when they order "complete destruction", it never refers to Creation. They actually don't want to destroy Creation, they really just created the Deathlords so someone would find a way to end them, but the Deathlords only understood "Death" and "End", and went "Well, sure, let's End Creation by killing everything!". So, the Neverborn are actually pretty pissed of because the Deathlords are such assholes about the whole thing and do stuff the Neverborn never actually wanted them to do.
            I would hate for this to be the official canon, but leaving it a possibility (by never going into to much detail) seems fun.

                                    I also had the idea once that Neverborn weren't actually "dead", per se (which, in fact, also explains the term Undead), but merely "crippled" Primordials.
                                    In this concept for Primordials, said Primordials are independant of their Jouten-bodies. Jouten were more like libs, used to interact with the world, but the Primordial itself was outside of existence. Also, each Primordial had a limit to how many Jouten could manifest at once. Solars "killing" Primordials merely destroyed their Jouten permanently, reducing the number of Jouten a Primordial could manifest.
                                    The Neverborn were Primordials who lost all their Jouten, which left them unable to interact with the world. This "untethered" them from the world, but the "metaphysical weight" of their titan-souls attracts lesser (read: mortal) souls, creating the Underworld.
