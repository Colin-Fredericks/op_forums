                  I actually have a strong opinion about this, so you asked for it!

                  Occult in 2e was terrible. It was separated into isolated, distinct systems. 'Mortal Thaumaturgy' was something that Exalted charms just never interacted with ; in fact, the game kinda tried to shame Exalted who would use mortal thaumaturgy.

                  This was a side effect, of course, of those sections being written by different people who were not coordinating.

                  This is lame. An Exalted using Occult is doing the same things a human does, except better. Accordingly, I would like the themes and conventions of the mortal thaumaturgy system... walkaways, prayer strips, etc... reflected in the Exalted systems as well.

                  An Exalted with a wide array of Occult charms may not be a 'Sorcerer' in the strictest sense of the word, but they have a firm command of peasant magic and remain exceptionally dangerous and resourceful.

                  TLDR; Exalted occult skills and mortal occult skills were isolated in previous editions. Make them the same skill.

                                          This is something I've been pondering lately and the conclusion I reached is pretty straightforward: Sorcery doesn't make you a sage wizardy fellow- being a Twiligh Solar does.

                                          Sorcery is there for a lot of different things- you can model the powers of all sorts of different characters with it- a holy prophet, a voodoo witch, a spirit caller, an enlightemed strategist and so on. In the end sorcery just makes you a sorcerer and ideally it should, along with Martial Arts and Evocations be able to complimemt any type of a character.

                                          Now being a Magus... That's a different thing. In Exalted Occult is the propper aplication of rules of the Universe not immediately apparent. It's basically spirit-physics. I could see it as something that when combined with Lore, Investigation, Medicine and Craft would allow you to do stuff that we atribute to sages of legend, like Hermes Trismagistos, King Salomon, or Merlin. I feel like where Lore is "know what", Occult is "know how".

                                          It will, inevitably, always, step on the toes of Lore, but I feel like in the same way nobody should pick Presence without Socialize, or Melee without Martial Arts or Dodge without Athletics, nobody should pick Occult without Lore and vice versa. Ideally, no character should be able to pick just one skill and express their entire character concept with it.
