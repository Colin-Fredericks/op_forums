        Originally posted by Chant View Post
        I think there's a widespread meme that Good Is Boring, and that's why people want Solars to be nigh-inescapably disposed toward being shitheads. And I don't agree with that notion.

        The thing about the Unconquered Sun is that he embodies every aspect of good, despite many of those aspects conflicting with each other when applied to the real world. Different Solars will very frequently have different ideas about what it means to be good, and view each other's actions in different moral frameworks. The Eclipse who accumulates power in order to ultimately free the Threshold from the grip of the Guild is good; the Twilight who binds elementals into dangerous servitude to fight the Fair Folk in her quest to protect Creation from dissolution is good; the Zenith who starts a bloody revolution to build a free and equal society is good. All three of these people could think the other two are wasting their time. All three could accuse the other two of doing something outright immoral! Good comes into conflict with good all the time, and that's why it's more interesting to me that Solars are inherently good than that they subvert the bland-good-guy stereotype by actually being inevitably terrible.

        What about the one that works to take over the Guild themselves? What about the one that treats with the Fair Folk as useful tools for their aims? What about the one that builds an equal society through bloody empire and conquest?

        My contention is that the folks that *aren't* good folks, and have just as much power as the 'good' folks, are what makes the setting interesting. How is making the default Solar condition 'good guy' (in more words with more nuance, obviously) helping the setting?

                    Originally posted by UltraFennec View Post
                    My contention is that the folks that *aren't* good folks, and have just as much power as the 'good' folks, are what makes the setting interesting. How is making the default Solar condition 'good guy' (in more words with more nuance, obviously) helping the setting?
                    I'd argue it's helping the setting by defining the baseline mode of conflict between Solars, and against Solars, as being conflict rooted in moral ambiguity. You don't come into conflict with the Bull of the North because he's evil; you come into conflict with him because you think the good he's invested in, of enriching his people and toppling an evil empire, is outweighed by another good, such as the preservation of life or the allocation of the Realm's resources to fighting Deathlords. Saying "all Solars are good by default" and then describing the ways in which different Solars see different flavors of good in themselves and others is much more interesting to me than saying "Solars are people with the will to be great". Beings with the will to be great, and the power to achieve greatness, are all over the setting without throwing Solars in the mix. Saying that being Solar has no moral implications, that Solars are simply the greatest of the great and recognised for that, bores me to tears, because it's just a distillation of Creation's status quo premise, which is that life sucks because there are super-powerful entities running around being selfish and amoral. What is the point of the Return Of The Solars if they aren't there to defy the Sorrows that define this Age?

                                        Solars are agents of change who oppose the status quo; if they were totally happy and satisfied with how things things were going, they probably wouldn't have ever Exalted.

                                        Fortunately, there's a lot of things that are wrong with the current state of Creation. The Guild, the Realm and most nations are various forms and degree of corrupt and evil. And so the Solars, as people who often want to change the world, find themselves in a position where it's very easy to make things better. Or make them worse.

                                        The Bull of the North is the classic example. He's great for the Icewalkers; he's leading them to a new era, he's helping to change their lifestyle, he's helping to form them into a civilization. Awesome. To them, the Bull is a great man and a "good" person. But to the many of the people he's conquered and the nations he's invaded or slaughtered he's viewed as a dangerous monster.

                                        The comparison to Genghis Khan was quite apt.

                                        The Solar's aren't "good."But they are heroes. They will change the world. Ideally for the better, but possibly for the worse.
