                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                        Are you never going to homebrew up your own fun, custom Charms in order to cover a method or image space that you consider fitting for Solars (or have otherwise had inspired by some appropriate fiction), and not really part of the current selection? Between John and Lucy and the fact that this thing was ordered by Backers of a certain level, it sounds a lot more like that than anything. What were they supposed to do; come back to those Backers and say "sorry about your money and your enthusiasm, but it turns out that there are too many Charms, so your fun ideas have to be dropped from their self-contained supplement"?
                        Sure, and the backer charms are a definite yay. The 33 extra I'm not so sure about.

                        Originally posted by Omicron View Post
                        "Charm bloat" in 2e was not really about there being too many Charms. Charm bloat was the perverse way in which, from a relatively small corebook, the Charmset of certain splats grew by addition after addition in every single supplement, inducing a constant power creep to mechanics that weren't stable to begin with. In the end, the power-set of Exalts was scattered around a dozen different books that veered wildly in nature, theme and power from their original basis, taking the game in bizarre directions and forcing you to constantly cross-reference everything. That was bloat. "Having a lot of Charms" is not bloat, and it's not inherently bad. Neither is "adding new Charms," depending on how you handle it.
                        Well, that was a big part of it, yeah, but 'too many Charms' was and is absolutely a problem and a form of Charm bloat as well. On the development end it means there's a wider array of effect-packages to consider when balancing anything new, and on the player side it increases the buy-in price of time and effort needed to read and understand the rules before you can start playing.

                        Exalted has always been a game that asks a lot of that; the 2e corebook wasn't a small volume by RPG standards. The D&D players handbook, with its general rules, spell lists and multiple class progressions, has never topped 320 pages. The Big Three of the World of Darkness/Chronicles of Darkness, Vampire, Mage and Werewolf, are 312/294, 309/400 and 309/317. The Shadowrun 20th anniversary core is 368 pages. The 2e corebook was 403 pages, and the single biggest section was an 82-page Charms chapter even before the various additions from Glory to the Most High, Ink Monkeys, errata, Dreams of the First Age... And then 3e blows that out of the water with a 176 page Charms chapter, plus about another 40 for Martial Arts. There are rulebooks smaller than that, so, yeah, I call it a problem.

                        There are no problems, only plothooks.
