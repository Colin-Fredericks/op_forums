                                        Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                        Well, we know little enough about Evocations to say for sure, but some possible reasons include:

                                        (2) Evocations may be able to be bought from a separate XP pool than Charms. If Solar XP is implemented (it was mentioned previously and is listed on the 3e wiki) as a second pool to buy things aside from Solar native Charms, double your pleasure to increase your power.
                                        (3) Evocations may be always-on effects for the person who unlocked them. As in, no mote/Willpower costs. That's a definite advantage over a Charm.
                                        (4) Adding on to (3), Evocations may not have a Type, unlike Charms, so may provide bonuses to Combos that an otherwise-equal Charm could not because of Type rules for Combos (not being able to use 2 Simples at the same time, for example, or Supplementals only being able to be used to supplement the Simple in the Combo).
                                        On these 2-4, none of these are *bad*, but they do feel like "Use these because there is an arbitrary rule that incentivizes you to" kind of bonuses.

                                        They don't just come from the nature of evocations as distinct from Charms in a deep, elegant and simple way*, unlike evocations specifically being able to do what Charms can't, or look cool in a certain way doing it even when they can.

                                        Having two pools of Charms that feed off separate exp / resource and separate activations *would* encourage you to use both. But would that actually be interesting design?

                                        *I think of deep, simple and elegant as when you look at a thing and think "Could it really be any other way?" and at the same time its full of interesting possibilities and combinations. And you don't think "Aha. That's a button to make me use / do ...".

                                            Originally posted by Coik View Post
                                            Ehhh...I honestly hope that this is the least reason to use Evocations. But then, I despise Black Mirror Revelation and Primordial Principal Emulation and think that they never should have been written. I'm a bit hopeful in that regard, though, if only because it would be rather silly to par Eclipse charmsteal down to a vetted list and then turn around and say "Solars can steal any theme and call it an evocation."
                                            Evocations are not Solar-only, no more than Sorcery or Martial Arts are.

                                            And let me flip it around and put it like this: It is bad to say "you cannot create an artifact to do X", "you cannot create a spell to do X" or "you cannot create a martial art to do X" simply because an unrelated splatbook on the other end of the gameline also has a take on X. This is because a theme (as it applies to types of Exalted) is more than just one charm or one power or one aspect of your flavor; it's the overarching narrative behind the whole subject in question. Volcano Cutter presumably does fiery things, but to pretend that that makes Fire Aspects obsolete is to ignore everything that makes Dragon-Blooded what they are and (probably) everything that makes Volcano Cutter what it is.

                                            The Eclipse charmshare was turned into a vetted list primarily for balance and design reasons (specifically, it sucks to have to design every charm around the possibility that Solars will get it), plus perhaps some thematic concerns over what it implies about what charms really are, in-setting. But Solars continue to be the most straightforward exemplar of all sorts of 'human' heroes, and this includes, say, being a hero who is primarily defined as master of a legendary sword that can master fire, or one what can reflect visions of the future along the mirrored edge of its blade. Such artifacts will have unique and distinct stories, which means not directly and completely overlapping with someone else's overarching story -- a daiklave called "LunarExalt" which gives you all the powers of a Lunar Exalt is obviously dumb.

                                            But the purpose of playing (for instance) a Sidereal is to explore the Sidereal narrative. That overarching narrative should be unique to them, and the game should avoid things that would actually damage it (eg. revealing someone who is flatly better at SMA than Sidereals, or giving powerful future-predictive powers to nearly everyone so mastering it no longer has any special meaning.) But that doesn't require saying "only Sidereals can predict the future" or anything like that, and making that statement would be bad for the game (because there are many angles to explore predictive magic from.) If what you're looking for is "I can do some broadly-defined thing Solars can't ever do by any means, no matter what, even through sorcery or artifacts", then... you're SOL, mostly, because that isn't a healthy desire for the gameline.

                                            With two important caveats, though: There are limitations on what Solars can do, of course. But those limitations are based around what preserves the Solar narrative rather than in an attempt to make you feel better about playing something else. And all of this applies to everyone, not just Solars -- that is to say, this is the reason Alchemicals can have really good crafting charms even though Solars had craft in the core; this is why Sidereals can turn into a raven or Infernals can take on monstrous Shintai forms even though shapeshifting is so central to Lunars. Solars tend to be the most flexible splat in terms of what you can build with them, but the entire game is designed around "tell this interesting story", not "make sure the asshole across the table doesn't get his dirty foot on your story." If you're worried about -- say -- a Solar player introducing Wayfinder, the Sword of Wisdom in your game and focusing on its evocations in a way that might conflict with your Sidereal's role in the story, that's an issue to be resolved at your table, not an underlying issue with the game (because you get the same issue whenever two people want to do the same general thing.)

                                            The other important caveat is the term 'broadly-defined'. As you define things more narrowly and specifically, they become increasingly inextricable from one specific story, which makes it generally dumb to reuse them elsewhere -- eg. 'manipulate fate' is broad, but 'Sidereal Astrology' is specific; a sword that can cut through fate is cool, but a needle that can weave Sidereal Astrology directly and specifically should probably only work for Sidereals, because making that available to other people genuinely would undercut the Sidereal narrative.
