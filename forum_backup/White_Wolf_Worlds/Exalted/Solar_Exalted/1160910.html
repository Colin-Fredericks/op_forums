    Solar circle workings

    11-20-2017, 01:49 PM
    Reading the thread there we make judgments on sorcerous workings made me ask the following question: On one hand, solar circle workings are supposed to be the pitch of sorcercy in game's universe. It's explicitly written that if something feels like reachable by magic in Exalted's setting, it should be doable with an adamant working. On the other hand, in a number of cases, things that feel setting-wise doable also feel deeply game-breaking:

    - granting the whole kingdom sorcerous abilities;
    - invoking permament equivalent of the Rain of Doom over the whole Blessed Island;
    - forcefully stopping the reincarnation cycle in the whole Creation;
    - letting all the people in the creation become creatures of singular and supernatural power at the cost of falling out of the reincarnation cycle
    - bending the world (to make it round-shaped);
    - taking the ability to perform sorcery back from the mankind.

    All of these seem to me to be within the tone of the setting. On the other hand, it would seem awkward if players were allowed to perform these at Essence 3 and to actually have a good chance at succeeding at them at Essence 5. This applies essentially to anything that you would describe as non-subtle changes to the metaphysics of the setting.

    Note that at least some of the above examples are imaginable at least as story hooks (things attempted by NPCs). However, at such an ultra-high fantasy game as Exalted, I would be strongly disinclined to just leave the specifics of how is it done to ST's fiat. Player characters should be given some specific conditions upon which thery are allowed to attempt these workings.

    So what do you think about it? Maybe such ambitious deeds are in fact not within the scope of the sorcery? Or maybe one should introduce a subcategory of Solar workings available at higher Essence, so that players are given possibility of performing them but much later in the game than Essence 3? One answer, I would be rather unhappy with is "just handwave it," as it essentially amounts just to not having a well-specified answer (which is not a bad thing in general, but seems rather unpleasent in Exalted, where the whole point of the system is to allow people getting at this absurdly epic stuff you don't do in other games).

              My reasoning was basically as follows:

              1. The limits of sorcery are given quite explicitly and the listed workings do not go beyond these limits.
              2. All the listed working seem thematically something that could happen in Creation some day, but this would be all be completely world-changing and epic even for the standards of the system. Some of them would virtually start a new era.
              3. I could imagine each of those workings as the part of the storyline of the system (like some crazy events somewhere in primordial wars).
              4. To me, large part of the fun with Exalted is that there is no wide gap between what is doable in the setting and what is doable in the actual play. There are actually precise conditions how to do all this epic stuff that happened long ago in forgotten eras.
              5. They seem inappropriate as Solar circle workings precisely for the reasons you have both given.

              You both strongly support the answer that none of these should be ever available in play. I partly understand this. On the other hand, are you really willing to say that none of the listed events could ever happen in the Creation? (Putting the question whether players should be able to do it aside.)

              Basically, what bothers me is this dissonance between what sorcery ultimately seems capable of in the setting and what may expected within the game experience. Again, in the games I normally run this is not a problem, but this dissonance seems rather unsettlingin Exalted, where it is almost the point of the system that those legendary stories are part of gaming experience.

                          I don't necessarily agree with all of those as being possible Solar Workings, but the ones I do agree with are pretty much Ambition 3, which requires 75 successes. A maxxed out Adamant Solar Sorcerer still only gets about 11 successes per roll and, on these sort of things, you want Ambition 5, so you're subtracting 4 successes for 7 successes per roll total. So, we'll want ten rolls.

                          Which is...unfortunately, not as infeasible to pull off as I'd hoped.

                          I will say that all Workings are supposed to be conversations between the Player and the ST and, at this level, it definitely seems like the other players should be included. If the Player wants to Rain of Doom the entire Blessed Isle, then it should be discussed with everyone at the table and figured out, before it gets attempted. I can see some games where everyone is like "Oh, Yeah! That's totally what we want!" and everyone having the best time planning for how to carry that out and using that to break down the Realm while the Dawn's armies work through the Satrapies and so on.

                          But, if it's going to wreck the game, this is a thing you need to talk with everyone about before you allow them to attempt it. Simply going "...oh, no, that's in this other category of workings and you can't do those yet" is only going to delay the problem not actually deal with it.


                          There was a GURPS book on Time Travel which described this as the "Hardware/Software Mismatch problem." They said that, if you're setting up a time travel game and you intend for it to be a sneaky game of undercover time cops trying to stop similarly undercover time crooks from creating paradoxes and being creative with period tech, but what your players want to do is hunt Nazis for sport, there are going to be issues that can't actually be resolved by game mechanics or in setting rules. Saying "Temporal Mechanics won't let you take the BFG 9000 back to Ancient Rome" is going to provoke a long series of debates where the players try to find a way to do that anyway or, barring that, build one out of local parts the moment they arrive.

                          The way to solve the problem is by talking to the players about what they really want and if everyone wants to go back to Rome and fight the legions with machine guns, you need to at least meet them half way.

                          Alternately, if the players are down for the "Sneaky Time Cops" game then saying "Time Travel works like in Terminator, only living things can be sent back in time" or whatever, should be perfectly fine because it helps enforce the game everyone wants to play.


                          So, my suggestion is, don't worry that a Working is theoretically capable of breaking the game in the abstract. Apply yourself to making sure you and the players are on the same page and then, IF someone comes to you and says "....Can I do a Working that destroys the ability of everyone ever to do Sorcery?" you should at least know why they want to do that and might be able to suggest something less...drastic. Or it'll make complete sense to you why their character wants to do that and maybe it'll make for a cool game. Heck maybe the player doesn't care if they succeed at the attempt, but their character is deep in grief and limit break and this is definitely what they'd try to do then, so now everyone need to try to talk them down from this, before it implodes messily.

                          I'm not saying that this is a bad system or trying to rule zero it or anything, mind you. I'm saying that, if you have someone trying to break the game, then that's indicative of a deeper issue that no amount of game mechanics will solve and that you need to talk to your player at that point.
