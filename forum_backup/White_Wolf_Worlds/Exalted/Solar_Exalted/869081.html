    Solar Role Charms

    04-20-2016, 12:03 AM
    So a few months or so ago I had this idea for a character who was kind of like a Creation version of an Adeptus administratum official. Basically he's got two retainers that carry around his massive fucking book that might as well say "Da Rules" on it and he leverages the massive power of the bureaucracy to his advantage. If you want to deny him access to your records, or tell him he can't rent you a ship he flips the book open to Chapter 4.2, secton 1, paragraph a, sub-paragraph (j) which has this terribly specific little rule that would make things very very hard for you. He'd use this to duck superiors, leverage people, get places he wasn't supposed to, and in emergencies he'd pull out the short daiklave hidden in the spine of the book and kick some ass. He's just a small cog in a big machine, but he uses that to his advantage. After all, you can argue with the boss, but you can't argue with "I'm sorry sir, it's the rules. Take it up with management if you have a problem. Now in the mean time let me past and give me your helmet."

    The thing is, the Solar charms don't really do a lot of that kind of stuff and I wonder if it's by design. Some of the bureaucracy charms don't even function if you're a little cog in a big machine, they only really work when you're da-boss.That got me thinking, what other areas are there that Solars don't really go because of their inherent "I'm the big important hero here." style. Giving quiet advice?

    Solars certainly can give advice, but they seem to be a lot more like the Avatar from Last Airbender, where their advice is more of a prophetic warning with a huge weight to it, and less a friendly whisper from the dude behind the throne. Getting bonuses from being subservient as well, for example I don't see a Solar charm getting free dice if they're acting under orders from a superior. Or maybe a charm that specifically only works when they're working under somebody, or retracing lore or crafts from a long ago master.

    Solars are kind of the frontline fighters, the kings, the prophets, the generals, the champions, the savants. That leaves a lot of room for the advisors, apprentices, retainers, squires, ect. Obviously there's a lot Solars can do that aren't covered by charms, but am I just seeing an omission of boring effects, or a design choice?

      Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
      The thing is, the Solar charms don't really do a lot of that kind of stuff and I wonder if it's by design. Some of the bureaucracy charms don't even function if you're a little cog in a big machine, they only really work when you're da-boss.
      I think it should work. You can always structure the organization so that the Solar is in charge of a significant branch of it so that they can use most of their charms, but not high enough to be calling the shot. I imagine most Solar Guildsmen will be in charge of their own business, but they won't have a seat at the Hierarchs' table, and they'd certainly be able to use their Bureaucracy charms.

      For everything else... I guess it depends on what kind of character you're playing? Solars are naturally better, so it's normal for them to gravitate towards positions of leadership in any meritocracy, but if you play a character with low social abilities - or social abilities that don't lend themselves to managing groups - or a character who's hyper-focused and doesn't have access to secondary abilities that support their given profession, you'd be more likely to fill a support role. It's kind of like Doctor Strange. You might be the greatest medical practitioner in Creation, but you won't be performing surgeries if you have Dexterity 1. Just apply that to different professions.

                                      What my intuitive response would be is that's stuff would seem right as "Any human archetype up to 11" (if that's what we think of the Solars as being).

                                      It's more subversive than I would think of immediately for Solars (they don't by default seem to me to challenge or subvert the idea that the big, obvious, heroic, important, inspiring, mythic looking guy is actually as important as he looks, so much as play to it, even though yes, that heroic guy can be secret, not so much hiding in plain sight, or innocuous and plain). But, not verboten, to me.

                                      The only thing I'd try to intuit about why that stuff is lacking (if so, and if intentionally so) is that it might more pragmatically be a thing of nudging Solars' players towards high responsibility and leadership roles and away from being the second to NPCs and other Solar PCs (even if they actually hold power).
