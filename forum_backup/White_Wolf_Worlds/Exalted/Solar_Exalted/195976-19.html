                Disregarding all other texts, I have had a few thoughts regarding the new Edition having an idea of Lunars possessing the role of being guardians of the Realm (as a matter of appointment and relationship and a suitable common application for their particular talents, rather than Guardianship being part of their makeup), and the idea that this meant there were occasions in the history of the Realm where the Lunars collectively held more political power than the Solars. At the very least for having a greater unity of purpose than contemporary Solars might have had.

                I do think the writing needs to strike a suitable balance between the power of Exalted based on what they are, and on what they've done. It doesn't quite need to be unambiguous, but maybe a bit acknowledged. The Solars are the mightiest of the Exalted, but the Dragon Blooded are still the rulers of Creation (and even though the return of the Solars shakes the status quo, that doesn't necessarily mean that the Terrestrials aren't coming through the Time of Tumult with a lot of their prestige intact). Solars might have Charms that convey the motif of being the mightiest swordfighter in the world, but the actual title goes to... well, maybe any number of people based on all kinds of criteria, but generally folks who've won lots of sword fights, so it's still up for grabs by other Exalted (in a manner that doesn't fall neatly into "winning by cheating" or even "winning despite not being as good").

                    My problem is, again, that if you look at it like that (as a game of trying to find niches that "even a Solar could not fill" inside of areas a Solar would normally be presumed competent at) you risk turning Solars into the jack-of-all-trade, master-of-none Exalted as everything important gets claimed by someone else.

                    Obviously a Sidereal or a Lunar can do things a Solar can't (there's no Solar charm quite like Efficient Secretary Technique, for instance); but in terms of overarching themes, I don't feel you can give Sidereals a generic "Sidereals can learn faster than Solars" statement without cutting too deeply into the Twilight caste -- and it would only take a few cuts like that to turn Twilights from masterful scholars to generic jack-of-all-trade scholars.

                    Especially when your other example is that Lunars are as good or better than Solars at social ability, which is, uh. Look. Solar preeminence is the central conceit of the game. That doesn't mean that other people don't sometimes get better charms or specific tricks that exceed Solars, but in general the idea is that Solars are overwhelming in raw power or skill within their areas, and at straightforward expressions through those things; other Exalted define themselves according to more specific overarching narratives that are deliberately set up to be tangential to the Solar one -- and to each other -- in terms of their overall direction. It is this overall direction that gives you your broad area of competence as a Sidereal or a Lunar, not bits you carve off of the Solar Exalted. If you want to be the overall best scholar or emissary or whatever, you're probably best off with a Solar; if you want to be a mysterious sage of the stars, a hidden adviser or a secretive old master, you might be better off with a Sidereal.

                    You'll have specific Sidereal charms to help you play a learned and wise character in your own unique Sidereal way, but the best ones (the ones, like Efficient Secretary Technique, that do things Solars don't) will generally have a unique Sidereal flavor. Efficient Secretary Technique is a Sidereal charm not because Jenna Moran said "I'm going to make Sidereals even better at learning than Solars," it's a Sidereal charm because it operates via a weird cheaty mechanism that doesn't really make sense for anyone else, with broad efficient power at what it does but unusual limitations related to the Sidereal thematic (it doesn't just make the statement that Sidereals are wise, it makes the statement that they are wise in a specific unique Sidereal way.) So what you won't see is Solars getting a learning charm that learns at speed X, and then Sidereals getting a learning charm that just learns at speed X + 1 because lol let's give Sidereals the niche of learning faster.

                    You're not entirely wrong in that Lunar emissaries are (notionally, at least) good at having a versatile skillset being able to switch tracks quickly -- especially from negotiation to violence -- but if you're talking "best at raw social force", no. That's not part of how Lunars are defined anyway.

                                        No, but I think concluding that there can be no Twilight Charm that enables learning at that sort of speed, because Air Aspects are "the fast-learning Exalts," would be unwarranted.

                                        You can look at powers like Elemental Bolt or various bender-style Dragon-Blood powers and understand why Solars don't get them - elemental manipulation is not their thing. Solars don't generally get Charms to create a swarm of entangling, poisonous vines, or to shape the battlefield with tornadoes or spontaneous bulwarks of earth. These tasks are not in line with their themes - the closest they're likely to come is a Survival Charm to mitigate nearby hazards or construct shelter, and even then it's going to fall closer to "angelic miracle" than "elemental manipulator."

                                        On the other hand, is there any particular reason that "read and absorb the contents of this book at super-speed" is outside the Solar paradigm? Well, no - that's an application of peerless mastery at scholarship, which is... kind of the Twilight's bag. That seems like the kind of thing a Solar absolutely could develop Charms to do, even if there aren't any published. (Which may not even be true - Harmonious Academic Methodology will let you buy a dot of Lore after a five-hour training montage.)

                                        The point is that you can't look at Charm lists and conclude that any specific thing they don't have a Charm for is necessarily someone else's bailiwick - that pretty literally misses the forest for the trees. Solar Charms derive from the overall Solar theme of "surpassing excellence at all human endeavors," and their hypothetical Charm space has to be interpreted in a way that supports that thematic notion. The place where the other Exalted stand distinct is when they pursue something that isn't what we'd generally think of as a human endeavor, like "become a primal shapeshifting monster," or "command the elements," or "weave the skein of Fate from the palace of the gods."

                                                            Gayo,

                                                            While I deeply respect your views on Exalted, I find I must disagree with your disagreement about the role of, basically, ethics in a game of Exalted. However, I do fully agree that the only way to handle these issues is starting from a point where the Solars are the most powerful Exalts.

                                                            The Solar Exalted are Kings. They may not deserve it, but that's what they are. (Note: It's not necessarily who they are.) And yes, those Kings fell, and fell horribly, to the point that they lost the Mandate of Heaven (in a very Chinese sense). However, unlike the Chinese sense of the term, legitimacy was not conferred on their successors in the Shogunate or, worse, the Scarlet Empress. This is noted by the Ministry of Heaven's regard (or lack of it) for those successor states. Note: Being a King doesn't make you good (or evil). It makes you in charge.

                                                            Now, the Solar Exalted are returning. Should everyone bend over for them? Hell no. Because while being Kings is in their Exaltation, does that make them right? Emphatically, no. This goes all the way back to Euthyphro where it is asked, "Is something good because the gods will it?" The answer in almost all Western (classical) ethical systems is no, because the alternative is arbitrary.

                                                            I fully agree that everyone's reactions to the Solars is, 'How do I benefit from them?' But that's their reaction. What is the Solar's reaction? You have a human being who is basically given phenomenal cosmic power, and one of the core questions asked in the game, narratively speaking, is, "What will the Solars do with their power?" They're given little-to-no-guidance or limits on their power. So what do they use it for?

                                                            I think this is what's behind the 'humanizing' aspects of Solars in 3E. What does it mean to be human? Are you defined by your limitations? Your actions? Your desires? All? None? With the Solars, you take away the limitations of being human... and nothing else. (Lunars increasingly lose their human shapes, Sidereals lose their Freedom, etc.) What happens? Is a 'human being without limits' still capable of doing human beings, or do you lose your humanity when you lose the limitations of humanity? Note that the setting and rules don't answer the question; they ask the players to answer it for their characters.

                                                            These are all fascinating questions, and should be explored in a Solar game. I wish I could quote the 1E core book, but I'm fairly certain this is exactly what's called out.

                                                            Now, this doesn't mean that they're 'good'. They can repeat the mistakes of the First Age. But that's not a guarantee. The Great Prophecy summarized several possibilities, and the Sidereals in their hubris and risk-aversion decided on the 'safer' option. If you reduce the Solar to 'most powerful splat' without tying it to ethical dilemmas, you just made a mockery of the pivotal event in Creation's history, the Usurpation. But that also doesn't mean that the Solars have some sort of untainted legitimacy that means people should not just do what they say, but be happy about it.

                                                            With all respect.
