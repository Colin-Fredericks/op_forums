                                    Originally posted by Ferryman View Post

                                    Nah. The first couple dots of a Favored or Caste Ability is one of the most cost-efficient uses of XP in the game, and lots of entry-level combat Charms have low Ability minimums (he is a Dawn, remember). He can pour XP into those areas while relying on high Attributes to shore up his dice pools in the short run, and in the long run he'll have higher pools and dice caps in his physical areas than he would otherwise since he won't have to pay punishing costs to raise his Attributes with XP later on. Any short term pain will disappear very quickly, and long term he'll probably be better off since the alternative would have been paying 2BP per dot to raise an Ability to 5.

                                    It's not just a good build. Given the circumstances it was almost certainly the optimal one. It probably shouldn't be, but it is. Like I said, not a new problem.
                                    Mmm, it's a very fun, interesting build, but I'm pretty sure it's not at all optimal for an Ability Exalt. The issue is that nobody really needs the kind of uniformly huge Attributes this build creates, and an optimizer wouldn't generally shoot for them except for how temptingly cheap the tertiaries are, so you would typically not be doing that with your XP in the first place. Hence, comparing the XP cost of raising Attributes to that level rather than raising Abilities is a bit of a red herring, because under normal circumstances you'd just pick Physical primary, dump one or two stats of little value to you in your other categories (which are much more dump-friendly) to ensure you have good ratings in the things that matter, and then not do much to raise Attributes after that. From the sounds of it, this character ended up with around 34 dots in Attributes, which is almost unheard of -- nobody without Attribute charms takes their Attributes that high with XP because it's just not really worth it. Hence, figuring out how much it would cost to get them to that level in play isn't terribly informative. I'm not much of a bean-counter, but if you're going to bean-count it only really makes sense in terms of some hypothetical end-state build you plan to converge on regardless of what you look like in chargen.

                                    Bear with me a moment.

                                    Let's say you hope to end up with three fives and two fours in your Abilities, which is a pretty realistic middle-of-the-road spread for an experienced Solar. If you start with all those things at 3, that's 46 XP. Unlike the huge attribute build, you probably will actually want to get these dots one way or another, since they're prerequisites for things. If you got those 8 dots in chargen, it'd cost 16 BP, so the optimization question is: what can you do with your Attributes for 5 BP and 46 XP?

                                    From the way this was described (and looking at it from an bean-counting optimization standpoint), I'm going to suppose this person is 5/5/4 physical, 5/5/1 primary, 5/3/1 secondary, where the ratings are assigned however you want within categories. It might not be exactly that, but that's the "best" setup from a purely bean-counting perspective. Now, if you put physical primary and don't put any BP in Attributes just yet, you end up with 5/5/1 physical, 5/3/1 secondary, 5/1/1 tertiary.

                                    Put 4 BP into raising your secondary 3 to a 4. Spend the last BP on, I guess, a Merit dot, since what else can you do with 1 BP with no Favored Abilities? At this point, if you wanted to get up to 5/5/4, 5/5/1, 5/3/1 it would cost you 52 XP, which is indeed a little more -- more than is offset by the extra Merit dot, at least. But let's say you stop at 5/5/4, 5/5/1, 5/2/1. That's 44 XP. Do you really want that one extra attribute dot in your least-important attribute category more than anything else you could get for 8 Solar XP? I can't see it -- in practice, most people would stop at much lower Attributes if they were buying them in play, so it looks to me like those sales that encourage you to "save" money by spending it on something you don't really want. Even the 6-XP advantage shown here evaporates quickly if you aren't willing to stomach the two Attributes at one.

                                    This is probably kind of a long digression for me to get into given my well-known antipathy toward bean-counting. But I just really like math problems.

                                    Edit: Actually, I'm a bit daft here -- you can still do better than what I posted. On Abilities, just get the three fives for 12 BP, buy the secondary Attribute 3 to a 5, then use your XP to buy the two Ability 4s and the remaining 5 attribute dots, and that actually gets you to the exact same end state for precisely the same amount of XP, except you have one extra merit dot. Optimization! It's stupid but fun.
