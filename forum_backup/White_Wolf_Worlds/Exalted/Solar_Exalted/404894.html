    How have you assembled your PCs' Solar Circles?

    02-13-2015, 03:23 PM
    I'm gearing up to run an Exalted game fairly soon (whenever the backer PDFs hit) and while I've been trying to roughly sketch out plot notions, something else has come more front-and-center for me: how the PCs all know each other to "group up" together, whether that group has formed wholly in backstories, or happens due to events in the first few game sessions. I'm not sure I'd prefer if everyone in the group knew each other, or even knew everyone was a Solar, when the game starts (at the very least I'm going to ask that every PC have met at least 1 other PC before the game starts, to create a little web of acquaintance and prior plot to draw from). But I also want to avoid something cliche like "you all respond to a posting for 'strong-backed labour' in a bar" scenarios as well.

    And I'm talking specifically Solars, since a lot of other Exalt types have built-in reasons for individuals to be assigned with and work together (not to mention, y'know, Solars being the only playable splat in the 3e Core).

    At any rate it stands to reason that with chargen Solars having been Exalted for a year or two already, they may have made friends and enemies before the game starts.

    So I'm wondering, what reasons have you/your groups provided for why the Solar PCs are working together when the game starts? Or what circumstances have conspired to get a group of people who don't know each other to work together?

                          Here's an approach:

                          Assign a "big bad" of the game. This is important, to ensure commonality.

                          Bad Scenario that Taught Me this:
                          I once was in a game where the Eclipse was totally friendly with ghosts and the dead, a fearless messenger who thought it great to preserve dead cultures (and maybe feed criminals to them); meanwhile, the Twilight was a refugee from Thorns who despised Mask of Winters and all things undead -- not a friendly duo. In that same game, we had the Dawn who dreamed of crushing the Immaculate Order (and many, many Terrestrial Exalted) under his golden-heeled sandals -- while the Night Caste was a Patrician who still loved the Realm as the height of civilization and sought only to peacefully earn their favor --- another horrid duo.

                          Then use that "big bad" in a one-shot session with one of those players. It doesn't even need to be formal - play video games and just talk it out - but you need to "end" with them there.

                          Ideal Scenario:
                          The Night was hired to spy on Thorns - purely business - and while doing so, during the session, spies a valuable artifact. She tries to steal it - but the session ends with her captured.

                          The Twilight claims to be a refugee from Thorns. I suggest rumors of friends and family - still living - reach him, and he's motivated to sneak back into the city.

                          The Dawn decides she was a Golden Janissary in life - so she'd oppose Thorns on principle. Great. Undead attack her dojo (seeking to stop the spread of the style) and the clothing on their corpses suggests Thorns. The Dawn opts to investigate (to try to intercept any other corpses on the way - and maybe stop the source).

                          The Eclipse must understand that Mask of Winters is the "big bad" - but that's fine. He decides he's dealt with a couple other Deathlords before and is being sent as an emissary between nations, nothing more. He's not friendly, simply fulfilling his duties. In the session, I play up the more horrid aspects of Thorns (ensuring it's far more vile than whatever he's encountered in other places).

                          etc.

                          Then try to force them together during the first session together.

                          Mask of Winters is in Stygia at the moment - but a few Abyssals remain in Thorns. Perhaps the Dawn goes apeshit and begins smashing undead in the streets. The Night sees the light from her jail cell - and by pure luck, the Dawn hurls a body shattering through the wall, providing a means of escape. The Eclipse is in the streets during the commotion, having just delivered his package and message - and then panics upon seeing the fighting, thinking the locals will associate him with them. The Twilight sees the uproar and noticed the Eclipse' caravan - and so hijacks it, giving it to his family so they can flee the city, staying behind to deal with the locals.

                          Now you got 4 Solar Exalts in various stages of distress, fighting undead and making a scene.

                          From then, pray you can keep them together. >_>

                              I pretty much always start Exalted with "You are all in the same market when shit goes down."

                              I try to identify enough about the characters so they they will all take the bait, but it works as a natural way to assemble the group. Everyone fights for the same cause, at least in this one moment, and all know that they are probably targets of the Wyld Hunt and much more likely to live if they stick together.

                              "You are all in the same market when shit goes down," is pretty much the Exalted equivalent of DnD's "you all meet in a Tavern."

                              Never run a DB or Sid Game, and Abyssals and Inferanls have always been a mixed game.

                              A Dynastic DB game would basically have all of the characters having known each other since childhood. An all Sidereals, or Abyssals, or Infernals game would be assigned to work together.

                                      For games where the party hasn't met in advance and the game doesn't come equipped with a pretext that everyone has bought into, I've seen it done two ways: the first is that the party happens to be in the same place when at least one of them flares anima, and that's interesting enough for them to come together. This requires a measure of buy-in, but you pretty much need a willingness to roll with it to run a circle that spontaneously assembles in the intro.

                                      The other option is A Sidereal Did It. In Solar games, Gold Faction Sidereals fill the roll of the tavern wizard/Mr. Johnson/vampire elder/etc., but beyond that, Sidereals are an in-setting justification for narrative contrivances, which is helpful given that you often need narrative contrivances to kick off a game of that type.

                                      It always works best if the GM can assemble everyone's motivations into a single plot hook, but epic motivation made this surprisingly tough.

                                          In my previous campaign, all PCs started out on the same ship. Some had previous links, some didn't. One (the captain) was a pirate and a smugler, and the others were part of the passengers for this particular trip. During their travels, they encountered other pirates, an uppity sea-god asking for human sacrifice, and strange artifacts coins that brought the dead back to unlife. Needless to say, when they finished their weeks-long trip, they had a good idea of who the others were, and a sort of camaraderie had already started to appear.

                                          I also started a campaign (as a player) fleeing from rumors of an approaching Wyld Hunt. We met each other in the wilderness, found out that we were all going the fuck away, and ended up grouping up together to have a better chance of surviving.

                                          One of the best way to do it IMHO is to have links between at least some of the PCs (long-lost childhood friends, previous business partners, adventuring partners, whatever...) that are present since before the start of the campaign (either from before or after their Second Breath). That way, it gets easier to go from the initial contact to a situation where they're willing to stay together for a while and watch out for each other.

                                                  I started my most recent Solar circle by having each player decide how they knew two other PCs as mortals; when character creation was complete, I began play with all of them falling, five miles up in the air, animas at full brilliance, a floating sky-citadel further above them, and a band of hawkfolk attempting to either kill or recapture them, with no memory of where they were or what they had been doing immediately prior.
