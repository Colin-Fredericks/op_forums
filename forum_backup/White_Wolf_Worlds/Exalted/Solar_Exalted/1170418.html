    If you could change the Solar Charmset

    12-25-2017, 10:00 AM
    What are the greatest strengths and weaknesses of the Solar Charmset, and how would you change it if you could?

    The reason I'm asking is because I'm working on a Solar conversion for Machineborn and I don't think that simply referencing corebook Charms is fun. I could use the opportunity to try to improve the Solar Charms instead. I've already done some changes to prevent bloat and to alter effects to match the balance of my own setting, but I'm aware that some people have strong opinions about certain aspects of the Solar Charmset and I'm curious to know what these opinions are and if I can address them.

                      Craft has some clear problems that have come up in play for a lot of people - myself included, and I've seen other PCs in games I've been in face the same problem. The more Craft charms you have... the less you craft. You go from the guy who's always building interesting things, to the guy who does nothing, then suddenly generates an artifact.

                      Other than that, nearly all the charmsets are frankly great. I would change war, but I'm not fond of the war system (battle groups just arent fun to command), but that's more a system problem than a charmset problem. Investigation is the only other one I feel needs changed. Not to any existing charm, they're great, besides maaaybe Mind Manse Meditation, but the charmset is just... tiny. Did you know Investigation has the least charms of any ability? As low as War is, about 9 charms less than the next lowest. I've been writing up solar investigation charms in my spare time to expand the charmset and build off of what's there, since I think it'd be a wonderful ability and very appealing supernal with just a little more content to it.

                              Originally posted by Maris Streck View Post

                              I would remove the whole Charm system and replace with something simpler, like the Mage spheres system. I hate spending so much time checking every single charm for what it does, even 15 of them make the game a mess of constant skimming though the manual. Exalted is born with emphasis on coreography and narration, I'd have more of that and less rules.
                              I actually jumped into the thread to post exactly this.

                              I would love to have something like a row for each Ability (or action type, for Lunars and the like) and a column for each modification. Much along the lines of the Arcana and Practices.

                              This could become the replacement of one mess with another mess, so let me explain what I'd like to see.

                              So, for example, we could see that in Melee, we would have a Charm that changes your Defense, and a Charm that doubles your Withering Damage, and a Charm that lets you resist Overwhelming. Melee would be the analog to the Arcanum, while the Defense, Withering Damage, and Overwhelming Resistance would be analogs to the Practices.

                              Since this turns into a bit of a grid naturally, we could see right away what the shape of the "trees" might be. What's missing, what's redundant, what might be good places to work in an Evocation, and so on. It would also give a good hint how expensive something should be, if a new Charm or Evocation was made and added.

                              And we could also see visually what an Ability is meant to be good at all across the game line.



                              I'd also really like a rethink on Charms that are triggered by weird conditions, like rolling a Yahtzee in your successes. And about a Charm resetting after certain things happen a certain number of times. That's basically writing a Charm to be forgotten during play, or if not forgotten then to bog the game down with book lookups. I can't imagine being able to keep up with multiple Charm sets in detail, in any sort of mixed Circle game.



                              Also, nonstandard rounding (sometimes round up, sometimes round down) needs to be nuked.



                              I have one last idea for this post, but it's half baked so far. (It's baking as my home table does some more playtesting!) I wish we had some sort of board or play sheet or "heads-up-display" view of what a turn looks like. Every place where the rules can be modified would have a spot where a matching Charm Card could be clipped or filled in, and that would show you exactly what you're rolling and what the results turn into - and if you trigger any funky alternate rules or Charm opportunities.

                              In my head it looks sort of like a diagram on a Blackjack table, with places to put all your Charm cards: When you elect to activate a Charm in your upcoming turn, you can put your Essence tokens on top of those cards, and then as you work through your roll you have a visual reference for all of the modifiers you're invoking.

                              Check out Momentum Exalted!

                                  I think the biggest thing to address in general, is standardizing effects. The game presents quite a few standard dice-trick options, and then way too many Charms invent brand new ones. Especially dump any that involve rerolling dice, or rolling more dice as the bonus, because that's annoyingly slow. I'd rather a Charm give me double 9s and says I can't botch the action being boosted, than reroll 1s until none appear. It's not the WoD were 1s subtract successes and eliminating them is useful beyond negating being able to botch a roll.

                                      Strengths
                                      I personally like that different Aiblities value different dice effects more. For instance, in Craft and Awareness, you basically see things going all-out, while in Melee you see some things limited since if they had every single dice trick as easy as those first two, it'd break the system in half. I also am one who thinks that some Abilities having some pretty cool "to themselves" takes is pretty neat, like the "roll a straight" one in Melee, or the Occult one needing a 666. I like the variety of dice tricks a bit myself since it helps in giving Abilities different feels, and knowing that there is a toolbox of them helps I think for the game's future development, as some effects the Solar ones touch on might make for good stuff to distinguish other Exalted for instance. I could see a Chosen of the Dice being all about risky effects that cause cool effects on doubles, straights, and all that.

                                      Tied to this a bit is how the use of some mechanics are not shared as a way to distinguish Abilities too. Melee doens't have thes ame toolkit as Brawl, as Thrown, as and so on. Presence does different stuff from Performance, even if they apply to similar rolls in game. Things like that I find pretty cool and interesting.

                                      I like how exotic Solar Charms can get in 3e, but still to some extent fill in aspects of "Badass events characters in movies and literature do as being really smart, powerful people." God-King Shrike to me is like the scientist who figures out a disastor is coming and has to warn people. Dual-Magnus Prana has this feel of trying to repliate some of my favorite scenes in heist films. The Persona stuff in Socialize reminds me of things like The Great Gatsby or Fight Club. So generally cool stuff there too.

                                      Exalted since 1e is about the kind of widgety feel fo Charms and I'm not myself of the feeling that it does it service to go to something freeform. The combat engine is interesting, but I think that it doesn't really by design lend itself to something more Mage-like, which has a very stripped down overall resolution system before magic (Chronicles of Darkness at the end of the day is a system without a lot of wiggle room in itself and I think this shows with how powers can break it so easy or get repetetive in otherw ays at times). Anything freeform or standardizeda st talked above probably results in a fundamentally different game from what the Exalted system we have.

                                      I like that Charm trees are deep and have a lot of Charms as an idea myself. The 3e Charmset has this thing to me I like wherein two characters can Supernal an Ability and probably have pretty different takes on it or depths. I like how most Abilities have a vareity of avenues and themes to them to explore, which also helps in this. Even Abilities like Dodge have things to support the nimble character who gets in and out of fights versus one who cranks her Evasion up, or one who fights this kind of "Initiative sink" style.

                                      Personal Issues
                                      So yeah, litany of issues despite I think perks.

                                      First, I think that while I don't think the system needs to be as mechancial as I have seen some fan rewrites, some interactions and systems should have been defiend ahead of time, more clearly, and with more formal langauge. Like, I like the bolding of decisive and whithering attacks. I like the use of she for user and he for target of Charms (I have a personal hate-on of second person writing that some rewrites try for). And I like the use of double-N as a term in places. I think more formal bits could have been done like this, such as:

                                      - More terms like double-N. Things like eliminate-N for stuff that is the "roll N until N no longer appears" effects. In fact, It hink that's the biggest one of "Why wasn't this keyworded?" for me.
                                      - Full Excellency as a term should have been defined in the Excellency section, not in Charm effects later on.
                                      - Similar on things like "winning Join Battle" not being defiend in one place.

                                      I think the definition of Supplemental and Reflexive wasn't stuck to enough and some effects could hav worked better with that.

                                      Some Charms I think over-estimate their effect, or at least in context of other Charms. This is an issue with Craft for me (see my houserules for more there as self-plug, hint-hint, nudge-nudge). Craft overvalues points on early Charms, undervalues them in later Charms, overvalues slots in general, and ludicrously overvalues repairing stuff.

                                      Some charms get a good mechanic and kind of run it into the dirt at the expense of other Charms. I Like like though that there are chains on that, but soem chains probably end up being expaned at the expense of others for still overly binary effects. Survival could have more depth in the survivng, had less in the Familiar buffing, for instance. I think Ride could have had more stuff like Hero Rides Away, less on charging around. I actually honestly think more stuff like Salty Dog Method and Seasoned Criminal Method would have been awesome around for more Abilities like Craft, Performance and such.

                                      I think a lot of upgrade Charms more and more are better just done as repurchase for wordcount reasons and for keeping Charm trees a bit less "linear". I hate how deep Craft goes though at times pretty dry and "upgradey" effects that a character might not care to have all of.

                                      The Performance double-9 Charms kind of annoy me in a way that no other Charms do. If all they do is double-9s, they shouldn't be separate Charms. If they are to be so, I think most could have had soem of their "follow up" effects rolled into them. Make them "Some thing and double-9s as a bonus" and less "Double-9s as gateway Charm" as they are I think.

                                      I would probably get rid of effects that encourage decision peralysis. Like, my houserules simplify the hell out of Bright-Forging Prana. Rather than pile of Charms that you have to pick on a laundry list, just have a simpler "power up" effect.

                                      I would probably also get rid fo anything that manipulated the xp economy, though I don't mind the one in Craft to use wxp to fuel other Charms (that seems about what that shoudl be I guess). But the Lore tree probably could do with having its xp-manipulation shortened, or put somehwere in branches that will caues less havoc for STs if they want to keep them in there.

                                      Personas are a great idea. Their execution is terrible with their furthering of using xp as an economy for it and the way you build characters. I think it should have just...been simpler. I honestlyw onder if Personas would have done better to be built as a form of QuicK Character with simpler effects and Charms, rather than the xp mess they are by default. That's at least how I'd do it. Right now they're the worst of both worlds in the previosu two issues.

                                      There's a few Charms with an air-breathing mermaid feel, like the one to train characters with learning disabilites, which is also kind of a basis for too much other things. I think if a Charm implies something overly narrative in its basis, it shoudl probably be its own thing, or lead elsewhere. Or just don't put them in if they only exist to solve questions that the base system doesn't have resoultion for. Or make them just...do more if they do. Again, nothing wrong with more Salty Dog Method-style effects that consolidate ab unch of little things.

                                      Some charms have a tendency to "rot" on the character sheet after a point, not because they're replaced by better Charms, but their effects become increasingly meaningless as the game goes along. Brass Scales Falling si a good example of this. It doesnt's cale really at all, and later Charms and enough crafting makes it ultimately irrelevant. Charms like that should just scale a bit more in general I think.

                                      I think the weapon-summoning Charms could have been a bit better. THey feel mopey compared to just spending Merit dots on Artifact weapons. (My houserule here is they can be used as part of a Ready Weapon action or can charge weapons ala the armor charms).

                                      Multi-attacks probably could have done well to have just a general discussion in the core rules. Even if the base system doesn't allow them, they feel like something which shoudl be discussed in the base game's architecture. I think that most multi-attack Charms also might give too many attacks without some fall-back. Or by most I mean Melee's.

                                      I think Fivefold Bulwark Stance is too strong and probably shoulnd't be so able to negate like, everything.

                                      War and Investigation could have had deeper trees I think. I coudl see War especially havings ome neat strategy and such things. But I think that's where we need a more robust Strategic Warfare system than we have.

                                      Some Charms have some clause-based issues that could have been clearer. This is a result of them at times having this "String of thought" feel to them, and this resutls in what their total effect being not quite being clear. This goes with tigher writing and I woudl say that at times Charms in the 3e Solar set can be sloppy, for lack of better word on that front.

                                      And yeah, that's the gist of what comes to my mind. A lot of these are things I will probably at some point houserule for my games. Like...soemthing of a simpler Persona system really sits in my head there.
