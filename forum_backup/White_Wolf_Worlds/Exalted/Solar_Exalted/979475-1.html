                Plenty of times family isn't really mentioned in PC backstories since it doesn't really matter or the player isn't really interested in it, and that's fine. As for my own Solars over the years I've had:

                -Ex-slave. Parents never really mentioned, but a big part of the game was getting back to her far-Eastern tribe.
                -Con-artist. Parents still alive, but I don't remember them being an important part of the game.
                -Army medic. Parents never mentioned. Probably still alive. He was married with three children, however, so family was important.
                -Assassin. Father had died from a battle wound, mother killed in a retaliatory raid. So here we go, an orphan. She was raised by her grandparents, however, who are still alive, along with her elder brother. She's got plenty of family.
                -Daughter of a storm ,other. Considering the whole character concept revolves around it, her mother, at least, is still active. Her father was murdered by her mother.
                -Blind investigator. Family was killed before game starts, but it happened while they had already been married, had children, in their thirties. Not a child when it happened, so not technically an orphan I guess.

                So I've had only one orphan, and a mix of family importance otherwise. It really depends on the group, the player, and that player's experience, IME.

                    I inherited my current group of players from a really awful GM, and it took years for them to move past all the things they started doing in response to things the really awful GM would do - most relevantly to this thread, that would be having literally any friend or family that your character was stated as having be immediately and intensely used as a weapon against your character.

                    Character has a sister they like to visit on the holidays? First story arc is that she's been kidnapped and is suffering terrible abuses because someone is mad at your character.
                    Character has parents they regularly speak with? Their house is on fire, they are inside, what do?

                    So the players spent a lot of time playing characters that either didn't have anything/anyone they cared about just trying to get any kind of action besides "save your background details from utter annihilation!"

                    In my experience, all it took was time spent seeing that I wasn't going to do the same things as their really awful GM would do, and the players started wanting to have all those details and connections for their characters so that we could have some of the other kinds of stories that involve family and friends, like pleasant down-time between difficult events of the chronicle, or even mild troubles which they could be helped out of like "I need a new roof but I don't have the money to hire roofers" instead of ransom demands on threat of death.

                    And all that said, if my group ended up coming to the table with a squad of orphans - fate seems like the kind of thing that would draw orphans together into a tight-knit family-like unit, so lets tell that kind of story this time.

                    Not so noble anymore.

                                        Originally posted by Ketrus View Post
                                        The more I think about the original post, the more of an opportunity it seems to be. Your players only think that their characters' parents are dead, after all. You should have a NPC make some honest inquiry as to the PCs family history. As each of them tells the story of how they were orphaned, take careful notes. If you have an Obvious Bad Guy, drop oversized hints about how much he or she resembles one of the PCs. Throw references to the events they mention into every description of every scene, item, character that you can manage. Get your players convinced that not only is the Big Bad one of their fathers or mothers, but that EVERY LAST ONE of their parents is secretly alive somehow.

                                        Of course, none of them are. They're orphans. But allude to the possibility until they strangle you.

                                        That's kind of what I do, though not every single dead person is alive. That is why I find prologues important, a lot of the random NPCs and such in the backstories are going to be brought back throughout the game. A lot people got set on fire too... that's a thing.
