        Originally posted by Omicron View Post
        All right, so...

        The notion of "transhumanism in Exalted" is completely wrong-headed.

        Firt off: all Exalted are transhuman. Exalted, as a game, is neither a transhumanist game, nor a transhumanist setting, nor has any prevalent theme of transhumanism.

        The first thing to realize is that the notion of "transhumanism in Exalted" as presented in this sort of (recurring) discussion is wrong-headed, because it ultimately comes down to an extremely trite defining criterium, which is itself bound up into a problematic perception of the mechanics of the game: transhumanism is seen as Charms with a duration of "Permanent." Crowned With Fury, Murder is Meat, Cosmic Transcendence of (Virtue), all that jazz. Even Shintai Charms belong to that category: they reflect a permanent ability to treat your physical shape as something plastic and malleable.

        What it comes down to is that "transhumanism" in Exalted is defined by its fans as "I don't need to spend motes to do it." This is a problematic perception because it ties into the conception of Charms are individual 'things' that are powered by an inner engine full of discrete 'power units,' and so not truly a part of you. Someone who uses Scar-Writ Saga Shield is transhuman because they permanently make their flesh as hard as steel; someone who uses Spirit-Strengthens-the-Skin is not because they are activating a single power to get a temporary effect.

        This is absurd from an in-setting point of view. A Solar who learns SSS has actually become a hardier person whose flesh is like steel - the fact that this resilience is tiring to sustain and suffers from points of weakness does not change the fact that the Solar has learned, in a very concrete fashion, to change the way their body operates. The fact that Lightning Speed Technique costs "motes" to activate might or might not be a reality in setting, but that doesn't really matter; what it translates practically speaking is that you can go faster than any human being, but that tires you. Wow, that's... literally no different from a futuristic cyborg who would use high-tech implants to run faster. They would probably still get tired. But because Wind-Born Stride has a nice duration of "Permanent," there is this pervasive idea that it transforms your character in a deeper or more meaningful way than LST. It doesn't.

        Solars are transhuman; in improving their own skills, they actualize themselves in a way beyond human achievement, they become more. They are faster, stronger, their memory works better, they have better sensory faculties, they make faster mental connections between elements of which they have knowledge and intuit greater logical leaps. They transform themselves, progressively, into a person with greater faculties, and remove some of the weaknesses of the human condition, making themselves less vulnerable to disease or wounds - though their progression has, of course, limits, and no Solar can do all of that at once. Solars are transhuman, and so are Dragon-Blooded, Lunars, Abyssals - every Exalt acquires capabilities beyond the human range and progressively make themselves immune to some human vulnerabilities.

        And none of this matters. The Exalted are transhuman, and so what? So is Spider-Man, so is Cyborg, so is a 20th level Fighter in Dungeons and Dragons. This does not matter because their setting is not a transhumanist setting. Because the setting does not give any narrative space to the idea that you can, should, or should not change 'humanity,' improve it, make it more or different from what it is. If Exalted were a transhumanist setting, the Exalted would be the avant-garde, the futuristic 'men of tomorrow' presented as an example of what everyone could become. It would ask questions about whether or not that's a good idea and the practical considerations of implementing such a transition for mankind as a whole. It would allude to the possibility of providing the benefits of Exaltation to the unExalted - and I am not talking about Power-Awarding Prana.

        Infernals, to a very, very limited extent, had glimpses of that. Cecelyne Charms allowed you to change people and societies - to abolish hunger and twist humans into mutated, 'improved'(?) forms. Pyrian Charms allowed you to transform minds... but mostly it was about brainwashing, the same way Cecelyne was mostly about turning people into grotesque abominations suited to a life in an unforgiving wasteland, not about the betterment of mankind. Infernals were transhuman (like everyone else who starts out human and gets incredible power), but they were not transhumanists.

        What Exalted is, however, is a humanist setting. The fact that humanity as a whole cannot acquire the powers of the Exalted is never presented as a bad thing - it is, in fact, never given much thought at all. Exalted does not offer transformation into something that does not suffer from human weaknesses and limits as a solution to mankind's woes; it offers these solutions as being achievable only through human means. How do you solve war? By facilitating international relationships and securing peace between nations. How do you solve hunger? By arranging a better distribution of wealth and produce. By putting an end to the societal conditions that led to starving people. How do you solve sickness? By reducing war so that they are no armies of soldiers shitting in the water supplies!

        Exalted heroes have the great might to effect these changes and better the world, but they are not, inherently, the key to that change. People (mortals and Exalts alike) could improve this situation themselves; it's just that you are given greater power and opportunity to do so. The solution to the world's plight is not to sit around waiting for a supernatural being to cure it; it is to get up and do something about it - and Solars just have greater incentive and opportunity both to do something about it and to get other people to do something about it as well.

        You change things through reason, through the betterment of society, through education and from freaking common sense ("stop shitting in the water" is 'reason' when you first realize that it's the cause of cholera, 'education' when you tell everyone about it, and 'freaking common sense' once everyone does know about it). You change things by helping people achieve their own potential. The most significant Solar Charms when it comes to the bettering of people are the two Training Charms in Lore and War: they do not transform people, they do not mutate them or give them inhuman gifts. They help them achieve their own potential. They help them realize themselves. These Charms have no sustained costs: your Tiger Warriors do not lose their skills once you cease committing motes to them, they remain perfectly accomplished warriors, just as your Lore-trained scholars remained doctors and scientists and thaumaturges and diplomats and what have you. You have made people better: you have spread reason and education.

        There has always been, in the fanbase, the common idea that a way to better the lot of mankind in Creation is to enlighten mortals. That was a transhumanist element, though it was never conceived of as such by most who advanced it, because 'transhumanism' is a loaded world that evokes Cyberpunk 2077 and Eclipse Phase rather than minor magic. It was also a pretty weird and nonsensical idea. There was this "Essence," this vague power that everyone who mattered in the setting could use, and humans could not, except if they went through a special enlightening process and then they always could forever. Of course having that power would be better than not having it - nevermind that it actually did nothing aside from allow you to skip the fluff of thaumaturgy (and its Resources cost) and just wave your hands and say 'it's done,' or, more problematically, learn Terrestrial Martial Arts and Sorcery. how that's thankfully going away.

        Why thankfully? Because, again, Exalted is not a transhumanist setting. The notion that improving the lot of mankind can be done by giving everyone special magical powers flies completely against the messages and themes of Exalted - it is, in its transhumanist elements, antihumanist. You don't solve the troubles of Creation by giving everyone special magical power; you solve them by facing them in a human way, by finding the human factors at the core of these troubles - greed, apathy, endemic warfare, prejudice, ignorance - and solving them in a human way - with reason and education and humanity.

        A deathlord is like any other warlord, scaled up. He is a conquering man with human greed and human desires, and you solve the problems he pauses in a human manner. Yes, it's scaled up; yes, diplomacy involves Eclipse Oaths and spirit immunities and necromantic binding, and warfare involves armies of zombies and giant lumbering behemoth-corpses, and personal drama involves great passions and excesses and the Curse of ancient gods. But at its core, the Solar facing a deathlord is a human story, solved in a human manner - as presented through the medium of myth and the epic imagery of the Exalted.

        I might have more to say, but this post is already so long no one is going to read it in full, so let this be my conclusion: the fact that individual Exalts have transhuman power matters not one whit. If you want Solars to have transhuman powers, it's all right: they already do. And if you really need these transhuman powers to feel like permanent changes to your being by having no mote cost, that's all right: Ex3 will have plenty of entry-level, Permanent duration Charms. But it won't matter. Exalted is not a transhumanist story. It has no interest whatsoever in the questions and concerns of a transhumanist story. It is profondly, deeply humanistic, and the greatness and magic of its Chosen heroes serves only as an epic medium, a larger-than-life portrait, of its humanist concerns. It's a game that is about you, today, your flaws and your merits and what you can accomplish with them - not about how you can transcend these flaws into something more.

        And that's why it's so damn good.
        I'm sorry, I just...

            Originally posted by HK-87 View Post
            There's an old quote somewhere on the old forums about a Solar who goes searching for cosmic transcendence and eventually figures out that he already has it by virtue of being a Solar. This has always struck me as a cop-out when compared to "real" transhumanists who have mechanical support for their transformations into "something else" like the Infernals and Alchemicals. Could someone explain to me where this position comes from? I have difficulty seeing how a Solar can be called transhuman any more than Batman or Son Goku can be called such.
            It's mostly a problem of genre.

            When Adam Jessen is almost beaten to death and survives because much of his organic parts are replaced with cybornetics, the story presumes a clear dividing line between what's human and what isn't. Those fleshy, organic legs he was born with represent the abilities of standard humanity; the plastic, prosthetic ones he's given can overcome the limits of those fleshy legs.

            Cybernetic legs transcend fleshy legs. Adam now gets to do things humans can't do... but Adam is still human. In that specific paradox is a transhuman; a human who has somehow surpassed the limits of humanity.

            Solars don't transcend a predefined humanity. Even when they leap over mountains, they remain firmly within the setting's concept of 'totally human.' Nor do they suggest a possible posthuman existence. If you took the conceptual underpinnings of Deus Ex and applied them to Solars then Solars are transhuman. Then again, if you took Deus Ex's conceptual underpinnings and applied them to beastmen, those wouldn't be humans at all.

                Transhumanist fiction is about asking how advances in technology will change what it means to be human. For example, what would you be like if you could live forever, walk on the outside of an asteroid without a space-suit while you feel the solar wind on metallo-organic carapace, see radio waves, terraform worlds with the aid of your multiple idiot-savant clone minds that only exist inside your head and have been created for this specific task after which they will be erased, etc, etc.

                Exalted answers this with "Well, pretty much the same as you are now" so it's not really transhumanist although it certainly could be if one wanted it to. Lunars could be said to have a transhumanist thing going on, but it's really more of a God-Monster thing. I suppose the same could be said of Infernals. The reason I'm saying this is because transhumanist literature generally focuses on the question "What will we become?" and Exalted just states "This is what you will become". Although Infernals doing their own thing are an exception I suppose.

                                    You could tell a transhumanist story using nothing but the golden Ancestor Sashes from An-Teng or the panoply of artifacts a Gunzosha soldier uses, but the default assumption is that you're not doing this because that's not what Exalted is about. If you're running a campaign about what it means to be a Gunzosha soldier then maybe that's different, but most games are going to be about using the Gunzosha armor to accomplish some end. A story about Major Kusanagi wondering if she's human or machine is transhumanist fiction, a story about Major Kusanagi punching people in the face is an action movie. Exalted tends to identify with the action movie path.

                                    So, yes, WST lets you take off to deep space and make your own society in the same way that a collective of people might live in an asteroid and redefine themselves with radical changes accomplished by nanoassemblers and casual psycho-surgery, but generally WST is going to make something that's recognizably Creation with people that consider themselves the same.

                                        I think that transhumanism (in the sense of transforming the human condition) is an entirely valid goal for an Exalted character, since fighting against the limitations of human condition is part of the heroic narrative (you can argue that eg. anyone going on an enlightenment-quest or the like is seeking a transhuman goal, especially if your character is seeking to become a Bodhisattva or the like and spread enlightenment to everyone.) However, achieving it would be the end of your story; the goal they are seeking would be a world with no need of heroes. (In practical terms it would probably be more of a "distant ideal" and not an achievable goal -- "eliminate all warfare forever" or even "eliminate death" is very much a valid goal in a heroic narrative, but it is probably not something you are going to see actually happen in play.)

                                        And it's not like they're going to have transhumanism powers in that line any more than there are specific "live happily ever after" powers.
