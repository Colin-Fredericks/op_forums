    Originally posted by Quimeradc View Post

    I absolutely believe so too. But that doesn't mean "all solars are heroes and heroes are good" (hwhich is a simplified version of the part of your argument most of your opposition argues against).

    And in a world where the only true objective evil (​i can think of) from creation inhabitants point of view is anything that attempts to destroy creation or submit it to its will, what a good guy is is not pretty clear unless the antagonist in question is one those external creation destroying/submiting threats.
    What I like about Exalted (core) is that it positions the player no differently than the DC superhero line. You are a hero and you have awesome powers! Go forth and collapse the evil Realm! Abolish slavery! Save the helpless village from the horrible fae folk horde!

    If you want to, you can embrace that completely and have awesome games where you wrestle a rotting zombies the size of the Empire State building in order to save Creation.

    But what Exalted does that DC superheroes doesn't support, is also give you a setting where you can explore what it means to be a hero. You can tell stories where toppling the evil Realm has serious economic and political consequences. Where saving the day is more than beating the shit out of a giant monster/evil organization. Where you can look across the field and see that the guy you're fighting is also trying to be one of the good guys.

    Not entirely true if solar are being introduced as heroes in the world changing agent sense instead of heroes in the good natured wolrd saver sense.

    Edit: in my opinion, as i can't speak with any one else, the argument is not about the statemente "solars are heroes". Its about the definition of heroes or the types of heroes that will be represented in the books.
    Right, what I think ought to remain subtext - 'What does being good mean?' 'But the god who picked me isn't a god of 'good,' he's a god of virtue.' 'Being a hero in Creation isn't necessarily the same as the popular, modern concept of heroes.' - they want to be the text.

    Alternatively, I am completely for positioning the Dragon-Blooded as "heroes." That they're a nobility that's slid into decadence and corruption is something I think should be front and center.

    If the Lunars manual had introductory fiction where a Lunar sig tore out the heart of her foe and devoured it, I'd love it.

                                        That SLS post doesn't say that Solars are the good guys. It's important that the game support playing Solars as if they are good even as it asks the players to question just what they are doing and what kind of legacy will be left by their actions. The latter part can be left out for groups running "paladins and lawbringers fighting evil and achieving peace" which Exalted supports too, if that's their thing.

                                        Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
                                        Oh dear, my thoughts on Solar exalted fail to meet the criteria for a sound argument. Well, at least my opinions are valid, no need to slit my wrists.. yet.

                                        You and Blaque may feel that this discussion would benefit from me expressing my thoughts as 'valid and sound' arguments, but I do not. My justification for Solars being heroes is that the human soul craves beauty and nobility, and I'm not even prepared to present an argument for the existence of the human soul, that beauty and nobility are quantifiable, or that even if they were that an RPG about mythic superheroes is at all the right vector for them.

                                        But I do think that it should be the case for Exalted.

                                        It's one thing if you disagree, I expect people to disagree, but the idea that me starting this thread was a bad thing boggles my mind.
                                        No offense, but it seems you might be unaware that "valid and sound" are formal terms used for discussion of logic in argumentation. Your argument is valid in that your conclusion follows from your premises. Soundness comes after if all of the premises hold true. If you are ok with your unsound arguments, it means that you are admitting your premises are false; your use of a modern definition of hero and its appeals to goodness is the incorrect premise many posters here have identified. If you feel that your position is correct obviously we cannot convince you otherwise, but the critiques here have shown how this damages the central thesis of the game, its incompatibility with historical and mythological inspirations, and how unsupported it is by evidence within the setting.

                                        Whether individual Exalts are good is a question for us, the readers and players and storytellers, to toy with after the fact based on the intentions and outcomes of their adventures and our own cultural frame. Most of them will be incredibly complicated people who defy such a label, as great historical figures tend to do.
