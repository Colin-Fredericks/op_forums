                                            Dragons of a Different Colour are said to come from mingling ghost-blood and terrestrial-exalt heritages. So of course their death-tainted exaltation post dates the creation of the underworld.

                                            That taint merely causes sickly pale skin, a wan appearing anima, and the potential to initiate into necromancy in place of sorcery. Only if they do so initate do their anima and aspect markings then start to truly show an underworld stigma; and that's still only descriptive. Besides that, they are no different from other dragon-blooded, as they don't have different charm sets, limit breaks, anima powers, nor anything else such as marks abyssal exalts as distinct from solar exalts.

                                            They are dragon-blood goths who might learn creepy magic, not undeath-powered exalts.

                                                        I've got no big problem with Dragons of a Different Colour: people like to play their super-special snowflakes, and its doing it in a way that doesn't cause mechanical issues, so I don't mind. It's just not necessary.​

                                                        I recently used one as an antagonist for one story. The fact she was a Dragonblood (and thus one of the rulers of a country that followed a heretical version of the Immaculate Philosophy) came up of course, as did the fact she was a necromantic crime boss who used ghosts to control the underworld (which rather confused the PCs, as they kept mixing it up with the Underworld).
                                                        The local people believed she'd been Exalted by the sixth Dragon, the Dragon of the Void, aka the Ebon Dragon (again, confusing the PCs a little), but she didn't actually need to have a weird anima banner for that to work (she perpetuated the idea to keep people scared, so could have done that anyway with a regular Wood Aspect anima banner).
