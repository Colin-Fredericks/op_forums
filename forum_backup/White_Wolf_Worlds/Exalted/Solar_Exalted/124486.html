    "Second time's the Charm?" Is this appropriate Solar Charm-space?

    05-03-2014, 10:48 AM
    Traditionally, Solar Charms enable Solar Exalted to succeed at tasks. Overwhelm any obstacle, overcome any difficulty, rise to any challenge, and reject the possibility of failure or defeat. Solars may fail or fall short and then try again until they win, but their Charms have traditionally been focused on shooting for success the first time and every time. As opposed to focusing on making a rebound, being inspired by defeat, learning from mistakes, or improvising a new solution with depleted or ruined resources.

    But then Tiresias posted a novel idea for a Solar Charm which does precisely that:

    Originally posted by Tiresias View Post
    Investigation:

    Lawman's Sudden Realization
    If the Solar fails an Investigation roll to identify the culprit of a crime and the criminal begins to plan a second crime in the Solar's general vicinity, the Solar may reroll the Investigation roll in order to gain sudden insight into the culprit's behaviour and stop her before the second crime is committed. In addition, if the Solar uses the Investigation Excellency to enhance the roll, the excellency's cost is reduce by one mote for each of the culprit's crime scenes that the Solar has investigated, successfully or not.
    I could see an entire new Host of Exalted being built around the theme of "come back stronger / learn from mistakes / recover from false starts", and I wonder if expanding into such a tactical space would dilute Solar themes of excellence and perfection.

    Solar Resistance has traditionally focused much more on preventing the Solar from suffering any harm at all than on recovering from harm already suffered. Oh, they have their Ox-Body Technique and Body-Mending Meditation, but the Lunar Ox-Body Technique has always been superior, as has Lunar health-regeneration. Solars have never been the best at surviving and healing from injuries which could have been avoided, because they didn't need to be; Solars were always the best at avoiding getting injured in the first place.

    Similarly, Solar Resistance's first (if not only) answer to fatigue has been to reduce the fatigue ratings of armor, rather than give an exhausted Solar a second wind. Likewise, has there ever been an official Solar Charm for reducing wound penalties?

    Solar Integrity features Transcendent Hero's Meditation. Unlike most of Solar Integrity, but like most of Solar Resistance, this Charm is an "in case of failure" second line of defense. If your DV fails, use a soak Charm. If your MDV fails, use Transcendent Hero's Meditation. It is perhaps the ultimate expression of refusing to acknowledge that one has been "defeated"; of rejecting failure or compromise and grabbing for total victory and freedom.

    But compared to that, a Charm like Commanded To Fly has much more in common with Lawman's Sudden Realization. Both of these Charms accept that the Exalt has fallen short of some challenge, and try to work with that. Commanded To Fly appends a change to the compulsion imposed on the Lunar, possibly even turning it into a desirable goal for the Exalt. Lawman's Sudden Realization doesn't just need a criminal to plot a second crime, it needs the Exalt to have failed a previous Investigation roll in order to better succeed at this one. It is a Charm that literally cannot work unless the Exalt fell short and lost to a challenge.


    Should there ever be a Solar Charm that requires an Exalt to fail a crafting project and produce something unserviceable or undesirable before trying again to make something different but useful? (This sword came out all wrong, but I think I can make a wicked dagger or spear from it!)

    How about a social influence Charm that capitalizes on a previous social faux pas you committed, so that your target underestimates you and ends up agreeing to your proposal because they can't believe you might get one up on them?

    A dodge Charm that triggers when you're knocked off-balance or to the ground by an attack, allowing you to flail around on one foot or roll around on the ground in such a confusing manner that your opponent cannot manage to hit you the second time?


    If one did design a new Exalted Host whose best powers require them to fail at least once, it would make sense to deny them Charms that straight-forwardly enable success on any given try, especially the first.

    But to alleviate that weakness somewhat, as well as to provide for some truly spectacular failures to recover from, they might be given "First Gambit" Charms. Something which actually makes the action more likely to fail... but at the same time, prevents the Exalt from suffering a botch or other sort of catastrophic, irrecoverable failure, and also offers great rewards should the Exalt succeed.

    You thus get a reckless breed of hero who is encouraged to charge into situations they're bound to screw up, because it's only in such dire straights that the rest of their Charmset shines.

      It's really easy to do with Investigation because it's basically how cop shows work. Criminal enters the cops' orbit, commits a crime, cops can't find any conclusive evidence, criminal commits a second crime, cops find more evidence, cops have a sudden epiphany and stop the criminal before a third crime is committed.       House applies the same concept to Medicine.

      Burn Notice does the same thing with Larceny. When someone's cover is blown, they just lie more to sell it harder.

      You could do it with Craft. The prototype that fails for the first 55 minutes of the episode and miraculously works as intended in the last five minutes is a fairly common thing.

                      If not the Solar Exalted, does any other known splat seem to fit the theme of "come from behind victory"?

                      The power level would seem to fit the Dragon-Blooded, but those guys have a strong elementalism theme which has so many possible mechanical expressions that this particular theme doesn't seem necessary. Especially when Terrestrials also have the mechanical theme of "stronger in numbers".

                      Rerolling makes sense for Sidereal Exalted because it seems like telling reality, "lolNope, try that again". But beyond rerolling-type Excellencies, I don't see their Charms doing much in the way of "try harder next time".

                      All of my examples of "Charms that work with failure" were Lunar Charms. Further examples include the Toad-and-Scorpion branch of Charms whereby a Lunar is encouraged to seek out and experience suffering in order to learn how to inflict it. "Fail first before you can be awesome" sorrowfully fits a lot of Lunar setting material in previous editions. Since Lunars don't have any strong aesthetic like "Elements" or "Fate", having this methodological theme looks like it might give a real defined shape to their Attribute Charms. But I couldn't swear to this theme being appropriate for the 3rd Edition Lunars that the developers have in mind.

                          Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                          If not the Solar Exalted, does any other known splat seem to fit the theme of "come from behind victory"?
                          There is both a certain Lunar-ness and Sidereal-ness in a second chance. Sidereals because, as you say, stepping outside of normal causality into their own private context seems appropriate, and Lunars because they are supposed to be the great 'overcomers'. The one's chosen for striving against (but not necessarily overcoming) adversity. But personally I think second chance fit the Terrestrials best because of the way the probability falls out.

                          At low chances of failure the re-roller has the advantage. Sure neither of them is likely to fail, but if it does happen then Wisdom goes down and Heart gets another roll. Because of the maths Wisdom actually needs to square the size of Heart's dice pool to have the same chance not to fail. If the target is relatively modest and you just need not to fail (as opposed to succeed by a large threshold), a second chance is great. If you have enough Exalted you could build a relatively stable empire on that.

                          (Gee, who might I be thinking of ...)

                          When your chances draw more even then Wisdom begins to draw ahead. Heart's re-roll can only give him the very odds as he had the first time, so it continues to protect him against performing poorly but if he rolled broadly in the expected range and he looks at his dice thinking 'will a charm make this better?' the 50% or the time the answer is going to be no. Wisdom, however is always getting an advantage from his Charm, even if that advantage is moderate.

                          When your chance for success is low, Heart is boned. Each reroll has the same low chance of success and it never improves There's a hardMeanwhile limit in dice on the amount of effort he can put forward and no matter how many rerolls you're allowed, you're going to run out of essence before they all come up 10.Meanwhile Wisdom has an improved probability profile. He may only have one roll and he may only have covered half the distance, but he still has a chance. And when you have a chance is when things get interesting.

                          Now, yes, sure you can come up with a lot of different ways to handle this. Maybe if Lunars are the second-chancer's then they automatically apply half their dice pool is successes or some other wizardry, but there's one thing about the sheer maths of this that I really like:

                          If you make Terrestrials the second-chancers then they're better at not-failing than Solars are. Not better at succeeding, not able to achieve the same heights of majesty, but better at not completely dropping the ball. And if you have decided to do something terrible in order to save the world, if you'd come out of an age of magnificent wonder that had turned horribly wrong, and you had to make sure that the world was going to have to go forward in some way, even a diminished way, just to be able to tell yourself that maybe you were justified ...

                          Maybe you turn the world over to the people who are best at not-failing.

                                              What if instead of framing it as a second chance after failure, you frame it as piling up attempts at success?

                                              For example, a successful Investigation roll automatically tells you if two crimes were committed by the same person, you get a 1-mote discount on the Investigation excellency per investigated crime scene when investigating or tracking down a culprit and after analyzing (6 - Investigation) crime scenes, you get a roll to find out the culprit's next crime if they're planning one.

It's better to design them around the idea that harder problems will take time and involve some give-and-take, and to giving them charms that will look / feel awesome even when you're suffering a temporary setback. Obviously in an interesting fight you are sometimes going to miss or get hit, and obviously in a complicated investigation you are sometimes going to follow a false lead; if you don't have Solar charms to cover those situations, you'll basically cease to be a Solar during those moments, because the books won't have given you clear Solar thematics in terms of "this is how a Solar responds to failure." It's better to think about how Solars respond to failure, and to give them some charms in that model, so players can still feel like they have a chance to be awesome even when things go wrong.)

                                                          This discussion is a bit different from the way we usually approach the topic, in that it's implicitly edition-agnostic because we're speculating about 3e stuff. In first edition and to a lesser extent second, retries were outside the Solar design space, and this was (in part) a reflection of "the ignominy of failure" being unsuited to the greatest of the Exalted. But it doesn't follow, I don't think, that rerolls should or must have that connotation or that it a charm that only operates on failure is inappropriate for Solars. Earlier editions selected these mechanical linkages to fluff somewhat arbitrarily on the basis of the game as it stood then, and because they're somewhat arbitrary, they should always be re-evaluated when the game is retooled. 3e will undoubtedly have strong, gimmicky mechanical differentiation between the splats in a way that ties into their themes, because the Ink Monkeys have always been good about that, but it may be very different from how it's been done in the past.

                                                          Here's an example of what I mean. Let's say that for whatever reason we've decided to go back to the paradigm implied by the 1e core, where each splat has one unique way of boosting rolls, and we want to decide if Solars should get the First or Second Excellency effect. Well, you can frame this two ways:
                                                          • Success-adders are "more Solar", because they represent effortless mastery and perfection without possibility of failure. Dice-adders are less appropriate because they're blunt and unreliable.
                                                          • Dice-adders are "more Solar", because they represent going "all in" on bold risks and smashing through obstacles with impossible and overwhelming displays of ability. Success-adders are less appropriate because they encourage you to do as little as possible and succeed in a prosaic and minimal way.

                                                          Both of these are perfectly legitimate cases for a particular mechanical distinction, but they say completely opposite things. If the game gets published and chooses one of these two paths, that becomes the "right" one within the context of that edition, and we can talk about what's "Solar-appropriate" in that context. But staring down the barrel of a largely unknown third edition, which seems (based on the handful of previews) to be broadening every splat's mechanical palette, neither argument is really correct -- it's a matter of choosing the mechanical distinctions that have the best knock-on effect for the rest of the game out of the ones that are somewhat plausible, then reverse-engineering the justification.

                                                          The Magic: the Gathering design blog recounts an injoke among the Magic dev team. The joke posits two global enchantments: the first is a blue enchantment called Guys Are Sneaky which has the effect "Creatures can't be blocked", while the other is a red enchantment called Guys Are Panicked which has the effect "Creatures can't block". The joke, of course, is that these two effects are mechanically identical and assigned to "opposite" colours, but each version makes perfect sense for that colour. Now, if they actually printed both of those cards, it would be obvious to most people what they'd done, but if they just printed one of them, people would mostly look it over and say "yeah, that seems about right."
