          Yes. I'm a rather vocal proponent of the general idea and all.

          So, the "ability min = Essence min" isn't a horrible starting place, but it goes too far in the opposite direction. The conflict between "Charms are an extension of the character's aptitudes," and, "you can block a mountain thrown at you with Melee 2," doesn't really mesh. If every Charm scaled in some fashion to your Ability rating, it would be easily to drop Ability minimums in the first place, because their wouldn't be that disconnect in the first place. If you look at, say, Dragonblooded Excellencies, the difference between Ability 1, and Ability 5 + Specialty is readily apparent in how much you get out of being good at mundane skills reflecting through your Charms (Solars complicate things by their dice-caps being Att+Abl and all). Essence minimums just play into the potency of the Charm (in this example, it's just extra dice, which is good to have but far from world shaking power).

          Without that.. it really becomes an art as you have to weigh all the factors that go into a Charm: does it scale in a fashion, is the effect on the potent side for its Essence rating but justifiable to be available earlier, how many pre-reqs does it have, is the Ability minimum keeping you away from a potential trap because a Charm isn't actually mechanically useful at lower levels, etc.

          Whether you want to start with a really simple thing like abl min = essence min, or some more complicated formula, in the end you're going to be making a lot of individual rules calls (there's a lot of Charms), to the point where you might want to just skip some formula-based quick-fix for not really being as quick as it sounds.

                  I think you're overstating Supernals... though it depends a bit on the Ability. While Supernal is a nice bonus, I rarely see players start with more than one or two Essence 3 Charms at max. While you can, in theory, jump up to Essence 5 stuff, you're such a one-trick pony in the process it's the only thing you'll be Exalted-level good at to start. You don't get that many starting Charms after all. After playing some of the other Exalted in 3e, it doesn't really change much, and the longer games go, the less Supernal actually impacts things. While probably a topic for it's own thread, instead of dumping Supernals, consider some other boost that will help set your Solars up to really have an edge in the Ability that's supposed to help define their legend.

                  An example of the problem I pointed out in another thread: the Dodge Charms. If you want hand Essence 2 Dodge Charms, you have to have at least Dodge 4, and if you want Essence 3 Charms, you need Dodge 5.

                  Lets say you're an Archer and you want Dodge because it gives you some decent Defense and you want to be able to boost your disengage rolls so that you can stay away from opponents. Going beyond the Excellency and getting one Charm to boost your Evasion, and one Charm to boost your disengage, means starting with at least Dodge 4. Dodge 4 is the minimum investing in Dodge to boost your Disengage beyond the Excellency; and not even some of the more potent high level stuff; that's just to get double 9s on one attempt for 2m.

                          I thought that rationale was fairly evident from the implementation? I know they're dealing with making this work with how the first 3e developers set things up, but it's clear 3e started with the idea of taking a few 5s at start was the default way of things, and there were plenty of public statements about how if you wanted to go from zero to hero than you should clearly appreciate the game making it an actual effort to do so by a specific person.

                          I don't think it really simplifies down to "just handwave," though. Having Ability mins does let you have some fancier powers at lower Essence that do feel like they should be for high Ability characters. I don't think anyone is annoyed by the idea of Heavenly Guardian Defense being a Melee 5 Charm, or that putting it at Essence 2 is good for Melee focused characters so they can get some serious defenses faster. It's things like War Lion Stance jump up to Melee 4 where, yes, it's a solid Charm, but why does "being able to apply Defend Other more easily," have such a high bar for something far less character defining in terms of the kind of legendary things a Melee focused Solar is going to get up to?

                          It also really doesn't address how the rapid jump up in Ability minimums doesn't really leave Charms further down the tree anywhere to go. Anything building off of War Lion Stance has to be 4+, and we're still in the Essence 1 Melee section.

                          As I said elsewhere, part of the problem is how 3e defines what Abilities represent. A 3 in Melee is already a battle-hardened veteran. That's already someone that most people think of as a warrior/fighter/gladiator/etc. when thinking about that person's life. It might not be the one defining thing (but then again, that's supposed to be Supernal, not Ability picks, for Solars), but it's certainly an important part of that character as it is. Melee 3 with a specialty in Swords is already someone with renowned swordsmanship. The rather nebulous status of what 4 and 5 mean above that makes how quickly the Charms ramp up to 4 and 5 harder to really make sense of, and harder to implement any sort of hand-wave approach to the matter.

                                  I'm going to assume that this character, as an Archery specialist who is dipping into Dodge, has Dex 4 and Dodge 2 (as Favored/Caste). For fun, we'll also assume a 1-die stunt on the disengage. This character can expect an average of 3.5 successes on Dex+Dodge+[1d stunt], and can pay 6 motes (max excellency) to average 6.5 instead.

                                  Dust Motes Whirling is a Dodge 4/E1 Charm that costs 2m to give you double 9s on a disengage roll. It will cost this character 8 vanilla XP. The character can still expect 3.5 successes on most Dex+Dodge+[1d stunt] rolls, or 6.5 for 6m with max excellency. Without excellency, that's 2m to go from 3.5 to 4.2. Combining both, they can spend up to 8m to average 7.8.
                                  ​
                                  Going from Dodge 2 to Dodge 4 will cost this character 8 XP of either type. As a result, their average successes on ANY Dex+Dodge+[1d stunt] roll will be 4.5, and they can pay the same 8m to raise it to 8.5.

                                  In short, DMW is an icing-on-the-cake (or maybe, "win harder") Charm. Ability dots and excellency motes are usually (always?) going to be a better deal than a double-whatever or reroll-whatever dice trick, because those Charms make you better in proportion to how good you already are.

                                  You would only pick up DMW after you have bought all of the other basic bonuses, *if* you want to be the best at Disengage.
