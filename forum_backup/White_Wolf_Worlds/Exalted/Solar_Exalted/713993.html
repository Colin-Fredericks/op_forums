    Can a Solar Fail to Master a Human Ability?

    10-22-2015, 04:42 AM
    I realize that OOC, the player can distribute attributes, abilities, and charms as they desire. This is a question about how Solars work within the setting.

    Let's imagine a Solar Dawn named Unfurling Steel Blossom. She's a graceful dancer and fighter able to carve a deadly path through her enemies. She has a Zenith circle-mate named Harmonious Mountain can dive into lava, trek tirelessly for weeks through frozen lands, and shrugs off the blows of a dozen men like they're bug bites.

    One day, Unfurling Steel decides that whole 'shrug off mighty blows' thing sounds interesting. Harmonious Mountain agrees to teach her the exercises and techniques he learned as a young monk/goat herder on the misty slopes of Mount Terrimungu.

    Is it possible that after weeks/months of diligent effort, Unfurling Sterl Blossom is unable to internalize these techniques? She becomes a bit more hardy, but no more so than a hale and weathered mortal?

    Again, I'm not asking if it's 'okay' for this to happen at a table. I know that this is completely up to players.

    Given what we're told of Solars, however, is it possible that Unfurling Steel Blossom simply can't become that hearty and resistant? It's just a limit she'll have to live with outside of external magical forces or the like.

    Come and rock me Amadeus.

      I'd say it's possible. Maybe not because the Solar can fail to understand the technique, but because they can fail to invest enough work and attention into it. Learning patience, for example, is something that takes effort and a willingness to change as a person, which isn't necessarily something Solars are great at (Solars sometimes being impatient, cruel, inattentive or having other character flaws are after all one of the staples of what makes them interesting). You could justify it at the table with how the particular technique requires some certain mindset or personality.

      That's not to say Solars can't overcome their personal flaws, of course - they obviously can - but this also seems to be the one area where they can fail, and fail spectacularly.

          This... is an arguable one.

          Personally, I fairly firmly say that yes, a Solar can fail to get better. But that's based on how I view Exaltation, and I'd require a good reason for it. Exaltation reflects qualities already within the person. A great musician becomes sublime, the fast duellist becomes even faster. If you lack that quality to start with, you can build on it... but no more than a mortal can. If Unfurling Steel Blossom grew up on the streets, and already was a bit tough, I'd say she can improve and learn her friend's techniques, at least to a degree. If she grew up in a life of privilage and simply isn't that tough... training does no more for her than it does a mortal.

                      I've talked in favour of this before in response to questions of immediate adaptations, but considering in the long term...

                      A few days ago, while browsing through Youtube, I saw a thumbnail to a video that seemed to be depicting some kind of anime scene in which a character was dancing. It set me off on a train of thought about how much anime, and media in general, might contain imagery or characterisations that I would find striking or compelling, as well as the possibilities of actually dancing, that I will never intersect with because there's just too much to be encompassed in a single human life. That also got me to thinking of depictions of enormous libraries (such as the Duke's Archives in Dark Souls), and how no one person could read everything in them.

                      It made me think about what it would be like to be able to live a lot longer; to have the time for so much more.

                      It's come to be part of my image for what the possibilities of a Solar's lifespan are; not necessarily about learning everything (certainly unlikely to get 5 in all Abilities), but the idea of breadth and permutations, of expanding yourself laterally. A person who spent several centuries reading an entire library. I find it more engaging and comprehensible than the idea of a Solar who somehow expands the one ability beyond the usual boundaries, this idea of a person who cannot relate to anybody.

                      Personally, I would actually think that such breadth means you can relate to even more people; your interests and experiences can expand to include things that even more people would share with you, and your life is long enough to actually do that sharing. Sure, the short-lived people in question are only ever experiencing a tiny fraction of your life, but really, that's the case for their interactions with one another as well.

                      And even with all of that, the Solar is still so small...

                      As an answer to the original question, this is all something that leads me to think: "eventually". If she needed to relocate herself to his homeland and try to absorb an approximation of his formative experiences to really get it, it's something she would be far better equipped for than any mortal.

                      (This is in no way me saying that any player who wants their character to build up Resistance 5 and associated Charms needs to have a lifetime of conditioning for it, it's more of an expansive setting thing)

                                  I'd say yes they can fail, but not for long if they push it. Just bringing it back to the game mechanics.
                                  Any mortal, if unskilled will absolutely fail a task.
                                  A Solar, on the other hand, can at least try. I mean, yes, the chances you'll succeed the 2 difficulty roll on 2 dots is fairly low but it can happen. That you can channel your virtues and motes, increases the possibility.

                                  But that's for one task. Mastery implies the capability to generate a result reliably over and over.

                                  So, same mortal wants to master an ability. How does he do it? In game terms, expend XP. Or storywise, he devotes his time and effort on training that ability. But then again, mortals don't really get that much XP do they? Since their daily lives revolve on the mundane and not dying, it may take their whole lives to get enough XP. Also there's that dying thing, a mortal's gotta survive first and that's not easy.

                                  Now, Solar dude over there wants to train the same thing. But this time, his live revolves around the epic and legendary. Even if he doesn't do anything AMAZING for decades, he gets more XP out of just existing and going about his life. Then the fact that he can expect to live longer, devote more time to personal pleasures, and just all around have more free time than any mortal.
                                  Then it just boils down to economics. How much XP does he bank, and how does he spend it? If he wants to be MASTER CHEF, Lord of Falafels, but consistently spends all his points into The Art of Making Flatulence an Acceptable Musical Instrument, then that's failure right there.
                                  And again, he doesn't even have to spend the same XP as the mortal. He can spend motes just to make himself better, and no one's gonna fault him for that.

                                  tl;dr: Yes but no with minimal effort and guts

                                                  Part of me agrees with Isator that Exalts have a relatively-unique opportunity to devote decades or even lifetimes to develop their skills, but I also feel inclined to point out that for the most part, the distinction is academic; most Exalts won't experience those tens of decades, and even the ones that do are just as likely as the rest of us to have crises of faith in which they wonder if all the effort they're sinking into it is "worth it." It's very possible for them to never master a skill, even when they really want to.

                                                  My own example:
                                                  Shichirou was my first Exalted character, and before the game imploded he'd spent about forty in-game years as an Exalt. He always saw himself as a brawler, but wanted to be a martial artist (ignoring 2e rolling the two into one Ability; even in my early days I thought the idea of a martial art built around "fighting like a Solar" was dumb). His father was a martial artist, his rivals and enemies were martial artists, and he was fascinated with the idea of the deeper truths hidden deep in the styles.

                                                  He had plenty of opportunities; one of the first places his circle visited was a Gold Faction manse with a magical AI and a dojo; he wrestled with Jade Mountain Stylists, Orgiastic Fugitives, and Fire Dragons alike; later he even had the opportunity to learn Sidereal Martial Arts!

                                                  Still, every time, he looked at the opportunity offered, and said, "Sorry, I got shit to do." (Even taking into account that a huge chunk of that forty years was spent either in training or training others!)

                                                  In my headcanon, wherever he's gone, he's still a brawler.

                                                          I'm personally of the belief that anyone could potentially learn anything, given previous knowledge and skills to build it on. I therefore do believe that as one develops, options are in fact cut off, if only because your body changes and your mind has chosen paths that, while leading towards many worthwhile perspectives, lead away from others. Given infinite time, one might be able to learn everything, but I'd think one would start forget previous knowledge way before that.

                                                          Mechanically, a Solar can buy dots in every Ability she wishes to without restriction or condition. Narratively, there are reasons for every Ability she has at 0. Mostly, it's probably that she hasn't need of it, but it could also be that she's uninterested, actively opposed to it, or maybe just can't seem to get it. These descriptions don't prevent you from putting some dots in those Abilities later, which would be a great break-through for the character.

                                                          In short, I think it's unhelpful to think of what a Solar can do, because a Solar is such a classifying and mechanical term. Instead, what your character can and can't do is up to how you imagine her, which influences her stats, which in turn influences how you imagine her. Some Solars are probably able to learn everything. Some, for some reason, probably are not. It's not really something that benefits from being decided on a category level.
