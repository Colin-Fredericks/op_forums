              Some Solars are good. Some Solars are bad.
              There are 150 of them, after all.

              I think the books are written with the expectation that most people are going to make relatively good PCs (if more heroic warriors than Jesus), because that's what players tend to do (or at least, think they're doing).
              But there's no particular reason people have to make good characters.
              Same with DnD I guess, though since there's no objective measure of PC morality in Exalted, the ST can't really say "no Evil characters please".

              Maybe talk to the ST and other players about what kind of game people want, good or bad (the ST may be as surprised as you). It's also possible of course, that the other players don't realise they're playing bad guys (I see this a lot). Or maybe they do, and love it, you know them better than I.

              (One note: Solars are written with the imagery of good guys. Shining gold paladins, rightful kings, chosen by God to go forth and smite evil... but the original writers deliberately didn't actually make any rules that said you had to be good, or got some benefit for it: it was a bit of a deconstruction of the whole paladin/returning king thing, though I don't think that's something the current writers are so interested in)


              A good few years ago, having played a lot of Dragonblood and one Celestial game, I decided to run my own Solar game. I wanted to do something different from jaded aristocrats oppress the Threshold once again. And my friends played, well, not exactly moral paragons (they were PCs after all), but certainly characters who considered themselves heroes. Sure, the Lunar ex-monk slept with prostitutes, and the Dawn caste wanted to conquer the area, but when villagers were being attacked by zombies or oppressed by evil Kings you could rely on them to try to save the innocents and be big damn heroes.

              Then, I decided to try running a Solar game for some other friends, so they could play too. I was utterly shocked when local villagers begged them for help from a beastman horde, and the Pirate and Mercenary said "how much can you pay us? Nothing? Nope, we'll leave you to die."
              Because Exalted has no moral system, you can play an evil Solar if you want. It was just jarring. But again, I guess that's the same as with most role-playing games that let you make a wide variety of PCs.

                                          ... Wow. I'm kinda surprised at how many people came out in favour of 'Yeah, Solars can be evil'.

                                          Roanas, while I agree that Evil Solars are a thing, the particular evil Solars you describe feel.... weird to me. Not just a swordsman, but one of the greatest in the land. Most of the canon Solar villains were similarly epic in scope, or working on it. If your group is running with 3E fluff on this, I'm struggling to see why 'rape and murder pirate' was Exalted.

                                                      I have to agree with Croakamancer for the most part, while the player is free play what they wish it is difficult to see how some of those guys Exalted...
                                                      That being said I never actually saw the Usurpation being at all about the Solars being 'evil' a word which comes across as really silly for this setting where you have a hilarious number of factions that flat out want kill/destroy literally everything. The Usurpation's justification came from a large number of Solars being insane which by extension led to gaps of incompetence and Creation itself being at risk.

                                                      Sidereals are more than content to support a competent tyrant (like the empress) whose entire ruling style is basically domestic peace at the price of untold millions suffering from their activities abroad because they dont have to worry about her destroying Creation out of insane boredom (even 'sane' Solars have little to no self-preservation instincts when in the face of their own curiousity).

                                                      The more important question you should be asking yourself is how your character was special and exceptional enough that the literal god of awesome still made them Exalt, morality should be a secondary priority. But boring people that dont seem like they would do something awesome woth their powers are highly unlikely to exalt.
