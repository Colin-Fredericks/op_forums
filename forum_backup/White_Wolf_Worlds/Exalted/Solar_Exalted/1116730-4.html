            While I like a basic background meshing with the things that players put on their character sheets I try not to be a hardass about it. Months or a year as a beginning Solar allows lots of opportunities to upgrade their Merits in impressive ways and the system is designed to allow it. Obviously my tolerance for whats permitted can change with the kind of campaign I'm running. The mortal game I'm running now has completely different degrees of expectations than it would for Exalted Demigods - it made sense that no one had artifacts or manses!.

            As far as justifications I've had everything from 'found in my Solar's tomb' to 'bought in a flea market at a stall of antiquities, unrecognized and waiting' to 'dusty and unused in his grandparent's attic' to 'inherited it from his Lunar mentor' to 'I collected my artifacts off of a Dragon-blooded/God/Sidereal/demon's body'. All these justifications are a bit cliche but they work. I don't need a player to be as creative and witty as Shakespeare for what is essentially background material to fill a paragraph or a few sentences.

            PS: I like encouraging players to pick something other than 'my Solar tomb' because i like those as easy adventures to run for later with suitable personalized rewards.

                        Originally posted by Octopoid View Post
                        Don't misunderstand - I'm not saying everything should be allowed at every table. Some things ruin games. Some things are overpowered. Some things make the game not fun for everyone else at the table. But how I got my daiklave doesn't affect anyone else. You might think it's boring. That's fine, think that. But don't tell me I can't do it because you think it's boring. It's bad form, it's stepping on my fun, and it guarantees I won't be coming back to that table.

                        My fun is just as important as anyone else's fun at the table (GM's included), and when someone else at the table tells me I can't do what I think is fun just because they think it's too cliche, I walk. I'm sure I'm not the only one.
                        What ruins a game for someone might be the very thing that makes it worth playing for someone else. Yes, I agree, everyone's fun is equally important. So someones fun on the expense on another player should never be accepted (shared fun on the expense of a character is another thing). Personally I put in quite a bit of effort communicating my expectations, in an attempt to minimize the risk of finding out incompatible wants before anyone gets invested in the game (unfortunately, not always successful).

                        I think you should walk away from a game that isn't fitting what you think is fun. If it seems to be worth it, I think it is a good idea to talk it over and see if there is a common ground; but I also see that as something optional. Only you should decide when you might make compromises on your preferences.

                        I don't mind cliches myself, but I do mind if it isn't fitting the setting (from that point of view, a cliche is rarely a problem for me). If the reason for an artifact is "I raided my previous life's tomb", I would want an explanation on why the character didn't get more stuff. Maybe it was already looted and this was the only thing left, maybe was chased off before managing to get anything else, maybe was robbed on the way from there, maybe it was an ad-hoc funeral and this was the only thing that previous life was buried with, etc. When something clashes, I want to figure out what part of it is important to the player (or the ST in the rare occasions I'm the player) and see if there is an alternative that fit both. Still, it matters how a character got its daiklave at my table. It might make us incompatible, or it might not. If we ever are to game together, it would be something we would have to figure out. If we would be incompatible, it wouldn't make anyone right or wrong; just different.

                        However, the thing rubbing me the wrong way is all the posts about other people "doing it wrong". I mean, I wouldn't play at a table where you actually bribe the ST for in-game boons, so if that is what Coik's group is doing, it wouldn't fit me. But if it fit everyone at that table, great for them.

                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post

But then other players will play a local ruler or influential merchant and spend 2-3 dots on followers, resources, influence, backing, and contacts, and find 10 points still isn't enough.)
                                        Yep.

                                        If you want Resources, you have to describe it. Describe your business, you land-holdings, your so-on-and-so-forth. That rule comes straight from the book.

                                        If you want Influence, you have to describe how it works, or how it came about. Why are you so influential in this region?

                                        If you want Retainers (note that I modified the Retainers merit, so instead of one uberpowerful retainer, you get a larger 'lesser' retinue), you gotta describe where they came from, where you picked them up, how/why you recruited them, why they are loyal to you, so on and so forth.

                                        If you want Command, you have to describe why the soldiers follow you, and how you keep them supplied.

                                        If you want Backing..... who backs you, and why? Why are you a member of this organization, who is your superior, how do you feel about each other?

                                        So on and so forth, for the rest of the Story merits.

                                        .....perhaps I misspoke. It isn't that I actively dissallow my players from taking Story merits at character creation, it is more that I, well, actually use them in the story. And I prefer to have them gain the Story merits in-play, as a result of their actions, as opposed to just marking things off during character creation.

                                        For example, let us say Awesome McBadass starts with a Daiklave, looted from his previous incarnation's tomb. So, per the merit, you get your Daiklave...... but, people are going to talk about how someone looted the Anathema MacEvil BigBadEvilGuy's forbidden tomb, braving the dangers within, and escaping with the treasure..... and people are going to ask questions. About how you can carry a blade only the Princes of the Earth can wield, how the hell you survived the place in the first place. Local authority figures are going to be interested, at the very least ...... and depending on how much 'noise" you make looting the joint and surviving the perils, the Wyld Hunt might be called.

                                        Or..... a Thrifty McMerchant runs a Solar-powered business empire, making beaucoup bucks. Just the Resources merit, right..... but other companies are going to try to make a move on your market, pirates/bandits are going to raid your stuff unless you do something about it, local, or even regional, authority figures are going to try to muscle in on your cash flow, and all the while, resources need to be moved, products need to be produced, and expansion needs to be undertaken.

                                        All things that, in and of themselves, can, and in my mortal campaigns, often are, stories in and of themselves. In my games, you don't just "get" a merit and mark it down on your character sheet. There are consequences for everything..... not necessarily bad consequences, but things will change, fates will shift, and stuff will happen.

                                        That sort of thing.
