Once we get this done, we will play conquering this land. We will switch between worldbuilding with our Solars, with our regular ST, and a more low level varied "mission" stories, with new DB characters who are part of our Solars' retinue, assigned to them by the Expedition Force, STed by me.

    These missions will be varied, such as diplomatic overture to natives, war with natives, monster hunting, mapping of places of power, collection of precious resources, establishment of outposts Wyld Border inspection, etc...

    So yeah... no real "end" to the endgame in sight.

                            To go back to the social roles theory that I've put forth before:

                            The "default" Dragon-Blooded endgame is to become the ruler of the Realm because the Dragon-Blooded are the social Establishment, and being the top member of the power structure is the ultimate expression of achievement/progress as a member of the establishment. There are other possible Dragon-Blooded stories, but this is the fairly obvious greatest ambition.

                            The "default" Sidereal endgame is to become the leader of the foremost Sidereal faction and puppeteer of Heaven, because the Sidereals are the Secret Masters, and being the master conspirator is the ultimate expression of achievement/progress as a conspirator. There are other possible Sidereal stories, but this is the fairly obvious greatest ambition.

                            The "default" Solar endgame is to transform Creation, because Solars are the Great Men* who impose a vision on the world, bending the course of nations. The specific transformation is necessarily unique to each Solar, because the whole Great Man* concept is that they break the world's old paradigms and do something new. If the Solar is an Exalted Napoleon, it'll look something like the Dragon-Blooded endgame, except that she comes from outside of the establishment instead of from inside it. But other kinds of Solars will do other things; you could have a religious messiah, an abolitionist whose social movement turns all Creation against slavery, the sorcerer who brings Creation into contact with Autochthonia, the explorer who sailed west into the Wyld until she opened the way to continent thought lost because of the Baloran Crusade, etc.

                            *The gender-loading here is simply because the historiographical theory/model being adapted for fiction/gaming here is known as the "Great Man" approach.

                                    Mind, between my personal world view and my perspective on the Great Curse, I envision the ideal end game for any given Solar to consist of finding a way to reconcile their own personality flaws with the complexity of the world in a manner other than trying to have absolute mastery over everything, accepting the limitations of power and control and engaging with people and the world more on their own terms, and dedicating power more to purposes of understanding, personal and societal benefits and improvement, and creating the stage in which people can find self-actualisation and enlightenment, while continuously navigating the difficulties that emerge from many complex parts grating against one another, and protecting against those threats that come from the Outside.

                                    ​Because they tried the whole "take over the world" thing before, and it didn't really work out for them. For all that one might refer to Great Man Theory, it's interesting that the Solars fell beneath the weight of a dissatisfied majority. Not to mention any portrayal of the First Age (which is kind of most of them) that presents it as having continuously generated challenges and rebellions for them.

                                    ​I don't really see the solution to that being "do it all over again, but Even Harder". Oh sure, it makes for a compelling and resonant narrative when people just repeat the mistakes of the past and set themselves back on a trajectory of disaster in the next few millennia, but I'm willing to indulge myself with a bit of my own idealised setting.

                                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

                                                            End game is for your characters to complete their Motivations and do epic things along the way.
                                                            This is the closest thing to my perspective I've seen so far. But I would go further (maybe too far?) and say that:

                                                            Exalted isn't designed to have an "endgame" in the first place, because it's not a game with an end state. Being an RPG means it "ends" when folks decide they want to start again (even then new characters might start in the world the previous ones created) or play something else, or just stop showing up. Even a big goal like "take the throne" isn't naturally an end state, because now you have a throne. What are you going to DO with it? Who is going to try to take it from you next? Does anyone really get the impression that ruling in Creation is less fraught then conquering?

                                                            Instead, I would think of stories having "closure" instead of a game having an "end". And closure is going to be based, as AnubisXy indicates, on the specific motivations of the characters, but also the players. If taking the throne happens to wrap things up neatly for everyone and creates a natural stopping point, then great. But I don't think there's anything particularly "default" about that.
