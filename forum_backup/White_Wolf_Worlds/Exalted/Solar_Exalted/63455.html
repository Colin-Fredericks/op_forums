                              In 2e, Solar Stealth covers a very broad range of effects; compelling people not to notice them is one approach, but not their only approach.

                              Easily-Overlooked Presence simply makes you hard to spot; nothing in it suggests it works via mental influence, and mental defenses don't help you against it at all. And Invisible Statue Spirit simply makes you invisible. And their most powerful stealth charm is of course probably IAM, which when used by a dedicated stealth Night caste realistically makes you impossible to detect unless you're going up against someone who expects you.

                              As for EotUCS beating Invisible Statue Spirit... I mean, we've been told that 3e will have fewer hard scenelong counter kinds of charms (because they're a bit boring), but the basic idea that active counters to stealth need to be able to beat stealth for them to be worthwhile is sound. Stealth is about your target not knowing you're there. EotUCS can't be kept up permanently, so if they know to activate one of the most expensive charms in the game, you're probably screwed, yes, because the fact that they know to activate EotUCS means you've already failed at stealth.

                              Or another way of looking at it: Anti-stealth charms are only useful in one specific situation (when you're going up against stealth.) Stealthiness, though, is useful almost all the time, even against people who have perfect anti-stealth charms, as long as your target isn't actively using the very best and most expensive detective charms right then and there.

                              (In fact, as I pointed out above, 2e actually gave far too big of an advantage to stealth even in a Night Stealth Ninja vs. Night Awareness Watchman matchup, because the Ultimate Awareness Guy can't keep IAM up for Awareness all the time -- and the Stealth guy gets to choose when he's going to sneak past, so he will always have IAM for Stealth up when it counts. This means that realistically, the Stealth guy will have a massively larger dice pool and will trivially sneak past an Ultimate Watchman who has lots of excellent charms that he won't get a chance to use.)

                              Obviously things are a bit different if you want to use stealth in combat, but at that point you're slipping into a bottomless abyss of 2e problems (starting with the fact that unexpected attacks are instadeath if you don't have a way to totally negate them, which makes everything related to combat stealth completely fall apart).

                              But if we're talking about it in the abstract, look at it like a rock-paper-scissors thing. Stealth as a strategy beats obvious strength, which beats alertness, which beats stealth. If you try to focus exclusively on stealth to the exclusion of everything else, you're sometimes going to run into a situation where everyone is on-guard and stealth is, at the very least, severely disadvantaged -- "STEALTH NO LONGER WORKS FOR THE REST OF THE SCENE PERIOD FULLSTOP" might be too extreme, sure, but using stealth tactics is inherently a losing hand against people who can anticipate exactly when, where, and how you're going to approach. That's in the nature of what it is.

                                  It's funny: the core book goes out of it's way to say this:

                                  Unnatural mental influence is magical mental influence.
                                  Targets recognize the supernatural force behind the character’s
                                  actions                                  . If this influence is hostile, inappropriate, or used against
                                  targets who value their liberty and independence, unnatural
                                  mental influence makes enemies.

                                  And then proceeds to treat UMI as the subtlest shit ever. (re: Stealth)

                                  So, you're a guard, right? And are guarding something. You don't see anyone, but you recognize that some supernatural force is fucking with your mind.
                                  And even if you choose to ignore it (which, unless it's compatible with your Motivation, you can't, so you must spend wp to resist it (Core p.180-181)) as a bad feeling and nothing more, you can still tell your superiors "Yeah, I didn't see anyone but something was fucking with my mind, and then the thing I was guarding was gone".

                                  Or you're a shop owner, and a Solar is talking you into lowering your price with UMI. You can actually stop (spend wp to resist) and say "Hey! Stop it with the UMI shit!" or just yell for guards, because UMI is some anathema shit (or so you're told).

                                  That's kinda... I don't wanna be throwing insults around so I'll say "poorly thought out". No matter what UMI is used, you will always know there was UMI involved.
                                  "I don't remember what I did (Hypnotic Toungue Tech.), but when that one charismatic-looking dude talked to me I definitely felt his magic getting into my mind and staying."

                                  And since characters are actually obligated to spend WP to resist it unless it aligns with their motivation or they want to, everyone you would wanna use it on, would usually be obliged to resist it as much as possible.
