    Solars as NPCs (old forum salvage)

    11-03-2013, 07:51 AM
    Posted by: Mostlyjoe - Monday, June 10, 2013 4:05:55 PM
    Solars are the default Exalted and they have some serious prescribed ideas as to how to 'build' your character. Especially with castes acting as the skill backbone for development of powers.

    That said, Solars do offer some benefits that other Exalted do not:

    1. No predisposed social groups, no Silver Pact, Dragon Blooded host, Deathlord mentors, Yu-Shan. They are on their own and build from the ground up.

    2. Wider mix in groups. Solars can come from ANYWHERE in Creation at all strata of life. Playing the prince and the pauper works in this game.

    3. Maximal Skill Flexibility. Solars just get nicer more general bonuses to skill development Especially in 3E. Pick up 1 charm for an Ability and you get the Excellency? Hell yes.

    But the same benefits also have drawbacks:

    1. No support structures. Lack of allies outside of what you buy in chargen.

    2. Wild mix of cultures can sometimes make silly groups who in the case of a few games don't share common language! (What do you mean you don't know Riverspeak? Why are we travelling together?")

    3. Showy charms and effects. Solars are VERY iconic, and unless you are a Night caste, doing anything beyond subtle charms will be identifiable by people with enough Lore.

    But wen building a Solar there are some interesting ways to build, play, and handle them as NPCs. This thread is some of my more interesting ideas and suggestions that might help. I'll kick out with some character gen examples. But go into Solar Tips and Tricks. And PLEASE if you have your own advice, post it.

    Justify the Caste:

    Here is a fun exercise. Take the baseline idea for a character. Job, background, etc. And disconnect your thoughts from caste and charms right away.

    Then sit down and write a few sentences justifying each of the 5 Solar castes and why he was chosen for it.

    So: Bob the Scavenger Lord, born in Halta but moved with his family to Nexus at a young age, something of a dandy.

    Dawn:     Lead a revolt against the Dragon Blooded who were looting a local shrine and reformed his ways honoring the local gods, was exalted as Dawn because of his tactical thinking.

    Zenith:     Discovered old lore about the UCS and slowly converted to worshiping him and offered teachings and help to those the Immaculate Faith abandoned. Eventually was attacked for heresy and Exalted as Zenith.

    Twilight:     Slowly learned more and more 1st Age crafting techniques by studying his finds and began working on teaching manuals to share with Nexus crafters. Attacked by Deathlord agents who wished to keep the secrets, secret. Caused him to Exalt as Twilight rogue researcher.

    Night:     Proved to be the best at getting into impossible tombs and locations. Slowly developed into a peerless acrobat and tomb raider. Came into a fight with rival Scavenger Lords over territory and discovered their illegal deals with Yozi. Attacked them using his skill and Exalted as Night.

    Eclipse:     Mastered the Scavenger Lord social scene and promoted more and more 'research' into lost cities and culture. Eventually began grand expeditions far afield and Exalted as Eclipse at the discovery of a lost city near the elemental pole. Much to the surprise of his fellow explorers, but his social skill smoothed it over...so far.

    I've found this trick helps break expectations on characters. Sneaky ones don't always have to be Night, nor do social ones have to be Eclipse. Take a profession and see what castes it can mix into.

    Sometimes I even intentionally go counter to the prescribed caste just to see the result.[/INDENT]

    The "Support" Solar

    Playing the support Solar can be rewarding and it doesn't suffer the downside of playing say a Cleric or support Wizard in D&D. One of the benefits is you tend to be a skill monkey and it's really ease to pick up followers that you can buff with gear and training.

    Keep in mind it's more than just playing a Solar Crafter. That's one potential method of supporting a Circle. But a true supporting Solar thinks in terms of logistics.

    For Example:

    Often a group of Solars is over optimized for Combat, be it social or physical. Rarely do they spend he points or time to develop a character who has contacts in multiple fields, followers to handle day to day issues, or has a mix of status and resources to get into and out of some situations.

    Mind you, Solars are optimized to develop these links and to build that support system since it's one of their favored tricks, but I've seen far too many Solar groups have to 'build up' over time and it really slows down their long term goals.

    Playing a Support Solar is about thinking about those longer term goals. Let the rest of the group deal with the day to day issues. You are the Force Multiplier. You handle the ground work at establishing a base of operations, you equip followers with weapons and gear, you plan out social events the team and sneak into and lay on the charm, you keep contact with the Guild contacts and work out contracts for later jobs and expeditions.

    Don't just be a Solar crater or a Solar social butterfly.

    Think of yourself as the Solar CEO. Adventure Inc. You make it happen.

    The meta-actions in a solar campaign are half the fun of playing a Solar. You just happen to specialize in it.

    And if you want to fight, it's not a big deal to pick up 1 or 2 charms and a mix of artifacts and gear you build or bought and enjoy the rest of the day.

    But you are damn sure the next town they come too you are back on the 'job' of making sure the group has food and lodgings as well as recruiting some more followers to get the next adventure done.[/INDENT]

      Solar NPC "The Fixer" -

      Ever had a player with a less than impressive back story? Lack of decent motivation? It's not always their fault, some players never really had to deal with such an immerse world and some folks don't like their early thoughts on the setting to influence how a character develops. They take the tabula rasa idea of PC creation and go a little too far.

      Enter the "Fixer". Now this concept doesn't have to be a Solar, but I find Solars work better for a few reasons. Here's how the fixer works.

      1. The Fixer is modeled after the PC that needs a little work. So if they are a sneak thief Night caste, they are a lesser sneak thief, maybe with their ability points too spread out. The point is they have fewer charms (like 5 or so) and they JUST Exalted.

      2. The Fixer has a LOT of bad NPC issues. Usually a Dynast is hunting them, or they owe a Deathlord favors, etc. The Fixer has backgrounds that jive with the underdefined PC motivation but are not exactly like them. So if the PC hates "Deathlords" the Fixer has a specific problem with Walker in Darkness. Etc.

      3. The Fixer gravitates to the PC that needs help and treats him/her like a mentor. Acts civil, friendly, etc. I often find the fixer being a kid, or just younger than the PC helps them establish the bond. The trick is don't be obnoxious about it. The NPC is there to learn from the PC, and to offer backup. Nothing to obvious, just friendly help and maybe offers a favor or there for the PC training them.

      Once the Fixer is in place, it's time for them to do their work. There are a few ways to do this:

      1. The Fixer expressly asks the PC for help against their background problems. Some players are open to this. And if the character spends a lot of time in game working to help the Fixer's issues I quietly suggest they update their Intimacies (motivations) to match the Fixer. Or maybe as a counterpoint to the fixer. If they are not down with revenge, then maybe they can offer more positive support. Either way, intimacies get updated and the PC gets more interesting. Point is the PC gets to play mentor and some players REALLY dig this.

      2. The Fixer comes under attack. Backgrounds catch up to people. This is more a 'pull the whole group in" solution and that's fine too. It adds complications to a game and gives the group a larger pantheon of baddies to deal with. The Fixer needs to be grateful and helpful to the group for their mentorship, and as always offer to allow people to build intimacies and change motivations.

      3. The "Death Vow". This is a last use option. If the NPC is beloved of the group...kill them. But do it in such a fashion the PC's get a chance to talk to them before they die. Especially the PC who the fixer is there to fix. The NPC asks them to vow to avenge them so they can die in piece, knowing their friend will carry out their last wishes. Save the Fixer's family, take out the NPC that killed them, etc etc. Solars die. NPC allies of the party dying needs to be a serious moment. Let the group ham it up. Fixer has done their job and needs to move on. Dying is one way to handle it, but not the only way.

      In all times, make the Fixer "adoptable". Something the group can see a clear bonus to taking the NPC under their wing. Solars being on the run tends to gravitate to other Solars for protection and alliances. Use this!

      It works with other Exalts too. Adopting a Solar "Fixer" in a Sidereal game works too. Lunar, you name it. The weird switch of seeing a Solar, typically powerful and self assured, in a position of weakness tends to draw PCs to them like moths to a flame. The Fixer works wonders if use carefully.

      ]The "Scout" Solar -

      Some people are just better at finding problems. Some Solars can be built to do just that and it's a benefit to the circle they are a member of.

      When I say Scout I'm not just talking about a hunt in the forest, tracking NPCs on foot kind of character. That's a type of Scout, but the "Scout" I'm talking about fits into a weird mix of technical, social, and survival skills. One part investigator, one part tactician, dash of spymaster, one part human lightning rod the Scout is adept at locating problems, surviving their initial outbreak, and then reporting spotted weaknesses back to the group.

      A good Scout focuses on situational awareness. Enhanced vision, good reflexes, and a solid mix of identification and informational charms. Also they pick up contacts to spread their information network around. Good social networking skills allows for Social Hacking and discovering clues the rest of the party missed.

      Stuff like "Know the Court", "Keen Senses", etc. Is the first half of the build. The second half is loads of and loads of Lore, Occult, Bureaucracy, Socialize, and War. The "knowledge" skills that allow you to take what you learn and convert it into useful data for the rest of the party.

      Good scouts tend to avoid direct confrontations with foes with out their party, and pick up dodge, social get away, etc charms to back that up.

      Finally consider some secret message techniques so you can call for help and pass along intel to the group.

      A good scout character plays well in Night, Twilight, and Eclipse castes, but is no joke with a properly built Dawn or Zenith. And they are invaluable to a party who is overspecialized in 'doing' things and not so good at finding out what needs to be done.

          Even Bob The Farmer has a dark past. - aka. Life Pathing!

          Have you had to cook up a character for a game and you JUST COULDN'T COOK UP A BACKSTORY?

          No problem.

          Say Hello To Life Paths!

          Introduced back in the day in games like Cyberpunk 2020 and carried forward in stuff like Smallville the idea is a randomly rolled up background. Mind you, today's variants focus more on building up inner party connections and that's fine too. But lets get generic. The idea is to write up 10 or so Events in vague enough terms you can flesh them out from there. Oh hell, here is an example:
          Quote:
          Dark Past Table: Roll 1d10

          1. The Loss - In your youth agents of (1-3 The Realm, 3-6 A Deathlord, 7-0 A Local God) murdered your family and sold you into slavery. You swore vengeance.

          2. The Madness - Years ago you stumbled your way into a Wyld zone, either lured or lost the Fair Folk captured you and were just about to feed when you were saved by a mysterious stranger. To this day the Fair Lord seeks you as sport.

          3. The Legionnaire - You were claimed from your local village by a Realm Legion looking for bodies. You've spent the last few years being drilled into a passable warrior, only to run away recently in a bid to return home. Only, home is now quite strange.

          4. The Murder - A local god blood was tormenting your villiage and you and a few of the local youths banded together and attacked the Godblood hoping to drive him away. Only you wound up delivering a lethal blow. Now the local God is looking for murderer of his son and you have gone into exile to avoid your family getting punished.

          5. The Beach - You awake on a local beach, two jade coins over your eyes, and a change of close laying on your chest. No other memories of your life remain. The only clue you have is non-local flowers that were in your hands.

          6. The Changeling - One of your siblings went missing in their youth, only to 'return' a few days later. You've always known the child that came back isn't your sibling and you've been discovering a growing series of disquieting crimes the being has done as you grown older. Can you prove it?

          7. The Grandfather - When you were young your grandfather was killed in a local forest. Everyone warned you from going out there, but you did because your secret was you talked and spent time with your grandfather's ghost. Recently he claims he found the people who killed him and begs you to do justice.

          8. The Betrothed - When you were a borne a local god demanded you as their consort in payment for a favor for our family. You've been groomed over the years to server your new spouse, but recently discovered the god tricked your family into the agreement.

          9. The Heir - You had a child in your youth but were separated from your lover and child due to scandal and family meddling. Recently you have heard your lover was murdered and your child has gone missing.

          0. The Deal - You made a deal with a demon. Once your family was dying from a plague and a demon offered you a cure for some rather vague favors down the line. Now it's come sneaking around asking about you!


          So suddenly Bob the Farmer has a cool dark past.

          The trick is to write up a few of these when you feel creative. Like an idea notebook of stuff in lots of 10. Mix tables together and roll up for different life events. Like Your Past, Romance, Disaster, etc.

          It's almost as fun as making a character and you can reuse it!

          Have fun!

              The Last Sane Solar NPC -

              This variant NPC is a rare thing. A Solar Elder! Specifically a Solar who saw the purge coming, or was isolated from his/her fellows and went underground to avoid murder. And was so good at it, to this day the Sidereals still have no clue where the Solar is. They know he or she is there, but can't move against them because of the cost involved.

              I call this NPC the Last Sane because that's what they are...barely. Sane. The idea is to grant players 2 opportunities. The first is to take a Solar stepped in isolation and paranoia of hiding for all these years, willing to approach a group of new Solars because of a desperate need to contact, allowing them to 'redeem the elder' as a plot. The second is to give them someone who can describe to them just what being a powerful Solar can be like, and warns them about the excesses of their fellows. (Something that few Solars hear about from a Solar perspective.)

              This has to be handled carefully though. I usually apply a few controls:

              1. The Solar is not a crafter and doesn't know how to re-establish the full scope of 1st Age wonders. They might have a treasure trove of info for Twilights that want to rediscover things on their own. This is very specific to avoid shutting down that style of play.

              2. The Great Curse DID effect them, making them paranoid and overly secretive, when it comes down to it the Elder will allow the new generation of Solars to be the BIG DAMN HEROES. This avoids overshadowing the players.

              3. The Elder doesn't have all the answers. After all, they've been out of touch for hundreds of years. They have a foggy but useful memory of what the 1st Age was like, but also remember the worst parts of it and especially how hubris caused the Solar fall. This allows the Solars to consider humility and the wider implications of their actions.

              4. At most the Elder is essence 6. They didn't have a lot of time to meditate and build essence because they were being low key. They DO however have a wide range of Stealth, Perception, and Survival charms, with a healthy mix of what I'd consider a crazy loner Solar survivalist might have. Making them a wonderful teaching Patron.

              The key is to have the elder listen to what the players know about the world at large NOW. To the Elder everythign is new and weird, they know the small safe place they call home (maybe a pocket in elsewhere, or somewhere in the Wyld), and they have their memories. Play up the quiet mentor side of things. They offer advice, and will point them at old Solar caches of resources and intel. But they won't outshine the group. When the players catch in raw Charm development and Essence down the line in the campaign, the Elder might feel more at ease and come out in the open WITH THE GROUP as a more active Ally. Especially when negotiating with other Elders.

              Think of the NPC as a diplomat mentor.

              Also, it's a great tool to show just how much the great curse eats at the mind. The Elder is 'sane' but that's a whisper away from showing the cracks in their mind.

              The Con Artist Solar -

              There is a sucker born every minute, and having someone in the group who can exploit this actually goes a LONG way to handling complications that come up in places. Unlike a pure social character a true con artists has a mix of skills that include Performance, Presence, Larceny, Forgery (both Linguistics and Craft), and the perception to spot openings. Think of this character as the ultimate manipulator. Their largest goal is to bypass local laws, throw off enemies, and get what the party needs as quickly as they can.

              Some of their techniques include:

              * Passing themselves off as agents of distant nations, or heavenly powers.

              * Starting rumors to throw off the Wyld Hunt and other foes from find the PCs.

              * Forging travel papers, forms of ID, and money depending on location.

              * Creatively securing artifacts, sorcery, and various 'goods'.

              * Mapping out useful agents and cultivating contacts in criminal groups, dissatfied local nobles, and various marginalized peoples.

              * Sabotaging investigations and local governments attempts to derail the PC's plans.

              Consider them Social Sappers. They weaken and break down hostile national and social powers while keeping an eye out for attacks along the same vectors. This mix of sneak thief/social character is quite rewarding to play.

              You'd think a Night would be perfect for this...and they are to a point. But I've seen Twilights and Zeniths pull it off almost better. Just depends on the player's style.

              Yes, Zenith Criminals. It's fun as hell.

                  The Hilarious Implications of Tiger Training Method -

                  Ah. Tiger Training Method, so useful, so bizarre, and so exploitable.

                  There are other more useful training charms out there that teach other specific skill sets to NPCs, but Tiger Training does 3 things we desperately need.

                  1. It turns mook NPCs into Heroic Mortals.

                  2. It grants them a decent baseline in combat skills.

                  3. It gives them a strong sense of unity and teamwork.

                  Ya so? Ya, everything! What they don't tell you is you don't just have to use that charm to turn small villages into trained militia, or your personal followers into a deadly army.

                  No, you can use it on ANY social group with the right motivation. That includes your contacts in other areas. So, by quietly using the charm in your down time you can create:

                  Kung Fu Cooks, Battle Librarians, and Martial Midwives!

                  Own a nice villa. Imagine the surprise when assassins and criminals discover your maid can drop kick them into the other room.

                  Think of Tiger Training as Kung Fu Hustle the charm. You can take a impoverished hovel in a city and turn it's residents into some of the most dangerous people around. Transform a brothel into assassins and go all Sin City on the local realm rulers. Pop out trained insurrections in a matter of weeks!

                  That's hilariously useful.

                  Better yet, set up a dojo as a cover id for your Solar and crank out hyper skilled students that go out and bring in more clients. Given time you can crank out instant armies and battle groups.

                  Smooth things over with some social charms and everyone will think it's the way things have always been, and should be!

                  Ah Tiger Training. How I love you.

                  Solars Needs No Tools - The MacGyver Way

                  Craftsmen Needs No Tools, useful, helpful in a tight situations, synergies with solar Excellencies allowing them to churn out useful items with little or no notice. It's application is obvious, right?

                  Oh no. Using it just to craft weapons and wagons is just the beginning!

                  Read and learn!

                  Explorer Needs No Tools -

                  Craft Earth your way up a mountain or cliff, carving out perfect hand and foot holds AS YOU GO! Make sure to transform a vine and some sticks into perfect guide lines and pitons.

                  Craft Water to boil and cook just about anything organic into a delicious stew in seconds. Gator, Claw Strider, Leopard, rocks, tree trunks, whatever. It will taste wonderful and be healthy too.

                  Craft Water to eat dangerous plants and spit out poisons into vials.

                  Craft Wood to crank out rafts, instant tents, log cabins on the go. Leave safe camps in the worst terrain.

                  Craft Earth/Air? to make igloos in minutes.

                  Did you know with Craft Earth, Crafting Charms, and maybe a Athletics Charm or 2 you can dig out a underground mine or cave system almost as fast as a Mercury Ant? Neat eh? Secret Base HO!

                  Clean and excavate a ruin with your hands safer than with a full crew!

                  Socialite Needs No Tools -

                  Spin sand into perfect glass wear...in your hands. In minutes!

                  Repair and clean clothing with a wave of your hand. You ARE the ultimate seamstress.

                  Why yes, my ball gown was made from the curtains, and yes it is QUITE stunning!

                  No other chef in town can match your home made food.

                  Easy Ice Sculptures anyone?

                  With the right mix of performance charms your dinner display will be the talk of the town for years to come.

                  Instant Costumes!

                  Shave, style and dye your hair, brush your teeth. All on the go with your bare hands!

                  Snap your fingers and dust leaps off of things!

                  Even a shanty town with scrap metal and wood will look like the upper class district of town in a week or less if you are around.

                  Peel a Perfect Apple as a stunt, with your fingers!

                  Spit and rub out stains on clothing in seconds.

                  Grab some incense and make yourself smell like you were bathing in oils for hours.

                  One Glass of Water + One bunch of Grapes = Impressive Party Trick.

                  Don't have in invitation, mix in some Linguistics charms and YES YOU DO! (Just carry some ink and paper in your pouch.)

                  Leave official stamps with your thumb!

                  Criminal Needs No Tools -

                  Lock Disassemble!

                  Eyeball Forgeries of rare items with cheap raw materials to crank out great forgeries.

                  Cut class with your fingers.

                  Saw through walls with the back of your hand.

                  Table cloth + random junk = Instant traps.

                  Again, perfect costumes!

                  The list goes on and on.

                  You can think of creative ways to reapply this charm!

                      Have You Considered Living On a Boat?

                      Frankly I'm surprised that all 1st Age Mansion were not actually artifact boats that Solars were the 'captain' of. No honestly, look at the benefits:

                      1. Charms that allow you to ignore penalties.

                                              It's all about building a family

                                              First off, ever thought of using a local DB as your party's Face? Remember, Dragon Blooded tend to have just as much raw skill as a Solar just not the crazy high power level to apply. If you have a DB with massive social skills, let him or her lead the conversation with Solar's acting as passive support. In many ways charm use might get ignored with the DB acting as the distraction from the rest of the party. And it's impressive as hell when a previously minor local Dragon Blood shows up with Sorcery, social, and technical powers at his or her command.

Sure, they have to run the sacred hunt, but acting as a spotter, and assisting in the hunt isn't a bad thing. Or better, looking for and creating ways for the Lunar's 'new' face to enter a social situation allows for crazy good deep cover. Solars are the ultimate social monkeywrenches, and a lot of their charms if used to distract and sooth social questions and fears can take a Lunar ally and embed them in just about any location with little to no questions. One of the great synergies of Lunar to Solar is Lunars sneak why Solars distract. Use this to your advantage.

                                              Etc, etc. The idea is to look at other Exalts and morals as additions to your character. Remember, contacts and allies are the great balances for you own power or skill holes. You don't have to be good at everything, even though some Solar players think this is the case.

                                              In the idea of adopting others into your family, it can be literally.
