                    Whether Conan is Exalted is... questionable. Most of his prowess falls on the upper end of the human spectrum, but his endurance may be off the charts. The crucifixion in A Witch Shall be Born is the clearest indication that Conan possesses superhuman resilience (read the details in my post above). You might also have a look at the bear trap from Red Nails, which has the following choice passage.
                    "The jaws of an iron trap had closed on his leg, with teeth that sank deep and held. Only the ridged muscles on his calf saved the bone from bei

                    I'm not well versed enough on human anatomy to state definitively either way, but any argument should factor in those two passages before passing judgment.

                    As for what type of Exalted (supposing he is), I would still go with Solar. Solars are the archetypical heroes, whose feats of prowess are all extensions of mortal ability. Conan definitely fits that mold, and he's a poster boy for modeling a Dawn Caste (whether or not he is Exalted).

                    I'm wary of electing him to the ranks of Exigents. I tend to think of the Exigents as purpose-driven in a way that doesn't correspond well with REH's Conan.

                    So, all that said, if someone just said "Conan is a mortal with maxed out stats in several Attributes and Abilities", I wouldn't put up a big argument. He definitely hovers on the edge.
                    Honestly, there's this fuzzy area between "Newly Exalted Solar" and "Jacked up Heroic Mortal with 5s in everything and 30 dots of merits and stuff" where you could argue either way about people.

                    Personally, if I were trying to translate Conan over as a Solar, I'd put his attributes and ability ratings at a bit less than what might be indicated from the books and make sure he had excellencies in all the main areas. Because Conan doesn't have to have superhuman prowess all the time and adding excellency dice will allow him to pull off those big rolls when he needs to.

                    Another thing to remember about translating other fictional characters into Exalted is that, narratively speaking, the Exaltation is Creation's equivalent of the "cinematic pulp action rules" or whatever that makes that person a Bad-Ass. So, Sherlock Holmes, Conan, and Batman would all be Exalted in Creation, because that's how the game is designed, in much the same way they'd all be Inspired if you were playing Adventure or have High Tier Strange Skills if you were playing FATE Kerberos Club.

                        The interesting thing about Conan is that he likes civilization. He just hates what civilization does to people. They inevitably become amoral, corrupt, indolent... and evil. Exalted's setting reflects this viewpoints. The decadent corruption of every Creation-spanning empire reflects the decayed cultures depicted in Xuthal of the Dusk and Red Nails. A land which becomes too civilized ultimately devours itself. Whether this is better than the alternative - reverting to barbarianism - is an interesting question that Conan struggles with. Conan's life as king is one of the most interesting aspects of his character, because it reflects a central question of the Solars: not whether they have power, but whether they should exercise it. He makes himself a king, but it forces him to civilize himself. The corruption never quite touches him in the stories, but it infects his followers (as best seen in The Phoenix on the Sword), and he forever faces the temptation to abandon his responsibilities. Howard never wrote an ending to Conan's life, but speculated that he was ultimately forced into a war of conquest for self-defense, and may (or may not) have died in one of the conflicts.

                        I recommend using this as insight into adventures for Solar heroes who becomes rulers. You may slay the cruel satrap and his puppet king, then take their place, but who do you put in place of their cronies? How do you handle insubordination from the people you are trying to protect? When you hear the call to adventure, who sits upon your throne (remembering that Conan had no heir, which caused its own problems during a rebellion)? How do you deal with well-intentioned traitors? How do you punish people who have become corrupt only because of your largesse? And who do you trust during a revolt?
