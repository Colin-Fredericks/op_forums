    This level of reasoning is possible for the Solar Exalted! [Solar Investigation]

    06-13-2014, 02:13 AM
    (Note: Since we're between editions at the moment, I've left off some details, like costs, and have left the rules for resistance in a vague 2e "spend WP" state; I suspect it should be easy enough to adapt these charms to whatever, though.)


    Unquestionable Detective Authority
    Duration: Indefinite

    The Lawgivers have the authority to investigate all the crimes of Creation. This charm is activated to investigate a specific crime, and endows the Solar with several abilities, which are applicable only while actively pursuing that specific investigation; others can resist these rights via spending WP to resist, which inoculates them from that particular rule for this particular crime. Additionally, this charm cannot be used to 'investigate' a crime which the Lawgiver actually committed themselves. Otherwise, the authorities granted while this charm is active include:

    * The Lawgiver has the right to investigate all potential crime scenes and inspect the bodies of anyone who may have been murdered.
    * When they thoroughly investigate a possible crime scene, they automatically detect any mundane entrances or exits which a hypothetical culprit may have used to enter or escape the area.
    * So long as they are actively investigating a particular crime, the Lawgiver may not be accused of committing it themselves. (Recall, however, that Unquestionable Detective Authority cannot be invoked if they are actually guilty.)
    * In a broader sense, all characters intuitively recognize the Lawgiver as the detective for this crime, and will instinctively defer to them in matters directly pertaining to it, to the extent that they would normally defer to someone entitled to investigate the crime. Apart from what is listed above, this does not compel anyone to grant the Lawgiver any authority beyond what they would normally accord to someone entitled to investigate a crime; remember that in many parts of Creation, there is little formal framework for investigation, and therefore the additional respect accorded to their role as the detective will be mostly in the form of loose customs rather than guaranteed rights.

    Other characters are never consciously aware of the precise operation of these rules, so the inapplicability or or inability to activate Unquestionable Detective Authority cannot be used to place the Lawgiver under suspicion for a crime under any circumstances.


    Inviolate Lawgiver Seal
    Duration: Instant

    In the hands of a Lawgiver, even the most humble mark becomes an inviolate seal, trapping the wicked in their own lies. Activating this charm, the Lawgiver touches one room or structure, silently placing an intangible seal upon it. At any later date, the Lawgiver can, by touching it again, instantly detect whether anyone has broken the seal by entering or exiting the room or structure in question. No form of transportation can bypass the seal without breaking it; even intangible creatures, teleportation, or summoning will cause it to break. The seal cannot be detected by any means, save by the Lawgiver who placed it.



    Cardinal Axiom Declaration
    Duration: Instant

    It is not enough for a Lawgiver to uncover the truth; they must burn it, searing-red, into the minds of those who would deny it. This charm enhances any declaration the Lawgiver makes regarding an objective truth they have personally verified with an Investigation charm or some similar magic (other than effects which merely enhance or modify rolls, such as Excellencies). All onlookers intuitively accept this declaration as true provided they have no compelling reason not to. The Lawgiver may never state anything they themselves believe to be untrue with this charm, nor can it be used to state abstract philosophical or ideological points – only basic, fundamentally-objective facts can be stated using this charm. Motives, however, and emotional states relevant to them, are considered fundamentally objective for this purpose to the extent that they can be exposed with charms.

    For the purposes of this charm, the Lawgiver may assume that their charms are flawless and incontrovertible in their function, to the extent that that function itself is designed to be flawless; for instance, if they have investigated a room with a suitable Investigation charm and turned up no murder weapons, they may declare as absolute fact that none existed there at that time. The Lawgiver is flawless, and therefore evidence that they failed to turn up in their investigations simply does not exist – it cannot be presented even in the hypothetical.



    Chains of Cerulean Logic
    Duration: Instant

    The Lawgivers bring the light of truth into the darkness, revealing the crimes of the unrighteous and forging them into chains from which there is no escape. By activating this charm, the Solar presents a target with a hypothesis for a particular crime they are investigating, explaining some element of how or why it was committed. If the victim is able to refute some element of the Solar's accusation, they must attempt to do so; however, the victim cannot knowingly lie or even state anything they less than absolutely certain about in their response, nor can they make any claims that violate something that has previously been established with Cardinal Axiom Declaration over the course of the Lawgiver’s investigation of this case -- if they attempt to do so, they find their words catching in their throat. Likewise, they must present some refutation if they are aware of any; they can accede to the Solar's logic only if they honestly have absolutely no valid arguments capable of refuting it. They may refuse to answer entirely only via spending WP to resist in the usual manner.

    As with Cardinal Axiom Declaration, refutations must be based on objective truth. Unlike that charm, refutations do not have to have actually been previously verified, but they must rely on evidence that is at least hypothetically verifiable via some means; it is never sufficient to merely assert someone's innocence or to deny the Lawgiver's hypothesis without asserting some specific, verifiable fact or alibi that would contradict it.

    If the Lawgiver activates Cardinal Axiom Declaration immediately after a response to this charm to dismiss some element of the victim’s refutation, they may activate this charm a second time for free to present the victim with an identical or slightly-modified hypothesis, which naturally must be answered without violating the new declaration.



    Boundless Executioner Authority
    Duration: Scene

    Though the unrighteous may hide behind lies, deceptions, and undeserved honors, in the end the great equalizer is death. Invoking this charm, the Lawgiver calls upon their authority as the chosen of the Unconquered Sun to personally execute the sentences they have delivered. This charm may be activated only after a victim has failed or refused to refute a hypothesis the Solar presented using Chains of Cerulean Logic which, under local laws, would implicate them in a crime the Solar is investigating using Unquestionable Detective Authority and which normally carries a sentence of lifetime imprisonment or death. (In completely-lawless areas, the Solar may instead substitute their own primary legal or moral principles.) This expands the list of authorities granted to the Solar under Unquestionable Detective Authority, granting them and anyone acting on their behalf the right to execute the victim immediately and personally; save by resisting as per that charm's rules, no one else may interfere or hold them responsible for a successful or attempted execution under this clause.

    The victim, however, may defend themselves normally, and others are not compelled to assist the Solar's actions against them, merely to stand aside and let justice take its course.



    ...what? No, of course these are not supernatural means of investigation. These just demonstrate the logic that is possible for the Solar Exalted! What do you think, everyone?

              My 2 cents:

              Unquestionable Detective Authority
              - this honestly seems to function more as a presence charm... "I have the right to do this, BECAUSE SOLAR!"
              - in that way its very similar to Authority Radiating Stance, because it goes "I have authority over you, BECAUSE SOLAR" which can easily be spun into "I have the authority to supercede any local crime investigators"

              Inviolate Lawgiver Seal
              - I like the idea of the charm, but the way you describe the function it sounds almost more sidereal or lunar themed
              - lunars already have a charm to magically mark the area around them and then alert them if someone intrudes...
              - If the idea of the charm is to make something to seal off a crime scene, then solars already have a linguistic charm via Glories: UCS that makes a text they write remain hanging in the air as glowing letters if the piece of paper they were written on is burned... I could easily imagine that being used to create magic glowy police tape that you have to burn away first to 'activate'

              Cardinal Axiom Declaration
              - Again this sounds more like a presence charm. "I believe this is true, and thus you must all believe it too" - or a performance charm
              - solars have charms like Judge Ear to find actual truth.
              - if you want a charm that determines if X is true, then that charm falls more under Sidereal territory in that they can consult the loom to see what truly happened

              Chains of Cerulean Logic
              - there is a solar charm somewhere, an essence 4 one, that allows a solar to mark a target as a creature of darkness.
              - and this just sounds like a charm that reads "it makes the solar's statement undodgable, and makes it impossible to parry the statement with lies"
              - also, again as with Cardinal Axiom Decleration, then certainty of absolute truth is kinda of a sid thing if they're supposed to just be able to magically 'access' it. Sure, solars can be really good at discovering the truth the old fashioned way, but a charm that lends certainty to their action... that sounds sid-ish.

              Boundless Executioner Authority
              - this charm lets the solar do what... claim the right to kill a criminal worthy of the death sentence?
              - I would argue that this charm is either needless, or at the very least kinda weaksauce.
              - also it sounds like a presence or possibly even a bureaucracy charm in that its a "You have an offical function here that I want to do, i use magic to usurp that authority for a while!"

                      They're a little strange in that they seem to be about controlling how other people think and behave and inserting the Solar into a social and story role by magic, rather than actually about thinking, gathering and organising evidence, forging relationships with sources and informants, making convincing arguments and using tricks to force crimes to reveal themselves.

                      Charms that say "I am the person in charge, right?" and "You can't do (thing which would make my investigation more difficult) because magical authority" rather than better at finding things out or convincing people that they know the truth.

                      They're useful for solving crimes, yet they feel like they should work through the social system. Like if you wanted a person not to lie to you, you'd use Solar magic skill to find a belief they hold or an emotion they feel that makes it very difficult for them to lie to you, and then inflame it. He's made a promise to his wife, she secretly wants everyone to know how she got her revenge, they fear torture. (Or if the Solar was going to use a sort of Solarified mesmerism, they'd just do that, openly). In fairness, this is a decent enough stab prior to 3e's (hopefully better) social influence system, that is less of a mind control system.

                      I'm trying to think about why these feel less positive to me than the "I appear to be a Master Merchant" Charm. I think it's because Solars are *supposed* to feel like these meritocratic heroes who are gain authority and position by using and demonstrating their skill (for all that one might be an complete arsehole, as a person). Making yourself obviously a master merchant, and then using this to gain a social role is not quite the same as inserting yourself directly into social relationships as the investigator in charge. And, culturally, I think we demand less meritocracy and integrity of our merchants than we do investigators.

                      What are the kinds of pulp story example characters these fit? What Solar inspiration source characters would use them?

                      Specific comments -

                      Inviolate Lawgiver Seal - Would feel more Solar if I imagined a character subtly arranging the room's layout, or leaving some physical mark to be disturbed (perhaps in some ultra essence infused way that was almost undetectable), then re-examining the room for evidence and eureka-ing on their return, rather than if I imagine them placing a glowing sigil.

                      Boundless Executioner Authority - So like the local cop goes "No, this isn't right." and tries to step in, then he needs to spend willpower? Or if a local community of outlaws or foreigners who don't give a chuff about the local law exists, they're magically compelled to care about the law unless they spend the willpower?

                          In general, these charms seem to allow you to emulate pulp fiction and classic whodunnit detective types, in which a person who has no legal authority to investigate crimes goes right ahead and does that, and people just play along. Sherlock, Batman, Poirot, a bunch of contemporary mystery drama TV shows do all of the things you have described, except possibly Boundless Executioner authority.

                          I would join the chorus of people who say "This is more like a social skill than Investigation," but based on the 3E charms I've seen so far, I am not convinced that is the case. It appears to me that in 3E, all Abilities have a certain sphere of related activities to them which do not totally exclude things that aren't written into the definition for the ability. So, rock on.

I feel like this is right on the bleeding edge of the Investigation sphere - it's thematically appropriate for an Investigation-oriented story, but not for all Investigation-oriented stories.

                                      My two cowries:

                                      Unquestionable Detective Authority seems like it should lose the clause that protects the Solar from being accused of the crime. From a thematic perspective, I'm not sure how being a renowned investigator would keep people from suspecting you of wrongdoing (except insofar as you can use your knowledge of criminology to cover up your own trangressions). From a story perspective, detectives get framed for things all the time in fiction. "Our hero must prove his innocence by finding the true killer" is practically its own subgenre with how often its used, to say nothing of all the "Sherlock is Moriarity" theories floating around out there. It'd also be cool if the deference clause were more specific, but that's a little hard to do seeing as we don't have mechanics yet.

                                      Inviolate Lawgiver Seal should be less explicitly magical. I'm with Ghost: Make it so that the Solar immediately notices something out of place, no matter how subtle, that indicates an intruder's presence (and add [Essence] successes on all Investigation rolls to find out what the intruder was up to).

                                      Cardinal Axiom Declaration and Chains of Cerulean logic kind of seem like they're stepping on Presence's toes. Still, I totally see where you're going with those, so I'm kind of split.

                                      Boundless Executioner's Authority seems like too much. For starters, it's imposing a particular cultural value on bystanders ("Summary execution is A-OK!"). Also, I'm not sure what it has to do with Investigation.
