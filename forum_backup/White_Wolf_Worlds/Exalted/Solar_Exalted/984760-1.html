            Well, for one thing you'd need a Bureaucracy system to actually exist, and a rulership system that doesn't come down to "throw Merits at the problem," and advise against the characters actually getting directly involved in solving problems on the grounds that if the PCs actually do things, they're neglecting their responsibilities "at home." The way the section on leadership is written, if a terrible monster is ravaging the countryside and your Twilight Caste Solar casts Conjuring the Azure Chariot to carry the circle to the right area, his Zenith reassures the locals and leads them away from the combat zone, her Dawn and her friend's Night kill the monster in short order, that other player's Eclipse takes a survey of the damage and available resources and sets up an ad hoc relief and reconstruction organization, and the whole circle's back home in time for tea, you're doing it wrong. The correct method is to use your Followers Merit to have people investigate the monster sightings, then your Command Merit to dispatch the army, then your Resources Merit and Followers Merit to have supplies and workers sent to assist in the rebuilding, and your Cult Merit to have priests assure the populace that you are always doing the very best ting for the kingdom. Yes, I do recognize that the circle can't always attend directly to a problem, and often shouldn't. But the Leadership section as written actively discourages any direct involvement, and the devs have directly stated that they don't want any system that allows Solars to throw lots of dice at their problems, which kind of works against the theme of returned and glorious god-kings. Why bother with kingship when you're actively encouraged to not do anything any ordinary ruler couldn't?

                            "[N]ot as robust as [I] might like" consists of "roll some dice, make stuff up." And the "roll some dice" part is only implied. Seriously.
