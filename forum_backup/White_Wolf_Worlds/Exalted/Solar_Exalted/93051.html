    First Solar Eclipse In Over 1,000 Years (RY 763)

    03-17-2014, 07:22 PM
    I would like some assistance with ideas for what might happen during the First Solar Eclipse in over 1,000 years, which takes place in RY 763. I plan on using these ideas in my Chronicle, but I see no reason that they can't be used in anyone's particular Chronicle -- either as backstory/prelude or as something that takes place in the future (For Chronicles set in the past, like mine).


    First though, some quick primer on my Chronicle.
    It is a Realm Dragon-Blooded game set in RY 700, so I have a few decades to go before the Jade Prison is opened and the Anathema are released upon Creation (Solars, Abyssals and Infernals are released upon Creation -- Not counting the 20-or-so Solars who have been Exalting since the Usurpation...). The Realm is Ascendent, the Satrapies are acquiescent, the Tepet Legions still exist, and the Dragon-Blooded are the Supreme Rulers of Creation. In other words, it is as the world is described before the Jade Prison is opened and the Empress disappears.
    Take a read of the below, and I'll catch you below with my thoughts:

    Originally posted by Ink Monkeys Ultimate Collection, Page: 163
    Solar EcIipses
    "Rarest of all solar events, the last solar eclipse occurred in RY 763 and signaled the return of the Solar Exalted. A solar eclipse had not been seen for more than a thousand years before that, and predicting the next one is a feat worthy of only the most skillful astrologers. No one knows why Ignis Divine orders the moon to cover the sun, but when he does, all Creation takes heed."


    ...and...


    Calibration
    "The Daystar flies a lonely vigil through Creation’s skies during the five days of Calibration, banishing the absolute darkness of a starless night when it rises and calling down an ebon curtain when it sets."

    "Calibration is an eerie time aboard the Daystar. The Ebon Dragon seems to writhe and smile in those murals which depict his hated visage, relishing the fact that for five days, Creation will experience periods of absolute light and dark, paying homage to the duality that defines his relationship with the Unconquered Sun and his continuing mark upon the world. Strange echoes rattle through the superstructure, and Gardullis stirs uneasily in his sleep. The lumina emerge from the walls and floors of the Daystar and walk its halls for these five days; occasionally, they find the Things That Dwell in Corners coalescing in quiet, out-of-the-way parts of the station, and wordlessly slay these least among all demons."

    "Each year, on one day during Calibration—the precise day changes from year to year and is impossible to predict—the Daystar is unable to monitor Creation. Its screens show only blackness. This fact is one of the most closely guarded secrets of the Station Indomitable."

    ...also, see page 162 for Daymoons & Auroras...

    The Solar Eclipse isn't specifically set during Calibration, but I was going to have it happen during the First Day of Calibration. I was going to have the Solar Eclipse be sudden, with no warning (Per the above...), and have it take place at Mid-Day and last for several hours on both sides of the mid-day hour (I have entertained the idea of it being a full-day Solar Eclipse....).

    I can imagine that, with all the tension and superstition surrounding Calibration, that there would be panic and perhaps some violence. Among "Mortals", only a few ancient Exalted remember the last Solar Eclipse. The rest of Creations population knows only legends and tales of the Darkened Sun, which happened over a Thousand Year ago (Perhaps during the Usurpation ??).

    So, I was wondering, what sort of things -- Mundane and Supernatural -- do you think would happen during the Solar Eclipse? What about the rest of this particular Calibration? Let your imaginations loose.

    Thanks in advance.

    Peace.

    (Note: I'll post some of my own ideas a little later. I don't want to "taint" anyone's ideas, per se...)

                                      Copypasta from my PBEM game:

                                      Four years ago, Creation saw its first eclipse in over a millenium.

                                      Not many records still exist from the High First Age, when the last eclipse was seen. Most of Creation's people didn't even know it was possible for the Sun and Moon to come together in such a way. Nearly all saw the event as a portentious omen from the gods, although whether for good or ill was in hot debate.

                                      Fadaku of Tandesh was due to name and launch his new ship that day. The leaders of the Western Trade Alliance, which had commissioned the ship and mortgaged it to him, met and concluded that the day should be considered a day of rest for all operations, officially to honor the gods but actually motivated by an aversion to risk. But, by the time they concluded this, Fadaku had already gone ahead and named the ship in honor of the eclipse. He argued that when the gods embrace in the heavens, this is to be acknowledged, not avoided. They braced their temples and shook their heads, but once a ship is named, it is widely acknowledged to be bad luck to give it a new one. Thus, Heaven's Embrace took to the sea.
                                      The eclipse is an extremely portentious event, but nobody agrees on what, exactly, it portends. You're going to have a lot of people more or less afraid to do anything that day, and a lot of people seeing it as a sign they should finally do that risky thing they've had on their mind forever, but since the eclipse isn't followed by any obviously related major event in most places, people will ultimately just shrug and get on with their lives.

                                      Think of real life events which have major implications on the future of the world. Russia has declared it's going to annex Crimea. What does an ordinary US citizen do with that information? Some might stockpile food because they think it's the first step to another world war. Most will probably shrug, decide the problem is out of their scope, and do what they would have done anyway.
