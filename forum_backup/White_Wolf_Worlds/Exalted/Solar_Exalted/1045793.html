    How Focused are your Solars?

    02-08-2017, 11:52 AM
    I had a conversation with a fellow player in my upcoming game the other night about just how many charms are in Ex3.

    It takes 300XP to reach Essence 5. Assuming that is spent only on Caste or Favored Solar charms, that is about 53 charms once we add in our starting charms. There are many individual charms trees that have more than 53 charms, and with ten Caste/Favored Abilities, it's easy to imagine many characters whose charm wishlists run into the 100+ range.

    This discussion piqued my curiosity. For those of you who have been running Solar PCs for a while now (or maybe STs who bother to track XP for their NPCs), are your Solars investing broadly in many Abilities, or are you focusing more narrowly on one or two? As a follow-up, do you feel your Essence is advancing too fast, relative to where you stand on charm investment? Finally, I'm wondering if anyone feels a bit overwhelmed by all the charms they think their character "ought" to get at some point?

          My (now finished) game had the characters reach 350xp. The least-focused Solar I think was the sorceress, who had a bunch of spells and workings, but also some melee, ride, and a few bits of other stuff. The Twilight had a lot of Craft (you basically have to) and some larceny, melee, dodge, investigation. The Zenith had lots of performance and presence. The Dawn had I think 20 war charms (all the ones in the Core and Miracles), 10 resistance charms, 4 melee charms and 4 bureaucracy charms. Though that was at 320xp, I'm not sure what he bought since then.
          Basically, Solars seemed to have 1 ability with a lot of charms, 2-3 other abilities with a moderate amount, and then a handful of charms from another 3-4 abilities.

          I didn't get the impression at this point that many of the players were desperate to buy a whole bunch more charms. I can tell this because at some points a couple of them had 75+xp on their sheets unspent. I think the only one who had a big wish-list was the sorceress (who had a wish-list of workings to do).

          I'm also playing a 375xp character myself (215 regular XP, 160 solar xp, I think, give or take 5-10 xp), and I've honestly got most of what I want. I deliberately didn't take any combat charms (or abilities, besides Brawl 1) so that I'd have something to buy in-game, as I learn Tiger Style from another PC, the martial arts expert. I actually still have about 100xp unspent, of that 375. I'll spend another 50-ish finishing Tiger Style I guess (I buy about one charm a session, so that it feels like I'm progressing), and after that, I dunno what I'll spend XP on. Maybe abilities when I feel it's appropriate.

              Depends very much on the character. I just had a character reach E5, on him I've purchased maybe 7-8 charms that are not caste/favored, and I have one favoured ability that I still have no charms in.

              Generally, there are few things that play into how rounded your character is going to be as they progress.

              1) are they optimizing around a handful of abilities? If so, then you're going to have a character that invests heavily in those abilities and leaves the others largely untouched. This varies on a player by player and even character by character basis.

              2) are they optimizing around a particular style or effects? This is important to consider, because if you have someone focused on being a circus acrobat for example, you're not just going invest in athletics, you're going to invest in resistance, sail and many other abilities because they support the concept or have effects that allow you to do things that are cool and work with the concept.

              3) changes over time in play. In the example of my E5 character we started with a Twilight player who then had to leave. Since my character is based around the idea of "The Ultimate Criminal Salesman and Shyster" and he needed things to sell to people so I ended up buying out of caste/favored crafting charms. It's also why the rest of the circle is walking around clad in artifact forgeries (unsurprisingly when it looks like you're wielding enough artifacts to equip a minor realm house, people treat you with respect or fear).

              4) how many of the XP and Solar XP sinks you are invested into. Sorcery and Martial Arts are Solar XP sinks, there's no practical end to the amount of Solar XP you can sink into those things and it only gets better for the character. So if you have both of those, that's going to eat up most of your Solar XP. If you have neither of them, that's a lot of Solar XP to spend spreading out your abilities and attributes. Likewise with Craft, which is (while a valuable thing) a large sink of XP and Solar XP if you're going all-in and becoming a master crafter.

                  The PCs in the game I GM have just reached Essence 2, though they haven't had the opportunity to actually raise it. Their distribution of charms by ability is as follows, with supernal first and grouped broadly by category:

                  Dawn: Melee 11, Resistance 3, War 1, Athletics 3, Awareness 2, Presence 1
                  Twilight: Lore 10, Occult 2, Craft 5, Resistance 1, Spells 4
                  Night: Larceny 10, Stealth 4, Martial Arts 3, Athletics 3, Socialize 1
                  Eclipse: Linguistics 7, Socialize 2, Presence 1, Integrity 1, Dodge 1, Melee 1, Resistance 2, Awareness 1, Ride 1, Investigation 1, Spells 2

                  As you can see the Eclipse is much more varied in his purchases than the other characters, in part because he's bought entry level charms in some abilities primarily to get the excellency, and because he used his Supernal to make a beeline to a powerful capstone charm (Unbreakable Fascination/Twisted Words Technique) rather than spreading out as the others did. This player also has a gigantic charm wishlist on his character's talk page, encompassing .

                  XP distribution between different categories is as follows (note that we use a flattened advancement system, so some expenditure values may not be possible RAW):

                  Dawn: Solar Charms 48, Abilities 20, Merits 6
                  Twilight: Solar Charms 48, Spells 8, Attributes 6, Abilities 16
                  Night: Solar Charms 48, Attributes 10, Abilities 21
                  Eclipse: Solar Charms 38, Spells 8, Abilities 32

                  In general, I feel Essence advances slightly too fast. It's only overtaken our first major story arc by a few sessions, but most of the characters still have a lot of Essence 1 charms they want to take.

                      It's interesting; neither of my characters have never had charms that aren't at their Essence, including their supernal. This is unlikely to ever change for the Dawn, who just hit E3 (since there are barely any E4+ melee charms), but this is going to probably rapidly change for the Zenith, who is a presence supernal. I'm making a beeline for the high Essence Performance charms, basically.

                      Overall, I try to make characters with a wide range of charms, but with 2 or 3 "shticks". The dawn is awareness and melee, the Zenith will be probably Performance and Lore and Sail.

                      Likewise, I feel like Essence advances very fast because it advances on a "by session" level, and while I feel like a lot of people have 6+ hour sessions, ours usually run shorter than that by a wide margin, usually between 3.5 and 4.5 hours.
