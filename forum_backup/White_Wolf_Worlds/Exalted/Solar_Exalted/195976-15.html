        When Solars triumph over demons and Fair Folk, yes fine, myth hero overcoming the supernatural, if you like. Not Sidereals or Lunars, or really any other sort of Exalted, except maybe those whose story is about becoming inhuman, like Abyssals and Infernals, but even then, that's a fact of their political position, not nature.

        I think it is more that the Sidereals and Lunar's powers by their nature distance them from being human. The more powerful a Lunar gets the more obvious it is they are something Protean, Primal, Savage. The sid becomes more and more distant, detached, and a Plutocrat. They become so wrapped up in their convention and area that they start to seem like the gods. Always worried about their reports and domains rather than the people who might come to harm. They are not becoming monsters just their power and it's nature distances them from the very things that make a person human.

        Solars on the other hand (and to some extent Dragon Blooded) draw from humanistic elements. Their nature makes them draw closer to the emotions, thoughts, and mind of being human.

                                                            Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                                                            On this, this is all far from unreasonable, but I would like to say, when a Solar beats a Sidereal or Lunar, I never have really seen that as more some kind of laudable triumph for the human spirit over magical forces any more than when a Solar who uses a sword beats a Solar who uses Sorcery (and sorcerors being intended to arguably the strongest amongst Solars anyway) or in the new game, a Solar who uses blatantly magical evocations.

                                                            Such Solars are defeating a human who has triumphed over and mastered magical forces and their self through painstaking talent, effort and work (using human aptitudes of sorts the Solars don't necessarily have), not humans who are tools of supernatural forces, or who'rebecoming inhuman somehow, or who've luckily landed on supernatural power through random unfairness.

                                                            When Solars triumph over demons and Fair Folk, yes fine, myth hero overcoming the supernatural, if you like. Not Sidereals or Lunars, or really any other sort of Exalted, except maybe those whose story is about becoming inhuman, like Abyssals and Infernals, but even then, that's a fact of their political position, not nature.

                                                            I think its bad news for the game if Sidereals and Lunars start to become to be seen as tools of supernatural forces to be defeated or monsters to be triumphed over, rather than as people who've mastered particular magical skills and abilities that just aren't in the Solars' genre. I can't see any reason for it other than to try to justify Solars in a way which is unnecessary and misleading anyway]

                                                            Yeah, it's more of a subtextual thematic. The game doesn't actually say "the Solars are the strongest because they are the most Exalted and everyone else's power is compromised by alien aspects", because the setting doesn't work that way and probably shouldn't -- it isn't supposed to inform the experience of playing a Lunar or Sidereal to that degree, and stating it outright would, as you say, warp people''s understanding of the Exalted. It's a thematic and aesthetic undertone. You aren't supposed to think of Sidereals and Lunars and what not as alien monsters who exist to be defeated by humanity -- they're still Exalted, after all. But this is the reasoning that I think underlies the design decision to make Solars the most powerful, and regardless of how visible it's meant to be to players, this thread of discussion drags that stuff out into the open. This is the same underlying design process that made Sidereals the Secret Masters of Everything, the Lunars the Punk Rock Monster-People from the World's End, etc, which are both equally compelling concept-spaces.


                                                            Originally posted by NewK View Post
                                                            Also, we should note that the writers have stated that 3E solars, while still powerful are NOT as far beyond the other exalted as they were in earlier books. So there's that. On the other hand, if a Solar isn't more powerful than any other splat for a given XP level you run into a problem called "the entire premise of the game falls flat" Supposedly, the realm and Sidereals are both terrified of the danger of letting solars gt their feet under them so they make damned certain to kill them before they get that power for the reason that the solars can outstrip the other exalted if given time to do so.
                                                            What I surmise from the writer quotes is that the goal is to put Solars, Sidereals and Lunars on roughly even footing out of the gate, with a few nominal concessions to Solars, like "here, have an extra Charm and Favored ability". I wouldn't even be surprised to see Sidereals a little stronger at chargen, though there's been nothing said to that effect. Then Solars pull ahead a little bit at a time, and end up significantly more powerful at the end (by which time the others have had the opportunity to fully build on their own gimmicks in a mixed game, but most games are likely to end by Ess 3 anyway). I mean, there's no knowing what we'll actually get, but that seems to be the plan. Of course, from an in-setting (rather than in-party) perspective, the Solars are going to be dramatically weaker at the start, since they're virtually all freshly Exalted.

                                                            The general structure of the Time of Tumult for established non-Solars with long memories is that the Solars are nuclear weapons, and the tension is in "man, what do I do with all these Solars?" because you know that they are vulnerable to manipulation and attack, but will eventually ripen into unimaginable power, and you have to decide how you want to deal with that. Do you ally with their enemies to put them down? Do you try to kill any who fuck with you, while otherwise leaving well enough alone? Do you wind them up, point them at something you want gone, and then stay out of it? Do you try to make friends and sell them on your own agenda, aware that you have to be careful because one day they may be too powerful for you to control?

                                                            Making the Solars' overwhelming superiority something that they have to grow into plays to these themes while preserving the integrity of mixed-party games, and you can always make Solars "feel" stronger out of the gate with inconsequential changes like making their charms cost 1 xp less, giving them an extra favored pick, and so on.
