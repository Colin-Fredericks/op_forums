        Originally posted by Totentanz
        The net result for Solars is some characters are defined by Supernal, but most are some degree of generalist or hybrid specialist. A Dawn Caste who builds around Melee, Thrown, and War is going to feel very, very different from one who builds around Brawl/MA Supernal. So different, in fact, that I doubt they would conceptually step on each other much (other than they both fight things, and don't most Exalts fight to one extent or another?).
        This is something I find very important when players start fretting about characters having similar abilities.
        Some things can feel niche-stealy when two PCs have it. For example, you flee to the river and need to escape quickly on a boat. The Night-caste Pirate steps forward and says "time to break out my Sail charms! Awesome!" And then the Eclipse does the same too. Redundant.
        On the other hand, combat abilities are virtually never redundant, because fights generally involve all the PCs anyway. Occult is largely similar: after all, in a fight against spirits, everyone's going to be fighting the spirits if they can (not all Occult charms are about fighting spirits of course... but the majority are). And it's useful for multiple people to have some social skills, because sometimes other people want to speak besides the Face. Last session several of my players decided they needed to buy Performance, because you can't rely on the Zenith to do all your talking for you, when other PCs have different opinions.

        This brings me to another point. Some "concepts" are just really big. Martial Arts and Sorcery are conceptually and mechanically broad enough that they can sustain a character concept all on their own. MA has enough neat tricks and combinations that a character who invests heavily will always have something to bring to the party. Sorcery, assuming you are willing to update spells to 3rd Edition, is also pretty broad, because a Sorcerer can do Workings to enhance the party, make minions, alter terrain, etc.
        My game includes a Celestial sorcerer and Solar sorcerer, and they both are useful to the party. They know different spells for the most part, but also workings take a long time, so two of them means they can do two workings at once.
        And MA, of course, is really a whole bunch of different charm sets.

                                I guess the issue is that Zeniths are based more on the biblical figures I find less interesting than the ones I find more interesting.
                                Are you speaking in terms of the canon Zeniths on offer, or what you feel is the conceptual space of Zeniths in general? If you're speaking of the former, I tend to agree (right down to Panther being the loud, black preacher who shouts about his sins). If the latter, I think you can do a lot more with them than that.

                                Or maybe this is more about the "leader" aspect of Zeniths, and less about the spiritual overtones?

                                Anyway, I think you can take just about any profession/avocation/political role and make it the core of a Zenith. You just make their defining trait something they believe in, not something they do. A Zenith scholar isn't a scholar because he likes books and knowledge; he knows that knowledge is the key to saving his people, making the world a better place, etc. It's not an intellectual/analytical conclusion, but a matter of faith, the way many people simply know God exists. If you can give the character that gut-level belief in something, they can be Zenith, even if they aren't overtly religious or remotely interesting in traditional leadership.

                                Sample of RL Zeniths
                                Gandhi
                                Bloody Mary -- faith was the inspiration, or perhaps merely the excuse, for her depredations
                                Patton -- the man treated warfare as a religion, not simply a calling.
                                Mao -- uses ideology to win support for himself and fuel his rise to power.

                                                    I think that saying Zeniths are the priests is like saying Dawns are fighters, or that Twilights are sorcerers. Not wrong, but nowhere NEAR the full story.

                                                    To me, the Zeniths inspire. The thing is? This can come in countless ways. A Zenith should, after leaving you life, change you in some way, make you want to be greater. They could speak and convince the commoners to rise up against their oppressors. They could show up and rally the people to rebuild their home in the wake of a disaster.

                                                    The wandering Righteous Devil that came in and cleared out the bandits keeping the town in fear? SHE was a Zenith.

                                                    The nameless man who came through and set all the slaves free, and told them to find their own future? Well that was probably Strength-of-Many. Or it could have been a Zenith.

                                                    The former Dynast who left her old life behind, and now wants to try and make this world better (despite having NO goddamn idea how to start that?) SHE can be a Zenith.
