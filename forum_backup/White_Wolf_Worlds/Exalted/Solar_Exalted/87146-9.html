                    Solars will be heroes to some and villains to others. Just like most real life "heroes."

                    General Custer was viewed as a hero and martyr by many United States citizens. But ask the Native Americans who he slaughtered what they thought of him. Pancho Villa is revered by many as a revolutionary hero, but lots of people consider him nothing more than a murders bandit. Genghis Khan is a hero to the Mongolian people, but few non-Mongolians view him in nearly that much of a positive light. Hannibal was a hero and legend of Carthage but reviled as a demon and destroyer by the people of Rome. Achilles was the hero of the Trojan War, but the people of Troy didn't exactly love the guy.

                    Being a hero doesn't mean being good. It doesn't mean being kung-fu-Gandhi. Being a hero in Exalted simply means being a person who does amazing things that can and will change the world. Some Solars will be generally nice dudes and try to do their best to bring good to the greatest number of people. Others will be like the Bull of the North and have a more narrow set of people they plan to bring "good" to. And some like Havesh will bring good to themselves and damn everybody else. They're all Solars, they're all heroes, they're just not all sunshine and rainbows.

                    *EDIT* Ultimately the Solars are human, just like every other Exalt, and no more prone to be paladins than any other Exalt is.

                        Originally posted by HK-87 View Post
                        I'm not confusing anything, perhaps you just have a more optimistic outlook on the whole Thousand Dooms situation than I do.

                        As it stands, 1E is canonically doomed no matter what the Exalted do. 2E not being like this is entirely unsupported by the books when there's an apocalyptic threat lurking under every rock and everyone who might be able to do something about them is either gearing up for a civil war, too busy doing celestial paperwork, or invariably being driven crazy by the Great Curse because they have no ability to resist it (setting aside the Infernals, who are probably the best bet for the whole saving the world thing on account of Torment having a release valve packaged with it).

                        It really isn't that much different from 40K -- the main difference is that Exalted has player characters who matter and can influence things, but even they have limit tracks and no ability to resist when the game decides to take away their character sheets for a few scenes.
                        That's not true. 1st ed. assumed that there was a chance you could do everything right and still lose, but it never said that was a certainty by any means. From what I recall, 1st ed. hated saying things with certainty so your assertion is a very strange one to make. 2nd ed. liked to create plot hooks with apocalyptic overtones because people seemed to really latch onto those. That doesn't mean everyone is doomed, it just means the supplement writers thought that writing for Exalted meant turning things up to eleven for the sake of a crap definition of epic-ness (I have no doubt a lot of the blame for this is on the internet echo chamber bullshit that surrounded/surrounds Exalted). The Great Curse is an easy target for 'everything is awful', but once again, it's not a guarantee everything will be awful forever. It just means that there is a guarantee that something bad will happen occasionally/eventually given time.

                        Originally posted by Chant
                        Not every Great person is doing something they think is Good. Wizard of Oz offered a good example of Alexander the Great, who conquered for the sake of conquest, self-aggrandisement, and self-enrichment. Not because he wanted to reshape the societies of those conquered, not because he wanted to set the world in some state of affairs that he thought was best, but simply because he had a drive to be great. And, as Wizard of Oz pointed out, that doesn't fit with even my lax definition of "good".
                        Alexander was going well into megalomaniac territory and may have been planning on creating a one-world-one-empire situation. He took steps to integrate the Greek and Persian peoples and a lot of his generals hated him because he was trying to fuse the two cultures into one*. When you get down to it, Alexander killed a shitload of Greeks. Whether mercenaries in the service of Persia, rebels fighting against him, or casualties of his ambition plenty of the people he was trying to "help" died as a result. It's just that Alexander's version of Good was wrecking that place next door and taking all their stuff and giving it to his men. Which is a big thing in Exalted and why we're all here today I bet. We look at stuff through a set of beliefs that would be laughable to people from Alexander's era when it was commonly held that "It is the right of the strong to rule the weak" and "It may have been wrong to take our empire, but we have no choice except to hold onto it now". Not a lot of credence given to Christian ideals such as universal forgiveness, meekness, or martyrdom.

                        *That being said, Alexander's integration attempts were probably motivated by realpolitik and creating a counter-balance to the Macedonian military that he was reliant upon and thus subject to, as was amply demonstrated past the Indus river. However, the mythological Alexander was a visionary god-king that wanted to unite the people of Earth under one empire with one culture (namely, his culture plus the parts he liked from other cultures).

This is not a thing Essential to Solars; it is not what they are categorically for. But it's what the world needs at the moment, and it's the standard the Unconquered Sun is holding his Chosen to (and hey, once it's reasonably considered to have been fulfilled, we can start talking about that whole "King of Heaven provides divine assistance to the greatest civilisation on Earth).

                                                Contrast with the Abyssal Exalted, the Solar seen as the final nail in the coffin of a dying world, as interpreted by ghosts who've had a thousand years to brood over their deaths and decide that the Sidereals were right about how Solars can't be trusted for the sake of empowering themselves (emotionally, that is) and who, having died and lost the reason to live, hate the living world, and assume that all their Chosen need is to have the same losses and they'll hate it too.
