            Now I'm thinking about some of the circumstances surrounding changing levels of esteem for gods in a few societies, such as Roman and Egyptian.

            ​Even in cases where one of the Exalted doesn't take their personal relationship with the Unconquered Sun as a reason for worship, there are advantages to championing an obscure deity. It can provide a framework by which one establishes themselves as a new political bloc, invoking the legitimacy of something distantly familiar while also using the way it is uncommon as a means of distinguishing yourself, allowing your achievements to stand out more and garner more popularity. It also gives you the advantage of appealing to people disillusioned with the status quo by selling them on something with the advantage of novelty. Alternatively, it can serve as a means by which one projects their own rise to power, becoming the language through which your ascendency is stated and discussed. That one isn't even necessarily about making people worship the god, so much as making monuments to it the visible symbol of your presence in their lives

            ​Those are the kinds of things that happened when the adherents of Cybele (a deity of Phrygian origin) in the Roman Senate held her up as a key to Rome's salvation during the Second Punic War, or how Amun rose from being the god of the relatively minor city of Thebes to being conflated with the sun god Ra.

                    None of my Solars worship the UCS for precisely this reason.

                    Varied Paths: I noticed after assigning her mechanical stats that all of her merits were physical in nature (giantism, pain tolerance, etc etc) so I fluffed it that they came BEFORE she Exalted. So she worships Zelos, a god of strength who is responsible for most of her pre-exalted prowess. This might change in the future if she meets UC(ena)S, but I doubt it. She would not take the knowledge that he essentially abandoned the world to its fate well, especially given how badly the Realm is screwing everything up.

                    Not-Saber: This one I don't remember the name of, but you can guess what she was like from the name. She did follow the sort of ideals UCS wanted his Chosen to follow (leadership, helping others, bravery, etc etc) but she had no opinion on the god himself. (I've since reworked her as Varied Paths' first age incarnation - there she very much worshipped the sun, but that's the first age, so not really an issue).

                    Silent Mask: This guy was an assassin for hire that was only loyal to his Khan and to the Tri-Khan. 'Nuff said. But he was in a game where one of the other PCs was from a secretive cult that worshipped the Sun and guarded a gate to the Underworld from which a demon rampaged out one day. (I was new to Exalted at the time so I didn't realize how confusing this was and didn't really question it)

                    Boisterous Fire of the Welcoming Hearth: This guy's game never really got off the ground, but he was a Zenith, so he probably would've heard something from Sol. I never really decided what that was, but given that one of his goals was also to build a giant harem for the sake of political manipulation and manuevering, I doubt he and Sol would have gotten along. (On the other hand, Hearth also genuinely wanted to help people who had been hurt or traumatized by their lives, so... make of that what you will)

                                                            Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
                                                            Possible. But what would a couple of thousand thinking about you matter, compared to a few hundreds devouting their thoughts (praying) or several millions just thinking. Just in a way where thoughts are like the moist in the air, while dedicated prayers are a spring.
                                                            ​This is one of those examples in which I think that this level of granularity doesn't really work. The Cult Merit is too straightforward, it doesn't accommodate questions of how it works when a certain subset of a Direction is especially dedicated compared to the more casual, even perfunctory worshippers.

                                                            ​On the subject of the Unconquered Sun's obscurity, I had an amusing thought about how it would likely be something contrary to the usual assumption of the setting being driven by the Great Man Theory.

                                                            ​I was thinking about factors that would drive such obscurity, and how I wouldn't find it very credible for them to come directly from any given agenda. I thought of the idea of it coming from the schemes of the Sidereals, which was immediately quashed by the sense that they wouldn't have the audacity. From there I got the sense that nobody would be so bold, assuming they necessarily had the will to try (I somewhat doubt the early Immaculates cared).

                                                            ​So from there I got the impression that worship of the Sun would fall out of fashion not because anybody specifically discouraged it, but as an emergent property of changes in the social context. Breakdowns in the Second Age changed the incentives behind why people worship in the first place, and alter the market of what gods are at work, and eventually you get a scenario in which worshipping the Sun is something that hardly anybody has the time or attention for. Eventually, the lack of reiterated attention means that you simply get generations of people who grow up with references to the Most High sun god being something they occasionally hear from their grandparents, at best.
