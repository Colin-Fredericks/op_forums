                                Originally posted by Mechalith View Post

                                A working that puts a whole satrapy to sleep is going to be reversed by someone using a working that makes them sleep and wake normally regardless of whether you consider the original working to be 'undone', the only difference is that you can't specifically use the counterspell or spell distortion rules on it. (Which I'm pretty sure was what the author meant by that section.)
                                So, let's take "put a whole satrapy to sleep." I'm the Dowager, and I tell my Storyteller I'm going to do that, Solar Circle working, everything goes as planned. Finesse 5 means I pretty much have full control in designing the working, and then let the Storyteller do the dev work to make sure it balances out. So, what's the duration of the curse? I'm not going to submit a sleep plague that lasts forever indefinitely and unconditionally, because any ST can have the sense to shoot that down. A year and a day? Until a queen's blood is spilled? More likely to get a yes. Other common sense questions, like "can I just stab a sleeping dude?" are probably going to come up at some point, and at that point I need to answer them. There are limits keeping me from omnipotence in obtaining my goals through the working, but they're largely limits that I get to pick.

                                Once a sorcerous working is done, it's done. When one sorcerer tries to tear down another's working, the intent at the heart of the counter-working is not "undo that," but a positive or constructive sorcerous accomplishment that negates its effect on the world around it. When you roll into the Sleeping Satrapy, you can't wake everyone up as a working. If you want to fix things, you'd need to engage with either the effects of the working ("I tear down the veil between waking and dreaming, and everything goes back to being mostly normal except for being a gestalt shared reality") or its limitations ("It's just going to rain queens until there's enough blood, damn it!").
