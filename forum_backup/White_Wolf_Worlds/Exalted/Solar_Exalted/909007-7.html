                                            Plus, Lunars and Sidereals.
                                            I mean, yes, Ma-Ha-Suchi, Raksi and Chejop Kejak, sure. But there's plenty of good Lunars and Sidereals. I'd argue the majority (I mean, relatively). They generally want to improve Creation. Most Sidereals, Lunars and Solars will, if they see some kids about to be eaten by Fair Folk or demons, save them. Alchemicals too actually. Most of them do want to improve Creation, and protect it from supernatural threats, they just argue about how. The Lunars' powers encourage brutality and murder, but they are noble savages who care about the world. The Sidereals' powers encourage trickery, and their politics encourage a "do-whatever-it-takes" mentality, "end justifies the means", but their end aim is to protect the world.
                                            The story of Solars, Lunars and Sidereals isn't about bad people who want to rule the world, it's about great people with noble aims who fight to the death over different views of how the world can be saved. Some may be personally bad, but they're not bad inherently from what they are.
                                            Some of course, do just want to conquer and rule, but for most that's at most a means to the end of fixing the world.

                                            Whereas the majority of Infernals and Abyssals are bad people with bad aims (though, in 3rd ed, hopefully there are plenty who are good, not just a few).

                                            As to Dragonblood... they are, as people said, rather mixed. The Dynasty as a whole is evil, but a)not in a simple way, it also does good things, and b)that doesn't mean all its members are evil, and c)only about half of Dragonblood are dynasts, there's another 10,000 outcastes, who occupy the same kind of place as Solars, just more respected and less powerful.

                                            And finally... I don't think the Alchemicals are evil because they drink soylent cola. I mean, the bodies are dead after all. Cannibalism is repugnant, and dangerous (because of prions) but if you didn't kill the people, I don't see it as necessarily evil. Especially in the low-food environment of Autocthonia.
                                            Actually I often use cannibalism for barbarian characters to show this character's morality is different from others, seeming savage, but they're not actually evil so I don't mind playing them.


                                            And finally... I'll be honest, to many of my players, Lunars and Alchemicals have always appeared the most moral. I have one player who won't play Solars, he doesn't like them at all.

                                                I think it is perfectly moral in-setting for great people to conquer and rule by force, under the assumption that the power gives them the right to rule, and their power will protect the ruled. Average people in the Threshold probably prefer the yoke of the Realm to the uncertainty of warlords and petty kings. The concept of rights is largely foreign, so they just count themselves lucky the Realm garrison keeps bandits away most of the time and only takes 30% of their harvest. Solar/Lunar Alexander the Great believes the world exists to be conquered, and that his rule will bring peace and stability to the world.                                                 Creation is also a brutal world. Sometimes hard choices must be made, and it's their job to make them.

                                                I think the Dynasty is incredibly evil, as a whole, but again we have to take things in context. I wouldn't say it excuses their behavior, but it does allow the portrayal of characters who believe in the Dragons and the Realm earnestly and yet are not black-mustached super-villains. From their own in-setting PoV, most Dynasts are fairly moral and loyal scions of the primary source of stability in the world.

                                                I'm not sure what leads your players who use primitive people as disposable shock troops are moral, but the crux of both our posts is morality is fungible within the context of the game, eh?

                                                            Originally posted by Totentanz
                                                            I'm not sure what leads your players who use primitive people as disposable shock troops are moral
                                                            I think they view Lunars as trying to help primitive people and protect them from a rapacious Empire. Which sometimes involves leading them in heroic wars of native resistance.
                                                            (I should perhaps note that several of my players are anarchists. I suspect this is related. There's one player who's a liberal: he ironically plays somewhat authoritarian characters, for example at the moment the Queen of a country, which sometimes leads to clashes. The others don't have strong political views. I'm a socialist, but hope it doesn't affect my game: except that I can't stand playing aristocratic heroes, unless they're disgraced or bastards or something. Aristocratic villains are fine of course.)

                                                            Originally posted by Totentanz
                                                            But the crux of both our posts is morality is fungible within the context of the game, eh?
                                                            I'd like to claim that, because it's a good point, and easier to defend. But it wasn't actually what I mean, I must admit.

                                                            What I mean is that, broadly speaking, the majority of Solars, Lunars and Sidereals, and a sizeable number of Dragonblood, have the aim of improving Creation (and another sizeable number of Dragonblood just want . They may disagree about the means, and may do bad things to achieve that, but (again, this in wide generalities), and may interpret it through limited lenses (perhaps fighting for their own tribe), but a world where the Solars, Lunars or Sidereals, as a group (rather than individuals, who may just care about their group), have won, is overall probably a better world than now. They all want to keep the world safe from monsters. Even the Dragonblood also do that (though they have the problem of being aristocrats).

                                                            Whereas the Abyssals (from a 2nd ed perspective) want to destroy the world. And the Infernals want it to damn it to hell and enslave it under mad god-monsters (again, from a 2nd ed perspective).

                                                            Now, you can see there are a lot caveats: this is a generality, doesn't apply to everyone, etc, etc.

                                                            But overall, as a very broad rule, this is how I think of Solars, Lunars and Sidereals: imperfect heroes who fight over different aims and loves, but still heroic. While the Abyssals and Infernals are villains. When Sidereals turn up to murder the PCs, it's because they think the world is better and safer without them, that people will be safer and happier if the PCs are dead. When Abyssals turn up to kill them, it's because they love killing people.

                                                            Essentially, Solars, Lunars, Dragonblood, Alchemicals and Sidereals are supposed to be heroes (in-universe, I don't mean the ST should tell the players they have to be heroes), even if they fail to live up to it. Whereas Abyssals and Infernals are supposed to be villains, and become anti-heroes by rebelling against their nature and purpose.

                                                            I wouldn't say this is actually how I run the game: right now, the villains the PCs face are Solars, and they'll later face DBs, Lunars, and maybe Alchemicals, while the local Abyssal circle is friendly and rules the local Underworld peacefully.

                                                            But this is how I think about the setting.
                                                            It's perhaps not very nuanced.
