    Would the Solars have created new Dragon Kings if they could have?

    05-31-2016, 03:31 AM
    We know it is possible to create new Mountain Folk, so it makes you wonder why the Solars never tried to restore the numbers of the Dragon Kings. Assuming it was in their power, why do you think they didn't? Is it just to reinforce the "tragic, fallen heroes of yesterday" vibe they have going, or do you think there are good, in-game reasons for why there numbers weren't increased?

      The problem in 2nd ed was that the Dragon Kings lost many of their souls during the Primordial war, and thus couldn't rebuild their population. Even if new souls could be made, they wouldn't be as powerful, as Dragon Kings got a lot of their power from their memories of past lives, so the amount of effort the Solars would have put in wouldn't give that much short term profit.

      Also Solars probably didn't trust an entire civilization of high powered dinosaur people. Look what they did to other races during the early First Age, the Dragon Kings were only spared due to their shared Loyalty to the Unconquered Sun and their choice of fighting against the Primordials in the Primordial war.

                  I wonder if it's the sort of thing that they might go "oh, wouldn't it be nice if we could make more" but no-one was willing to do the kind of massive Solar workings necessary, because in a way... they didn't want to make more Dragon King souls.

                  See, they obviously had to say "Oh, we love Dragon Kings, they were our good allies", but Solars were humans, and it was convenient for them to not have many DKs left. They could put them in some out-of-the-way areas that humans didn't want and not worry about them too much. The Solars wanted a world of humans. The fact the DKs shared the same religion as them wasn't enough to want to share the world with them. Enough that they wouldn't, say, massacre or drive out the DKs, but not enough that they'd want to reivigorate a race that once oversaw humanity, a race far more powerful than humans.
                  Oh, Solar poets might write paens to the lost glory of the Dragon Kings, Solar historians write books extolling the virtues of ancient DK civilisation, but if someone suggested actually letting the DKs produce more kids... oh dear, the sorcerers would explain, that's just not practical. No, no. The metaphysical soul-forging integration matrixes just won't align. No, no, can't be done. But how about this working to create a massive island of greenery in the snowy wasters of the North, for human settlers to live in? Yes, that can definitely be done.

                  It's like how western newspapers and commentators will decry how terrible things are in Syria, people and governments will donate money to those poor, starving refugees, but share our country with them? No, no, that's just not practical. No, they should stay over there.

                  I mean, Builder is right about the OOC reason. But I think that's a possible IC reason.

                  STing Bronze Age Exalted

                                  I think they would have, if they could have, once all the debate and argument hashed out. I don't really feel they'd have generally considered the Dragon Kings a threat. The First Age Solars I would view as on balance tending more towards arrogance or hubris.

                                  (By which I mean, I wouldn't credit them with on the average taking seriously that they even could be threatened. If anything I'd view them as tending towards being much like a lot like some people in modern Western democracies (people few in number, loud in voice). People supremacistic in their view of their own society and history, and in its capacity to absorb and persuade everyone to their worldview (to them the self evident fruit and pinnacle of rational progress to freedom desired by all humanity, except a few knuckle-draggers among their own society to be showered with contempt). People for whom it just isn't thinkable that any of the folk they are urging to be included and absorbed and shared with could ever disagree with their fundamental ideas of what society should be, could have some sense that they are a distinct people apart with their own leadership and loyalty and who might stand apart from a common idea of society indefinitely.)

                                  In the median high First Age Solar view, I'd imagine, Solar led civilization will conquer all, and all under Heaven can be included, absorbed and led by the natural elite. Why fear resurgent Dragon Kings? They'll obviously get with the Solar program. Speculatively, look at the kind of complacency, in thinking that they couldn't possibly be threatened, with the Usurpation.

                                  So I imagine that it's that the Solars they just couldn't make more souls, any more than they could replace the singular and imperishable behemoths forged in the world's dawn, when those were slain and lost. Magic, at least in 3e, is an art, and the conditions and inspiration cannot be reproduced where they are lacking. Being able to make a thing as grand as a Dragon King soul, and give life to a being as grand as Dragon King, yes, yet unable to control conditions so well they can make that specific things.

                                  (If not that, they were likely busy with other projects.)

                                                  I think part of it is a definitional thing. The Dragon Kings were immortal souls crafted at the behest of the Primordals, and empowered by countless lifetimes of memories. Even if a Solar were to create something indistinguishable from a Dragon King, it wouldn't BE a Dragon King. To understand this, imagine two identical Carcano Model 91/38 carbines. One was used to assassinate president Kennedy in 1963. The other was not. One is a major historical artifact. The other is moderately priced deal at a gun show.

                                                  If the Solars were to create new Dragon Kings, those creations would not be accepted as part of a Dragon king society where friendships and organizations last through series of incarnations. They would be instead perceived as an attempt to replace the deposed rulers of mankind with something which owes its existence to humans. This is just my headcanon, but I think it makes sense.
