                                    First off, Zelbinnean thank you for responding to me. I will do my best to clear up my meaning for your benefit and hopefully explain my meaning for others along the way.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    You seem to be* arguing that because 3e is deliberately not going into great detail about the nature of the Exaltations and their in-setting mechanics (as 2e did, at length), that the setting does not make sense or that there's a plot hole.
                                    Not necessarily. To my mind we don't need an actual word of god "This is what exaltation is and that's how we're running it." Same way we don't need an exact explanation of how her redness went missing. Or how the abyssal exalts were created. Or who the 'enemies of the gods' were that cursed the exalted.

                                    Except in the case of the abyssal exalted, the enemies of the gods, and I think exaltation itself there is an actual answer that the writers have in mind. The scarlet empress scenario is kinda like exalted mad libs. Choose whatever works best for you and go with it.

                                    My problem is that they didn't treat these issues the same way. They're written as if there's a one true answer that they hint the crap out of in the case of the abyssal exalted or make absolutely no assumptions about in the case of the enemies of the gods.

                                    With exaltation they make assumptions about the behavior of the exaltations without effectively (to my mind) hinting at any good one answer. This is the worst when they're talking about past lives or reincarnation. They talk as if it was you in the first age. Not your exaltation, but you. What are new players supposed to interpret that as?

                                    That's why I titled the thread as I did. Because from the text of 3E the most probable explanation is that someone is somehow born a solar but only gets access to that power later on. What else does talk of reincarnation of solars (not the exaltations as separate things but the solars themselves reincarnating) and talking about your past lives where you were a solar suggest? I don't think the authors were trying to come across that way but...

                                    If they were going for actual ambiguity then why didn't they treat it the same way as they did for the "enemies of the gods?" Strip out any reference to who the actual enemies were and just use them as a setting piece.

                                    Here's what I mean: Instead of talking about the usurpation as "Most of the Solars were murdered and their Essences sealed away in a jade prison—for such was their power that they could reincarnate upon death." Talk about how "Such was their power that new solars would otherwise endlessly spring forth. Or something along those lines. It shows that there was a need to lock away the solars without actually implying that the new solars are reincarnations of past solars. You can do this with every reference to past life, solars or exaltation.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    You seem to be arguing that the 3e authors should be presenting the nature of the Exaltations in an objective, factual, unequivocal way, like 2e did, or at the very least giving a set of options for how the Exaltations should be presented.
                                    I don't necessarily think we need a "one true explanation" but I honestly feel that they've written as if there is one true explanation and it comes across like they just forgot to share that with readers. If they really wanted to create pure ambiguity then treat it the same way they treat the 'enemies of the gods.' If they're trying to set it up to be discussed further in another book (IE the exigents), then I feel they should have treated it similar to the abyssals. Make it mysterious and hint at things but don't reveal the mechanism.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    You seem to be saying that 3e may be Doing It Wrong because it's not going into exhaustive detail about the Exaltations, like 2e did.
                                    hmmm. No I don't believe that. I acknowledge I may have come across as thinking that though. I do prefer an explanation to not having one, but it's not a bad thing to have it

                                    Thing is the writers had several topics that they very specifically weren't going to pin down in the core: The empress, the abyssals, the 'enemies of the gods,' the exaltations etc.

                                    The empress they give alternate theories (in character so fallible), the abyssals they hint and foreshadow the crap out of as they always have, the enemies of the gods they reference only slightly, but the exaltations they reference all the time, they don't offer even vague or in character explanations. It's like they just didn't care enough to explain why solars got visions of past lives, or why the sidereals had to lock away these 'solar exaltation' things and didn't think that anyone else would wonder why at all.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    *of course, these are totally based on my perception, and you might read this and say, "I really don't think I'm doing that!" So I'm not trying to say you objectively are doing this, just, from my subjective perspective, this is what is coming across to me and where my responses are coming from.
                                    Which is why I asked you to explain where you were coming from. Hopefully I've cleared up my position somewhat.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    This is maybe where I started thinking you were carrying something forward. I'm not seeing how Exaltation in 3e is written as if things that were 2e are still true. And I'm not sure that a clearly-delineated process of Exaltation is actually needed for the setting to make sense (as was said, it really wasn't explained in 1e, and people didn't give a ton of shit then, which makes it seem* like because 2e went into great detail, that 3e is morally obligated somehow to go into great detail or something, because detail was given at some point and retracting detail going forward is bad for some reason? I'm not sure)
                                    I see that Isator Levi posted what he could find in 1E about exaltations for which I thank him. I've never read 1E, nor have I ever felt I needed to.

                                    I think the contrast between how it's handled in 3E and 1E is very clear to me now.
                                    Exalted are those humans deemed worthy by the greatest of the spirits to taste the sting of power. Since the truth of the nature of the Exalted has never been made known, at least not since the beginning of the current Age, their precise nature can only be speculated upon. Mystics and madmen ramble on about one superstitious theory or another, but most of them are lying, deluded or, perhaps, something in between.
                                    This quote is important.

                                    This single paragraph is the difference between being deliberately ambiguous with a concept and not thinking the concept is worth any explanation whatsoever and thus leaving it ambiguous by omission.

                                    This is exactly the word count that I wanted in a sidebar that you said you felt would be wasted earlier in the thread.

                                    Deliberate ambiguity is fine. Ambiguity by omission is a mistake.

                                    On another note,
