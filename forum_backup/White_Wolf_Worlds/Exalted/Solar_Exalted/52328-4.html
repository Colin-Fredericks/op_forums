            Performance has traditionally been pretty solid, but I always wanted a sort of "Immortal Ballad Triumphant" effect. The idea is that you perform an act (song, dance, oral story, whatever) so well that it becomes a legend in its own right, inextricably tied to you but also with a life of its own, speeding its propagation through the culture and preserving/enhancing whatever core message your seed performances sent. Sort of the "I Have A Dream" speech effect. Traditionally, the closest you could get to this was stunting it, taking a specialty in that specific performance, and maybe fluffing your more general effects as being related to your signature piece, but it'd be nice to have something that gives you substantive bonuses to your signature because it's That Thing You Do, while also ensuring that it has fire-and-forget repercussions on the broader culture.

            I'm imagining something where an incognito Solar pens a song capturing the zeitgeist in an unstable satrapy and performs it to steadily increasing audiences at a teahouse in town, and because it so perfectly expresses the prevailing unrest and she so perfectly performs it, it becomes the anthem of a revolution and if a year later she shows up during a riot and performs it on a rooftop it's showstopping not just because she's an impossibly skilled musician but because she's the person who sings that song and she's singing that song.

                    Originally posted by horvagab View Post

                    One is more direct than the other imho. Also more sure. You can persuade someone that the current system sucks and the regime needs overthrowing. You can also inspire someone that the current system sucks and it needs overthrowing... but they might say that the current system sucks and that is why they'll work to improve it from within (where possible). In this way, inspiration has broader targets/effects, not as scalpel-like as persuasion?
                    You inspire someone to do something that they already want to do, but otherwise do not have the motivation to do it. It is a lowering of inhibition and a raising of ambition.

                    Persuasion is where you convince someone of something that they did not believe prior to your interventions.

                    Ie: "The Big Lebowski is awesome. You should make that stage production of it that you've always been talking about!"
                    "You know, you're right! Hell yes! Let's do this!"

                    Vs

                    "I know that you think "Dirty Dancing" is the best movie ever, but you have failed to consider the merits of "The Big Lebowski."
                    "Oh yea, you're right. The Big Lebowski is the best movie ever."

                                            Though I was able to eventually kinda make it work, I was perpetually kinda disappointment by the Presence / Performance / Linguistics / Craft spread and how they related to art in general.

                                            I'm not sure this design problem can really be fixed. It comes from the binary perception, the idea that something is either lingusitics or it is not... in reality, I find that most art forms are in fact complex blends of many, many skills. Abstracting them into a single talent rating is appealing from a game standpoint (simplicity) but a very poor model. How to fix it? No idea. Sorry I'm not more constructive here but maybe my problems / observations can help somebody else be insightful.

                                            Here is a thought - in the current model, art is a very adversarial thing. It's a weapon you use to convince people of your opinions / positions. That's a very... I dunno, kinda overly 'nerdish' reaction to art? And I say this as both an artist and a girl nerd. The nerd aspects of my personality want to break art down into the cause and effect, A to B, 'I do this for this reason' object sorta 'aspbergers' way of understanding it. And then the artist aspects of my personality are like, 'No, I absolutely and completely do NOT do this for those reasons, or to achieve those goals'.

                                            So why -do- people make art?

                                            What, of value, is created?

                                            Is there some way to abstract this beyond adversarial 'opinion weapons' that would give meaning to an artist beyond making them a social weapon?
