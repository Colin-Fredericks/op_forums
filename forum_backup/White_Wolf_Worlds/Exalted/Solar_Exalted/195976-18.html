        I have to disagree, sorry. The fact that the Solars are substantially stronger and can do things others can't is the #1 thing that makes their return so important. "Legitimacy" is definitely the wrong tack to take, because Exalted very self-consciously undermines the notion of legitimacy, and to make that the one reason the Solars' return matters, you would have to pump it up to a degree that I think would be unhealthy for the game.

        The thing is, Exalted is a game about realpolitik and harsh, ground-level maneuvering. It is not going to radically change the setting if you increase the number of Anathema by 50%, except these Anathema are good-hearted rightful rulers (implicitly casting everyone else as illegitimate and corrupt by default). That isn't worth anything in the setting without something to back it up, and again, to strengthen those themes to the point where it's useful you would have to throw out important, deliberate setting elements like "The Solars' burgeoning corruption brought about the end of the Golden Age" and "The people with the best claim to being 'rightful rulers of the world' are the ones the Exalted murdered before history." Solars have a thematic of rejuvenating, fresh-faced righteousness, and if you're playing a Solar yourself the "reclaiming your rightful throne" thing is likely to be important to you, but these inform their role in the setting, and especially the way they relate to other characters, far less than you seem to be imagining.

        Like, okay, let's say the Solars are just on par with Lunars except with different power themes and a thematic of righteousness. As a Dragon-Blooded, I want them all dead, so this isn't really going to be any different to me than a bunch of new Lunars. If I'm a Sidereal, again this is basically the same situation I've been dealing with all along -- any protestations to the natural righteousness and legitimacy of Solars are going to wash over the people who specifically targeted the Solars for extinction due to their excesses (a move which comes off a lot worse if the Solars weren't stronger). If I'm a Lunar, I probably only care about this on a personal level and have an eye on how it will disrupt politics due to the influx of new players. Certainly I am not going to be that sympathetic to the idea that all these returning Solars are a big deal because they're more righteous and legitimate than I am. Trying to push this angle is actually much worse for non-Solars, I think, because the only way for the setting to make sense is to presume that those characters are intrinsically irrelevant in a post-Solar world, except as people for Solars to take the reins from.

        The context of the game is that the return of the Solars be a setting-defining event that everyone is reacting to, and for non-Solars the game involves figuring out how to exploit or secure yourself against this new upheaval. This cannot work if that's entirely dependent on the Solars legitimacy and pure hearts, because those things are neither especially important nor, crucially, are they measurable in the setting. Literally everybody has a tenable claim to legitimacy and people are generally going to favour whichever claim suits their biases and personal interests. The thing is, yes, there is an undertone and thematic of Solars as righteous, but the game is very explicitly questioning what righteousness and legitimacy are, and acknowledging that people do not tend to agree. People are not going to be able to look at Solars doing stuff, and then look at Lunars doing stuff, and say, "Hmm, these Solars are definitely more virtuous and upstanding". Insofar as it comes up at all in-setting it's more like "It says in this old book here (this book from the Golden Age ruled by Solars!) that Solars are the most righteous and upstanding, and we can perhaps extrapolate that from the properties of their patron." If a Solar rolls into your territory and recruits 5000 able-bodied workers to join her revolutionary army and then fucks off again, that is not worth much, and it's insult to injury if the setting is heavily pushing the notion that the Solar is in the right Because Solar.

                                Solars are not the "leader Exalted". They are, in an abstract historical sense, the leaders of the Exalted, and leadership is one of their themes, but having the one defining Solar property that sets them apart be that they're super inspiring and everyone wants to follow them and do what they say would be straitjacketing. Making this the Solars' defining gimmick -- I mean to the point where it implies meaningful effects on the setting, which is what I'm hearing suggested -- also forces you to make other Exalted worse at it, in fact probably worse than they actually will be in 3e as it stands, so it has a narrowing effect on everybody else as well. For example, currently Lunars are pretty damn good at doing the Second Coming thing, so you'd have to really cut back on that ability in order to make the Solars' comparative edge big enough to singlehandedly justify the setting conceit that the Solars' return is a huge deal. The fact that aesthetically a Solar rabble-rouser tends to evoke liberation theology and divine revelation in a way that other splats don't is cheap coin in Creation.

                                If a Solar swordsman isn't scarier than a Lunar swordsman by virtue of being a Solar, then no Solar whose gimmick is "being a swordsman" is more noteworthy than a Lunar simply by virtue of being an ancient returned king of the world. Probably less so as of RY768, because the Lunar is much more likely to be experienced and powerful. If there's one specific niche that makes the return of the Solars scary, then any Solar without that niche is an also-ran, which has a narrowing effect on what is meant to be the broadest splat in a game full of broad splats. The Revolutionary Prophet is a good meat-and-potatoes Solar concept, but almost none of the Solars I've seen, and none of the ones I've played, looked anything like that.

                                The big question the Solars pose to those with a long view of history is: if left to their own devices, will they return the world to the heights of the First Age, or will they finally turn the world into the hellish place the Sidereals foresaw in the Prophecies of Gold and Darkness? This question requires a context: why was the First Age so wonderful at its heights? Why would the world in which the corrupt Solars were left unchecked be nightmarish and broken? Answering this question with "because the Solars are chosen prophets of the Most High, and people eagerly flock to their banner" does not give satisfying results, because it implies that the defining feature of the First Age was that everyone worked together in mutual love and friendship because of Solar leadership, and the corrupted age would be bad because, I guess, they would all go to war on one another or use their leadership ability to make people do bad things? It just doesn't work as well -- it sucks all the blood out of nations and armies until they feel like abstracted units with an unrest statistic.

                                            I feel as though phrasing it in terms of "Solars are better than you" is still providing an answer to the wrong question, because it misconstrues the meaning of other Exalted being differently powered, namely that they're around to do and be different things.

                                            It's like... look at Bureuacracy. In a system with a good way of representing bureuacracies and how the relevant Ability interfaces with them, with good hooks for Charms about them, a Solar has all kinds of ways to be amazing at running and harnessing a bureaucratic system, ways to make procedure go faster or to tie people up in precedents and regulations, the works.

                                            A Sidereal uses Bureaucracy to do things like destroy an organisation, end an argument, and pose a fairly lethal threat to spirits.

                                            I don't really see these people as competing in the same arena.

                                            The Solar still comes across as more powerful than the Sidereal because they're better at directing bureaucracies to do the kinds of things you'd use them for, but I think they're a bit hard-pressed to have an individual Charm as powerful as End Debate, and can't really use the Bureaucracy Ability to do anything like Terminal Sanction at all. End Debate and Terminal Sanction alone don't make the Sidereal a more powerful bureaucrat than the Solar, and even their place in the overall Bureaucracy cascade don't really help make the Sidereal much good at bureaucracy at all.

                                            But they're fairly good for doing the kinds of things that matter to the Sidereals.

                                            It also doesn't mean they're useless at running bureaucracies altogether; I'd say a big part of making a system that's fun for playing mortals (in which they can present meaningful challenges even for Solars) is that you also implicitly producing a system in which a playing an Exalt can be fun even if you're not a type who'd have Charms for doing that thing directly. A Sidereal with Bureaucracy 5 will still be good at actions and challenges based on that Ability even if they don't have Charms that do other things for it (they may also still have some degree of recourse to target number alteration).

                                            That's not to say that the absence of organisations that, implicitly or explicitly, could only exist if run with the level of expertise or complexity that only a Solar could manage is not meaningful to a Sidereal. Part of the idea of the Age of Sorrows is living in a world where the capacity for such a thing has not been present for a long time, with a meaningful detrimental effect. Part of the narrative premise of a bunch of Sidereals is that they saw that as a worthwhile sacrifice; the game should really try to sell one on the credibility of such a position, I think.

                                            But it's still a matter of how Sidereals respond to a problem that exists outside of themselves; it's still not quite an arena that they're competing for the same space in.

                                            Still, one of the areas that Sidereals are focused on is concerned with how they engage their response; they might not be able to run super-bureuacracies, but they do have certain of the necessary tools to alternately bring about that desired sacrifice of the Solars, or to guide or manipulate them into making such organisations, as suits the Exalt in question.

                                            That doesn't make the Sidereal equally powerful to the Solar, but it doesn't make them useless against them (or alongside them) either. Indeed, it's a dynamic and a tension that is directly informed by the idea of a disparity in their power.

                                            This is part of why it helps to view Exalts holistically.

                                            I think you get much the same deal with Evocations; mastery of powerful weapons isn't really a thing that's part of the Sidereals, even though they're affected by the presence or absence of people who can, and do have their own means of affecting that presence.

Holy fire is not a Good-aligned weapon, it's divine judgment against things deemed Unclean to Creation - a definition which, in some cases, may be rooted in ideas of "that thing is icky and/or unnatural, so probably bad" rather than them being Evil.                                                             Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                                                            When this book says "the Lunar Exalted were the most powerful emissaries of the Exalted Court"
                                                            Sometimes books just say dumb things. It's easy to get caught up in your subject matter and say something that doesn't make a lot of sense when pulled out of context.

                                                            (I mean, not to pick a fight over that one line, but if you're going to hang your hat on that you're going to be disappointed. That quote is not and was never intended to be literally true, and if an author said otherwise they screwed up.)

                                                            "the Sidereal Exalted were far more talented at astrology and learning than any others" (comparing the Twilight Caste to the Sidereals), then this edition meant it.
                                                            Astrology yes, learning no, and again, if you're going to focus on statements like that you're going to be disappointed.

                                                            Obviously the First age was more than "the Solars did everything forever on their own, the end" and the story of the Second age is not "the Solars arrive and change everything personally with no interaction with anyone else, the end."

                                                            But as a general rule of thumb (keeping in mind that generalizations always miss something, of course), the mightiest emissaries of the Exalted were probably the Eclipse caste, and the Twilight caste are at least as good at scholarly learning as anyone else (it's a broad and ill-defined topic, so I'd want to hedge a bit; but saying that the Sidereals were "far more talented" is flat-out wrong); this is central to both their definitions. And those things are not even really central to the definitions of the Lunars or Sidereals -- the Lunars as powerful emissaries thing seems completely out-of-the-blue, to the extent that it would be just as true with the name of almost any other splat inserted. Sidereals, of course, are wise, and more knowledgeable in certain matters than anyone else -- even putting aside their obvious advantages in the second age -- but they're probably not eg. the very best magical or theoretical researchers outside of their specific fields.

                                                            But again, I see statements like the ones you quoted as fundamentally unhealthy for the line -- those are bad quotes. You shouldn't say "X is the very absolute best at Y, far more talented than anyone else" unless it is absolutely essential to what they are. Even then, there's almost nothing where I would include the 'far more' qualifier, for anyone. That's basically saying "nobody else can meaningfully touch this, fullstop." For astrology, maybe, but for learning? Don't be ridiculous. That's an author getting too caught up in excitedly expressing how wise the Sidereals are without considering the implications of their statement, or how learning is at least as important a concept for others.

                                                            Even for Solars the books are usually pretty conservative about using lines like that. (Mostly it comes up with regard to crafting and gestalt prowess, which are both essential to the setting's history and therefore hard to hedge about.) But taking that caution and using it to say "aha, it doesn't specifically say Twilights are the most learned Exalted! Here is a place where Sidereals can be better!" is missing the point. You do that and first, the concept of gestalt Solar power and Solars as a whole as (overall) leaders in their fields will suffer a death of a thousand cuts as everyone gets defined as better than them at some core Solar thing, turning them into the jack-of-all-trades, master-of-none Exalted; and second, you'll end up cutting into more than just Solars, as each type of Exalted has to carefully write around the carelessly-broad "nobody can come close to X at Y!" statements made by everyone else.

                                                            (Or, more likely, you'll end up with those statements becoming an empty joke as writers just ignore them -- certainly, while they have a number of unique and powerful effects to reflect their theme of supernatural wisdom, Sidereals do not have the "best at learning overall" reflected in their charms, and Lunars are neither unusually powerful nor particularly talented emissaries compared to other Exalted in either edition. This is because, thankfully, other writers wrote their subjects in whatever way was best for them and the overarching definition of the setting, not constantly worrying about how some writer carelessly wrote a line saying that Lunars NUMBAH ONE at emissary-ing or that whatever you have doesn't even compare to Sidereals at being learned.)

                                                            It's a cheap and thoughtless way to make your subject look awesome at the expense of everyone else. That thinking was how we ended up with a list of "greatest martial artists in Creation" longer than your arm back in 2e.
