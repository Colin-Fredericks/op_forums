                        Yeah, the Fire Aspect anima is the only one that leaves an actual elemental effect in its wake (stuff burning). You would think, for example, that if a Water Aspect unleashed their anima, at the end of the scene anything affected by it would be soaked, covered in water, etc, but that isn't the case.

                        My group finds that a bit boring, so we allow for actual elemental effects when someone uses their anima. So a Water Aspect's anima might leave things soaked afterwards, or an Air Aspects might leave things crackling with static electricity or ice cold, an Earth aspect might leave dust or sand covering everything, etc. It's a fairly minor change but it's fairly cool from a visual standpoint.

                                ...yeah, when I run games, Terrestrial anima banners are real elemental effects. When you get enough water essence someplace, then you get water, because that's what water is.

                                Fire aspects in my games need to worry about burning down the village. This is, I think, intended by the game: Hesieh is the Immaculate Dragon who teaches restraint and patience and the "don't burn down your house to remove a leech" sort of wisdom, and it's noted as a particularly important sort of lesson for Fire Aspects to have. The ability to start a fire that spreads is an ability shared by peasants with torches, I don't feel like giving it to Exalted warrior-princes is unbalancing in any real way.

                                Water aspects in my game can conjure water (or, in one memorable case, shards of swirling ice-blades) from nothing to nourish a desert caravan on a long journey, and that water is real water, and nobody drinks it and then hasn't been hydrated because the essence dissipates. It's a handy trick, if the other people in your caravan are desperate enough to brave your tempestuous anima and risk life and limb for a drink.

                                Earth aspects in my game can create rocks. If someone wants an anima like Mnemon's and talks to me about it and spends the points on Resources to make it make sense, I'll let them say their anima causes crystals or sharp-edged gemstones in a poetic color that matches the aesthetic they're going for erupt from the ground and orbit them in pretty glittering blades, and I'll let them keep their diamonds afterwards. The Terrestrial Exalted are divinely empowered nobility, I'm okay with them conjuring precious stones or metals for free (though no Magical Materials or gold or silver allowed-there's a reason the Guild uses the silver standard and the Realm uses the Jade one, and I try to play realistically with supply and demand and market forces in my games).

                                Wood aspects can similarly create rare and deadly toxins, or plants with useful herbal properties. They can't change their anima in play without serious spiritual growth and change and a character arc to go with it, but if your anima is grape vines erupting from the ground and lashing the people around you with spiny whips...well, I'm not averse to letting you eat some grapes or make some wine. Same goes for deadly nightshade or medicinal herbs: I'm gonna want you to specify what herb it is and what it's medicinal effects are. It'll be handy in certain situations, but not always.

                                Air aspects can do the ice-in-the-desert trick with frozen winds, or they can do lightning effects. While they get less "it hangs around after I'm gone" tricks than the other four, their animas in my game are nonetheless real. An Air Aspect who goes into an undersea cavern can go totemic and fill it with breathable air, for instance, in order to create an undersea supervillain lair.

                                I like these sort of powers because they encourage players to play cleverly, and they make the animas of the Terrestrial exalted feel like a very grounded, earthy part of the world. The anima displays of the Anathema are just big shiny lights, but the anima banners of the Exalted are the very flesh and blood of Creation reaching out to serve the Dragons' Chosen. The Dragonblooded should feel like part of the world whose power they are channeling, and making their animas real makes them feel more real...and opens up so many more stories! Perhaps some pirates are holding a teenaged Earth aspect prisoner and forcing her to produce rubies for them! Perhaps the Air Aspected craftsman has created a giant cannon fueled by the lightnings of his own anima so that nobody else can operate it! Perhaps there's an outcaste Water aspect who has founded an oasis-citadel in the South and has set herself up as a goddess worshipped by the locals! Perhaps the monk who is hunting you with the Wyld Hunt is afraid to actually fight you with his full strength because his anima has burned the innocent before, and rants furiously at you for using these innocent villagers as human shields!

                                There's good stories here!

                                If it's just a light show? Not so much.

                                So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                                    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                                    And Medicine.

                                                    I'd say it isn't at will, it requires spending motes. I'm not sure that generating your own Resources income solely from your wood or earth anima is the best idea, though.
                                                    Well...yes. It's not at will. Making your anima flare requires spending motes. That's how animas work.

                                                    And seriously, using your anima to make potable water's the least problematic thing on that list. Earth Aspect's the most broken (the one I'd make you actually pay resources for if your anima generates salable goods, though I'd do the same for Wood if your anima was a rare plant or poison you planned on producing in any real quantity). But minor bonuses to Survival rolls? Really? If you're in a blizzard in the North, huddling around the Fire Aspect's warmth and waiting through it is a fun scene! Using your anima to generate water in a desert, or air when you're stuck in a prison cell in a sinking slave galleon, or some spinach to munch during a famine, or a rich swirl of loamy earth in a rocky wasteland you can use to plant crops? That's awesome!

                                                    Now, to be clear, not every terrestrial can do all of these tricks! You pick your anima at chargen! If you're an Earth Aspect and you decide your anima banner is a swirl of stinging razor-edged black volcanic glass, that's really neat! I'll let you gather up piles of black volcanic glass and melt 'em down in a forge to make cool obsidian glass weapons, it might be a useful component in making an artifact or something...but you're not going to be able to sell 'em to the Guild in any huge quantity. If your anima is jagged marble spires erupting from the earth to impale your opponents, I'm fine with the spires sticking around afterwards (seems like you could do some neat landscaping with that anima, maybe make a kickass garden), and maybe you could carve them into statues or sell the blocks of marble, but you're not feeding people in a desert. If your anima is a tear-shaped blades made out of rubies slashing through the air around you, you can sell the rubies (and I'm going to make you buy dots in Resources at chargen), but I'm not gonna let you stunt your anima making marble spires to stand on to get across the Lake of Boiling Lava: your anima is rubies, not stone spires.

                                                    I'm not only fine with the idea of dragonbloods each having one really neat trick, I love the idea: it encourages them to get creative and to try and find situations where they can use that neat trick to their advantage. The Fire Aspect whose anima is a cloud of roiling black smoke illuminated by burning embers is probably going to fight differently than the Fire Aspect whose anima is a shimmering haze of ridiculous heat that ignites anything it comes into contact with! The first guy's probably going to do great at being a sneaky ninja or a beekeeper! The second guy is gonna be good at wrestling or flying through the frozen northern skies in a self-propelled hot air balloon with a metal basket! Both of 'em have to worry about lighting places they're fighting in on fire, but they're going to be inclined to approach their lives in different ways! And I think that's awesome.
