                To some extent, a character can choose to invest XP where they wish. They might not be aware of the out-of-game mechanics, but I think most PCs grok 'If I want to get better at X, I should find a way to practice or study X.'

                I assume that Dynasts know that you can strengthen your body through by increasing the flow of your chi in your muscles or something. Whether through yoga, martial arts,breathing exercises, or meditating on the element of earth. In the real world, you can learn to be resistant enough to take a hit or walk across burning coals. In Exalted, you can learn to be resistant enough to take a sword hit or walk across burning lava.

                Neither of these happen by accident though. They're both the product of someone going 'I want to be able to X, so I shall do Y.'

                Of course, there are charms you learn whether you want to or not and times when you want to learn things but can't. If a terrestrial exalted who loves music and the arts is imprisoned to do hard labor underground, hes probably going to 'learn' charms that help with hard labor, keeping healthy while eating little food, and navigating in poorly lit areas. Likewise, if he's spending 14 hours a day mining for jade with his bare hands, his desire to get better at studying or preparing delicious meals will probably not be satisfied.

                                                Originally posted by Crumplepunch View Post

                                                Well, in his backstory, Negezzar initiated into Celestial Martial Arts and learned the pre-form Wood Dragon Charms in an attempt to heal his leg.

                                                I suppose you might make an exception for MA Charms and other things that require tutelage, like Sorcery. In any case, I disagree with your interpretation.

                                                I understand that the 1e idea of learning Charms from manuals is being downplayed, but I find the other extreme of characters not being able to choose how to harness their own Essence to be thoroughly silly.
                                                I think they're overstating something that's a bit more like this:

                                                You can't choose what you're good at. If you really want to and are really dedicated, you can perhaps potentially learn some of it through painful dedication, but it won't be easy and not everyone is up to that sort of thing.

                                                If you're a swordsman, though, you can totally choose to focus on defense or offense; if you're a doctor, you can choose to focus on learning how to cure certain diseases vs learning how to treat injuries. The point is that Negezzar can't simply choose to be one of the greatest Terrestrial doctors just because it would be useful to him.

                                                (A PC in that situation could tell the story about how their character, through painful dedication, manages to become a master of medicine anyway, buying all the charms at an unfavored cost etc. But it's not something most people are realistically capable of. It's assumed that PCs can write their character's story to an extent, essentially, especially when it comes to personality, so if you insist that your character can find some hidden reserve of will and learn Medicine despite having no talent -- and are willing to spend unfavored costs and endure long training times -- then they can; but the barrier is meant to be higher, thematically, than just spending a few more XP. The only reason it's relatively low mechanically is because it's better for the game to give you the option to tell stories like that if you want to.)
