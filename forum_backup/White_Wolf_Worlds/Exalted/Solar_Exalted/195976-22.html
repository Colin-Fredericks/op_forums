    Instead of going round and round rehashing the same arguments over and over again and theorycrafting, why don't we try and give examples from real campaigns.

    Now, we have played several mixed campaigns with a majority of the PC's being solars.

    I played a social Chosen of Serenity in a party with a social Solar. Vastly different play-experience from the solar. The Serenity was a whole lot better at behind the scenes manipulation than the Solar could ever be. She could build up or tear down support without ever being known while the Solar always needed to be seen. It was also much, much easier on the xp to be competent in important area's (information gathering, infiltration, combat) while not losing your social focus. Oh, and having access to Yu-Shan and it's recources is also a major gamechanger. I certainly did not feel underpowered.

    I had a similar experience with a Pretty Kitty social Lunar. Again, in raw mass-influencing social mojo, the Solar has the edge, no question. But in smaller scale situations, and especially if subtlety and deniability were even remotely required, send in the Lunar. Also, similar to the sidereal, it was much cheaper to be competent in other area's as well as the social arena. I was having a different experience, which I felt I could not have with a Solar. I was not weaker than the Solars and the Sidereal.

    Dragon Blooded, while definately not having the same powerlevel as the Celestials, especially in combat, bring plenty of other, unique things to the table that the Anathema by their very nature cannot. I certainly had fun playing one in a Solar party, though yes, you will feel weaker if some niche-protection is not observed. But even then, the fact that you're simply not an anathema, though not as easily quantifiable as the strenth of your charm-set or shapeshifting, is a pretty big advantage.

    In our current party we have a Full Moon Lunar combat wombat. He has other strengths than my Dawn Invincible Sword Princess. He is more invested in protecting others, taking the blows and regerating and slowly grinding oponents down than the quick win. I have the edge in a straight one on one fight against him (not a guaranteed win though in 2.5), but hey, he is built to be the big guy/bear protecting everybody else and he is certainly better at that then my character (or any solar can be, really).

    We also briefly had an Alchemical at the table. If he went for his specialised load-outs he could compete with the Solars in combat or in the sneaky stealthy scout department. It definately looked like a different play-experience, but I did not get see much of it, so can't fully say how they compare or where their strenths really lie in comparison to Solars.

    TL/DR: Play to your splat's strengths and your character will not be weaker than your friend's solar (if you use the same xp & advancement tables). You are probably much broader in capabilties, thanks to shapeshifting/fate stuff/arrays for the same xp. Solars really can't do everything at once in actual play. Even then, Solar's are not very subtle in most areas, being golden, shiny leadertypes, while Lunars and Sidereals certainly can be. Play to the splat's strengths and you'll be just fine.

                Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                I never got why Siddies were supposed to be "the sorcerer" because they don't even get the top rung. Sorcery has always been the solars' thing.
                I've seen a several different people use this argument. Here's the thing though, Sids are the sorerous archtype because they don't actually need Sorcery (at least in terms of Exalted's mechanics) to get spell-like effects.

                The confusion is that the kind of magic Exalted calls Sorcery is different from the magic that the archtypical sorcerer uses. In D&D, the big difference between a sorcerer and a wizard is that wizards learn spells whereas sorcerers are born with them and use them as innate magic. In the same way, Sidereal charms/astrology can summon elementals, banish demons, magically heal, and create magical disguises. Sidereals can't use exalted's sorcery to summon a 3rd Cicle demon, but they could make a go at it with their charms. They could use sorcery to caste a giant flaming bird at someone, but it would be cheaper to load a lit candle into their powerbow and shoot that. So no, Sids aren't as good at using Exalted's version of sorcery as Solars. Compared to other splats, though, this shortcoming affects them the least since their native charms can, in part, bridge the gap.

                    Going back to 1E Lunars and Abyssals, I think part of the problem was that nearly all their powers were "like Solars, but a little bit worse". It wasn't until Sidereals & Alchemicals that the rules actually backed up the idea of "good in a different way", rather than "sub-Solar". If you were a Lunar, you were strong-armed into taking Deadly Beastman Transformation, because it was the only thing you could do better than a Solar (including shapeshifting into other people!)

                    And then the 2E Manuals did the same thing again by botching Sidereals and Abyssals. I'd wager one of the reasons Infernals was so popular was that again, it did "You're as good as a Solar, in a novel and interesting way." rather than "You're worse than a Solar, deal.".

                                However sitting down and writing Solar magic to be able to mean that they are the best at every trick isn't necessary either really.
                                I'm not saying they should be the best at every trick. I'm saying they should be the best at every field of human endeavor. That doesn't mean you have to literally read through every Charm of every other Exalt type and add one to all of the numerical effects - again, no one thinks Solars should have Even More Efficient Secretary as a Charm. But it does mean that you run into problems when you want to parse, say, learning, and conclude, "Oh, these other Exalts will always be faster," or whatever. "Can read fast" is a pretty straightforward application of being the Bestest Scholar Ever; that makes it an unlikely nail to hang someone else's hat on.

                                Now, if you want, say, a Fire-Aspect or Abyssal Charm where you burn down a library and absorb the knowledge as the books go up in smoke - well, okay! Solars aren't going to have that, because that's not really about learning, it's about your connection to elemental fire or to destruction. (One might quibble whether this is an appropriate Charm for either of those splats, but that's not really my major point.) Perhaps that's a means by which you could end up with the effect of absorbing information faster, but it's not because they're the Learning Exalts more than Solars are.

                                    Originally posted by Coik View Post
                                    Jay...I find the concept of UNIVERSAL ARCHETYPES to be the sole and exclusive dominion of Solars to be...problematic, let's say.                                     So, for instance, "The Vampire" is not a classic heroic archetype - and it's an archetype that Abyssals can/should do better than Solars. Again, each of these subdivides, and I'm not providing an all-encompassing list - but the point is that these are different archetypes, and ones where the other splats tell the story better (and in a different way!) than Solars can.

                                    These are also narrower archetypes, yes. And they're primarily means- rather than ends-focused, yes - they do not outperform Solars, in general, at accomplishing tasks of raw power. That's why the Solars are the default player splat.
