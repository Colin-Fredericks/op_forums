        The danger is not that a particular class is unfit to govern. Every class is unfit to govern.

                            Righteous is as ambiguous a term as "good" in my view. The term itself more or less means "Does actions which are right". Quick look at the Goolge definition, it is even outright said as, "(of a person or conduct) morally right or justifiable; virtuous". No one gets to decide this in Creation. Even when the Unconqured Sun decides it, it's his view of it. We seem to give weight to him on that choice, rather than just assume his choice is arbitrary and he gets to press it because he's king.

                            Virtueousness is also a poor criteria for selecting Solar Exalted. Virtue is not an inherent good as the Virtues lead to contradictory and at their extremes, not especially liked behavior. Compassion makes you unable to commit harm against some who might need it, or do harm to those who you mean to help based on your biases. Conviction makes you monofocused monster. Temperance makes you detached from the world for order. Valor makes you a gallavanting asshole. Virtueousness is not goodness either then.

                            Greatness is something that, by contrast, has less ambiguity. The Sun seems not so much caring with what sort of person you are with power. It's that you are someone who can be great wtiht hat power. Great doesn't mean you aren't going to always be good.

                            Note in all of this it also sort of ignores the fact that great as applied to other Chosen. Luna still Exalts people who can be great, simply in her view of greatness (which is monstrosity to some extent). Sidereals are chosen form great potential destiinies. We'll likely see other bits about. But all Exalted are great. They are all heroes in the classical sense.

                            Hippokreme's defintiion of heroic as pertains to Solars ignores other Exalts in its definition and in a way takes that heroism from other Exalted. Exalted's main premise of all sorts, the tagline lost in 2e has been "What legends will they tell of your deeds?" Not how you'll save the world. How they'll make it a better place. But what you will do to become history. Solar Exalted are Chosen not for their goodness or heroicness. They're chosen because they are exemplars of skill and people able to use power as the Sun might think works out. THey might fail in actually implementing that power in a way the Sun wanted, but that's on them entirely. And if legends are told of that failure, than the game works out. If you are remembered as Cao Cao, Vlad Tepes or Hassan-i Sabbah, you left your mark just as much as anyone else. Whether or not it was for what you thought was right for the world, or right for you to have more control of the world.

                                                Originally posted by Blackwell View Post
                                                At what point did I say "righteousness" was completely objective? Why this insistence that these words (good, hero, righteous) can't be somewhat subjective without being meaningless? And why can't the Sun's personal opinion of what is right inform what sort of person his power Exalts? Why can't Exaltation take more into consideration than a count of your Ability dots? And while I'm at it, why exactly can't this mystical, metaphysical process be a little ambiguous?
                                                Righteousness is as arbitrary a term in this case as goodness to me. It says someone has moral weight. The very term itself means "what's morally right" in a way. It's still in Creation simply an opinion, sure, but it creates a sort of moral imperative on the Exaltation (you were Chosen for your righteousness) which implies you have some moral authority, as it also assumes the Sun has moral authority.

                                                Or you could just say the Sun wants greatness. Which seems to me something folks can generally agree on more: bigness, ability to stand out, things like that. Which would fit the Solar magic thematics as well (which are about making you a great swordsman, businessman, assassin, survivor, whatever).

                                                For righteousness to be a criteria of Exaltation, it would result in righteousness (or goodness or whatever), things which we tend to put moral weight to, meaning shit. Because the Exalt himself is not impelled in any fashion to actually act righteous, good or whatever hwen they Exalt. It means the Sun's views are naive, since the person himself might not live up to his views. And it creates this idea that the person getting the Exaltation was morally more upright htan someone who got another Exaltation or didn't get one at all, because you're obviously more righteous than other candidates due to you know, haivng the Exaltation.

                                                I guess in moral argument form, look at it like this:

                                                P1: Solar Exalted are chosen from righteous people (whatever that is).
                                                P2: Solar Exalted therefore are righteous people.
                                                P3: Other Exaltations or sources of power are not necessarily chosen from righteous people.
                                                P4: From P3, this means there can be sources of power with no righteous people.
                                                P5: Righteousness by definition means moral weight.
                                                P6: Because Solars have a minimum of all being righteous, while other sources of power have a possibilty of no righteous people, there is an assumption that more Solars are righteous than other sources of power.
                                                C: Because of P5 and P6, Solar Exalted have more moral weight than other sources of power.

                                                Replace righteous with good. Or hippokreme's definition of heroic (which is good but co-opting other stuff). It leads to the same weight. Solars are either more good, more moral, or more whatever, inherently, than other Exalted. Something the game works to avoid. And something that isn't even what Solars are really meant to be more of.

                                                Solars are meant to be more powerful than other Exalted. They are chosen by paragons of skill. Solars are chosen about what they can do, not how morally upright they are. Solars are chosen because they'll make good Solars. It's somewhat tautological, but putting qualitative statements to things which often have moral or societal preferences involved is a route of folly and more or less a rejection of the game's premises.
