                            06-10-2016, 10:23 PM
                            Originally posted by Jen View Post
                            Bonestrider, Necromancy, Dragon of a different colors, Necrotech. Are there a reasons why Abyssal has so much "Our own toy" compare to other splat ? Is it because they are goth and thus don't use mainstream things ?
                            A chapter on the special necrotech artifacts of the underworld that get their own special mechanics is an easier back-of-the-book promotional sales point than a chapter on more artifacts, except they're underworld-y. Same with helltech, hence an entire chapter in Infernals filled with the rules for hellforged wonders instead of e.g. an entire chapter filled with artifacts that happen to be hell-y.

                            I don't think we're doing that this edition. We don't need artifacts, and then also necrotech artifacts that use a different set of mechanics from artifacts, and then also hellforged wonders/helltech that uses a different set of mechanics from artifacts and necrotech artifacts. Likewise, the number of warstriders in the setting is being vastly reduced and the subcategorization of warstriders into scout/[whatever the other two categories are, I can't remember] is going away, so why would we keep an entirely separate category of warstrider-like-things that behave like warstriders but aren't called warstriders because they're all death-flavored?

                            Bonestriders are almost certainly going away for a number of reasons that fit together like Lego pieces:

                            1) The number of warstrider-like-things in the setting is being reduced; bonestriders themselves were always sold as "cheaper, mass-produced-by-necrotech Underworld warstrider equivalents," but since we're removing mass-produced artifact-type-stuff from the setting as a rule, the inclusion of bonestriders would seem not to be in line with that.

                            2) The profusion of superfluous types of artifacts with their own custom creation and maintenance mechanics is also being pared down, because they never served a strong purpose other than to sell people books on the promise of, not just new toys, but entirely new categories of toys.

                            You can still have a giant robot made of skulls and metal forged from screaming souls, obviously. It's just be "a warstrider."

                                                            Originally posted by Caffeine Delusions View Post

                                                            Because Abyssals get their own special 'forged from ghosts in a horrific torturous process' versions of Daiklaves instead.
                                                            Again, many Abyssal artifacts probably are made out of bones and soulsteel and sinew and such, just like they were in First Edition, years before the term "necrotech" was ever introduced into the gameline. Back then, if an artifact was made out of bones and screaming ghosts and looked like something off the cover of an Iron Maiden album, it was called an "artifact." That's how it will work in 3rd edition.

                                                            Abyssals (and Infernals) will still have their same cool niche for artifacts made out of left-over body parts and demons, it's just that the process for making those sorts of artifacts won't be getting special snowflake treatment, being called something different and given their own special subsystem for making them.

                                                            What this means is that, yes in Third Edition if a Solar or a Dragon Blooded wanted to make a suit of armor out of screaming souls and bone and sinew, they can. But they could always do this in First Edition and they could do this in Second Edition. The only difference was that they needed to use a totally different subsystem for making such a device than for making the (mechanically) exact same artifact out of different materials.
