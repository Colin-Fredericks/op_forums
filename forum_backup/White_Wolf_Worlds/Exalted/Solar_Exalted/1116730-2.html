                            My players tend toward the "weapon exalts with you" style. The Dawn caste, as a mortal, had a sword that was daiklave sized because he thought it would add to his warrior reputation*. When he Exalted, the blade became part of his legend and incrementally grew in power with him. It became a Major Artifact in our 3ed game (we accidentally unmade the world in our 2.5 game, 3rd is a Universal reboot). The Zenith used his warhammer to kill a powerful shadowlands beast and his weapon became an Artifact because of his intense zeal and puritanical hatred**. The Night found hers in a lost jungle temple. The Twilight and Eclipse didn't use an artifact at start.

                            *Like Guts, he trained with a longsword as a child and kept "trading up" in size as he grew. Even though he could barely wield the thing with any control, it was enough to make him a good caravan guard.

                            **He had his reinforced with Oricalcum after. Actually, they both did, as raw Oricalcum was a quest reward.

                            Personally, when I design a character I never design them as a level 1 scrub, I design them as being good at what their schtick is and compromise as needed with the rule system of the game. The fact that I can start Exalted with my Legendary Weapon and Supernal Skill is a massive draw for me. This isn't DnD with it's ragtag rat-hunters starting quests, you are Living Legends, andhalf of our mythic heroes are inextricably linked with their legendary weapons.

                                Originally posted by Coik View Post
                                Artifacts, particularly weapons, are weird because the game wants them to be fabled weapons of legend all, with storied histories and pedigrees similar to champion racehorses, while simultaneously wanting every Solar to be able to trip over a half dozen of them within seconds of Exalting so they can pick out the exact one that’s absolutely perfect for them, personally.
                                Since, fluff-wise, your evocations from a daiklaive are going to be different from my evocations with the same daiklaive (just the same general theme), then I think it's more that whatever daiklaive they pick up is appropriate for them, rather than they find the appropriate daiklaive.

                                Unless you're talking about them getting the panoply of their previous incarnation (which, as much as it is a stereotype, I haven't actually seen very much at all in Solar games... or maybe not at all? I don't remember any examples from games I've played/run), in which case the answer is Sidereals.

                                You see, Gold-Faction Sidereals are always trying to help Solars meet their destiny (and helping people meet their destiny is what Sidereals do).
                                Sidereals guide luck and coincidence so 4-6 disparate Solars happen to be in the same town when a massive army of Raksha turn up to duel any local heroes for the souls of the townsfolk, and these 4-6 disparate Solars happen to be the reincarnations of Solars who knew each other in a previous life. And said Sidereals will also help guide them to run into Abyssal villains who were their friends/rivals in a previous life.
                                And the Sidereals guide them to coincidentally find an ancient tomb of their previous incarnation where they find the destined daiklaive and armour of their previous life.

                                In my game, there was a couple of years where every 4-5 months the party's Full Moon would leave and be replaced by another Full Moon even more optimised for combat than the previous one, but with surprisingly similar personalities and reactions to things.
                                Presumably this is because the Sidereals screwed up and created a fate loop.

                                            Heh, I had an image of somebody decrying the supposed convenience of their being a tomb containing at least one Artifact available for every Solar, when, y'know, the whole reincarnation thing means that every single living Solar (and the ones of ambiguous living status) is going to have at least one tomb with the panoply of one previous life in the world.

                                            ​Y'know, [I]maaaybe[I] a daiklave that you got in a tomb is well-suited to you because it belonged to you in five previous lives.

                                            ​The complaint of almost everybody seeming to have an Artifact contradicting the perception of Artifacts as uncommon seems to be assuming that the main characters of the game are regular people rather than members of a highly exclusive category. It would be something else if you were just playing Conan, where he's just this guy who happened to stumble upon a tomb containing some Atlantean steel, and then kept encountering these other regular people who just happened to have the same experience. The whole being Chosen by God, and these being divine weapons made to be wielded by those Chosen by God kind offff standardises the variables a bit. Also adds a certain aspect of mysticism.

                                            ​Like, the Exalted can experience visions irrespective of past life memories and the workings of Fate, and an uncanny intuition for the location of the final resting place of a previous life might be amongst those, especially if it's housing an ancient mystical weapon that might be really enthusiastic about coming back into your hands that might be very subtly calling out to you.

                                            ​Hehehe, how's that for convenience? "Oh, I just happened to stumble upon the tomb of a past life. Right? That's how brains work, right? You know, 'free will' and all that."

                                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                ​The complaint of almost everybody seeming to have an Artifact contradicting the perception of Artifacts as uncommon seems to be assuming that the main characters of the game are regular people rather than members of a highly exclusive category. It would be something else if you were just playing Conan, where he's just this guy who happened to stumble upon a tomb containing some Atlantean steel, and then kept encountering these other regular people who just happened to have the same experience. The whole being Chosen by God, and these being divine weapons made to be wielded by those Chosen by God kind offff standardises the variables a bit. Also adds a certain aspect of mysticism.
                                                This is pretty big, yeah. A lot of people still seem to look at Exalted characters as if they're 1st level D&D adventurers made with the baseline stat arrays or 3d6 in order -- random bumpkins with no connection to the world, no history to them, and no particular significance beyond "is played by a player.". Just by being an Exalt you're part of an incredibly exclusive category of people, and being a Solar puts you in an even narrower one. Additionally, while anyone can potentially Exalt, in the roughly a year following Exaltation it's assumed you're going to have done stuff, practiced, trained, adventured, etc. That's why you have a 8/6/4, 25 ability points, and 15 bonus points statline, rather than something closer to the Militia statline, whose best rolls are 4-5 dice; if you were a mere conscript or brigand at exaltation you've probably done a lot of stuff and rapidly mastered your newfound talents in the last year.

                                                Obviously if the game starts with "and suddenly you're goldshiny and have no idea why", then yeah, you might need to adjust and set artifacts as "Things I hope to find" rather than "things I start gameplay with", but, "suddenly you're goldshiny and have no idea why" is definitely not standard assumption.
