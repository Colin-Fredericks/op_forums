So far I have yet to see presented a view of good or a word that's good-but-not that isn't in the end, "I don't want this bad guy I don't like to count as a Solar." My response is that the moment you put such a qualifier in things, things go stupid fast. It's an unsustainable premise.
        I would classify it as 'someone who does great things', but that's just me, let's move on.

        I'm talking about a general feel or idea for what a Solar is, not what ABSOLUTELY MUST BE OR FIRE RAINS FROM THE HEAVENS AND EATS YOUR GOLDFISH. My view isn't 'a solar must be good or he's not a solar', it's 'solars seem like good people because enough of them ARE good people'.

        Hence, 'righteous'. Usually seen as good, but plenty are just blood templars.

        The only thing I generally am for as a quality for Solars is greatness. And as I have defined it (besides more or less "goodness or arbitrary mroalness") it is more on scope. Greatness isn't a quality of character, but a scale of act. And Solar Exalted are capable and gifted to be great people. People who do big things.
        The two are not mutually exclusive, and I would argue that one can flow from the other. People with great characters do great things, and people who can do great things become great people in the process. Especially in a world where you are so monumentally powerful that the only challenges for you are things big and bad enough to threaten all of existence.

        In the context of Creation, the idea of some thing outside of what a character subjectively judges as right or wrong is fanon. The game is premised greatly on an idea that morality is not something that exists out in the world but what judges things is instead something that sapient entities in Creation do. Any attempt to say that an Exaltation should be that is fanon. Any attempt to say that the books should highlight folks who aren't some pre-conceived view of "not good enough to be a Solar" is trying ot elminate the subtext of the game by making them weird and aberrant, instead of the logical outgrowth of great people with power and no real check on things that the subtext was supposed to emphasize.

                                He's not saying that you play the game wrong if you say "Solars are good people", or that the game shouldn't allow for such deviations. He's saying that our conversation was started by hippokrene saying "The default way to play the game should be A" and that we're arguing about just that.

                                Saying, "No, B should be the default game" isn't saying you can't play the game as A. Saying B should be the default is not saying A is not a good way to play the game around your table, it's not saying the book shouldn't have a sidebar or a page highligthing A, C, D, E other ways the game can be played.

                                It's just that starting by giving out an opinion on what the default should be, and then saying "but you can play your game at home this way, how dare you accuse me of badwrongfun!" when people don't seem to agree is not finding common ground, it's moving the goalposts. And that's disingenuous.

                                Everybody can play the game the way they want it to be. That has always been true, and nobody said the opposite (not even the writers). It's just that we're talking about THE game, not YOUR game, and both are very different things. In order to get away from what Lea (or was it Holden?) called high-attraction plots, THE game needs to have strong thematic decisions, even if no-one is forever bound by law to agree with those.

                                (It's just like the Yozi not escaping thing. THE game is saying "The Yozis will never escape" because the devs know that some group will play that anyway, but they don't want all groups to play that scenario, and if you leave it open, a whole fucking lot of people will, to the point that not having the Reclamation in your game will become badwrongfun. You still can have the Reclamation in YOUR game. Same for Solars as Good People. A lot of groups will play that way anyway, but if you make it the canon, it will become inescapable, because it's just an easier narrative and a more simplistic thematic.)
