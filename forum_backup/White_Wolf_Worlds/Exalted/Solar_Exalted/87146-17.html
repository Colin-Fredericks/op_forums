            Second: you make a fallacious leap of logic that assumes that the converse of a statement is true, which an Intro to Logic level mistake. Specifically, you take the statement "most bad guys are Lunars" and turn it into "most Lunars are bad guys". I really don't like how hostile it makes me sound to point this out, but you can't do that if you're trying to make a logically consistent argument.

            Third: you arbitrarily limit your analysis to Exalts. Aren't there bad guys who are gods, and heroic mortals, and raksha, etc. etc.?

            Fourth: you seem to predicate your conclusion on the notion that good can only come into conflict with bad, and vice versa. Exalted is a setting which offers a lot of moral complexity, and a good Solar (who is good because he wants to end slavery) can fight against a good Sidereal (who is good because he wants to ensure the insanity of the Solars never brings the world into peril again) without the "good" of either being diminished.

            To illustrate the absurdity of your analysis, I pose an alternate proposition, which I neither believe nor intend to defend: All Exalted are good. By the methods you've demonstrated, this assertion is impossible to analyse. It breaks down where you try to tabulate the likelihood of a bad guy being a member of each Exalt type. You get a 0% probability across the board, and the conclusion is that bad guys don't exist.

                                                            I'm still thinking about a person like Nobunaga, where whether or not he's doing a thing that's "right" is more for society and history to judge, but might not really be the standards he's holding himself to.

                                                            People like to add a bit of edge to their assessments by saying "most people do what they think is good or right or what have you", but that's not really it; most people don't give profound moral consideration to their actions, and to the extent that Solars do, they're likely to be doing it in accordance with their commensurately greater power, but even then a person really having the perspective that their powers are a big deal that needs to be weighed carefully, that's still unusual (and probably more than a little bit melodramatic).

                                                            I would say that this is because most people aren't really in a situation where their capabilities or decisions are actually in a position to have the kind of impact that makes careful consideration seem warrented, but that's probably the default perspective of people no matter what their place is. Maybe it's a bit different when you've got democratically elected leaders, where the culture and system incorporates the idea that you only have position that you seek out and have validated, but historically most people of power have not been like that, even when they were peasants who rose up to become emperors, and it's certainly not what the Exalted are. Most people view their lives as small, because they can only ever see it from the perspective of one person, and when your life is small you don't guide yourself with onerous moral abstractions, you go with what suits your wants and needs and circumstances.

                                                            This is not to say that people are profoundly selfish, they're often quite the opposite. But when you do things that are helpful for other people, you do it because they're right there in front of you, not because of your adherence to an abstraction. But that also means that what you do comes out of what's around you. What was around a person like Nobunaga was war and destruction and retainers, and that generates motivations to make it safe to go down to the shops for a bit as well as to burn down temples serving as refuges for warrior monks and their protectorates and families.

                                                            The Chosen in Creation are not immaculate philosophers descended from Heaven; they are children in war torn regions given a gun. You are not a bad person. You are not a good person. You're a person amidst a complex and unstable melange of societies, with a random assortment of people around you to drive your motivations and responses. You are your experiences. You are your friends and family. You are your villains. Perfect Soul, despite the ironic name, is not a moral exemplar, she is ruler of a kingdom, she is enemy of House Mnemon, she is Chosen by a god who sympathized with her sense of being abandoned and betrayed that she expressed in a violent denouement. She will do what makes sense as those things play out around her, to the best of her ability to decide what makes sense, not because she's got good intentions but because she can't do nothing and because she can't sit around internally arguing about what best fits some abstraction while things fall apart around her. Sometimes this means comforting and inspiring the beaten down and destitute, and sometimes it means leaving disemboweled Mnemon soldiers outside of a fortress to get them to fuck off.

                                                            The Solars, the Exalted, are heroes, but they're the uneasy, unsanitized kinds of heroes you get in a world that is struggling not to die, right down to how such a world produces people who find more release in wild abandon than in struggling to make sure that there's a tomorrow, or that tomorrow looks better than today, whatever that should happen to look like to you.
