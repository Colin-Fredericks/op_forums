    Rising Elemental Fury
    Cost: 1m; Type: Supplemental; Mins: ??? 1 Essence 1; Prerequisites: none; Keywords: Uniform

    The Terrestrial exalt claims the tumult of battle as their own, and commences their onslaught with an eye for capitalizing on the chaos of a coordinated attack. This charm supplements a physical strike, and adds a number of non-charm bonus dice to the attack roll of a withering or decisive attack equal to the target's current Onslaught Penalty. If the target is suffering Onslaught Penalties from another Terrestrial exalt when this charm is invoked, then the user may claim these non-charm bonus dice even if their target is using a charm or effect that allows them to ignore Onslaught Penalties.

        So we concluded the War Pig fight last night. If you have forgotten how the first round went here is a refresher:

        I built a Full Moon Lunar soak-health beast hoping to create something that would at least last a couple rounds with him. He has better soak than Octavian: 11 from artifact heavy armor, 5 from Stamina, and +4 for his war form, for a total soak of 20 which is what my Dawn has. He has 28 Health levels, 14 of which are -4s. He wields a Dire Lance (Heavy Artifact, Piercing, Reaching) and rolls eleven dice to hit, with +23 raw damage (another +4 for its war form). His Parry is six. He has all Physical attributes and melee/brawl at five. He rides a stat-boosted Hellboar, which is also his totem animal, and gains all of its creature powers in war form. I gave him several of Octavian's powers for good measure but halved their mote cost to account for his smaller mote pool.

        Here is what happened:

        My Dawn got 22 on his join battle roll to my Lunar's 18. My Dawn rides an upgraded Simhatta (son of the Simhatta god) and they both rushed each other and clashed on round one. Both are using the charm Seasoned Beast-Rider's Approach and roll their mounts into combat on their tick. My Lunar gets 18 to hit on the clash but my Dawn rolls 20+, then does 14 withering damage. The Simhatta also wins the clash and does substantial withering damage, but does not crash the Hellboar. The Lunar and Hellboar continue charging past the Dawn, aiming for the nearby town of soft targets. So the Simhatta gives chase and uses a willpower to reflexively attack again with decisive damage, killing the Hellboar in a single strike. My Dawn then activated Peony Blossom Attack, striking at the Lunar for the second time that turn. The Lunar attempted to react with Invincible Iron Bastion and a full excellency, but Parry 13 did not defend it from an attack with 21 successes. The Dawn did a total of 14 decisive damage, putting the Lunar just barely into its -4s in round one. However, my Lunar activated The Tower Still Stands, putting him back at 16 initiative. Both fighters are now unmounted, as the Solar lept off his mount to deliver that blow (SBRA allows you to do this).


        So now that it's over I can share with you all that War Pig is an Essence 5 Lunar with all the charms for the Barbarian Warlord on page 546. He adds his wound penalties to his attacks, damage and soak. The Octavian Charms I gave him were Crimson Spray Blur, Invincible Iron Bastion and Superior Strength Reversal. I further added some custom charms that let him reflexively roll a disarm gambit before making a withering or decisive attack, with the gambit being unblockable; he can reflexively re-roll tens on his attack and damage rolls by spending one mote per re-roll; and he can spend health levels to power his physical excellencies, gaining (Wound Penalty) dice per level of damage he sacrifices.

        Here is what happened:

        On round two War Pig was ambushed by the party Night in tandem with her Owl-Griffon using Deadly Onslaught Coordination but his soak was so high that both rolled ping for just a handful of damage. The Dawn also suffered an ambush attack from the Trickster Lunar, but his soak was so high that she also rolled ping, doing no damage at all. Then my Dawn and I traded blows, I lead with a withering attack using that reflexive disarm gambit but he used Heavenly Guardian Defense to parry it and the withering attack despite a full excellency, willpower and major stunt for both rolls. He also counterattacked with Flashing Edge of Dawn, reducing War Pig to just two initiative and taking a chunk of his health. On his own turn my Dawn attacked and I attempted to counter with Superior Strength Reversal but I had no motes left and used my last two health levels and initiative to power the charm, hoping to at least do some withering damage. But instead my Dawn won the clash, having saved more motes than me, crashing War Pig even as he was passing out.

        I was more than a little put out by this outcome, so I asked my Dawn's player to redo the fight with me as a simulation starting from turn one. He somehow did even better than the first time. He lost his sword to the reflexive disarm but then took War Pig's lance with an improved disarm gambit (I set this at diff 4) and crashed him on turn two. The NPCs piled on the decisive damage since he had no hardness at that point, and War Pig never got to make a third attack.

        I spent four weeks designing War Pig. I often wondered if I was over-doing it, and I honestly don't think I could have made him any more difficult than he was. So does anyone see something that I'm missing? I'm at a loss here, guys.

I suspect for example that you would have more luck with two or three potent combatants than trying to stat up one mega creature. Also if your dawn has soak 20 some form of soak negation seems like a must. Otherwise he will never be crashed and with heavenly guardian defence there is little point in deceisives until he is.

                A few things jump out at me, reading that.

                First, the Lunar only has 11 dice to hit and Parry 6.                                             In a Clash, the attack roll essentially counts as a defense.

                                            Originally posted by page 202
                                            Clash Attacks ignore both characters’ Defense. Instead,
                                            the two attacks act as an opposed roll (p. 189). The character who accumulates more successes wins, striking
                                            his opponent, while the loser’s attack is thwarted. If a
                                            Clash Attack is withering, then it adds the threshold by
                                            which the winning fighter beat his opponent’s roll to its
                                            raw damage.
