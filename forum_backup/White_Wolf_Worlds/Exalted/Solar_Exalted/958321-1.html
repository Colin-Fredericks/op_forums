                            You specifically mention shapechanging and manipulating fate. Those are specifically things that are the focus of other Exalted types. So let's do some comparison there.

                            1. Solars are culture heroes, exemplars of skill. If study or practice can make you better at a thing in this setting, then Solars, if they choose to study or practice that thing, can become the best at it. This extends to things such as sorcery and shaping the Wyld to your will, as well as Evocations.
                            2. Lunars embody maximum raw potential, have multiple natural forms, and can steal others. Their tricks are (in theory) more versatile, but (in theory) can't quite reach the heights a Solar can.
                            3. Sidereals' power is, in a way, similar to the Solars' but it is limited to weird, esoteric expressions that often can't be applied directly to the problem. The only things they have that Solars don't are astrology and easier access to Sidereal Martial Arts.
                            4. Abyssals are even more similar to Solars, but interpreted through themes of death. They're better at Necromancy and have patrons and probably better gear, but that's about it, in terms of things Abyssals have that Solars don't.
                            5. Dragon-Blooded have elemental-themed powers, institutional power, and lineage themes.

                            Looking at these five, any one of them can be said to "suffer" by not having everything that the other four do, if you want to look at it that way... but if we're talking about versatility in terms of groups as a whole, I'd say that Solars are easily the most versatile. A Solar can be an expert in anything you like, while certain limitations and expectations are built into the other four types. In addition, if you want powers themed on the elements, blood, death, or whatever, just pick up an appropriate Artifact and start learning Evocations.

                            Now, a Lunar or an Alchemical might be more versatile on an individual level, and that's useful, but most Solars are good at the things they want to be good at.

                            It gets weird when you talk about Solars "suffering" in terms of versatility because they can't shoot laser beams from their eyes. I mean, a lot of people suffer from not being Superman, but it's not really something you precisely miss when you don't have it. Or so I assume, anyway. Eye beams are an extra capability someone has. Lack of eye beams isn't really a detriment to be counted as a negative in most conversations. What frame of reference are you using, for the base assumption to be eye beams?

                                            Versatility may be best expressed in terms of sorcery and artifact evocations, two systems that allow any splat to go out of theme and accomplish damn near whatever the player wishes. Solars excel at both, and have an entire game system shoehorned into one of their charm trees (wyld shaping) that allows them to recreate reality where the substance of the world is weak, which helps with the more onerous requirements of sorcerous workings and artifact manufacture (gathering materials and ingredients.) Solars have a strongly human theme, but that includes the human power to reshape one's environment and use technology (of a sort) to accomplish their goals.

                                            Versatility may be best expressed in terms of the forms one can wear. There was an excellent post in the context of an edition of days past, one that compared Solars and Lunars. Imagine every single one of them was individually tasked with infiltrating an identical fortification, to acquire some macguffin in secret without getting caught. Solar stealth and larceny specialists would be the best at this, and have the greatest success, but dang near every Lunar would have some shapeshifting form that would at least lend them the ability to try, even those who don't have a single dot in Stealth. Solars would have the best successes, but Lunars would have the highest success ratio. The same logic applies to combat, and their beefier forms, as well as any other primarily physical challenge.

                                            Versatility may be best expressed in terms of connections. It doesn't necessarily matter what you can do; what matters is what you can get done. The Sidereals and the Dragon-Blooded respectively, are unmatched in terms of heavenly and terrestrial resources. Though their own charms are limited, numbered, and bound by strange metaphor to exceedingly specific tasks, the Sidereals have access to astrology and all the favors they dare accept from the gods of heaven and earth. They don't necessarily have to do anything directly; they just have to discover who can, and what that person wants. That might seem quite a task, but they have direct access to the Loom of Fate itself. They can literally brute force search all of reality for the proper pawn, if necessary. The Dragon-Blooded do not have to try nearly so hard. They rule the mightiest civilization in the world and even the poorest among them (pitiful Nellens!) have armies, fleets, and cities at their disposal. For the vast majority of what they might want to accomplish, they do not do, they simply order it done. For what requires their direct intervention, they have dozens of family members, each a legendary hero in their own right, that might assist.

                                            Or maybe versatility is just a word embodied beings used to describe an advantage in a game that has no winners. What you can do, what you can be, who you know, these are all limited sets of certainties, which, quite frankly, sounds like a trap option to me. That there could be an obstacle to acting upon ones whims is foreign to the Fair Folk, who are their whims and have none. If you leave your pitiful identity behind, that which was you can very briefly experience a taste of formless infinity before becoming the Wyld, which is honestly the best any of you poor "souls" (ha!) can hope for.

                                            (And as for Deathknights? They don't particularly care what you can do, since what everyone must do is die. The rest is just details.)

                                                        Not that Essence 6+ is especially relevant in most scales, but I would say yes, because both the Solar and the Lunar SHOULD have their native charms' scopes limited in some way even as they ascend that high [even if 2e DotFA laughed at the idea of in any way limited Solars].

                                                        In general, culture heroes and whatever Lunars are don't simply think things and the world immediately changes to suit their desires, or whatever; they have to act, and their actions have to have some logical progression towards the consequence that is desired. Sorcery bypasses that to a degree; walking a circle around an area doesn't impart abundant fertility to the land, but Benediction of Archgenesis does just that, and the Solar can do that [as he has Solar Circle Sorcery] while the Lunar cannot. The Lunar may have some means within her Essence 6-8 charmset of doing something in the same conceptual direction, but it is most likely limited in some way, requires a greater investment of effort or sacrifice, etc.

                                                        Furthermore, the Solar finds Adamant Circle Workings far easier; he requires 2 fewer successes per roll, his rolls are an order of magnitude more frequent [base 1 week vs 3 months], it costs him less experience to cement the working. And they are pretty significant in general.
