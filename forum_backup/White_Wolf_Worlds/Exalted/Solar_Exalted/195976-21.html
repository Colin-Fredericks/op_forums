    I like the "Surpassing excellence at human endeavor", because it allows other splats to be "better" in a non-human way.
    Solars are fast learners that can read and teach at amazing levels.. Sure, but a Sidereal can take on the destiny of a warrior and just know how to fight while wearing that destiny - he never "learnt" the skill, and it might go away if he doesn't pretend in the right way, but it is an instant thing vs the solar learning things faster... (as a poor example)
    You know, I think this might be the crux of all the "I hate solars" sentiment. In my experience when people build a character, they tend to think in terms of board archetypes. "I am a great general who wants to lead her nation to greatness" "I am a shrewd diplomat to wants to establish peace through a complex web of inter-dependent trade" "I am a cunning rogue who want to make a fast buck", etc etc.

    The problem here is twofold: First, Solars were specifically made with these archetypes in mind. They are intended to be the very best at these things, and to perform at them better than anyone else, as is their nature as the "exemplary human skill" Exalted.

    Secondly, and more importantly, people have a favored Exalt-type that is NOT a Solar, but will still try to make them fit in to a playstyle that they were not designed for. A Sidereal for example is not meant to be a great general, a shrewd diplomat or a cunning rogue. They can certainly have a go at it, but Sidereals are intended to be (among other things) the "men in black" Exalted. They are the tacticians and strategists, the behind the scenes manipulators and the spies and assassins. Trying to force them to fill a roll that they aren't intended to fulfill will end up being unsatisfying because their charmset and thematics are build around the sideways and indirect approach, thanks to the fact that Fate and the Maidens who run it are very much sideways and indirect things/gods. As a result of their favorite Exalt not being able to satisfactorily full the role that they've chosen, they tend to view Solars with animosity and start demanding that their favorite Exalt type be brought in to parity with the Solars, all the while failing to realize that they're trying to make a square peg fill a round hole.

    In short, the classic archetypes are reserved for Solars, because the classic archetypes are prime examples of exemplary human skill, which is a core theme of the Solar Exalted. Trying to force a Sidereal a Lunar or a Dragon-Blooded in to these roles is possible, but since you are trying to work out of your native themes, you are going to be much more limited and have a harder time getting the results you wanted, and will be left feeling unsatisfied.

                        Originally posted by Cenobite451 View Post
                        You keep repeating this phrase, but it occurs to me that their current status of Jack-of all-Trades, Undisputed-Master-of-Frickin'-EVERYTHING is arguably no less boring, and at least as problematic. I mean, to use an earlier example: why can't Sidereals be better than Solars at learning? Surely there's enough nuance to Solars that they could still be a viable choice of protagonist without hammering so hard on the Better Than You nail? (And if not, that too seems like a problem that needs addressing.)
                        Solars aren't masters at everything, but they are masters at everything Solar, which encompasses every field of human endeavor and most of the basic archetypes for purely human heroes. They are not necessarily entirely peerless at everything in those fields (like I said, Sidereals might be able to match Solars at learning in certain ways, and have powers that exceed them in others), but the basic premise of the game is that Solars are the mightiest of the Exalted, and that this might is expressed in the basic thematics of their 25 abilities and 5 castes.

                        Therefore, while your Sidereal might be wiser in certain ways, or more knowledgeable about certain things, you're not going to be better at generalized learning than the Twilight caste; and the areas where Sidereals (or other splats) do things Solars don't will generally be by going outside the bounds of humanity or by exploring specific concepts unique to themselves.

                        The nuance and depth any individual Lore-focused Twilight caste character has is going to come from their personal story, background, and goals, combined with the way that their powers burn these things onto the world; but their definition as a Twilight is that they bring a level of scholarship to the world that it has not seen since the fall of the First Age. That's the core definition of what they are, in the same way that Sidereals are astrologers and advisers and martial-artists; it doesn't mean that those Twilights are the only supernaturally-talented scholars, or that there's nobody capable of competing with them, or that Sidereals can't have tricks they don't, but it means that no, none of the Exalted are going to be overall better than them at the broadly-defined field of "learning."

                        You're talking about this as though they're MMoRPG classes, like you can nerf Twilights a bit at this and they'll still be viable, or as if scholarship is just a number on a sheet that can be moved around a bit. That's not what splats are for. They tell specific stories, and the Twilight story is fundamentally about being the greatest scholar. You can still beat them at that (just like you can beat Dawn who strives to be the greatest swordsman), but...

                        Let me use the Dawn as an example, because I think that's clearer. If you wanted to beat the Invincible Sword Princess using another splat, what would you do? How would that story go? I mean, there's lots of ways. and it's certainly not impossible to just beat her at her own game -- but I think that the most logical path, for any non-Solar, is going to at some point involve leveraging their talents at things other than swordsmanship. You can build a Lunar who can beat the Invincible Sword Princess at a swordfight, but if you don't involve some thematically uniquely Lunar power in that fight, you're fighting with one hand tied behind your back, so you're going to be at a disadvantage. That isn't (just) because the Invincible Sword Princess is the best at swording -- it's because, while you can make an utterly lethal Lunar swordsman, the overall thrust of the Lunar charmset isn't about swording and doesn't really contain trees that are purely focused on that, so if you, say, took a bunch of strength and dexterity charms and used them only for swords (and ignored all parts of the trees that don't apply to swords) you're not going to be using them at full capacity.

                        Which is a roundabout way of saying that, more than just their power or preeminence -- if you want to describe the overarching design and thematics of each splat, you shouldn't focus on comparisons with Solars at a Solar narrative, because nobody's charmset but Solars tells purely Solar narratives. You can make a lethal Lunar swordsman or a learned Sidereal scholar (and both those things overlap heavily with the core of their narratives, so they're going to be very good at it), but thematically and magically, a Lunar is more than just a simple swordsman and a Sidereal is more than just a simple scholar; they may have some charms that help a lot at those things, but the overarching thrust of their charmsets is slightly different from that, whereas the Solar charmset expresses those concepts squarely.

                        That is why Sidereals cannot be better than Solars at learning. The Secrets caste knows a lot of things (in some ways, more than a Twilight, even before you start to talk about the setting's default state), but their overarching definition is aimed at a subtly different target.
