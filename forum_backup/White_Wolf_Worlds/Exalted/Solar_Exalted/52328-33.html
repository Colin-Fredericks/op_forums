        Originally posted by Kazorh View Post
        Basically, priest-king = prophet.
        Pretty sure that no one would actually describe most of, say, the biblical prophets as looking anything like "priest-kings," so I don't think this is a good summation. (Those being, probably, the guys the majority of the fanbase would think of first when we say "prophet" - first impressions matter here.)

        Let's divorce things from the conversation a little bit: what is a priest-king? He's someone who fulfills two roles, and his fulfillment of these roles is linked. He rules the people; he stands for the people before the gods; he rules the people because he stands for the people before the gods. That's part of the core Zenith identity, as currently presented.

        And hey! Zeniths actually are pretty good at becoming kings, in past editions, because they've got all those crazy "Follow me!" abilities. They're also pretty good being priests, because that's (a) a caste power and (b) at least in 2e, also related to your crazy "Follow me!" abilities. We see this in the signature Zeniths that show up. Panther - for all that Panther is not a good signature character - is distinctly the one on a mission from (a) god. The Hierophant is both: intercessor before the Unconquered Sun, and person of great authority in the Deliberative because of that role.

        I'm not defending these signature characters, but I am saying - look, the game has been pretty clear in its signalling here in the past. That doesn't mean it can't change, but it means if it's going to, we need to recognize that this is a change. Zeniths as "the guys who don't really intercede with the gods, but do go punch them, which... I guess anyone else could also do just as well" is a big change in that identity, and it's one that dumps priest-king as an archetype.

        ***

        All that said, there is a real problem here with prayer becoming random magic wish-granting. But that problem is inherent in the current structure of how prayer works, and especially for how it works relative to the little gods - the ones you might actually try to bribe with it. And everything else aside, that really, really needs to be fixed.

            Pretty sure that no one would actually describe most of, say, the biblical prophets as looking anything like "priest-kings," so I don't think this is a good summation. (Those being, probably, the guys the majority of the fanbase would think of first when we say "prophet" - first impressions matter here.)
            I'm pretty sure none of the biblical prophets are Solar Exalted who live in Creation, nor do they live in any sort of pulp fantasy universe where an expression like "priest-king" is appropriate and thematic (rather like sorcerer-king). I'm also pretty sure the Christian God isn't the Unconquered Sun.

            I'm also pretty sure Jesus is actually referred to as the King of the Jews, so even though God doesn't have the same thing with leadership that Sol does, Jesus is still imbued by divine authority over mortals.

            I'm also pretty sure that neither Panther nor the Hierophant act in any way like it's been established that mortal priests do. Panther is out on a mission for God, rather like some sort of... prophet. The Hierophant is an intercessor for the Unconquered Sun, not for the myriad Terrestrial gods (who are generally beneath the notice of an elder Solar and had better do what they're told, because they definitely don't want to get punched by the Hierophant).

            You'll notice that a main point of my previous post was that a Zenith being a priest of the Unconquered Sun is fundamentally different from a mortal being a priest of the local head god, because the local head god has a relationship with the people that is fundamentally different from the relationship the Sun has with the people. In fact, a Zenith who is a priest of the Unconquered Sun is more like a prophet, in the sense of a singular individual imbued with great power and authority from On High to effect change, rather than a member of the temple bureaucracy. The mortal and the Zenith are both priests, but what it means in practice is different, because one of them is a mortal and the other is a Zenith.

            So, if you consider that Panther and the Hierophant are good enough examples of the priest-king, then there is no change at all. This is exactly the same thing that has been going on in previous editions.

            Just in case you want to use that argument: yes, ALL Solars are singular individuals imbued with great power and authority. You'll also notice that all Solars are warriors of the gods created to overthrow the Primordials, and that doesn't stop Dawns from being the warrior caste. The Zenith are those most meant to evoke the particular theme of wielding divine authority, helped along by their caste abilities (like the "Follow me!" Charms, as you mention).

            Putting emphasis on the punching is also misleading: Zenith do have social Charms. The point is that when they intercede with the gods, they don't do so as supplicants. The relationship between the mortal priest and the god is inherently unequal because the god is always more personally powerful than the mortal, even if we're talking about a situation where the mortal has enough political power to get concessions out of the god. A Zenith, however, is mostly going to be equal or superior to any gods he intercedes with on behalf of mortals. Even if he uses diplomacy, the fact that he COULD punch the god changes the relationship.

            tl;dr: I meant both priest-king and prophet as they're being use to describe Zeniths, not in all cases ever.

            Was there anything else?

                                So, just putting this out there, but the people in favor of prayer charms and the people against them are still talking about entirely different meanings of the word prayer.

                                The people against it are against it because they see it as supplication, submission, and begging for intercession by gods. They think it would either have effects too broad to be well-defined or balanced and would be mismatched with the themes of the Unconquered Sun and being lords of Creation.

                                The people in favor of it are in favor of it because they see it as issuing demands and statements of negotiation. They see prayer as a bargaining chip that isn't offered personally but is offered by mortals who are directed by the performer. They see it as a resource to be used personally or offered to a deity as essence, ambrosia, and quintessence to get a bonus in normal social influence. for the offerings/payments. They see it as manipulating and directing cults and using non-prayer-specific Performance through prayer the way you use Linguistics through written letters. They see it as an avenue of economics and communication.

                                And neither the people in support of prayer charms nor the people against it seem to have the slightest clue what the other is actually talking about, because they're both talking about fundamentally different things and assuming the others are talking about the same thing.

                                Oh, and I apologize in advance if this explanation comes across as condescending or generally dickish. I don't mean it that way at all, I'm just hoping this can take the conversation from a glorified 'yuh huh, nuh uh' to somewhere actually constructive.
