        Past life memories tend to be triggered by like circumstances. Lyta, for example, grew up on the Isle in a dynastic family, and having failed to Exalt probably spent a lot of time being belittled and tormented by Dragon-blooded... so when she Exalted as a Dawn during sparing practice, the first thing that came rushing back were her memories of the Usurpation and the betrayal by the TerrestriaIs, and she went on a rampage. Would probably have kept right on murder-raging until the Immaculates put her down had she not been spirited away by a Sidereal. She now spends her time with a cult that tells her she's a hero and burning captured Dynasts alive with a giant magnifying glass for the glory of the Sun.

        Which I'd say means the answer to the original question, do past-life memories of their own violent death ever make new Solars mentally unstable, is a definitive "sometimes".

                Not all First Age Solars were evil or particularly crazy. (Nor all First Age Lunars and Sidereals, if you're one of those with unusually intense memories.) But IMO no past life is an actual person -- they can't change, gain new long-term memories, learn about the current age or incarnation, or adapt to new circumstances. Suddenly being a Green Sun Prince would probably especially confuse them, and it's unlikely they would grasp the idea that nearby demons and Infernals are supposed to be allies, unless they were akuma in life.

                I read a quest where a character with a high Past Life background got to use that as a mentor for favored MA Charms as long as she trained slowly and carefully during downtime. When she drew on memories too quickly (in non-downtime) she tended to get possessed with nasty results, including the past life eroding or building Intimacies, and using her Charms to do permanent things to her and other people. He wasn't a bad guy, but could not learn to understand that he was now low-Essence and in wildly different circumstances.

                It was a quest, so the ST didn't have to balance her against other PCs.


                An alternate interpretation is that a Celestial exaltation is literally a soul, and if it retains especially strong memories it acquires the personality of whoever left the most. In that interpretation, you would have to negotiate with the dead "Exalt" and have an actual relationship, to convince it to teach you and maybe possess you less often. Or just shout it down to make it shut up most of the time. It would have strong opinions about other Exalted, mortals, gods, demons, how to rule the world, how you should behave, the suitability of your boyfriend... sorta like a coadjutor, or Baron Harkonnen-within. Your advantage is that you have social Charms, but the voice(s) in your head don't.

                                IRC, the Prophecy was much about whether they should reform the Solars or kill them or wait it out. That's what it was needed for. The first corebook for Exalted talks about "vast atrocities and terrible indulgences" being already apparent.

                                It wasn't a kind of "Well, we're seeing some disturbing hints here. But only hints. So what will happen in the long term?" extrapolation of a worrying trend. More of a "Which of these plans to stop these obviously ruinous tyrants is the go to plan?" - seeking the practical advice about which paths are available to stop an already obvious trend.

                                Broadly, I think the Usurpation was intended to be a complex issue because you can disagree that they took the right path. Should they have killed off one of the key components for their super society (the Solars) or worked to reform them? I don't think it's intended to be complex because, we're doubting whether there was really a problem to begin with, or if it was just internal discontent and loss of prestige and displacement.

                                        The Sidereal realized that things were getting bad so they held a prophecy. The Prophecy had three outcomes.

                                        In the first, the Sidereal did nothing and Creation was ultimately left a ruined wreck, civilization was gone and humanity was little more than a handful of scattered tribes left to be preyed upon by the Fair Folk.

                                        The second outcome involved the Sidereal working with the Solar Exalted, and there was a 50% chance that the Creation would be destroyed as per the first vision, or the problems facing Creation would be solved and the world would enter a golden era.

                                        The third and final prophecy showed the Sidereal committing the Usurpation, an act that would diminish Creation but would ensure, with 100% surety, that Creation would survive.

                                        The important part to note note was that if the Sidereal Exalted chose to do nothing, and allowed Creation to continue on its course, and didn't take an active hand in stopping the depredations of the Solar Exalted, Creation was doomed.

                                        While perhaps the Usurpation itself was not necessary, some form of engagement on the part of the Sidereal Exalted with the Solar Exalted was necessary because the actions of the Solar Exalted were "bad enough" that left unchecked, they were going to bring about the complete ruin of Creation if nothing was done to stop them.

                                                        They overlooked to ask how long the world would remain saved before being destroyed anyway, and if it would also be saved from all those things outside of Fate that their 100% voluntary, non-samsara Prophecy could not in the slightest degree predict or take account of. They just assumed they already knew everything.

                                                        Except that, like the Solars and Lunars, not all of them were elders up to their eyebrows in craaaazy lunatic mode. Some of the younger ones might have said "Wait a minute, haven't we forgotten..." and been drowned out or ignored. Or maybe not, since their Curse gets worse in groups.


                                                        To get back to the past life question, it's likely that the more memories you have in your Exaltation, the longer your past life lived before dying, and the crazier they probably became. So if your memories are especially intrusive, or frequently threaten to possess you, your past life was probably crazy, even if it wasn't obvious on a day-to-day basis. If your best-remembered past life was still mostly sane, they may have died young for a Celestial, and also have less influence on your current life. Alternately, you could have had a very long-lived crazy whose memories were scrubbed out especially aggressively. I'm sure that when an akuma dies, Lytek takes exra effort to remove everything. (And then was/is politically 'discouraged' from mentioning the existence of Solar and Lunar akuma back then, or mentioning Sidereal akuma now.)

                                                        Crazy doesn't have to mean evil, though. Misho in Keychain of Creation has a believably crazy First Age incarnation, but not in an oppressive-tyrant or destroying-the-world way. The man was a workaholic, spent most of his life obsessed with immortality, and finally killed himself to create an immortality mcguffin, and he can't handle the sight of blood. He's a very nice guy, but imagine having him freaking out in the back your head while you're trying to survive a battle.

                                                        However, for non-Infernals there's the possibility of having not one particularly vivid past life, but a bunch of scattered memories from several past incarnations, jumbled together confusingly. Possibly with jumbled bits of contradictory personalities left from multiple people. What psychological effect would that have?
