            Originally posted by John Mørke View Post
            It's not in the nature of Solar Essence to supplicate. Who, in all the universe, does the Unconquered Sun pray to?
            I see this point, and do not contest it.
            Yet I would also suggest that the view that prayer is solely about supplication to a deity is needlessly narrow for a few reasons, especially in a world like Creation.
            1. Sincere prayer can be about supplication, but it can also be about communication, friendship, and insight.
            2. Prayer can serve as an aid for focus, meditation, or expression, regardless of actual religious sentiment
            3. Insincere prayer can be used as a tool to focus attention on the pray-er, giving that person a platform to present their ideas as the group's, and so forth

            Honestly, I think 90% of what I want Solar's to do with prayer should be doable with their regular Performance Charms. And that is my point - in the past, the system for prayer really was such that the only Charms that could be used were Excellencies. The remaining 10%, if that, are things that really emphasize that at some level, the Chosen of the Sun carry a bit of the Essence of the King of the Gods within them, and so when they "pray" it means something a little different. Maybe they are asking nicely, because they are being polite, but...
            Or the effect they want HAPPENS, because they asked, and the god is left to fill out the paperwork...
            Either of those would go beyond the scope of social Performance, but would expand down an interesting Prayer Performance path.
            Anyway, it is late here - I hope this was useful. I will check back later

                        In general, I think it's possible for a Solar to take the majesty and position of the gods seriously even while being aware that the actual gods kind of fall short of their notional glory, and that doing so gives them the stature that something called a "god" really ought to have, even a corrupt one.

                        For Solars dealing with gods, here's an artifact I made up several years ago. It reads a bit clunky now but it still feels like a good example of how a Solar might interact with gods:

                        Five Directions Treasure Wheel: Artifact 5

                        (historical pablum snipped)

                        The wheel, a symbol of the righteous Lawgiver's authority, grants a virtuous ruler the power to call the gods to account. When attuned for a cost of eight motes, the wheel sparks to life in a corona of Solar Essence and hovers before its owner, reflecting his face. If necessary, the wheel darts from side to side to shield its owner from attack, providing the protection of a tower shield without requiring the use of either hand. However, this is only a fringe benefit.

                        When the master of the treasure wheel channels a Virtue on a prayer roll, the wheel's true power activates, granting him automatic success with a threshold of 10 successes. The owner need not know a god's name or attributes, but may invoke a god by his purview and be assured of evoking the correct divinity. (“God of this river, your charge grows unruly and blocks the passage of the Sun's Chosen! Hear my charge and answer!”)

                        The god called by this prayer will be pleased to receive an unexpected portion of Ambrosia, and then less pleased when the image of the treasure wheel appears before him, reflecting its owner's image in the central mirror. The owner likewise sees the god and the two may communicate and engage in social combat through the link as though they were present together. No other effects can pass through the link, but gods who may target petitioners with their Charms or other magic may do so normally. The link created by the treasure wheel lasts for the remainder of the scene or until the wheel's owner dismisses it. Should any effect attempt to sever it before that time, the wheel's owner adds an extra ten successes to the roll-off.

                        The power of the wheel is even more binding upon those gods who have grown fat and corrupt. If the god in question has suppressed the Virtue channeled by the owner in his prayer roll during the current scene, she is overcome by a sense of the owner's righteous virtue, suffering a penalty to her MDV for the scene equal to her rating in the given Virtue. This is an unnatural Emotion effect costing three Willpower to resist. If the owner has himself suppressed any Virtues during the scene, this power fails to function, but the operation of the wheel is otherwise unchanged.

                        The five-directions treasure wheel is explicitly able to establish communication with the Incarnae and the Primordials. The owner who chooses to force open a connection to the greatest of gods would be wise to have either a very good spiel or a perfect defense. The ruler who opens a window to the Yozis will be unwise even if he has both.

                                        Amergin[1] Glúingel ("white knees") or Glúnmar ("big knee") is a bard and judge for the Milesians in the Irish Mythological Cycle. He was appointed Chief Ollam of Ireland by his two brothers the kings of Ireland. A number of poems attributed to Amergin are part of the Milesian mythology.
                                        One of the seven sons of Míl Espáine, he took part in the Milesian conquest of Ireland from the Tuatha Dé Danann, in revenge for their great-uncle Íth, who had been treacherously killed by the three kings of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Mac Cuill, Mac Cecht and Mac Gréine. They landed at the estuary of Inber Scéne, named after Amergin's wife Scéne, who had died at sea. The three queens of the Tuatha Dé Danann, (Banba, Ériu and Fódla), gave, in turn, permission for Amergin and his people to settle in Ireland. Each of the sisters required Amergin to name the island after each of them, which he did: Ériu is the origin of the modern name Éire, while Banba and Fódla are used as poetic names for Ireland, much as Albion is for Great Britain.
                                        The Milesians had to win the island by engaging in battle with the three kings, their druids and warriors. Amergin acted as an impartial judge for the parties, setting the rules of engagement. The Milesians agreed to leave the island and retreat a short distance back into the ocean beyond the ninth wave, a magical boundary. Upon a signal, they moved toward the beach, but the druids of the Tuatha Dé Danann raised a magical storm to keep them from reaching land. However, Amergin sang an invocation calling upon the spirit of Ireland that has come to be known as The Song of Amergin, and he was able to part the storm and bring the ship safely to land. There were heavy losses on all sides, with more than one major battle, but the Milesians carried the day. The three kings of the Tuatha Dé Danann were each killed in single combat by three of the surviving sons of Míl, Eber Finn, Érimón and Amergin.
