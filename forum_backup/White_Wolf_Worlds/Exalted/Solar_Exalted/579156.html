              In our Celestial game, the core of the party is a family group: My Lunar sorcerer, his adopted Changing Moon daughter, and her Night Caste Husband (who is also my Solar Mate).
              The Changing Moon and Night Caste have a son, whose pregnancy she got done in 3 months by mutating her womb with Hybrid Body Rearrangement.
              So, they have this baby, Hamakai. And they just carry him around on adventures and stuff. They do often leave him with servants while popping out to do something dangerous though. Also, I used a custom Thaumaturgy ritual to bind his soul to a baby bear we found, so he has a Bear familiar, which is now old enough to beat up anyone who tries harming him while we're out. For example, once a guy tried to kidnap him so the bear clawed his face off. Which unfortunately means we have no idea why a guy tried kidnapping him.

              I also have now got the baby of the Slayer PC who died, and Ferand the third-circle demon; but I have no idea what to do with this demon-baby. Right now I'm paying a servant to babysit him while we're away, but I need some kind of permanent solution. I'm considering giving it to a monastery, but I'm worried they might mistreat him.

                                      The Old Man trope is just the one where a kid, usually a boy, usually around 16~18 years old, finally beats his father in a 1-on-1 game of basketball. It's a changing of the guard/coming of age/"whoa, my dad is just human" moment, suffused with realization and pride for the kid, and pride and bittersweet acceptance for the dad. Obviously, a dad using Perfected Jordan Prana isn't going to let that moment happen.

                                      Greyman's rundown of the Godparents list is spot-on. I think the idea of these four, super-powerful, widely disparate entities, all uncomfortable in formal wear, side-eyeing each other warily, yet united in a genuine Intimacy for the Solar and their baby, has a lot of humor in it. Kaneko and Ahlat haggling over branding rights to the whole "Bull" nickname; the giant Oliphem watching proudly as the Solar opens his gift, revealing a selection of First Age onesies, an Incarnae-themed crib mobile, and a floppy, plush Ramatheus doll; Raksi giving something practical, like an otherwise-lost Solar Circle spell she can't use, along with a Diaper Yozi; Ahlat's gift being a Yu Shan-crafted pull 'n speak toy that says things like "the bull says 'cunning and courage are the hallmarks of the righteous! Glory to Ahlat and the Unconquered Sun!'"; everyone just staring at Raksi, holding their breath as she takes the foil lid off her contribution to the after-ceremony potluck meal, and Raksi just rolling her eyes and saying, "oh, for Luna's sake, they're siaka croquettes, people! Like I'm going to waste perfectly good baby on you..."

                                      "Solar Soccer Mom" is also rife with potential. Like a Twilight mom, frenziedly cobbling together magitech artifacts to create "the ULTIMATE JUICE-BOX!," or picking up the team in a Five-Metal Shrike.

                                      And as for Plentimon teaching the toddler to gamble...well, everybody needs that cool uncle that's juuuust this side of shady, right?

                                                          When Dinesh, the Invincible Warden, fell in love with his circlemate Gentle Radiance, their romance was a tumult of epic poems and duels at sunset and races from mountaintop to mountaintop. A warrior without compare and a sorceress without peer, they conquered an empire and erected an impregnable citadel at its heart. Dinesh's sword guarded their lands, new-won from Chaos, from the predations of the madness beyond the edges of the world and beyond the Sun's light. Gentle Radiance's spells called rains and gave life to the soil and banished sickness from the land, and brought prosperity and joy to their people.

                                                          Together, they brought a child into the world, a daughter they named Chandra. She was born with her mother's skin, the color of night, and her father's eyes, the color of gold. As she grew into a girl, the tangled curls of her hair glimmered with jewels like stars in the vault of heaven, and the bell of her laughter broke through all the world's defenses like the smile of the Sun himself.

                                                          Dinesh and Gentle Radiance feared for their daughter, for kind and beautiful and clever as she was, surrounded as she was by peace and prosperity, she was not Exalted as were they, but was mortal. When she tripped and scraped her knee on the gleaming white stones of her palace, her mother grew cold as winter and summoned forth the gods of stone and stair, and bid them kneel before her. They made obeisances and offerings, but Gentle Radiance bade them swear a binding oath: neither rock nor stone nor clay nor soil, nor any tool carved or forged of the treasures of the Earth would they allow to bring harm to her daughter. They gave their word, and she lifted her hand and placed Heaven's Seal upon them. And so Chandra's scissors broke when her attendants attempted to cut her hair, and rough stones turned to dust when they tried to file her nails, and when she tripped and fell the earth grew soft and gentle to catch her.

                                                          Dinesh, journeying in the lands beyond the world, did battle with a great lord of nightmares and despair. He struck down his armies and brought him to flight, until the mighty Lord of Chaos gave him a vision of all that he loved in ruins, and his daughter dead at his feet. In a red rage, the Invincible Warden slew the Raksha and forged his Heart into a ring, and he quested in turn against each of the seventy nine Lords of Chaos and defeated them. At the point of his sword, they swore upon the ring that neither they nor any of the forces of Chaos would ever touch his daughter. Realizing that he had other enemies, he called together his circle and forayed into the lands of the Dead and into the hellish prison of the Yozis themselves, and between them they bound the Princes of Hell and the King and Queen of the Dead never to work against Chandra or do her harm.

                                                          When Chandra burned her finger making tea, Gentle Radiance called forth the gods of fire, and they bowed before her, and she bound them. When Chandra got a rash from poison ivy while hunting the woods of her estate with her father, Gentle Radiance called forth the gods of the forest and of life, and she bound them. When Chandra caught cold, Gentle Radiance strode into Heaven itself and tore asunder the gates of the Shogun of Ill Health, and on her way out she stopped by the hall of Old Man Winter, and she bound them. When Chandra woke with fright in a thunderstorm and came weeping into her parents bedroom, her mother and father together bound the gods of the wind to keep their daughter safe from harm. By the time she was fourteen, Chandra could be harmed by neither bird nor beast nor poison. Fire and ice could not touch her, nor plague, nor thunder or rain. She laughed as she leapt from treetops to glide gently to the ground, she danced around the footsteps of hungry Tyrant Lizards, she strode unharmed to study with her tutor in the cities of the dead.

                                                          All stories have an ending. Most times, it depends who is telling the story. Chandra is mostly forgotten these days. In the Violet Bier of Sorrows, where the story is sometimes told to boys and girls destined to grow up to become Joybringers, the story goes that her parents wanted only joy for their daughter and so they never thought to coerce the gods of love, and so Chandra gave her heart unwisely and was driven mad by grief, unable to commit suicide by any of a dozen means until Luna took pity on her and took her away and turned her into a constellation. I once heard the Crimson Banner Executioner tell the story, and the way he tells it her parents persuaded their friends in the Deliberative to pass a law barring the Solars from harming each other's children and everybody thought it was a good way to curb inter-Solar warfare and swore oaths to it, and so Chandra and her fellow Golden Children led the charge the fateful night of the Usurpation, the Solars' daiklaves twisting away in their hands and their spells exploding into chaotic essence as Chandra slew her parents. He was there...well, his suit was? So maybe he would know. I asked about Chandra in the Bureau of Endings, since I was curious, but they don't have any official record of her. Which is odd, because I asked a Chosen of Journeys who has a hobby of collecting legends from all over Creation, and she says according to her research Chandra was a real person, and several major road gods of her acquaintance told her they were personally sworn never to allow harm to come to the girl upon their roads or during her travels. I was beginning to grow concerned, since if a real person has no record in the Bureau of Endings it usually means they're still alive, so I asked Chejop about it over tea.

                                                          I'm not sure why he decided to answer my question, but I suspect it's because he's concerned my sympathies for the Solar Exalted might become a problem a few decades down the road. He thinks like that. So he sighed, and he took me up into the Forbidding Manse of Ivy, and past several horrible guardian spirits, and past several hideously intricate traps, to a pleasantly appointed room with cold iron bars in the windows. A beautiful woman slept there, with skin the color of night and long and tangled hair that glimmered in the lamp light. I gasped, and she opened her eyes, and they were the gold of the rising sun. She smiled at Chejop, as at an old friend, and he bowed politely and returned a book he said he had borrowed from her.

                                                          "No luck?" She asked.

                                                          "Not yet," he answered.

                                                          The Solars, it must be remembered, are creatures of perfection. They are not prone to making mistakes. They remember the mistletoe. Dinesh and Gentle Radiance met Saturn's blade long ago, but their daughter lingers on even now, for while the eldest of her Chosen work to find a solution, that Maiden has sworn an Oath. And in the meantime, a beautiful and ageless woman entertains herself in Creation's greatest library, and talks to the flesh-eating swarms of scarabs who flow affectionately around her, and smiles at visiting Sidereals when they try to console her by telling her that "There's always an Ending."

                                                          So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.
