                In my games, pretty much everyone knows the Incarnae exist. "My" take on the Immaculate Faith/Philosophy describes them explicitly as the Rulers of Heaven, and those who gave the Exalted the Creation-Ruling Mandate. (which is, to various shades of interpretation, true, both in-universe and in-book)

                The proscription of Incarna-worship is much like the proscription of 'regular' deity veneration, except intensified to the nth degree. It would be hilariously heretical to publicly venerate the Incarnae, outside of extremely-narrow circumstances, and even then they are seen more as patrons of things than gods to be worshiped. Chances are, you overwhelmingly-likely won't see a shrine to one of the Incarnae, but instead a shrine to the actual god of the thing in question.

                The reasoning on that is simple: they are busy, indescribably so, and it would be hilariously presumptive to try and distract them with veneration and prayers, which could much-more-easily be directed to their subordinate deities.

                Generally, the Incarnae are viewed/described more like Yahweh-style overdeities than the 'regular' gods.

                For example:

                -Mercury is invoked whenever a new road is built, or a port is opened..... but the road/port is dedicated to the road/harbor-gods, not Her

                -Venus is invoked during weddings, or when a child is born..... but the temple the wedding happens in is likely dedicated to another deity, not to Her

                -Mars is invoked when organized military forces are raised, or when a campaign is undertaken ..... but offerings are made to the regional war-gods, not to Her

                -Jupiter is invoked when universities, libraries, or other repositories of knowledge are built (She is often viewed as the patron of knowledge/intelligence in general, as opposed to 'just' secrets).... but the repository is going to be dedicated to other deities of learning and somesuch, not Her.

                - Saturn is invoked at funerals and such..... but cemeteries and other resting-places of the dead are dedicated to other deities, not Her.

                - Luna is ..... complicated. She is viewed as the patron of not only wilderness in general, but also hunting, fertility, sorcery, mysticism, and many, many other aspects of life. She is both the most 'well-known' Incarna, and the most-likely one to have actual worshipers. In my games, most 'barbarian' cultures across Creation venerate some aspect of Luna, which often causes them trouble when dealing with Immaculate 'civilized' folk.

                - Sol is ..... also complicated. Unlike both the rulebook and other people's interpretation of Him , He is known about, at the very least, across most of Creation, just not invoked for very much. He might be associated with agriculture, but not commonly. This is due to how His 'turning from Creation' is comparatively well-known in my games, unlike "vanilla" Creation, usually as an Immaculate Parable.

                - Gaia is almost-completely unknown. Most of her domains get folded into Luna's perview. Scholarly-types might know the name 'Gaia', but more as the fancy name for Creation than the name of a Ican'tbelieveShe'snotanIncarna.

                Yet again, Luna is by far the Incarna most likely to be venerated. She is also the one most likely to actually come down and visit Creation, in several aspects, and is the most humanlike and approachable.

                Both Solar and Lunar Anathema are known about, Lunars comparatively more so, and the existence of a third 'type' of Anathema is posited by scholarly-types; those who stole the power of the Maidens of Fate. Those who point this out are almost-always shut down by the argument 'where are they, then?" No Fate-Anathema have ever appeared, so the argument appears to be moot.....

                The existence of Anathema is usually correlated by how much the Incarnae interact with Creation, and is usually used as yet another Immaculate Parable on why the Gods should mind their own business. Luna appears the most, allowing the Anathema to steal Her power most often, so most Anathema are Lunar. Sol interacted with Creation also, at least until He turned his face from it, so Solar Anathema are the second-most 'common'. The Maidens of Fate have apparently never interacted with Creation, giving the Anathema no chance to steal their powers, so there are accordingly no Fate-Anathema.

                Of course, the recent resurgence of Solar Anathema has led to a mass "OH SHIT" and much hand-flailing among scholarly-types in the know...... but also, oddly enough, a glimmer of hope. Does the appearance of the Solar Anathema mean Sol has turned His face back to Creation? Not that it means very much......

                            It's been 1500 years, 90% of everyone died halfway through, and the world's largest government, its main religion, and the people who practically run Heaven and fate itself all want inconvenient parts of history to disappear. It's a miracle they still remember anything at all.

                            More importantly, though... one thing available to Solars, especially Zeniths, is to tell a story about how you restore worship of the UCS to the world. There's this really dumb Christian Evangelical narrative where they assume that whoever they're trying to convert has never heard of Christ before (you see this in eg. Chick Tracts all the time, "tell me more about this 'Jesus' person!") - it makes no sense in modern America, but it endures because it makes for a good story.

                            So Exalted is designed to set it up, to put your Zenith in a position where they can reveal the good news of the UCS to people who've forgotten it and play a holy-roller revivalist.

                            For that to work, he has to be mostly (but not quite entirely) forgotten. Hence, the bits about how the Sidereals and Immaculate Faith work to minimize him, but are too worried about offending him to actively suppress everything about him completely. Bits and pieces survive, enough so you can sell his narrative to unbelievers without it sounding like you're talking crazy-talk, but with enough twisted or lost or forgotten that you're still delivering the Good News to people who will react with gasps to this idea of a human-friendly king of heaven who cares about them personally.

                            (Yes, yes, I know. But that's how you frame it as a Zenith. Theological and practical doubts about the UCS are something else.)

                                        I would also add, regarding Solars specifically worshipping the UCS:

                                        Exalted generally treats Exaltation as a spiritual event, not just a powerup.
