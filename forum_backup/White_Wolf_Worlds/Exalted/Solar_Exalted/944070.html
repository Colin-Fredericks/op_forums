    What's a good way for a Solar dabbler to get into RAW crafting?

    08-04-2016, 10:06 PM
    As the title says. I have a Twilight with Supernal Lore and Craft as a Caste ability, but I didn't buy any dots of Craft until very recently. Right now I have two dots of Craft (Bookmaking), but no Craft Charms other than the Excellency. If I were to pick up just one or two Craft Charms to start grinding out meaningful basic and major projects, what would you folks recommend? I already have Heaven-Turning Calculations purchased, naturally.

    Also, what's a good baseline investment to start making Artifacts? A hundred or so XP down the line I'd love to try to throw together a sorcerous artifact as mentioned in the sidebar by the Talisman of Ten Thousand Eyes.

                                          If you want a dabbler who can do any mundane stuff and turn out the occasional artifact without bringing the game to a halt, I recommend the following six: Arete-Shifting Prana, Supreme Celestial Focus, Sublime Transference, Craftsman Needs no Tools, Flawless Handiwork Method an Bright-Forging Prana, requiring Essence 2.

                                          Arete-Shifting Prana lets you temporarily alter one of your Craft Abilities to a conceptually related one.

                                          Supreme Celestial Focus lets you buy additional Craft Abilities with gxp.

                                          Sublime Transference lets you convert one kind of crafting xp into another.

                                          Craftsman Needs no Tools gives you the ability to make things without tools and make basic and major projects really fast.

                                          Flawless Handiwork Method rerolls 10s on Craft rolls. Not spectacular on its own, but it's a prerequisite for-

                                          Bright-Forging Prana gives you three extra Craft Charms for one project per story.

                                          The first three Charms together give you access to virtually any Craft Ability. CNNT speeds mundane crafting immensely. Bright Forging Prana is for your occasional artifacts. I suggest the following to unlock with Bright-Forging Prana for an artifact project:

                                          Thousand-Forge Hands speeds the creation of an artifact immensely.

                                          Flawless Handiwork Method (Repurchase) rerolls 6s on Craft rolls.

                                          Experiential Conjuring of the True Void gives non-Charm dice and a success in exchange for some craft xp.

                                          With these purchases, you can make an Artifact 3 once per story with only a week of work. With a good Intelligence rating, you should be able to make an Artifact 4 when you hit Essence 3.

                                                      Speaking out of experience playing an supernal Craft in an almost RAW environment I can second most of what has been already said.

                                                      For Minor and Major projects Craftsman Needs no Tools is enough and let's you do crazy stuff crazy fast. It's probably everything you'll ever need if mundane crafting is all you are doing.

                                                      When it comes to artifacts Crumplepunch and LadyLens described what I am successfully doing (Bright-Forging Prana is amazing).
                                                      If you are playing a very specialized artisan you probably still want Supreme Celestial Focus, just to buy up the Craft (Artifacts) Dots. RAW Crafting is amazing at eating up all your regular and solar xp denpending on what you want to do and how you are playing the craft minigame.


                                                      Since charms are already mentionend I will be rambling a (long) bit about my experience in the following. I'm not some clever analyst or game designer, so take it for what it is worth.
                                                      .

                                                      The crafting system wants you to use the crafts you have to make meaningful contributions to the game you are playing. You get crafting xp for objectives that try to capture these contributions in mechanical terms. The problem with that is that those objectives can easily be interpreted in significant different ways. The example for that already got mentionend in this thread. The projects needs to strengthen or support an intimacy you have. For my ST this at first meant "playing out a crafting project in way that either makes the relevant intimacy go up one level (not possible with an already defining one) or making sure it does not go down". I was rarely able to fulfill this objective, so we talked it over and now it is "Crafting things you enjoy that are supported with an intimacy".
                                                      I mention this because it is important on how the crafting plays out. There was the cooking example and while I actually play a Solar Chef I don't "farm" crafting xp just because I make breakfast every morning. That is lame, not the system intent and you would probably better off just porting over how 2nd Edition crafting worked. At least in my opinion. I never liked the pacing-less approach of many systems out there.

                                                      Well you could most certainly farm every breakfast if you read the system this way, though. But even when not, cooking is still an amazing support craft for gathering xp. And it is a good way to make people like you. That is where the crafting supports the social system and the other way around. You can get even more points if you "educate" your fellow player characters or NPCs to like the things you do for them and instill a few intimacies. (Having all the other players have a major intimacy on your cooking skills feels good man. And them spending willpower to prevent it from going to defining because they just happen to be there as you use Performance to awaken the appetite in other NPCs even more so!)
                                                      Just be sure your ST does not think it is a good idea to seperate crafting xp for different crafts... Well it is a stretch to imagine that cooking can give you the "inspiration" (for which I see crafting xp as an mechanical abstraction) for a grand 10.000 years daiklave or a manse or anything even more unrelated. But well, splitting your xp pool is not the way to go about it. My ST had a big problem with it...

                                                      A good support craft is something you can do with very little input and little need for specific and most importanly huge and impractical tools. You don't want every NPC to point their finger at when you magical handwave a whole feast, statue, impossible elaborate dress into existence without tools within a few seconds.
                                                      Carpenter, cook, probably smith, tailor, maybe alchemist are all good professions, I think. The big ones like architect, shipwright less so. But honestly everything that you can see your character doing a lot and you as a player have fun with is good way to support crafting. Finding always new ways to aply craft within the context of a story is a fun for me. Just keep in mind that several crafts are just not that artifact friendly. Cooking for example won't bring you much options that are relevant in most games, I think. Well we are playing in the North so a few big infrastructure artifacts can be made. Tailoring and smithing are way more versatile in that sense.

                                                      But back to the "farming" interpretation.
                                                      If you do read it like this, the point-generating charms are almost worthless if you don't want to craft 10 artifacts every session. And if you don't they are amazing. And they are most certainly absolutely neccessary if you play a genius craftsman that only wants to show his skills on chosen occasions when his genius is sufficiently challenged (meaning he won't do any Minor and maybe even Major projects). Or if you have a ST with a very strict reading.
                                                      Ah and make sure you know what your STs regular "story" is. I had a slight missunderstanding with my ST once that resulted in me not getting to reset my "per Story Charms" (like Bright Forging Prana) once in half a year of weekly play.


                                                      Also Thousand-Forge Hands is amazing for speeding up R4 and R5 Artifacts but it does not give you the infrastructure to be able to work on artifacts at all. We are playing a traveling adventure so the time and means for crafting grand things are hard to come by. Again a talk-with-your ST thing. I probably can't avoid getting "Words-as-Workshop Method" somewhere down the line if I want to keep all my ambitions for my character. For dabblers all the project slot charms are something to avoid though. Even if you become more than a dabbler the point generations charms do the same thing more or less (reducing crafting costs through allievating the need to buy additional project slots vs more points to buy slots with). If you don't want a follow charm of that tree (like Words-as-Workshop Method) just leave it alone.

                                                      One last point and the main thing I don't like about crafting in Ex3 (which I enjoy immensely nonetheless) is how most of the craft charms are designed. Everything you need for mundane crafting is CNNT, maybe Thousand Forge Hands if you are playing an architect or a shipwright and if you are really into it Crack Mending Technique, Shattering Grasp and the durability charms (even though I still think there should be less of them that do more). And well that is about all you can do with craft in it's current state. Almost every other charm gives you only dice tricks for crafting roles which you absolutely not need for anything else than artifacts.
                                                      That means if you want to realiably craft R5 and NA Artifacts you need to invest a whole lot that does not do much else for your character. You can't actually do fancy stuff with those charms. While artifacts are awesome it still takes a whole lot of time and ressources and crafting xp. I think they have paced the artifact creation for high ratings a bit too much from a character development perspective.
                                                      My point with this is: As a "dabbler" with the charms Crumplepunch listed R4 artifacts are possible and R3 are a safe project. Everything more will take you a lot more of xp investment that may take a way a lot of otherwise more interesting ways of developing the character. My character needs to rely on buying Martial Arts Charms with solar xp to not be a liability as soon as it comes to a at least somewhat challenging fight for the combat characters in my group, i.e. everyone else. It is of course synergie problem with my group and the style we play, it is nowhere near a perfect kind of table for a main-crafter, but it works. And I think that is awesome even with the restraints the system puts crafting.



                                                      Wow that got even longer than I expected. I apologize for boring whoever slogged through it all.
