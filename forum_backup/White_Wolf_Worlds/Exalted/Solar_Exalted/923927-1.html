            I haven't so far noticed any Castes that problem players in general are particularly drawn to, only natural inclinations that align a certain Caste with certain types of problem players. Like the annoying "I'm going to be an impish kleptomaniac" is definitely going to be a Night Caste because it mechanically enables the sort of irritating behaviors that the player wants to engage in. And the player who wants to act like an overbearing moralistic bully will be drawn to Zenith. Et cetera. Problem players usually select the character type that they believe best excuses/justifies their bad behavior, or that which will be most likely to shield them from the RL consequences of it.

            But none these are problems with the Caste of the character per se. They're all problems with the player. There are no Castes that I dislike seeing at my table, but there are unquestionably players that I will kick to the curb at warp speed because I have no patience for that nonsense any more.

                I haven't played 3rd edition extensively, but so far Dawn is the caste I am the least excited to see at my table. Having a Dawn with a Supernal combat skill who focuses heavily on combat can create a definite power imbalance at the table, and require you to start having to design encounters around the one super-powerful character who can defeat nearly anything you throw at him, and the rest of the group who's also there, without those other players feeling too much like sidekicks.

                It's not as bad as 2nd edition where you had one Paranoia Combat optimized character and the rest with more or less "realistic" builds (I had that happen once, it was fucking horrible) - 3rd edition is a bit better and even players who aren't "combat kings" can still contribute, but it's still enough of an issue that if someone said they wanted to play a Dawn so they could Supernal (Combat Ability) I would inwardly wince a little bit.

                As to Twilights, the only issue I ever had was a player who was the "Hermit craftsman." The player wanted everyone to take a couple of years of downtime so he could craft a panoply of 5 dot artifacts for everyone in the group, an airship, essence canons, etc. The player would get annoyed when I would "interrupt" his crafting by forcing his character and the rest of the group to go out on adventures or deal with problems - "We should just wait and deal with everything from a position of absolute power once we have all of our end-game equipment," was his philosophy, (and to be fair to him he was used to playing games where there wasn't much of a sense of time progression and the party could just sit around and rest for however long they wanted without things really changing) and we ended up clashing a few times because of that.

                But that was more because of the player than his Exalt caste.

                                                            I don't actually have any experience with bad players being a problem in Exalted as my players are pretty fabulous. I think someone else said it well when they said that the problem they run into is balancing combat when you've got someone whose Supernal Melee means they have a perfect Parry of 7 versus basically everything. It's not bad, it's not wrong, it's not really out of flavor, but it can be tough to do combat and have it feel as exciting when one character vastly outpaces the others in combat. As one of the few mechanical situations where all players have to participate and one which has a lot of system, it can kind of stink when some of your characters have really cool styles of play that can't get enacted because the Dawn either wrecks everyone or gets into rocket tag with NPCs that the other PCs just can't handle.

                                                            I don't think it's unfair of the Dawn to be good at combat and for a player to want to play a dope mega warrior. I'm not sure what the way is around the problem honestly.
