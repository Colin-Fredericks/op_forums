    Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
    I'm gonna point out a particular problem I've noticed in the overall solar charmset but please correct me if I'm wrong. Almost all of the charms that grant free (non-craft) XP are in the craft tree.

    Considering that it's possible to get so much craft xp that you eventually just stop keeping track of it (according to some player experience) this would indicate that a craft heavy player could potentially start massively outspending their peers in regular xp. Even if this doesn't count towards essence this raises an eyebrow for me at least
    Luckily, the charm for getting tons of craft xp is at the top of one tree, and the charm for turning White XP into actual XP is at the top of a different tree. A tree chock-full of rubbish (I'd argue it's one of the worst trees Solars can buy; there's a few good bits, like the one where you make a free level 2 artefact once a story, or the pinnacle charm where you clone yourself, but a lot of it is speedbumps). And you have to have completed like 5 legendary projects before that or something, and it's once a story... honestly, it seems like it's totally broken, but I suspect no-one's going to be doing it* unless they've got something like 600xp already, or their character is rubbish, because they sunk all their xp into getting an xp-generating combo that doesn't go anywhere.

    *I've just read the post above this, and it sounds like someone has done it, but by cheating. I've only ever seen one legendary project in-game, I've never seen anyone do more than that.

    Still...
    I feel that any new craft charm set should do away with any ability to buy XP with craft XP. And also significantly drop the number of craft charms as the OP has suggested.
    This does seem pretty reasonable.

        Originally posted by Meianno Yuurei View Post

        I teach someone 10xp of stuff with Flowing Mind Prana. The story ends. I roll 10 dice and get 6 successes. I have 4xp committed left. Without TLM, I cannot recover more than 10xp, period.

            Originally posted by Kelly Pedersen View Post

            Hmm. That suggests to me that there needs to be more incentives to use the slots you've got.... One thing I definitely like the idea of is some effects that give you bonuses for having lots of ongoing projects, and others that let you commit more slots to a single project. I think either effect illustrates the idea that slots are best described as your "focus" in craft.

            The idea of filling multiple slots also suggests a way at least one of the "free craft XP" charms could be rebuilt - make it so that you get craft XP based on the number of currently-occupied project slots you have at the end of a scene/session/story. That would encourage players to keep multiple projects going at once.
            It's probably something worth considering, yes.

            I'd be a bit careful with rewarding people for keeping multiple projects open, as it might encourage them not to actually finish them, but it's worth exploring.



            Can the "100 basic projects a day" thing be mitigated by compressing them, whenever possible, into a major project? I know that's pretty much canonical for, say, making 100 arrows or knives or something, but even if they're making a teddybear, and then a bauble, and then a knife, and then mending a pot, etc., etc., can you just say "if you're spending the whole day crafting, you just need a major slot, and you get one reward"?
            That's how I've house-ruled it, but the rules don't really seem to talk about it.



            Although, how difficult is it to find a quickie basic project you can do? Or two, or three, if you can only satisfy one objective with each? It seems to me that if you've got enough time for finishing a major project to be helpful, you've probably always got time to throw in a few basic projects first to build up the silver XP to get started. That goes whether or not you've got Craftsman Needs No Tools, since it speeds up both basic and major projects, so you'd still have time, I'd think.
            The very first session I ran of 3rd ed, the Twilight with Craft (Weaponsmith) 5 wanted to forge himself a good-quality sword. That's pretty obviously a major project (it's one of the examples). It would take him 5 minutes with Craftsman Needs No Tools.
            Technically, he couldn't do it. Yeah, I could have said "go do 3 basic projects, each one only takes 5 seconds) but I didn't see any value in making him think of several basic projects, roll the dice, etc. Essentially, it's just odd, versimilitude-wise, and slows the game down.
            I mean, I don't think that Morke and Holden actually wrote the system with the idea that people would go "hmmm, I need to do a major project, I need to think of several basic projects I can shoe-horn into this scene." I think the whole point of the objectives was that you're supposed to do stuff that actually matters. (Which makes it even more awkward to shoe-horn into a scene just so you can get enough silver for a major project).

            In the end, I just house-ruled that he started with 20 silver xp to get him going. But I've seen this come up with other crafters too.


            That said, there might be room for a charm to get "emergency craft XP" - perhaps something that lets you craft a basic project that relates in some way to the project you actually want to make, and if you meet any objective, you automatically start the major project, ignoring the normal silver XP requirement. This could be extended too - it's probably not balanced to allow a "starter" basic project to work for a superior or legendary project, but I could see it "chaining", with the basic project allowing you to start a major, and successful completion of the major letting you start a superior, and finally superior lets you start on legendary.
            That seems reasonable.



            On the other hand, improving the rewards from the existing objectives seems like more fertile ground. The one about your own intimacies, for example, could give more craft XP based on the strength of the intimacy, and so could the one based on other people's intimacies toward you. I'm less sure about the "material benefit" one - that tends to be its own reward, so giving more craft XP based on the value of the reward seems a bit off. But there's probably something there. And an effect to give you more if you meet multiple goals sounds like it has potential.
            Those sound like good ideas. I don't think the one about meeting multiple objectives is necessary (there's already a big benefit, because you're already getting a fair bit of craft xp), but it's not problematic.

                Originally posted by Blaque View Post
                Solar Craft's main sins, to me, are a fewfold.

                1) It over-values some resources.
                Yes.

                2) It undervalues other things too. The extra craft xp Charms are not bad until you get to Peerless Paragon of Craft, which then more or less explodes the entire damned craft xp economy, invalidates every previous Charm, and to boot, kind of helps you circumvent ever doing things with rolls really to get craft xp again.
                Yes.

                3) A few of the Repair Charms have special highlight for being just really bad.
                Yes.

                4) The Power tree is actually not bad like, in powerwise. It lets Solars achieve pretty impressive feats of artifice. The issue is that I think while I like dice tricks, they kind of are insane about them. Things like triple-10s, buncha weird rerolls, the different double-Ns upgrade Charm.
                They're even more complex when combined. I had to write this to explain how to make a craft roll with 2 power charms:
                20 dice

                Flawless Handiwork Method (re-roll 10s)

                Bright-Forging Prana (1/story):

                [Supreme Masterwork Focus x3 (double 7s)

                Experiential Conjuring of the Void (1 success and 10 extra dice, all non-charm)

                Unbroken Image Focus (5 extra successes plus 1 per success rolled before double 9s, all non-charm)]

                So, in conclusion:
                Roll 20 dice with re-roll 10s

                7s, 8s and 9s are each worth 3 successes, 10s are worth 4

                Then roll another 10 dice with re-roll 10s and double 7s
                (so they're just worth 2 each this time)

                Now add 6 successes

                Now take away 4 (because the roll is difficulty 5)

                You need 100 total, and can't roll more than 6 times.


                And even a Charm that lets you get more Charms, which I gather is just asking for player decision paralysis.
                It's kind of good for people who just want to craft an artefact occasionally without buying a ton of charms. (It's once/story)
                I was going to buy it for my crafter, but then I realised that with 2 other charms I can make level 2-3 artefacts easily, and not just once a story.

                5) There's a general trend I think with above of over-complication in general actually. Like, Focus-Shifitng Arete
                Yes.

                6) There's a notable lack I think of cool "wiz-bang" effects. Craftsman Needs No Tools, Vice-Miracle Technique, Dual-Magnus Prana, and the such are cool powers. But there's not really so much there like those.
                Yes.

                I think this probably covers most of the issues.
