                        I think one of the big things the system needs to decide is, "How bad, to a Solar doctor, is a plague?" In 2e, a cholera epidemic was basically unmanageable by a Solar physician - or, more to the point, his Solar Medicine didn't allow him to do anything to manage it that a mortal couldn't already. Sure, you could cure a dozen people a day with confidence, but if the plague is sweeping across a decent-sized nation... well, it's not going to make much difference, is it? A Solar dropped into the middle of Black Death-ridden Europe is not, medically, going to be much help, at least pre-A Hood on Death.

                        (The Solar may rally the people, institute new sanitation standards, etc., but that's a function of rather different Charm trees, one imagines.)

                        The question, for me, is: to what extent is that the intent? Should I be able to protect my people from plague? It feels like the answer to this should be yes - so, all right, how? What does large-scale Solar medicine look like?

                        ***

                        Practically, if you're playing a Solar doctor, you have pretty decent odds of starting with the declaration, "I want to be House" - or insert characters from ER, etc. And House is interesting because he struggles with disease - because there's work involved in figuring out what's wrong and setting it to rights. Solar Medicine should definitely make that struggle easier, or make possible things that weren't previously... but in 2e, there's an awful lot of "and the problem is solved!"-type effects that don't really contribute to feeling like you worked for your victory.

                        There's definitely a place for that - splinting a broken leg should be pretty trivial. But there needs to be some more meaningful system of struggling with disease - perhaps, as previously suggested, something that requires you to hunt down the cure, or a Medicine Charm that tells you, "The city is befouled by bad air, because it's stuck in a valley; to alleviate this, let the wind blow through it once again." People viewing it from the outside should feel like you did something impossible with ease; actually doing it should still feel like you're working.

                        That's not really anything concrete, I guess, but it's where my mind goes.

                                                I think what's somewhat disappointing about Medicine, at least as previously presented, is that it is indeed person-to-person.                                                         Originally posted by John Mørke View Post
                                                        *taps chin* You don’t see this situation commonly in Creation. A Solar putting together a tent city to treat a plague would be a fairly monumental thing. Such a Charm would need to be a very close-proximity one, Solar-to-target enhancement, rather than an improvement of a large group. Such an event needs to retain the challenge in order to maintain drama, and the monumental feeling of what is happening. A Solar creates a bulwark of tents to stop Final Contagion. The withering darkness and the falling death are stayed at its border. Dare the Wyld Hunt burn these tents? Can the Solar possibly save everyone? Can her assistants possibly keep up with the tasks she has given them?
                                                        Right. Building a tent city as a bulwark against the Final Contagion should be as dramatic and engaging as raising an army to face off against the Hordes of the Last Ones.

                                                        My thoughts were more along the line of up to a half dozen-or-so assistants, however. Still, you wouldn't want Charms that just go "boom! you're awesome at medicine!" even to a half dozen folk, because that's also boring. I've given it some thought, and there's ways I think you could have interesting, engaging Charms for uplifting medical assistants.

                                                        For example, a Charm that improved assistants that have watched and helped you work, and have an Intimacy toward you (you've worked together for years and know each other's strong and weak points; now as a Solar, that synergy is even greater), and/or an Intimacy toward a specific patient (the patient's mother/son/husband stands by worried as you work; you show them some of your ways, and they take it to heart for the sake of their loved one). The former encourages you to build relationships with your long-term assistants, while the latter lets you leave capable nurses in your wake, making sure your patients survive even after you ride out of town.
