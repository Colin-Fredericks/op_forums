    Already wrote this in the Performance vs. Presence thread, but since it's relevant to the topic and since it looks like you're still reading this one, Morke...

    I want Presence to be about leadership and command, and Performance to be about emotion.

    Presence is about telling someone what to do. You can bark an order and the new recruits will obey and hold the line. You can whisper a threat your prisoner will tell you the location of the rebel base. You can demand that the other guy surrender or retreat, or that the king hear out the list of grievances you against his vizier instead of just throwing you out of the court. They'll do what you say, but you don't get to decide how they feel about it. Presence without another social skill to back it up can inspire respect and loyalty if things are going well...but if following your orders isn't working out well it can lead to mere obedience and simmering resentment.

    Performance is about telling someone how to feel. You can play music that breaks someone's heart or inspires love, or you can tell a joke that has the whole tavern laughing, or you can dance a dance so seductive that anyone who sees it starts to fall in love with you. Now, you don't get to decide what they do about those feelings: if your sonnet made the prince fall in love with you, he gets to decide whether to court you or have you kidnapped for his seraglio or whether to not act on his love because what he wants is less important than the political marriage he needs to make for the good of his city. His course of action is up to him, but he can't be a witness to your performance and NOT feel the love (or hate or grief or whatever emotional context you gave to the performance) you were trying to inspire.

    Now, as you can see, the two go together like peanut butter and chocolate. If you invest in both, you'll be way better at dealing with other humans.

    Note also: I want Performances to be rife with unintended consequences that make for good stories. If a PC singer is bandying about kickass supernatural melodies, she needs to worry about the Phantom of the Opera falling for her. If a PC is a poet, politicians will try to get her into their courts and writing complimentary things they can distribute. Princes will try to get you to write love songs to help them seduce their lovers, and then try to murder you when their lovers fall for you instead of the Prince. Your song inspiring the peasants to fury about their oppression might spark a riot, but unless you've got some Presence to back it up that riot is gonna be directionless fury and you might even get caught up in it. Your poem about the cruelties of slavery will make the Guildsman feel remorse...but he might commit suicide instead of freeing the slaves like you wanted. I want Performance to move people, but not always predictably.

    So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

            Okay, so I hate to continue with the prayer digression, but here is a thing that the discussion made me realize. I was envisioning a Zenith who leads the people in prayer of his favoured gods, either out of piousness or for leverage, and I'm thinking, this is a hugely important thing in the game world, but it feels like I'm just showboating for flavour if I do that, and why is that? Part of the problem with prayer, and why I think people (myself included) are clinging to what mechanics and effects we've seen for it in previous editions, is that it's really hard to envision what it does.

            Gods want prayer, and the game largely uses an "income model" to represent this, but unlike real income, players can't relate to prayer income at all, so there's no real sense of what it means if a god gets more or less prayer. Like, if a god loses 25% of their prayer, clearly they're going to be angry and worried, but is that an existential threat? Is it like with money where suddenly you're end up living way beyond your means because you can't unspool your expenses quickly enough? Gods don't need food or shelter, and aside from "powering charms" it's hard to know what they actually do with their prayer outlay, so it's very hard to connect this sort of thing to a real outcome. You could cross-reference Ambrosia and mote-regeneration rules against divine statblocks and plot out budgets and expenses and try to extrapolate from there, but I'm sure no writer has ever done that, so it's not going to necessarily produce good results.

            To someone with a congregation, the main value of prayer is leverage, but the fuzziness of it really complicates that from a player's perspective, because you know a god wants as much prayer as possible but you don't really have a handle on what it means if you can offer or threaten a source of worship. From a business perspective, you can't get within an order of magnitude of their BATNA unless you roll some dice and ask the ST to tell you, and that puts a lot of pressure on the ST, because they don't have any idea either.

            I don't know what the solution to this is, really. The best I can think of is to give some idea of what kind of Cult rating a god needs to sustain a particular lifestyle, and that's pretty handwavey and unsatisfying, not to mention that it'd be a bit esoteric for any book that's not focused on gods. But I do think this helps to explain why people are grasping for prayer mechanics and charms to an extent that they wouldn't for, say, food or money.

                            By no means am I an expert on Chinese culture and mythology. My knowledge comes from being Chinese. But given that the debate surrounding prayer, the Celestial Bureaucracy, and Jade Emperor as a comparison to Sol, I figured I'd chime in.

                            Originally posted by John Mørke View Post

                            No. The Unconquered Sun is highest in Heaven. He prays to no one.

                            Nor does the Sun peel onions, but your Solar is free to be onion king.
                            This. Very much this.

                            In Chinese culture, something like praying or bowing is only done by a subordinate to their superior. Full stop. Even in modern day China, you'd still see the same thing. I don't think you'd ever see a superior bow to their subordinate if they worked in the same company. The lower ranking employee is always the one who does it, it's a demonstration of respect to someone more powerful than you. And even when you have a situation where two people are bowing to each other, the person bowing more deeply is acknowledging that the other person is their superior. This happens quite a bit in the business world.

                            So no, the UCS would never ever pray or bow to anyone. It's virtually unheard of for the Chinese Emperors to do so, and since Heaven structure bares an uncanny resemblance to the Earthly one, the Jade Emperor would never be expected to bow his head, unless it was to the three Pure Ones but that's because they are superior to him. The only situations I could possibly expect a Chinese Emperor, Divine or not, to ever bow their head is either to someone who conquered them OR the person they are bowing to did a service so grand, so epic, that it thoroughly moved the Emperor to show that level of respect. In which case it would be expected that every one else present in the room would have to bow their heads even lower than the Emperor, because of positions of power and so on.

                            Now, if we're talking about Prayer as in Ambrosia, then yea maybe the UCS would hand it out, but it'd be the equivalent of handing out lucky money and you bet that the recipient would be groveling for all they're worth.
