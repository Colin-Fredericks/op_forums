                                                    Originally posted by Epimetheus
                                                    It just makes nonsensical because he hasn't turned his back on creation because he's still exalting these solars.
                                                    Converse to my idea that the Jade Prison only works while he rejects the Solars is my notion that the Prison was incapable of holding the Essences down when the Unconquered Sun finally decides that enough is enough. I certainly find it to reconcile the Jade Prison still being a thing in the setting with developer references to the idea that the return of the Solars is owed to the Sun.

                                                    In this model, a small number of them being around is a kind of mild lingering affection (predicated a bit on the fact that, despite the best efforts of the Sidereals, they weren't able to get all of them in time).

                                                    Originally posted by Epimetheus
                                                    It gives him too much say in the process
                                                    What does that have to do with freedom?

                                                    Originally posted by Epimetheus
                                                    So why does the process need to work with him alive.
                                                    I mean, from a Doylist perspective, I think the matter of writing it that way is not to address some logical consistency matter (which I think is in many cases one of the least interesting ways to write Exalted, see statements of why Alchemicals can't become akuma), and more because the idea of actually being chosen by a mighty god makes the subject matter more intimate and mythic. I think there was too much cynicism around the Second Edition depiction.

                                                    From a Watsonian perspective, I would see it less in terms of need and more in the idea that while the Sun lives, it's going to be a matter that is on his mind, which he wouldn't be liable to ignore or delegate even if he could. From that perspective, I actually see the difference less in terms of the Exaltations having a switch between manual and automatic, that will be flipped should the Sun not rise, but rather that they're a subject of such importance to him that his will on that subject can live on past the point of his actual death in the legacy of his Exaltations.

                                                    Originally posted by Epimetheus
                                                    it suddenly doesn't have to be a thing that interferes with the setting.
                                                    It still doesn't. Really, it only seems to if you insist upon a reading of the Unconquered Sun that excludes the possibility of some kinds of Exalted that you would want being in the setting.

                                                    Quite apart from the question of where in the Third Edition core you're getting this from, or why you would cling to Second Edition points that don't need to apply anymore, you really seem to be going in circles with this. People keep pointing out ways to characterize the Unconquered Sun that enable as diverse an array of Solar character backgrounds as possible, and they're just dismissed, or the talking point shifts. You want, what, an Unconquered Sun that is so righteous and infallible that he would never personally choose anybody that would have negative personal qualities, but you also want him to have allowed an automated process that not only does, but does with such consistency that it caused him to disown the Solars?
