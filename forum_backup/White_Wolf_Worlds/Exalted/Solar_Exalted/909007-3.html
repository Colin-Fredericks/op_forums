    A Solar is perfectly permitted to be as virtuous and great a leader as King Arthur, fighting to institute a just rule and defending his people while pushing back against vicious invaders. There is an unavoidable ambiguity in such a statement, however.

    (OTOH, that Gwrgi Garwlwyd is totally a Lunar.)

    EDIT: It's worth noting that a lot of characters in some of the pulp and fantasy stories that Exalted draws on are impressive people who are not without their virtues, but they're also not easily mappable to a notion of 'good'. (I'm thinking of Jirel of Joiry and the various protagonists of the Flat Earth stories, especially Azhriaz, for example. Hell, looking at the Three Kingdoms, Cao Cao can be as much a model for a Solar as the three heroes are, and it's not like the three heroes are terribly nice people.) None of this is to say that you can't play a character who is a decent person by modern standards, of course, but 'good guy' is a bit difficult to pin down in a meaningful and simple way.

                My take has generally been that Solars are heroes, and to me the litmus test is that some community or class of people somewhere, if they could hypothetically see the Solar's life and deeds, should be looking at your character and saying "she's my hero". Yes, that is incredibly subjective, because the things they admire just have to at least more than cancel out the things they don't admire. Just like when real-life people decide who to look up to.

                So a pirate-Solar might be admired by the people the West because he goes after the ships of the oppressive Realm, or he might be admired by other pirates because he is the best at pirate, and either group might ignore otherwise deplorable behaviors if a) they're visited on someone else and therefore easier to ignore and/or b) his greatness is so great that everyone can politely look the other way when he does bad stuff. And again, all the subjectivity there is a feature, not a bug. If you are a great figure but such a psychopath that even "your own people" (the ones who think you great) can't collectively, and hypothetically, look past it, to my mind you're an excellent candidate for Abyssal or Infernal Exaltation, but not a Solar one. Probably not a Celestial one in general.

                From my perspective Solars who don't meet this standard have either fallen off the path or are on their way to it, with all the drama that implies.

                I think this is at least consistent with how Solars have been presented in the game line, even if it's not the only way to play it. Can anyone think of a Solar who doesn't meet this standard? Even the oft-touted "Evil Solar" Havesh the Vanisher works, from the perspective of an oppressed pariah caste who think the fear he incites and death he brings to the oppressor castes is more than worth the immoral "fringe benefits" he gets along the way.

                                                    Isator Levi
                                                    There's evil and there's evil. Given their purview and purpose, it's hard to argue the vast majority of Abyssals and Infernals find it easy to be life-affirming heroes. I'd I'd say it's boring to want them to just be another flavor of Solar with black or green explosions instead of golden ones. They are, if heroes, vastly different sorts of heroes.

                                                    Abyssals are heroes of brutal and nihilistic expedience -- The Punisher is an Abyssal.

                                                    Infernals are heroes of twisted and eldritch causes -- Elric of Melnibone is an Infernal.

                                                    Then you can also have Infernals or Abyssals who reject their calling and seek to become pure, life-affirming heroes again. These are redemptive heroes. Darth Vader or Miltion's Lucifer.

                                                    For my part, I tend to have the vast majority of Abyssal Exalted be the horrible necromantic reapers of flesh they're mostly described as being. That makes the small divergent faction (which will likely include a PC or two) compelling.

                                                    Every last Green Sun Prince, meanwhile, is one of the fifty most horrible humans alive in Creation. At best, you have high-functioning sociopaths who can be reasoned with so long as your interests and theirs align and you plan to betray them before they betray you. If your Infernal Exalted don't feel like Apostles or the God-Hand from Berserk, they are very little like my Infernal Exalted. The Yozis, you'll recall, have only 50 Essences with which to turn Creation into Hell. They would be super selective about their recruiting.

                                                    That said, if I had a player who absotively had to play a Green Sun Prince, I would work with him to make the character some sort of Noble Demon or Magnificent Bastard. An anti-villain who could function in my generally quite heroic party.

                                                            I'll put my views simply: Can Solars be the good guys? Absolutely! Must they be the good guys? Absolutely not!

                                                            To elaborate, I'll use the circle I created for my rewritten version of Wu-Jian. The most straightforwardly heroic are the Dawn and Zenith, whose goals are to unite the gangs of the slums, thereby creating what will amount to a police force, and to promote trade and commerce between the islands and the West, reducing their need for the Blessed Isle and easing the tensions between the city folk and the other islanders. The Twilight just wants the Realm out before their greed destroys Wu-Jian's economy. The Eclipse is an actress and an infiltration expert, and the Night is a sort of Bronze Age Batwoman. Since there's no real law in the slums, she works on her own principles, and will kill when needed. For an example of morality we'd consider "off" she ruined the drug dealer whose product killed her mother. Not because he sold her drugs, but because he'd sold her tainted drugs. She hates that people need drugs to get by, but she's a slum kid; she understands the need. Basically, if you harm or cheat the gutter folk, she's sudden death. Offer an honest good or service, she has no trouble with you. You're a whore? Fine. Rob a client? Not fine. Hire a whore? Fine. Stiff one? Not fine. The one group she absolutely will come crashing down on every time? Slavers.
