If one group is required to be good, but no other group has this requirement, then any given member of the first group is more likely to be good than the other group. Lunars are not required to be good, and so are no better than a random sampling of the population as a whole. Solars are, and so don't represent the whole, but a pre-determined subset of the population. If were to point at the acts of a given Solar or a given Lunar, I am more likely to say the Solar is a good person and the Lunar is not. This goes with Solar and any other random Exalt.

            Originally posted by Chant View Post
            Second: you make a fallacious leap of logic that assumes that the converse of a statement is true, which an Intro to Logic level mistake. Specifically, you take the statement "most bad guys are Lunars" and turn it into "most Lunars are bad guys". I really don't like how hostile it makes me sound to point this out, but you can't do that if you're trying to make a logically consistent argument.
            I pointed out earlier that I'm not arguing the converse.

            (x)(Solar(x) -> Good(x)) has more or less been the argument, as "heroic" = "good" and all Solars are this definiton of heroic. Which means (x)(!Good(x) -> !Solar(x)), as a result. But that's a thing. It means that if you are a Solar, you are good. The logical result is you aren't good, you aren't a Solar. This in effect can be read as "Non-Solars are not good." By pinning down Solars as something, you define non-Solarness as including non-goodness to. Now yes, this is more complciated with necessary and sufficient instead of just true/false or likely. But it doens't change the fact that Solars apparently by the way their population is defined are always going to be people that are good, while no one else gets to claim this. And so SOlars in general are going ot be assumed to be doing the good thing, and everyone else is more easily made "villainous" as they have no sort of "Moral requirement so I don't risk folks going about being bad guys with no consequences".

            This combined with the likelihood above means that if I pick a random person, and they're bad, they're not a Solar. Apparently it's bad if Solars are evil. But anyone else is fine. They're not Solars and only Solars are defined as good as a requirement. In my view no sort of Exalt should have as a requirement a moral factor, period. The way to not have this issue at all is to have Exalts have the same "goodness" as a population as...the population of humans they come from.

            Originally posted by Chant View Post
            Third: you arbitrarily limit your analysis to Exalts. Aren't there bad guys who are gods, and heroic mortals, and raksha, etc. etc.?
            Sure, which is why I have through this thread said non-Solar entities. Becuase I do include raksha, gods and what have you in that, but Exalts are easier to point out since they glow different and in the case of Lunars, have numbers that are easier to compare with without speculation of headache inducing. And it still stands that Solars are apparently a unique group in requiring goodness to be a member of that group, while no other being has that, and therefore no other being gets to be as likely to be "the good guy" in a situation.

            Originally posted by Chant View Post
            Fourth: you seem to predicate your conclusion on the notion that good can only come into conflict with bad, and vice versa. ...
            Do I? I note outright that there's a good chance of good v. good fights. BUt the issue is that since non-Solars are more likely to not be the specific sort of good (good people doing good deeds with intent on chanigng the world into a better place) and people not necessariliy "evil" but not necessarily altruistic don't get to be "good" either, along with a greater amount of non-Solars than Solars total and they being subjected to more general population moral standards....totals to no matter how you math it out, SOlars are generally going to be Solar v. Not Good Guys more often than Solar v. Good Guy.

            I didn't do the math this morning since that requires me to sit and build the equations. But in a simple analogy, say i have two revolvers. One has five bullets in it's six chambers, the other has four This means if I fire both guns at a target, the likelihood that both fire is 27.7...%. This in a sense is like tow "good people" fighting. The likelihood of th efirst gun firing and the other one not however is 55.5...%. For completeness, both guns not firing is 11.1...% and the first not firing and the second firing is only 5.5...%.

            This extends with larger groups or smaller groups. When comparing two populations with uneven distribution of a trait, the one that has the higher trait is more likely to encounter whatever trait is higher in the second group. And since whatever the second group is is random or arbitrary, it is easy to skew or make what you want as you want. Which puts Solars at a moral advantage as they are requried to be better than the general population. And so conflicts with non-good groups are less likely because by the definiton given, most people aren't that good.

            So again, I don't reject the idea of good people don't conflict with good people. But the idea that one group is good and the rest arent' required to be causes problems I don't think are good for the game.

                            I selectively quoted a dev... in a post where I didn't quote a dev at all?

                            Also, your complete quote isn't complete. You quote two sentence from a multi-paragraph post. The complete quote (which you were quoting, not I) would be:
                            Solars, Lunars, and Sidereals occupy entirely different game-space in Exalted. Solars are myth-heroes. It is a myth-hero's purpose to subvert mythos and thereby define it. Solars fight, destroy, and ultimately recreate the status-quo, doing so by conquering the obliquely magical through acts of sheer will, force, and skill writ-large, like every myth-hero in literature. IE, a Solar built to win a foot race will win a foot race with the very Maiden of Journeys. The Solar will outrun Death itself.

                            Lunars are heart-eating, shapeshifting skin-walker legends and monsters. They turn into swift cats and soaring birds, fish that can swim and giant monsters that can rend down fortress walls. They are monsters and beasts. Compare them to the great monsters and beasts of legend. Less Hercules, more hydra and chimera. That is their game-space.

                            Sidereals are literally sorcerer-bureaucrats with kung fu. They are the most literal agents of myth that you can get. They are not subversive, but rather assimilationist. They occupy a radically different game-space than Solars.

                            Many members of both the Lunar and Sidereal Exalted have set themselves against the Solar Exalted. This will be an extremely interesting conflict. Just keep the different game-spaces in mind, because these spaces imply different meaning and purpose for all three Exalt types. This will be reflected in their Charms (and always has been).
