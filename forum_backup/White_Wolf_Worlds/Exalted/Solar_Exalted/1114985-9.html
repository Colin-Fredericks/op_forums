    Originally posted by Elkovash View Post
    The way I see it. Long ago when UCS was active in the world prior to the War and then during the first deliberative, it would not have been inconceivable for entire nations to honor him specifically and thus lots of iconography from that time bears the sun's image - but;

    On the turn of the age, when UCS turned his back on Creation and the deliberative was overthrown, all of mortal societies still worshiping him directly would have found their prayers unanswered or delegated to some other divine intermediary. So over thousands of years of no direct contact with the sun the lesser stations gained prominence as they were more immidiately accessible (and possibly willing to interfere) - this the decline of UCS worship is like a learned process over many generations which slowly reduces the iconography of the sun directly and leaves his dedicated temples empty.

    You'll still get fringe cases where some people still worship him directly, but either their prayers are never answered or (in the rare case of sufficient gravitas to require heavenly attention) superlative delegation has someone else showing up at your temple or (more likely) the problem is quietly fixed with no attribution to a being whatsoever.

    Couple all that with a deliberate project by the immaculate order and it's puppeteers to quash established worship as a religious tool unless your hands and soul are worthy enough, and the population of creation slowly veers away from the old brilliant god, still know he is there, but perhaps less immidiately relevant. Heaven is a bureau, after all, and even if you write a letter to the CEO, or President or whatever leader there may be, how likely is it, do you think, that they would even get to read it let alone know you sent it? On the off chance you get an answer, the letter is typed and a simulacrum of their signature is printed on the bottom.
    I think that that's the most plausible way to do it. When the Sun doesn't answer your prayers while the local forest goddess does (and sends wood spiders to attack your livestock if you don't pray to her), over time, people are just going to worship the Sun less and less.
    Plus, of course, the Immaculate Order gets upset over you worshipping local gods, but they get really upset over you worshipping the UCS.
    So... why bother.
    It's not that no-one ever worships him, there's probably small sun cults and so forth, it's just rare.

    And I'd guess forgotten means in the sense that maybe if you ask a scholar of religion, they might be able to tell you that the sun has a god called The Unconquered Sun, but your average peasant couldn't name him (whereas they probably could name the main gods worshipped by their culture, and some local terrestrial Gods). It's not that the idea of the Sun having a god is radically strange to them, it's just... well, who cares? It's not very important. He's the god of the sun, not the god of peasants.

        Originally posted by AnubisXy
        People in the real world often take their religious writings extremely literally and believe them to an incredibly high degree. Last time I checked, even today nearly half of Americans believe in a the literal Biblical account of the world's creation, flatly rejecting any idea that contradicts it. And, of course, if you were to go back in time to say, 10th century Europe you would find far, far more people taking the words of the Bible as literal truth.
        This is a far more recent idea than you're suggesting. People from the 5th century spoke of Genesis being allegorical. It was the Catholic Church's position at the time… and still is!
        And 3rd century.

        Specifically, the Catholic Church had (and has) no official position on the literalism of Genesis. Medieval Christians generally weren't that fussed about how literal the Old Testament was, since they assumed virtually every single bit was symbolic of Jesus (they lived in a very symbolic society after all, which until the 12th century was relatively uninterested in how things actually physically worked), and that was the important bit.
        Though they were very keen that the universe literally had a beginning, and hadn't existed infinitely (because that's what a lot of Hellenic pagans argued).

        So, anyway, to get back to Exalted, I wouldn't assume they are as interested as we are in what is literally true. Our culture (well, this has been a rising theme since the early 18th century) is, in historical terms, one that's very focused on literal truth (though perhaps less since post-modernism).

        Not to say that I'd expect people in Exalted to live in an entirely symbolic way, but I'd expect them generally to be less interested in what is literally going on in Yu-Shan. The details of the Sun God might be an idle topic of conversation, or of interest to scholars, but it's not generally something your average peasant cares about. The sun works without prayer, so it's not something worth bothering about.

                                            First, thank you to people that provided the information that confirmed that I was wrong about the Order's view towards the Sun! I looked up old 1e books even, and it's clear that the Usurpation not only involved the murder of the Solar Exalted, but the wholesale abolishment of the dominant world religion! Here is a relevant quote...

                                            Unconquered Sun: Mightiest among the gods, the Unconquered Sun was their leader when they rebelled against those who came before the gods. He created the Solar Exalted and advised them when they ruled the world during the First Age, until the Solars’ overweening pride caused him to turn away from them. His temples were pulled down after the murder of the Solar Exalted, and his worship was banned. Today, the veneration of the Unconquered Sun is practiced only by savages and isolated bands of heretics.
                                            So, proceeding from the premise that I know I was wrong according to the canon, I want to briefly discuss why I think this is really, really silly and poorly thought though.

                                            First, let's examine the political reality of the situation. The Usurpation itself is a hugely impacting event. It's gotta be shocking that the Solar Exalted were murdered, but maybe we can assume that'd not be overly chaotic because they'd already made such a scandalous reputation for themselves that more or less the population isn't that sad to see them go.

                                            But then we get into the widescale dismantling of the dominant faith of a quasi-modern mother society with population numbers in the BILLIONS and near universal acceptance of this faith. And, frankly, it becomes... difficult to envision success.

                                            Think of it this way - Let's say the Pope and all of his Cardinals are proven to be guilty of widespread and monstrous abuses. Let's even suggest the most favorable situation possible to the Usurpers and assume that knowledge of these crimes is widespread and commonly known. So not only is the Pope and all these high church officials guilty, everybody KNOWS they are guilty and is sick and tired of them.

                                            Under these circumstances it's perfectly reasonable and even perhaps desirable for an insurgent faction to remove these individuals and punish them on behalf of the people! But... can anybody honestly envision a situation where based on this, CHRISTIANITY AS A WHOLE would be dismantled? Keeping in mind that 99% of the population is devout Christian?

                                            I'd imagine the odds of even an ATTEMPT would be low... because it would be immediately seen as absurd! That is not how religion works! And even if such an attempt were made, it would be effectively impossible to succeed in any reasonable interpretation, and would almost certainly fail spectacularly.

                                            That's why I'm personally enamored with the idea that the Dragonblooded never forbade Sol worship. Why would they? They already have a stated position that the Solar Exalted are actually demon sorcerers who usurped power that didn't belong to them, so what is so bad about Sol worship that it needs to be abolished? The cost in political and social capital would have been immense for practically no benefit whatsoever.

                                            In addition, as other posters have pointed out better than me, the story of the Anathema relies upon the Celestial Gods as a key element. It seems counter intuitive to suppress knowledge of a key aspect state propaganda.

                                            I really think a more 'realistic' modern situation would have the entire Celestial Pantheon being worshiped, even perhaps given a festival day. All the better if the new Immaculate interpretations of these deities are interwoven culturally to the enshrined power structures of the Realm today - like for example, perhaps during the festival they have mockups of the Dragons and the Celestial Gods having tea together and reinforcing the idea of how good and perfect the Immaculate Order is.

                                            But of course, I do acknowledge I am completely wrong! After people corrected me here and got me digging at old books, I found that I was misinformed. I hope I've made a case for why I think my ideas weren't totally stupid however.
