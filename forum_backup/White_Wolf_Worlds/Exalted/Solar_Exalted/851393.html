    What is the Solar advantage in 3ed?

    03-22-2016, 06:30 PM
    So I have been looking at forums recently and it occurs to me that I don't see the advantage of Solars in 3ed... I am going to list my reasons for this assumptions in the hopes that someone can point out the mistake in my reasoning. I am generally trying to figure out why the other Exalted would serve the Solars in the First Age if they were not particularly powerful.
    -Sorcery does not really seem to be it anymore as it requires essence 5 which was supposedly absurdly rare even in the First Age now...
    -Evocations might be it but details are so scarce on them that I don't quite know how to define the Solar 'mastery' other than the ability to use several materials (given the required commitment that seems pretty trivial).
    -I have seen the opinion many times that a combat-spec'd Full Moon can defeat a combat-spec'd Dawn on the forums.
    -Solar's CAN use SMA but they cannot eclipse (no pun intended) Sidereal mastery.
    So that really only leaves Supernals (dubious considering that a Solar will face a near-universal disadvantage in terms of combat experience (not XP), Wyld-shaping and crafting as the main Solar advantages I see left...
    So ya I would appreciate it if someone could clear up where I am getting it wrong.

                                              Originally posted by Gigaton-Falcon-Emu View Post
                                              why the other Exalted would serve the Solars in the First Age if they were not particularly powerful.
                                              Solars are still the mightiest of the Exalted.

                                              Part of being so mighty entails not really having any areas that their form doesn't allow them to be very powerful at, and having means of being competitive and victorious against beings that are mightier than they are.

                                              You look at some of the other Exalted in terms of character concepts and methodologies towards certain goals, and there's always something that doesn't quite fit for them. That might be about the power of their Charms, or it might be in some other quality in how they function; a notable part of Alchemicals, for instance, is the need to place themselves in the hands of mortal technicians for their Charms to be switched around or their traits to be improved.

                                              That's an answer to the question of how, when all of the Exalted are swirled around in the melting pot of the Primordial War and the early First Age, and possibly the Time of Tumult, the Solars come out on top.

                                              For the question of why follow the Solars throughout the First Age?

                                              They're really good at running a civilization.

                                              Not to disregard the Terrestrial Exalted, but that they're the least powerful Exalted is generally accepted as a matter of course.

                                              So if we're considering other groups to run the show... how are the others really acting in the same zone?

                                              The matter of Solars as the Lawgivers of humanity is more complex and holistic than the Bureaucracy Ability, but it can be examined as a way of comparing how suited they all are in the area of running things.

                                              Solars apply Bureaucracy through the lens of glorious culture heroes, so they're really good at running things in complex and nuanced ways, aimed at things like getting projects through efficiently and making institutions adhere to a central thesis statement and the like.

                                              Sidereals interpret Bureaucracy through the lens of esoteric mystics dedicated to enforcing the plans of Heaven, so it affords them powers to do things like surgically eliminate problem individuals or issues, or strike down spirits who are contravening the Celestial Order.

                                              Lunars don't do Bureaucracy at all. That's now just a matter of them not having Ability Charms (although that helps); the Lunar image and structure doesn't really extend itself to the management and regulation of complex institutions. A Lunar, as an individual, might be involved in such a thing, maybe even apply some of their Charms in the course of it, but I don't think there are really Lunar Charms built around the concept.

                                              You might sometimes get an Exigent who'll be suited to the task of king of the universe, but Exigents come and go; I don't think you can really build something that lasts around the assumption that a particular type will be there to sustain it.

                                              Being a Lawgiver, a stand out figure who guides and preserves the entirety of human civilization, has more to it than just running things, but all of what that "more to it" entails is not just what Solars do, they are things that they excel at.

                                              You've also got to look at them in terms of characterization beyond their powers; as people who have been Chosen by particular kinds of gods. You might get a Lunar who, in a straight up duel, is capable of defeating a Solar warrior of approximately the same power. Read the description of the Dawn Caste and consider what kinds of people consistently get Chosen as them; whatever the merits of a given Lunar who eventually turns into a Full Moon Caste, are they necessarily people who are cut from the same cloth?

                                                      The Solar Advantage is having high-competency in all fields. His charms, for the most part, will add more dice, more successes, remove more failures, reroll more non-successes, etc, compared to his peers. He has a better excellency, and though excellencies are the least efficient of all his charms, he has access to charms that can give him situational free excellencies, or in the case of Melee, highly incentivize and pay-back for going for a full excellency. Other splats will do better than the Solar in certain fields (Lunars being more durable, having better ox-bodies, having charms to snap-cast spells when they dismiss a form, Sidereals can do some crazy nonsense with TN changing), but the Solar has high competence in all fields, and in many, is unsurpassed when he pursues them to their absolute peak.

                                                      Other splats will not have the Supernal. They will, from what Devs have said, have something that takes the role of the Supernal, but is not the Rocket to E5 At Chargen that Solars get. Thus, at Char-gen, Solars can access incredibly powerful end-game charms in their chosen field right off the bat.

                                                      Supernals are absurdly powerful. Take a Brawl Solar for example. Rush your way to Orichalcum Fists of Battle, and you've created someone who, though incredibly monofocused, can sidestep any and all issues of soak and no longer cares about building up initiative, save as a buffer from being crashed. A Solar could, at E1, have the entirety of a Martial Arts already learned, giving him a powerful offense and defense in many cases, while now free to spend all future charms on diversifying. A Solar has early access to Essence-Upgrades in his Supernal, as well; whilst an effect that adds (essence) to soak-penetration, for example, still uses his true essence, there are many charms that simply just get automatically better at essence breakpoints.

                                                      To put this in a Fluff Perspective; a Dragonblooded Fire Aspect studies with the blade for decades. Alongside it, he learns the art of maneuvering courts, bending others to his will with words, leading men into battle, and otherwise expanding his repertoire of skills. At the age of 200, he could consider himself quite the grandmaster with a blade. Few Dragonblooded could be considered his peers in the field of battle when he pulls his jade sword from its sheathe.

                                                      A freshly made Dawn matches him blow for blow, and even exceeds his talent. Though the Dawn has none of his oratory prowess, knowledge of war and strategy, or even his fiery speed as they leap from tree to tree and fight from shore to shore, a cold sweat beads on the back of his neck as he realizes this fresh-made stripling is BETTER than him with the sword, with only months of practice, where he's been swinging his sword for two centuries. And if he let the Solar age and diversify...too terrifying to think of. Best kill them now. But then the new Solar emerges from Exaltation as one of the greatest Archers in living memory. Or able to flick their wrist and bury a hundred knives in a hundred throats. Or finding their mind near-bursting with perfect battle-plans and stratagems, able to turn peasant rabble into the best mortal soldiers possible in weeks.

                                                      And that's why Solars are scary. With Supernals in play, they enter the scene able to compete with elders in a single field of their choosing. Yes, they don't have the motepool to last in an endurance match. Yes, they won't have the breadth of ability and a library of charms large enough to answer every situation. But that Presence-Focused Zenith could turn a city against you in the week of his Exaltation. The Night will be harder to find than a single grain of sand in the desert as she slips through your home and steals your secret. Their Eclipse will spend days in the courts and know every single persons secrets, motivations, and desires. Their Twilight will already be able to craft rare wonders the world hasn't seen in millenia as if it were as natural as their own breathing. And they will cover each others weaknesses. And they'll only get stronger and shore up more of their weaknesses in time.

                                                      Supernals are NOT a dubious advantage. Beyond the sheer strength of the Solar charm-library, it's arguably their most powerful current ability.

                                                          I think another, simpler way of looking at it is: Solars have Ability Charms that are dedicated to being really good at those Abilities. Sidereals have Ability Charms that are dedicated to being really good at evoking a set of obscure parables. There are some really impressive Charms amidst that group, and have all kinds of significant circumstantial uses, but in a situation where you're all about wanting to be really good at running things or knowing things or investigating things, well... a Sidereal may or may not be out of their depth (although one of the advantages they can cultivate is being able to foresee and avoid situations in which they'll be out of their depth, so it doesn't necessarily hurt them in a way that would interfere with what they're made for). Hell, even something as simple as not being hit by things... Sidereals probably won't have exactly the same Dodge Charms as in First Edition, but if we're analysing in terms of principles then you could find something in the idea that a Sidereal who wants to stay in a fight and not get hit in the fight and is good at dodging has Duck Fate and not much else (and if that Charm is anything like the former perfect defences of the Solars, it can't exactly be used with impunity). Think about that; Dodge is designated in the corebook as an Ability important to combat, so what differences in heroes in combat do you suppose the difference in the Solar Dodge cascade and somebody relying on Absence and Duck Fate produces? None of that undermines the power of Avoidance Kata or Neighbourhood Relocation Scheme, but that doesn't make it a context in which they're relevant.
