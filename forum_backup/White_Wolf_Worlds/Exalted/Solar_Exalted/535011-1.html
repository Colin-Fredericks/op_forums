            Gonna disagree with you both from play experience and from the fiction that Exalted draws on for inspiration: a number of Charms, spells, and artifacts destroy mundane weapons, from Octavian's Equitable Resolution destroying any mundane weapon that parries it, to Abyssals' Broken Toys Riposte, to Earth Dragon Style and DB Craft Charms… heck, I'm not a gambling man, but I'd bet the number of Exalt types that have at least one native Charm to fuck up your non-artifact weapon is greater than the number of Exalt types that don't, and I'm not even gonna count Solars' Heavenly Guardian Defense in that spread!

            You know who was an awesome pulp-style adventurer who didn't rely on an artifact weaponry? Conan, fuck yeah. His weapons tend to take a lot of abuse; the combination of the strength of his attacks (often when delivering a stroke he ends up splitting his victim's skull to the teeth, and sometimes shears all the way down to the breastbone) and his tendency to fight tough fights (the opponent is armored, or supernaturally resilient, or as skilled a swordsman as he) tends to result in some pretty bad damage to his weapons. They're often described as heavily-notched or otherwise suffering from some integrity problems; hell, in The Phoenix on the Sword, the titular sword shivers to bits after he smashes it on someone's helmet, and he fights the rest of his mortal opponents with an axe. Oftentimes he loses or is relieved of a weapon, and he never bothers retrieving it - the entirety of Rogues in the House takes place after Conan was jailed, and the whole time his only proper weapon is a knife he takes off his jailer's corpse (though at times in the story he fights with other things, including a joint of beef, and once, a stool); he leaves town after that, so whatever weapon he used prior to his imprisonment is left behind.

            I'm not saying everyone should be like Conan. That's not the point of this post. But weapons break. Fighting with one puts it under a lot of stress. It's one thing to imbue an object with sentimental value, and another thing entirely to expect to finish out a decades-long military career with the same weapon you started with intact at the end of it.

            Have a primary weapon, that's fine. But you're smart to carry a spare.

                            Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                            Second, GSS and its equivalents don't just summon a sword or armor - it's an artifact. Your spear won't resemble a wood-and-steel weapon, it is a shaft of molten sunlight, an orichalcum dire lance. Perhaps if your bond is with one mundane weapon in particular it might look like an idealized artifact version of such, cast in glorious sun-metal, but at minimum that means it's probably quite a bit bigger.

                            Third, barring some Ship of Theseus type replacement, I doubt an ordinary mortal's spear is going to last that long if she's seeing combat anywhere close to regularly for years and years.
                            I'm going to take the opposite point of view here.

                            GSS makes artifacts sure, but who says the artifact has to be huge. They typically are, that is the style of the game, but I don't see it as a requirement. Given the backstory here, I think it is very appropriate for the GSS to manifest as a duplicate of her well-known spear. With use, she may find that her new GSS is far more effective than her old weapon ever was though (it has artifact stats despite its appearance)

                            The Solar in the story has only been exalted for a month. She may have been in a large number of battles but most likely few were against supernatural foes. In fact the only really powerful supernatural foe she has faced at this point may have been the one she exalted against. A mortal spear could last through any number of fights against mortal or low power enemies, provided it is well maintained and repaired. This is a piece of fiction based on a game, not real life. I think most storytellers would at most require the occasional craft role to repair the weapon. Fun trumps realism when it comes to things like gear maintenance. The story does specifically say the spear has been repaired before.
