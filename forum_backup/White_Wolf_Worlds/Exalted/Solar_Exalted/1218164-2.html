                Originally posted by Clownpiece View Post
                IFotD is already stupidly strong, and stormcaller lets you crash someone with a cheap double 7s to damage withering attack

                Yeah, but you don't actually gain any initiative from that, except for the crash bonus... I mean, yeah, 6 extra initiative is nothing to sneeze at, but it's basically +1 damage die on each of your invincible flurry attacks. You're basically paying 2m 1WP for a benefit that is nearly on par with the 3m version of Increasing Strength Exercise, except the latter is scenelong and doesn't eat up your precious willpower (which you already need for flurries, Perfect Strike Discipline and TAP).

                Which is not to say that the ability to strip a fuckton of initiative off of someone without gaining it isn't without its uses (great way to deal with artifact heavy armor, for example, since it nearly guarantees a crash and therefore shuts down their pesky Hardness; also a good panic button if you know your opponent is going to decisive you next turn and you need to reduce their damage pool ASAP) - but in the overwhelming majority of cases, you're better off just making a regular attack enhanced by FSS if your goal is to load up on initiative (dump 3m into FSS and you'll have a minimum post-soak damage pool of 8, which averages out to 4i, +1 from hitting - the difference between this and FEF is a marginal 1i).


                Originally posted by Clownpiece View Post
                IFotD is already stupidly strong, and stormcaller lets you crash someone with a cheap double 7s to damage withering attack, then IWA with Hungry Tiger and Sleeping Devil, Wake. That's right, it's supplemental. 5m to add [intensity/2] damage to all attacks and gain 1 intensity for every 10 on your damage rolls. you hit 10 intensity very fast

                See, this is the part where our opinion seems to diverge. I mean - okay, cool, you can Hungry Tiger because they're crashed, awesome; but do you really expect to always have the resources to toss a full excellency on top of every attack? If not, Hungry Tiger honestly isn't much better than FSS in this case, a solid +3 damage on every attack (assuming your opponent has some way to deal with the mounting wound- and onslaught penalties - otherwise, yeah, Hungry Tiger adds up over time). Now, let's say you had an average join battle roll on a 20d pool, putting you at 13 initiative right out of the gate, and you crashed your opponent earlier (19i; let's round up to 20) - now you're gonna make five attacks with 4 (init) +1 (IFoD) +3 (Hungry Tiger) = 8 damage each. Let's also toss an E3 ISE on top of this for another +3 damage, for 11 total (which is starting to strain believability, given that you somehow managed to crash your opponent despite the fact that you've given him at least one free turn to build his initiative while you were powering up). This is a grand total of 55 damage - a very generous assumption, given that it's extremely unlikely that all of your attacks will hit against a peer-level opponent, but let's run with it to balance out that some Hungry Tiger results will produce significantly more than +3 damage. This nets you, on average, +5.5 intensity. Even if we factor in that intensity gained in the middle of the sequence adds even more damage to subsequent attacks, one flurry is extremely unlikely to get you to intensity 10. Intensity 6 on the back of one flurry plus FEF is reasonable.

                Now only one question remains: you just pumped 55 dice of damage into your opponent. To hit intensity 10, you'll need another flurry for similarly ridiculous numbers. What the hell is your built-up intensity gonna be good for, after having dealt over 100 dice of damage already?

                    Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post
                    <hungry tiger stuffs>
                    Now only one question remains: you just pumped 55 dice of damage into your opponent. To hit intensity 10, you'll need another flurry for similarly ridiculous numbers. What the hell is your built-up intensity gonna be good for, after having dealt over 100 dice of damage already?
                    But forcing that intensity up is easier than it looks, to be frank... You have to land decisives at init 15.
                    Get gangbanged at init 15 with Ready in 8 Directions.
                    Or IWA at init 15 that's 5 attacks at init 15. SD,W to improve on that.

                    Let's look at a dead start iwa+sw,d from a "sword saint". With a stunt 1 you're throwing 13 dice on your attacks and spent 10m, 2wp.
                    Against defense 7, that's a hit 48% of the time.
                    Against 6, 64% hit chance.
                    Against 5, 79%
                    Against 4, 90%
                    Against 3, 96%

                    Suppose the enemy is seriously scared of being hit and is pumping his defense to 10 every time and has an onslaught negator like bulwark stance (doesn't get much better than this!).
                    5 hits at 11% hit chance either. About a 41% chance to land one hit, and don't expect another. Your opposition spent at least 30m plus the onslaught negators' essence. To maybe get hit once anyway.

                    Without an onslaught negator you might as well quit. The 5-hit sequence against defense 12 goes 3%, 6%, 11%, 20%, 33%. Slightly worse off and almost twice as expensive.

                    With an excellent strike, since it is so cheap when you are throwing 65 attack dice, those numbers change to...
                    Against a passive 7er: 75%, 87%, 94%, 98%, 100%
                    Against a bulwarked 10er: 5 hits at 29% each (83% chance to hit once, 47% to hit twice)
                    Against a passive 12er: 10%, 18%, 29%, 44%, 60%


                    That dead-start Excellent IWA+SD,W has 4 damage dice per hit (this is the real defense against the cheap combo, hardness means you gotta pay up to land any of this). Against the kind of enemy you can rely on this setup (no bulwark, 3- hardness) you will likely build about 10 intensity on the round.

                    From that point onward the excellent iwa+sd,w will work from a baseline of 18 dice, 1 success rather than 13 dice, 1 success. Even a bulwarked 12er is going to get hit a couple times and you just got yourself a nice defensive -3 to all enemy actions.



                    Dueling another sword saint this isn't really viable. Add squishier enemies and suddenly you can get that tasty -3 storm right away, which greatly viabilizes dragging the fight a little and gives you juicier targets for that sheath.
