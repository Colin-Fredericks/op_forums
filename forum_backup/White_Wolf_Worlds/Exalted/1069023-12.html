    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
    Our vision of Lunars is very much built off the vision you see of them in the corebook. It's an incredibly solid foundation that our own personal visions our built upon. I'm going to ramble a bit about my how I see them and how that might inform their Charms, although of course, nothing's final?

    What are the Lunars (besides, you know badass shapeshifters who've earned the blessing of the witch-goddess Luna)? Lunars are divine apex predators. The theme of predation is already deeply rooted in them—there's a reason they need to consume their prey to take on their form, at least, at first—and it's reflected in their Charms. Whether it's dancing between shapes as you tear a Wyld Hunt to shreds, ruthlessly pursuing your political agenda in a court of scheming nobles, or hunting down the forbidden secrets of a god exiled to the borders of the world, you are on the hunt.

    But, of course, just because Lunars are predators doesn't mean they're limited to violence and consumption. Lone wolves rarely thrive. A Lunar who wants to engage with a society, whether it's a dominion that she rules, a cult that worships her as a living god, or just some people she wants to help, is going to do so in the manner of a predator conferring its assistance to prey animals. She might become a sacred guardian, stalking the bounds of a city to rip out the throat of any devil or ghost that dares harm her flock. She might be a heartless predator that culls the herd of its weak to make it stronger, taking on a terrifying devil aspect to temper a society. She might be the raven who steals the secret of fire, returning to society bearing the gift of wisdom. I refer to this as their theme of Symbiosis.

    Also, their badass shapeshifting asskickers chosen by the witch-goddess Luna. Can't forget that!


    Firstly, congrats! I'm looking forward to seeing what you do with the title.

    Secondly, in regards to this vision for Lunars... will they be able to do anything better than Solars?

    Shapeshifting is their main schtick, and I understand that noone else can match them in that regard, but... it doesn't really give them an edge except in a narrative/stylistic/book-fluff kind of way. Especially not compared to (in 2E, at least) Sidereal Martial Arts, or Dragon Bloods unrestricted charm use (interesting to see what takes it's place in 3E).

                    Originally posted by Crumplepunch View Post
                    Last I heard (and this was more than a year ago) Holden was still contemplating bringing back Aspect surcharges
                    Y'know, this might or might not be thematically appropriate or necessary, but I've always felt that the Dragon-Blooded and, for that matter, the Sidereals could use some psychological sleight-of-hand in this area.

                    People react better to the idea of a perceived bonus than they do to a perceived penalty even if the end result is the same.

                    I'm going to step way, way back into the dark ages of video game design here, like 2004, when World of Warcraft was in beta testing. Something the designers and testers were concerned about deeply, because of some of the crazy stuff they'd heard about Everquest players, was folks not playing the game until they, you know... died. So they came up with the idea of having "normal" experience gain for awhile after logging in, and then afterwards your PC would end up "fatigued" and gain XP at a slower rate to encourage folks to maybe call it a day and go do something else.

                    People hated it. Hated hated hated it.

                    So you know what the designers did? They re-named the fatigue state "normal" and presented it as the base level, and re-named "normal" as "rested" and presented it as a bonus. Precisely the same system. Just with different names and presentation.

                    People either loved it or were indifferent.

                    So in the context of the Dragon-Blooded... if out-of-aspect surcharges are a things the devs still want to do, you don't present them as out-of-aspect surcharges. You do not say "You pay normal costs for all Charms in your Aspect, and a one-mote surcharge for all Charms outside it." No no no. What you do is say "You pay normal cost for all Charms... except for Charms in your Aspect. On those Charms you get a one-mote discount. Think of the savings!"

                    Precise same system. Different branding.

                    If that had been the way the system were presented from the beginning, from 1e DB, I can almost guarantee that instead of fighting a fifteen-year war against it, people would be fighting a fifteen-year war for it. You'd have people saying "My boys in the Fire Aspect better still be getting their Melee discounts!" Folks would be asking anxiously if it were being removed or not.

                    By a similar token... both the Dragon-Blooded and Sidereals have traditionally had "mandatory minimums" on Abilities and sometimes on Charms. "You get X number of points to spend... but you must spend some of them on these specific things." That comes across as a restriction, a constraint on your freedom; "Man, I could totally get this other Ability I actually want, but instead I'm being forced to spend these dots in Archery. Stupid Dragon-Blooded society and their demand everyone in it demonstrate minimal martial competence."

                    Don't do it that way. Instead do it along the lines of "You get all these Abilities... for free! Yes, for free! Our gift to you. No no, thanks aren't necessary. And on top of that, you get all these points to spend as you like on top of your free stuff!"

                    It's all about perception and presentation.

                    "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."

Dev thoughts on that approach would also be very welcome.
