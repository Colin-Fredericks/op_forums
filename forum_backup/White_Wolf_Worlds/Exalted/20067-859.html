                                    08-24-2020, 12:59 AM
                                    Originally posted by Grod_the_giant View Post
                                    Ran into this issue tonight:
                                    Yeah, these are all issues with the naval combat system as written. It really doesn't do a very good job at handling what happens when someone besides the captain wants to get involved in the fight before a boarding manuever is achieved. My best advice would be to find one of the naval combat houserules out there that you like, and use that instead. That said, in an attempt to address these roughly within the naval combat system as written:

                                    Originally posted by Grod_the_giant
                                    When the Athletics supernal wants to use Mountain-Crossing Leap Technique to bypass the whole momentum system and start a boarding action all by themselves? [...] What happens when some of your players jump on Agata and fly directly to the enemy ship, while the last guy continues to make broadside/ram attacks?
                                    For these two, I would suggest in both cases that you treat the combat on the enemy ship like normal combat (probably treating the enemy crew as a battlegroup, with significant figures like the enemy captain as individuals), and treat each combat round the same as a naval combat round, giving the PCs' captain her own chance to roll for momentum and enact maneuvers. The timescale doesn't make a whole lot of sense, but doing it this way lets everybody have a chance to act. The enemy ship that the PCs have landed on should take penalties on their captain's (Wits + Sail) roll for momentum though, since both the captain and the crew are distracted.

                                    Originally posted by Grod_the_giant
                                    What if they then want to rip it in half as a feat of strength?
                                    I think that breaking the planks of a ship is probably comparable to kicking an oak door to splinters (min Str 5, 6 successes). Doing that should be good for one hull point of damage. For every extra hull point you wanted to do at once, I'd raise the Str minimum by one step, and just multiply the successes. So if you want to rip an Imperial trireme apart in a single mighty exertion, you'll need to do 4 hull points of damage, requiring Str 15 and 24 successes.

                                            I'm disputing this part, here.

                                            Mortal worship is fulfilling to said gods, but the higher up on the ladder they are, the less likely they are to intervene to fulfill an individual petitioner's prayers. The field guardian can only afford to send guiding dreams to one or two or three or so farmers; if he were to send these efficacious visions to more than a half-dozen, he'd have committed so much that he can't even materialize. Even if his cult was somehow expanded beyond the borders of his field, practicality is going to dictate that he's only going to bless those who are influential enough to get others to follow the words of wisdom shared in the dreams; he might enjoy the prayers others in the region will send him, but he doesn't have enough in him to answer them all.

                                            ​And that's just at the bottom of the scale, and assuming no other actors; remember also that there are road-gods and river-gods and city-gods and disease-gods who might step in and get upset at the impertinent field-god who is trying to curry favor from these mortals by blessing so many. They might attack him bureaucratically, socially, or even physically.

                                            ​Exalted's setting can't be approached from the assumption that most prayers are answered; there has to be room for charlatans, quacks, and high priests of a dead or non-existent god, because that's the setting where a cult devoted to the Incarnae or the Dragons makes sense..

                                                    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                                    I'm obviously not trying to break the setting, but I'm curious as to what people expect when venerating very high divinities in a world where praying for lesser divinities has results on its own.
                                                    Why not both? Particularly when it comes to big asks, like "please let my son survive the war." The local field god might be able to bless your harvest, but they're not going to do much when your mom has cancer. Or what if your local god is one of those Crom-style "I hate you, be strong enough to deal with it" guys, or you ask for something and they say no--wouldn't you like the idea that there's something you can still do?

                                                    The last one becomes particularly important if you portray gods as entitled jerks. They gain power from worship, but that's not why they exist--they were created to keep the world running smoothly, not answer to petty mortals. They're pretty much literally burocrats, prone to all the same sorts of self-importance and petty tyrannies as human. In that context, worship isn't a social contract, it's sucking up to the boss to stay on their good side. I like to treat local gods less as objects of veneration and more as forces of nature-- "ugh, Third Moon River King is in another bad mood, better sacrifice some boars before he decides to flood the town again."

                                                    In a context like that, I know I'd like to believe that there are worthier deities out there who care more about their people than their pride.
