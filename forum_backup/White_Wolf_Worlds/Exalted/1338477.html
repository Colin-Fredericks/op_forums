    Exalted 3E homebrew combat mechanics

    09-29-2019, 01:33 PM
    I'm looking for fan rewrites of 3E combat systems. I'm aware of Simon Darkstep's Momentum Exalted and Dragonmystic's Exalted : Reincarnated, but are there any others? I'll keep a list of them in this thread for easy access.


    3E Homebrew combat systems :

    Momentum Exalted - Simplifies initiative (renamed momentum) and uses a Battle Wheel to determine turn order.

    Exalted : Reincarnated - In-depth changes to the 3E core. Replaces initiative with Endurance, a secondary health pool that can regenerate. Work in Progress.

    Glory Overwhelming - Rework of the motes system. Exalts have near infinite motes, but frivolous spending ignites their Essence Fever, spurring them towards passionate and heroic action.

    Hissatsu - Rework of initiative. Creates risk/reward by making initiative your protection against decisive damage but also your fuel for Decisive Attacks and Gambits.

    Hissatsu Differences
    Initiative
    Initiative is no longer your damage potential, but functions more like a decisive soak. The higher your initiative, the less decisive damage you take. The more in the negatives you are, the more decisive damage you take. Initiative recharges automatically when you do not attack and when you crash enemies. Withering attacks no longer grant initiative.
    Withering
    - Withering attack damage does not get threshold successes added and post-soak damage is applied straight without rolling. Withering attacks do not give Initiative from hitting or damage, only on crashing.
    - Withering attack is one roll, attacking party doesn’t need to do damage math or alter their own Initiative.
    Decisive
    - Decisive attacks use Accuracy too. Decisive raw damage is based on the attacker losing 1-20 Initiative to serve as the base pool, modified by the target’s Initiative. Negative Initiative adds to damage, positive Initiative lowers it. Total decisive damage rolled is capped by Lethality based on the genre after Hardness check.
    - Initiative acts like Decisive soak, with negative Initiative increasing damage. Attempting to attack high Initiative targets makes attacker vulnerable.
    Gambits
    - Gambit Initiative check is 100% tied to Commitment. Commit 1-20 Init, roll Commitment dice without double 10s against gambit difficulty, on success you are in a position to attempt it. If you fail, you weren’t in position to attempt the gambit. If the Gambit targets an enemy, resolve decisive attack/social influence action. If the Gambit requires e.g. a feat of strength, resolve the check. On success, gambit happens.
    - Gambits are always available, but attempting them makes you vulnerable.



    Edit : added Lumi's Glory Overwhelming and Hissatsu

                  Originally posted by Epitome View Post
                  -lots of cases where the system falls apart if players don't play along, and the only thing keeping things in check is the Core book saying &quot;plz don't do dis&quot; rather than actual rules and limits.
                  I sincerely hope you find what you're looking for. (Following the thread with interest.) But almost every subsystem over every single edition of Exalted relies on the ST and players putting effort into not breaking the system.

                  I've never seen a robust set of houserules that addresses this -- but you're probably looking for complete rewrites like Exalted : Reincarnated.

                  Personally, I think you should run Mutants and Masterminds. The system isn't impossible to break, but it's pretty difficult -- especially if you insist on every Power being modelled on a published Exalted Charm. M&M solves all your issues apart from "conservation of ninjutsu" (which is massively exacerbated).

                  Otherwise:

                  1. The only way of fixing this is to track Initative on every combatant for every combatant... which is massively math intensive. Momentum doesn't try to address it (and Reincarnated is a system rewrite to the scale of Dungeons the Dragonning). I guess the advice on page 194 of the Corebook kinda helps? (SHENANIGANS!) But you don't need homebrew to apply that.

                  2a. Area attacks typically only award Initative from one target. Where they don't, houserule they do to fix that. Simples.
                  2b. I'd be tempted to do something like FFG Edge of the Empire and give Boss characters two Initative tracks (a bit like how Single Point Form works). But this is kinda a feature of the system -- the Exalted (especially Solars) are the biggest bads in the setting. Each individual PC is a world shaping powerhouse, that Wyld Hunts want to gang up on at least 5-to-1. If the Circle gets a numerical advantage... well they're supposed to wipe the floor with their foes. No "but what about" -- the Exalted wrested Creation from the nigh omnipotent beings from beyond reality who birthed dieties to serve them as slaves. They're supposed to be able to kick the ass of anything in the setting, and to kick the ass of most things even when outnumberered.

                  3. "plz don't do dis" is basically the Storyteller's Guide for Exalted. It's practically the slogan of the gameline. You need to houserule and you need players who'll work with you.

                  4. If battlegroups are posing that much of an issue, then don't use them. Roll in 20 individual Blood Apes. Or once the battgroup is defeated, roll in a single survivor as an individual combatant. Or cheat the battlegroup's stats higher. Or don't use the combat system and run the encounter narratively. Use the advice on page 212.
