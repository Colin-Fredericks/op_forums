            Here we go, after taking a tally of everyone’s input.

            ---------

            The next morning, your party sets out from Azu Castle, following the banks of the White River to the northeast. Only a few miles away from the castle, the post-stations become fewer, and as the land rises into forested mountains, stop entirely, as do the farming villages. As the elevation increases, your rate of travel decreases, and you walk Xuansu as often as you ride.

            Of course, your rate of travel isn’t helped by your companions.

            You are accompanied by the men-at-arms of Jianghu, released from their levy by the Lord Iysen. As a result, the men (and not a few women) are in high spirits, jovial as they march home, and you take the time to try and get acquainted with your new tenants and Oathsworn vassals.

            The peasants are friendly and respectful, if a bit standoffish, and the Oathsworn nobles are polite, even if they don’t believe your claims of nobility at first (you do have to admit, without armor or even fine clothing, you don’t cut that regal of a figure), and it takes repeated showings of Lord Iysens seal and letter for them to come around to the idea that they have a new liegelord.

            All in all, they seem like a pretty decent lot, standoffishness nonewithstanding. Nobody even gave you a second glance concerning your Hill-Tribe features, which is ….. odd, to say the least. Come to think of it, many of the men-at-arms had red hair and wood-grained skin themselves. Maybe integrating the Tribes will be easier than you thought?

            As a result of both the army on your heels and the poor road, it takes a little over a week to get from Azu Castle to the margins of Jianghu. Increasingly, the land rises steeper and steeper, even to the banks of the river, and the farm-fields you pass are criss-crossed with rough-laid walls of fieldstones.

            You cross the White river at the market-village of Liangzhou, where the term “sleepy” is the kindest phrase you would use to describe it (‘po’dunk’ or ‘deader than its own cemetery” are better fits, in your mind). Almost as soon as you leave Liangzhou, the road changes from rough cobbles to dirt, and the land becomes wild. Even the cultivated land close to the river is rough compared to the domains you just left, with copses of trees and rugged heathland breaking up the farmhouses, and you only half-jokingly say you can see more sheep than people. More than one line of hills is dark, uncultivated and empty, and half the orchards you pass (there are a lot of orchards, come to think about it) are wild and overgrown, grass and brambles slowly choking out the fruit trees.

            You realize with growing disquiet that Lord Iysens demands are going to be a lot harder than you realized. Just what have you got yourself into?

            As you progress farther and farther into Jianghu, you start to see that the farmhouses are incredibly fortified, along the levels of noble manor-houses in other domains, with heavy stone walled houses, palisades of logs, watch towers, dry-moats and earthen embankments. When you ask, the one common reply, heavy with dry amusement and unspoken long-borne resignation….

            “Bandits”.

            Well, that explains everything.

            Over time, in groups of ones and twos and threes, the men-at-arms and Oathsworn peel off from the travelling army, trudging up dirt paths to farmhouses and estates. The latter, you request that they meet you at Yagura Castle in a week, for a general meeting and debriefing. By the time you reach Yari Lake, dark and cold even in the middle of high summer, you are accompanied by only a scant few armed soldiers, and the knowledge that the region is heavy with bandit activity sets a weight deep in your stomach.

            Eventually, you come up over a rise in the road, catching a glimpse of Yagura Castle for the first time. Even bone-tired as you are, stiff and sore and worried sick, the sight nevertheless fails to drag a gasp from your lips, and Saiko and Blue-Pines cease their chatter in shock and surprise.

            Even at this distance, the whitewashed walls gleam in the noonday sunlight, and the towers rise almost gracefully over the water and forests surrounding it. Yagura Castle commands the entire valley, rising up almost to the level of the mountains in the background (not really, you chide yourself. Just a trick of perspective), and the tower on top of the keep has line-of-sight across the entire lake.

            You realize, with sudden recognition, that that is yours. Yagura Castle, almost all the farmhouses, orchards and pastures, the allegiance of all the soldiers and noble Oathsworn. All of it. Never, not once in your wildest dreams, could you have ever imagined this would happen. Sure, you’ve idly daydreamed about winning an estate, running a manor-house and maybe someday a family, but not this! According to Lord Iysen, you are, technically, the 4th most powerful person in Xia, her right hand…….. you, the bastard child of some backwoods noble, and half-savage besides!

            You shake your head, chuckling. Yes, this surpasses your wildest dreams, but from the looks of things, you sure as hell have your work cut out for you here in Jianghu. Saiko touches your knee at the sound.

            “What’s so funny, Kasai?”

            “…. Just laughing at circumstances, Saiko. Did you think that, a year ago, we would be moving into a castle, one I own, or owning a fief that would take several days to cross? I sure as hell didn’t.”

            “No, not really.” She admits, turning back to the castle gleaming on the bluff. “I’m still trying to take it all in, so I can only wonder how you feel. Still, this is all like what happens in the fairy-tales….”

            Blue Pines grunts, “Better hope there isn’t a troll, or a dragon. Kasai doesn’t really look up to fighting monsters right yet.”

            Grinning, “Nope, not at all. I’ll take a frog, though. Maybe if I kiss it, it will turn into a princess!”

            Saiko throws grass at you, “The frog turns into a handsome prince, you numbskull! Besides, that is how you get warts!”

            You draw yourself up in quiet indignation, “I thought I already had the ‘handsome prince’ side of things covered.”

            She giggles and rolls her eyes, and Blue Pines shakes her head, “And then you woke up.”

            “Sure, sure, insult the guy that pays your salary.”

            “…. You don’t pay either of us a salary!”

            “My point still stands. It’s the principle of the thing.”…..

            You bicker with your retainers as you move down the bluff. At the base of the cliff Yagura stands on, there is a ‘town’, more a collection of farmhouses around a central plaza. When you pass into the plaza, you get buttonholed by two men, one grey-haired in noble robes and the other in well-worn armor. Both the men bow deeply.

            The noble speaks first, “Good day, my Lord Jianghu. I hope the travel was not too grueling?”

            You shrug, “Easier than some and worse than some. Might I ask who you gentlemen are?”

            “Of course”. Still bent over, he places a hand to his chest, “My name is Kasahiro Manabu, one of the Lord Shiba’s vassals and an accountant by trade. I was asked by my liege to come here and assist you in the running and ordering of your fief.”

            The warrior steps forwards, knuckling his forehead in salute. “I’m Oxblood, Sergeant and drillmaster of her levies, M’Lord.” He grins, “I reckon I’ve trained more soldiers than you’ve had hot dinners, begging your pardon, and the boss sent me here to give you a hand with that.”

            Kasahiro continues, “Of course, my Lord, both our jobs would be made much easier if we actually had access to the castle…”

            You frown, ‘What do you mean?”

            “ Akio Masumi, the castellan in charge of the castle and the domain, refused us entry, even when I showed him the letter from the Lord Xia. He told us he would send for us when he was, and I quote, “good and ready”.” He sniffs, “that was 2 days ago.”

            Sgt. Oxblood grunts, “Bastard was as drunk as a skunk, at 9-bells even! That takes some dedication.”

            You sigh, glaring up at the castle overhead. Of course things have to be difficult. All you want at this point is a hot meal, a bath, and a nice soft bed, but nooooooo.

            Grumbling, “Follow me, if you will. I will see things straightened out.”

            Followed by Kasahiro and Oxblood, you ride Xuansu up the winding road to the castle gate. At least the guards were competent and watchful enough to notice your approach, and you are met by several (which is not how things should be. Another force could sneak up while the armsmen were distracted) guards watching interestedly from the gatehouse wallwalk. One of them calls down to you,’

            “Can I help you, sir?”

            You draw yourself up to your full height, “My name is Kasai Qianke, and by the edict of the Lady Iysen Shiba, Lord Xia, I am the duly-recognized Lord of the Jianghu Marches. As such, I would like these gates to be opened, so that I might enter my home.”

            After he picks up his jaw from the parapet, “I….. I’m sorry my Lord, but I can’t just open the gate to anyone who asks. I don’t have the key, first of all, and …. Uh….. how do I know who you are who you say you are?”

            You smile. The man has a point. “Can you read?”

            “Yes, my Lord.”

            Reaching into your jacket, you pull out the missive from Lord Iysen, signed and stamped with her seal. “This should clear things up.”

            He unfolds the paper, glancing at the wax seal at the bottom and skimming the rest. Sighing, he folds it up and tosses it back to you, “…. Looks like everything checks out, my Lord.” He bows, as well as the rest of the armsmen, “But I still don’t have the key to the gate. The castellan, Akio Masumi, does, and I will have to get it from him. I….. well, I’ve got to warn you. He has a temper, on top of being drunk, and he hates any dismissal of his authority. This could get interesting, if you catch my drift.”

            You grin, “Nothing can be simple, my good man. Bring him to me, and we will see things out.”

            It only takes a few minutes before you can hear the shouting echoing from within the castle walls. The argument gets louder and louder, until the face of the castellan, red and angry, appears over the parapet.

            Seething, spittle forming at the corners of his lips, “Who the hell are you, to tell me to open the gates to my castle?!”

            You refrain from letting your irritation show, instead lifting an eyebrow, “Technically, my good sir, this is my castle. You are just watching over it for me. As such, I think that allows me to open whatever I please, does it not?”

            “….What?”

            “My name is Kasai Qianke, and I am the Lord Jianghu. You may bow.”

            He reels back in shock, then after regaining his composure (and his balance. You can smell the drink on him from here), “…..bullshit. The Lord Iysen would have told me about this!”
            You toss the letter up again, “Technically, she did. That letter has everything you need.”

            He reads through the missive, “….. You’ve got to be shitting me.”

            “No, indeed I am not. Please open the gates.”

            He stares at you for a moment, grinding his teeth, then mutters a curse and heads down out of sight. A few more moments pass, then the air is filled with the sound of chains clanking and rusted metal screeching. Slowly, with much creaking and groaning, the gate to Yagura Castle grinds open.

            As you ride Xuansu through the open gate (more of a tunnel through the stone footing of the wall, really), you take immediate notice of the numerous impediments to access. The ceiling, really the floor of the gatehouse above, is lined with murder holes and machicolations, and the walls of the tunnel slope inwards, getting close enough at a point where a wagon would have to go slow to avoid getting caught up. Any enemy force trying to enter would have to be crammed shoulder to shoulder, easy targets for rocks or arrows from above, and a ram would be slowed to a crawl. The gate itself is a foot thick, made from iron-banded white oak beams, and supported by a porticullis.

            On the other side of the gate stands Akio Masumi, fuming.

            From on top of Xuansu, you lift your other eyebrow, finding it more and more difficult to withhold your irritation. “…. I am still waiting for that bow, sir.”

            He stares in confusion, then offers a perfunctory bow, little more than a nod and a twitch of the waist.

            “No, no, a real bow, one accorded to my station.”

            He glares, “In this mud?”

            Okay, that’s it.

            “ Yes, Akio Masumi, in this mud. I am the Lord Jianghu, and I will see the respect accorded to my station!” Your voice is filled with ice. “Don’t make me come over there to show you how.”

            He wavers, internally debating what to do, then slowly, hatefully, he sinks to his knees in the muck and presses his forehead to the ground. He holds the position until you give him permission to rise.

            “Good!”, you smile beatifically. Dismounting (painfully, with help from Saiko), you pass the reins to an armsman, “Now that that is all squared away, I would like a tour.”

            As you follow Akio into the keep, you buttonhole the armsman who helped you get inside. “I am sorry, I didn’t catch your name.”

            Dubiously, “My name is Tadao, my Lord.”

            “Excellent. Thank you for the help, Tadao.”, and he bows quickly before heading back to the wallwalk.

            Slowly, Akio Masumi leads you through the keep, pointing out the various rooms and functions. There is a general level of unkempt-ness through the castle, making your skin crawl. Dust piled in the corners, dirt ground into the wooden floors, cobwebs by the ceiling. People live here?

            When you get to the armory, your jaw drops. The place is a mess: spears are jumbled haphazardly in the racks, the crossbows are piled on the table, etc. You pick up one of the arbalests and pass it over to Blue Pines. “What do you think of this?”

            Taking the weapon, she gives it a quick once-over. “The stock is fine, but the trigger is sticky, which means the nut probably has to be replaced. The prod is ok, but the string is fraying at the nocks. I wouldn’t want to shoot it.”

            “Later on tonight, I want you to go through all this stuff, see what condition it is in.”

            “Got it.”

            Turning to Akio, “Tell me, sir, why are these arms such a mess?”

            He shrugs, “We’ve never been attacked.”

            “Doesn’t explain why they are in such bad condition.” All you get is another shrug. Seething, you follow him onwards and upwards.

            The great hall is ….. ok, although the walls are faded and the floor is dusty. The guest-rooms, under the eaves, are a mess, filled with cobwebs and mouse droppings. The Lords chambers……

            You stare. “….. What the fuck have you done in here?!”

            He starts, “Excuse me?”

            “You heard me!” You slowly make your way between the piled empty wine bottles and beer jars. This place stinks, of stale beer, old food, sweat and piss, to the point where your eyes water.

            Going over to a window, where the smell is at least diminished, you pull out another letter.

            “According to this letter from Lord Iysen, there should be keys for the main gate, the keep gatehouse, as well as a safe in these chambers, two of each. Where are they?”

            Sullenly, “In my room.”

            “Get them.”

            He stalks off to the Lords chamber (gods be merciful, you have to sleep in there!), coming back a few minutes later with two keyrings.

            “How much personal equipment do you have in here?”

            Confused, “Not much, why?”

            “Gather it. You have one hour, I want you gone by then. I can handle the rest of the tour on my own.”

            ‘What! You…. I am a retainer of the Lord Iysen, she will hear of this!”

            You grin, pulling your lips back from your teeth, happy to see him recoil. “Go ahead, tell her. What do you think she is going to say? You are fired, Akio Masumi, and based on your gross incompetence and impertinence, you should consider yourself lucky I don’t send you away with a horsewhipping.” You make to go into the office, “Remember, Masumi: one hour. Don’t make me wait.”

            In the office, after pushing aside yet more trash, you find the safe, made of cast iron, under the desk. Inside, you are happy to find 10 dirham in silver, stamped with the mark of Xia. At least you aren’t a pauper, and running a domain of this size will be expensive. On top of the desk are several messy piles of papers, and you start going through them idly at first. When some of them indicate the food-rent and tax records of Jianghu, you read them in earnest.

            Evidently, you got quite invested in your reading, and get startled when you hear Saiko curse at the level of garbage.

            “Lots of shit, huh?”

            “No godsdamned kidding! This is awful!”

            “Your room is over there. I haven’t even been in there yet, so be prepared for anything…”

            “My room?”

            “Yeah,” , untangling yourself from the hug, “When you are done jumping around, go down to the town and see if you can’t find some people willing to help clean this place up. I’ll pay, if that helps. The meeting with my vassals is in 3 days, and I don’t want this place looking like a pigsty.”

            Grabbing what papers you see as critical, you head back downstairs to the armory hall, easily the cleanest part of the keep. Tucked in a corner with Kasahiro, you go over the records in detail, trying to hammer out some semblance of sense of the lines upon lines of numbers. Eventually, Saiko comes back with several young villagers in tow, who cry out in dismay at the mess (although the copper you offer quiets them nicely).

            Over the next few days, you go over records with Kasahiro, discussing different levels of taxation, the traditional expected amounts of food-rent and labor-service required of serfs, just how much land you now own (a lot, only 2/3 of which is actually used), what economic effect releasing serfs from servitude would have, as well as many other topics. When the numbers and figures start swirling senselessly through your brain, you take a “break” by helping the peasant girls clean. Once the garbage is thrown away and the floors and walls scrubbed with ammonia, the place actually looks rather nice. Better yet, some of the girls found various carpets and tapestries in a linen closet, so now the place doesn’t look so empty. You can even move into the master bedroom, which actually has a bed, with linen sheets and quilted blankets. Even in Teriake Castle, you didn’t have a bed.

            So far, it is good to be the boss.

            Eventually, your various Oathsworn vassals start trickling in, where they get directed up to the Great Hall. There, at a long trestle table set up by your new servants, you sit and greet them as they come in (you actually agonized about how to set the thing up; whether you should sit on the dais at the head of the hall, be humble and sit with the retainers, or somesuch. Eventually, you decide to sit with them, albeit at the head of the table.). The servants bring out refreshments, including an ample supply of cider; as you noticed on the way here, Jianghu has quite a high number of orchards, which produce great amounts of fruit.

            When everyone is seated and sated, you stand up and introduce yourself to your assembled retainers.

            “Good evening, everyone. I have already met a great number of you, but for those who have yet to speak to me, my name is Kasai Qianke, and by the edict of the Great Lord Xia, Lady Iysen Shiba, I am now the Lord Paramount of the Jianghu Marches.

            They rise and bow, evidently a great deal more polite than Akio Masumi (something you are glad to note. If everyone acted like that asshole, there were going to be problems). One of the Oathsworn, an older gentlemen, asks,

            “My Lord, if I may ask without giving offense: how old are you?”

            “ I am two-and-twenty years of age, sir.”

            He shakes his head, in amusement or despair. “Dragons, my youngest children are older than you, my Lord…….. Again, if I can ask without giving offense: why would so young a man be entitled to such a prominent position?”

            “ Of course you can ask. I served our Lord Iysen faithfully for a year, and saved her life in the assassination attempt before that, during the Fire in Teriake. Most recently, I was instrumental in securing peace and a military alliance with the Dumnoniapoccasset Confederation of the Hill-Tribes, that for which I was granted the Lordship.”

            The last line is met with blank stares.

            “…..Do you not know what is happening?”

            The older gentlemen answers, “Forgive us, my Lord, but here in Jianghu we are isolated at the best of times. News comes late and slow, if at all. Could you please tell us what this agreement is?”
            After taking a sip of your cider (which is really quite good, you should try to export this), “As a result of the ongoing war with Yue, the Great Lord Xia did not want to potentially fight a war on two fronts, and therefore sought peace with the Tribes.”

            “I was tasked with travelling to their chief, the warlord Ousamequin, and discussing the possibility of a non-aggression pact in return for ceding certain parcels of land, trade agreements, so on and so forth. “

            “Seven of the twelve tribes agreed to join forces with Xia, and four of the rest agreed to neutrality. The only tribe that declared war is on the other side of the Confederation, making it by-and-large a paper tiger.”

            “The lands ceded to the Tribes include most of the hill-country and uplands on the border of Xia. In the case of Jianghu, this would include the mountains and highlands to the north and east. I expect them to start moving into those territories comparatively soon.”

            Your statement is met with stunned silence. “Is …..Is this going to be a problem? If so, there isn’t much I can do about it. The Lord Iysen was the one to decide which territories to cede, not I. Anyways, part of the treaty spelled out in specific terms that the people of Xia were not to be harmed.”

            “Well, to be honest, my Lord…”

            “If you are going to be the only one talking, sir, I would like to at least know your name.”, you say softly.

            “-ahem- Of course. My name is Shin Takeshi.”

            You nod, acknowledging it. ‘And while we are at it, you may refer to me as “Lord Kasai”. To be honest,” You admit, almost sheepishly, “I am still trying to get used to this whole “rulership” thing, and would kind of prefer to be eased into it.”

            Shin laughs, good-naturedly, “Very well, Lord Kasai. ….. As I was saying, the people of Jianghu have a ….. complicated relationship with the Tribes. While they do raid us, and we do fight them, we also have largely coexisted with them for quite some time. Many of the peasants are of partial Tribe descent…. Hell, even some of us have the Hill-blood within our veins.” He stops, the says quietly, “My first wife was of the Tribes, and my children from her are half-blooded, like you, Lord Kasai.”

            “Oh? What totem do they show?”

            “<The fox, Lord Kasai>”

            Impressive. Most lowlanders garble Forestongue beyond all recognition, but he is fluent despite a slight accent. You might want to keep him around.

            Shin Takeshi continues, “Anyways….. having the Tribes move so close is …. concerning, but more because any large body of people on the move is cause for concern, especially when they are as warlike and confrontational as the Tribes. However, if you say there is a peace agreement, then there shouldn’t be too many issues, Lord Kasai. So long as the agreement is kept, of course.”

            Pleased, “That is good to hear. Of course, if there is any trouble with the Tribes, bring it to my attention as soon as possible. I would like to hammer out any cross-cultural kerfluffles before they cause serious damage.”

            “Ah…… actually, my Lord. There has been some troubles, some minor raids on the isolated northern farmsteads. Nothing serious; no casualties, just scared farmers, that is all.”

            You frown. The Tribal elders were supposed to keep a tight lid on stuff like that. You’ll have to talk to them. On to the next!

            Refilling your cup, “ Part of my agreement with the Lord Iysen was that, in order to me to retain this Lordship, I have to be able to levy 1000 Men-at-Arms by next summer. Now, I already know the answer, but tell me; how many men are under arms in Jianghu?”

            The landed retainers turn and converse with each other. After a few minutes of muttered conversation, Shin Takeshi turns back to you, “Technically, between our combined holdings and your lands, Jianghu can levy about 500 or so soldiers. At least, that is what we marched to war with several weeks ago. Functionally, the number is probably less, as the Lord Iysen demanded we levy as many soldiers as possible; old men, bare-faced boys, that sort of thing. We recommend about 300 be the maximum in a non-emergency situation.”

            You nod. “That is what I was told. Now, on my personal holdings, I am planning on releasing a couple hundred serfs from serfdom, in order to pad those numbers.” Taking the slip of paper offered by Kasahiro, “By these calculations, that would bring the numbers of functionally-levyable soldiers to …… about 500. According to the terms of the agreement, I still need 500 more, and I would like to eventually at least compete with the Lords Wulin and Iruma, who are both capable of at least 3000. What sort of actions do you recommend to increase these numbers?”

            Again, your retainers turn and discuss with each other, this time for far longer. You take the opportunity to go over several of the figures with Kasahiro.

            After several minutes of deliberation, Shin Takeshi rises again, “ Surely as you travelled here to Yagura Castle, you noticed just how much land was empty, uncultivated?”

            “Yes, I did, actually. Why is that?”

            Another of your retainers (Aya Cho, you remember now) snorts in derision, “That useless waste of meat, Akio Masumi”.

            You freeze, blood turning to ice. “….. Just what has that blowhard done to damage my people?”

            She chuckles, “I see you met him.”

            “Met him? I sent him packing, and spent the last three days cleaning his mess.”

            Her smile fades, ‘Well, he took the same amount of care in governing this domain as he did in keeping this castle clean. Technically, he was responsible for administering all the lands now held by you, Lord Kasai, and we were not to interfere. You can imagine how that went.”

            “…..total anarchy?”

            “More like ‘total apathy’. I do not think he actually ever went to the places he technically oversaw. Entire regions were left to their own devices, in the face of bandits and the Tribes. Asides from local militias, they were entirely defenseless. Worse, when we tried to help, we were censured, and told to “mind our own business!”

            She continues, quietly, “I still think he was taking kickbacks. Nobody can give that little of a shit.”

            Softly, in shock, “What happened to the people?”

            She shrugs, “Some of them moved to our holdings, some of them left, the rest died.”

            You snarl, causing some of the closest retainers to scoot back.

            “Do you have any proof of collaberation?!”

            She shakes her head, “No, sadly, or we would have dealt with it ourselves. He never really talked to us; this is the first time I have been in Yagura Castle for years.”

            You are quiet for a long time, simmering over this revelation.

            “So …. What you are telling me is ; one, there are regions of my domain that are severely underpopulated, and two, you have basically been left alone for the last several years?”

            Shin nods, “Yes, to both counts. Jianghu as a whole is mostly empty, the hinterlands of the forests and the hills even more so. The bandits and lack of governance only compounded the problem."

            "As for ‘being left alone’…. Yes. Most of us have had to implement legal codes on our own.”

            You rest your chin on your fist, “How has law and order been?”

            He shrugs, “Not bad, which isn’t surprising. There isn’t any large urban areas for crime to grow in, and life is hard enough out here already without adding to it, leading to most people being well-behaved. Most of the commoners have strong clan-ties, and those clans largely police themselves. We serve as moderators and go-betweens, mostly.”

            You nod. “Very well. Carry on with your cases, I give you the authority to decide cases and punishments. I do want to at least hear about major cases, however. Murders, clan-feuds, that sort of thing. Petty cases are all yours.”

            “As for the underdeveloped and undersettled regions, I am planning on implementing several types of settlement and suchlike. For one, both to increase population and to pump up the size of the army, I am planning on offer land to prospective soldiers, so long as they serve for 5 years. My first tentative thoughts involved 40 acres and a head of oxen to start. How does that sound to you?”

            They turn and deliberate.

            “Lord Kasai, you come from the lowland valleys, do you not?”

            Amused, “Technically. I lived in Teriake for a year, but I am ultimately from Tsen, to the northwest. My families holdings are geographically similar to Jianghu.”

            “Regardless, how heavily-settled are the lowland river valleys?”

            “Heavily settled, to the point of being crowded…….. Oh, I see what you are getting at. Yes, many of the children of lowland peasant families lack much in the way of inheritance, and might jump at the chance to gain substantial land of their own. Was there something else?”

            “Yes. Now, while there isn’t anything wrong with spearmen, currently Jianghu has a dearth of combined-arms. Most of the prospective settler-soldiers are going to arrive with spears, which will only compound the issue. Maybe if you offer different grants of land to recruits with different weapons…..” Shin scratches out some computations on a slip of paper. “Let us say that 40 acres is the baseline. If a soldier brings their own weapons, they get 1.25 times that grant. If they are skilled in archery, they get 1.5 times, and cavalry would get 2 times. So…….. A ‘base’ soldier would get 40 acres, a soldier with their own equipment would get 50 acres, a longbowman would get 75, and a light cavalryman would get 120-150 acres. That might attract more specialized soldiers, my Lord.”

            You rub your chin, “And Jianghu could support such grants?”

            “From what land I have seen not under plow…. Easily. Not counting what will happen when forests and such get cleared.”

            ‘Excellent idea, Shin Takeshi.” You raise your glass in salute, praise he receives graciously.

            You continue, “Now…… what is this I hear about commoner militias?” The guilty looks on their faces tells you all you need to know.

            “The serfs have weapons, don’t they?”

            “They have to, Lord Kasai! We Oathsworn are usually several hours away, and..” She is interrupted by your hand.

            “ Believe me, I understand. Before I served the Lord Iysen, I wandered the Hundred Kingdoms. I know how “reality works”. I have zero issue with the people protecting themselves. I am assuming they hunt as well?”

            The silence is, yet again, all the answer you need.

            You lean back in your chair, resting your head on your hands (it is nice for the casts to be finally off, although the last two fingers on your left hand are stiff and purplish still).

            “So…. What you are telling me is that there is a substantial population here in Jianghu that owns arms and is somewhat practiced in woodcraft?...... Very well.” You lean forward, looking your retainers in the eyes. ‘Again, I have no issues with people owning weapons to protect themselves, or even hunting in “my” woods. The gods know I can’t eat every deer. With that in mind…… I want every single household in Jianghu, be they peasant or serf, to own at least a spear or a bow, and have at least one person from each household skilled in their use. From what you tell me, this is already a reality, so implementing this shouldn’t be that hard.”

            Aya frowns, “what are you getting at, Lord Kasai?”

            “I want to institute a formal militia to Jianghu. The Men-at-Arms, while doughty, are evidently not enough to drive the ruffians from our lands. This milita would serve to reinforce them, as well as legally protect the right, yes, right, of people to own arms. These militias would wait for a call-up, either from me or from you, and they could only serve within the boundaries of Jianghu itself. And that is an excellent way to segway to my next topic.”

            You twist in your chair, singling out Sergeant Oxblood. “Ladies and gentlemen, this is Sergeant Oxblood, on loan from the Lord Iysen’s forces. He is here to help me train and order the required soldiery. With that in mind, he is going to draw up a system of drills and exercises for us. I must ask; how do the Men-at-Arms receive training as of now?”

            Another one of your retainers shrugs, “To be honest, Lord Kasai, they drill whenever they can, and whenever we can get around to it. Most of us help with the planting and harvest, at least with the planning thereof, which doesn’t really leave a lot of free time. Other than that, the peasants train with each other, by actually using the skills, or through necessity in bandit attacks.”

            You nod, “Understandable. Fortunately or unfortunately, that is going to change. I am assuming at least a significant portion of Jianghu is Immaculate-fearing? I saw a shrine in Liangzhou. Regardless, what I want is for you landowners to call a general muster, every Sun-day, probably after Immaculate services for sheer practicality. There, there will be ordered drills, for both the Men-at-Arms and the new Militia. Marching drills, combat training, archery, whatever. So long as it is regular, and it is efficient. Sergeant Oxblood will see to the last, and I want you to see to the first. The sooner we start this, the earlier it becomes a habit. Is that possible?”

Aya stands up, “Yes, Lord Kasai, there is, and this is the most serious at all.” She takes a deep breath, then ,” Before the call to war came, early in the spring, we heard rumors that several bandit-gangs in the Akibara Forest, in the center of Jianghu, were forcibly consolidated into one by a charismatic leader. Eventually, we learned that this leader is, well, Oathsworn like ourselves, one who has the gall to call himself the “Lord of Jianghu”.

Concerns?” Nobody answers. “Very well, I thank you all for coming, and I appreciate your support. You are dismissed; if anyone wishes to stay the night, inform one of my servants, and a bedroll will be set up in a guest-room for you. Have a good night.”

If you are not able to travel to Teriake Castle-Town at this time, I am willing and able to travel to Yagura Castle.

                                                        Concrete evidence of his actions came to light about a year ago, although in hindsight it is evident he has been operating in Jianghu for at least a few years.

                                                        4) MO

                                                        He......well, actually he is pretty well-behaved, as well as bandit-lords go. Sure, sure, he extorts food from the farms on the boundaries of the Akibara forest, but it isn't all that much more than what the food-rent already is, and the bandits are comparatively well-behaved (no rape and murder, and what has occurred was dealt with harshly).

                                                        Asides from extorting food, Masanori and his goons spend most of their time attacking other bandits, which is a blessing in disguise. If a bandit-gang doesn't start following him and doing what he says, he wipes them out, and Masanori's bandits spend as much time fighting other bandits as they do extorting stuff from the peasants. Bandit attacks in general (except for Masanori's crew, of course) along the fringes of the Akibara forest have dropped precipitously in frequency and intensity.

                                                        If anything, and even Kasai's Oathsworn vassals admit this: he is technically doing the same thing any landlord does, that is, protecting farmers in return for food. The main problem is that he is doing it illegally, while Oathsworn retainers are given the legal right to do so. Plus, he is affiliated with bandits and other scum-of-the-earth as opposed to "proper" Men-at-Arms, but what-have-you.

                                                        The commoners are more pissed they are paying taxes/land-rent "twice" than anything else. They fought back initially, but after Masanori's bandits hung those who resisted, they started paying. They also sent word to Akio Masumi (who did fuck-all, as one might expect), and to the Oathsworn landlords, but the Oathsworn technically aren't supposed to do anything, as the land is legally Kasai's.

                                                        5) Base of Operations

                                                        It is suspected that he is operating out of an old Hill-Tribe ringfort, Dun Nashuon, itself located in the Toge hills, in the middle of the Akibara forest. The place has been a haven for neer-do-wells for years, and it is "close', relatively speaking, to the farmsteads that have been imposed upon, which means it only makes sense for him to be there. While the place is largely ruined, it is a fortification in rough terrain, which means it would be comparatively difficult to assault. And even bandits can hold ground when it is on a hill a hundred feet tall.

                                                        There are no roads to Dun Nashuon, and the region is heavy forest, so it is going to be a pain in the ass getting an army out there. Bandits would be able to conduct hit-and-run attacks with ease on our slowly-moving regiments. By comparison, that would be the terrain the Hill-Tribes excel in fighting in......

                                                            On an aside, Kasai's name and title in Dragontongue would be something like: Koshaku Qianke Kasai, Kugo-Qianke, Daimyo Jianghu-han

                                                            meaning, roughly, Marcher-Lord (or, Duke) Kasai Qianke, founder (or, member of the founding family) of the Qianke clan, ruler of the Jianghu domain

                                                            In Riverspeak, the given name comes first and the family name comes last, hence Kasai Qianke or Iysen Shiba.

                                                            In Dragontongue (or High-and-Low Realm, which Dragontongue is largely derived from), the family name comes first and the given name goes last, so Qianke Kasai or Shiba Iysen.

                                                            In formal events and soirees, nobles are probably going to be introduced in Dragontongue. 99.99999% of commoners don't speak Dragontongue (Riverspeak has 36 characters, while Dragontongue has hundreds, if not thousands), so most nobles go by the Riverspeak version of their names. Hell, most nobles only 'know' Dragontongue with it's importance to the arts. In most places, it isn't a 'conversational" language, but the language of calligraphy, poetry, high-literature, etc etc etc.

                                                            Kasai can read (and write) Dragontongue, as well as speak it, but he is rusty in both.
