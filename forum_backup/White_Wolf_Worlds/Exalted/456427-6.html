            I think, at the end of the day in a white room duel, a battle between a Dawn and a Terrestrial with similar builds and experience is almost always going to end with the Dawn winning.

            But, if a Water Aspect can pull the Dawn into a fight in the middle of a lake, or a Fire Aspect can fight in the middle of a massive burning building, or the Terrestrial can fly around and shoot the Dawn who only has melee charms and can't respond, the environmental factors might give them a huge or even insurmountable advantage.                             Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                            It's better to have new players be proud of the fact that they defeated the dreaded Wyld Hunt than to have the PCs be fatally blindsided by Dragon-Blooded competence.

                            I've joked before now that the Udon Comic captures the experience of 2e mechanical problems rather well 4/5 Dragon-Blooded get taken out the fight without much of a fuss, the 5th uses a bullshit charm the players hadn't prepared for causing the Dawn to kill the Eclipse causing their players to become disatisified and leave resulting in the game disintegrating.
                            Sometimes I play around with house ruling in a Fate Point system similar to what the WH40K TRPG line has. Something that gives the players an opportunity to burn a finite resource which can only be earned by achieving big things which allows them to survive an otherwise hopeless situation, if only just. In the stuff for that line that I have run, having fate points encouraged players to take some risks, live on the edge, and try some cool and interesting things without the fear that they will immediately gib their character. It wouldn't work for every game or campaign, but I think it would fit with a lot of the more pulpy Exalted campaigns. After all, narrow escapes from certain death have always been a part of mythology and pulp fiction. But IDK, it has always seemed to me like a good way to have your cake and not have it murder your players because they walked into a situation not fully prepared.

                            Originally posted by nalak42 View Post

                                    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                    Huh. Yeah that does seem to be the common exaltation narrative, doesn't it? I dunno what to say. It's something I hope 3E clarifies. Even though starting characters probably still default to 0.5-1 year after exaltation, it would be nice to know how it all goes, especially since "heroic mortals who exalt during play" is still a cool game premise.
                                    Okay, turns out this was in fact clarified. The Heroic Mortals sidebar has rules for Exalting mid-game, and the way it works is you get the basic 'Exalt' template goodies like healing and not-bleeding-out and not-dying-of-disease immediately, a Limit Trigger, along with 10 out of your 15 standard Character Generation Charms. Then at the end of the current story you gain another 5 Charms, 2 dots of Willpower, 2 dots each in your Primary and Secondary Attributes, and one dot among your Tertiary Attributes.
