                            This is really fabulous. You give a really high-quality summary of all of the Craft issues I can think of seeing or experiencing, including the ones you don't actually share - it's nice to see that in a compact, low-drama form.

                            The system is harder to evaluate mechanically, but I love the feel of it - almost everything you're doing there is grounded immediately in Here's Another Cool Thing You Can Do, as opposed to the canon system's Have Some More Abstract Resources. I was excited about playing a crafter, reading this, in a way I just... wasn't, reading the actual book.

                            My immediate feel, with no actual playtesting, is that your minion-making powers are overcosted; pulling a t-rex out of your pocket is amazing, sure, but for 25m you can do a heck of a lot - and it does require you to have a statue of a t-rex lying around, after all...

(Goodness knows 2e made it possible to game your way up to Resources 5 pretty trivially, but I'm not sure that's something to retain.) On the other hand, I really like the idea of the crafter saying, "Oh, yeah, I can do that, give me five minutes" for mundane issues...

                            ... hm. So, okay, Solar Charms sometimes work on something like voodoo logic: you go a tiny part of the way, and your magic and superlative skill fills in the rest. What if these worked on similar grounds - where any notional effort in the right direction can be reinforced into a normally-impossible result? No, you can't make diamonds from literally nothing, but given a steady supply of coal and a mundane furnace, you can churn one out as a project. No, you can't magic up a trebuchet out of thin air, but you can build one out of bundles of reeds, woven into impossibly-sturdy bundles. No, you don't have a wagonful of salt, but you do have a lake, and in an hour you can filter every gram of trace silts out of that water. In general, normally-impossible craft projects with basically mundane end results become possible as projects; with CNNT, they become basically instant.

                            Maybe that doesn't actually fix my issues, but it feels better to me than "Bing!"ing things into existence.
