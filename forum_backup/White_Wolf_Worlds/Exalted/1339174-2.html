                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                Are you against suboptimal choices in general (in which case I REALLY don't get the core complaint as there are far more egregious balance issues in Exalted like my Axe/Chopping Sword example)?
                Yes. That there are worse examples doesn't matter, the thread's about the Shield Tag. I house rule the Axe/Chopping Sword thing too. I've been considering redoing the whole base weapon/armor system to something closer to Storypath: get rid of the base stat blocks, and have everything based on Tags with rated Tag to build equipment without creating these sorts of issues (which got easier once Storypath came out and did the math for me).

                Or is there a specific issue with shields that makes this special which I don't get?
                There are specific issues with shields as well, mostly that the thing in the setting that's consistently show as using shields the most, have no reasons to use them mechanically: armies. A Small BG defending a commander or sorcerer with shields and a Full Defense + Defender Other combo can at least serve that function. A legion carrying tower shields has no real point to it.

                All published shields are medium.
                The Shield Tag is not restricted to medium weapons. The writers and developers have simply realized that the Shield Tag is useless or detrimental if your weapon doesn't have a bonus to Parry to it. The Shield Tag was removed from the backer version of the core book from at least one weapon because of this (on a weapon that isn't strictly a shield either), as the Shield Tag doesn't actually work as a general "defensive weapon" Tag. Thus the one mundane and two (IIRC) Artifact shields all being medium weapons doesn't really show much.

                I think we have fundementally different interpretations of what Withering damage is and what weapon damage represents.
                Possibly, but your arguments so far have been about how much "better" swords are for killing people, with isn't what Withering damage does, or what weapon damage represents. Withering damage dictates who has the tactical advantage in the form of Initiative. Weapon damage represents how good your weapon is at claiming that advantage on a hit. Big heavy weapons don't have high damage just because of how much force they impart when they strike, but because even if an attack with one doesn't impact to deal damage, it's more controlling. It's easier to force someone into an inferior position with a tetsubo than a club. The larger weapon requires moving farther to simply avoid, more energy to block, is more winding as you try to roll with the attack, etc.

                Shield bashes aren't "unserious" attacks. A scutum's boss is meant to break your ribs. A hopolite's heavy round shield's rim is reinforced for being used to break your neck or bash your skull open. Obviously they do more than this, but the point is when someone's using a shield to hit someone, they mean it, just like a sword, or any other weapon. So that part from the book is covered.

                Nothing your saying, whether you want to debate it or not, justifies why the Shield Tag has a damage reduction in it.

                    If you've got a light weapon (which if we're talking mechanically optimal play rather than rule of cool narrative then you almost certainly have), you're only using the offhand for defence and making all your attacks with your weapon.                     If you're being mechanically suboptimal for the sake of image and using a Medium weapon... and still want a mechanical advantage... then the same logic applies. You take a duplicate weapon. Or you give up Grappling to take a different tag -- and there's one tag that gives a defensive benefit.

                    Heavy weapon then you're still going Shield and Lance for the same reasons.

                    But if you want a defensive bonus, shields are already the mechanically optimal choice. And we're talking about buffing the best possible option. I'm open to that -- I made my suggestions for the Shield Tag and if you want to add other Tags to Shields, then I'd back Smashing (and be open to Balanced and Flexible if you really want to keep buffing what is already the best option in the game).

                    But to address your point: your reason for taking literally the best option in the game for Defensive bonuses is because it is literally the best option in the game. I don't see how you could make shields better at defending people without throwing off the balance. Which then takes you into the realm of using shields for other things, such as offensive weapons -- and clearly that's a direction some people would like to go in.
