                        Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                        Can I use feasible instead? Realistic?
                        Use viable. Use it fiercely and excessively, such that it is branded into the skull of your enemy.

                        Anyway, here are those rituals I promised you.

                        Eiderdown Death (•)
                        At sunset, the thaumaturge anoints the sick and dying with sunflower oil (or that of any local staple crop). This ensures that he will sleep peacefully until dawn, no matter what pain wracks his body. Should he die before the sun's fully risen, his lower soul accepts the way of things, and bothers no one unless they touch the corpse.

                        Wisp-Catching Shell (•)
                        The thaumaturge has knowledge of a certain symbol, the pronunciation of which is lost to the ages. By scribing it on some small container (such as a seashell or a cricket cage), she may use it to catch someone's dying breath. Once caught, that mote of dying Essence can be used in the treatment of the same condition that caused it; this reduces any penalties to a Medicine roll by 3.

                        A gathered breath only lasts for a few days unless stored in a hermetic container, but can be used as a component for antidotes or medicine with a longer shelf life.

                        Raiton's Delight (•)
                        By leaning close to a corpse and meeting its gaze, a thaumaturge may see what made it. Deep in the dark of its eyes, there is an impression of the last significant thing it saw in life, preserved until the skull is empty.
                        A blind thaumaturge may still use Raiton's Delight, but must sniff or lick the corpse's eyeballs to do so.

                        Beckon the Yellow Prince (••)
                        Through a hour-long ritual dance, a thaumaturge can turn the victim of an illness into a conduit for divinity. At the end of the dance, roll (Charisma + Occult) against the Morbidity of the illness. On a success, a little god of that disease is contacted, and may speak and be spoken to through the sufferer's mouth and wounds. On a failure, the same thing happens, except that the spirit is offended by the disturbance, ill-tempered by nature, or just an idiot.

                        Laugh in Saturn's Face (••)
                        Under the noonday sun, the thaumaturge cuts out the heart of another (or her own, if she can bear the pain). Whispering sweet nothings to the heart, she keeps it pumping and its owner alive, just long enough for a live songbird to be sealed inside his chest. Then the heart dies, while the subject lives on for ever and ever, or at least until the songbird slips free.

                        A subject of Laugh in Saturn's Face can heal lethal damage to his Incapacitated health level, and never dies from sickness, deprivation, or age (though he still grows old). If the songbird is released, he wastes away over the course of three days, his soul denied reincarnation and afterlife.

                        (Though, now that I look at them, I'm not sure they're what you were looking for.)

                                                The trouble with "If only there was a thaumaturge to keep the fires alive" type stories is that specific thaumaturgies are supposed to be ultra-rare. Thaumaturges are "rare," and most know only 1 ritual, and most will know unique rituals. The odds that your village gets the one specific thaumaturge they need are vanishingly small. The odds they do so repeatedly are statistically zero. It's way, way more likely to have an Exalt show up (particularly a Dragonblooded).
