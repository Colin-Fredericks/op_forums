    3e Sidereals: Better infernalists than Infernals?

    10-31-2025, 09:40 PM
    A discussion on another forum suddenly made me dwell on a point that really sums up my major concern with 3e Infernal Charm design. 3e Sidereals includes a Charm called Chains of Adorjan in which a Sidereal can command an actual manifestation of Adorjan in battle, in the form of a deadly vortex. This is far and away the most direct form of Yozi intervention in Creation portrayed in the setting so far. The Charm even specifies she can see through it and fall in love (a bad thing, of course!) just to make clear this is a portion of the actual entity.

    It also opens the distressing precedent in terms of Charm design that there could be other Sidereal Charms directly invoking the power of the Yozis. Somewhere in Yu-Shan there might be a Sidereal who calls on the ever-reliable advice of his friend the Ebon Dragon to look for tax loopholes, or a Chosen of Serenity who manifests Malfeas as a ballroom to perfect dance beyond mortal limit

    This is concerning to me because the most consistent thing I've heard about 3e Infernals is that the Yozis are supposed to be de-emphasised in their Charm design. That they are not using Yozi Charms, that they are lesser in terms of communion with their fallen titan sponsors. And I've always questioned what that actually contributes to the fun of playing Infernals. Now I question what the point even was when Sidereals can literally tag team a Yozi to come attack an Infernal-which on it's own, is fine, Adorjan is not known for mercy towards her allies. It's just shocking to me this is a Sidereal Charm while Infernals aren't even allowed to touch real Yozi power. I truly hope I'm missing something here as the kickstarter looms nearer, because on the face of things while it IS an intensely cool Charm, it is just baffling to me that Sidereals are allowed to use a Charm that is literally a more direct conduit to Yozi power than every non-RotS published 2e Infernal Charm.

They are still damn well capable of drawing on the Yozis' power, being able to start creating soul pantheons and inner worlds from the get-go. That's not coming from the Solar Exaltation, that's coming from the Essence of Hell that suffuses it. Not to mention 2e Infernals had to wait until Essence 6 to do that.

      If your Infernal power fantasy was being able to use the Yozis' Charms, then yes, you're going to be disappointed by 3e Infernals. Nevertheless, it's still going to be clear they're mainlining the Yozis' power, partaking of their nature.

                  Because those two things are worlds apart?

                  "Summon a fragment of Adorjan to do stuff on the battlefield" is the same as walking into Cecelyne and scooping a bucket of her sand up to do ritual stuff with. It's not communing with her or even, really, *commanding* her to do anything. The only thing it represents is Heaven's very small authority over the prison of Malfeas.

                  Channeling the Yozi's Charms was changed for several reasons.
                  1) It meant you couldn't design Charms that fit your Infernal. Even if they could do so accidentally, the actual design constraint is whether they fit the Yozi. (I know people largely ignored this to create Charms Of Therapy for the Yozi, but the text's intent is pretty strong.)
                  2) It meant every Charm had to be evaluated for what it "said about the Yozi" on some grander setting level, and that's just not a useful thing to saddle Charms with as a design restriction.
                  3) It limited what you could pull from. Malfeas had to build an entire sub-tree just to start *gesturing* to Ligier-themed Charms, rather than just... being able to plonk some Ligier Charms down directly.

                  but it all rings a bit hollow when there's no substance, no jump-off point, no narrative continuation to it.
                  This is a really extreme thing to leap to from a frankly not-that-significant slight metaphysical twibble to the Charmset.

                  If "using the Yozi's actual Charms" was really this load-bearing of a pillar for you then, as mentioned, yes, 3e will not satisfy you, but I think you're ascribing a lot more value to that concept than was ever really there.

                              I think I can see the issue that the OP sees, albeit I don't necessarily agree. I have a problem of extremes here:

                              2e Infernals were practically extensions of the Yozi that had to paint their individuality with the colours they were given (so to speak). That does give an "infernal" feel in that you're an extensions of the "gods of hell" (so to speak), but Infernals were supposed to have free will - you had the Akuma for being an extension of Yozi will. I can see how one would want Infernals to be "impressive on their own right".

                              But if the proposed solution it's doing a 180°, and trying to be as sepparate from the Yozi as possible, "Yozi like but having as little to do with the actual Yozi as possible", then I would agree with the OP: That doesn't feel very "infernal" to me. I wouldn't like if Yozi get ignored, after all, they are the demon-gods. They're Hell itself. Every demon has something to do with them. They're important to the Lore in ways the average demon isn't (that they were the original creators of the world that want to come back it's interesting), being a disconnected "Yozi-like creature" is hardly going to fell as badass to me.

                              That being said, I do think it's possible to get that "prestigious infernal feel" w/o needing to go back to 2ed where you were defined by your chosen Yozi patrons.

                              Heck, the very OP proves it: Does summoning a manifestation of a Yozi suffices to make Siderals feel like "good Infernalists"? ... well, why shouldn't Infernals be able to do things like that? (not the same thing, mind you, but akin to it - ways to "manifest/take power from Hell itself", that's easy to relate to the Yozi because they're the geography there).

                              I don't know what the authors are thinking to do, but it doesn't seem contradictory to conceptualize being the master of "infernal related powers" (including manifesting the will/power of the actual gods of hell), while not being extensions of two particular Yozi.
