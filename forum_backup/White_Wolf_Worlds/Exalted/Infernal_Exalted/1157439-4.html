            I find them to be interesting in theory, as something to read, but in game terms I agree with you about the limitations.

            I was thinking some more about the other splats. Lunars aren't really about being Gaia's lover, living on the Moon, per-se. Nor are Solars about having 4 arms or carrying a laurel wreath. Alchemicals aren't about being spherical and having robo-cancer and hating your family. Dragonblood aren't, despite the name, about being dragons.

            The point is really to have different kind of heroes, drawing inspiration from different mythos. Lunars are Moon-heroes, inspired by lycanthropes and so on. Solars are culture and mortal/demigod heroes. Abyssals draw their themes from vampires, mummies, liches, necromancers, Dracula, etc, not from He Who Holds In Thrall and Abhorrence of Life.

            Yes, in-setting, Solars draw their power-theme from the Unconquered Sun, but in reality they have Larceny charms so you can play the greatest pulp thief, not because the UCS is really good at Larceny.

            Essentially, Infernals should draw their themes from the things the Yozis draw from; the Greek Titans, Vedic demons, Cthulu-esque elder god-themes, the Chinese Underworld, Indo-European and Mesopotamian primordial chaos serpents, Mesopotamian mythology, ancient Jewish apocalypticism, etc. And throw in some green demonfire and Western occultism, sure.

            But I don't think the writers should be saying "Isidorus is a boar-singularity so we should write charms for that".

                                I don't find "Yozis Charms" lame or not lame exactly but....

                                The Yozis are to me not, at their heart, things that are like "mechanisms" or "robots" which are "made" of "Charm mechanics" which somehow literally and absolutely define them and which can only be applied in an exactly rigid and formula following manner by their Chosen.

                                They are made of mad, flowing, vainglorious power and monomaniacal principle which will flow as how it chooses, and which flow through the hands of their chosen as and when they choose, according to improvisation, caprice and whim, bound only by their internal passions.

                                Sidereals inspired some of the design for the Infernals in having rigid Charms which players have to be clever to get the most out of.

                                "Rigid Charms, which players have fun subverting" makes sense for the Sidereals - bureaucrats bound by rules and seers bound by Fate, that *should*, by all that is right and planned by Heaven's will, inhibit their ability to make things work out mostly as they please rather than as it must (and yet, and yet...)

                                But for the Yozis Chosen? (I mean, they're not exactly moe-robot papa Autochthon, who might actually, if anything is, be feasibly made of rigid, mechanical magic-forms that *literally* exist in the world of the setting...).

                                        You know, much as I dislike this kind of thread necromancy, of all thread I can say that this is one that at least has some basis for revival, since in the time since it was first discussed we've gotten two more Exalted Charm sets to serve as a basis for speculation on how those kinds of things can work.

                                        But that's not really a subject of concern to myself at the moment.

                                        It's a funny thing that a Wikipedia search of the topic of stomach rumblings made on a whim should ultimately lead me to the topic of the subculture in 17th century France of fortune tellers who also engaged in practices of poisoning, abortions and supposedly even the provision of Black Masses. The logic goes that they would start off learning what it was that their clientele hoped would be predicted for them and ultimately lead to the purpose of alternately providing potions of varying effectiveness to achieve those ends (then as now most such people would seek advice for grievances of love and rivalry) or outright claiming that they could perform ceremonies allowing the Devil to be entreated to bestow their heart's desire.

                                        There have been times in the past where I've been sceptical of the idea of Satanism in terms of people actually knowingly worshipping a deity that stands for evil, and that still doesn't seem to be the case. It looks more like... the right kinds of people can be conned into believing that their desires are being unmet by God, and thus there's an impulse to see if the opposition might provide for them instead. In cases such as that, it might be that the pageantry and invocation of the taboo is all about overwhelming the mark with the grandeur of it all in a manner that suckers them in (while also conveniently being such a violation of social norms that it can be hard to report having been swindled). It certainly seems to contribute a lot to the image of Satanists featured in a lot of more recent horror fiction. It's the kind of thing that I've often imagined to be the practical basis of Yozi worship in the setting, and even the message given to and promoted by the Infernal Exalted themselves; that Heaven takes you for granted, while Hell is ready to take you under its wing.

                                        And that image of infernal symbolism as a perversion of the norm that seems to pass you by, something about that struck a chord to me as what could be the structure of the Green Sun Princes.

                                        Each Solar Ability kind of portrays its own little miniature heroic narrative. The difference between Melee and Brawl isn't just in the weapon they use, there's a distinct but related personality to either of them; Melee is a bit more serene and precise, Brawl has things like pulling out hearts to incinerate in your fist. They all have a character that, even if it doesn't necessarily constrain the kinds of people that uses them, provides motifs to inform their design and make them easier to grasp.

                                        The developers still refer to Infernals as a twisted distortion of the Solars, and it makes me wonder if that could not work down to the level of the Abilities; take the driving character underlying each one and figure out a personality that would come across as a version that is subversive, appropriated to a more callous or self-involved nature or given particularly violent and domineering extremes. Within that structure, the aesthetics of the Yozis are merely the means of achieving that personality, rather than the personality in their own right. The alter might be decked out with a statue of Baphomet, black candles and skulls, but they're not really the object of worship they're just representative of the taboo that you're trying to thrive within.

                                        I think that kind of thing could ideally make the Green Sun Princes feel cohesive while their power covers a variety of divergent forms.

                                        One could even derive a point of theme from the idea that these are Yozi qualities subordinated to the image of the dread sorcerer king, that for all their pretences they still find themselves in the position of having to rely upon the Essences of the Solars, implicitly acknowledging the supremacy of those that overthrew them.

                                        I, for one, feel as though that image of being a figure of phenomenal cosmic power that so many seemed to find attractive before fits rather nicely as the twisted counterpart to the persona of being the teacher and holy prophet. A pretty large cross section of what a lot of people found so compelling about the Green Sun Princes of yesterday, the more philosophical and transformative stuff, that seems to me like it could all go under the umbrella of Lore alone. Like, maybe some of it spills over into Integrity.

                                        I have approximate knowledge of many things.
                                        Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.
