    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post

    He is whatever your storyteller wants him to be.
    now that I've had a coffee and woke up, I'm going to elaborate on my post about the UCS not giving a shit.

    First, What is the Unconquered Sun? He is perfection. He is the ultimate example of anything that anyone could do, and he can it it flawlessly. That is what the core of his essence is: perfection. So the UCS is born-made and the Primordial War happens and yadda yadda yadda, Solars, Usurpation, Jade Prison, let's go.

    The old lore is that the Solars grew vain/bad/hedonistic enough that he Sol Invictus turned his face away from them in disgust or what have you, and just stopped giving a shit about Creation. He is the unequivocal King of the Heavens by right of his being better than everybody at everything (yeah, there's some poilitics and Maidens but whatever. UCS is the Head Honcho).

    So the Jade Prison sat around for a millenia and UCS didn't give a shit. The Solars are back and he does not give a shit. and here's where my basic premise comes in: The Unconquered Sun CANNOT give a shit or there would be no story.

    Sol Invictus used to have stats (which, lets face it, was a bad idea). He could have easily, easily, whupped FaFL and MoW in a single day, but he didn't. There's a problem with having a God that is both Benevolent and Capable- it immediately raises the question of why doesnt he just solve the problem himself?. I'm not going to touch on real world religions and myths because its a derailing quagmire, but basically: there's a reason the gods are always unable to do stuff or too petty to do stuff. Hell, you can google "the problem of evil" and read a literal library of apologia about this subject. If the UCS cared about the world, he would have fixed everything. He's not the God Emperor of Mankind from Warhammer, who is tied down to a throne (and sorta dead). He's not the gods of DnD, who are bound by laws to not interfere because there's too much opposition on every side. So, the story depends on him not caring. There's literally nothing preventing him from coming down here.

    Accelerator 's post depends on the assumption that the Unconquered Sun, basically the King of Heaven, does not know about the Infernals and wonders what his reaction would be to finding one. Is there no one in Heaven, no member of the Celestial Bureaucracy, no God, no spirit, no one at all, who has noticed an Infernal in the last 5 years and told him about it? Not one single servant or messenger? No Lunar has ever seen one and told Luna herself? He never once looked down at his Chosen and noticed half of them have been warped or twisted? Ridiculous. Preposterous. Inconceivable.

    The only way the whole shebang works is if the Unconquered Sun simply doesn't give a shit about the Solars or Creation or anything, either because he's callous or he's too wrapped up in the Games of Divinity. There's nothing else stopping him from jumping down and killing each Deathlord one by one otherwise.

    It's up to the Storyteller to invent a reason why.


    Just for fun, here's a minor and slapdash things he didn't get off his glorious golden ass about:
    Balorian Crusade
    Contagion
    Usurpation
    Jade Prison
    Death Lords
    Abyssals
    Infernals
    Breaking of the Mask
    Wyld Hunt
    Scarlet Empress

    Now probably someone is gonna jump on this list and explain why, no really he did something on one of those or couldnt because xyz, but piece by piece it spells out what spent too much time talking about.

    The Unconquered Sun doesn't care about any of us.

    Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.
    -Chausse

                        2005 Sun (15 years ago, late 1e) probably reacts like Zeus or Odin who looks more Apollo - a canny, calculating god, who is "The Best" and holds pride of place because he is calculating in the use of power, with a fair bit of hubristic pride, lust, sentimentality and other human flaws and virtues which can be his undoing and make him more admirable and human than he seems.                                             My feeling has always been that his reaction to Infernal Exalted would be to laugh at the foolishness of the Yozis, who put in all the work to research and corrupt Exaltations and yet still clearly don't understand what Exaltation is, how it really works, or why it defeated them.

                                            Exaltation is a way for gods (and others) to bestow their powers on mortals. It isn't a tool or a weapon. It's not, in and of itself, a way to make loyal soldiers or armies. When the UCS first sent his Exaltations out into the world, he was placing profound trust in humanity. That trust has sometimes been betrayed or shaken, but it's still the cornerstone of how Exaltation works. The Creation-Ruling Mandate was just a formal statement of a reality that the Incarnae had irrevocably established the moment they sent their Exalted into the world - the reality that the world now belonged to humanity, not to the gods.

                                            Whatever they may have thought, the Yozis, in creating the Infernal Exalted, were not challenging or threatening the Creation-Ruling Mandate (let alone the Celestial Incarnae, who are largely retired in any case.) They were endorsing it - signing their names to it, irrevocably granting their power and glory to the very humans and Exalted who defeated them, in a surrender more absolute and final than the end of the Primordial War itself. Unlike the Yozis, the Unconquered Sun understands that it is the human heart behind an Exaltation that makes the actual decisions and that decides what that Exaltation actually means. They're not going to change that just by painting the Chosen green and granting them Infernal magic.

                                            That doesn't mean he wouldn't be concerned too, of course - Infernal Exalted are still dangerous, especially if they listen to demons whispering in their ears when they shouldn't (a problem all the Exalted have had in the past.) But ultimately he's going to know at a glance that this won't go the way the Yozis want it to.
