                Cross-posted from Improving Mortal Opposition:

                Originally posted by Blackwell View Post
                In D&D, the class progression table says that your level 20 bard must have 150hp and +8 base attack bonus, even if he spent all 20 of those levels doing nothing but playing magic songs on his lute. It also says he can't have a base attack higher than +8, even if he spent those 20 levels stabbing people with a knife. He can't cast Meteor Storm, or have an animal companion.

                Exalted doesn't create exclusive character types. The "wizard" choose to be good in combat as well. The option is there to be no better than a mortal in a fight, but it's not exactly weird to me that I've never seen anyone choose it?
                Yes, Exalted lets you build Minmax.

                The suggestion is you, uh, don't.

                Exalted works fine if you have a combat character. It works fine if you have a wizard who is also a combat character. It starts to fall down when you have five combat characters who are also wizards who are also master socialites. And this isn't a bug: if you want to play a hyperpowered game about face meltingly awesome super-heroes, Exalted lets you do that. But the story you're telling isn't The Avengers (Earths Mightiest Heroes united against equally powerful threats), it's One Punch Man (you HILARIOUSLY outclass the opposition and your story is about ennui).

                Unless you houserule like crazy to invent new antagonists who can match five world-class combatants with Solar powers generously laddled on top (see Sandact6's Mask of Winters or Kyeudo's idea of making Mortals Mini-Exalts with their own Excellencies).

                And there's nothing wrong with playing Minmax (he's probably the most interesting character in Goblins). And there's nothing wrong with houseruling. And there's nothing wrong with just winning all the time with no effort. Exalted is a game. The point is to have fun. This isn't a lecture on bad-wrong-fun. Enjoy yourself.

                But mortals are supposed to matter. They're supposed to be a source of drama and opposition under the default assumptions of the game. And the six sample PCs we're given (and the 1-5 difficulty chart and the explaination of the QC dicepools) all support this.

                If no-one has made Mirror Flag (Thrown 2, sample Eclipse) or Iay (Melee 2, sample Twilight) in your games and has always rocked Melee 5 on their wizard... then, yeah, sure, mortals don't matter to your group. But that's not how the game presents the setting (or the mechanics): that's an idiosyncracy of your games (and, I imagine, the games of most folks who started with Exalted in 2E, because 2E absolutely presented mortals as not mattering).
