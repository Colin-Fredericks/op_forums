                    For what it’s worth, yeah, I say Ragara over Cainan as winner in the ‘Hardest Published DB Tourney’ right now.                     - Ragara has more motes, more (a lot more) health levels and one more willpower.
                    - Ragara has 16 die base attacks v 14 die. He does 2 dice less damage but the same Overwhelming value, which is pertinent when you consider the soak involved (see below).
                    - Base Join Battle of 10 die v 9. Cainan can match him but only if he spends a mote.
                    - Parry 7 v Parry 6.
                    - Cainan’s horrific Essence 5 Anima Flux is nerfed, while Ragara’s soak and hardness boost and ability to ignore a wound penalty is untouched, which leads me to…
                    - Cainan’s soak/hardness is a respectable 13/7. At bonfire, Ragara’s is 21/11, even before we think about Impervious Skin of Stone.
                    - Ragara has the Holy Grail combat charm for DBs – a cap-ignoring accuracy booster – in Heart-Seeking Rapacity. Against Cainan, he’d very likely not have to spend any willpower to use it.
                    - Speaking of duels, when the motes get low Rumbling Earth Rebuke is a fight-ender here.                         So, I probably should have mentioned sooner: I'm a pretty cautious player, and when I fight against myself, the games can get a little grindey. You've been warned now, I guess.

                        Here is our Lunar, Sigiled Bolan:

                        Sigiled Bolan
                        Full Moon Caste

                        Essence: 1; Willpower: 6; Join Battle: 9 dice (+10)
                        Personal Motes: 16; Peripheral Motes: 38 motes

                        Strength: 4
                        Dexterity: 5
                        Stamina: 3
                        Presence (or any other Attribute): 5
                        Wits: 4

                        Athletics 3 (rushes)
                        Awareness 5
                        Brawl 5 (Tyrant Lizard form)
                        Dodge 3
                        Integrity 3
                        Performance 3
                        Resistance 3
                        Thrown 3 (improvised)

                        Intimacies:
                        Defining Principle: I’m a m*********ing T-REX!
                        Major Principle: I’m just a vampire for love.
                        Minor Tie: Blue Thumb, his white swan familiar (enjoys riding)

                        Combat
                        Withering Attack (unarmed): 14 (+10) dice (11 damage, minimum 1)
                        Decisive Brawl Attack: 10 (+10) dice; 9 (+10) dice to control grapples
                        Evasion 2 (+5), Parry 5 (+5), Parry 6 as T-Liz
                        Soak 15, Hardness 5 (Tyrant Lizard); personal Soak 10, Hardness 0 (heavy mortal armor)
                        HLs: -0x1/-1x2/-2x4/-4x2/incap

                        Charms
                        Finding the Needle’s Eye: 3m, Supplemental, reroll three (four if using tail) 1s on an attack, enemy’s defense takes a -1 penalty. Can attack through full cover, though enemy gets +3 defense.
                        Agile Beast Defense: 1m, Reflexive, gain 1i if defense is successful, gain +1 defense if enemy has lower Initiative and/or rolls any 1s.
                        Bending Before the Storm: 2m, Reflexive, halve all penalties to defense. If attacker has lower Initiative, ignore them instead.

                        Ox-Body Technique: you know this one.

                        Towering Beast Form: 15m, 1wp,Simple, assume Legendary Size form. Each round, make a Decisive attack, or spend 8+m on a Withering attack, or spend 5m to maintain this charm.
                        Ferocious Biting Tooth: 2m, Supplemental, +1 Overwhelming or add 1 bonus success to Decisive damage.
                        Divine Predator Strike: 2m, Supplemental, add 1 Decisive damage, or (attack 10s + 1) to post-soak Withering damage.
                        Snarling Hound Strife: 4m, Supplemental, when Clashing with an enemy with equal or lower Initiative, up to three of their 1s subtract successes.
                        Grizzly Bear Embrace: 2m, Supplemental, add grapple roll extra successes to the Initiative roll. Against lower-Initiative opponents, also add them to the control roll. If in T-Liz form, enemy counts as 5 Initiative lower for this effect.

                        I wanted competent stats, more optimized than Drogath but still not pushed to the systems limits. In particular, the charms represent a cross-section of common starting combat charms more than a specific plan. This is deliberate - I want to ballpark whether this fight works, not find the One True Build for Ragara-hunting.

                        And yes, Bolan would definitely be stronger as a No-Moon. I have no idea why the temptation is so strong to put the strongest combat powers on caster splats, but that sure seems to be a thing.

                        Here we go:

                        Bolan Vs Ragara
                        JOIN BATTLE
                        Ragara spends 4m and rolls (10+4=14) dice for 8 successes, starting at 11i.
                        Bolan spends 10m and rolls (9+10=19) dice for 9 successes, starting at 12i. His Anima is Glowing.

                        ROUND 1:
                        Ragara: 11i, 10wp, 12/32m
                        Sigiled Bolan: 12i, 6wp, 16/28m, 1a

                        Bolan will spend 15m, 1wp to assume Tyrant Lizard form. His Anima is Burning.

                        Ragara, we may assume, has some idea of what both the Realm’s archenemy and Creation’s most infamous predator are capable of. He knows his best defense against the coming storm is a clash, and knows he wants to aim first. He also really, really wants to attack now - a Withering attack that hit for even a single point of damage would put his action first next round, letting him aim then. But you need to choose your battles against Celestial Excellencies, so Raga will aim - and also spend 2m to raise his Parry by 1 against an attack he knows will never come, entering Fire Aura.

                        ROUND 2:
                        Ragara: 11i, 10wp, 15/32m
                        Sigiled Bolan: 12i, 6wp, 16/18m, 2a

                        Bolan senses a trap, but Towering Beast Form won’t let him play the waiting game. It’s time for a clash. Bolan spends 19m, 1wp to make a grapple Gambit with Finding The Needle’s Eye, Grizzly Bear Embrace, a full Excellency, a bonus success, and (after Ragara declares PCD) Snarling Hound Strife.

                        Ragara spends 9m, 2wp, and expends Fire Aura to use Portentous Comet Deflection and a full Excellency. He rolls (16+6+3=25) dice with one automatic success, for 13+1 successes… but he also rolled two 1s, for a final total of 12.

                        Bolan rolls (11+10=21) dice (rerolling up to three 1s) for a total of 13+1 successes. He wins by 2, which he’ll add to the remaining rolls for this grapple: an Initiative roll where (12+2=14) dice get way more than 3 successes, and a control roll where an unopposed (10+2) dice get 5 successes. Ragara’s Initiative drops to 8i for missing with a Decisive attack, his anima is Glowing, Bolan’s Initiative drops to 9i for the Gambit, his Anima reaches Bonfire, and we’re still not even halfway through the second round of this fight.

                        Bolan’s pretty sure that the Earth Aspect in heavy armor isn’t going to just slip away, and 5 rounds of control should last through a single turn, so he opts for a Savaging Withering bite attack. He spends 2m to use Divine Predator Strike, and rolls 11 dice for 6 Threshold successes (including two 10s), and thus (16-16+6+3=9) damage dice for 4 Withering damage. He’ll also drag Ragara one range band, because it’s funnier that way.

                        Ragara spends 6m to make a Withering attack at full Excellency, and Bolan is low enough on motes to leave his Parry at 6 (no grappling penalty due to Iron Jaws). Ragara rolls (16+6-1=21) dice for 10 successes, hitting for his overwhelming of 4 dice, 2 Withering damage. Ragara’s Anima reaches Burning, and he spends 2m to Reflexively raise his Integrity, and enters Earth Aura.

                        ROUND 3:
                        Ragara: 7i, 8wp, 10/25m, 2a
                        Sigiled Bolan: 12i, 5wp, 10/8m, 3a (3 rounds of grapple control)

                        Bolan (naively) hopes to end this, and cuts loose with Deadly Thrash: a Decisive Savaging attack followed by a Decisive Slam. The first attack roll 12 dice of Decisive damage for 6 successes - Ragara, however, pays 5m 3i to use Earth Bears Witness, reducing it to 3 levels of damage. Ragara’s wound penalty rises to -1, and his Anima reaches Bonfire.

                        The throw is where things get complicated. With 15 dice of Decisive damage on the line, Ragara spends 9m 1wp to fire off another Portentous Comet Deflection. The rules PROBABLY let Bolan retroactively declare Excellencies and the like to help him with the Clash, but he’d have to spend most (maybe all) of his motes to do that. This leaves him nothing for defense, and worse, it undermines to premise of this battle by turning him back into human form. So instead, he just rolls 11 dice into (16+6-1-1=20) - Ragara wins the clash, but his remaining 4i bounces off a Tyrant Lizard’s 5 hardness. Since he had no Fire Aura to expend this time, that’s Ragara’s combat action, too. Ragara resets to 3i for a technically successful Decisive attack, and is no longer in any Aura. Bolan resets to 6 thanks to Deadly Thrash.

                        ROUND 4:
                        Ragara: 3i, 7wp, 10/16m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 6i, 4wp, 10/13m, 3a

                        Bolan will once again try to grapple, spending 13m 1wp on an extra success, Finding the Needle’s Eye, Grizzly Bear Embrace, and a full Excellency. Ragara doesn’t like the look of that clash without aiming, so he spends 9m 1wp to raise his Parry by 5 (Excellency, willpower, Earth Anima), and ignore his wound penalty - it’s at (7+5-1-1=10), and Bolan rolls (11+10=21) dice, re-rolling three 1s for 10 successes exactly. With no bonus from GBE, he rolls 2 successes on 6 dice to barely pass the Initiative roll, then gets 6 successes on the unopposed control roll. He drops to 3i, and Ragara is back in the jaws of a T-Liz.

                        Next comes the savaging attack. Bolan rolls 11 dice for 5 threshold successes, dealing his minimum of 5 damage. He only rolls 2 successes.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, and Bolan spends 5m for a +2 Excellency and Agile Beast Defense, bringing his parry to 9. Ragara rolls (16-1=15) dice for 7 successes, missing. Bolan gains 1i for the successful defense, and Iron Jaws keeps him from losing a control round.

                        ROUND 5:
                        Ragara: 1i, 6wp, 10/14m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 7i, 3wp, 10/0m, 3a (6 rounds of grapple control)

                        Bolan makes another Withering Savaging attack, spending 10m on Excellency. Ragara fires off another 9m 1wp full-Excellency Portentous Comet Deflection. Bolan rolls (11+10=21) dice for 11 successes, whilst Ragara rolls (16+6-1-1=20) dice for a crazy 17, winning the clash (again without damage).

                        Ragara, perhaps for the first time in all his centuries, kinda wishes he were a Fire Aspect, and could thus keep using 1-die excellency attacks to enter Fire Aura, spend it for PCD, and wear down his foe. But in Anima Flux, he may no longer enter Earth Aura, and he has spent his combat action.

                        ROUND 6:
                        Ragara: 1i, 5wp, 10/10m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 7i, 3wp, 0/5m, 3a (3 rounds of grapple control)

                        Bolan, low on motes and unwilling to revert to human form, makes a Deadly Thrash attack. I’m not bothering to roll for the first half, which cannot break Ragara’s Hardness, but for the second, Bolan spends 2m to use Divine Predator Strike, bringing the total damage on a hit to 11. Ragara spends 9m 1wp to do his thing, and easily wins the clash, again for no damage. Bolan resets to 6i. Ragara leaves Earth Aura.

                        That also takes Ragara’s combat action.

                        (Why does Ragara buy a full Excellency even against an 11-die attack? Because, as noted above, Bolan can probably Excellency up after Ragara initiates the clash, and Ragara can’t actually see Bolan’s mote pools, so he can’t be sure whether the Lunar has one more full-dice burst in him. It’d be really obnoxious to let the two “bid” on attack charms forever, so I wouldn’t let Ragara buy more after that. Yes, the outcome of this fight comes down to details on clash-related Charm timing which have no answers in the books, and vague/contradictory ones in the Ask the Devs thread. That’s just life in 3rd Edition.)

                        ROUND 7:
                        Ragara: 1i, 4wp, 10/6m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 6i, 3wp, 0/8m, 3a

                        Bolan makes a Withering Claw attack, spending 8m 1wp to buy a success, plus 8 Excellency dice. Ragara, of course, spends 9m 2wp, for his usual bit plus an extra success. The dice sense the drama of this moment, and between the two of them, our combatants roll eight 10s on 42 dice, but in the end, Bolan wins 15-14. He rolls 3 Withering damage for 2 successes, crashing Ragara.

                        Ragara used his combat action to clash again.

                        ROUND 8:
                        Ragara: -1i, 2wp, 12/0m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 14i, 2wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        Bolan makes a Decisive Bite attack, spending 5m 1wp on success Excellency you get the picture. Guess what Ragara spends 9m 1wp to do? Ragara rolls (16+6-1=21) dice for 13 successes, Bolan rolls (11+5=16) for 7+4=11 (thanks, Furious Rampage). Ragara wins the Clash, for no damage and no turn. Bolan loses 3i for missing with a Decisive attack.

                        ROUND 9:
                        Ragara: -1i, 1wp, 8/0m, 3a (3lv damage, -1 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 11i, 1wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        Bolan makes the same attack as last round, wp and all, and rolls (11+5=16) for 10+4=14 successes. Ragara also does the same thing, just with one fewer dice as he finally reaches the limits of his essence, rolling (16+5-1=20) dice for 10 successes. Bolan has hit, and rolls 21 (thanks, Ferocious Bite) Decisive damage, rolling just 7 successes. Ragara’s wound penalty increases to -2. Bolan resets to 3i.

                        ROUND 10:
                        Ragara: -1i, 0wp, 5/0m, 3a (10lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 3i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        Bolan wants to hold onto his T-Liz form as long as possible, so he makes another Decisive attack, this time a Stomp, no charms. Ragara uses no charms either, and Bolan rolls 13 dice for only 1 success, which is weird, but Furious Rampage still makes it hit for 3 Decisive dice, 2 more levels of damage.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, also no charms. Bolan spends 1m on Agile Beast Defense for Parry 7. Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 7 successes, hitting for 4 Withering dice, 2 damage.

                        ROUND 11:
                        Ragara: 2i, 0wp, 10/0m, 3a (12lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 1i, 0wp, 0/9m, 3a

                        I’m going to finish this fight, because I really want to know the outcome, but if you’re bored, we’ve clearly answered the question. Proceed to the wrap-up, and thanks for hanging in there this long.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms. Bolan leaves his parry at (6-1=5). Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 7 successes, hitting for 4 Withering dice, 2 damage. Bolan is crashed.

                        Bolan makes a Withering Tail attack, spending 8m on Finding the Needle’s Eye plus 5-die Excellency. Ragara spends 9m on +3 parry from Excellency, and ignoring a point of wound penalty with Earth Anima, for a total of (7+3-1-1=8). Bolan rolls (12+5=17) dice, tragically only getting two 1s to re-roll, for 8 successes, hitting for 4 Withering dice, 1 damage.

                        ROUND 12:
                        Ragara: 1i, 0wp, 6/0m, 3a (12lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 3i, 0wp, 0/6m, 3a

                        Bolan reluctantly makes a Decisive Talon attack. He spends 2m on Excellency. Ragara really wants to see the big lizard crash from missing, but since he can’t raise his parry above 7, he uses no charms. Bolan rolls (13+2=15) dice for 6 successes, annoying Ragara and hitting for no damage.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, Bolan spends 1m on Agile Beast Defense, parry 7. Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 7 successes, hitting for 4 Withering dice, 2 damage.

                        ROUND 13:
                        Ragara: 4i, 0wp, 12/0m, 3a (12lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 1i, 0wp, 0/8m, 3a

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, spending 3m on Excellency, Bolan uses no charms, parry 5. Ragara rolls (16+3-2=17) dice for 3 successes, missing.

                        Bolan makes a Withering Bite attack, spending 8m on Finding the Needle’s Eye, Divine Predator Strike, and a 3-die Excellency. Ragara spends 9m on Excellency and Anima for parry (7+3-1-1=8). Bolan rolls (11+3=14) dice, with no 1s to reroll but 8 successes including 3 10s. He rolls (5+4=9) Withering dice, 4 successes, crashing Ragara.

                        ROUND 14:
                        Ragara: 0i, 0wp, 5/0m, 3a (12lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 11i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        Bolan makes a Decisive Talon attack, spending 2m on Excellency. Ragara uses no charms, leaving parry at (7-2=5). Bolan rolls (13+2=15) dice for 7+3 successes, hitting for 11 Decisive dice, 4 more damage. Bolan resets to 3i.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, Bolan’s parry is 6. Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 8 successes, 4 Withering dice, 4 damage. Bolan stays at 1i due to Legendary Size.

                        ROUND 15:
                        Ragara: 5i, 0wp, 10/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 1i, 0wp, 0/8m, 3a

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, Bolan’s parry is (6-1=5). Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 1 success, missing.

                        Bolan makes a Withering Tail attack, spending 8m on Finding the Needle’s Eye plus a 5-die Excellency. Ragara spends 9m on Excellency and Earth Anima, for parry (7+3-1-1=8). Bolan rolls (12+5=17) dice, re-rolling three 1s for 11 successes: 4 Withering dice, 2 damage.

                        ROUND 16:
                        Ragara: 3i, 0wp, 6/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 4i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        Bolan has to face the facts: wasting actions on sub-hardness Decisives is a mug’s game, and the periodic chance to crash his Legendary Size body makes it worse. So, he must accept the Limit for defying his Defining Principle, and stop being a Tyrant Lizard. He decides to make his final attack memorable, and spends 5m to make a Decisive Bite attack with Divine Predator Strike and a 3-die Excellency. Ragara leaves his parry at (7-2-1=4), and Bolan rolls (11+3=14) dice for 7 successes, including one 10: (5+2=7) Withering dice, 4 damage, crashing Ragara.

                        Bolan then reverts to human form. Ragara argues (with a 14-die Bureaucracy pool) that this technically makes him the winner, but unlike our combatants, Chejop Kejak still has WP to spend, and continues the fight.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, Bolan’s parry is 5. He rolls (16-2=14) dice for 7 successes: 4 Withering dice (now that Bolan is human, 3+ threshold successes will mean extra damage dice), 2 damage.

                        ROUND 17:
                        Ragara: 1i, 0wp, 11/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 14i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a

                        At the start of his turn, Bolan takes 1 die of damage from Anima Flux: 0 damage this time.

                        Bolan makes a Withering attack with puny human legs, spending 4m on Divine Predator Strike and a two-die Excellency. Ragara, eager to avoid a cycle of getting crashed every round as the Lunar laps up Initiative, spends 9m for his full defensive suite of parry (7+3-1=9). Bolan rolls (14+2=16) dice for 9 successes, including two 10s: (1+3=4) Withering dice, 1 damage, crashing Ragara again.

                        Ragara would like to see other options - and so would I, for variety’s sake - but he can’t take full defense, and aiming won’t help since his attacks are hitting just fine. In other circumstances, he’d run away (hopefully to meet up with some of the allies who have been headed this way in the 15 rounds since a Lunar Anima went Bonfire… but in whiteroom fights, there’s no way to get beyond Short Range, so even if Bolan never wins single movement-related roll, he’d just wait until he had 13 motes or so, then cut loose with an 18-dice-plus-Needle’s Eye 21i Decisive attack. Ragara must stand his ground and hope that Bolan misses eventually.

                        Ragara ruefully makes a Withering attack, no charms, against parry 5. He rolls (16-2=14) dice for 7 successes: 4 Withering dice, 3 damage.

                        ROUND 18:
                        Ragara: 4i, 0wp, 7/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 18i, 0wp, 0/6m, 3a

                        At the start of his turn, Bolan takes 1 die of damage from Anima Flux: 1 damage this time, still no wound penalty.

                        Bolan makes a Decisive attack, spending 6m for Finding the Needle’s Eye and a 3-die Excellency. Ragara spends 6m to bring his parry to (7+3-2-1=7). Bolan rolls (10+3=13) dice, even re-rolling three of his five 1s, he ends up with 4 successes, missing. He drops to 15i.

                        Ragara makes a Withering attack, no charms, against parry 5. He rolls (16-2=14) dice for 5 successes: 4 Withering dice, 2 damage.

                        ROUND 19:
                        Ragara: 7i, 0wp, 6/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 13i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a (1lv damage, -0 wound penalty)

                        At the start of his turn, Bolan takes 1 die of damage from Anima Flux, and this time Ragara feels safe enough to spend the 2i for Earth Anima. Bolan does take 1 damage, raising his wound penalty to -1 and he is knocked prone.

                        Bolan flurries rise from prone with a no-charm Withering attack. He rolls (8-1-3=4) dice for 2 successes to stand up, then (14-1-3=10) against parry 5 for 1 success, missing the attack.

                        Ragara makes a no-charm Decisive attack, and Bolan also declares no charms, leaving his parry at (5-1-1=3). Ragara rolls (16-2=14) dice for 3 successes (even the dice now seem tired): 5 Decisive dice, 2 damage. Bolan’s wound penalty increases to -2. Ragara resets to 3i.

                        ROUND 20:
                        Ragara: 3i, 0wp, 11/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 13i, 0wp, 0/10m, 3a (4lv damage, -2 wound penalty)

                        At the start of Bolan’s turn, he takes 1 die of damage from Anima Flux (Ragara doesn’t spend Initiative): no damage again.

                        Bolan whispers something which SURELY could not be “sic semper Tyrannosaurus,” and spends 10m to make a Decisive bite attack, human mouth notwithstanding, with Finding the Needle’s Eye and 7 Excellency dice. Ragara spends 9m to bring his parry to (7+3-1-1=8). Bolan rolls (10+7-2=15) dice, with no 1s but still 10 successes: 13 Decisive dice, 5 damage. Ragara’s wound penalty rises to -4. Bolan resets to 3i.

                        Ragara makes a no-charm Withering attack, and Bolan also declares no charms, leaving his parry at (5-2=3). Ragara rolls (16-4=12) dice for 6 successes: (12+3-10=5) Withering dice, 2 damage.

                        ROUND 21:
                        Ragara: 6i, 0wp, 7/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: 1i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a (4lv damage, -2 wound penalty)

                        Ragara makes a no-charm Withering attack, and Bolan also declares no charms, leaving his parry at (5-2-1=2). Ragara rolls (16-4=12) dice for 9 successes: (12+7-10=9) Withering dice, 5 damage. Bolan is crashed.

                        At the start of his turn, Bolan takes 1 die of damage from Anima Flux, and Ragara spends the 2i for Earth Anima. Bolan takes no damage.

                        Bolan is now in the same position as Ragara back in Round 17: trading Withering attacks won’t let him catch up, and even worse, Ragara won’t even have to risk a Decisive attack, since he can just wait for Anima Flux to finish the job. But, as a Lunar, he does have more versatility, even in the whiteroom.

                        Bolan spends 5m to shapeshift into an owl.

                        ROUND 22:
                        Ragara: 17i, 0wp, 12/0m, 3a (16lv damage, -2 wound penalty)
                        Sigiled Bolan: -4i, 0wp, 0/5m, 3a (4lv damage, -2 wound penalty)

                        Ragara now must finish this before his assailant flies away into the boundless skies of the whiteroom. He spends 6m to make a Decisive attack. Bolan saves his motes to Disengage, hoping for a very bad damage roll and keeping parry (5-2=3). Ragara rolls (16+6-4=18) dice for 6 successes: 17 Decisive dice, 8 damage.

                        Sigiled Bolan is dead, his grand duet with the Fae prince Duke Of White Stardust forever unfinished.

                        The simplest takeaway here is that this fight could go either way. There were a number of points where the outcome could be changed by a slightly different dice roll (a single additional wound outright kills Ragara!), or a slightly different action (I'm still wondering if Bolan should have chanced the grapple on Round 9), or less fortunately, a slightly different rules interpretation. The best/worst thing about 3E fights is that when they're close, 1m can be the difference between victory and defeat.

                        There are plenty of other takeaways:
                        • No DB without Portentous Comet Deflection (or some other way to defend in a grapple) would have stood a chance. If you just have to sit there and take Deadly Thrash, it lives up to its name.
                        • Because PCD-spamming is the order of the day, I think Cainan is actually the best choice for this fight. As I mentioned briefly, he can stay in Fire Aura more, and thus get more chances to combine PCD with an actual attack. Plus, inexplicably higher Overwhelming damage. Then again, Ragara didn't get to use his unique tricks to greatest effect because...
                        • I totally dropped the ball on Heart-Seeking Rapacity. I had meant to use it when spending WP on Round 7, but then counted that as just buying a success, and totally forgot to use it late in the fight, once Bolan's wound penalty hit -2.
                        • I still think it's nutzo how QCs get their full Withering dice on Decisive attacks, and how none of the writers seem to think this is a game balance issue.
                        • This fight goes worse for Bolan if he can't declare charms after a Clash is created (if you wanna get into that, start a thread - it's not a small issue), but much better if I had made the totally reasonable but technically non-canon decision that T-Liz bites are a Heavy Weapon, and thus get +2 to Clashes.
                        • Lunar wound penalties are harsh. Those guys need an Unfeeling Earth Meditation. Then again, Ragara also would have done much better if he'd had that charm.
                        • Artifacts might have helped Bolan by providing a more-reliable Withering attack or more durability after demorphing, but they also would have shortened the clock before he ran low on motes and needed to start making those non-optimal Decisives.
                        • Honestly, as cool as giant monsters are, this fight once again cemented my opinion that you don't really want to fight skilled opponents that way - the mote costs just restrict your actions so much. Bolan probably could have done better using a Pestletail or something (with the caveat that this may say more about how overpowered Pestletails are).
                        • I need to make some more all-in attacking builds, so the next one of these I run is short enough that someone actually reads it all.
                        • I listen to too much 70s rock.

Bolan spends 17m 1wp to make a grapple Gambit with Finding The Needle’s Eye, a full Excellency, a bonus success, and (after Ragara declares PCD) Snarling Hound Strife.

Ragara makes a Withering attack, spending 8m on Heart-Seeking Rapacity and a full Excellency. Bolan rolls (12+9-2=19) dice, re-rolling 3 1s for 14+1 successes, Ragara rolls (16+6+3=25) dice for 14+1 successes.

                                                    ...and the rules alone can’t tell us what happens next.


                                                    The exact scenario I’ve been dreading in these clash-heavy whiteroom fights has happened: a tie. This clash will be won by the better stunt, but of course, there were no stunts. In most Exalted fights I’ve seen, one side stunts and the other doesn’t - stunting is a mechanic which encourages a specific PC behavior, and rewards it by smoothing out the probability curve to help shield PCs from the caprice of dice when they roll for actions a zillion times more often than NPCs do. To decide which of these two has been stunting is to decide who is the protagonist.

                                                    And the protagonist wins.

                                                    The winner of this clash will probably crash their opponent, which is a nigh-certain win for Ragara, and a pretty likely one for Bolan. Perhaps more to the point, this battle is full of crucial rolls where the expected results are within a point or two of each other, so if someone’s been able to get +2 dice for free every round, it probably wouldn’t have gotten this close.

                                                    Just because this fight is a toss-up decided by who has more narrative significance doesn't mean this matchup is. That first clash will go Bolan's way more often than not (some quick math implies that Ragara averages 11.5 successes to Bolan's 12), and if Bolan wins and Withersavages Ragara, he’s back around 12i - ready for Deadly Thrash to roll 28 Decisive damage. Even with a fully-loaded Earth Bears Witness, that should mean 9 damage levels, bringing Ragara into his -2 HLs, and leaving Bolan sitting on ~23m 7i, ready to try a second grapple, this time with a real chance of crashing Ragara. That’s bad news, and what most people probably expect here. But half a success is, obviously, not super reliable.

                                                    That's the good news for DB fans. The bad news is that you can't really make Ragara as a player - his biggest advantage here is getting to apply his full Withering attack pool through Portentous Comet Deflection. A PC will only have 13 dice (with a Heavy or Paired weapon) instead of 13, and must also find their own source of non-Charm dice to simulate Blood Zenith. Of course, there is always Fire Incites Water To A Riot Of Clouds, which lets you keep your full Withing pool once per fight - technically, that's all Ragara needed here (though landing on multiple clashes by chance is a little unusual).

                                                    The good news for Lunar fans is that Ragara is probably the strongest Terrestrial in print, and Bolan is a one-hit wonder outside the UK- I mean, a merely-okay starting Full Moon with a gimmickey combat style. If he had spent his combat charms less on all-around staples (many of which aren't that useful until Essence 2-3) and more on a focused plan, he would look a lot more solid. Conversely, if this were less about testing the T-Liz, then during Round 4, Bolan probably would have flung Ragara out to Short Range, then Reflexive moved to Medium. It’s an ST call whether he can find anything that works as a Thrown weapon for that form, though, and I didn’t want to revert early and make this into yet another referendum on kiting in EX3. The alpha strike potential of a Tyrant Lizard is incredible, but I still question how much it adds if that strike fails.
