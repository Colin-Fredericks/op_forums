            so to get back to the aura bypass idea, what if you just treat all charms other than aura keyword as being balanced? That and ignore aura-lock from bonfire anima.

            You'd only be able to use individual aura charms, thus avoiding overlapping the most powerful effects. You'd still need to set up for the most powerful stuff, but you could freely combo charms that are less powerful but normally off-limits because of elemental associations.

            You'd still end up with more powerful DB overall, but I think you'd still limit the use of the ultra-powerful stuff somewhat. Plus you'd get access to all the stuff you purchased as prerequisites to get to the higher end stuff.

                                    He had melee as well, and had the options of using Stoking Bonfire Style flame-bourne interception and blinding spark distraction. On the next attacks, he had the option of using withering attacks with Stoking Bonfire Style, which would reduce in cost as is was being used. Then, as he gained initiative over the target, he could do fire dragon attacks. And if the target got crashed by him or other DB, he would take Anima damage (or bleed motes if he had a charm).

                                            Originally posted by Alistair View Post
                                            I'm not disagreeing with you regarding Steel Devil pairing (although I abhor how poorly written and balanced Steel Devil is), I'm saying that Fire Dragon's whole shtick is to go all out on the first round and absolutely obliterate someone and then spend several rounds doing nothing or attacking/defending without excellencies to go back to a decent number of motes, which I don't like. Alpha strike builds per se are dangerous to the game. You fight a single foe and insta-kill them? That's boring! You fight several ones and are now tapped out of motes after defeating a single one instantly, hence making you rely on your friends if any in order to recover some motes and do anything else at all? That feels bad!

                                            Let alone how much better styles like Earth and Water Dragon are in that they can simply allow you to hold your own for a combat of any length. Earth remains stable and powerful at any time, and Water feels like climbing uphill, with each wound penalty you take making them more and more dangerous. Fire feels like being amazing for three seconds and then useless the rest of the fight.
                                            Yeah, Steel Devil is definitely not balanced well with the other core styles, and I've never found a way to make it work well against foes with onslaught penalty negators. It's not completely useless, but it's definitely harder hit than other styles when they come up against a type of enemy that they're bad at fighting. You could say it's balanced out by how amazing it is against the enemies that it's good at fighting, but I'm kind of eh on that logic. It's really just got one schtick


                                            Fire Dragon though, I totally disagree. The only charm that encourages you to do a turn 1 alpha strike is Flash Fire Technique. In fact Flame-Flicker Stance, Perfect Blazing Blow, Essence Igniting Nerve Strike, and Smoldering Wound Attack all encourage you not to do that. They're all either defensive charms with bonuses against lower initiative foes, something you're not likely to get after resetting to base initiative, withering attacks with bonuses against lower initiative foes, something that's not going to alpha strike, or decisive attacks with extra bonuses against crashed foes, something you're not going to get off turn 1.

                                            Flash-Fire Technique gives you the option to just kill somebody right off the bat if you want to, sure, that's your choice and maybe in some cases it's the right one. Like maybe the Raksha lord that you're having dinner with has just implied that he's going to splinter the mind of one of the other guests, and you want to kill him before he gets the chance to act. That'd be a good time to do it. After that you're spent and need to rely on your allies, but that's what allies are for, and if you weren't there to kill that Raksha in one chop who knows what could have happened.

                                            On the other hand if you're facing a pair of Blood Apes with your circle and don't really feel the need to blow out your entire mote pool and half your willpower killing one of them, then don't. Remember, the lesson of Hesiesh is knowing when and how to restrain yourself. In that case just forgo Flash Fire, or use it raw without enhancing both rolls if you just want consistency, and fight using Searing Edge Attacks and/or Essence Igniting Nerve Strikes if you think they're going to be crashed. With Fire Dragon Form up you can save 2m per attack and have about the same odds of hitting after all.

                                            Then if you want you can Perfect Blazing Blow, or Fiery Blade Attack them to finish them off, or even just regular decisive them. Or be a total dick and hit a spirit with a God-Immolating Strike and a Fiery Blade Attack and set them on double-fire. There's tons of options that aren't turn 1 decisive alpha strikes.

                                                    The thing I've been doing for my games is a slightly less powerful version of omni-aura.

                                                    1. Everyone is assumed to be in every aura at all times
                                                    2. Anything that expends Aura takes you out of Aura until the beginning of your next turn.

                                                    This makes the workhorse Charms more powerful but doesn't let you combo a ton of Aura expending Charms together all at once, and makes Aura expenditure still significant because it weakens you across the spectrum of Aura Charms.
