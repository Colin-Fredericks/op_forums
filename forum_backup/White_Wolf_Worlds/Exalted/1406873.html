    What breaks if you shrink the tiers of sorcery?

    08-07-2020, 09:38 PM
    So in all the versions of Exalted, there are 3 tiers of sorcery.

    Dragonblooded and mortals get Emerald. Lunars/ Sidereals get Celestial. Solars get Adamant.

    Kinda like martial arts, only that you can't punch through tiers.

    What if there are charms that let you get higher? Dragonblooded, at Essence 4/5, can start trying to learn celestial circle sorcery. There's an extra surcharge of xp, but its possible. Lunars and Sidereals get to use Solar circle sorcery if they hit essence 5/6. Solars remain unchanged.

    Presume this for either 2e or 3e.

                              There's one other thing to consider: Is this the rule or the exception.

                              If The Dragon-Blooded can learn higher tier sorcery it breaks down the barrier between the exalted tiers, which is less important at the table (Where meaningful player contribution and impressive legendary challenges are what matter), but more important for the status quo of the setting, the less of a disparity there is between the Terrestrial and Celestial Exalted, the less significant the threat of the Silver Pact or the returned Solars is to the Realm. DB's outnumber the other exalted put together by about 20/1, so greater feats of prowess are needed to be able to combat the lands under their control, after all, unlike a Lunar cult leader or Solar chosen king, the DB can be practically everywhere at once.

                              If this Dragon-Blooded can learn higher tiers of sorcery nothing breaks and it becomes a cool way of building up a player or introducing a new threat. Much like how celestial-level exigents in 3e present credible threats of great power, but being unique their threat to the status-quo is localized. Creation at your table is the place your game is taking place in, and that can be as small as a single city whose legacy was shaped by an exigent that is fundamental to your game, but to Creation as a whole, the Exigents contributed less because they've always acted as individuals rather than a longstanding faction. A single dragon-blood who learns higher tiers of sorcery will be considered a champion of their house, and a credible threat to the Anathema scourge, that makes them uniquely suited to whatever game is being run (either as a pro or antagonist) since they stand out from their fellows.

                              In Essence that's one of the things they're apparently aiming for; artifacts and secret techniques that can heighten the power of individual exalted (such as the Mantle of Brighid) to better enable mixed play, without changing the overall status quo of creation. In short it's better to keep these as exceptions that apply to characters in this particular game, rather than the entirety of the setting.
