    The Neverborn have been insane from the moment they died, but their nihilistic obsession took time to develop--only as they acquired enough moments of semi-lucidity to understand their agonized condition, and only after their attempts at self-negation come to naught, did their moans and Whispers truly turn to all things Oblivion-borne. Compounding this dawning horror at their unthinkably dead state was the (un)natural "static" interference wrought by the ebon death-stuff of the Labyrinth.

    These are the reasons why, in the first few days after the first of the Primordials was slain, Creation's thinnest shallowings shuddered in horror as Cthonic shrieks from beyond the veil of death demanded, "CAKE! WE...MUST...HAVEGIVEDEVOURINFLICTBRINGFLOODWORLDWI TH...CAAAAAAKE!!!" Later, of course, such "Whispers" as were heard corrected the insanity-caused error, and their utterances of the word "cake," along with the soul-numbing flood of emotional resonance that accompanied them, were replaced with the word "death," as the Neverborn had actually intended.

    Ironically, the cake/ death incidents imprinted so strongly upon the survivors of that War, that "cake or death?" has become a common phrase in all languages, meaning "which will you choose: this terrible option, or this other, unexpectedly pleasant one?" Indeed, "cake or death?" has served to diminutize the existential terror normally associated with death ever since. To this day, all sentients in Creation instinctively celebrate the anniversaries of birth with some sort of cake. And to this night, from a Deathlord at his most magnanimous, to a shadow-eaten spectral berserker, no dead thing sworn to Oblivion's cause will ever offer a guest cake; neither will they steal, target, tempt with, or even acknowledge the presence of any form of cake.

    Note that Abyssal Exalted can, and often do, enjoy cake. While this infuriates the Deathlords and greatly discomfits lesser ghosts, the Neverborn so loathe the very concept of cake, that they prevent cake from causing an Abyssal to either gain or trigger Resonance in any way-- their Dark Fate completely ignores cake. As such, it is the only gesture of defiance that an Abyssal can safely indulge in. A Deathknight who eats an entire wedding cake while being given orders from his Deathlord will likely be punished for exhibiting "signs of life," but one who devours a skull-shaped cake will never be so punished.

        WHISPERANTO

        When the earliest Exalted students of Necromancy first began to hear the Whispers of the Neverborn, they were concerned. When they realized that every Necromancer eventually heard such murmurings, they knew they had to act. The morbid, nihilistic, and entropic thoughts and concepts necessary to ponder and advance the Art acted as paving-stones lining a road to their psyches. Their response came in the form of a language, one designed to be used only by Necromancers: Whisperanto.

        Whisperanto used diffusive case/tense interactions, annoyingly simplified transitive structure, increasingly hyper-normative inflections and tonalities, overly stultified definitions-by-committee and many other linguistically overcomplicated simplifications as a means to trap, neutralize, and contain the nihilistic thoughts of the Neverborn as brought into Creation by those who suffered from the Whispers of the Abyss. Though infuriatingly inefficient at the start, the new language worked, and its hyper-precise case structures bored the horror out of dark concepts even as its imprecise and confusing pronouns diluted any mounting dread such discussions might inflict.

        Sadly, it is believed that all knowledge of Whisperanto was lost in the Breaking of the Mask.
