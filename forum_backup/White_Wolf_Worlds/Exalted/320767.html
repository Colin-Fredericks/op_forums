      Prior Warning isn't really the problem - that one gives you a general idea that something bad is about to happen a few minutes in advance, but doesn't specify what the danger is. You can get around this by making it so that whatever the danger is either of a kind that the characters are not expecting (poison gas attack when they expect armed thugs, fire when they are expecting poison, pit full of spikes when they expect fire), or that the amount of time they have to prepare is not enough. Also, splitting the party to prevent the Awareness Sid from alerting his buddies would fix several problems.

      Expected Pain is worse. That one makes you effectively immune to surprise, mechanically speaking. Again, though, my solution would be to make it so "an instant" is not enough warning time. For example, Expected Pain might alert you to the group of thugs that is about to ambush you, but it doesn't really matter because they'll be there in less than five ticks and your only escape route got locked down ten minutes earlier.

      Now, if your Sidereal has Prior Warning, Expected Pain, AND Avoidance Kata, there may simply be nothing you can do.

                  Awareness also contains the rediculous Wise Choice charm. For a small cost, you can know which choice (of any finite set you list) will lead to the best personal outcome for you! This has some obvious Paranoia uses: which path home should I take, should I get in the first carriage or the second one, should I trust Vizier Judas Starscream or not, etc. It can also be used to predict exactly who is going to try to assassinate you next (most useful if you know someone is gunning for you). Have a friend set up a betting pool, where people can guess names and whoever guesses right gets a prize. Then, get a bunch of tiles with letters on them, and assemble a name using Wise Choice (which tile should I place on the board next, given that I'm going to keep doing this until I get a complete name?) and then enter the result into the betting pool. Since the best outcome for you is both a) winning the bet and b) finding the assassin, the charm will give you the correct answer every time (barring cases where knowing your would-be killer is worse for you than not knowing, in which case you should be fine not knowing). Of course, this is an incredibly ineficient O(n) method that would drain motes like crazy, and only works for within the next month unless you exploit recursion, but I think optimizing information gains from Wise Choice is slightly outside the scope of this post.

                  As a side note, you would be amazed at how much you can get done with Wise Choice and a grid map of Creation.

                                                  "Being hard to pin down if they don't want to be pinned down" is kind of a Sidereal schtick -- as antagonists, they're supposed to be like the one-armed man from the Fugitive, or the secret master villain who mainly works behind the scenes. I don't think I'd want that to go away, because it's a part of their appeal, but it'd be nice if their abilities were designed more with thought to how they might play out in that scenario when you're not the Sidereal. But, I mean, "being one step ahead" is pretty much where you want Sidereals to be.


                                                  Originally posted by SaintedPhysician View Post
                                                  Since the best outcome for you is both a) winning the bet and b) finding the assassin, the charm will give you the correct answer every time (barring cases where knowing your would-be killer is worse for you than not knowing, in which case you should be fine not knowing). Of course, this is an incredibly ineficient O(n) method that would drain motes like crazy, and only works for within the next month unless you exploit recursion, but I think optimizing information gains from Wise Choice is slightly outside the scope of this post.

                                                  As a side note, you would be amazed at how much you can get done with Wise Choice and a grid map of Creation.
                                                  Wise Choice isn't quite this good, happily. The 2e version requires you to define what you consider the best outcome, which means you may end up optimizing for something unwise. Additionally, while the 2e version casts ahead up to a month and can be extended through teamwork, the 1e version just gives "short-term results", which absent further instructions I would tend to interpret in the context of the choice. So either version can give you a very bad long-term outcome in exchange for a good short-term one -- especially problematic when the best short-term outcome isn't a clear winner.

                                                  As a Sidereal, you can and should try to use it cleverly, but making it your one-size-fits-all solution for figuring things out will probably lead to more surprises than you might like.
