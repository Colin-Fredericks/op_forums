                                    Originally posted by Crowley View Post
                                    Can The Limit Break trigger mental derangements as a way to cope with what has an exalted done?
                                    Here's my understanding of it. I think I'm on point here, but I'm sure wiser Forumites than we will correct me in the (hopefully, few) details that I get wrong:

                                    The answer is no, the Great Curse will not trigger a Derangement to help the Cursed cope with their heinous acts, because the Curse is already something very close to a Derangement, and it's this property that makes the Spite of the Neverborn so insidious. You see, under most circumstances, committing an atrocity could definitely make an Exalt eligible for a Derangement. Let's say that Zenith Caste lawgiver Drazak the Defenestrator has been chasing the elusive Forbidden God, Juggler-of-Kittens, all across Creation for months. But each time Drazak has had the Juggler cornered, the wily god has somehow escaped, and the growing number of ruthlessly juggled cats weighs heavily on Drazak's mind. So when he accidentally runs into the Juggler at a charity event, Drazak instinctively activates Orphanage Hurling Spasm, and picks up the building around them, targeting his quarry. If he realizes that hundreds of children will die, but throws it anyways (not! one! kitten! more!), he might gain a Derangement out of guilt. If he is so focused on ending the Juggler that orphan casualties never occur to him until after he throws the orphanage, he might gain a Derangement out of regret.

                                    But, if Drazak has the Conviction Flaw: Fanatical Devotion, or the Valor Flaw: Unhesitating Rage, and if the maddening thought of the Juggler getting away again pushes his Limit into uncontrolled Break territory, then Drazak will throw the Orphanage, splat the god and orphans both, and feel GREAT about doing it. His Willpower will refresh, and his Limit track will drop from 9 points of itchy, annoying Limit to a cool, soothing zero points. And if someone brings up the innocent victim body count later on, Drazak will either rationalize his actions as being unpleasant, but necessary (Fanatical Devotion), or he'll "consider''' the high number of dead orphans to be an indicator of the Juggler's extraordinary wickedness(Unhesitating Rage).

                                    With the exception of a few Compassion Flaws and a Temperance Flaw or two, an Exalt who enters full Limit Break will not consider his mindset or behavior while in Break to be aberrant or out of character. Nor will he look back upon his actions with shame, sorrow, or bewilderment. The undetectability of the Great Curse prevents Celestial Exalts from noticing its effects on themselves, so the only things that Drazak comes out of the orphanage-throwing incident with are the following:
                                    • the normal satisfaction in finally vanquishing his quarry,
                                    • either complete faith in the correctness of his actions (Fanatical Devotion) OR pride in how quickly and accurately he struck (Unhesitating Rage),
                                    • the rush of self-worth and confidence that accompanies a full Willpower refill, and,
                                    • relief from from angst, dissatisfaction, mental fatigue, and negativity, in the form of an emptied Limit track.

                                    So Limit Breaking feels good, restores confidence, relieves angst, and gets things done, all while preventing any guilt, shame, regret or unease that could lead to honest self-assessment. It's no wonder that the Solars of the First Age spiraled into monstrosity.
