    Running a Lunar, Solar, and DB campaign, any issues?

    04-24-2021, 11:01 AM
    Hello all, I am going to be running a new 3rd edition campaign soon and multiple different types of Exalted are going to be in the game. So I was hoping you had any advice or warnings for issues you have experienced running mixed campaigns? I am a long time ST & player for Exalted, all the way back to the 1st edition of the game, so I understand there have been major balance issues in the past, but I have been told that the narrowing of the power differences have alleviated this quite a bit. So I assume the biggest issues will be the themes of the different games and motivations of the characters that I need to be most aware of.

      I think Lunars and Solars will work together pretty well, just as long as the Lunar players don't try to compete with the specific niches of the Solars (basically, what the Solars are Supernal in). A Lunar wanting to be a great crafter is pretty much going to be overshadowed if there's also a Solar with Supernal Craft. Note, though, that some areas are a lot broader than others, and have much more room for multiple people to be good at them. Combat is a good example here. While a Lunar shouldn't expect to make "the greatest sword-master in the world" work alongside a Melee-Supernal Dawn, they can still be extremely effective in combat overall, and contribute meaningfully. This is particularly true because Lunar charms tend to be applicable to multiple types of fighting, making the Lunars more versatile. As the most basic example, the Lunar Dexterity Excellency can be added to any attack, pretty much, meaning that the Lunar can be pretty good at fighting armed or unarmed, and with bows, thrown knives, or just rocks, in a way that a Solar might struggle to match.

      Dragon-Blooded, however, are a bit more dicey. While the power differential has definitely been lowered in 3e, with no bullshit "this charm completely fails against a Celestial Exalt" mechanics, the DB charms are still notably weaker than Lunar, and especially Solar ones. A DB in a party of Lunars/Solars runs a real risk of feeling useless. Some suggestions to compensate for this:
      • First, the most RAW approach is to make sure the DBs' unique status as the most "legitimate" type of Exalts in Creation is emphasized. Encourage DBs to take "social" merits like Command, Backing, Influence, etc. at character creation, and in play, be a bit more lenient about giving them those merits as story awards. Also, they should run into far more people with positive Intimacies towards DBs in general, and less suspicion from the populace. They're the Princes of the Earth, saviors of Creation. Make sure that comes through.
      • Starting any DBs at higher Essence is also a decent idea, allowing them to spend the XP they would have needed to reach the Essence level on whatever they want. I suggest Essence 3 for this, as it lets them take Signature charms, which are some of the really nice "toys" in the DB charmset.
      • Straying further from the RAW, but adjusting the XP requirements for gaining higher Essence for the DBs is also a good idea. Right now, they "plateau" for a long time at Essence 3. Even if you gave them the 75 XP needed to start at Essence 3, as above, they'd still only be hitting Essence 4 after 175 XP, only 25 XP before the Solars and Lunars were hitting Essence 4 (i.e., about 5 sessions). If you give everybody the Solar/Lunar XP progression, on the other hand, then the DBs would be hitting Essence 4 at 125 XP, exactly when the Lunars and Solars are hitting Essence 3.
      • Another idea, if you're not willing to start DBs at Essence 3, is to allow them access to one Signature charm in an Aspect Ability at Essence 2, a sort of "mini-Supernal" thing. Even if you start them at Essence 3, giving access to multiple Signature charms in a single Aspect Ability, again as a kind of mini-Supernal, isn't a bad idea. Normally, a DB can only take one Signature charm in an Ability, until they hit Essence 5, at which point they get a second. If in the one focus Ability, they can take, say, 2 Signature charms at Essence 3, 3 at Essence 4, and 4 at Essence 5, that's a nice little treat.

          We are playing a mixed Lunars/Solar campaign. But one Solar poses as DB within the satrapie of Prasad (well my version of Prasad that was written before the official material was written) and limits himself to stuff that would be expected from a DB.
          The other player is a Anathema that was indoctrinated through the Pure Way and they happen to became buddies.

          The Lunar is a freshly exalted Lunar comming into this group hiding/living in Prasad. She started at Essence 1 while everyone was almost E4. She was a player new to Exalted so we decided to go with 10xp/session while the others get 2/session so that she will catch up soon enough while learning the system. Besides this danger of being overwhelmed (that high XP DB start characters would also run into) from all the charms there are absolutely no problems. She chose a focus the others don't have and won't be going for. As long as the niche is protected even an E1 Lunar in an E4 Solar group does not feel off or weak. So the same should be true for Dragonblooded, I guess. I neither played or gm'ed a game for a DB yet so I can't really comment on that.

          Though as I said, one of my players poses as a Dragonblood. And he is smart in doing it, limiting demonstrations of his supernatural prowess for when it's necessary. That also means that many of his skills are not usable in certain situations. Like Kelly Pedersen already mentioned the social structure and capital a DB has can be incredible useful. Less so in stories where raw individual power is wanted but in a city setting (most of our time is spent in Kamthahar) it's incredible powerful.

          Sometimes even frustratingly so. The other player plays a Solar that "just" lives there and plays the role of a mortal sorceress. The "DB" player can leverage a lot of soft power over the other players if they don't want to behave in ways that garner unwanted attention. At least if it is public.


          In addition I don't know how you handle artifacts at your table but in my stories they are quite rare and powerful. From Solar PCs I always expect a very good reason and story about how they got their hands on those. Most of the time this already means that they don't take the artifact since they only want the +1 weapon that looks cool instead of the god-shaped creation that can potentially doom or create several kingdoms if it existence and carrier's name becomes known. DB can have heirlooms and more importantly they don't really care that they are known as carrier of an artifact. And in my opinion a DB carrying the Almighty Stick of Benefection or the Terrifying Bludgeon of Dragons has a way easier time creating and maintaining his reputation.

          In comparison a non-DB that becomes a carrier of a mighty artifact becomes target of other greedy entities and the Wyld Hunt. It is easier to hunt down a single individual without any support that might be very mighty or hunt down a known actor of an incredible powerful empire that would absolutely go to war with several legions lead by gods to reaquire the stolen artifact.

                  The balance between the party members is actually a lot more dependent on how the players build their characters rather than their splat.

                  If you look at the only currently published adventure for Exalted 3e, it's got a sample circle. You might have somebody who builds their Dawn Solar like that Dawn is built. You might also have a DB who's the party's sorcerer, who's stacking the Mela's Coil, a 5-dot jade daiklave, the spell Invulnerable Skin of Bronze, and optimized combat stats. In that case the guy who's supposed to be the sorcerer of the group, and also who's the DB of the group, will make the guy who built the Dawn look like a chump, in addition to being an awesome sorcerer.

                  By far the biggest balance concern is not splat, it's how they're being built.


                  On the total flip side, if each character has their niche and you do a good job as an ST, most of that won't even matter anyway. In Exalted people who are good at talking are extremely dangerous, sailing a ship is extremely dangerous, there's horrible magical diseases, soul extinguishing poisons, deadly environments, and massive labyrinthine bureaucracies of supernaturally mythic proportions.

                  If your Dawn caste Solar is three times the warrior that the Dragonblooded doctor is, that's fine, because in the next scene when the Dawn is on the verge of literally* puking their guts out due to a magical virus that he can't just apply his daiklave to, it doesn't really matter that the terrestrial isn't as good a doctor as he could be if he was a Solar. There is no direct balance comparison here. The Dawn is only going to live to see another sunrise, ironically, because this Dragonblood was there and can cure their black-stomach fever.

                  That's really on you though, if there's a DB doctor, absolutely put in that kind of disease that will straight up 100% end that Dawns life, and give him a chance to be saved the way he saves everyone in a fight.


                  *I am using this word correctly.

The fact that they use Martial Arts and Sorcery moreso than native Dragon-Blooded charms might have something to do with it.

                          Basically, if there do end up being any DBs there needs to be VERY little overlap between their primary areas of expertise and those of the Celestials because anywhere there is overlap the DBs will be adding little in relative terms.

                          In scenarios where everyone will or might be contributing in similar ways (e.g.- combat, certain sorts of social situations), as a corollary to one of Kelly’s points try to make sure that a good portion of the time that happens there’ll be witnesses if anyone flares their anima banner. Since Celestials will think at least twice about flaring while DBs all but specifically want to make a light show of things this will effectively reverse whose dice caps are higher in practice.

                                                      So Alhat isn't much of a threat on his own, except to a very young and inexperienced circle who are not very focused on combat. The kind of backup that he gets might be in the form of his brides, who have a pretty impressive Evasion 5, but only a soak of 4, and no ability to negate penalties of any kind.

                                                      So if your character isn't as crazy combat focused as the Dawn, those allies to Alhat are the ones you should be focusing on. Alhat needs those helper characters to be effective, take them out and you're golden.

                                                          Back during the DB KS I made a DB in a group of all solars. She worked just fine. I'm not saying its a perfectly balanced thing but a nice thing was due to the fact many Exy's could do successes. A lot of social work doesn't need the massive dice pool of solars, as a couple successes can usually meet what you need.

                                                          Another thing is knowing your role. She was a performer in the Eclipse' circus. She was pretending to be a mortal who was ACTING as a fire aspect. (hiding from her traitorous uncle). As a fire aspect she was skilled in swordplay but wasn't better in a fight than the combat invested solars. But she wasn't in anyway useless, especially as she was the only one who used mounted combat with a fire horse. (modified Aughdeihe). As the Jade Phoenix Soul her whole thing was being a distracting presence in her performances. This allowed others ability to do things while eyes were on her.

                                                          Now would I have changed some things? yeah, now that the book has fully been out for a while some things would have changed. I would have also requested some of the balancing stuff from above like a free signature, or changing the XP to various essence ratings. But if you talk with the other players on what to expect, make sure to find roles, and have the ST give everyone time to shine, the game can work. Its tricky but it can happen.

                                                          Lunars and Solars on the other hand work just fine together. I've yet to run into a problem where Specialized McSupernal and Adaptable McBroadskill can't work well together. It's important to remember that Solars have a supernal, which gives them a lot of power in one field, and their starter charms in anything they invest in usually bring a strong power to bear. Lunars can become the bear. Their toolkit is absurdly applicable, and due to how they can just add to it by doing a murder, they are VERY capable of having a way to solve problems.

                                                          The key things are it depends on the table, the ST, and what you are looking to get out of the game. I've done mixed very well and thrived as the lone DB among celestials, but i'm probably the exception and not the rule.
