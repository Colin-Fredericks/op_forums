    Terrestrial VS Celestial: How big is the power gap now?

    04-21-2018, 11:12 AM
    I have happily backed the dragonblooded kickstarter and have been thrilled with everything I have read so far. I am incredibly impressed with the manuscript so far, and have tons of confidence in the new dev team.However I haven't had as much time to sit and tinker with the new Charms as I would like, and was wondering what the wise minds of this forum might think about them.

    So how big is the power gap between Solars and Dragonblooded now? Could something like an Essence 3 Dynast be a deadly threat to a newborn Dawn? Are the 2e days of combat specced Solars laughing in the face of dozens of Wyld Hunters long gone?

    Eager to hear peoples thoughts.

      It's kind of a hard question to answer, since there isn't exactly a unit in which one can measure power gaps...

      ​Across the board, Dragon Blooded are still weaker. For one thing, their mote pools and dice caps are smaller, and for the latter, they need to buy the Charms rather than just having the Abilities. Anima powers are generally less extreme. And there are certain big ticket events, such as shaping the Wyld or grappling gigantic enemies that they don't get.

      ​What they have that might compare to Solars is less powerful, but not insignificant. They also have a number of powers that, in the actual use of them, are very strong, some at least as strong as what an effect laid down by a Solar might be. But several require a lot of setup, such as big Initiative leads or having one's Aura prepared, and can only be used once per time interval with either no reset condition, or fairly big ones. The Aura mechanic itself presents a principle in which a number of powerful Charms are incompatible, or one might be prematurely ended by using even a minor Charm from another Ability. Approaching a Solar might require some care, assuming that they've gotten some Essence 3 or 4 Charms in a Supernal that can be applied dangerously. They've also got the often powerful Signature Charms, in which buying one means not being any of the four others.

      ​However, they do have a few things that compensate them for this a bit. Quite a few more Charms to give them dice or successes that don't count against their cap, as well as several to provide temporary specialties to help increase their cap. Plus some of the benefits they can get from Sworn Kin in a few Charms.

      ​The Dragon Blooded have a few methods for punching above their weight class, it just costs them in a few ways or requires them to apply their powers carefully.

      ​(Also, with Artifacts, they've got some good stuff for making them, but that will have a difficult time with anything above a certain level; at the same time, the Artifacts that they're actually in possession of already can also help them gain some powers above their usual standard)

      I have approximate knowledge of many things.
      Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

              A few scattered thoughts:

              Among relatively young Exalts, any dragon-blooded who can survive the Solar's big essence spend is going to have some advantages in a long fight. Their excellencies have to be purchased, sure, but they come with free dice tricks. Those little edges in efficiency that shouldn't be dismissed. A Solar who can afford to buy and use scenelong effects is probably going to overwhelm that advantage.

              The deadliest Dawn I've ever actually played could probably kill two, maaaybe three members of a Wyld Hunt before going down, but she'd definitely go down The nature of decisive attacks (extra successes don't matter, if you have the init and you hit, they're screwed) strongly favors raw maximum dice pool in a fair fight between peers. The inherent swinginess of the dice system means that same gap isn't enough to save you from numbers, and Solar charms aren't so mighty as to give outright immunity to attacks (unlike first edition).

              Running third edition somewhat casually, I've learned there's two modes most Solar PCs operate in: casual essence expenditures, and balls-to-the-wall defining character moments. An Exalt can get a lot done with only four motes of essence per action, which is what it takes to spend peripheral essence without bumping up their animas. When PCs go all out, they spend everything they can for a moment where the actual dice roll is almost a perfunctory formality, either openly flaring or dumping their entire personal pool.

              In that context, consider the Dragon-Blooded. Within their specialty, they will spend more than four motes at a time, because their anima is strictly an asset outside of fragile environments. Their defining moments might have a slightly smaller dice cap by default, but they can probably make up the EV with the free dice tricks and with their big scary signature charms. I imagine they are close enough in utility for a mixed circle to work just fine.

                  Does anyone else feel that the DB social charms are much closer in power/utility to Solar ones than the DB combat, craft, stealth, etc, charms?

                  They seem to have a lot more charms where I go "hmmm, that'd actually be really useful", like a number of charms instilling Major intimacies, and a fair few charms for instilling/inspiring that require Decision Points to resist rather than just a WP. Mostly I'm talking about Presence, but a bit in other abilities as well.
                  They're much less likely to be able to get the extremely high amount of successes Solars can get, admittedly (our Zenith sometimes gets 20+ when preaching), but sometimes with Solar social characters, in my experience, they're easily able to throw 12+ successes at you (nearly always enough to break your Resolve), but this tends to just lead to people spending WP.

                  Whereas with other charm trees, I can definitely see how they're weaker than a similarly-built Solar.

                  (Also, Aura can be a minor thing or quite awkward, depending on your build. I was looking at the Water signature charm in Melee, which is brutal, and there's a couple of other fun Water charms in Melee, but I can't really see any good way to get into Water Aura without making Brawl attacks for a round or something.)
