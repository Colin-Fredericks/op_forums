                                    Years and years ago I played with an AD&D group that, if your character died and you weren't rezzed, you have to make a new character and start at level 1. It sure was a blast when everyone else in the group was between levels 12-14 and you were the level 1 wizard.

                                    "Oh, the Beholder looks at you. You're dead."

                                    You'd have to spend the next several sessions hiding behind the other players and not doing anything to attract attention (because you'd be one shot by anything that glanced in your direction) while the rest of the group power-leveled you.

                                    They found it fun, I guess. Or maybe they had always just played that way and it had never occurred to them to play differently. I know I didn't enjoy it.

                                    I think there's a huge line between "Character death needs to be meaningful" and, "Character death needs to be punishing." There are lots of ways to make it meaningful without punishing the player (or the ST as is often the case when a PC dies and half his story plots and hooks suddenly evaporate).
