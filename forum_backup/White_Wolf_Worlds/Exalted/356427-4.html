            Yeah, I think it's still a pretty valid point, perhaps more valid. His worry that he might have been possessed by a Djinn and not really chosen by Allah to become the Seal of the Prophets, is probably the best parallel you're likely to get between Solar Exaltation and a real person's experiences.

            As to the beliefs he'd previously practiced, Muhammed and his tribe were all pagans (the Kabbah, the big black rock in Mecca, was a centre of pagan worship), but the southern Arabs had for a while been Jews, and there were a number of northern tribes who were Christians. While Muhammed's family weren't Judeo-Christian, he'd almost certainly have met many Jews and Christians in his life, so figures such as the Angel Gabriel were presumably familiar to him before he started preaching monotheism.

                    There are two activities often conflated into "roleplaying".

                    One is acting. I had a great deal of fun (for about three hours, anyway) playing a homeless drug addict while almost everyone around me had godlike power.

                    The other, however, is storytelling. And a "broken" player piece is a very poor fit indeed for influencing narratives in the direction the player wants to direct them,


                    Even for actors, it can be *hard* playing somewhat suicidally depressed... if you actually make yourself think like your character, and have some of their attitudes bleed over... playing someone suicidally depressed is probably a really bad idea.


                    And if being suicidally depressed is not something you have to *imagine*, well... it's not exactly a great fit either, now.

                        I think it's an interesting character to play, it's just not one that necessarily has a very long shelf life. If you're actually suicidally depressed, well, you might just kill yourself.
                        One of my players' Lunar PC was extremely depressed... so she put herself into hibernation. Thus ending the character, in the short term at least (the other characters considered trying to solve her depression through magic, but didn't come up with anything good). Another character became extremely depressed after killing a bunch of innocent people, and killed himself. Excellent roleplaying, very interesting, but obviously not much good in the long term.
                        But not every character has to work long-term. It's probably something to discuss with the ST.
                        Alternatively, your character might get better.
                        (There's also the issue of what is causing your depression: terrible things that have happened to you, or a chemical imbalance in your brain. The latter can probably be sorted out pretty quickly with Solar medicine charms. Or a Dragonblood medicine charm, for that matter.)

                                                I played a character kind of like this.

                                                He was a middle-aged Autochthonian. Had a pretty great career, had been the head Preceptor of a city and then of the nation as a whole and had basically stepped out of that role to do political outreach and had missed being in the field. His nation was attempting something like Project Razor and he was really interested in it but while he was a well-known public figure, he was "past his prime" and had lost a lot of political clout. Favors from old friends got him placed in charge of security of the initial expedition, though.

                                                Well, he wound up Exalting as a Solar once in Creation after saving some men at the great risk to himself and great cost to his own health. Went home to get checked up on by his nation's best doctors and while they realized it was something close to what happens when they make an Alchemical, they didn't really get it. His solum started getting a strange gold corrosion on it and they thought he had some kind of Essence-based illness native to Creation. Based on what he described from Charm use, they figured it was empowering his chakras but would eventually burn them out. So he resolved to go back into Creation and do as much as he could for God and State with the little time he had left.

                                                Of course after a certain amount of time he figured out what was happening by meeting other Solars, but the "dying national figure using his last months to make our Nation the best it can be and give it the strength in the Creation that it deserves" was too good a narrative for him to pass up, so he kept working in the context, vowing to one day fake his own death and slip away, leaving a heroic legacy for his children to be proud of.
