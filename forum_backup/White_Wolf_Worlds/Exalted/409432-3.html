                                                        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                        The thing to remember is that Redemption doesn't necessarily have anything to do with redemption. You could actually end up a worse person after the fact.
                                                        If so, then calling it "Redemption" is needlessly confusing. "Redemption" has very specific moral connotations.

                                                        I'm in the "Redemption as written needlessly limits Abyssal concept-space" camp. Even if you assume that Redeeming an Abyssal Exalt has no implications at all for the Abyssal's personal morality, or that objective morality does not exist in the Exalted setting, you still have the basic problem that the game is forcing you to play your Abyssal a certain way, or you'll turn into another kind of Exalt.

                                                        None of the other splats have this problem. Sidereals who go ronin get stripped of their heavenly job benefits, but they're still Sidereals. Lunars who won't join the Silver pact have an elevated risk of chimerism, but they're still Lunars. Dragonblooded who aren't noble are either outcaste or dead Dragonblooded. Alchemicals who develop Gremlin Syndrome are still Alchemicals, only crazy and armed with flesh-warping powers.

                                                        Also, consider what Redemption does in tandem with Resonance. Every time an Abyssal resists unnnatural mental influence, channels a virtue, or commits "any deed that actively promotes or defends life", he gains Resonance points, which can manifest a variety of destructive effects.

                                                        The net effect is that any given Abyssal character can effectively only play one of three character arcs:
                                                        1. Omnicidal Nihilism - the Abyssal follows the script and murders everything. It can be challenging to do that without being a one-note character.
                                                        2. Defiance - the Abyssal goes off the script and gains Resonance like crazy but persists until eventually the Neverborn and the ST give up and say "Fine, evidently you don't want to be an omnicidal nihilist. Here's a different character."
                                                        3. Enlightenment - the Abyssal 2E book mentions how an Abyssal might stay in the underworld and be a hero to the dead instead of the living, but provides no actual detail about how that would work, what that would mean, or how the ST is supposed to handle it.

                                                        Based on what I have read of this thread, the people who are most disappointed in Abyssals are the people who are most interested in Enlightenment and wish the game provided more support for it, or wish for more possible arcs.
