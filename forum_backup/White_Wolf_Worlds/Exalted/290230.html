    3E: Solar Secret Societies - Being sneaky while building a power base.

    10-22-2014, 11:06 PM
    There are a few ways to play a Solar. By their nature they tend to stand out, but even a Dawn when motivated can downplay what they are, act covertly and still be heroically awesome when doing it. So let's say I want to play an Agent's of UCS game. My Solars are going to be sneaky when they can, and fade fast when they can't. And lets also say I have a number of players down with that style of game.

    Where would I start?

    Obvious tricks would be using Socialize Charms to send covert messages. Mix in some Bureaucracy and social influence with the right people to make people turn a blind eye. Call it the Batman effect. Gordon must know Bruce Wayne is Batman by now, but doesn't care. The good he does out ways the vigilante he should bring it. In the case of the Solars they have respectable 'Day Jobs' in the local community and when the threat of Realm bullying, Deathlord meddling, etc happens they quietly pull out the Daiklaves and clean up the mess. Then go back to smiling at the major as if nothing happened.

    Zeniths and Eclipse would dominate this style play.

    I could see Dawn's by virtue of firepower and Nights by virtue of stealth being next in the 'fast but quiet' tier of tricks.

    The only cast, or player style that might run afowl of this is the Twilight or Sorcerer characters. Just way too much raw magic being thrown around.

    How would you resolve that? What sorts of adaptations would you make in possible 3E builds to be sneaky while effective?

          My default play style in a lot of the games I was in was to appear as human as possible until I got around Essence 3-4 and had two or three dozen charms under my belt, so I'll post some things I learned / strategies I developed.

          Only use your personal motepool. Your peripheral motepool? You don't have one. It only appears if you're about to die. Your charms are super efficient compared to the other Exalts, use that to your benefit.

          For combat, get charms that have a scene long duration and use them like your life depended on it. Shining Razor Wind, Iron Kettle Body and Flows like Blood can all help you not die while still keeping you at superhuman level without it being overtly supernatural.

          Lion's Roar Recovery is your best friend. Increase your stunt value by one step? Yes please!

          Sagacious Reading of Intent + 2nd Presence Excellency will make you very capable in the social arena until you can afford to branch out a bit more.

          Beyond this, many charms don't have the obvious keyword, so careful resource management can help you a lot when you need to use non-combat, non-social skills.

          Stealth has a charm that lets your Night share their stealth effects with their team. Spreading the Night's Shroud, I believe.

          If you can get away with it, try stunting any Obvious flickers of Anima as being Fire Aspect instead of Solar. This won't help when your Caste Mark starts to shine, but will help you with any Obvious Charms.

          Human teammates can be an excellent force multiplier at lower essence levels. Work with them to avoid using more essence than you absolutely have to.

                  Generally? Play to your strengths, do something that seems plausible to explain away your abilities, or act in a way that puts you way outside of suspicion.

                  In the past I have had an Eclipse in a 3e playtest that simply avoided ever showing his anima and had strong coloration indicating he was a wood aspect (born into a major house of the isle) and that was pretty much it. He kept it subtle and wasn't especially good at combat.

                  Let's see, I had another character that when pressed referred to himself as "A Prince of the Earth very strongly aspected to Water" Which was technically true as he was a Defiler chosen of Kimbery. He stuck mostly to martial arts especially the water themed ones and easily managed to pass himself off as a very strong DB when it mattered.

                  I've also gone the exact reverse of subtle and stole a classical idea... He was a Zenith rich retired from the guild man turned "Anathema Hunter". He would periodically lead "Wyld Purges" into the countryside, and when not doing that would charge around Nexus attacking/accusing people of being Anathema. After which of course his servants would wander by, apologize for the crazy old man being a crazy old man and pay for the damages. Even gave him a shaving basin for a hat, 'cause you might as well go whole-hog when you rip something off.


                  One thing to remember is that what you have to hide is very much dependent on the audience. The OP sorcery example, you don't need to hide the power/level of your sorcery, except from other sorcerers. The tales about such people are wild and fanciful and terrifying, so your typical army grunt isn't going to have any idea that you're not supposed to be able to throw around forces like that, with that level of ease... that's exactly the kind of thing that he's heard of sorcerers doing.

                                      I feel as though the main thing one would need is some cause or recruitment mechanism that necessarily draws the socially disaffected, if not outlawed altogether, who might be driven towards coordinated or mutually supportive activities either in service of a subversive goal, or generally aiding one another in a manner that is desired to not be subject to scrutiny by society at large.

                                      Actually being sneaky isn't necessarily difficult, just a matter of making sure you know a decently out of the way place to which people can arrive in a staggered manner (or just find or build places with secret tunnels, and make sure the proper people are bribed and records destroyed), and then select an arbitrary form of code words and hand signs.

                                      If one wishes to play an Exalt who is managing the thing in a fairly decentralized but meticulously managed system, I'd say Larceny and similar Charms are very good for passing yourself off as several different figures who are all ostensibly part of a network of intermediaries between the cells and the mysterious leader. Or Bureaucracy to actually have such a network. The key image, I think, is that everybody perceives things in terms of reporting to somebody who reports to somebody who reports to the leader, and only ever knowing the step immediately above and below you, and it's never actually possible to trace it to the top because if you're not in disguise as a courier, then you're just intercepting the reports as they pass between people who never actually know how far they are from the top.
