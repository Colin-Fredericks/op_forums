        If I can chime in here, with a slightly different perspective...

        I agree with Lion Hamser's premise - in the setting, sorcery is supposed to entirely change your identity or outlook in some way. The rules as written do very little to enforce this, because sorcery as written is a minimal investment. You buy your circles, and you can be a sorcerer. Hell, from an efficiency point of view there's very little reason for every Exalt ever not to take Terrestrial - you get a spell (charm equivalent), the ability to make workings, and a shaping ritual. That's a lot of bang for your buck.

        And then... that's as far as the system goes. It feels like Exalted, as a relatively crunchy system, tends to veer towards those elements of the game that have more mechanics to them. This means that charms get the lion's share of many character considerations. Sorcery, being mechanically a few things like charms (spells) plus a structured system for storyteller fiat/homebrew (workings), really doesn't. And so that leaves characters which dip into sorcery alongside other concepts, as they don't have much room within the system to expand any sorcery-based concepts.

        I think the comparison with D&D is warranted, not because of spells, but (from what I remember about 3.5E) with skills. Both fighters and magic users get points to spend on skills. How these are spent would look quite different. In Exalted, a sorcerer's charm spread will have the Circle charms and maybe a few utility charms here and there to help with casting and/or workings, but will be maybe 2/3 the same as a similar concept without sorcery. That's what I see with this system, having ST'd in stories with sorcerer characters who were:
        • Rurouni Kenshin with sorcery
        • Street-fighting smith with sorcery
        • Alchemical scholar/teacher with sorcery
        • Slavery rebellion leader with sorcery
        Of these, only the scholar felt at all like a sorcerer is portrayed in the background, because the rest could easily dip in for a grab-bag of assorted abilities for one charm pick, and focus their key abilities in other areas. Exalted's system just doesn't support the conceptual character depth for sorcerers that its setting describes.

        One possible patch for this, now I think about it, is to mandate that each sorcerer take a defining intimacy to do with their sorcerous abilities; maybe a tie to the object of their initiation, a principle that was introduced to them through their shaping ritual, something to do with how they've been treated by others subsequent to becoming a sorcerer. For each circle they advance in, they need to take a new Defining (or maybe Major for the second and third?) Intimacy. That feels like it may give the character's involvement and the "shift in perspective" that supposed to be part and parcel of the sorcery experience a bit more weight, with the minimal in system reworking.

                                            A thing of note.* Exalts only have to buy a charm to gain access to sorcery, but mortals need to acquire a 5 dot merit to get their first shaping ritual. (core pg 470)** Considering Sorcery is described in part as learning to feel and wield the essence of the world. Exalts are already used to wielding essence, so sorcery being wielding power that is entirely external to them might be weird, but its more extreme for a mortal to deal with than an Exalt.

                                            *I mean there's always the thing that if the player doesn't want to play having access to sorcery as anything more than another tool in their belt that's how its going to be, but this might be of note.

                                            ** Yes I know merits are not a distinct thing in universe, but the dots tend to describe how powerful ,and consequently impactful, said merit is on a character that possesses it. So that getting a shaping ritual for a mortal is a 5 dot merit is probably not a small means.
