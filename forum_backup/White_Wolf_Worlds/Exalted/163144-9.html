    Mother's Gift to the Unborn
    Panoply Charm of the Dowager of the Irreverent Vulgate in Unrent Veils

    The Dowager looses a cruel curse that reaches far and wide. One that seizes upon expecting mothers and the children yet to be born. It sits idle while the pregnancy proceeds, but strikes suddenly at birth. It all but assures death in the mother, though almost seems to increase the child's survival. Becoming motherless is not the curse's only cruelty for the newborn.

    Through whispers and dreams, the curse teaches the affected child that his mother loved him, regardless of the truth, in a way distinct, deeper, and dearer than any other ever could. He develops a Defining Intimacy of love for his departed mother, which rebuilds itself if degraded. While many such mothers might rest easier knowing their children understood the depths of their love, this knowledge is but a tool to the Dowager's curse.

    This underscores to the child that this great, boundless love that was his birthright has been denied him, and renders all other affection he may begin to feel hollow and empty. All Intimacies of love or other fond feelings toward others are more difficult to establish and degrade quickly.

    The exceptions are any such Intimacies developed toward the Dowager, who knows upon seeing the child how to assume his mother's shape and effect her mannerisms flawlessly. Should the Dowager build a Defining Intimacy toward herself in the child, it takes the place of the Defining Intimacy of love for the mother, which falls away. All other effects of this Charm remain. The Dowager has made use of this horrendous method for several in her orphanage, and sees great promise in its application for selecting a Deathknight.

    Only if the cursed develops a Defining Intimacy of love or affection toward another can the curse be broken, absent other magic.

                    New Charm: Mantle of Misplaced Things
                    Fair Folk Cup Charm
                    The raksha dips her fingers into her Cup and spreads about her target a subtle curse. Rolling Cup + Essence against her target's MDV, she fills nearby objects with a playfulness to hide from the target's desire for them. When making an Investigation roll to find something, the target finds its difficulty increased to 1 greater than the threshold successes on the raksha's roll, as objects quietly shuffle themselves around just out of sight and engage in other subterfuge to better confound his efforts.

                    The impact is still worse, however, as even searches for which no Investigation roll would normally be called for now demand such a roll, albeit at a difficulty of only 1 fewer than the threshold successes on the raksha's roll. Men have gone mad simply trying to get dressed, as their unworn clothes mysteriously relocate themselves the instant they are out of sight.

                    This curse does not afflict another person attempting to find the item, nor does it apply if another person assist in the search or even watches the search unfold. The curse likewise presents others with no difficulty in simply giving it to one so cursed. That said, if normally easily found objects have relocated themselves significantly from the target's failed attempts to find them, anyone else may need an Investigation roll of her own to find them, albeit typically only at difficulty 1 or 2.

                    A repurchase at higher Essence allows the raksha to instead (or in addition) name one of the following as the source of the target's addlement:
                    • Structures and locations, as small shuffles in terrain (signposts, turns in the road, etc.) disorient him in his search.
                    • People, whom the surrounding world subtly contrives to keep the target from finding.

                    A further repurchase at higher Essence may be used to make the effect permanent, and yet another may even extend its effects down a family's bloodline. Some say the family of a Lunar who wields a massive iron umbrella has suffered from this affliction for generations.

                    Glamour (3-9): This Charm may be placed in an outward-facing Oneiromancy, afflicting characters who undertake a certain sort of action specified during the creation of the spell, for a number of days equal to its Lorekeeper's Essence. For every 3 points, the base effect of this Charm or of one of its repurchases may be applied. Each effect may specify its own sort of action, if appropriate.

                            Come with Me if you Want to Live
                            Sidereal Drive, building off of Predestine Escort Coincidence.

                            Whenever the Sidereal is driving a vehicle, she can use this Charm to call out to another character, urging them to get into her car if they want to survive. This is a reflexive action and is not a social attack, so it can be ignored without Willpower cost (though this Charm may be comboed with Predestine Escort Coincidence to make it a social attack). If he agrees, however, he comes under a Compulsion to get into the Seer's car by the end of the scene. This can be resisted with two Willpower. Until then (or until the Compulsion is resisted), he gains a +2 non-Charm bonus to his DVs and (the Sidereal's Drive) aggravated, lethal, and bashing soak and Hardness — by agreeing with the Sidereal's plea, fate conspires to keep him alive until he escapes with the Exalt.

                            If the Sidereal did use Predestine Escort Coincidence on the target, then the aforementioned defensive benefits continue to apply until that Charm's chosen destination is reached.

                                        Weaving the Bloodline
                                        Lunar Knack (Intelligence)

                                        The Lunar dips into her treasure trove of stolen shapes, and fashions freshly alloyed marvels of the flesh. The Lunar may select a number of shapes equal to her Intelligence, pay a number of motes equal to the shapes chosen (on top of any for a shapeshifting action), and create a facsimile form between them. She may select traits between them as she pleases, so long as there is an obvious visible similarity to all component shapes, as though through parentage (however impossible). Anyone who would recognize any of the component shapes receives a bonus or penalty related to the relative number of component shapes recognized or unrecognized.

                                        A higher Essence repurchase allows the Lunar to weave the bloodlines of one or more others. Paying the cost of using this Knack herself, the Lunar may affect a number of nearby targets equal to her (Intelligence - 1) who have an open wound. The Lunar may include forms from her Heart's Blood library in the donations, and may include either one such form or herself without counting against the maximum number of targets. If successful on a (roll) against each target's (roll/defense), blood flies from whichever donors and recipients she chooses, mingling their blood. A recipient character is unharmed (if donated blood is poisonous or acidic, it loses these properties), but the character's blood and bloodline thereafter become a facsimile of its original owner's and any donors for all relevant effects. The Lunar may freely decide the traits of this facsimile as normal. Any child born of an affected character treats this facsimile as one of its parents, in place of the character. The Moon-Chosen who mingled the blood may at any time return an affected character's blood to normal, if the affected character possesses an open wound through which the tainted blood may escape.

                                                    Hounds of Baying Midnight
                                                    Abyssal Survival

                                                    The deathknight reshapes a ghostly familiar into a terrible beast of the hunt and puts them on the scent of a quarry. The beast immediately seeks to track down and tear its quarry apart, and the quarry feels an intense fear when it hears the monster's baying or detects any other sign of its coming. Wrapped in protective midnight, the beast can even survive for a number of days in the sun's light equal to the Abyssal's Survival, though it loses a lethal health level for each such day in the sun; such health levels cannot be restored by any means until the transformation's end. The transformation lasts until the hunt is successful or the Abyssal calls off the hunt (which may only be done when the beast is subdued).

                                                    A repurchase of this Charm allows an Abyssal to apply this transformation to any ghost, though unwilling spirits offer resistance. Other than the enhancements from this Charm and a new Defining Bond to track down and kill the quarry, the ghost retains its other traits. The Abyssal may treat it as a Familiar when it is on the hunt. Regardless of the hunt's conclusion, this Charm's effects end after a number of days equal to the Abyssal's Survival. When this transformation ends, the ghost may voluntarily take on a bestial shape, evocative of both its original form and this Charm's transformation, and may choose to become the deathknight's Familiar. At higher Essence, the Abyssal may attempt to force this last effect.

                                                            The Softest Agreement
                                                            Solar Bureaucracy

                                                            The Quills of Heaven understand well the power of a contract. What's more, they understand the power of the appearance of a contract, no matter how toothless. With this Charm, the Solar may negotiate a one-sided contract with another party, offering concessions to this party's benefit in exchange for nothing or near to nothing. These "concessions" need not go beyond what the Solar or his party intended to do anyway and need contain no effective mechanisms for control or sanction, so long as the terms are still favorable to the other party. For instance, a Solar ruler may vow to encourage his people not to encroach on a Lunar's territory (never mind that he and his people hold no interest in the land and it is unfit for cultivation) or a Solar merchant may assure a Guild functionary that he will not pursue a city's prime export (even if the Solar is actively positioning himself for just that).

                                                            Once struck, this agreement may be leveraged as though its terms are far more burdensome to the Solar or the Solar's party than they actually are, granting (bonus) to do so. Likewise, should the Lawgiver or his party fail to uphold the agreement, blame for doing so shifts seems to fall obviously on the other party's negotiation. If leveraging this fact, this reduces any appropriate penalties the Lawgiver or his party would face by (Bureaucracy - 1) not to extend beyond 0 and increases those faced by the other party by (Solar's Bureaucracy - 2).
