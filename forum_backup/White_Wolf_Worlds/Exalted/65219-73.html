                                                        Since I love costume porn.

                                                        1e Exalted - the Dragon-Blooded went into some detail re: what the people of the Realm wear. I collated the data and added some of my ideas for particular items of clothing.
                                                        • peasants wear rugged, worn clothing, sometimes even ragged, in drab, undyed colors, usually wool or cotton. Examples include uwagi-like jackets, sleeveless or with loose sleeves up to the elbow, or loose/shapeless sleeveless tunics, plus loose trousers to the knee (if it's not too hot), all tied together with an obi-like sash around the waist. If it's really too hot, just a loincloth will do. If it's wet/chilly, they add a small cape, often quite ragged, sometimes with a hood. They often go bare-foot, or they wear East Asian-style wooden clogs
                                                        • patricians wear white linen or silk stolae wrapped over their left shoulder, down across the torso, widening to cover the whole belly, folded into the belt (with belt pouches for money), then going down to cover the legs, over drab tunics and trousers or tubular skirts going down to the ankle (the most beautiful outfits of this type look a bit like wedding sarees, but are not mistaken for such b/c by long tradition even in the Realm wedding dresses are in shades of blue, to homage Venus, although they'll deny that). If it's wet or chilly they add wide, long, white cloaks, sometimes with comfortable hoods. They usually wear leather sandals that reach up to the ankle or even just below the knee
                                                        • Immaculate monks shave their heads, but can keep their beards if they want. They dress in unadorned robes tied with sashes at the waist (the higher the rank, the more rich the material of the sash, up to silk for Enlightened Masters), with East Asian-style wooden clogs or bare feet, and if they have to travel they wear typical East Asian conical hats made of straw or rice. They can't wear jewels, makeup, tattoos or piercings. On holy days or special occasion they wear ceremonial robes identical to their more common ones, but colorfully embroidered with intricate geometric shapes or images from the Immaculate Texts, and often images of dragons
                                                        • dynasts can wear (irrispective of traditional bi-gender norms, but some items are preferred by one or the other, while people with less traditional gender identities generally wear whatever they want - as long as their children Exalt! Or at least as long as they bring some kind of advantage to their Houses)
                                                          • trousers (usually either loose with a low crotch or quite tight, often stringed close on the front, often embroidered - with aniconic patterns as to avoid ruffling someone's feathers, but exceptions do exist - , sometimes fringed)
                                                          • skirts to the ankles (tubular or open over a petticoat, normally embroidered, sometimes trimmed with different colors and/or materials or fringed)
                                                          • skirts down to half the tigh or the knee, sometimes asymmetrical - longer on one side or on the back, sometimes with delicate pleats, sometimes trimmed with long colorful feathers or fringes - over usually tight trousers
                                                          • sometimes delicate scarves and shawls, sometimes fringed
                                                          • tunics and jackets fastened on a side or on the front, with straps, buttons, frogs or clasps. Since showing your arms in public isn't considered crass, they're usually sleeveless, although they can also have loose sleeves (sometimes hanging down from elbow to ground), tight sleeves coming down to cover more than half your hands, or detached sleeves that bare the shoulders and sometimes the upper arms. They're also usually embroidered, and sometimes incorporate (largely ornamental) hoods, usually left down on the back. They can even be fashioned in leather in the form of a coat, sometimes studded, sometimes trimmed with different materials and/or colors. Alternately, white shirts with puffy sleeves, often covered by leather bodices/vests
                                                          • belts (usually wide and sturdy) made of river dragon or siaka skin, with scabbards for swords and daggers (and quivers and bow cases if you rely on archery to get the job done) and sometimes loose drapings hanging from one side to various lengths. They normally don't include belt pouches because your slaves will carry anything you need and monetary transactions for dynasts don't actually involve them paying in actual coins
                                                          • if it's cold or chilly they'll wear fur-lined cloaks, with precious metals clasps and often elongated, pointy hoods
                                                          • unlike the Immaculate monks, they can and do wear bracelets, rings, tiaras, earrings, necklaces, anklets and other jewelry; they use perfume, kohl, nail polish, lip gloss, lipstick, powder, rouge, glitter etc; they wear tattoos (usually abstract motives as Immaculate culture is aniconic, often on the arms, neck or hands, which are the most usually seen parts of their body other than their faces, but sometimes elsewhere); they wear piercings; and they wear their hair asymmetrically and/or in braids, buns, cornrows, dreadlocks, hime cuts, odango and many more styles, some so uniquely outlandish they can't last more than an evening without constant application of cosmetics. Again, there's no differentiation between men and women: some women wear makeup and some men wear makeup. Women are widely considered more ideally beautiful/perfect and thus allowed slightly more leeway in how they adorn their faces and bodies; men tend to darker, more subdued colors in their makeup, if any, and lesser amounts of it (but it's not a given)
                                                          • a party where no Exalted are invited isn't considered "in public", and short skirts are enough, allowing one to show off not just their arms but their torso and legs as well without being considered crass
                                                          • the trendiest parties in warm enough climates see the dynasts trying their best to show off as much skin as possible while still being considered "clothed", which involves such outlandish items as sheer transparent dresses (worn by men too), chainmail tops (which are emphatically not considered armor but still appeal to the warlike proclivities of most Dragon-Blooded), clothing with "windows" on chest, abs, thighs, back and/or buttocks (sometimes barely held together with beads, thin straps or small chains of precious metals), vertiginously plunging necklines both in front and on the back, skirts with very revealing slits on any or all sides and/or a very low rise, crop tops, and sleeves connected with straps leaving the whole torso bared
                                                          • when they leave home they must always be ready for a fight, so they wear armor, and if it doesn't include gauntlets and sollerets, gloves and river dragon/siaka skin boots are almost mandatory. Armor too, if including leather parts, uses river dragon or siaka skin. There are 3 main styles of armor across the different types (buff coats, reinforced buff coats, breastplates, reinforced breastplates, lamellar, articulated plate...):
                                                            • White Rose style has a predatory/hunting animal in the middle of the chest, with gemmed eyes and ornamental depictions of its habitat and hunting scenes involving it on the rest of the armor (which on a lamellar can mean every platelet is individually tooled). Tolerated on Exalts because the Immaculate Philosophy categorizes it as "giving homage", and thus orthodox, but fundamentalists still don't like it
                                                            • Thundercloud style is similar but uses wholly imaginary beasts or Wyld creatures, many elders and parents will not allow a DB to wear it but it's popular among the youngest, boldest or most blasphemous dynasts
                                                            • Elemental school incorporates literal and metaphorical images of your element, and if the armorer is very good even images from legends of the Immaculate Dragons. If it incorporates a face mask it's either shaped into a dragon snout or into the appearance of the human incarnation of a particular Immaculate Dragon
                                                          • Clothing and armor colors usually reflect elemental aspect: pure white for earth, shiny black for water, shades of orange and red for fire, shades of blue for air, and shades of green for wood (embroideries and ornamentation can break the color scheme). Houses Ragara, Mnemon, Ledaal and Peleps mandate for their scions to wear the House aspect color (but some Ledaal and Peleps dynasts just won't), even if you're not a DB. V'neef, Cynis, Cathak, Sesus and Iselsi let you wear whatever color you prefer, usually the one related to your own aspect (mortal dynasts tend to wear either very drab and subdued colors to avoid attracting too much attention or motley patchworks of bright, clashing colors to compensate for their un-Exalted status).
