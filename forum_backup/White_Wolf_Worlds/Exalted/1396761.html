    3rd edition Melee vs MA question

    06-15-2020, 08:32 PM
    Our group is doing some serious chatting about what to do when we are done with Curse of Strahd, and we've settled on Exalted 3rd edition. Our GM is an old Exalted hand, and this will be his first chance to run the new edition. We've all picked our castes, and I've ended up as the party Dawn, which I'm pretty happy about. Now, as a Dawn, I gotta kick some sort of butt, and I've put together the concept of a sword-master princess with dreams of grandeur and empire.

    But to my real question. I'm torn between building her with Shining Point in the Void Martial Arts style (the iaijitsu style) or with normal old Melee charms. I know that both choices have their merits and downsides, and I'd like the opinion of some others, especially people who have actually played the system. I know the Void style is a bit more limited, but very flavorful and "special" (she's a princess, and there's every chance her family wanted her trained in a style that was a little unique compared to more traditional swordsmanship), and Melee is broad and straightforward, but it's also more general.

    Any thoughts?

      Well melee is just better all around, it has more tricks, waaaay better defenses, and is a bit more generic, so it's easier to flavour however you want. On the other hand you can take all of Single Point style right off chargen if you want, and still have six charms leftover for whatever else you're interested in. That lets you just take those 9 charms and study them really well to see how they work and when you want to use them. Plus it has flavour built into it that you don't need to even think of with it's single doom strike flash abilities and stuff.

      Flipside, you could take all your charms in melee and spend the next 50 sessions buying only melee charms and you still won't have them all. It's also a lot more simple and straightforward. It has charms like "Ignore all penalties to your defense." and "add up to 5 Withering damage for up to 5 motes." whereas Single Point has charms like "Roll in your sword as a separate character into this combat, with these specific restrictions on what it does and how it works, and when it's crashed you get forced to drop your Form, except if you're a Solar where you stay in the form but the sword vanishes from the initiative track for 3 turns..."

      Then again as an MA Supernal you get access to EVERY Martial Art, so if you wanted to mix styles with your slashing sword you can pick up Steel Devil Style, or even just branch out into other styles so that you have options for when you're armed with your sword, and also insanely deadly if you get disarmed.


      Either way, whatever one you take you'll be able to kill pretty much anything at your leisure, so you don't really have to worry about one being a "trap" option which will eventually mean you suck.

          3E's signature Dawn is Volfer. He's the default character showing you how to build a Dawn and what a Dawn can do.

          A reasonably optimized Single Point Solar can kill Volfer in one round.

          In fact, power up Volfer with permenant Yozi venom, and tie the Single Point stylist down by giving them half-Willpower and not letting them flare their anima... and Single Point kills Volfer flawlessly in four rounds.

          The Exalted are crazy strong. You are not meant to optimize them. You are meant to play a fun and flavorful concept that interests you.

          If Single Point interests you, you should play it.

          It is MORE THAN strong enough to work for you in regular play, even if you don't optimize.

          That said, it's worth talking to the ST and other players about how much combat you will have and how good everyone will be expected to be at combat.

              I agree with John; as the party's Dawn, chances are whether you're okay for a Dawn, good for a Dawn, or broken strong for a Dawn, you're setting the bar for the top level of combat power in your group. So I wouldn't worry too much about whether you're powerful enough. If you take Physical attributes primary, your combat ability at 5, and supernal in that combat ability... you're going to be really good at combat.

              If you've not played Ex3 before, I'd recommend Melee because it's much simpler and covers more bases. But that depends how good you are at mechanics. If you're good at mechanics Single Point should be fine.

              I'd say, at the end of the day, it's probably fine to think "how do I imagine her sword-fighting to be like", and if it's Iajitsu with a katana, take Single Point, if not, take Melee.

                      Another thing to consider as you have stated that you'd like a rarer style of combat...is that single point is rare and unique but not among it's adherents. Martial arts students taught the same style will look relatively the same. After all you're learning the same katas utilizing the same weapons, especially in single point which only uses slashing swords and their artifact equivalent the reaper daiklave. Melee charms, all exalted type charms, are no longer sorceries as they were in the past editions, but are now natural extensions of your characters abilities.

                      So a spear fighter with the same charms as a sword fighter would still look differently in a real fight. So your sword princess can have whatever unique style she likes because of what type of sword you chose and how you portray her fighting with it. Martial arts charms on the other hand are not natural abilities of the exalted but rather deliberate shapings of essence more akin to sorcery. A single point swordsman looks differently from a melee swordsman, but not so much a another single point swordsman. Two melee using reaper daiklave wielders might look completely different based on their style descriptions, their stunts. Not to mention melees bigger tree allows for a greater divergence of supernatural skill, which can easily help distinguish your characters style. At least that is my belief, hope it helps.

                                                          If he has a lot of 2e experience this kind of thing will probably be familiar to him. It’s a much easier problem to deal with in Ex3 than 2e. At least in third edition you can take that 30% of antagonists who can be useful and piles them on in great numbers and with extreme environmental effects. It can still be fun, but my first four or five Ex3 campaigns were plagued by trying to use creatures with 6-8 dice attacks to hit PCs with a base defense of 7, and then trying to get past 13 soak with 11 withering damage.

                                                          Also the book makes no distinctions in the antagonist chapter about what’s actually supposed to be an antagonist, and what’s just put there to act as mounts/familiars for players, or animal shapes for Lunars. So you get things like the Stegodon style Siege Lizard, which looks like it’s supposed to be an antagonist to fight, but it ends up being there for players to take as a pet instead.
