                Trying to incorporate some 3e material:

                OTHER EXALTED II: THE EXIGENTS

                After the Spiral War had ended in the flight of the rebellious Exalted and the exile of Luna, Autochthon and Gaia there was held in Othrys a grand Triumph to the victors of the conflict. Shofars and bugles proclaimed victory far and wide; drums of space and harps of time rethreaded broken and glitched constellations and galactic clusters; the souls and jouten-bodies of the Titans danced together in joy(save one) proclaiming hard-won peace. Captured enemy plunder, troops and Exalts were paraded in abject defeat; Dragon-Bloods soon to be enslaved as breeding stock on the warren-worlds, mortals to be sacrificed by the million in funeral games to ease the Neverborn's slumber, weapons and artefacts to be stored in the great trophy rooms and treasuries of Othrys. Though the captured Solars managed to assassinate a Primordial in a desperate bid for escape, nevertheless at the end of the ritual procession Sol Incarnate dismounted his orichalc chariot, pulled by twelve fiery simhata, and entered the Ring of Doom alone.

                What words or thoughts passed between him and the All-Emperor then none save them can speak of; and they emphatically do not talk about it. But when he returned Sol Tyrannus, the Tyrant's Sun, bore on his brow the Radiant Corona and in his right hand the fire of Exigence.

                Since then the Exigents have been a consistent feature throughout the Spiral, made through the most extraordinary process. Through fervent prayer to the Sun, gods and devas are gifted a glimpse of that eternal flame and bathe themselves in its furious refining heat until an Exaltation reflecting their own nature is forged. The process is certainly not painless, nor is it safe; lesser gods have been completely consumed and even the greater powers are sapped of willpower and Essence until they recover.

                Currently there are 2,000 or so Exigents at large in the Spiral, compared to 480 Titanics, 150 Synodics, 200 Argentides, and the numberless Dragonblood Akuma slave-armies; not counting the rumoured and unconfirmed Liminals and Getimians. None is alike to any other; if one witnessed the meeting of any two Exigents together you would see no resemblance in those before you, and could scarcely imagine they both sprang from the same source. From a social perspective Exigents are on the level of the Argentides and Synodics - a good degree below the Titanic Exalted but still in a position of honour and far above the wretched state of the Dragonblood Akuma.

                The Exigent Exaltation is passed on in a number of ways. Quite a few of them simply die when the bearer dies, their cosmic sparks insufficient compared to the esoteric values and qualities that allow higher Exaltations to live on indestructibly. Some follow the bearer through reincarnation, following the higher soul through the process of lethe and Exalting him at an appropriate time; others are passed on through bloodline in the Terrestrial fashion; yet others simply fly free like most Exaltations do and Exalt a person chosen for some great action or value.

                Though the Exigence is firmly in the hands of the Tyrant Sun, infiltration of the Gunstar has determined that within its heresy protocols and other theodefences the Betrayers have somehow gained Exigents of their own. How have these Betrayer Exigents come into being? Where did they come from? Is there treason amongst the Titanics, the Exigents, or even with Sol himself? Have the Betrayers managed to somehow copy or siphon off the Exigence? Could it be that the Exigence heeds traitor and loyal god alike somehow? All these and more theories are constantly discussed and turned over among those who know this disturbing fact, and lead to much worry.

                        OTHER EXALTED III: THE SYNODICS

                        In the final battles of the Spiral War the Rebel Exalted were driven from lost battle to lost battle with all the time to adjust of a leaf caught in a tornado. The Primordials' sally out of besieged Othrys caught them flat-footed enough; but when the Sun revealed himself among their forces captaining the Daystar eclipsed in his furious anger, when Merela - torn in two by love of him - declared for the Primordial cause and turned her flagship's broadsides on the Solar fleet, when the Titans unleashed unspeakable powers and forbidden weapons that burned galaxies and dissolved star systems - even they had to admit they could not win. But some reasoned that if they could not win, they would try to make it a draw.

                        Flawless Dusk Wanderer, a Sidereal of the House of Journeys, resolved to follow this ethos. Risking his life many times over, he snuck into Othrys and in a series of cunning tricks and daring adventures he reached the Loom of Fate. Before Primordial forces could respond to his presence, he stole the entire building and took it with himself to Autochthonia.

                        Across the Spiral, time stopped completely. Laser beams flung between starships hung in mid-vacuum. Exalted and mortal fighters stood like perfect statues. In the Sulvan Rift, as Daystar and Silver Chair did battle, the Daystar's coiling prominence-chains stood poised to strike at Luna's chariot, traitor Granalkin's arrows frozen in mid-flight as he shot at it. On Rathess Dragon King priests and warriors craned their necks to look at their doom, frozen in mute horror at the soul-shredding storm that filled the sky.

                        But the Primordials had lived before Fate existed, and as all things were frozen like insects in amber they alone could move and think and act. And so under decree of Theion they gathered up loose strands of Fate, unfrozen by chance from the loss of the Loom, and using powerful rituals and incredible concept-forging techniques made the Design. Time flew forward again, and when the Design wove its first moment, sounded its first tick of the cosmic clock, they appeared to tend it.

                        Hermes is a young boy with straw-coloured hair, dressed in yellow and white. Hyperactive and easily distracted as any small child, his quicksilver wings constantly beating and fluttering on his back and feet to hold him aloft, he nevertheless possesses an ageless wisdom terrifyingly beyond his years.

                        Eros, a mature and vital young man , glows with the strength and virility of youth. He wears nothing above the waist, proud to show off his perfect body and locks of lustrous hair, half-copper half-gold. Indulging constantly in pleasure and dramatic displays of affection, nevertheless he is cold and task-focussed underneath.

                        Ares, about the age of 30, has a feral gleam in his eyes and a savage demeanour. His armour is iron (often covered in blood) and he has iron teeth. He carries a multitude of weapons at all times, but his favoured killing tool is the black sword War Master. He too has gold hair, but the lionskin cape he wears often obscures it.

                        Poseidon is of late middle age and in his eyes, framed by greying blond hair, one can see all the mystery and selfishness of the ocean depths. All knowledge drifts in the currents of his water-world orbiting Othrys, where it can only be fished up by his bronze net and trident. In the deepest depths, where even he fears to tread, are the most ancient and critical secrets of the Spiral, touching on Oblivion, on the Primordials' origins and nature, on the secrets of the Wyld and on the fundamental nature of all things.

                        Cronos is an old man with silver hair, who walks nevertheless with a strong and relentless gait, and is never noticed unless he wishes to be. He wears only black and black is the colour of his eyes. In his right hand is a great hourglass made of black lead - in his left is a sickle made of the same. It can kill with the smallest cut.

                        Collectively these gods - governing over Fate through the portfolios of Journeys, Serenity, Battles, Secrets and Endings - are called the Ananke Brothers. The Primordials have noted similarities to the traitorous Maidens, as well as one notable and strange difference - but not even the Brothers themselves know (or will speak of) their provenance. All that is left is speculation and conjecture.

                        Once the war was ended and the Exalted Rebellion had fled in defeat, the Ananke Brothers bathed in the fires of the Exigence for a year and a day. From this ordeal they created the Synodic Exalted, replacements for the Sidereals who had fled on the Gunstar.

                        The Synodics function primarily as advisors and mentors to the Titanic Exalted and also to mend errors in Fate. Their powers function as a clear analogue to Sidereal abilities but have wild aesthetic differences and many distinct flourishes and abilities unknown to Sidereals.

                        They do not resonate with Starmetal, instead resonating with one of 5 Magical Materials based on Caste - Hermium, Cypris, Siderite, Thalassite and Plumbum; which resemble the common mercury, copper, iron, bronze and lead respectively.

                                Not-Lunars are up!

                                OTHER EXALTED IV: THE ARGENTIDES

                                As the Daystar pursued Autochthon, driving through the Exalted void-fleet like a bullet through a barrel of fish, none of the Exalted Host thought they could survive. Prominences swung through the void, vicious whips of plasma that incinerated battleships. From the surface of the Sun-fortress a thousand thousand sunfire-bolts, each enough to boil continents, pulsed every second. Crackling solar pulses disabled communications and sensors in fluorescing waves of electromagnetic fury.

                                Then a lissom voice of moonsilver spoke to Sol Incarnate over the furious crackle of plasma fire and the Sun heard, furious beyond mortal anger in pure rage and hate of his misbegotten children -

                                "Catch me if you can."

                                So did Luna, the Argent Madonna, dare the Sun away from pursuing the Exalted host. The Daystar swung away from the Exalted and towards the Silver Chair, which reversed direction and led it on a merry chase through the universe. The stories of that chase are the stuff of legend. The Sun and Moon weaved through a constellation of newly-formed black holes, skirting event horizons and dodging spears of twisted space-time flung by the Pyrian Queen to unweave rebellious star-clusters. They dared the rim of a collapsing galaxy, slingshotting to the very edge of the Spiral and almost back again. Eventually Luna and Sol arrived at the Sulvan Rift, an extrusion of Udr around which are Albus Agnus and Norn Tertio and Perela of the floating islands and many other prophet-planets which are dedicated to scrying the Rift to try and predict the future.

                                Here, Sun and Moon fought. Luna turned herself toward the Sun (for she had no heart to flee him anymore) and the two god-chariots fought in battle for the first and only time in history. From hangar-bays and battle-stations and orichalc-nests thousands upon thousands of Sol Incarnate's forces descended. There were lumina blazing bright in battle-fury; sunbirds racing fast on wings of plasma and magnetic flux; void-fighters and chariots and war sphinxes and aurichim and garda birds and coal-black embergiants and fire elementals and crimsonjade battle-golems.

                                On the dusty, rocky surface they fought with Luna and her troops: humbabas under Granalkin the Renegade Sirrush; argentim; living ornithopters that killed with music; silver tendrils that rose from the ground and dragged enemies into the Tenemos of Luna; and even three Lunar Exalted - a Full Moon, a Changing Moon and a No Moon. But one side could not win. At the end, after bombardment and warfare had driven her forces into disarray, Luna emerged herself and did battle with the Sun. With six arms she threw a dozen poisoned blades straight at his heart. With scything whips of metal she battered at his armour. As a snake covered in thousands of silver feathers she constricted him. With sword and claw and tentacle and seduction and argument and reality-shaping she assailed him - and he withstood all with ease. His Aegis ensured no less.

                                And he responded in kind. Six times he threw the Godspear at Luna - each time he struck a wound that would have killed any lesser being and the faithful weapon returned instantly to his side. Finally, he cast a seventh and lethal throw of his Valour at the Fickle Lady - but she was not there. The Godspear lodged deep in the Chair's surface, and tore a whole third of the vessel's structure off in the ensuing quakes.

                                The Sun looked to where Granalkin had been, and where the Exalted had been, and where Luna had been - and then upward to see Gnosis streaking away, already beyond his ability to pursue. Gaia had taken Luna and the others she could far away, beyond the Spiral and into the Deep Wyld.

                                But not wholly. For the Sun's blows against Luna had damaged her deeply, fractured her psyche and spirit in ways that would have taken millennia to heal. When the Silver Chair had split, through coiled strands of sympathy and intimacy the wounds had been deepened beyond recovery, and Luna herself had split in two. Moons that could have shone, Lunar gods and goddesses that could have been, now had independent existence as a rival Incarna of the Moon.

                                Selene now shone over the Spiral as a replacement for Traitor Luna.

                                In four seasonal phase-faces she sends her light upon the Spiral: as Catallesta, the Silver Moon, she shines bright as the Silver Chair once did all the time - going through her own shifting phases of full-waning-dark - before turning over to Abraxus, by whose reign the moon is a great shadowy eye with a blood-red star-pupil gazing on secrets and hidden things. Abraxus then gives up the role to Hecate by whose dominion the moon is covered in mist and almost completely indistinct; before the mists fade and Ia Sin shines with awful yellow-green light before ultimately going back over to Catallesta at the start of the new year.

                                The Argentide Exalted number 200, two-thirds of the number of the Lunar Betrayers. They are divided into four Castes derived from the four faces of Selene:

                                Catallestans are empowered by Catallesta, the Silver Moon. As befitting the Arrow Bride, they are highly combat-capable. Ranged weaponry is their forte, and they wield it with skill surpassing almost all others. Catallestans are also highly skilled at martial arts and survival, and are incredibly capable at hunting and tracking down quarry - making them excellent assassins.

                                Abraxans come from Abraxus, the Dark Star. Swathed in occult secrets and mysterious darkness, they have strong facility with magic and especially Sorcery. All hidden and secret things are plain to their supernatural perception, and they have great skill with social manipulation as well as their more obvious abilities.

                                Hecateans have power from Hecate, the Shrouded Moon. Illusion, secrecy and misdirection are at their core and they make excellent spies. They have the most skill at shapeshifting among the Argentide Exalted and can even turn themselves into mists or flowing water!

                                The Lamassu are empowered by Ia Sin; Mother Madness, the dreaded Moon in Tatters. Among the Argentides they have the most skill at Shaping reality and the Wyld - they also have power over madness, and can inflict or remove it at will, give themselves or others multiple personalities they can put on or off at demand, cause hallucinations to become real and deadly, and so on.

                                The Argentide Exalted attune readily to moonsilver and adamant.

                                Their social status in the Spiral is high, comparable to Synodics as a degree below the Titanics and above the Exigents. They have no special relationship or bond to either the Titanics or the Solars. While their shapeshifting ability is less than that of the Lunars they have a unique capacity to form bond-links with totem animals and can even use Charms through these creatures without being bodily present.

                                    Seems nobody is interested enough in the Argentides to bother commenting. I'm hoping this little piece on the Spiral's Magical Materials will stir up interest (there's no crunch, due to me not being very good at it).

                                    MAGICAL MATERIALS

                                    The Six Magical Materials collectively are the true paragon of all forms of matter - existing simultaneously on the material, spiritual and conceptual levels of reality. No other element, compound or substance will be a competitor in the construction of quality Artifacts and magitechnology. Moreover, they resonate uniquely with the essences of the Exalted, allowing for easier attunement and use of more powerful Evocations for types that synergise.

                                    ADAMANT
                                    This rare crystal, in colour ranging from azure blue to midnight black, is a solidification of the Oramaic Firmament which helps maintain the stable reality enjoyed by the Spiral (the Firmament continually sheds parts of itself as Adamant, and continuously regrows). Commonly found in deep space, it can only be cut by another piece of Adamant (though it can be shattered under extreme circumstances), and is thus often used as armour and. The best Adamant is wholly opaque but this is extraordinarily rare - its crafters gauge quality by degree of transparency, the weakest and most prone to shattering being like coloured glass. It is also commonly used to build autoklaves. Adamant resonates with Dements, Rhapsodes, Hierarchs, the Argentides and, if they exist, the mythical Nocturnal Exalted.

                                    ORICHALCUM
                                    Sun's Blood. Dawn-Metal. Living Gold. All these describe the king of metals, the supreme substance, Orichalcum. Orichalcum shines with an incomparable lustre - it even glows faintly in the dark - and is by far the most commonly used Magical Material. Orichalcum is used in armour, weapons, starships, Warstriders and far more. It has a noted ability to wound deeply foul Creatures of Darkness, and is best at containing and directing plasma and electromagnetic radiation. Orichalcum is most typically gold mined and carefully exposed to Solar essence and high-temperature plasma which recreates the core of a sun; or is taken from where it bubbles up in the furious particle winds of stellar surfaces, by miners who work in terror of sunbird attacks - the solar elementals lay orichalcum eggs, and often their nests are looted to gain the precious substance, awakening bitter enmities.
                                    It resonates with the Sovereigns, Quaestors, Theurgists, Tutors and Chimebringers - and with the hated Solar Betrayers.

                                    MOONSILVER
                                    If orichalcum is the king of metals, moonsilver is the queen. From Silver Chair shines Selene's Wyld-light, and this light is reflected through the countless lesser moons of the Spiral as their phases march in accord with its own. From there it impregnates fields of silver growing deep in planetary crusts, which become moonsilver. The metal lives, and forging it is more a matter of coaxing it into a desired shape than heating or tempering it. Moonsilver is useful against the Fair Folk but not so much as Siderite, their dreaded Bane-Metal. In all fields where speed, finesse, agility or subtlety are necessary, moonsilver is commonly used. It resonates with the Dements, the Ecstatics, the Anchorites, the Benefactors, the Mountebanks, the Revilers and the Dramatists; and also with the Argentide and Lunar Exalted.

                                    STARMETAL
                                    Starmetal is made of solidified destiny. When a god dies, his essence and form turn into that substance as his narrative crumbles away. When a fate is averted by any means or culled by the Design and its ministering Synodics, it turns into starmetal which appears at the site where said event happened. Of itself, starmetal is of lesser use except for parts in other constructs, as no Exalted in the Spiral resonate with the pure metal. However, in areas under sway of the Design - when exposed to resonance of Journeys, Serenity, Battles, Secrets or Endings - starmetal becomes hermium, cypris, siderite, thalassite and plumbum, metals unknown by the Gunstar. Starmetal and all its derivatives seem to slightly bend fate in favour of their wielders.

                                    Hermium
                                    Hermium is a semisolid silvery metal which is formed by resonance of Journeys. It has a strong tendency to flow into shapes reminiscent of travel - wings, sails and wheels are common. Hermium is a common material in anything that is meant to move swiftly, and is also very suited to use in projectile weapons - both as weapon and ammunition. It resonates with the Reflectives, the Scourges and the Pilgrims of the Synodic Exalted.

                                    Cypris
                                    Cypris is a coppery colour, is highly reflective, and is formed by resonance of Serenity. It typically forms into aesthetically pleasing curved shapes (sometimes suggestive of carnal matters) and is worthy as a magical material, though it doesn't have much combat application and is best suited to use as magical pieces of jewellery, items of clothing, statuary and musical instruments. It resonates with the Galateans of the Synodic Exalted, and with Theurgists, Ecstatics, Chimebringers and Dramatists.

                                    Siderite
                                    Siderite resembles common iron but often has a reddish colour reminiscent of freshly-spilled blood, or more rarely is jet black. It often grows uncontrollably at battlefields, forming great thorn-forests of blades and guns, statues of combatants still posed in struggle within. It is one of the very best materials used to make melee weapons, and is even more devastating against the Fair Folk than regular iron. It resonates with the Juggernauts, the Inquisitors, the Benefactors and the Battleborn, as well as the Huscarls of the Synodic Exalted.

                                    Thalassite
                                    Thalassite resembles mortal bronze and comes from the resonance of Secrets. It is commonly found at the bottom of deep seas - on abyssal plains and in deep-sea trenches. It naturally forms into nautical shapes reminiscent of sea life. Thalassite is good for all purposes but excels to make items aiding stealth or subterfuge. It synergises with the Fiend, Quaestor, Reviler and Puppeteer Castes, as well as the Spymasters of the Synodics.

                                    Plumbum
                                    Plumbum is an exceedingly heavy jet-black metal that is associated with death, often en masse. It naturally forms into shapes reminiscent of death and ending - bones, skulls, scythe or sickle-like shapes and tear-drops. Often it forms into circular or lemniscate shapes as well. Plumbum is useful for anything specifically used to assassinate or kill en masse, and causes unnatural fear in enemies. It resonates with the Devourers, the Inquisitors, the Fiends and the Reapers of the Synodics.

                                    JADE
                                    Jade is the solidified essence of the Six Prime Elements (Fire, Water, Earth, Air, Life, theion to), which are the most fundamental building blocks of the Spiral and lie behind its periodic table of hydrogen, carbon, uranium and so on. It often forms deep underground planetary crusts under Demesnes of elemental aspect or in the paths of dragon lines. Most Jade artefacts are wielded by the dragon-blooded Akuma-slaves of the Primordials, but Yellow Jade resonates with the Sovereigns and is commonly used by them along with Orichalcum.

                                    SOULSTEEL
                                    Soulsteel is a foul and disturbing metal consisting of iron fused with souls. The metal is covered with agony-wracked faces, screaming and moaning in pain all the while - some have even reported it to weep tears. It is very common in Black Non and is rarely used in the Spiral, though it is resonates with the Fiend and Dement castes. Abyssals and certain Alchemicals in the Gunstar use it commonly though.

                                                        One issue with this that really stuck with me is that you've really inflated the numbers, and made the universe, quite frankly, too big. I understand that you wanted to put an emphasis on just how grand reality was in the Age of the Primordials, and how much has been lost, but things are so big that they seem meaningless to me. When you've got billions of galaxies, even those as powerful as Elder Exalted are little more than footnotes, barring unusual circumstances. What does it matter that your Titanic lord of blades can fight off an entire army by himself, when the scale of things is so large that any destruction he can wreak will be lost in the statistical noise of a thousand thousand battlefields? What does it matter that a great hero seduces the master of an entire solar system and turns them to the side of the primordials, when said system is merely one of billions - were you to map the area controlled by the primordials, the change the hero brings would not even be visible to the naked eye. What does it matter that a Pyrian lord might bring forth a planet of perfect and beauteous order, when swarm-fleets can encircle and devour the entire galaxy in which it lies?

                                                        The scale at which these things occur is just too large for human stories to be meaningful - it makes the dramas, battles, hopes and dreams of the Exalted about as important to the universe as the desperate struggle between a pair of anthills would be in the Exalted of the Age of Sorrows.
