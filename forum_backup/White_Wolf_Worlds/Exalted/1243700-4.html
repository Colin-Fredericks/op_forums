                    Rush vs. Disengage is a pretty niche case. The timing of their resolution only matters if both parties are acting on the same tick (which I do realize is the theme of this whole thread), and even then it only matters in a few very specific scenarios:

                    1.) Without Charms, Rush vs. Disengage only happens if Character A and Character B are in Close Range of each other (or A moves into Close Range with her Reflexive Move before B declares his actions), and then one of the following cases occurs: a.) A declares actions first, guesses that B is going to try to Disengage, declares a Rush, and then B actually does declare a Disengage, b.) B declares actions first, declares a Disengage, and then A declares a Rush to try to counter it, or c.) if actions are declared simultaneously, A guesses that B is going to Disengage and declares a Rush to try to counter it, and is proven correct.

                    In scenarios 1a. and 1c. I would have no qualms with rolling the Rush first. If either action fails, the resolution is obvious. If both the Rush (rolled first) and the Disengage (rolled second) succeed, my interpretation is that the Disengager would move out to Short Range, then the Rush's reflexive movement would trigger and move the Rusher back into Close Range, and then... the phrasing is trickier, but I think a common-sense interpretation would allow the Disengage's reflexive movement to trigger, allowing the Disengager to move back to Short Range. Ultimately, however, unless a third party intervenes, the Rusher wins that exchange, since the Disengager had to spend Initiative to get away from her, meaning the Rusher's turn will come up next, and the Disengager's reflexive moment from winning a Disengage is already spent.

                    Scenario 1b. is slightly trickier, but only slightly. If B declared his Disengage first, I might be inclined to roll it first. In that case, if B wins, he moves to Short Range before A's Rush is rolled. A can still Rush from Short Range, but since B's movement occurred before the Rush was rolled, no further movement would happen on this round even if the Rush succeeds. If the Rush failed, the resolution would be obvious, but if the Rush succeeded, and there was no outside interference with Initiative, A's turn would come up first on the next round. If A then moved from Short into Close Range with her Reflexive move, B's Reflexive move from Disengage would trigger, moving him back to Short, then that movement would trigger Rush's Reflexive movement, moving A back into Close. Again, if both the Rusher and Disengager succeed at their rolls on the same tick, and no one else tampers with their Initiative, the Rusher effectively 'wins' the exchange: order of resolution only changes the timing of whether all the movement is concluded on the same round they Rush and Disengage, or if the last of their movement occurs on the next round.

                    2.) I had a bit here talking about edge cases with Charms, but most of them (at least involving Solar Charms) are actually fairly self-evident, with even my slight confusion over Godspeed Steps having been answered in the past by one of your questions to the Ask the Devs thread. My only remaining confusion is Fleet Dreaming Image, and my confusion regarding that Charm is somewhat tangential to the question of this thread, so I think I'm going to ask Ask the Devs instead of expounding on it here.

                    The only specific note I'll make is that Sunlight Bleeding Away is a way of answering the "Rusher effectively always wins if both Rusher and Disengager succeed at their actions" issue I noted in part 1, since it would allow the Disengager to win instead in that scenario.

                    Alternative: This would most certainly be a houserule, but if you wanted quicker resolution and a more definitive result, a Storyteller could choose to resolve an opposed Rush and Disengage on the same tick as a single opposed roll of Dex + Athletics vs. Dex + Dodge instead of two opposed rolls, one of Dex + Athletics vs. Dex + Athletics and one of Dex + Dodge vs. Dex + Athletics. You could then allow only the winner of the contested roll's action to succeed, removing the possibilities of mutual successes or mutual failures. (Incidentally, I will note that a mutual failure would also effectively favor the Rusher, just like a mutual success does, since the Disengager would still end up at Close Range with lower Initiative than the Rusher.)

                                                Immaculate Earth Dragon Style's clash potential and general tactics Breakdown!

                                                Earth Dragon Style allows the heaviest of armor and provides plenty of toughness in its Charms, so it is well-disposed to survive a clash gone bad. The armor's mobility penalty makes it harder on you if your opponent responds to a delay with a disengage action, and especially if they succeed and you need to rush, so hopefully you can declare a smash attack after they declare disengage, and resolve your smash attack before their disengage is rolled.

                                                Earth Dragon's tetsubo is a horrible defensive weapon, as it actually penalizes Parry by 1. Artifact equivalents offer a neutral +0, equal to the bare-handed Parries also allowed by the Style. The Style by itself offers no way to parry lethal weapons or projectiles with your bare hands, forcing you to eat a tetsubo's penalty if you don't have an artifact hammer or a Charm like Become the Hammer or Fists of Iron Technique.

                                                Basically, an Earth Dragon stylist seldom relies on her Defense to avoid getting hit, but just to mitigate the damage of withering attacks before applying her prodigious soak. This is why Stone Dragon's Skin does not bother trying to improve her poor Parry Defense, but merely to keep as much of it as possible, even after previous blows have successfully injured her.

                                                Clashing is a better defense for the Earth Dragon, when she can manage it. Weapon-Breaking Defense Technique is a rare Charm that allows a Dragon-Blood to make a reflexive clash attack that doesn't count against her action that round or the next, even if any Exalt needs to meet a reset condition to use it more than once in a fight. Weapon-Breaking Defense Technique can't hurt the Earth Dragon's opponent, but it can make following defenses, attacks, and clashes easier since the enemy is deprived of their preferred form of attack and defense.

                                                Should the Earth Dragon choose to clash with her hands or with her hammer? Her hammer enjoys the +2 clash bonus that gives it just two less dice of basic accuracy than her bare hands (one die less, as an artifact), making it a fair answer to a mundane medium weapon or any sort of heavy weapon. If your opponent would rather Full Defense than clash you, you're better off chancing an unarmed attack. But sometimes, you just really need your clash to be a smashing attack, and Earth Dragon Style isn't compatible with fist weapons.

                                                Force of the Mountain doesn't directly help win a clash, but helps you capitalize on a win, especially with a successful clash's damage bonus, and if you used the hammer instead of your fist.


                                                Originally posted by Clashing and Smashing
                                                Before we talk about the rest of Earth Dragon's Charms, we need to talk more about how important it is for you, when fighting alone with this Style, to use a clashing withering attack (the "setup" attack) to secure a "followup" attack against a target before they have a chance to act. Two of the Style's Charms absolutely depend on this tactic, if you are forced to fight alone, but each of them merely expand upon the inherent advantages of this tactic.

                                                Smart opponents will go after your weak Defense rather than challenging you to a clash, which could lead to this strategy only becoming available when you hold the Initiative advantage. Even then, it requires your opponent to dare meeting your attack with an attack instead of attempting a Full Defense or Disengage action.

                                                Even assuming that they take the dare, they could win; on top of the Initiative you lose or the injuries you take, failing the clash means suffering a total Defense penalty of -3 to both Parry and Evasion from onslaught and clash failure, not counting the Parry penalty from using a mundane tetsubo against a lethal or ranged attack that you can't parry bare-handed (at least without Become the Hammer or Fist of Iron Technique), or counting the Evasion penalty from any armor you're wearing.

                                                If your enemy has the Initiative advantage after that exchange, they can act first in the next round and exploit all of those penalties on you without needing an ally to do it for them. Without your Defense to mitigate damage, you'll have to rely entirely on the considerable toughness that Earth Dragon Style can provide.

                                                But if you succeed at the clash, the considerable damage that Earth Dragon can inflict really shines. Plus, it is now your opponent's Defense that will suffer a total -3 Defense penalty. You won't need a second clash attack to improve your chances of landing a hammer blow while they're in that condition, though since you're acting first, you certainly can delay to take the risk. This is an excellent time to make your follow-up attack decisive, but we'll talk more about that later.

                                                Now we want to factor in smashing attacks, which make this tactic even more risky and somewhat more expensive, but far more powerful. Mote-wise, this advance tactic is actually cheaper if you are using Earth Dragon Form, which adds dice to smashing attacks. The risk comes from the costs of attempting a smash attack, which penalizes your Defense by 1; that brings your Defense penalties for failure up to -4. Further, beware of the fact that delaying to clash and then attempting a smash attack means spending a total of 4i, which punishes you that much more if you lose and makes it that much harder to act first in the next round even if you succeed.

                                                Not that you need to do better than basic success if knocking your target away sends them over a cliff. That can end the fight right there, without any Charms or follow-up attacks. But absent that or any kind of obstruction (like an ally to keep them embattled in their new range band), merely knocking someone away is worthless. Since they aren't also knocked prone without magic, they can simply take a reflexive Move action to close the distance and attack you without penalty, while your own Defense is penalized by -2 from the onslaught of the clash and the cost of the smash attack.

                                                In a less tactically interesting environment, it's better to knock your opponent prone. Even if you fail to act first in the next round, your opponent will suffer a -3 attack penalty (one point greater than your Defense penalty) from being prone or from flurrying a rise-from-prone action with an attack. If they fail to rise-from-prone (the difficulty is only 2, but the action suffers a -3 penalty on top of any armor they're wearing) their attack will instead suffer a -6 penalty. A flurry would also penalize their Defense by 1. Also, they can't take a movement action to disengage that turn, one way or another. If they're still prone by the time you attack them, their Parry is reduced by 1 and their Evasion by 2, before adding any penalty for a flurry or for wearing armor.

                                                But if you succeed well enough to act first in the next round after knocking them down, your opponent's Parry will be penalized by -4, and their Evasion will suffer a -5 penalty even before including any mobility penalties from their armor. Plus, being prone means that any Earth Dragon attack made against them, smashing or otherwise, gets bonus dice. Again: You won't need a second clash attack to improve your chances of landing a hammer blow while they're in that condition, though since you're acting first, you certainly can delay to take the risk. This is an excellent time to make your follow-up attack decisive.

                                                If you want to make the followup attack a smashing one, now is a better time to consider knocking them away, since they'll still be prone when they land, and thus will be incapable of simply moving back into close range with you on their next turn. That could buy you time to activate a Simple-type power-up Charm, or start running away without needing to disengage. Beware that a decisive smashing attack still costs 2i, so you're effectively reducing your decisive damage pool to buy that time. So without particular Charms, there's no reason to make the follow-up attack a knockdown smash, and if you don't need to buy time, you might as well just make a normal decisive attack.

                                                Unless, of course, you chose to make a non-smashing attack with the setup withering attack. In which case, they're not already flat on their back, and you're currently in the best position to put them there before you reset yourself to base Initiative, to afford yourself protection from their best efforts to hurt you.

                                                Perfection of Earth Body makes it easier to shrug off failed clashes and capitalize on successful ones, and further encourages you to smash your target on either the setup attack or followup attack, if not both. However, using the Charm costs you Initiative, and therefore makes it harder to gain enough advantage to delay and clash.
                                                Originally posted by Impudent, Impatient Dragons
                                                Avalanche Method has an Essence Minimum of 4. If you want solid, Earth-aspected followup attacks to finish an opponent off, there are easier alternatives outside of the Style.

                                                Rolling Boulder Blow (Brawl 3, Essence 2) is like Avalanche Method Lite, but has the bonus of adding threshold successes on your attack roll (of which you'll have plenty with the above tactics) to your decisive damage pool. Earth Dragon Form cannot assist Brawl attacks, but I dare say that you won't need the discount to Become the Hammer. Also, knocking them out of your range band and prone means that they can't attack you immediately after you reset to base Initiative; they would need to use their movement action to rise from prone, so they can't close the distance with you immediately.

                                                Firmament-Shattering Impact (Brawl 5, Essence 3) is also cheaper than Avalanche Method, and has similar knockback range/damage potential. It should even be able to work with Hungry Earth Strike like Avalanche Method does.
                                                Okay, now we're ready to talk about Stillness-of-Stone Atemi. This Charm is most powerful and useful in team fights, when you're fighting with an ally against an enemy who relies on Evasion and movement actions. But getting any use out of this Charm when you're alone demands that you understand the advanced tactics described above.

                                                As a Withering-only Charm, Stillness-of-Stone Atemi is a natural partner to the setup withering attack. It can supplement a bare-handed attack, a hammer attack, or a smashing hammer attack.

                                                Beware that delaying to use this Charm in a clash costs you 2i, which leaves you with less Initiative to spend on Stillness-of-Stone and still have the Initiative advantage on your opponent. Delaying to use this charm on a smash attack in a clash costs you 4i, which makes it worse. Having that Initiative advantage is important, because unless you crash your target, the mobility penalty will wear off at the beginning of his turn... and also, you'll have failed to take advantage of all the other penalties that you inflicted, which would really suck. It sucks beyond measure if you were trying to setup for Avalanche Method.

                                                Anyway, if you do succeed in crashing your opponent, you're not only assured to act first in the next round, but you'll also enjoy an Initiative Break bonus that will help a followup decisive attack. Plus, the mobility penalty from Stillness-of-Stone will last until your target recovers from crash, which makes it harder for your target to rise-from-prone, if you knocked them prone with the setup attack or will knock them prone with the followup. It could buy you more time to escape or power-up if you use the followup attack to smash them away after knocking them prone with the setup. Alternatively, it will make it harder for them to disengage from you even as it extends your window of opportunity to destroy them with Avalanche method. And they're just plain screwed if they rely on Evasion for Defense.

                                                If the target you crashed was a spirit, you can also reflexively slap them with Ghost-Grounding Blow. Beware the Initiative price that could cost you a followup attack.

                                                A normal clash can be an opportunity to use Shattering Fist Strike instead of Weapon-Breaking Defense Technique, saving you seven motes and the use of the reflexive clash for later in the battle. SFS is a gambit, and can be used as a followup attack if you need your opponent's weapon gone more badly than you need the opportunity to kill them.

                                                I already talked about WBDT.

                                                There is no point to delaying Earthshaker Attack, which is unblockable and undodgeable and can’t be clashed. This Charm is one reason why an Earth Dragon Stylist might find herself needing to duel and clash an enemy even when she has allies in the fight, since her friends want to avoid EA's radius. Since EA can knock opponents prone, it's an opportunity to enjoy the benefits of using Earth Dragon Form with a hammer blow against a prone opponent for your setup attack, making it easier to achieve a followup attack.

                                                Hungry Earth Strike is a powerful setup attack Charm, but the 6i cost strains combination potential. If your target isn't already prone, you need to smash him down, which means using a hammer blow, which means you want to use a clash attack for maximum accuracy (but your target might refuse to clash and force you to roll against their Defense), and that means the total Initiative cost raises to 10i before you start spending any Initiative on Stillness-of-Stone Atemi. At least you don't need to pay the Initiative cost of Ghost-Grounding Blow. Unless you are absolutely sure that you can crash the target with this setup attack, "Delayed Clashing Smash + Stillness-of-Stone Atemi + Hungry Earth Strike" is probably not worth it, especially if the target isn't being smashed into an earthern surface.

                                                If your target is already prone (perhaps from Earthshaker Attack), you could just make a normal unarmed attack without delay and enjoy the Earth Dragon Form's dice bonus to prone targets, counting on the Overwhelming bonus of Stillness-of-Stone Atemi, the 8-doubling of Hungry Earth Strike, and the raw damage boost of Force of the Mountain (and possibly also Perfection of Earth Body) to make up for the damage potential lost by not using the hammer.

                                                Stillness-of-Stone Atemi + Hungry Earth Strike could also be used to supplement a withering followup attack, after a relatively cheap setup attack has left the target extremely vulnerable and potentially given you plenty of Initiative to spend. Since Hungry Earth Strike requires crashing the target to achieve its full effect, the mobility penalty of Stillness-of-Stone Atemi would also be extended, which extends your window for using Avalanche Technique so that it doesn't need to be the followup attack.

                                                Ideally, you use Avalanche Method to murder someone wearing full-plate armor after your buddies wore down his Defense with onslaught. Without any of that, this thing is a headache to access. As a reminder, Hungry Earth Strike does not inflict mobility penalties; nothing in Earth Dragon Style does except for Stillness-of-Stone Atemi.

                                                If they're not wearing armor, you need a setup attack with Stillness-of-Stone Atemi so that you can use Avalanche Method as the followup attack, or you need to crash them with Stillness-of-Stone, ideally on a followup attack with Hungry Earth Strike.

                                                If Avalanche Method is used as a direct followup attack for a non-smashing SoSA setup made against a target that wasn't already prone, the target is suffering from a starting Defense penalty of -3. If the target was already prone or the setup made them prone, then the target is suffering from a starting Parry penalty of -4 and Evasion penalty of -5 (plus mobility penalties), and Earth Dragon Form gives you bonus attack dice even if none of your attacks with Avalanche Method are smashing.

                                                Why would you not want to use smashing attacks with Avalanche Method, especially if you have the target trapped with Hungry Earth Strike? Because smashing attacks still cost Initiative, so each one you use lowers your decisive damage pool. However, the damage pool of each attack can't fall below her base Initiative, so after certain a point, there's nothing more to lose.

                                                If you wanted to start with a smashing attack, would you need to pay the Initiative before or after activating the Charm? I don't know. If so, you'd need 14+ Initiative.

                                                You certainly would need 14+ Initiative to delay to attempt clashing with Avalanche Method without starting with a smashing attack. But would you want to delay and clash?

                                                If you don't have a hammer, then there's no point to delaying; just use your fists or stomp them with your feet. But without the hammer, you can't smash. If you want to smash, you might want the +2 clash bonus, especially if the target is only suffering a -3 Defense penalty at the start. But if they're suffering a -4/-5 penalty... And each attack should inflict another onslaught penalty, which means you could start with a non-clashing punch or two before switching to smashing hammer blows.

                                                Though the number of attacks you can make is limited by their total mobility penalty. How well did you do with SoSA, and how many penalty points did you dare pay for?

                                                Do you take the risk that you might miss with any Avalanche attack and lose the rest, or are you currently in Earth Aura and don't need to sweat that?

                                                Are they even trapped with Hungry Earth Strike? Or is there a fatal fall off a cliff at long range?

                                                For all that this Charm can be inflexible to access, it can be astoundingly flexible in execution.
