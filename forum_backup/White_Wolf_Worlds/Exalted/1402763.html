    A Devil's Bargain (Roll)

    07-14-2020, 04:44 AM
    Originally posted by Exalted Corebook page 216
    Bargain: Bargaining is similar to the persuade action, but doesn’t depend on your target’s Intimacies. Instead, you must offer a bribe, gift, or favor that the character you’re convincing believes is worth the difficulty or danger of the task you’re asking him to perform. The Storyteller should take into account the Intimacies, wealth, and social status of the character in deciding what they will consider sufficient payment (a wealthy noble is not going to be moved to favor your cause in court by the gift of an apple, though a starving beggar might be).
    If a Bargain action is used against a PC, who decides if the character being offered the Bargain thinks the payment is worth the action they're being asked to perform?

    I think it's the character's player, as they are the one who invented the character and knows what makes their character tick.

    A strict RAW reading might suggest it's the ST. What does Exalted Forum think?

          My character is addicted to opium. I have a Flaw "addicted to opium". I have a Major Principle "I like taking opium".

          Sally Salesman offers me a hit of opium if I let her past the door I am supposed to be guarding.

          If I am playing in good faith... wouldn't I just take the deal (and the 2 Bonus XP) and let her go past? Why would you make Sally roll for that? Sally could have 4 dice. She could have 13 dice with double 7s. I want the opium, she wants to pass, just make the trade.

          If there's an important, compelling character reason for me to not let Sally past, is it unreasonable for me to say "I know I'm playing a drug addict, but I'm a functioning addict.           I say the former example just makes sense. Don't bog down your game with pointless rolls.

          I say in the latter example, the player knows the character better than the ST and the other players at the table and is entitled to make that call. That doesn't mean Sally doesn't get to pass, it means Sally has to offer something more than a single hit of opium. And when she offers something of sufficient worth... we loop back round to the first scenario -- why would I not just accept the trade?

          Now... I can see a middle ground where the player accepts that the trade is something the character might agree to, albeit reluctantly, and asks the NPC to roll against their Resolve. But this is a really niche middle ground, and, assuming good faith on the part of the player, I absolutely defend the player's right to say "I am uncomfortable with this deal, I don't think what is being offered is sufficient".

          If you are playing in good faith, and someone offers your character sufficient payment to do something then you should do it. That's literally the definition of "sufficient". If the payment offered is insufficient it means that you don't think the payment is worth what you are being asked to do... so the Bargain auto-fails. Just like a Persuade action auto-fails if doesn't hit a sufficient Intimacy. If you don't think the payment is worth the action, then the payment isn't worth the action. Period.

          *****

          Now if a player at the table is playing in bad faith, you can't fix that with a dice roll against their character's Resolve. And there's a larger issue that you need to fix with an OOC conversation.

          If you try to beat down a PC by throwing dice at their character that is not going to fix poor player behaviour. I literally can't think of anything more counter-productive than that. I know Exalted doesn't have a Storytelling Chapter but this class in DMing 101 is called "How to Lose Friends and Alienate People".

                                                      I object to players just being able to say “no he didn’t convince me, no roll.” just because they don’t want to be convinced.
