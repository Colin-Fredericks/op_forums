                                                Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post
                                                Hard Bargains are definitely a cool idea, but I'm reserving judgement until I see them in play. I like to think I'm a reasonably creative person, but I'm just not sure whether I'll regularly be able to thinki of something on the spot which seems to be (1) in the spirit of the original influence, (2) not quite as bad, and (3) not totally toothless. I would argue that, in the example, Heather's metafictional Storyteller failed at the second point: "sell out the other real-life people I'm playing with" is usually a much harder sell than "sell out this minor NPC". Would I have done better?



                                                The Exalt takes a Hard Bargain. Now what?

                                                Offering up something else in exchange for the Pictarious Rex isn't a Hard Bargain, because it would be literal bargaining in-character. That's no different from refusing outright. I suppose that I could offer to let them refuse this offer only by personally deciding that opposing Gazban matters more than being the rightful heir, renouncing that Intimacy, but "radically alter your character concept" isn't a much better ask than "betray the PCs." Reducing an Intimacy from Major to Minor (as the book seems to suggest) has the opposite problem: it just doesn't seem to change very much, even assuming I'm insistent on them not increasing it again the next time they do something king-ey.

                                                Maybe in actual play, really ambiguous cases like that don't come up often. Maybe I'll quickly start presenting nastier social rolls with fallback positions already in mind. I don't know. I hope so, because as I said, they're a cool idea.
                                                I didn't understand it to mean that the Hard Bargain has to be 'less bad' than the original influence; it just needs to be comparable. It's perfectly fair if the player decides to stick with the original influence rather than the alternative--that's the 'hard' part.

                                                I think your Intimacies and concept could be really helpful in determining what the bargain might be, but you can also work in future plot points. Let's say you want to have a local town turn against the Circle so that they are forced to go on a quest to win back trust. Your players have Virtues like Justice and Ambition which fit this story.

                                                So let's say the influence is to inspire the PC to rage against a person they care about and don't want to hurt. You could give the PC an easy option to just break something valuable instead (trickier with the sanctity of Merits, because shattering an Artifact weapon would be a good swap for hurting a beloved Intimacy, I feel; but perhaps smashing a local icon or holy site will do, as this feeds into your intended plot), or might allow the PC to take their anger out on an influential third person instead (say, the local magistrate, because she represents denial of the PC's freedom at a time when they feel they are being controlled by others and resent it), or might fall back on the tried and tested: combat (you rule that in fighting their new urges, the PC misses the Wyld Hunt which has now circled them and prepares for an ambush).

                                                I use Intimacies in my current Vampire game, as I love them so much, and for every Intimacy a character has, I try to plan at least 3-4 plot hooks which I can use whenever I need inspiration. I intentionally plan for NPCs to have opposing goals to create conflict and I update them to fit with the story I have sketched out ahead.

                                                I think you could do the same here. It's just a matter of thinking, 'How can I turn this person's Virtues or Intimacies into an opportunity to propel the story forward in a way that's interesting?' And when the thing that propels the story forward is a conflict, and one that challenges a PC's weaknesses, you have something ripe for a Hard Bargain.

                                                    Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post
                                                    Essence, please. I'm not too proud to beg. Please please please please just pick whether reflexive Charms create reflexive or "real" actions, and specify every exception. Alternately, specify every time. I really want to love this game, and to know whether I do, you need to tell me the rules.
                                                    When it comes to Charms and the limitations on actions per turn, I think a lot of the ambiguity here comes from not having clear designation for cost, action type, duration, and so on for each Charm (especially action type). On top of that, it seems like most of the usage of Steps (given that Charms are Step 1 by default and you can only use 1 Charm per Step) is actually just to reinvent the Simple action type in a way that takes more words and that is slower to understand than just saying so.

                                                    Imagine if the rule about the number of actions you can take was written this way:

                                                    Each character can use a maximum of two Simple actions per round. Even if a character uses a Simple action generated by a Reflexive action (such as a counterattack Charm), that still counts against the limit. If you would use a Simple action but have already used two Simple actions this round, the action doesn't happen.
                                                    Now take that and clearly mark every Charm and every non-charm combat action as Simple/Dramatic/Reflexive, and have a generic Simple attack action (let's call it "Strike" for clarity) that Charms can also give you. Then you just need consistent templating for directly-used Simple actions and for generated Simple actions:

                                                    Basic Action: Strike
                                                    Action Simple

                                                    You attack a target, using the resolution rules on page XX.
                                                    Glorious Exalted Bolt
                                                    Prerequisite Ranged Combat 2 or Sagacity 2
                                                    Cost 1m; Action Simple

                                                    The Exalt forms a blazing bolt of Essence aligned with their nature to strike their enemies.

                                                    This Charm acts as a ranged Strike using either Sagacity or Ranged Combat, except that instead of using a wielded weapon, you generate an essence attack, which you treat as a heavy ranged weapon with short range and the Artifact and Ranged tags. You can add 1a to the Cost to increase the essence attack's range to medium.
                                                    Swift Counterattack
                                                    Prerequisite Close Combat 3
                                                    Cost 1m; Action Reflexive (Step 8)

                                                    Reading her enemy’s movements, the Exalt unleashes a deadly counterattack.

                                                    Make a melee Strike using Close Combat against one enemy who attacked you this round. This Strike suffers the flurry penalty as if it were part of a flurry.
                                                    (Note the difference between "acts as" and "make"; the latter invokes the Simple action limit, while the former avoids it since it's already been invoked by the Charm being Simple.)

                                                    And, there you go, no more ambiguity.

                                                    Pathfinder 2e uses a very similar structure to this for actions-that-create-other-actions. For example, Flurry of Blows is a 1-action ability (Simple action equivalent, basically) that lets you Strike twice, and each Strike resolves as a full action even though it's only costing you one of your actions for the round. That system doesn't use it for a hard cap on actions-per-round, but it does carry across an escalating penalty on multiple attacks per round, which works very nicely as a soft cap to prevent people from optimizing just for total number of attacks per round.
