                                    There's a YouTuber who goes by the name FMF who does a lot of discussion, analysis, and deconstruction of RWBY. Some of it is absolutely ridiculous; he always puts the worst possible spin on any information in order to declare a plot hole or retcon. He insisted for two straight years that Mistral must have a way to tame Grimm because of the Kingdoms of Remnant board game, for example, rather than accepting the much more likely scenario that in the context of the board game, players would have the option to play cards simulating Grimm being drawn into, and participating in, battles. Because, you know, it also has a Sandstorm card, and it seems unlikely Vacuo has weather control technology. He got really angry about Remnant having things called Kingdoms that are actually democracies. (I wanted to ask him if he gets really angry that the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland calls itself a kingdom.) Stuff like that.

                                    But a lot of his takedowns are super on-point and insightful, and one of them is how the Breach is constructed. He really catalyzed that for me and got a lot of things that were floating around in my head to come together. To wit:

                                    The Breach in Volume 2 is structured as a narrowly averted crisis; Grimm got into the city, and some people got hurt and there was some property damage, but it was swiftly contained with limited loss of life. Team RWBY is happy and celebratory afterwards! They don't act like people whose city has just gotten its asshole reamed with Grimm with immense loss of life and property, they act like conquering heroes who just got their first major win as budding Hunters. The narrative treats them like this.

                                    Then we get to V3... and suddenly it's like "Whoops! We need the Breach to have been a huge tragedy. We need it to represent an enormous failure on the part of those responsible for keeping Vale safe, because Cinder's whole plan relies on that. What if we just... ACT like it was a huge tragedy? We'll just have everyone act like that and hope our audience doesn't notice!"

                                    They almost, almost get away with it because we the audience were primed to want to believe that anyway, and only Team CFVY showing up and being absolute combat monsters that made it not so.

                                    (Cinder's entire plan holds up great if you don't look too closely at it, because in the broad strokes "incite a riot, attack Beacon with a huge army of Grimm backed up by White Fang, use distraction to score Maiden powers and/or Relic" is a great plan. But man, if you dive into the details your head starts to hurt.)

I mean, in V5, nobody on Team RWBY even cared enough about him to go "we're super shorthanded, we should call up our old buddy Neptune and see if he can pitch in." Sun didn't even consider, apparently, re-assembling Team SSSN for the minor little matter of "the White Fang are about to do to Haven what they did to Beacon" which you'd think would be of some minor concern to Neptune, Scarlet, and Sage.
