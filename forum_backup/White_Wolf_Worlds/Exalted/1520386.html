      I guess it depends what you wanted out of it?

      As the storytelling chapter that the core book never got? It's good. It's very nicely cohesive, so when it talks about different modes of playing the game, and then moves on to other topics, it makes sure to relate how different things work with or against the different modes and how to deal with that.

      The optional rules are solid, but they're not as extensive as I think a lot of us were hoping to see. Actually having things for Bureaucracy to do is nice and I think helps edge Bureaucracy and Crafts closer, so when characters want to build things that require teams to construct, there's more for leaders and logistics types to actually add instead of such things either being some sort of small bonus for a main crafter, or the core's hand wave it approach. The Craft fixes are fine, but I doubt are going to completely satisfy people that don't like 3e's base crafting premise; but it is easier to make someone that isn't a craft-machine and still does craft stuff on the side. The Naval Combat fixes are probably not a lot of people's first concern, but it definitely addressed the issues people had with the system in a way that should make naval fights a lot more appealing for group play.

      I'm not sure entirely how I feel about the new stuff with Virtues (even if those were largely lifted from Essence) and Limit. Some tweaks to Stunts was unexpected and I'm still contemplating how I feel about them.

      I was disappointed that we didn't get a much more simplified QC system. The book doesn't do anything bad here, I just wanted to see them go farther. Running large fights with multiple Exalted NPCs is still going to be daunting if you want it to While I'm glad they didn't just spend a 1/4 of the book on combat, the complexity of managing NPCs is by far the biggest issue I hear from people that are nervous about running the game. I'm not sure this is going to get someone sitting on the fence off it. For those of us that are older hands at the game, the fixes to the other stuff are going to feel more impactful because we've already factored in Exalted resource tracking in combat as just a part of the game. For people that have the core book and are scared of running a fight where some Wyld Hunt Dragonbloods attack the PCs in a big action set piece? I'm not sure this is going to help them enough.

      So... would I recommend it? Sure. There's nothing that struck me as bad, and a lot of good, even if it's not prioritized on things I might have.

          My Feedback/Review on CoL:-


          Errata Needed:-

          Opening fiction: Is Wandering Teeth meant to be a Solar or Infernal? He is described as both.
          Lots of sections with XX instead of page numbers.


          Disappointment:-

          Nothing about a System for Occult. Was not really expecting it but hope springs eternal.

          Orientalism section is not that useful as in I can't understand why it was there?

          Would have liked to see something on how throw off the 'magical' detection ability of (Sidereal) Wyld Hunt. - I can hand wave it as an ST but as a player I would like to know what steps I can take to counter it specifically.

          The alternate settings were nice but heavily focused on removing different exalts. I would have liked other changes like increasing/decreasing the level of magic to be considered.


          Wait and See:-
          Craft System seems decent. I am not full savvy at Mechanics so will wait and what the more dedicated fans make of it. In all honesty I have slowly gotten to like the base core craft system, it's the Solar charms that need...work.

          Mandate of Heaven : Better then the nothing that was there previously. Again need to see what fans make of it and how it comes up in actual play. I did want more focus on how the bureaucracy ability is used with this system.
          Layout Issue: I would have preferred the actual mandate rules first and then 'lite' rules that hook onto the Core system second.

          Virtues : I consider them interesting, not sure with the seven specified but adding/removing is easy but I do have a question:- Are the 2E virtues still on the table? The initial text implies they have been replaced but later it mentions Conviction. Not sure if this is on purpose or error.
          Unified Experience Point Costs : Again a wait and see. I am a bit surprised at the lower merit & willpower cost as well as the increased charm, evocation & spell costs.



          Good:-
          Section on Styles of Play was excellent.
          The reminder on 'Spending Willpower to resist Social Influence' Facts being introduced by any ability now.
          Great Curse Section is great.
          Legendary Social Goals explained and Expanded.
          The "How Fast Do I Go" Sidebar was needed.

                                  I think the Orientalism piece is a bit of an impossible needle to thread. While what amounts to an extended disclaimer of "We know Exalted is guilty of plenty of Orientalism, but it's bad and we should all aim to not do it," isn't terribly actionable... there's not a lot of actionable you can do with such a limited amount of space. And I would argue there's at least some actionable stuff in there like pointing out that if you're playing in English, use English words over trying to "spice up" your language with borrow words as you're not borrowing from Low Realm if you borrow from Thai to make your English language play sound more special.

                                  If the section was 10 pages, they could have done a lot more actionable material in it, sure, but there's a diminishing return: the more people care about not doing Orientalism, the less they need a 10 page essay about it in a RPG supplement, and people that care less about such things aren't going to have the patience to read through 10 pages of stuff they're not sold on the base premise of. People write full scholarly books about these topics.. there's a point where a section in a ST supplement for Exalted has to just point out that this is a thing, and trust people that care to put in work for themselves to go farther if they feel called to do so.
