    The Broken-Winged Crane

    For quite some time, you've been dreaming of a book that is not in your library when you awake. But these dreams feel less like dreams, and more like memories. Indeed, as you begin to try to copy down your dream-visions into notes, to make sense of them, you get a rushing sense of deja vu, like you are living out your premonition, forging that memory now that you have already experienced.

    As you work, the memories become clearer, and you recall without sleeping what you will have written. Perhaps not perfectly, but how could it not be, when you only can remember what you've read?

    At some point, no less than five days after you began to have these dreams, you will encounter a copy of this book in another's possession, and learn it is titled The Broken-Winged Crane. You may acquire it for yourself, to better finish your own copy or simply to abandon the effort, now that you have your own. Or you may simply borrow it, or steal moments to read from it - these moments are when you are most thoroughly enmeshed in the deja vu, as if THIS is when you forge the memories from which you wrote your own (perhaps not yet complete, and perhaps not 100% accurate) copy.

    In any event, the book, your study of it and your transcription of it, have taught you the underpinnings of reality, and how distorted what SEEMS to be is from what IS. And from these twisted differences can you draw power to shape the warps and cracks therein.

    Shaping Rituals

    * The future's true blasphemies are a source of power, but the difference between a prophecy and a promise is very fine. When you write a future event in your copy of the book (making it more complete from memory of future events), and make public your prophecy of that event which is to come in no more than five days' time, you gain 3 sorcerous motes at the start of each scene, which may only be used on spells and workings shaped specifically to bring this prophecy to pass. If the prophecy fails to come to pass, you gain a Derangement deemed appropriate by the Storyteller as your mind fractures, trying to reconcile the truth you know your words contained with the lies Creation presents to you.

    * The Broken-Winged Crane contains encyclopedic knowledge on the summoning of the denizens of Malfeas. When casting any spell involving demons, you gain an extra sorcerous mote with every Shape Sorcery roll. These motes do not fade until the end of the scene, even if you're forced to abandon the Shaping for extended periods. If Demon of the First Circle is your Control spell, the demons called by it will serve you for five days even if you fail to bind them, even if you don't bother with the proper binding circles.

    Merits

    Walk the Desert on Broken Wings (*-**) You have learned from the tome you write how to traverse to a mystical city across an endless silver desert, inhabited by beings of wonder and terror and wherein things strange and marvelous can be obtained and learned. The trip either way always takes five days from wherever you set out, and is not without its own perils. For one dot, you know the way, but are a stranger without protections you do not bring with you when you arrive. For two dots, you also know the local customs and present yourself in a manner which receives a certain measure of respect from the denizens of that city. While it does not wholly protect you, you are as safe there as if you were in a major city in Creation, and receive no special molestation for your strange, vulnerable, human appearance.

    The Scarlet Bargain (*-*****) You have, in your copy of the book, the instructions for making a bargain for long life, but at the end of your life, you will be required to complete a task of commensurate worth to the extension of your life. The longer your life has been, the greater the task to which you must agree. The first dot doubles your lifespan, restoring youth and vigor immediately and preserving it until the time added by the pact is over...at which point you will feel age creep upon you once again. The second and third dots add this time again, each. The fourth dot preserves your youth and vigor for the entire time, extending your life for an additional century beyond the others before your death is brought about at an agreed-upon time for you to begin your posthumous task. The fifth dot grants true eternal life...but the piper must still be paid his due, and an agreed-upon task that will last the rest of your eternity (or until you are robbed of it by unnatural death) will begin at an agreed-upon time. Usually immediately, but sometimes with a grace period. Whatever the task, be it "marrying a third circle demon" or "pushing a boulder to the peak of Qaf," it will be forever, and will have implications greater than you probably know.

                                    Trying another crack at the prophecy shaping ritual:

                                    * The future's true blasphemies are a source of power, but the difference between a prophecy and a promise is very fine. When you write a future event in your copy of the book (making it more complete from memory of future events), and make public your prophecy of that event which is to come in no more than five days' time, you cast spells without having to first gather the sorcerous motes as long as the spell is directly applicable to bringing to pass your prophecy. You may perform the mote-gathering actions at any later point before the prophecy is completed, and if the prophecy comes to pass, it will turn out that any motes you have not yet gathered were retro-actively accumulated by the turning of events leading up to its fruition. If your prophecy is proven false, however, the consequences are dire. For the next five days after the falsehood of your prophecy is proven, you must gather as many motes as you had not yet paid back before taking even mundane action, or the action fails on a success, and botches on a failure.

                                        Originally posted by Segev View Post
                                        * The Broken-Winged Crane contains encyclopedic knowledge on the summoning of the denizens of Malfeas. When casting any spell involving demons, you gain an extra sorcerous mote with every Shape Sorcery roll. These motes do not fade until the end of the scene, even if you're forced to abandon the Shaping for extended periods. If Demon of the First Circle is your Control spell, the demons called by it will serve you for five days even if you fail to bind them, even if you don't bother with the proper binding circles.
                                        Summoning demons is usually something you do in private, in safety, and with some leisure. While the subsidiary benefit is nice, that should probably just be homebrewed into Demon of the First Circle directly. I'd instead consider something like, you can draw sorcerous motes from a summoned demon within the same range band at a steady pace, or a one-time large burst that will consume the demon entirely.


                                        Originally posted by Segev
                                        * The future's true blasphemies are a source of power, but the difference between a prophecy and a promise is very fine. When you write a future event in your copy of the book (making it more complete from memory of future events), and make public your prophecy of that event which is to come in no more than five days' time, you cast spells without having to first gather the sorcerous motes as long as the spell is directly applicable to bringing to pass your prophecy. You may perform the mote-gathering actions at any later point before the prophecy is completed, and if the prophecy comes to pass, it will turn out that any motes you have not yet gathered were retro-actively accumulated by the turning of events leading up to its fruition. If your prophecy is proven false, however, the consequences are dire. For the next five days after the falsehood of your prophecy is proven, you must gather as many motes as you had not yet paid back before taking even mundane action, or the action fails on a success, and botches on a failure.
                                        Free spells for five days based on a big risk of ruining your next five days is the kind of extreme balancing act that I don't think is great for the system. In particular, it's kinda hard to screw up when you can just spam every spell you know for most of a week.

                                        Maybe instead, when you write down a prophecy scheduled exactly five days into the future, you gain a Defining Intimacy towards bringing the prophecy to pass and shaping actions benefit from that Intimacy as if they were social, gaining the usual +4/-3. Completing the prophecy immediately grants, say, 10 sorcerous motes that persist for (Essence) days and are nonstackable. Failing the prophecy causes you to lose instead of gain Willpower each morning for the next five days.

                                        The persistent issue is how to define what's suitable as a prophecy. "In five days, I will execute this prisoner that I've already captured" or "in five days, I will make another prophecy" aren't exactly ideal game-states. Needs a clear way of communicating that the event should be initially out of your control and significant enough to pose a challenge to at least a heroic mortal.

                                                First off: Hi, I'm new to this forum, and fairly new to Exalted, starting my first third edition game in early January. I did study 2E pretty extensively, but never got the chance to play it except in a single rather short one-shot.

                                                I've been spending the past couple of weeks tinkering with my character, a Dawn caste sorcerer-general, including his custom artifact + sorcerous initiation combo which, upon finding this thread, I decided to share with you guys for help with fleshing it out.

                                                The Demonblade
                                                The idea is that he has this daiklave that he took from a barbarian warlord (outcaste DB or exigent maybe) he defeated in battle. When he attuned to the blade, a voice started whispering into his mind, offering him power. It claimed to be a demon, imprisoned within the daiklave by a solar in the First Age. In fact, the demon went on, that solar was the previous incarnation of my character's exaltation, and he bound him there so that he might seek out the next incarnation of his essence and teach him sorcery, thereby preserving his most valuable tool between reincarnations. Whether this is actually true is up to my ST to decide, I just assume it will be interesting either way.

                                                The daiklave itself is a red jade leaf-shaped double-edged daiklave, inlaid with oricalchum Old Realm runes spelling out its name. The blade is patterned like folded (Damascus/wootz) steel, and when held by an attuned wielder, the pattern seems to shift slowly. The hilt is solid oricalchum set with emeralds, and green-dyed leather is wrapped around the grip. It does not exhibit any obvious outward signs of being possessed.

                                                Shaping Ritual
                                                The demon thirsts eternally, and drinks eagerly whenever the daiklave draws blood. Indeed, blood staining the blade seems to quickly seep into it and vanish. When the sorcerer makes a decisive attack with the daiklave to an opponent that bleeds, he gains sorcerous motes equal to the health levels inflicted as damage. Withering attacks against battlegroups instead grant one sorcerous mote for every three points of post-soak damage. Sorcerous motes granted by this ritual last until the end of the scene and are technically stored in the daiklave rather than the sorcerer himself. As such, they may only be used if the sorcerer still has the blade within reach (it does not need to be unsheathed). Only up to (Sorcerer’s Essence + Permanent Willpower) sorcerous motes may be stored in the blade. When the sword is holding any sorcerous motes in this way, flames seem to dance inside the emeralds adorning the hilt.

                                                I didn't create any custom merits yet (being new to the system, homebrewing seems kind of intimidating) but I did snag The Burning Name, mainly because being able to throw fire around at will seems awfully useful and the kind of thing a sorcerer should be able to do and a demon able to teach.

                                                The things I need help with:
                                                • The identity and name of the demon, name of the daiklave (note that it was created before the demon was bound to it)
                                                • Does the shaping ritual I wrote seem balanced?
                                                • More shaping rituals
                                                • Associated custom merits
                                                • Evocations for the daiklave (these should probably go in a separate thread, which I'll gladly set up with some more info if anyone expresses interest in this part)
