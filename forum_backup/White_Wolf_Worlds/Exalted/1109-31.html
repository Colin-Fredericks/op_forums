                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                            I understand that games need balancing, and if they say Lunars will not have the ability to use evocations while DBT because of balancing issues - fine. What I don't like is the sell that it give a Lunar character more flexibility, when demonstrably all it does is place limits on what Lunars can do.
                            Try not to think of it in IC terms. It really has more to do with player build options than character "what can I do this turn" options."

                            It's more like, DBT was always a no-brainer for Lunars who wanted to be combat-savvy. Which was in some ways a real shame, because Lunars should be fighting in many forms, not just that one. But practically, with DBT being exempt from the shit that was form-lock, DBT was your go-to fight form unless you had a giant spirit shape and even then, because DBT isn't going to show up on a street in quite the same way that a yeddim might.

                            So the game is basically telling you "if you want to be a fight Lunar, you need DBT" (in the same way that the game said "if you want to play an Abyssal, you have to do it this precise way or you get punished"). Which led to basically every person who was going to play a Lunar needing to think about whether or not to invest XP in that Knack and its line, and probably needing to come up with a good reason for "why shouldn't I take this even if I'm a socialite, because at some point I'm going to have to fight and I don't want to suck at it!"

                            Now the system could have allowed this to be a real choice. It didn't. Form-lock meant you can't take a combative animal form because if you spend too much Essence, that's gone. The awful tattoos meant you were closed off from some of the better buff effects in the game (sorcery). You can still do Martial Arts, sure, but Martial Arts + DBT persistent bonuses is still better than Martial Arts alone.

                            Now going forward, we know that Evocations are a thing, that they offer some degree of doing stuff outside your normal splat thematics. We can also assume DBT will remain because it's an iconic Lunar power, and was historically their best way to fight, or at least a way to fight pretty damn well for relatively minimal XP investment. Paired with the changes to the system as Aquillion pointed out, you go from one One True Build problem to another if Lunars both get a form that gives persistent dice-additions and also get access to Evocation effects.

                            At the same time, it's a character-building-flavor thing. Some people want to play Lunars who do shapeshift, but more for utility than combat and don't want to have to go Crinos to fight effectively. Now they have that option by focusing on Evocations instead of the DBT tree, and not feeling behind the ones who go DBT. But it's clear that if DBT and Evocations completely play together perfectly, you've traded last edition's One True Build problem for a new one.

                            Iit is a balance thing. But I don't agree that it reduces flexibility. By that I mean it does not reduce overall flexibility - just flexibility on a turn by turn basis which frankly I am OK with. You're not necessarily behind anyone if you invest in both DBT and Evocations, assuming DBT isn't 5th down in a Charm tree or something. If anything you've expanded your toolkit to meet more challenges: fighting with DBT in certain situations may cause a huge panic that simply manifesting Evocations would not, but sometimes you also just need to be a fuzzy chainsaw who doesn't need tools to play the chunky salsa game as well as anyone.

                            Meh? I don't know why people think hard numbers were ever necessary.
