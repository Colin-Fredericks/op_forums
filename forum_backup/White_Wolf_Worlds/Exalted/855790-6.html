                            I think that just isn't what the material proposes Martial Arts are about.

                            I stand by what I initially said about it. Not every coded way of fighting is a Martial Art Ability (I write this in all caps when I'm referring to the specific game mechanic construct). If they were we wouldn't have the general combat Abilities. You wouldn't have Melee or Thrown, you would have Lookshyan Spear Fighting and Harborhead Slingshotting as discrete Abilities. That is not the case.

                            I don't believe there is any esoteric value to "I'm a snake, just better!". There is very little cultural potential. Martial Arts are are a sum of technique, culture, metaphysics (shallow and corny as some may be) tradition and shared experience. I see them as a "human" endeavor.

                            There are 2 types of Martial Arts. Regular and Sidereal. For a Regular Martial Art, it should be something that can be learned and used by any "regular mortal" and accessible to any Exalt splat without requiring any other "magic".

                            So if you need shape shifting to use a martial art, it is not a Martial Art Ability. It is something else. I can understand some external (to the MA) power having a particular synergy with a MA, but that should come from outside the MA, like DB Anima enhancing a Immaculate Style. Not the Immaculate Style enhancing the DB Anima.

                            Solars (roids?) are the undisputed masters of Abilities. Theirs, and Sidereal's, splat description specifically calls them out as Masters of Martial Arts.

                            I won't speak for Sidereal Martial Arts because we know close to nothing of their 3e iteration, and 3e is what I'm interested in discussing.

                            Powers, or fighting techniques, that depend on alien physiology to even have use outside of Charms are not what I, or the book seems to, consider Martial Arts.
