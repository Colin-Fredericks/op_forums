            Originally posted by Epitome View Post

            No worries, everyone is making good points.

            About the 5-6 combatants trumping the one Exalt, I see the point you and others are making that they should basically be a battle group or trivial opponents if they're not significant threats. I guess I just need to get used to the notion that the Combat Engine works from the assumption that every combatant is meaningful until the ST makes them a trivial opponent by fiat.

            On battle groups, letting battle groups use Charms is an interesting idea, I'll probably try it out.
            The numbers thing is a pretty constant factor across all RPGs-- it's REALLY hard to balance a single boss against a group. If you inflate their defense too much it becomes frustrating; if you boost their offense too much you wind up getting, like, one turn where you annhiliate a single PC, then you get beaten down in short order. (And that only gets worse in a system like Exalted where one character can be orders of magnitude better at fighting than another). The problem isn't numbers, it's action economy, and you can't really solve that without giving bosses more actions than any individual player can muster.

            Mutants and Masterminds was mentioned earlier. I've actually used it to run Exalted, and it works... fairly well*, though you lose a lot of the non-combat depth that makes Exalted so fun. But even then, with a group that's pretty heterogeneous in terms of combat power, I have to give bosses extra turns if the party gets much over 3 characters.

            If you don't want to go down that route, the best you can do is to pack a lot of cheap reflexive abilities. Miss me in melee? I counterattack and throw you down the street! Start pulling a bow? I get to toss a knife at the string! Flank me? I dodge and leave the two of you swinging at each other!

            *As long as everyone goes gonzo on their powers-- M&M is pretty bad at distinguishing, say, two martial artists. Unless one of them is using kung-fu magic to have his sword fly around the room and the other is transforming into a bear.

                Eh. That's what stunting is for.

                And I'd make the PCs base their powers on the published Charms, including prereqs, mapped as faithfully as possible (which won't be very faithfully in M&M -- but PL is so easy to balance and design encounters around, it doesn't really matter if the PCs do go gonzo).

                To simplfy M&M somewhat:
                It's a system designed for players to invent their own super heroes and hand craft their powers from scratch.
                All characters have a Power Level which determines the strength of their attacks and defences.
                Robin is PL 8, Nightwing is PL 10, Batman is PL 12 and Superman is (the extreme upper boarder of) PL 15.
                When Robin punches someone, it's a PL 8 attack. Batman's punches are PL 12. Batman's Batrangs are also PL 12. The machine guns on the Batmobile are ALSO PL 12. Whether Superman is punching you, using his ice breath or his heat vision, all his attacks are PL 15.
                This makes designing encounters a breeze.

                In play, you don't say "Batman punches you for PL 12, then Aquaman also punches you for PL 12, then the Flash punches you for PL 12". That's like saying "I Wither him to gain some Initative".

                The way you differentiate Snake Style from Tiger Style is Stunting -- because in M&M you'll be making PL 14 Unarmed attacks.

                            Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                            Eh. That's what stunting is for.

                            And I'd make the PCs base their powers on the published Charms, including prereqs, mapped as faithfully as possible (which won't be very faithfully in M&M -- but PL is so easy to balance and design encounters around, it doesn't really matter if the PCs do go gonzo).
                            Still, Stunting gets old if there's not enough crunch (relative to the rest of the system, at least) to back it up. M&M can do... fairly well, but obviously not as well as an absurdly crunchy game like Exalted.

                            For reference, the "Exalts and Excellencies" character I played in that campaign had these "charms"
                            Eye of a Thousand Battles Perception Affliction 5 (Vulnerable and Impaired, resisted and overcome by Fighting), double limited degree, limited: only against you, Subtle, Insideous
                            Iron Whirlwind Multiattack on Damage 8; Variable Descriptor 1: Any weapon/unarmed, Subtle
                            Celestial Fencing Technique Feature 3: No attack roll on close attack, Penetrating 7, Incurable; Variable Descriptor 1: Any weapon/unarmed
                            Body-Annihilating Strike Close Affliction 8 (impaired, disabled, incapacitated, resisted and overcome by Fortitude), Subtle
                            Flawless General Technique luck control 2 (spend points on allies, force rerolls); 4 luck; subtle; sense-dependant (hearing)
                            Sevenfold Step Teleport 3 (accurate, limited: must move through intervening space)
                            Marathon Step Technique Speed 2, Activation (move)
                            Ox Body Technique Healing 1 (Reaction, Effortless, Limited to Self, Limited to degree of damage dealt by the last attack)
                            Ancient Warlord's Knowledge Danger Sense; Precognition, Limited to Martial matters, DC 13 Exp: War check required
                            So, like, some good stuff, some unique stuff, but another melee fighter is probably going to have a lot of similar powers. It gets worse the farther you go from combat.

                            To simplfy M&M somewhat...
                            Eh... sort of?

                            M&M is a d20 game with a build-your-own-superpowers structure. There are discreet list of Effects and Modifiers, which you combine to make unique abilities. A lightsaber, a mind-melting psychic attack, and a missile might all be built off the Damage effect, but with different Modifiers to make them behave differently. It is not built around attrition in the slightest, nor does it stop to think about what it means that I can, say, get myself the ability to turn dirt into gold for 2 power points (out of 150 points for a standard starting character). There's a certain amount of player buy-in required to keep from obliterating the setting or breaking the custom power system wide open, but the latter is tempered by the fact that it's usually really obvious (to everyone) when you're doing something Wrong.

                            It achieves balance through Power Levels, which impose hard caps on all of your numbers. On skills, the limit is high enough that it's not worth worrying about; for combat stuff, the neat thing is that the limits are in the form of sums. Your attack roll plus your damage can't exceed this. If you want to be hard to hurt, you're forced to be easy to hit. Stuff like that. That's how you can get Batman and Superman in the same party (or at least Robin and Superboy)-- Batman makes up for power with accuracy and evasiveness. (In theory, at least-- in practice, if you trade off too far in any direction you run into math problems, so it's best for Batman to pack some C4 and high-PL nerve strikes just in case). If you've seen shows like Justice League Unlimited or Young Justice, it winds up being exactly that sort of paradigm, where Wonder Woman flies around punching tanks while Green Arrow shoots explosive arrows that are equally effective, and then they both get blasted across the room by the same burst of lightning.

                            Putting together powers takes some getting used to, and the damage system is a little odd-- the victim rolls a save instead of the attacker rolling damage-- but it plays fast, smooth, and is gloriously fun.

                                                        The main thing I'm starting to consider for house rules is some tweaks to battle groups.

                                                        The area attack is potentially a bit of a problem. There's often not much variation in defence values so the attack tends to either flounder ineffectually like what a lot of people seem to experience or roll really well and be capable of putting the entire circle on the backfoot (barring the use of charms that can be activated after the roll has already taken place).

                                                        I think my preference would be to borrow from the rules for Environmental Hazards because in practice I've found that if the players are rolling vs. a fixed difficulty it's more likely that a couple of them will fail despite having good dice pools on paper. There's some disadvantages to the fix, most notably the more I smooth out the irregularities the harder it becomes harder to translate the individual profile into a battle group profile.
