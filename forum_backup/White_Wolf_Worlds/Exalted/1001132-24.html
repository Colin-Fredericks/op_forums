                        Originally posted by Jairain View Post
                        Is there a reason we could've just make human sacrifice the method of execution for capital punishment? I doubt it's any cooler than any other form of execution. We could probably even stipulate some kind of humane execution method.
                        Considering how the Immaculate Faith finds the idea of human sacrifice abhorrent, it would probably be a bad idea.

                        If it got out that Kasai was using capital executions as sacrifices to gods, he would almost certainly be declared Anathema. If a Wyld Hunt didn't come a-knockin', a lynch mob would certainly take him down.

                        The Hill-Tribes (and other cultures that practice human sacrifice) can 'get away with it' specifically because they aren't Immaculate, and live a goodly distance away from Immaculates, on top of not practicing human sacrifice openly, or even all that often.

                        Generally, capital punishment in the Hundred Kingdoms depends on what social class the perpetrator belongs to.

                        Commoners will be hanged, generally speaking, using either the suspension or short-drop method as opposed to the long-drop method (WARNING: pictures of people being hanged) [this means the condemned will expire from strangulation as opposed to a broken neck], almost-always in public. Sometimes, but only rarely, commoners will be decapitated instead of hanged, but this is viewed as being more merciful than hanging, which is why it is far less common.

                        Nobles, on the other hand, will almost always be decapitated in the rare case they get convicted of a capital offense, usually privately. A noble would have to fuck up bad in order to be executed publicly, like "grand treason' fuck up. Anyways, many if not most nobles will commit suicide if they get word that they are going to be executed, as a private suicide is less painful, faster, and less embarrassing than an execution. Usually, a retainer will 'assist' them by decapitating the suicidal liegelord, and in fact many Magistrates will, effectively, execute people by sending an Oathsworn retainer to their place of residence, in a form of "the writing on the wall".

                        While the Hundred Kingdoms are based on feudal Japan, it doesn't have the same culture of suicide. Nobles committing suicide isn't Seppuku per se, but a means of avoiding unnecessary pain and embarrassment. If Kasai fucked up, pissed off the Lord Iysen, or dishonored himself, he wouldn't be ordered to commit suicide, for example.

                        As the Marcher-Lord of Jianghu, Kasai is functionally 'above the law', in a sense. In Jianghu, he is the law, and while people can bring cases against him...... he controls the Magistrate, and people would basically have to go to his boss, Lord Iysen, to have any real chance of getting a case heard. And, even then, there are functionally very few things he could actually get convicted for. He would have to basically stroll through town, cutting people down while singing "I'm a Dentist" and giggling to himself before he could get slapped with a murder charge. Rape is another one. In other domains, he has less 'immunity' to conviction, but he is still a Lord (Duke-equivalent), and most charges against him will likely be dropped or settled out of court.

                        Bandits, as people effectively outside the law (which is where the term 'outlaw' comes from) are, well, outside the protection of the law. In the Hundred Kingdoms, even the rudest of serfs is theoretically protected by the laws of the land, from murder and thievery and stuff like that. Bandits are not.

                        Bandits in general are assumed to be murderers. rapists, thieves and scum like that, and hanging is the traditional method of dealing with them. Most bandits ultimately end up being the aforementioned scum, due to lack of legal protection (and associated ability to work within the law), and while many/most bandits are deserted soldiers, a decent portion of them are 'regular" people fleeing from the law. If Papa gets charged with the murder of some guy in an accidental drunken taphouse brawl, he very well might flee the retainers of the Magistrate and go live in the woods for a while. Since he can't exactly work on the farm anymore (and while his family might try to support him as much as they can, illegal as that is), he overwhelmingly-likely is going to have to steal in order to survive.

                        That is why Kasai offering a blanket pardon in return for military service is such a goddamn good deal. Overwhelmingly-most, close to all, bandits will end up dying either from wounds, sickness, or at the end of a rope. What he is offering is, basically, a new beginning.

The few trees and boulders that are in the way are used to mark the sides of the track, or to fill in low spots and mud-holes. You aren’t stupid), they eventually warm up to your presence. After a few jokes and pulls on a bottle of liquor and a smoldering pipe, they all-but forget you are there, at least until the work-gang goes on break and new workers come up. Two: You have damn good flank screens. You haven’t heard a peep about any bandits, much less word of an ambush, have you?”

Men not afraid of getting attacked march faster, and in this case, work harder. I, personally, prefer to have a lot of lighter troops, so they can serve as scouting-and-screening in-depth. This evening, you are a little over 12 miles, with not a single hide nor hair of a bandit yet.

While you are certainly glad to have not been ambushed yet, this yet-unbroken string of good, even great, luck has to have an end. After all, doesn’t your book on philosophy say, “All things must end”?

                                                            Hell, you already pray to Luna, what is another two Incarnae? You are already a piss-poor Immaculate, why not add to the pile….. at this rate, you are going to be a first-class heretic before the year is out.

                                                            I apologize for the misdirection, but I had to make the meeting ‘look real’ in front of the other bandit-captains. Now that you know battle is quite-possibly imminent, you are anxious and unsettled. Images of failure and loss flicker through your mind, and you push away the noodle-soup. Sergeant Major Oxblood gets shit done, apparently), you can’t help but notice that the same emotions are running rampant among the soldiers, the serf-militiamen most of all. Walking over, leaning heavily on your spear, you see one of the regiments of light cavalry dismounting. Their subofficer walks over to you, backsword swinging from his belt.

Unfolding it and passing it to you, you see that there is a rough sketch of the surrounding terrain, centered on Dun Nashuon with hills, forests and other features marked out.

If you wanted to make an educated guess, you would say those are ventilation-shafts from when the barrows under the hillfort were constructed. Narrow, certainly, but they might be wide enough for a man to crawl down….. that is, if you wanted to send a man down deep into the earth, straight to the bosom of angry ancestor-ghosts.

                                                            Sounding the general-alarm.

                                                            Cursing, you grab Moonlight Over Water from its rack and hurriedly leave the pavilion, heading towards the sound of the horn. Soldiers rush to and fro around you, and at some points, have to wait for the men to clear a path between the tents and supply depots. Long before you reach the embankment, you hear the Sergeant’s parade-voice loud and clear over the clatter of weapons and the patter of footfalls. From what you can make out, he is yelling for people to “stop rubbernecking and get back to their posts.” Spurred on, you pick up the pace as much as you are able.
                                                            When you finally reach the eastern embankment, you are greeted with the sight of not just your Men-at-Arms standing in formation, ready for battle, but several dozens of militiamen wandering about, trying to catch a glimpse of something outside the palisade. You limp forward and arduously climb up onto the wallwalk, peering between the uprights.

                                                            Marching out from the treeline are several blocks of men, standing in formation, with weapons at the ready. As you watch, three regiments of spearmen emerge first, locked into a shieldwall, followed by two groups of archers in skirmish formation. For Men-at-Arms, they appear to be well-disciplined, much less for bandits!

                                                            A path opens up between the formations, and Masanori rides out. A distance in front of the regiments, he dismounts and waits.

                                                            You turn to the Sergeant, who nods and points at several of the halberdiers. Quickly, they form up around you, and led by Sergeant Oxblood, you push open the gate and make your way outside the fortress.

                                                            As you walk up to Masanori, he goes down on one knee, and with a clatter, so does the rest of the soldiers behind him. Once you get closer, he looks up, and even from this distance, you can see his face pale once he sees your limp.

                                                            “My Lord, here I am, as promised, with my regiments……..please tell me I didn’t actually hurt you.”

                                                            “No, no, this was from before, have no fear.”

                                                            You cast your gaze over the gathered soldiers, then look back at Masanori, “So….. you pulled through. How did you get out of the fortress, might I ask?”

                                                            He grins, “Believe me, that is a story worth telling…..inside the encampment. I have plenty of other intelligence you will find interesting as well.”

                                                            “Very well. Sergeant, find room for these additional soldiers. If you have to expand the palisade, go ahead.”

                                                            On your walk back to your pavilion, word evidently travels fast, and most of your Oathsworn officers and retinue are already there, with more filing in behind you. Once they are all settled, you gesture to Masanori,

                                                            “ Oathsworn officers and vassals, I would like to introduce you to Masanori …… ah, how would you prefer to be named?”

                                                            He shrugs, “ I have no clan-name as of yet, so Masanori will be sufficient, if it please you.”

                                                            “Masanori it is. For those who don’t know, Masanori was the leader of the largest and most active bandit-group in Jianghu, which is currently encamped in the fortress of Dun Nashuon. As I have said before, I met with Masanori a little over a week ago, and we came to an agreement. In return for his desertion of the bandit-group, he and whomever he could persuade to join us would receive a full pardon for any and all crimes, as well as the standard land-grant, in return for five years of military service. Now then, just how many soldiers did you manage to bring with you?”

                                                            “About three hundred, my Lord Jianghu. Most of them are from the bandit-crew that followed me, as I found them the most skilled, not to mention the most trustworthy, for what my word is worth. Most of them are deserted soldiers from other domains, so they are already well-drilled and equipped. Three regiments of spears and two of longbows, I believe.”

                                                            One of your vassals speaks out, “Just how did you manage to leave the fortress unnoticed?”

                                                            Masanori grins, “I did not, if you want to be particular. The soldiers were known to be on a “flanking maneuver’ by my former bandit-captains, and so their absence was expected. Myself, on the other hand……” He points in the direction of the fortress, illuminated by the rays of the setting sun. “You can probably hear the screams from here if you listen closely.”

                                                            You frown, “What exactly do you mean, Masanori?”

                                                            “I ….. well, took the liberty of sabotaging as much of the defense as much as possible, my Lord, before I escaped.”

                                                            The assembled officers erupt into noise, and you wait for them to calm before you question Masanori.

                                                            “Care to go into detail?”

                                                            “Of course, my Lord.” He pulls out several pieces of paper from the inside of his sash and passes them over to you.

                                                            “This map is a detailed drawing of the defenses of Dun Nashuon; where the walls are ruined, where they are strong, the internal details of the encampment, so on and so forth.”

                                                            “This list details the forces remaining inside Dun Nashuon; their makeup, their morale, their commanders and their dispositions.” He points at several names on the list, “ You can ignore these commanders, I killed them in my escape.”

                                                            “And this list details the amount of materiel stockpiled in Dun Nashuon. Days of food stored, the number of arrows and projectiles stockpiled, etc.”

                                                            The last list is criminally short. “Excellent work, Masanori, but I must ask …. Why is there so little food stored in Dun Nashuon?”

                                                            He shrugs, “ By-and-large, we were dependent on the food stores we could extort from the farms along the border of the Akibara. It is the end of summer, and stores were winding down. I was at the point of ordering men into the woods to forage and hunt, but I recalled them all when you told me you were coming.”

                                                            Confused, ‘Why is that?”

                                                            “I wanted to burn through whatever food stores I had left, my Lord. While Dun Nashuon is a ruin, it is a defensible ruin, and I assumed you would want a siege over quickly. Based on how quickly we used the food stored in the souterrains, there can’t be much more than 5 days of food stored in the fortress.”

                                                            You nod, and Sergeant Oxblood pipes up, “How about water? You can go a week or more without food, but only a couple of days without water. Does the fortress have its own water supply, or did you have to use the river?”

                                                            “The fortress had several wells on the surface level, but they either went dry or were filled long ago. I spent two weeks having my troops excavate one, to no success. 30 feet of rubble, at least. We were forced to crack open the vault under the stone tower, but that cistern was not large enough for our purposes. Eventually, we found a larger well, in the undercaverns.”

                                                            This last bit, when translated to the Hill-Tribe warleaders, causes them to react in horror and outrage.

                                                            “<That sounds like the sacred well! Tell me this ignorant flatlander didn’t contaminate the sacred well!>”

                                                            When you relay this info to Masanori, he winces. “You know, in hindsight, I regret ordering that thing opened. There were many artifacts; weapons, jewelry, things like that in there, and much of it was ….. uh…..looted, when the well was in use. In addition, there are several passages down there, and I lost several men who went wandering down there. Eventually, I ordered the passages sealed up, but I am almost certain I didn’t get all of them. Will that be a problem?”

                                                            “Almost certainly”, you reply with a sigh, “By removing the artifacts, you desecrated the well, and the men who wandered through the catacombs almost-certainly disturbed the dead… “<Will the desecration of the well and tombs be a problem, at least regarding this attack?>”

                                                            “<Hopefully not. If anything, removing the ones who disturbed the sacred site will mollify the ancestor-ghosts. If we can, however, take some of the interlopers alive…..>”

                                                            You shudder and turn away. The Tribes are terrifying when brought to wrath.

                                                            “Back on topic.” You glance at the list detailing troop numbers.” So, Masanori, just how many fighters do you have inside the fortress?”

                                                            “About 1200, my Lord, divided into several different and often-infighting gangs. They were loyal, for a given sense, to me when I was present, but now that I am gone, they will be widely suspicious of each other. They don’t ‘play well’ together, and when I led them, I often had to keep them wide apart from each other.”

                                                            You turn to your retainers and officers, “And how many men do we have here?”

                                                            Sergeant Oxblood speaks up, “ 500 Men-at-Arms, plus the 300 sir Masanori brought over, so 800. 500 miltiamen, plus 500 or so Hill-Tribe warriors. In total ……… 1800, not counting the Oathsworn officers and retinue. Close to 1900 fighters, all told, and when the two regiments of militia under sir Shin Takeshi gets here, we will be close to 2000.”

                                                            Back to Masanori, “Would any of the fighters left in the fortress take me up on my offer?”

                                                            He shrugs, “Almost certainly, but …… if I may speak plainly, my Lord, I would not trust them. The men who joined me did so because they want ‘out’ of the bandit-life. Men who are interested in saving their own skins will say anything, but they will also do anything. The men who joined me are disciplined; the men left in the fortress are ……not. More like wild dogs than men.”
                                                            “ Hmmm….. How much food and materiel do we have stockpiled here?”

                                                            One of your retinue speaks up, reading from a list, “In dried goods, we have about 5 days of food. If you dispatch the Tribes to hunt and forage, that number can be safely increased to …. Ah…… probably a week at least, ten days if we skimp. Plus, sir Shin Takeshi should only be a few days behind, with the materiel from the rest of Jianghu.”

                                                            You sit down in your camp-chair, pondering the best course of action.

                                                            “Alright, so by the looks of things, we have two main courses of action.”

                                                            “One”, you hold up a finger, “We can assault the fortress. We have the advantage of numbers, if only slightly, and the benefits of a unified and central command. If we strike while they are disjointed and confused, we might just be able to seize the day. That will, however, take a toll in blood and lives.”

                                                            “Two”, holding up another finger, “We lay siege to the fortress. We have the advantage of food supply on our hands, and can outlast them if need be. Plus, we have more food and more soldiers on the way. On the other hand……. Just how long do we have before the army has to leave, in order to make ready for the harvest?”

                                                            “ Truthfully, Lord Qianke, there is about two weeks left before the summer harvest actually begins, and that will be pushing it. If we lay siege to the fortress, we very well might not have time to effectively get the troops back in the fields before the harvest starts, and that is if the fortress capitulates in 5 days. Anything longer and the harvest-yield will start to suffer.”

                                                            You think about what the best course of action to take is:
                                                            1. Meet with the current commanders of the fortress, and make the same offer you made to Masanori. If you can get more fighters to change sides….
                                                            2. Prepare equipment for an assault: mantlets, a ram, ladders, etc
                                                              1. Order the troops to attack without you. You are in no shape to charge up a hill, much less fight! (‘standard’ morale)
                                                              2. Lead your army into battle. You wouldn’t order someone to do something you wouldn’t do! (units close to Kasai will receive a hefty morale boost)
                                                            3. Prepare equipment for a siege: mantlets, sanitary facilities for the encampment, more advanced defenses, so on and so forth.
                                                            4. Ask the Hill-Tribes if they would be willing to sneak into the underbelly of the fortress through the ventilation-shafts your scouts found. Since the ghosts are kin to the Tribes, they might be willing to forgive a trespass, especially if the desecrators are removed, and putting a couple dozen angry Tribesmen up the enemies ass will cause a lot of damage.
                                                            5. Something else? (write in)
