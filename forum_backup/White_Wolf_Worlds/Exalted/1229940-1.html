    Originally posted by Blaque View Post
    To add to the pile I guess, I generally use the base system (slots and all) since I actually do like the goals it has of incentivizing a certain approach to crafting and encouraging players to solve problems by making things along the way. I also use the same samples for basic projects.

    That being said, I think a big issue with Craft is more tied with Charms than with the mechanics. I think that there's for instance, more than enough points in normal games to get enough slots that there's just way too mcuh on that,a dn the Craft Charms actually in a way feel like they "skip" the incentives created by the base system.
    I do somewhat agree. I think the Craft system could be a bit simpler (I don't really see the point of silver XP, for example, I understand gating artefacts but why gate swords? And like Lioness I don't find major slots add anything; they've literally never come up), but generally, while at first I thought the Craft system was the issue, I realised it's 90% the charms.

    There's a lot of charms that do things that really aren't useful (mostly about slots), and the Crafting XP charms are mostly pretty poor until you get to the last one, which is ridiculously good.

    We were actually talking about the Craft system the other day at the end of a game, and one player said he hated it. I said that, despite its failings, it did do the one main thing it was designed to do well; that is, stop people churning out artefacts like a production line.
    He pointed out that my character has already made a level 2 artefact and a level 3 artefact, only about 4 games after I joined.
    This isn't due to the base system, though, it's my 13 craft charms. Which let me churn out artefacts if I have the materials, as well as raising a whole bunch of Craft abilities... but with a lot of book-keeping.
    When I was running the game, I eventually just told the Twilight player to not bother counting his Craft XP anymore, since it went up faster than he spent it, due to charms.

    This is not to say that the Craft system is perfect (I remember the first session I ran of 3rd ed, where I had to explain to the player of the 250xp Twilight with 7 craft abilities at 5 that he couldn't make himself a sword until he'd jumped through several hoops), but I suspect that it would work better with more even, better thought-out charms.

    On that note, has anyone tried a Dragonblood Crafter?

                    Really, the two biggest issues in the Craft system itself are the objectives and the gating of Major projects behind Basic projects.

                    The objective list encourages gaming the system. Crafters are incentivized to all take an Intimacy of "I like making things" because then they automatically score one objective on all projects, with almost no downside. They are incentivized to sell tons of simple things to score a second. This incentive structure pushes Craft heavy characters towards sameness.

                    The gating of Major projects behind Basic projects just rubs some people the wrong way, partly because the system is telling people to solve problems with trinkets when they would prefer to use something more substantial and partly because the system is telling you that every problem you solve without using Craft is a missed opportunity to garner craft points.

                    The issue with the Solar Craft Charms is that those Charms are balanced in a vacuum. They don't need to provide competitive options because they rarely compete against anything. They can deliver unexciting effects at various points because there isn't anything exciting to take.

                            I really think the issue is how Basic and Major projects are defined more than the objectives or the gating.

                            If Basic, Major, and mundane Superior projects were all defined by their level of impact on the game state (and be more consistent about batching) rather than simulating crafting effort - including a minimum level of impact for Basic projects - those problems would go away.

                            A Basic project is one that will only have a temporary effect on the game for a single or small group of characters, but has to be significant enough to matter to the outcome of at least a scene. Your Melee specialist Dawn had their Diaklave stolen from them, and you make them a sword to use until they get it back is Basic rather than Major because it's not about the sword, but the impact.

                            Major projects permanently change things for a single character/small group, or temporarily change things for a large group.

                            Mundane Superior projects permanently change things for large groups, or temporarily change things that could impact multiple nations.

                            This gets rid of "farming" small items with an Intimacy because they still need to impact the scene to matter enough to qualify even as Basic. Sameness is decreased because, again, it has to matter to what's going on, so it can only be as same-y as the scenes allow. It also reduces the "My swords smith can't do anything because swords are always Major." thing because swords can be Basic as long as the impact is small.

                            I think the objectives could use a revisit, but they're not ruining the system.
