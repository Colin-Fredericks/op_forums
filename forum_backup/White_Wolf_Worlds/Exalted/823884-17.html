    Shrieking Wraith's Requiem (Soulsteel Powerbow, Artifact ***)

    Forged by the Mask of Winters as a prize for a loyal deathknight, he abandoned his plans for the bow when a Solar offered him her own powerbow in trade. Genuinely impressed by the honesty and generosity inherent in the naive gesture, the Mask of Winters offered her Shrieking Wraith's Requiem as a gift in exchange, forming a tiny but existent spark of cooperative goodwill within the heart of the Deathlord. Now, the Night Caste named Hua Li wields Requiem, unaware of the power that slumbers within.

    Shrieking Wraith's Requiem is a recurve powerbow of soulsteel, with a grip wrapped in hide peeled from Juggernaut himself and a string of tightly-coiled human hair, treated with a wraith's Essence for strength. The risers are sculpted into gleaming soulsteel skulls, while the limbs are carved into elaborate bone motifs. When fired, the bow sometimes seems to scream softly, the skulls' mouths opening and closing with the motion of the bow.

    Evocations of Shrieking Wraith's Requiem

    A Solar or Abyssal Exalt who attunes to Shrieking Wraith's Requiem for five motes gains one dot in the Whispers Merit for free. Additionally, while attuned, the user can increase their Whispers rating by spending experience points. All Whispers gained in this manner, including the initial free dot, are lost if attunement lapses, but return if the user re-attunes to the bow.

    Shriek of the Void
    Cost: 1m/die or success; Mins: Essence 1
    Type: Supplemental
    Keywords: Dual
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisites: None

    When infused with Essence, Requiem literally screams as she releases a nocked arrow, sending the missile on its way trailed by spiraling streamers of necrotic Essence. On a withering attack against a living target, the Exalt spends up to her (Whispers) rating in motes before making the attack. If the attack hits, each mote spent adds a single die to the post-soak damage of the attack. This strike has the potential to increase minimum damage (see Ex3, p. 184).


    Requiem for the Damned
    Cost: 5m; Mins: Essence 1
    Type: Simple
    Keywords: None
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisites: Shriek of the Void

    By charging Requiem with her own Essence, the wielder may temporarily awaken the spirits of those dead trapped within the bow, calling them forth into a phantasmal dirge. The skulls on the bow literally move and sing, a blasphemous glossolalia of dozens, if not hundreds, of spectral voices. This Evocation is a Performance-based Inspire action, using the wielder's (Charisma + Performance) and adding her (Whispers) as automatic successes against the Resolve of her audience. If successful, the group is struck with either extreme sadness and lassitude or fear, at the Storyteller's discretion, depending on the context of the scene. This Evocation functions equally well on the living or the mindful dead, though the dead almost always experience depression rather than terror. It generally has no effect on mindless undead. If used in conjunction with the Dawn Caste anima power, however, even mindless undead can know fear.


    Clutching Wraith's Embrace
    Cost: 5m (+1W); Mins: Essence 2
    Type:Supplemental
    Keywords: Decisive-only
    Duration: Instant
    Prerequisites: Requiem for the Damned

    Those slain by Shrieking Wraith's Requiem may know no peace; only an eternity of sorrowful wandering in the inhospitable wastes of the Underworld. This Evocation supplements a decisive attack. If the target is slain by this attack, at the end of the scene his hun soul will rise as an unquiet ghost, while his po will stir fitfully in his corpse. At the Storyteller's discretion, the corpse is more likely to become a hungry ghost, while the hun spirit WILL become a ghost. If magic such as the Zenith's anima power is used on the corpse before the end of the scene, this Evocation's effect is negated. If used against a battle group, this Evocation costs an extra Willpower to activate, but it can animate the entire battle group.

        I'm going to give a very quick review of your two artifacts from DOOM.
        They're overpowered and break the rules of the system.
        A very important part of game balance, especially with 3E, is that each charm tree plays very strong to it's niche.
        This is very much intentional.
        By making each charm tree very strong at it's intended niche, no charm tree is useless or theoretically better than the others.
        This is the very reason why card games like Magic: The Gathering have survived for over twenty years, with the Legacy metagame supporting over twenty to thirty different archetypes and decks.
        For a more modern example, DOTA 2 is also like this where in a very large number of heroes are picked every TI because they are all so niche that they can co-exist.

        Niches and specialization breed diversity in gameplay and builds.

        Let's examine this in the context of Exalted 3E combat abilities:

        VERY LONG WALL OF TEXT AHEAD ON GAME AND MECHANIC DESIGN

        Archery:
        The majority of the cascades focus on combat at short and greater range, enhancing combat at medium range, with a very strong focus on sniping and finishing a target but with NO stealth options outside of "snipe them from extreme ranges".

        Athletics:
        The only tree that gives you a variety of movement options around the battlefield and supplements your join battle and disengages in ways that few other trees can match.

        Brawl:
        The only tree with a focus in not only grapples but also heavy dps. Brawl is the go to tree for raw, pure, damage. That is it's niche and it fulfills the fantasies very well.

        Dodge:
        The perfect defense tree, and focusing only on that with a smattering of utility. Everything in the tree is built around dodging, evading, and completely negating onslaught.
        This is also the only tree with a true perfect defense in 3E with Seven Shadows Evasion.
        Yes. This is VERY much intentional by design.

        Thrown:
        Ranged attacks with light weapons, but unlike Archery, focuses on delivering them through stealth, offering ways to deal heavy damage in bursts. In addition, this is the only tree that has debuff charms as part of its core. Furthermore, this is the only combat tree with charms that do true AOE damage. Other trees have multi-attack charms but no other ability lets you hit multiple targets in a rain of kunai like Throw does.
        In addition, Throw is the "alpha strike" ability with a whole cascade of charms that work together to instantly defeat foes from the very first attack. There is NO other ability in the game that provides support for such play style.
        Again, all of this is VERY much intentional by design, providing clear distinctions between the two core ranged combat abilities as so both types of characters are viable.

        Melee:
        Melee is special in that it's the single jack of all stats combat ability. No other combat ability has the sheer versatility of Melee. It has offensive charm cascades in Excellent Strike. It has defensive charm cascades in Dipping-Swallow Defense. It has utility charm cascades in things like Call The Blade. It has heavy damage and DPS abilities. It has strong burst abilities. It has evocation boosters and dice tricks.
        Melee, by intentional design, does it all, but, it doesn't do it nearly as well as any other of the combat abilities.

        Martial Arts:
        MA is unique in that it sits in between Charms and Sorcery in terms of sheer power. Go through the charms. Mote per mote and by cost, MA charms and Forms offer far more raw power than any combat ability.
        Shining Point is the strongest DPS tree by the very nature of giving you a second attack action from it's sword.
        Crane offers defenses and counter attacks that make Melee users weep in jealousy.
        Dreaming Pearl gives evasion that no other combat tree par Dodge can match.
        Righteous Devil trades the range of Archery or Throw for sheer, raw, power at short and close range, in addition to, with very clever mechanics if I may add, shifting the ideal combat range from Far->Short to Medium->Close.
        Steel Devil is the only tree that focuses on duel-wielding swords and multiple attacks, offering ways to inflict massive onslaught penalties.
        White Reaper is the inverse in that it's the only tree that offers so many charms that completely turn onslaught on its head to rival, if not surpass, Brawl.

        MA again follows the same idea that every martial art is specialized and niche to the outright extreme, trading away the utility and sprawling charm cascades of the core abilities for very specialized MA charms and forms that are very strong at what they do, but absolutely pitiful outside that niche.
        It then offers the unique advantage of being able to flow between the forms to match the situation, offering versatility and utility with heavy investment, but still unable to match the level of utility of Melee or a combination of the core combat abilities.

        In this way, every MA is viable in some way. There is no "One True Build" because each ability has so many glaring weaknesses that they can't possibly be relied on to deal with everything.


        It's on this that the balance system of Exalted's combat thrives and why it's so damn good in 3E, which is why breaking it is the worst possible thing to do, especially with evocations, which lack the same sort of investment MA or charm cascades do.

        SIDE NOTE
        It's this specialization and niches to preserve balance and variety in builds that makes me dislike @Daerian's "Danmaku" and "Thrown" charms and consider them to be bad game design in the context of Exalted 3E. I am massive Touhou and shmup fan (I've 1cc'd every mainline Touhou game on Normal+ etc. blah blah). While the fluff is cool, it's attempting to turn Throw into something it's not, pushing it into the niches that are occupied by Sorcery and Archery (consistent AOE and range respectively). In addition, the LAST thing the tree needs is strong defensive charms because Christ almighty is a balance breaker. Danmaku, however wonky, can be fluffed away under the existing charms for Throw, anyways. In all honesty, I would never allow that homebrew at my table and would reject it outright. It's excellent work and reasoning but it's just going at it the wrong way. If you wanted to make a true danmaku spell, with a huge AOE damaging attack that could rival something like Barrier:「Boundary of Life and Death」(personal favorite spell card), a sorcerous spell at Celestial level is the way to go. The game explicitly already has Love Sign:「Master Spark」in it anyways (Death Ray).

        Archers aren't supposed to be able to kite extremely well in Exalted.         It's why Righteous Devil Form exists as the single exception to this, turning Archery on its head and letting you Aim and Reload in one motion, because it wants you to use Firewands in close and short range, but then limits you to firewands that can't shoot past Short range (Medium with charms).

        Remember, evocations aren't supposed to be strong as charms. They're supposed to do cool and unique things, but they will never be able to match the efficiency and power of a charm.

                                            Torrid Pugilism (Red Jade Smashfists ***)

                                            No one talks about the underground brawling clubs of the Blessed Isle. When people think of combat arenas, they think of colosseums and Martial Arts tournaments, both legal and illegal. But deep in the seediest districts in the heart of the Empire, no words are spoken: fists do the talking.

                                            The Brawler’s Challenge was set up as an illegal fighting tournament to see who is the best of unformalized combat, but in reality it is a deathmatch where few contests end with 2 survivors. Raging Horse, an old Dragon-Blooded boxer who retired from the navy, was the champion for a full year, a duration never before achieved in the Brawler’s Challenge. Tougher than nails, it was thought the old Fire-Aspect would reign forever. Not all rulers fall eventually.

                                            It was a young officer, barely 60 with hair of bright orange and brilliant gauntlets of Red Jade, who walked into the ring and challenged Raging Horse to a match. Raging Horse accepted, eager to put the young pup in her place. But as the old veteran soon found out, the woman with the Jade gauntlets should not have been underestimated. The match was over in mere moments, with Raging Horse a broken heap on the floor, and blood dripped from the now burning smashfists of the young officer. As the crowd cheered, the officer raised her fist as if to claim her new title. “My name is Pai-li Burning Petal, agent of the All-Seeing Eye. You are all under arrest pending investigation of further crimes against the Empress and the Empire.”

                                            That was the last night of Brawler’s Challenge. Many more such tournaments still exist, but they are more careful of their reputation, for fear of the uncanny power of the young officer with the Red Jade Smashfists.

                                            Torrid Pugilism is a pair of Smashfists made of ironsilk with plates and studs of Red Jade. They extend almost halfway up to the elbow and are heavily reinforced on the knuckles. When in combat, the air around them quivers with waves of heat, but to the wielder, they feel light and cool, the ironsilk allowing the gloves to breathe. There is a hearthstone socket on the back of each Smashfist.

                                            Evocations of Torrid Pugilism
                                            A Solar or Dragon-Blood who attunes to Torrid Pugilism causes the smashfists to radiate extra heat when in combat. The first Witheringattack the exalt makes against any given foe each round adds an extra point of Onslaught penalty. This effect can occur throughout combat, but only against foes that the Exalt attacked first, for Torrid Pugilism favors the bold. Any foe that has made an attack roll against the exalt first is ineligible for this bonus for the rest of combat.

                                            Oppressive Buffet
                                            Cost: 2m Mins: Essence 1
                                            Type: Supplemental
                                            Keywords: Withering-Only, Stackable
                                            Duration: Until end of next turn
                                            Prerequisites: None

                                            The brawler who strikes with single-minded fury is unrelenting and Torrid Pugilism is fueled by such a warrior. This evocation enhances a witheringattack causing 1 point of Onslaught penalty to persist until the end of the Exalt’s next turn. This power is stackable up to a maximum of (the higher of 3 or the Exalt’s Essence), but is cancelled if an attack is directed against any other foe. This evocation is incompatible with Battlegroups as the gauntlets prefer to battle one-on-one.

                                            Nimble Blitz
                                            Cost: 3m Mins: Essence 1
                                            Type: Supplemental
                                            Keywords: None
                                            Duration: Instant
                                            Prerequisites: None

                                            Torrid Pugilism seeks weakness in its foes and rushes to punish such weakness. The wielder of these smashfists may activate Nimble Blitz to add the foe’s current Onslaught penalty as a bonus to the exalt’s own Rush pool as non-charm dice. This bonus cannot exceed a maximum of (the higher of 3 or the Exalt’s Essence) and can only be used once per foe per combat, unless reset by raising a foe’s Onslaught penalty to 4 from 0.

                                            Searing Reversal
                                            Cost: 4m, 1wp Mins: Essence 2
                                            Type: Simple
                                            Keywords: Decisive-Only
                                            Duration: Instant
                                            Prerequisites: Oppressive Buffet

                                            With a backflash of fury, the exalt turns their disadvantage upon their foes. This evocation creates a difficulty 3 gambit where the exalt touches their foe and sears a brand upon them. If successful, this brand expels the exalt’s current Onslaught penalty resetting them to zero, and transfers it to their foe. This penalty lasts for (the higher of 3 or the Exalt’s Essence) rounds, though the exalt will resume accruing Onslaught penalties after activating Searing Reversal. Onslaught penalties are gained after the attack is resolved.

                                            If the foe is currently under the effects of Oppressive Buffet, Searing Reversal may also copy the exalt’s current wound penalty for a single round. This does not remove the penalties from the exalt, and the copied penalty fades at the beginning of the foe’s next turn.

                                            Special Activation Rules: Searing Reversal can only be unlocked by landing a Decisive attack against a foe that had an Onslaught penalty of 4 or greater.

                                            Inferno Smash
                                            Cost: 5m, 1wp Mins: Essence 3
                                            Type: Simple
                                            Keywords: Decisive-Only, Perilous
                                            Duration: Instant
                                            Prerequisites: Nimble Blitz, Searing Reversal

                                            The relentless strikes of the brawler are a sweet melody to Torrid Pugilism, but the final act, the climax of the song, is the sweetest of all. The exalt may activate Inferno Smash to create a Decisive attack in an attempt to finish off a battered foe. This attack grants the foe’s current Onslaught penalty in bonus dice to the damage roll. Additionally, the exalt may double up to (Essence) 10’s on their damage roll should any appear.

                                            If the foe is not incapacitated by Inferno Smash, their current Onslaught penalty after attack resolution persists for an extra round (on top of any other persistent effects), resetting at the end of the exalt’s next turn. Inferno Smash may only be used once per combat per foe.
