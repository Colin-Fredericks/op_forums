                Now, let’s see how one of the new guys holds up.

                Okay, gonna disregard having seen that Caul preview a year ago for a moment… oh hey, here’s a highly prominent and successful Lunar leader who specifically originated in the Shogunate. I like the previous two shahan-yas, but we certainly need more younger ones to drive home the point about the First Agers not being the driving force of the Pact.

                Now, for something actually new; I spent a long time thinking of Oka as being external to the Pact, but ultimately like the group being portrayed as something that is diverse and accommodating enough that his personal crusade of open warfare is valid within them, as well as providing the background for many others to flock to him for greater or lesser tours. What I find particularly interesting is that he reflects that same accommodation back on those who join him, not gatekeeping or proselytizing to them. It helps create the picture of what would be so personable and attractive about him that would attract so many, particularly in contrast to the prior, older ones. Come to that… I’m forming a picture, let’s see where it goes…

                So yeah, his whole thing about having repeatedly faked his death, in extremely public ways. Quite apart from being a fun reversal of the old adage that Lunars only ever accomplished things by dying in the process, it’s another nice way of creating a distinctly Lunar kind of accomplishment (I mean, sure, a Solar could probably do some of it, but it feels less readily as a signature move for one of their luminaries). Alluding to his experience with the disappeared Caul helps add some mystical weirdness to him, although what I find most interesting there is the timeline; if he was born into the Shogunate, but the Caul vanished shortly after the Usurpation, he was most likely not a Lunar for very long when he went with it, helping cement his endurance as not just a feat of immense power. And I’m bemused by the contrast with the prior Lunars, being more widely admirable but having less personal intimacy. I actually particularly like the idea of a Full Moon who has an inclination towards obscure ritual and contemplation of mystery.

                So I’m reading the line about the military advantages of the Caul being under Pact relations is to show that his military action is not just about the war itself, but making a big show to demonstrate the potential to other like-minded Lunars. Possibly also sometimes Lunars that aren’t as desirous of such battle on the whole, but still sometimes want a theatre to cut loose in. Oh, but I like the addition of others arguing that he’s emboldening Lunars in their own theatres to engage in strategies that are less functional there. All of this so far has been doing a lot of the same good work as the Great House write-ups, creating meaningful structures for actual politics and intrigue and actual, functional factionalisation. Right now I’m picturing a scenario in which Raksi, jerk that she is, is being unrestrained in condemning the desires of some enthusiastic Lunars in council, which doesn’t bely her actually being kind of in the right, or at least well motivated. I’m taking from the description of Oka’s own take on things that, for the most part, he’s not strongly partisan in the sense of having no issues with shahan-yas that maintain a focus on the attrition warfare, only rejecting those who have rejected him outright. There’s a description of Leviathan here, but I’ll hold off on considerations about him until his own write-up, and just infer that while the Black Lion has no issue with attrition, he’s a lot less fond of those that opt for defensive withdrawal.

                Hmm, my picture of him is evolving a bit from Brian Blessed to Sean Boromir (particularly the scene from Two Towers Extended Edition where we actually get a direct picture of Boromir as a great captain of Gondor).

                It makes sense that his instruction is both fairly comprehensive and multi-faceted in pursuit of the many avenues of an active war, and impersonal when he’s got a lot of other responsibilities to attend to. I can actually imagine some Lunars seeking his tutelage just for the range of educational topics rather than personal investment in the war (even if they do become obligated during their tenure to take part in it). It’s also interesting to get this picture of how Sha’a Oka is experienced by others, especially that idea that when drawn into conversation he’s only partially present. I find something strangely relatable in a lot of the arrangement.

                After the prior two, I’m also interested to see what it’s like to have a Lunar’s prominent adherents consist themselves of older and more prestigious Lunars who serve as lieutenants. Let’s see what they have… heh, Oka’s undying commitment… anyway, yes, I think there’s some goof stuff from the outset with Third Daughter of the Leaves, with her perhaps not altogether metaphorical description of focusing anger in excess of her years into attacks of witchcraft, as well as specifically being less loyal to His Divine Lunar Presence than the actual war to control the Realm.

                Skathra Venomchild sounds a lot like somebody from the Contagion era, and I like the description of how and why they’re so close to Sha’a Oka, as well as that particular expression of being a Lunar Exalt (by which I’m referring to the ecstatic madness). There’s an intriguing sense of backstory in the idea that nobody has shown them the sort of kindness or understanding that Oka has. On top of all this, the description of revelling in violence and terror suggests that they’re not exactly a nice person, so it’s fun to come with that at the end of all of the more sympathetic stuff.

                Sandswept Garda-Empress is fine, although less interesting to me than most of the other described adherents so far; nothing wrong with that, and her place and purpose are still clear. But then there’s the very interesting idea of a Lunar who not only still identifies as one of the Lintha, but an actual intermediary between the Silver Pact and a local branch of the Family. That presents an insight into a way in which the Pact might maintain alliances with external groups while preserving the integrity of its own system. Likewise I think there’s something strong in the idea of Flowers Unbending in the Storm being truly loyal, but still having a major disagreement that has caused her to act a bit subversively.

                And it helps to convey the scope of Oka’s legend to make some references to Lunars far afield that still have a connection, whether it be a former student off doing their own thing or somebody pledged to his belief system without any kind of personal connection.

                My ultimate assessment of Oka is that he gives a good feel for a Lunar that is at the head of a system that is large and complex, something informed by him but beyond him. The other two had that to an extent, but even with Raksi it felt a bit smaller and closer, primarily like living in a tense household. Oka’s school radiates out from him, extending feelers to the furthest reaches of Creation and knitting together a variety of personalities and interests. Between that and the focus on how indirect he often is even when he’s not pretending to be dead… it’s good at giving him presence without him needing to be around. It’s a good approach, and it’s executed well.

                    Anyway, my thoughts on the previewed Charms.


                    Chimera Soul Expression: Heh, who would have thought that we'd ultimately get a chimerism without any flash? I mean that in the best way possible, that it's proper for it to be a basic meat-and-potatoes thing for a bit of character customisation. I do wonder if the definition of mutations in the book proper will be a bit more comprehensive, or if this simple one says all that is needed.

                    Hybrid-Body Transformation: Between Eska's write-up and ensuing discussion, the function of this one has been known for ages, but I do like the particular language employed to convey the accommodation in the appearance. I was never very enthusiastic about Deadly Beastman Transformation in the past, but when the idea is that it's just about overlaying some animal traits on the human form, that those Merits can include anything with physical benefit, and none of them need to be dedicated to appearance, I'm much happier with something providing a suite of mutations.

                    Totem Beast Embodiment: I like that idea for how a Lunar might harness and apply the power of an animal they possess the shape of, especially for how the Protean keyword provides both an incentive to do it in animal shape and a process for expressing the power while still in a human form. I wonder if Lunar free Excellencies can also raise the die-cap by stunting.

                    Ohh, an Essence 5 Charm, what more impressive shit do they have...

                    Silver Ripple Mirage: Uh-huh, so obviously Lunars have a preceding Charm to turn into images in reflective surfaces. It's fun that it's not only a more witchy power, but one under a Changing Moon Attribute. For the Charm itself, I've always thought that dematerialization was a bit of an awkward fit for Lunars, but what sells this one is the particular process and image, something more rooted in the Lunar milieu to keep it from feeling like part of a generic grab bag. The image of momentary physicality and being able to slip back into reflections also lends itself to Lunars engaging in some freaky avenues of attack, and the ways in which it might shape the behaviour of enemies. I also think it's interesting and useful to create novel methods of fighting against it within the Charm itself.

                    Wolf Devours Shepherd: Another nice simple one to invoke some of that predatory image, as well as show the benefit of the Totemic Keyword for diversifying characters.

                    Debt of Borrowed Skin: Like the other Charms for alternative sacred hunts, I appreciate how this one doesn't let the Lunar off easy, with the requirement for the payment in exchange for the shape needing to be a big deal rather than just a trivial endeavour.

                                All right, show me Leviathan.


                                I’ll admit, while I can see the intention behind contrasting the modern day roving sea monster Leviathan with him having once been the top admiral of the Realm navy, something about that backstory never really moved me. I don’t really have an elaborate reason to prefer the idea that he was always a Lunar whose activity was more master ocean predator and preferred to live out there in that, I just do.

                                ….Is the implication that Leviathan is trans?

                                Anyway, still has the element of severe grief over losing his mate, but… is kind of both less and more of a weirdo about it. He’s still retreated from society to just protect Luthe while spending all of his time as a giant orca, and doing so for a long time, but at least he’s not terrorising the descendants of the city’s Dragon Blooded and violently murdering any who Exalt… I presume.

                                I like how he’s tied into that revised history of expansion to the West only being relatively recent, and how it’s framed as a bridge too far for him (as well as a bit of delving too deep and awakening an ancient horror). From the description of Luthe, I’m getting the image of a city that would be on an open war footing but for the limits of being under miles of water, but is nevertheless doing a lot of damage there, at least until the enemy is weak enough to emerge en masse and launch a true invasion. I think there’s something especially fun in the idea of a lot of younger Lunars finding the appearance of this powerful First Age elder really abrupt and hard to get a read on, while the few others who knew him are excited the prospect of him getting back in the game. Kind of a Dark Knight Returns thing going on there.

                                And that lends itself to a rather interesting twist on the image of the overwhelmingly powerful elder; somebody that is really old, but nevertheless has a huge amount of catching up to do and hasn’t quite recovered his social skills. Especially the idea that he maintains an open invitation for Sha’a Oka despite the rejection, but the rejection might make any actual meeting between them go poorly. And I enjoy that his side of reconnecting with the other survivors of the first generation amounts to them being the only people he has left that he can properly relate to, except being separated from the context of them for more than a thousand years means that he’s out of the loop in the many complications in their relationships that have cropped up since, and he can’t figure out which one to consolidate while breaking ties with the rest. It’s kind of… relatable, but also alien. This idea that Leviathan has been away from his humanity for so long, but has a need and desire for connecting with some of his peers, in a manner that amounts to him having a significant struggle. It both makes me empathise for him, and is a surprisingly elegant way of curtailing the extremes of his potential power.

                                I’ve got to say, so far the book has done a very good job on capitalising on the sense of how old a lot of these people are. Which for the First Age ones in particular, seems to often amount more to a liability, whether it be an excessive focus on the long view of things or having accumulated more bad blood and bitter memories than they can effectively handle. I’m picturing Tayan Silver-Crowned to convince Feather Drenched to place himself under her authority simply by effectively making that argument to him.

                                And all of that seems to come with some pros and cons for him as a teacher; he seems overly reliant on his old prestige drawing more experienced Lunars that can actually function as the mentors to any youths that wind up with him. But it otherwise makes him a lot less partisan than even the Black Lion, and possibly more effective on that front if he should effectively marshal all of his resources; the ocean provides a lot of resources and strategic avenues, and if the right collection of Lunars should manage to get a distribution network going… yes, I could imagine such a thing in the future greatly magnifying the power of the Silver Pact.

                                Hmm, is it worth pulling my copy of Scroll of Exalts out of a box not a meter and a half away to remind myself exactly what Swims in Shadows was like in the previous Edition… ah screw it. Just a second… okay, that backstory of having been a deep diving shaman who Exalted by pushing himself down far in search of a spirit willing to fight off shark boy, discovered his god was an old Lunar, and started applying his prior skills to proselytizing to spirits on Lunar behalf is actually pretty neat. Assuming there’s no comprehensive overhaul later, I’m going to view this writing as a supplement for the new context rather than rewrite. As to that content, yeah, I like Leviathan’s context emerging in a Lunar who already had a teacher becoming enamoured of him, splitting his service between them, and finding himself in the awkward position of the former one expecting him to function as a go-between.

                                It seems like Leviathan’s abrupt entry lends itself well to a lot of divided loyalties, with one of the Black Wave Reavers being like-minded and owing a personal debt, but keeping that to himself when his circle mate is a dedicated follower of Oka. I think that’s good for giving a sense of the Silver Pact’s cross sections of philosophy through distant adherents that end up more closely associating. Like I said before, this is how you do factions.

                                Ah good, another beastfolk Lunar. Oh, Gjalla makes for a nice change of pace with a much more mercenary, opportunist Lunar who’s only in it for access to the ancient super city and personal treasure and giving her family a place of unimaginable luxury and security to dwell in, while being singularly unimpressed with Leviathan himself. I really like her; she makes me want stories of Luthe’s disastrous downfall that have never so much as crossed my mind before. Not out of malice, of course, but imagine how dramatic it would be!

                                I don’t know about anybody else, but Leviathan as a shahan-ya brings a lot of really interesting new dynamics to the character and the Silver Pact as a whole to me, and I could see a lot of really cool and fun stuff coming out of it. Even just as a basis for a lot of undersea adventures, whether based in Luthe or with that as an element that other people have to deal with. Hell, Luthe as being on a war footing while also a uniquely secure and beneficial home base for Lunars makes that city more intriguing than it’s ever been (hence why I care enough for me to want it dramatically destroyed). When I talked about wanting the book to give me something that I hadn’t known I would always want, this right here is what I was talking about.

                                And thinking some more over Sha’a Oka’s perspective, I also think it’s good and valuable to have at least one major figure look on Leviathan’s return and be really pissy at him not having contributed earl…

                                Oh dang, Luthe is underwater Wakanda.

                                            The answer was apparently yes.

                                            {slap slap}


                                            All right, if a quarter of the Charms chapter is being put up this soon, then I’d best see if I can’t polish off this chapter within the next few hours, because that is going to be an ordeal. I would personally prefer a longer window in-between previews to have some breathing room, but what are you going to do?


                                            Okay Amatha, fingers crossed for showing me a shahan-ya from the Realm era…


                                            Oh, snap, can’t get more Realm era than “actual child of a Great House founder”.

                                            I’ve wondered, sometimes, if the new layout of the Realm might lend itself to some abusive husbands when they find themselves with wives they rank higher than. And when it’s the actual child of Cynis, well, sounds like a nasty situation all around. Good on her to flee from that, although I do find something indefinably creepy in the idea that the first shape she acquired was that of her husband’s sister (even if she applied it to canny use).

                                            Wait a moment… this whole time I’ve been presuming that Amatha Kinslayer was Yena Bone-Breaker from the core book. Okay, adjusting some expectations…

                                            So yeah, just a general saboteur with a more personal grudge against the Realm and one of its Houses. I actually see an interesting hint of the Cynis worldview in her aspirations to profaning the Scarlet Throne. And of course, as a child of Cynis, she’ll likely be Well-Bred, lending itself to having her own Dragon Blooded child… ah, my head was initially going in the direction of the daughter being with her, but no, she was left behind. Given the description of how Amatha brought her up, there’s got to be some lingering bitterness there in having been abandoned, even apart from the Anathema thing.

                                            Oh my, Amatha’s sisters are still running the House, aren’t they? I’ve got to imagine that her identity as a Lunar is not widely known, since it seems like it would be a strong recurring disgrace for a trio of matriarchs. I’m getting this weird picture of her as a Lunar that thrives on dysfunction.

                                            I guess it makes sense that a younger Lunar would have a less elaborate description of Pact relations, even if she has a bit of a head start in political dealings from that Dynastic upbringing… which ironically also holds her back, because she’s dealing with people that would hold a Dynastic upbringing (and left behind descendants as a source of possible conflict of interest) against her. And then that upbringing also instils a sense of pride that means she responds to the snubs by being an incorrigible prankster in council… yes, definitely lives for the dysfunction. I like her.

                                            After a bunch of much older Lunar mentors with various… issues with aloofness, it’s nice to see one who is more self-critical and puts up confident fronts, and it helps that she has a genuinely valuable set of insights and frames of reference. That she’s also a very sink-or-swim kind of teacher and tries to cultivate a wild abandon, maybe even a certain joie-de-vivre, also makes it seem like the experience of learning from her would be more dynamic, even fun for the Lunars involved.

                                            Now, some of those in question… I think it’s cute to have the aristocrat bringing up an urchin in whom she sees a lot of potential, especially that idea that they make an educational game of promoting opposing social trends as a weapon against satrapies. I also think it speaks well of Amatha that she sees higher value in having Nida speak openly against her in council than having somebody in line behind her; like Ma-Ha-Suchi putting up with students that irritate him, it paints a helpful picture of a Silver Pact where contrary relationships don’t need to be inhibited by power differences.

                                            Trasenji Yilean’s backstory is interesting enough, but he’s less of a vibrant character than Nida. And now, let’s see if this book can finally redeem Strength-of-Many… hmm, unfortunately not really. He’s got a bit more interplay with other Lunars within this setup for the Silver Pact (and maybe something mildly interesting in being apart from the society as a whole), but still has little deviation or elaboration from his one note of fighting slavery. He can’t even have an interesting dynamic with Amatha, only associating with her because the grudge with House Cynis is relevant to his interests, and not even being a proper student. Set among the rest of these, he comes off as especially bland; if this book can’t figure out a way to make the character a bit more interesting, I’m afraid nothing can.

                                            Hmm, Amatha’s write-up felt shorter than the others, maybe for the chapter winding down… keep going.

                                            All right, a completely new person that I had not mistaken for somebody else at all, Ruksara-Who-Remembers (probably a Dune style “living archive through rote memorisation” thing).

                                            Okay, no ambiguity over Rukhsara being trans. So her prior incarnation being a Lunar with in impeccable memory and millenia of experience means that Rukhsara’s experience of past life information is… a lot more extreme than the norm. Presumably, even if she’s only getting a fraction of it, it’s still far more than she’s yet capable of processing. Interesting. And in the meantime she’s putting her power to good use with getting some ancestral homelands back and trying to shore them up against retaliation, while mortal and Exalted adherents argue that she should take a more aggressive stance.

                                            I’m guessing… that her school is structured in terms of the vast repository of information that she is, creating more variation in her students and resulting issues with difference in desired approach to the Pact’s mission statement. I’ve actually got an interesting image now in which her investment in her clan means that many of its mortal members form close rapports through common cause with her students.

                                            Her status in the Pact owing to what’s provided by her former incarnation is interesting, particularly Raksi’s uncommon delicacy. Oh wait, Aqadar… I think I remember from the Fajad preview… he’s got some kind of big tower, doesn’t he? And a rapport with another Lunar in a similar boat. It’s a very peculiar approach to depicting one Lunar’s Pact relations, broadly well-received but with some particularly intense and personal bonds. In the lack of overt description of political agenda, I’m seeing a sense that she’s had to be more dedicated to investing in her personal project and managing her inheritance; I can imagine that lack of affiliation helps with general popularity (the reference to a preference for gradually grinding down notwithstanding).

                                            Yes, I find my prior suppositions corroborated by the description of her teaching style. It’s all about getting them to provide benefits to her people, and a more mutual approach to forming policies on various frontiers.

                                            Ho-oh lord yes, are her adherents tied into her clan, right down to a Lunar becoming her grandson-in-law. Somehow, I kept seeing the picture of Lunar dominions as very solitary affairs, and even with the preview of the Lunar schools… I’ve never given much consideration for dominions with multiple Lunars, and some of the implications. And still always imagining them as so distant from the inhabitants, rather than including instances of them being important as some of the few places where Lunars don’t need to hide. The funny thing is that this approach is doing more for making such locales and societies interesting and vibrant than the Thousand Streams River, in base concept or provided example, ever did.

                                            And then there’s this asshole, one excessively devoted Echo-Prince Piyo that is of the opinion her mentor is not living up to her status and determined to have her adopt a more opulent splendour. See Strength-of-Many, why can’t you be more like Piyo? Followed by one No Trouble Zhang, who… takes a similar path to a very different conclusion, concerned almost entirely with acquiring his teacher’s occult lore. This lad strikes me as somebody mostly opposed to the Realm in the abstract; too dedicated to the goal in its own right, rather than driven by an underlying reason for it. He strikes me as a bit ruthless, possibly to the point of amorality; no trouble indeed.


                                            And, finally, a little section on some of The Rest.

                                            Yyyep, Aqadar is the one with that creepy tower. Kind of a terrifying sorcerer mendicant, way too focused on securing vengeance. Bit of background for Tayan’s purpose in making and leading the Vanguard, with her own military origin and its value in having inflicted damage on the Shogunate enemy.

                                            Ah, so Feather Drenched isn’t somebody under direct authority so much as a kind of consigliere, although the point about maybe lacking clarity from too much grief could still stand. I can actually see the relationship being mutually beneficial; Tayan gets the highly valued expertise on the Sidereals (which probably goes a long way towards helping such a close concentration of Lunars keep afloat), Feather gets somebody that can pursue her vengeance in a more structured, efficient manner. And sure, nice to see a Lunar with a lot more focus on their side of the Usurpation.

                                            Okay, you’ve got your name, your presence in some of the art and comic story, a connection to a spider Lunar I enjoy, and like… half of a good design, show me something worthwhile to tie it all together Sublime Danger… rrriigght, basically a Lunar who applies her power and nature as a high-level thrill seeker, barely concerned with the political disposition of Creation, and is just philosophical about it as to take on the like-minded and put them through their paces (or maybe she just finds it amusing), with a touch of having designs on her mate that would make Yo-yo boy’s quest probably not end in a manner most to his liking. Yeah, she’s all right; I’m imagining a personality of a kind I think is fun, and for all that the book ultimately dashed my dreams and built something better from the shards, there might as well be at least one prominent Lunar who desires only complete freedom and to live a life of intrigue and adventure.

                                            You’ve got the interesting presentation of a former duo of shahan-yas who worked together in the esoteric region of the Dreaming Sea, utilizing a combination of mundane and mystical means to harass the Realm’s interests in the area, divided now over a question of whether to destroy Prasad or splinter it off into a rival of the Blessed Isle. And finally, one more for the first generation with Ul the Burning Eye, who has taken Lunar grievances to the greatest extremes and plots comprehensive genocide of the Terrestrial Exalted (and presumably all mortals who carry sufficient quantities of their bloodlines).

                                            There’s a smidge left, but… here I’ll call it another night.

                                                Okay, finally finish off, and then… on to the Charms. :S


                                                Silver Pact’s relations with external powers. Being what it is, there’s no unified idea or policy, just a broad consensus with lots of personal variations, seemingly informed by a rather proprietary attitude towards their grudge with the Realm.

                                                Now, what’s the definition of an independent Lunar? Err, bpperr… so, you can still get favours and connections, which is a bit more association than I would expect. I guess then to be an independent means having neither hostility to the Realm, adherence to a shahan-ya or participation in councils, as well as possibly disregarding some of their customary standards of relation. The example of how Yena Bone-Breaker handled an independent who incurred her wrath gives a certain sense that relations between those and the Silver Pact… kind of favours the independent, depending on perspective. It’s at least interesting that the Pact has such a strong interest in being attractive to other Lunars as to consistently treat them with a delicate hand.

                                                Heh, my perspective on Ma-Ha-Suchi having not been a member of the Silver Pact was not entirely incorrect, it just lacked some elaborating details. That kind of variation in perspective does help to emphasize the informality in the group, anyway.

                                                What about (presumably non-Realm) Dragon Blooded? So as long as it’s not somebody claiming a descent from the Shogunate or otherwise engaged in extending territorial power, most Lunars are willing to approach them with caution. I’m actually imagining the reluctance to do things in the long-term as ultimately more beneficial to the Dragon Blooded in the equation, if that kind of thing can bring the wrath of the Realm down on a group less equipped to withstand it. And some loners can even find places in mixed circles or schools, although the circumstances and attitudes that might prolong this into allied family groups are probably still exceedingly unlikely. Still, it’s nice that the attitude to Dragon Blooded is fairly accommodating, and focused on treating with them as peers rather than just a resource to cultivate and harness.

                                                So with Sidereals, on the whole they’re in the same boat, in a manner that complicates interactions with independent Lunars. Whatever I may have said about it in the past, I ultimately think it’s a good thing for Lunars that understand the nuances of Heavenly politics to have a willingness to engage with those who aren’t complicit in their persecution when they have common cause, even if there are some less well-versed Lunars that basically attack on sight. That the Gold Faction is trying to put feelers out helps strengthen the sense of credibility for the Lunars, and apart from their uncertainty about what they want with the Solars, I think it’s clever for there to be Lunars that are wary of such communications just because it might give them away to the Bronze Faction. The funny thing is, now more than ever, a Silver Faction would make sense (although still perhaps a stretch; possibly too many Lunars have gone too far for even independent Sidereals to want to entrust Creation to them).

                                                And now for the Solars themselves; I was worried that the chapter wouldn’t get around to bringing them up. Throughout the Second Age, the few survivors seem to have been a marginal, if weighted consideration, especially for their mates. It seems to me that those Solars didn’t transform the overall landscape, just generally serving as a circumstantial wild card element, either beneficial or concerning depending on the details. But now they’ve returned on the whole, and the Lunar plans have become disrupted. Too many Solars that might have their own contrary agendas, too many older Lunars who aren’t willing to let the Solars be on the top of the pyramid again, and the tumult of a lot more revived bonds and associated powerful emotions; if Lilith is anything to go by, that’s going to be the roughest on those few Lunars that actually remember having mates and have had to spend all of that time with their absence. Suddenly, I have a picture of many of Leviathan’s adherents and allies specifically working to prevent the reincarnation of his mate from reaching him, lest it disrupt his limited early momentum.

                                                Right, so there were Lunars in a position to gather early warning of the Abyssals even before their grand reveal, and since them most of them are in a position of waiting and seeing and judging by first contact, but for the realisation that some of them are the subject of Solar Bonds. Anja Silverclaws’ attitude was already more compelling, and I think the idea of giving her an Abyssal mate effectively compounds that. Ahh, and the point about the ongoing vendetta against the Realm makes a good point about a priority that may decide in Abyssal favour, possibly to the Lunars’ disadvantage.

                                                And now, we’re getting our unofficial introduction to the Infernal Exalted of Third Edition, before their proper debut in the Storytelling Guide. Oh, it’s… literally three sentences. Well, isn’t my face red. Okay, so most have no idea, just a few have been determined by mystical intelligence gathering, a rumour and a distant name insufficient to form any solid opinion on their implications, and so few encounters that almost none of them have revived their Bond through an Infernal. I’m suddenly reminded of how the origin of the term Green Sun Prince was in Ingosh Silverclaws last vision, and I’m kind of attached to that little bit of trivia.

                                                Exigents are reinforced as having too much variety to form a common standard, albeit with a focus on a few examples that have distinctly made common cause with the Pact. While Liminals are too diffuse to form a common standard, and a lot of the impetus seems to be on their side, in whether they want the assistance of Lunar magic or are opposed to its use in harnessing the dead improperly. I like the examples that we get, Withari’s Child finding a useful refuge with Ul in exchange for enduring his weirdness, Dreams-of-Rain being kind of caught in the crossfire of Pact conflict with the Dynasty such that he’s got a grudge against the Lunar that did it, and any others that get in his way.

                                                Ahh, I had kind of forgotten about the Getimians, even though there’s been a lot of questions about how their fight with the Sidereals might interact with the Lunar conflict. Sooo… right, they had high hopes at the first appearance, but learning that Rakan Thulio is their leader has poisoned that well too much. So the best that can be managed is trying to direct encountered Getimians to disrupt Sidereal plans in ways beneficial to Lunars, as such opportunities arise.

                                                All right, getting to the bottom of the list now. The Guild has enough presence and power to be a major consideration, while also being structured in a manner resilient against Lunar infiltration, and motivated in a manner that makes it more tool than friend. Where the Guild undermines the Realm, it’s unmolested or supported, where it helps or takes liberties with dominions it gets to feel the brunt of some Lunar anger. I’ve been liking how many of these come with focused examples, it keeps feeling like a good lesson taken from Masters of Jade, and here that gets a full confluence with an example of a factor who made an exchange agreement with one of Ma-Ha-Suchi’s crew, up until she also took up with a Realm satrap and is being used by the Lunar as a source of disinformation. I’d say that’s all about what one would expect of the Guild, itself being too diffuse to heavily politicize dealing with it.

                                                Most of the spirit stuff is fairly standard as any policies towards them go, although the Lunars maybe get a bit of an edge from how the opposition has a dedicated policy of managing and suppressing spirits. For examples, I like the manner in which Aqadar provides assistance to Fajad’s spirit prophets, even if his intentions are largely self-serving. The most interesting is the idea that there are Lunars who seek a particularly strong affinity with those gods under Luna’s purviews, whether motivated by spiritual considerations or the sense that their patron gives them an inroad. Tethys on the Untrammeled Path is one that I view as the more understated and well-written elements of Glories of the Most High, so I like seeing her brought back in, and I find something delightfully weird in the idea of an eclipse god being a giant god-bat.

                                                Who’s left… among the dead, there are plenty of ghosts that would simply flock to Lunars as powerful figures that can aid and protect them (probably helped a bit by Lunars having had some of the strongest affinity for the dead through necromancy in the Second Age), as well as some having the benefits of their associated living being under Lunar dominions. And the plain fact of many Lunars having lived long enough to have enduring friendships among the dead. I like how creepy the associations of the example Lunars are, one offering their powerful blood in exchange for secrets, the other using victims of the common enemies as fuel to unleash dreadful weapons in vengeance. I am curious about how, between this and the Abyssal section, the Deathlords don’t seem to be on the Silver Pact’s radar.

                                                Being almost entirely detached from the Wyld now, the Fair Folk seem like a much more marginal concern for the Silver Pact. It seems to me that what violence is dedicated towards them is primarily motivated by the memory of the Balorian Crusade and desire to not allow it to be repeated, I feel both for the sake of protecting the world and not letting them interrupt Lunar vengeance again. Other than that, they’re just another potential weapon against the Realm, albeit one whose history and appetites can draw severe reservations, and a fine line against going too far for most to tolerate. It’s a bit less specificity towards the Raksha than I might have expected, but I have no strong attachment to that and the Lunars certainly have a lot else on their plate.

                                                And the very last of them all is some standards for the interaction with various regional powers. It’s obviously not a question for the Silver Pact as a whole, instead a matter of how people with their agendas, as well as schools, might deal with their proximity. The Confederation of Rivers is a big deal to Lunars throughout the East, divided by ones who see it primarily as an extension of Lookshy and thus a target, and ones who want to encourage and strengthen its members to counterbalance the Shogunate remnant-state (see, factions!). The Dreaming Sea certainly seems like it would be a particular hotbed of powers to deal with, with interesting multi-faceted schemes such as turning Prasad’s rivals against one another so that those Dragon Blooded are freed up to rebel against the Realm, contrasted with Lunars who view those rivals as more tolerable and allies against a feeler of Realm power in the region. The idea of Lunars being so entangled in the local politics enhances the Dreaming Sea’s mystique to me, particularly with the idea that some of them are as at cross-purposes as the Silver Pact will allow. I’m particularly imagining some princes of the states and empires recognising this dispute, and actively playing Lunars against one another for the benefit of their own power; like, say, a leader of Volivat delicately getting benefits from both, in the hopes of rising to dominance over both Ysyr and Prasad. And we already knew about some of Sha’a Oka’s association with the Lintha, although it’s a nice place to reintroduce Ten Stripes, and good to point out that more distant Lunars might have protectorates that would benefit more from fighting the Lintha off.

                                                And that’s the end of chapter two, a very strong thesis statement on the Lunars, and full of delightful personalities, agendas and methodologies. I think that even for people who don’t quite like this direction, the strength of this portrayal can’t be denied.
