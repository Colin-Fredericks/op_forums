                                The Japanese have a fairy tale called, "The Boy Who Drew Cats".


                                A farmer and his wife had many children; the youngest son was too small and weak, and spent all his time drawing cats instead of doing his chores. So, they took him to the temple to become a priest. He learned quickly, but continued to draw cats everywhere with his calligraphy brush.

                                The old priest marveled at the skill with which he painted the cats; it seemed as though they might leap off the paper at any moment! But this could not be allowed to continue. The old priest delivered his ultimatum: either the boy must finally cease all painting and devote himself only to his studies, or he must leave the temple to see if he could survive in the world as an artist.

                                The boy chose his freedom.

                                On his way to town, he spotted a large, abandoned building - a temple with paper walls, such as he had grown up within. Seized by a childhood desire long suppressed, he made his way to the temple and drew cats across every paper surface, floor to ceiling in all of the rooms. Eventually, night fell, and he found a secure place to fall asleep.

                                Unbeknownst to him, who spent so much time painting that he paid so little attention to gossip, this temple had been abandoned because a goblin-rat had invaded and made its home there, driving the priests away. Exorcists and warriors who came to kill it were never seen again.

                                That night, the young man dreamt of a gigantic, angry rat that meant to tear him apart for the affront of painting his home with cats, but a swarm of cats leapt upon him and tore him apart.

                                When the young man awoke, he was shocked to see all of his painted cats grinning down at him with mouths wet and red from blood, and the floor spotted with bloody paw prints, with gouges all over as if carved by massive claws.

                                Then he found the corpse of the enormous goblin-rat, ripped to pieces.

                                The boy went on to become a famous painter who only drew cats, and the city where he dwelt never feared for vermin or bandits or monsters again.

                                            I've seen a few people suggest Morgiana of Magi as a Lunar Exalt. Sadly, she wouldn't seem to meet these criteria because she did not seek to escape her captivity from Jamil on her own.

                                            At least, not on-screen. It's possible that Morgiana once attempted to escape, failed, and was not killed or crippled in punishment. In world where Lunar Exaltation is possible, she might have taken the Second Breath. But then, she wouldn't be the same woman we meet in the first episode.

                                            The Morgiana first seen on-screen is broken in spirit and doesn't dare to even think about taking her freedom. She only gets her freedom because of the actions of others.


                                            She's later taken prisoner again, and has an Exaltation moment when she frees herself, but this time she's trying to break her bonds in order to save someone else rather than secure her own freedom. Also, the moment is punctuated by a shining savior figure back-lit by the Sun itself telling her that she is no longer a slave. So, I'm thinking she became a Solar Exalt and received a vision from the Unconquered Sun when he Chose her.

                                            Even ignoring the solar imagery, I repeat that she broke her chains for the sake of someone else rather than for her personal freedom. She sought the power to make a difference in the world according to her new vision of the world, and the Sun chooses to grant his power to people with the vision to use it.


                                            Any objections?


                                            (Even though it has all the trappings of an Exaltation moment, Morgiana doesn't actually become any stronger than she was before. She simply sheds all of her old fear and stops holding herself back.)

                                                Although... she's said to have been a Chiaroscuran woman taken by debt-slavers. I mean, the actual people trading her were from the Realm, but Chiaroscuro itself still practices slavery, so it would seem to suggest she was somebody being transported as a matter of the debt of herself or somebody else, so there's still an element of being bound by your own society, even if that society proceeds to trade you to somebody else.

                                                Either way, I think the manner in which that one was written up contains two features that still distinguish it from a general narrative of trying to escape captivity.                                                 The other is the fairly extreme standard to which she would pursue freedom, throwing herself to death rather than living as somebody's toy.

                                                I wonder if it's reading too much into it to speculate on the nature and significance of her debt... probably. Regular people have debts, some people get enslaved for their debt; I think I actually prefer the idea that she was a conventional person until faced with the possibility of becoming a slave.
