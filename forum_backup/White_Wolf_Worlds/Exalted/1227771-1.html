    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    Solars have too much ego and arrogance.
    Solars are exactly what you imagine them to be.



    No, wait, that's ambiguous. Let me try again:

    ​Solars are an entirely fictional construct where, even within the context of their fiction, they're a distinctly unrealised element that is coming in unshaped by a prevailing trend, as goes their own personalities. Hence, their traits are what the one creating the universe and playing the characters would ascribe to them. As the default playable group, they are afforded a remarkable degree of carte blanche as regards the personality, motivations, goals and relationships of any given Solar.

    ​It continuously amazes me that people don't seem to want to utilise this privilege, or are even aware of it. Between this and several other things, such as reading Alchemical Genesis womb Charms as though they mean that Autochthon has some weird hang-up about reproduction, or substituting how Ma-Ha-Suchi is actually written in the new core in favour of how he was written in Second Edition, and any other innumerable small things, I sometimes get an impression that many people only kind of half read the books. They come in with their own presuppositions on what they'll say with regards to the subject of powerful demigods (often shaped by what has been gleaned from hyperbole in Internet discussions), and just assume that the books conform to that.

    ​So they miss out on things such as the varied personalities of the Solar signature characters*, and especially the Caste books, and the wide variety of characters that they present; not just varying degrees of arrogance and humility, of amorality and empathy, but just how much of an eclectic selection of amazing weirdos that they often are. It makes them small, and homogenous, and unflattering.

    ​And then they attribute those boring people vastly disproportionate power and point it inerringly at the setting, ignorant of all of its diverse wonder and the potential therein, seeing the future of Creation only as a gold-tipped boot stamping on a human face over and over again, forever, with a face attached to the end of it shrieking "conform, damn you".

    ​The appeal eludes me.

    ​* Dace and Swan have too much ego and arrogance? Really?

                The Dragonblooded have "won" before.

                In the time before history, they waged war against the hosts of the Primordial and defeated them and made the world safe for humanity. A party of Dragonblooded PCs playing in this era would be running a war campaign with special ops missions assigned by Solars and Sidereals, with great epic battles and romances, trying to forge a world where their children can rule one day. That party of Dragonblooded PCs took down Celestial traitors, they defeated demon-god generals, they rescued captured Lunar spies and brought vital Intel that would change the course of the war back to HQ. They won, and ushered in an age of glory.

                When that age of glory was done, and the Solar tyrants had grown decadent and corrupt and mad in their gleaming golden citadels, another group of Dragonblooded PCs showed up. They saw the atrocities of their rulers and the grudging complicity of their houses, and they decided that risking death and dishonor was better than allowing such evil to stand unchallenged. They had help from a wise old man with stars in his eyes, and from a shapeshifting queen who had grown to fear and despise a man she had loved when the world was young, and from like-minded family members and old war buddies and a cute but surprisingly intelligent robot made of glittering blue ice. In secret, they built a rebellion, moved forces into place, desperately murdered to cover their tracks when some Night Caste got wind that something was up, sacrificed friends and lovers and like-minded rebels by acting loyal before the time was right... they gathered the secrets of their masters to turn them against one another, and when the time was right...they ruined a dinner party in the grand climax of the campaign. They saved the world, and ushered in a new Age! Their names would be remembered for thousands of years, and they would become legends and then religious icons in the retelling. They won! And they ushered in an age of freedom.

                When that age was done, a great darkness fell upon the land. Sickness laid waste to the world, and the hungry dead outnumbered the living. The shadowlands yawned wider, and at the edges of Creation a vast and alien host of nightmares and madness began to consume all things. In Creation's hour of need, a group of Dragonblooded PCs arose. They battled with warring factions of the disintegrating Shogunate, and with Balor's deadly assassins. They delved into ancient ruins and forbidden tomes and bargains with demons in search of cures for the world killing plague or weapons with which to defeat the Terrible Gaze! They had help, from a wizened Dragon King and an ancient Anathema's ghost and a mysterious old man who gave them hints and clues because they reminded him of some other brave young heroes he had known when the world was young. They braved traps and curses and the depths of Hell itself and in the end, at the climax of their campaign, they sacrificed themselves to save Ceation, giving a weapon that could defeat Balor into the hands of a brave and pragmatic redheaded NPC the ST hadn't even named because she was meant to just be a one off captain of some local troops but the whole party adopted her as PCs are wont to do. They won! They saved the world! They ushered in a seven hundred year empire!

                Anybody who thinks a party if Dragonblooded PCs can't save the world and ushered in a new Age doesn't get how Fate works.

                            I'd say you could make a plausible story out of it, but you'd need to have a Hearth of like, multiple heads of houses, or maybe one super powerful house or something I think. It's not plausible if you end it still at war with the Lunars AND the Solars though, you'd need some kind of setup where the Solars agree not to be in charge, but to still work with you. That'd be a hard sell, and you'd never convince a lot of them if they really didn't want it. It's not beyond the scope of reason that the majority of celestial exalted could be convinced that they screwed up the last time they were in charge, so they might settle for a cushy life where they get most of whatever they want but aren't in charge at the very top.

                            Also if your Dynasts are like 20-30, I feel like that's stretching credulity, at least if you want your players to feel like they're driving the story. They're less respected, wealthy, old, and in all ways powerful than most of the other DBs in the Realm. They're much less numerous too. They're kind of cogs in the machine at that point, very important but ultimately small. Changing around a satrapy to be a good place might be more their speed.

                            If you want to do more than that you're going to have to do what more classic RPGs and honestly stories in general do and give them an out. Give them an exhaust port on the Death Star, or a One Ring to destroy. It's not a bad thing, the rebel fleet is no match for the concentrated force of the imperial fleet, even Aragorn doesn't stand a snowball's chance in hell at facing off against a fully reincarnated Sauron, it's a pretty classic story which is really fun.

                                                I keep thinking of historical examples in which younger people came to power and made significant social overhauls and accumulated great authority for themselves.

                                                ​Now, in fairness, a number of these were instances of hereditary monarchy, but that doesn't necessarily mean that they were acting without constraint; still instances in which they might need to do things in defiance of regents or minsters or the general status quo of the conservative aristocracy.

                                                ​Not to mention instances of people coming from backgrounds that were not predisposed to significant power and authority getting into the game, and proving highly adept at it; swineherds who join the lowest ranks of the palace guard and become the new emperor, and the consorts of their sons who start out as seamstresses and become rather significant administrators and legislators.

                                                ​And those not having the additional advantages that can be afforded by varying kinds of magic (which can be diverse in application in a manner that affords options even if the opposition has some that is stronger).

                                                ​One should not constrain the construction of their settings or development of their characters by buying too heavily into narratives saying that the conservative status quo is something too entrenched to be overturned, or too advantaged to be outmaneuvered; not when they've been refuted on numerous occasions in reality. For any conceivable setting in which the player characters are limited by the idea that their elders know better, there's another in which one can approach from the idea that their elders know things that have become outdated, or at the very least are sufficiently set in their ways that there are possibilities that they do not see or will not consider.

                                                ​Sure, the setup of the Realm is fairly effectively devised to cultivate conformity in each generation, but the Time of Tumult represents possibilities for significantly overturning the status quo in a manner that may be beyond the institutions of the Realm. One can play characters from a basis that their adventures in the Threshold in this time of significant change has them coming back to the Realm with ideas and inspiration for seeing it through to the Third Age that the hierarchy is incapable of considering.

                                                ​That might not necessarily be something that helps them achieve power in and of itself, but I think it's worth considering.

                                                ​Mind, depending on how one's story develops... Lea has pointed out how the Roseblack really makes more sense as a candidate for the Scarlet Throne when the Realm has been at war for a few years, a lot of the schemes of the old leadership have come to naught (and some of them dead alongside it), and her status as a charismatic, accomplished general might be the kind of thing that draws support simply because people want the fighting to end.

                                                ​Imagine some substitutions for that which could be found through the process of characters who, amidst a Realm that is falling apart, advocate for an adjustment in how the Realm conducts itself in world affairs, and some alterations to the culture and lifestyle of the Dragon Blooded.
