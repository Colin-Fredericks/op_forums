    A little political Sidereal guidance in the campaign I am in please.

    06-19-2015, 04:30 PM
    I'm playing a Sidereal in a campaign and the character requested the help of an associate for a mission he was on. The price is sending a message to a localized neighborhood god (It's a gated community) in Paragon who has been acting up or causing some small troubles for the assocate (sidereal).

    I have a couple of ideas of what to do and I am unsure of how easy it would be to implement.

    My Sidereal is currently does not have a lot of social charms, my fault, and only has Icy Hand/Palm (the charm that makes bureaucrats do the right thing) and Fortuitous Fellowship.

    My first idea is to use Fortuitous Fellowship on the people living in that neighborhood/gated community to create a social policy against this local god, goading them to burn down his shrine. However, with the Paragonese oaths, how viable is this? Would the oaths prevent them burning down his shrine?

    2. Find out everything that has gone wrong in that neighborhood, grab an unemployed city god in Yu-Shan and use Fortuitous Fellowship to build a cult for that god and set him up as a competing god. How much paperwork is there in offering that unemployed god such a cult or position, and what can the original god whose cult and domain are being cut into do about it?

    3. This could maybe be factored into part of 2, but use Icy Hand to speed through any investigations of that particular god's actions and get him censured/rebuked.

    I am open to other ideas as well.

    Thank you,

      It would be pretty problematic to have them burn down his shrine, because then they would all suffer the effects of their oath and be in excruciating pain (or die) unless they presented to a magistrate.

      2/3 are the better ideas, though you could use Fortuitous Fellowship to create a social policy to ostracize or not to pray to that god. That's less of a danger to the people than inciting arson which probably won't do much but piss the god off frankly.

              Originally posted by Uknown DarkLord View Post
              My first idea is to use Fortuitous Fellowship on the people living in that neighborhood/gated community to create a social policy against this local god, goading them to burn down his shrine. However, with the Paragonese oaths, how viable is this? Would the oaths prevent them burning down his shrine?
              They absolutely will not prevent it, but they will punish anyone breaking the law in Paragon (by, say, committing arson) pretty harshly. If I were your GM I would require some fairly mammoth lifting to provoke a Paragonese population to riot; the Perfect has them very cowed.

              2. Find out everything that has gone wrong in that neighborhood, grab an unemployed city god in Yu-Shan and use Fortuitous Fellowship to build a cult for that god and set him up as a competing god. How much paperwork is there in offering that unemployed god such a cult or position, and what can the original god whose cult and domain are being cut into do about it?
              I would advise against this. This isn't "sending a message," this is "starting a long-term, grueling turf war between rival deities." The deity in question might not even know why someone has started a turf war with him.

              Question; what exactly do you mean by "sending a message" here? That can mean anything from "let this city god know he is on notice" to "murder the balls out of him." What are you expected to do here, exactly? Is it your responsibility to make the god stop acting up, or simply to threaten or cajole him and that fulfills your end of the deal?

              I am open to other ideas as well.
              Violence can work. This guy isn't even the City Father of Paragon, he's just some podunk neighborhood god, right? Slap on a Resplendency for disguise, walk into his sanctum, and comment on what a nice place it is, it would be a shame if anything were to happen to it. If he doesn't get the message, slap him around a little, make it clear if he doesn't play nice the next visit is going to involve broken kneecaps.

              "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."
