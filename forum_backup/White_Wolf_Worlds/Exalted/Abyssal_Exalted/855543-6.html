                                                    Okay! For the reference of everybody who thinks that a Deathlord can/can't easily replace an Abyssal, here is some math:

                                                    How many candidates does a Deathlord have to choose from in a given day? Well, our own planet has some 7 billion people, and on an average day 151,600 people die, according to Google. Factoring that out, we find that we lose about 0.002% of our population every day, worldwide. Let's assume that Creation has a similar rate of death to our real world, which for reference it would not. In the real world our biggest population centers live in close proximity to advanced medicine and clean food and water, considered luxuries by the vast majority of Creation. Also, we do not have actual monsters trying to kill us, just each other. And while terrorist attacks are scary, do they kill more or less people than fairy raids? Lots of factors, but 0.002% seems conservative to me.

                                                    Now, how many people are in Creation? If anyone has a hard number I'd love to hear it. The best I could find was that the Blessed Isle has 100 million people following the end of the Great Contagion. Now that is the smallest of the five Directions, but assuming each Direction has at least that many people then that's roughly 500 million people in Creation. If 0.002% of them die a day, then that's 10,829 deaths each day.

                                                    Now how many of those people are Solar material? One suggestion was one in every hundred, which would mean a Deathlord with an Abyssal exaltation has 108 souls to choose from on an average day that might make a half decent Abyssal. If it's more like one in every five hundred, then that would be 21 candidates. If it were one in every thousand, then just eleven. And if only one in every ten thousand mortals has the qualifications to be a Solar, then that's just one candidate a day, Creation-wide.

                                                    On top of that, you need to match the exaltation's caste to the person. Now I have absolutely no idea what percentage of people are warriors, diplomats, priests, sages and thieves in Creation, but assuming an even split in potential you would take the above numbers and divide by five. So 21, 4, 2, or one every five days, depending on how uncommon you think Solar-worthy heroism should be. That's assuming they have their first pick of course, meaning no other Deathlord is currently in the market for a Deathknight. Which should be pretty rare I would think: they aren't exactly easy to kill.

                                                    Now to me, these look like pretty expendable numbers. Like if I'm a Deathlord, I think I'm gonna live to see the end of time, and I'm willing to take my chances that with this many candidates popping up a day, that in any given week I'll find a really promising candidate just ripe for a bargain. And if not, there is nothing stopping me from creating a little chaos here or there, building up the death tolls and blaming the next Deathlord over if this produces a promising champion. As for my little problem Abyssal, well if I kill them directly through their monstrance then I loss the exaltation for a bit, which could be a problem, but nothing is stopping me from infecting them with the Plague and sending wave after wave of Bone Lions to wear them down and free up my prized black exaltation for a new, more compliant host.

                                                    This is of course assuming that they rebelled in the first place, which is hard to imagine when you consider that at some point I brought them to my home in Stygia and gave them a stirring lecture with at least Performance eight and every solar/abyssal charm I qualify for, encouraging them to devote their life to my cause. Then, I sent them downstairs to meet my boss, who after looking into the depths of their soul and maybe even infecting them with Whispers, determined with infallible accuracy whether or not they believed in at least the core spirit of our cause. And if they had been found wanting, they would have died and their exaltation would have been returned to me at that time.

                                                    ALL OF THIS needs to be addressed moving forward if we are going to have any hope of playing free Abyssals. Ignoring its presence will not make for a better setting or splat, it will just leave these problems for the next wave of simulationists to find and suffer through.

                                                    TL,DR: There are plenty of heroic mortals dying in Creation, any one of which could replace a rebel Abyssal.
