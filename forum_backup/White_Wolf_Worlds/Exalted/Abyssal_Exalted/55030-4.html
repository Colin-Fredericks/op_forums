            I have to say that I like Abyssals to be doomed. I like that they start out as weapons with no choice but to murder the world - willingly through their actions or unwillingly through Resonance. What I don't like is that the story ends there. The Abyssals are too doomed. There are no escape clauses in their contract except for redemption into a Solar. They can't branch out.

            Strangely, the best example of how doomed I want them to be is Elsa from Disney's Frozen. She's got powers and they are destructive to everyone around her - she freezes the whole country into eternal winter on accident and almost kills her sister. However, she kept it together for years practicing discipline and in the end manages to figure out how to control it completely. In the meantime, those powers are wonderful and useful - she builds a massive ice castle in a matter of moments. Now, she gets off a little easy - it is a Disney movie after all - but that's sort of what I hope Abyssals can be - people with at least a third option.

                The "third option" is a very attractive idea, but as an end state it's destructive to (my preferred interpretation of) Abyssal themes because it makes redemption superfluous.                             Originally posted by Schleiermacher View Post
                            If you have the option to avoid your doom and remain an Abyssal, there is no argument to be made that being an Abyssal is something you need to redeem yourself of.
                            That's the crux of an Abyssal player's conundrum. He wants to play an Abyssal, not a Solar. Otherwise, he would have skipped playing an Abyssal and played a Solar. So, from a playability perspective, Abyssals must be a splat that you can play without being forced to turn into another splat. A player should not feel bad or punished for playing an Abyssal instead of a Solar.

                            I think we need to change what we mean when we talk about "redemption" for Abyssals. The "sin" that an Abyssal needs redemption from is not being an Abyssal. Their "sin" is agreeing to kill the world. An Abyssal can be a tempted being, more able to solve problems with indiscriminate murder, but if he wants to recant on that promise without becoming a Solar, the game should let him. Sure, make him take the hard road. Deny him some of the most effective tools. Let his Deathlord see him as a traitor to the cause (or maybe not - some of the Deathlords really don't want Creation gone). Have him bear some pain for denying the purpose for which he was created. Just don't make him go gold instead of black.
