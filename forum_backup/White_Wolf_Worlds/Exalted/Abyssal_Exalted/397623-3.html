    Originally posted by Mizu View Post
    I'm thinking that maybe this was one of those cases where certain writers working on the book didn't like what the guy in charge of the book was going for, it would explain why part of the ST section suggested just saying the Deathlords were full of **** and couldn't actually kill you by tossing the Monstrance into Oblivion if your group wanted to run a renegade abyssal game.
    And page 42 says that if an Abyssal's Monstrance is tossed into Oblivion the Abyssal dies.

    I remember Neph discussing this not too long after Abyssals came out. What happened was, it had been recognized that the Abyssal Killswitch was dumb back in First Edition, and when 2nd edition was being written, it was planned to remove it. Abyssal killswitch was no longer supposed to be a thing. So Neph wrote in the book about the Monstrance's destruction restoring an Abyssal's willpower, because that's how it was supposed to work. Unfortunately there were some communication issues, the authors working on Chapter One and Seven hadn't been informed that this was changing, so they mentioned the Abyssal killswitch (even using that direct term in Chapter 7), and it wasn't caught in final editing.

    So we ended up with two rules - the one in Chapter One/Seven, which says and Abyssal dies if his monstrance is throwing into Oblivion, and the other rule in Chapter 4 that said the Abyssal regains his willpower when his monstrance is destroyed. But it was intended that Chapter 4 be the official statement on what happened when you destroyed a Monstrance.

                    Originally posted by Murcushio
                    What I hope is that they still have some sort of interesting mechanic that ensures or attempts to ensure that they do what the Neverborn want'em to do. This is partly because of verisimilitude, which is important to me as a player and as a GM, and partly because aesthetically speaking I really hate the idea that you can sign onto Team Nihilism, get awesome death powers and pseudo-immortality, and there not be really, really powerful strings attached.
                    Sidereals are probably more function-oriented by far than Abyssals, and they never needed such a thing. All they needed was being given powers that ensured they could only do what they were supposed to, and a presentation that made playing a Sidereal within their particular idiom was compelling and credible.

                    I imagine that Abyssals might still have their own modified version of the Great Curse to emphasize their deathly, infective nature and also create in-character incentives to adopt all sorts of deathly trappings and create drama around things like having a child or answering to your living name.

                    But I don't think they need anything to make them feel like they're doing what the Deathlords (and implicitly the Neverborn) want other than having powers that do those kinds of things and writing a setting that instils the desire to play such characters. They don't need a leash, they just need a sense that the methods and agendas of the Deathlords are reasonably diverse such that almost every character you can play as an Abyssal is fulfilling it one way or the other.

                    This also requires the Neverborn, or at least a sense that they're constantly demanding the end of the world, to take a step back, so that such Abyssals can comfortably feel like they're ending the world incrementally, which both enables and removes the pressure from characters who would fulfil and resist. I mean, I still like the idea of Redemption as an option for Abyssals who really no longer want to be harbingers of the eschaton, but I also want Abyssals who are harbingers of the eschaton while still being basically decent people, and more overtly demanding Neverborn and controlling mechanics kill the latter of those. The Neverborn need to back off so one can play a pleasant, cheerful, even noble character who is all for the idea that everything is going to be dust in ten thousand years, without being a lazy bastard.

                    The Abyssals can be a fulfilment of the cold, grim desires of the Neverborn, but if the Neverborn are actually in Abyssal stories than it makes it hard to have any story featuring Abyssals that isn't about ending the world right now.
