    Abyssals, New Take [redux]

    12-10-2013, 12:01 PM
    My first post was a vast jumble, so I decided to redo it, try to make my arguments more clear and valid.

    This is just an exercise. I don't really see anything coming from this.
    Abyssals annoy me. In the wide wonderful world of Exalted, why do we have this one splat that seems convinced in putting up a stock villain? You have three paths with them: Loyalist dark death murderman, anti-“hero” murderman (i.e. murder-hobo), and redemptionist “I’m really just a Solar in black makeup.” Why should Abyssals be so restricted? Abyssals are of the Void, Death, and Undeath, they bring that to Creation just by existing; they don’t need some outside mandate to bring that to Creation by an arbitrary Resonance murder count.

    The point of this post is to scrap Abyssals and rebuild them from the ground up, specifically to avoid these problems with current Abyssals, (arguments in spoiler tag):
    1. They’re one dimensional Villains. We need to round them out, let some be healers, musicians, kings, rulers, and true heroes while still being Agents of the Void. (Murder deathspark is still favored, don’t worry.)
    2. Their current tie to Solars diminishes the Solar story. A connection between Solars and Abyssals is interesting, but not in “here’s the fallen paladins.”
    3. The “devil’s deal.” Having to agree to be “evil” just reinforces how “evil” Abyssals are. Isn’t it interesting to play someone who didn’t want to gaze into the Abyss?
    1. One dimensional villains.
    As it currently stands, there’s only three viable paths for Abyssals, all very limited. Hopefully this will change in 3e…but from the kickstarter spoiler, I’m not holding out hope. Basically, you’re either going to be a Loyalist murder villain, an anti-“hero”, or a redemptionist. The first works just fine, it’s what they’re tailored around. The redemptionist is a Solar story, not an Abyssal one. You’re a Solar that starts out in black makeup and sheds it. It’s okay as a story, but a heroic Abyssal shouldn’t be forced to be a Solar to be heroic.
    That leaves the anti-hero. I’m not a big fan of anti-heroes from the beginning; but the Abyssal version doesn’t even work for me. “I only kill bad people.” Yeah. Sure you do. You’re just a villain pretending to be good. Disagree with me, it is an opinion after all, but even if you do, it’s still a very limited role.
    So, basically, you have two workable Abyssal concepts, that are both narrow. This needs to be changed.
    1. The current Solar tie.
    The thing I love about Exalted is there is no morality system. Nothing definitely says you’re good or bad. Solars are both the greatest heroes and the worst villains of the system. Or, that’s how it would be if Abyssals weren’t fallen Solars. Oh sure, lore says that the gathering of Solar shards was completely random, but we all know what Abyssals are: fallen paladins, deathknights. Abyssals have the whole “redemption” storyline, where they become good again and therefore a Solar.
    All this starts to draw implicit connections: Abyssals=evil=inverse Solars, therefore Solars must implicitly be good. I know the books try to make it clear this doesn’t happen, but it’s not effective. Western society is still hung up on black and white morality, DnD, and Lord of the Rings. You have to take care when you’re trying to subvert these preconceptions, and unfortunately when you put the Abyssals into the “evil villain” camp (see complaint number 1), you also separate Solars into the “good hero” camp.
    There should be a connection between Solar and Abyssal, but it shouldn’t be a “fallen” Solar type. Rather, it should be a sibling-type, “I can see him in me.”
    1. The “devil’s deal.”
    Aside from Infernals, no other Exalt type requires you to agree to serve your new God. It just…happens. I don’t like it for Infernals that much either, but it’s not as intrusive there: the Yozi already have some control issues. Aside from the Ebon Dragon, they all have an issue with this whole “free will” thing, considering how they got stung with it in the past. Even then, all you’re doing is agreeing to serve the Yozi, which in itself doesn’t really have clear goals, and can be interpreted in good or bad light: the Yozi, are neither good nor bad. It’s just a different master, particularly to someone who might find the rule of Yu-Shan unfair.
    For the Abyssals, though, you have to agree to destroy the world. If that’s not pure evil, I don’t know what is. That’s not a good start to a well-rounded splat, and just reinforces #1.

    With all that said, here’s my attempt to rework them from the ground up. Because I can, I’m tearing it down to the ground and ignoring all but the core concepts, much like 3e. It’s going to be in a more “developer” voice, and will probably have some holes in it since it’s just an idea.


    The power of the Sun is so great and well defined, that even his absence takes form and has power of its own: his shadow alone is a mighty God in itself. This glory was likewise passed into his greatest works: his Solar Exaltations. For each Solar that he created and imbued with his power, a shadow of their greatness was also formed; an aspect of darkness, but wonderful and powerful in its own right.

    The Sun was aware of these shadows and cast them into the Void, that empty space that formed when the Primordials created reality out of the Wyld. They were supposed to stay, were it not for the Neverborn and the Deathlords.

    The Neverborn were cast into the Void, and became aware of these shadows of the Solar exalts. However, they could do nothing with them, as beings of unreality they had no power over reality. That changed with the Deathlords. Imbued by the Neverborn with the force of the Void, but containing just enough remnants of Creation, they were the bridge between reality and the Abyss. With the whisperings of the mad Neverborn guiding them, the Deathlords forged these Solar shadows into Exalts of their own rights, forces made from and encapsulating the void—the Abyssals.

    The Abyssal Exaltation is based around the shadow of the Solar Exaltation. Like the Solar, it is guided to those in their moment of greatness. Unlike the Solar, though, this candidate must have a connection to the void. More often than not, this simply means that they are dying. Perhaps their moment of glory was one of self-sacrifice, and the Abyssal Exaltation comes upon them as they lie in a pool of their own blood. But there are other ways. Perhaps the person is void-tainted, or in their heart lies a darkness in itself. It matters not what the connection is, as long as there is something for some seed of Abyss in the candidate.
    Another key difference between a Solar Exaltation and an Abyssal Exaltation is that when the Sun chooses his exalts, he can peer into their soul and see if their moment of glory was based upon true heroism or selfish greed. The Neverborn and the Abyssal Exaltation cannot, or rather if they can, they do not care. They will Exalt those regardless of their intentions—heroic or villainous.

    The Abyss in these newly exalted cries out inside their souls. They are driven to fill the void in whatever way they can. Many destroy what they can in rage of the consumption in their soul, but others try to fill it in whatever way they can. Some, even, on looking into the Void within themselves, realize how precious reality is and work to preserve it.

    The Abyssal Exalted were created to be servants and agents of the Deathlords and Neverborn. In theory, that means they are quite restricted and commanded by their “superiors.” In practice, though, the Abyssals have a great reign in their actions. The Neverborn and Deathlords are often content to just let the agents of the void exist in Creation, for they are confident their Abyssals will bring about the destruction of the world simply by existing and being an anathema to all of reality. Occasionally, though, the Deathlords do attempt to assert their authority over their deathknights, to varying degrees of success.

    “New” possible concepts.

    The Hedonist.
    The Abyssal saw the void, and realized how wonderful reality is. Like a starving man who finds even the simplest bread a glorious meal, this Abyssal revels in everything that reality has to offer. Everything to her is the best possible thing, and they spend their life consuming and trying everything possible to pleasure herself.

    The Musician.
    When the air is filled with nothing but music, it blurs together into nothing but meaningless sound. The musician knows how powerful the empty notes are, bringing emphasis to that lone note. They inspire through the love of absence, forcing people to treasure more of what they already have.
