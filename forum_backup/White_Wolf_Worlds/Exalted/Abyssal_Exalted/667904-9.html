    I definitely think that there's a point at which people discussing practical solutions to problems or collaborating on implementing those solutions should take a straightforward, pragmatic approach. After reading Seeing Like a State, I developed this image of a scene opening on a Solar king crouching down to comb through the soil with their fingers while a local farmer casually lays out what the recent problems have been and the king make out-loud observations and aside questions... I've grown attached to it as an idea of what a Solar who's genuinely for the people would be doing. Similarly, accounts of people like Napoleon or von Blucher exchanging ribald jibes with the rank and file, asking after their conditions, occasionally tasting their rations. It's... warm.

        but I actually like that comic because to me its the juxtaposition between mystery and mundanity: to me its mysterious but to them, its normal and mundane and I like it for that. when I want to see a setting, I want to know whats normal for that setting so I can know what the baseline is and how to interact with it in a day to day manner- exceptional circumstances are well that: exceptional circumstances. my reactions to those circumstances are formed from them happening in game and deviating from the normal living. to me its awesome something like that can be mundane, because it shows how people can consider anything mundane and its only when we look sideways at it all and realize that we're currently communicating cross vast distances using devices no larger or smaller than books while also using them to play videos and games that we realize how awesome the thing is when we mostly take it for granted.

        to me its saying "I'm a mortal technician, and I'm so used to this horrific undead stitching stuff and whatnot, that when a Daybreak comes up to and asks for help with it, I'm like whatever, because I'm that cool. Sure any lesser guy would be cowering in the corner, but me, I've been doing this for years, you learn to get used to it, because I'm so cool, I can find this boring. Making undead monstrosities, talking with Daybreaks, gathering up dead parts? just another day on the job. I deal, thats all."

        I mean, I'm sure there are spirit exorcists out there who stop being all fearful or emotional about their job to. Not everyone gets super-emotional and invested into everything they just learn to live with it and get things done.

            If it was a scene from Alchemicals and the Deathknight was a Champion I would have zero problem with that scene.

            As is, the The Seven-Degreed Physician of Black Maladies is a deathknight with Charisma 1, Compassion 1, a penchant for torture, forced body modification and taking villagers full of people and slowly vivisecting them one at a time while the others watch so he can build a spine chain from their corpses. Outside of the mentioned torture, he has zero social skills beyond four dots in manipulation to mitigate his awfulness. He lives in a Manse that he calls the Sanatorium Sepulchral that is literally his evil lair.

            Having a mortal or ghost assistant speak to him the same way my mechanic would speak to me when telling me that my truck has some damage and it will take a week to get a part in to fix it strains my credulity past it's breaking point. Maybe it would sit better with me if he was torturing the guy or his family because he didn't follow the deathknight's orders on how to repair the rhino zombie. Maybe a better scene would be him teaching enslaved ghosts how to be necrosurgeons by having them practice on their own corpses first. Maybe a better Necrosurgury comic would be the guild delivering a bunch of slaves to his Sanatorium Sepulchral and the majordomo signing off on their receipt and when someone refers to them as slaves the majordomo responds with "They aren't slaves, they're supplies" as some stitched up zombie monsters come out to drag them down to the laboratories.

            There's a lot of ways the Necrosurgury chapter comic could have gone and I think "Deathknight brings his Rhino in for a check up" was boring and immersion breaking.

                                    so, i may have figured out a bit of backstory and info on brutality now, let me know what you think of it.

                                    He serves under the first and forsaken lion.

                                    He was the champion/executioner of a minor kingdom in the south.
                                    He was chosen for his great size and strength, later on he showed his lord that he was not just a big brute but an expert in psychological warfare.
                                    However after loosing a important battle his king ordered him bound in chains and locked in a dark room.
                                    Above him drops of water would fall on him (barely enough to keep him from dying, not nearly enough to quench his thirst) and delicious food where placed just out of reach (he was feed a few scraps of it after it had cooled down and become disgusting to taste).
                                    It was in this darkness that he would have faded away...
                                    The First and Forsaken Lion had other plans though.
                                    The man that was to become Brutality, was offered a chance to once again rampage across the battlefield this time serving a master that was worthy of his loyalty.
                                    Brutality accepted and thus now he was once more free, he left the burning ruins of the city he had once bleed and committed atrocities for to serve the one that freed him from his prison, the one that made him Unbound!

                                    here's a bit of picture discussion so to speak.
                                    At first i had thought about giving brutality a couple of manacles around his wrists with broken chains, i dropped that idea, he does however still have a metal choker around his neck that was once attached to a wall in his prison.
                                    the two metal plates that are attached to his shoulders are either there as punishment or he had them attached himself as a sort of vow that until he has managed to free his master from his metal prison he will have the metal plates attached.
                                    the gallows on his back is a pretty horrifying artifact, it works kind of like those torture cages in Yu-Shan, except the victim only needs to have the noose around his/her neck(it only works when the noose is tightened around the victims neck and once it is it burns in such a way that no one can keep it on voluntarily, so no using it to become immortal in battle).
                                    The gallows is used to punish those that dare to betray the Lion, making brutality a walking reminder to all who serve the lion of what will happen to them if they dare to betray him.
                                    The last part just felt fitting, because even though the deathlords relations with the deathknights is supposed to be that of pupil and master (if i have understood it correctly) not that of slave and master.
                                    It felt fitting for the lion to have some way of reminding his servants exalted or not that he's the Lion, not a kitten and those that forget it gets to hang from brutality.
