    Fair enough. I guess I'm just trying to think as a GM, "How would I make her useful/interesting" vs. "How did the writers/artists portray this character", and they aren't the same thing.     Creation may not, as a whole (though certainly some places probably do; Creation has more land than Earth, and a myriad of different cultures, each with its own mores).

    However even if Creation generally does not, the real world does.

    The real world is a context in which the game exists, and the people who wrote the game should be mindful of this.

    There is a huge difference between saying "all imagery which could possibly be interpreted as erotic should be eliminated" and "blatant sexualized/fanservice media that effectively play into real world notions about the badness of women's sexuality have no place in the books." People need to stop conflating the two.

        No, no, I get the difference. I'm just concerned that trying to eliminate the one thing [fan-service art] will lead to the other [sanitizing of the products]. It went too far on more than one occasion, but it got the hobby on a road to recognizing that the audience includes a lot of adults, and that sex, violence, religion, and other sensitive topics shouldn't be taboo in RPGs. Putting a Muslim terrorist, who uses their version of their religion as a weapon, in an action RPG doesn't mean you think all Muslims are terrorists [they aren't]; putting a female character who uses sex as a weapon in an RPG doesn't/shouldn't mean you see all women in a negative light. The problem is a lack of balance, in ALL forms of media.
