    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
    Not trying to specifically rag on you, but if this is all they got, I don't think they should be playable, and not much would be lost if they weren't.

    It's equally unpalatable if the whole reason for loyalist Abyssals to exist is for them to go renegade and seek redemption.
    Important things to be aware of? First, Semicasual, who Raziere is paraphrasing and agreeing with, said these are "some" of the options, not all of them. Second, the guys you're responding to are fellow Exalted players and storytellers: they've got some ideas they like to use for loyalist Abyssals (and some of 'em are really popular playstyles!), but assuming their favorites are all that the developers intend to support is probably incorrect because, as you say, it's a rather limited palette.

    Here's an off the top of my head list of archetypes Abyssals take on in my stories.

    1. The Slasher: I mostly use these as antagonists, because I'm a Storyteller, but every now and then you get that player who wants to be Belkar or Black Mage, and Abyssals are usually right up his alley. I'm rolling "the Joker" into this one, because let's be honest, there's not much difference. Xykon fits here too: methods differ, but when your goal is Evil for Evil's Sake or Evil for the Lulz, Abyssals are your guys.

    2. The Messiah of Death: Nihilist buddhist wankery is one of the great joys of Exalted evil. Death as release from the cycle of agonies that is reincarnation can be a noble goal for a certain sort of player, who enjoys moody philosophizing. Sometimes they spend tons of time agonizing about the pain that the world forces them to inflict to free people from still greater suffering and whether it's worth it, sometimes they just want an excuse to murder their enemies. Either way, fun to play in much the same way an introspective Zenith usually is.

    3. The Necromancer: Lot of genre territory here. Some players want to do the whole "conquering army of the dead" thing, some people want to do the "obsessed with immortality and willing to bathe in the blood of virgins to get it" thing, some people just want to do the traditional wizard "I want to unlock the secrets of the universe" thing with a deathly flavor.

    4. The Speaker for the Dead: Abyssals as envoys and defenders of the dead can be loyalists and still fulfill a vaguely "good guy" sorta role. Again, there's a lot of different character concepts playable in this area-I've seen Deathknights who are all about forging the souls of slavers and rapists and drug traffickers and abusers of various kinds into soulsteel because "just because Creation has no natural justice in the afterlife doesn't mean I can't deliver justice unto the wicked and grant the righteous a better life in my Kingdom", I've seen players who are all about trying to connect the living and the dead so that they get along better (Silver Prince and Bishop loyalists are big into Ancestor Cults and can be loyalists and True Believers without wanting to murder their ghost friends' families right now, because it's not like they're not gonna die soon anyway).

    5. The Usurper: You can be loyal to your Deathlord and still think the Neverborn are asshats and screw those guys. You can be loyal to the Neverborn and still think your Deathlord is an asshat and screw that guy. This sort of player enjoys skullduggery and sneaky politics (and, in my experience, likes to wax poetic about Vampire games past) and wants to take over the Boss' job because they think they can do it better or they think they can have more fun doing it. Just because you're not a renegade doesn't mean you don't get to have the fun of plotting against your Dark Masters! That's the whole point of having Dark Masters in the first place!

    6. The Obsessive: Deathknights have gothic imagery for a reason, and this archetype is one of those reasons. Sometimes it's fun to go with the over-the-top interpersonal drama and trappings that go with death. Whether you sold out Creation to save your child, or you study Necromancy in the hopes of one day bringing your lost love back to life, or you sacrifice screaming soul after screaming soul to the Mask of Winters because he holds your boyfriend prisoner, or you legitimately fell in love with the Lover one day and you want to go out kill the world and bring its heart to her on a silk pillow, the players who enjoy this sort of character really like playing up the whole "what won't I do for X," where X is someone important to them, thingy. These players also often enjoy playing Lunars with a difficult Solar Mate, in my experience, and their stories are often tragic (unless the object of their obsession is another PC? For some reason those usually seem to work out better for the main characters). A subtype is the revenge fantasy, which is the same thing inverted: "Am I willing to murder this orphanage to get to the guy who slew my father? LET'S FIND OUT!"

    7. Baby Death: It's not as common, but I've had players who like the Messiah thing and like the Usurper thing, but take it to a different place. They're the guys and gals who go "Wait, why am I Death's Chosen? I wanna be DEATH." These are the players who, rather than trying to bring peace and an end to suffering, decide to personally embody Death. They do it for different reasons (sometimes it's because they chafe at being subservient to Deathlords or Neverborn less in touch with the Void than themselves, sometimes they do it because Death is the only enemy worth conquering, sometimes it's because trying to become death is somehow comforting in the face of its inevitability, and in least one case it was because "Fuck Saturn, that's why!"), but they try to embody the principles of Death itself. They anthropomorphize it and try to behave as Death does. Sometimes this ends in becoming a horrible Avatar of the Void whose breath causes stars to fall and blood to cool to ice, sometimes it ends in eating the corpse of a Neverborn ("if they didn't want to get eaten, why'd they give me these fangs?"), sometimes it winds up with taking a more "Death is part of nature and the Void is not, therefore I shall kill this unhealthy Death and fix the natural cycle" approach. The players who like this sort of thing also tend to enjoy playing Infernals who go in for Shintais, and some Sidereal players are drawn to it to for the poetry and the arguments about the nature of Abstract Principles like Death.

    8. The Outcast: Death is unclean. We burn the dead or hide them away. We bribe them with false gifts of wooden toys and clay statues so they will not come to us asking for our assistance. We try to put them out of our minds. We dread someday becoming like them. Players who want to play Abyssals as outsiders often go for the Appearance 0 mummyfaced guys as opposed to the high-appearance vampire seductresses. They like to look at what it would feel like to be dead when you care about what the living think of you. They like to be ugly, and either tormented by their ugliness or perversely proud of it and fond of shoving it in the faces of the living to say "just wait! Someday you will look just like me!". These guys like Abyssal medicine, usually, and they're fond of diseases. Sometimes they become champions of the downtrodden, of caste-system untouchables or criminals or the mentally ill or the Jackal tribes. Sometimes they become champions of the dead, like in Number 4. But what they're all about is railing at an unjust world that hates and fears them for being what they are. Sometimes they love the Deathlords or Abyssal allies who accept them, sometimes they resent them, but it's all about social status and bruised pride and bugs crawling out of rotten flesh with these guys. This is a playstyle that's a little harder to pull of with other Exalt types, because "you hate me because I'm gross" is usually more an individual thing than a splat-thing with those, and you don't get magic gross-powers for being a gold or silver colored Anathema. Formerly beautiful seducers and formerly diseased lepers seem about equally common in this group.

    9. The Inevitable: Some Abyssal players in my experience like the idea that Death is inevitable, and as its champions so is their victory. These guys like Resistance charms. They like doing the thing where you just. Do. Not. Die. Sometimes they like playing Full Moons or Resistance focused Dawns. But Abyssals do it better, because you can beat an Abyssal to death and he claws his way out of the grave several days later and he's back. They're great as NPC antagonists (I absolutely love the Abyssal "play dead" charms!), but some PCs absolutely love being essentially unkillable because they've "got an arrangement" with Death. Some people love to play the tank, and the phrase "But...I killed you!" is music to their ears. Usually the character has some sort of personal goals to which they're very devoted, and this one tends to overlap pretty well with players who are into the Obsessive, because being the Determinator tends to appeal to the same sort of mindset.

    10. Dead Sexy: There's nothing wrong with wanting to be a vampire ladies' man or a fell succubus of the night. Cliches get to be cliche for a reason, and that reason is usually mass appeal. Abyssals are a good way for the players who want to be dark and dangerous and brooding and sexy to find something in the game that appeals to them. Most of 'em in my experience have more interesting backstories (and often more dramatic tragedy-filled backstories) than our friendly local Lady of Boobs and Skulls, but there's no denying the mass appeal of this archetype. There's always that one player whose answer to the invading army is "I can seduce their general," who dislikes the Immaculates more for the celibacy and holier-than-thou attitude than for the trying-to-kill-me, who enjoys telling people about what their character is or isn't wearing. I do not wish to denigrate people who want to play social-focused Abyssals who like to get kinky. You can get this sort of fun playing a Serenity or a social-focused Solar or a naughty Lunar, but Abyssals are all about the sexy danger in a way that the others rarely match...and half the fun is playing "who is going to get the other one to betray their friends first" when playing cat and mouse seduction games with seducers of other Exalt types.

    ...the hour grows late, and now I wanna play Abyssals.

            I kind of hope it does change: it's a bit restrictive on character concepts.

            Anyway, to the OP, it sounds like the problem is just lack of variety. Black and skulls, again. So, I think the suggestions of a wide variety of cultural views of death that people have made are good.

            Though it depends a little on the STs idea of the trappings of death. Does a hunter bedecked in lionskins count? He's wearing literal dead creatures, but it's hardly a philosophical treatise on the nature of death.
            Whereas, say, a funerary priest, or a poet who writes tales of death, or someone whose skin is marked with a wasting disease... well, they're not wearing the trappings of death.

            What about a Jiang Shi? They generally just look Qing officials, but dead. Does dressing like a Qing official count?

            On the philosophy of life-hatred, again, it sounds like the issue is just lack of variety and subtlety.

            I hope (and am somewhat optimistic, though not entirely) in 3rd ed we'll get a lot more variety.
            Though, saying that, lots of variety isn't necessarily required: better discussion of the philosophy would also work. I mean, the Bishop's Shining Path is a proper philosophy, based on Buddhist ideas of the bliss of non-existence, but everyone else is just "Grrr I hate life!".



            But it also sounds like this is an issue with signature characters. Signature characters, even though they're just 5 random people who are not given much info, often inform the way people see the splat. The Abyssals on the front are pretty generic black-and-skulls. The Day caste is literally just a guy with white hair wearing black clothes. And this is an issue with the Abyssal art generally (and, weirdly, for such generic guys... none of them actually looks like the Grim Reaper!).
            There's a lot more variety that could be done: pharoanic death-masks, cloaks of human skin, death-Buddhists, people who actually look like undead monsters (Falling Tears Poet does, but you can't see it because he's masked) who can wear whatever because they still look like undead monsters. Tattoos or face paint to take the form of the undead. Etc. Rather than just black-and-skulls.


            Though something to say about the black-and-skulls: one of the issues is whether the design makes sense. Take chain-tits. That's not her actual name: we call her chain-tits because she looks so ridiculous. Why does she wear chains across her breasts? Isn't that terribly impractical, and uncomfortable? But no one complains about Falling Tears Poet because it makes sense: he looks like a zombie, so obviously he wears robes and a mask to cover it up.

            So half of the issue could just be solved with better character design rather than an entire rewrite.

                                            What I suggest is you imagine yourself as a regular, everyday person in Creation. Imagine your quaint village, your nice, happy family, and your quiet, happy job. Imagine your sexy wife/husband. Imagine your side guy/gal that you bang hard as hell when you get a spare moment. Come up with names and appearances for them, just like you were making a PC. Write some short stories. Do a doodle of them or two...


                                            Then imagine seeing a Dusk Caste walk over the hill and slaughter everyone in front of your eyes. Imagine the screams, the pain, and the anguish as you watch everything good in your life destroyed. Imagine what it would feel like to see that gothy, Dusk Caste plant four feet of steel in your spouse's skull. Think about what it feels like to be the last person to die.

                                            When a daiklave is run through your belly, I doubt your last thought would be "Wow, that's an ugly outfit, girl, why you showing off 'dem boobies?"

                                            It's easy to make light of death, pain, and suffering until you are staring it in the face. Just a thought.
