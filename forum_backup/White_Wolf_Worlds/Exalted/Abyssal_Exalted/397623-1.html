    Originally posted by The Hug Ninja View Post
    On an individual basis that's essentially meaningless. The Abyssals may replace the Deathlords eventually, but that hardly matters to the Abyssals on the recieving end of a capricious 'You Have Failed Me' moment.
    Does your Abyssal have to stick around to be Vader-choked if they don't want to? Because as I recall, they don't. They can decide that their Deathlord sucks and bail out of their service and short of actually hunting them down, the Deathlord can't do a damn thing about it. (I may be wrong about this. I seem to recall that the Deathlords have an extreme sanction on-hand. Something involving destroying the Monstrance? But I recall that had a huge downside for all involved.)

    Unless the Deathlords can somehow enforce loyalty by thinking about it, their Abyssals are no more their "on demand" lapdogs than a Dragon-Blooded in the Vermillion Legion is the Roseblacks "on demand" lapdog. They can always pick up stakes and head out on their own. That might get them killed, mind you, but that's the flip side of being part of a larger society.

    What the Neverborn would want is irrelevant even if we assume they're fully fledged actors rather than tomb bodies that occasionally groan something meaningful.
    In the context of 2e, we don't have to "assume" that. They explicitly are conducting complex diplomacy with the Yozi's and disciplining their subordinates in concrete, logical fashion. I would submit that that's fairly dispositive.

    They have stolen Exaltations that just might not accept certain paramiters and their means to alter them came from the Yozis, who even if we assume acted in completely good faith despite incompatible long-term goals have failed spectacularly in their attempts to leash their own servants.
    The simple fact that Abyssals can be returned to Solars is far more destructive to their long-term plans than 100+ Abyssals doing whatever they want save for the limitations of their charmset.
    I do take your larger point that the Neverborn can only twist the Solar shards so far, and that you can use this to justify a lot of limitations on their control over the Abyssals.

    However, there comes a point where it would be breathtakingly stupid for them to deploy those shards if they can't exercise some degree of perceived control over them. You reference the Yozis and their failed leashing. But the Yozi's did try to leash them, and that leash makes sense within the context of Malfeas and how he thinks, which is that yelling and screaming and beating people up is the way you enforce obedience to his perfected Tyranny.

    I don't see the Neverborn deploying the Abyssals without an attempt at a control mechanism. Given what happened the last time they tried that it seems like they'd go "No. No way, no how. We'll simply sit on these shards for eternity rather than rolling those bones again."

    That said, the control mechanism should be a hell of a lot more fun to interact with than the 2e control mechanism was. Now, from a narrative standpoint? The 2e control mechanism was a thing of sublime beauty. "You're going to destroy the world for us. No, really, you are. It literally doesn't matter what you do. It's baked into your being. No complaining! You signed up for this, and no backsies. You can try and run if you want, but in the end you'll do what we want. It's in your nature."

    The downside was that it wasn't fun. It wasn't interesting to interact with. There was no way to make it work for you or to trick or stunt your way around it, and unlike the Limit Breaks it was really hard to embrace it in a productive way.

    That, I submit, is the part that has to be fixed. I know a lot of people really want Abyssals to be completely free as a bird and be able to fuck off away from the Neverborn's agenda with no consequence, but I've always found that idea to be both illogical from an in-setting standpoint and something of a cop-out from a meta standpoint. You're playing a weapon aimed at the throat of the world. Your character agreed to be a weapon aimed at the throat of the world. That should carry a more meaningful weight to it than "you get awesome death powers that have no downside." If you don't want to play a weapon aimed at the throat of the world, perhaps Abyssals are not the splat for you.

                                    Okay, so. apparently, I can't read. Aside from it being a complete failure of my information-handling skills in general, it means I've run no less than two games completely wrongly and they didn't turn out the way they should have, from my perspective.

                                    I mean... wow. That idea is just so bad I must have blocked it out. I absolutely stand by my opinion there should be a leash mechanism, but giving that kind of absolute whip hand to the Deathlords is insane. It's unfun and of dubious utility from a playability standpoint and more to the point can't be engaged with constructively. Is every single Abyssal that wants to go rogue supposed to orchestrate a Monstrance heist? (Monstrance heists are pretty cool, but... every time?) That's idiotic.

                                    Regardless. I was wrong on the facts. That's on me. I'll try to do better. I withdraw all comments about Abyssals being lapdogs-on-demand, as they... kind of are.
                                    Why? What are they going to do? Kill them again?
                                    One of the constants of Creation is that things can always get worse.

                                    But more seriously, the Neverborn would presumably prefer their agenda be advanced. Loosing a bunch of Solar shards, even tainted ones, into the world with no guarantee they'll do that seems like it is a dubious proposition, especially since those shards will be bound to people who are suddenly near-immortal fonts of power. You know what near-immortal fonts of power like to do? Keep on existing so they can exercise that power in ways pleasing to them. Taking action to preserve Creation (in which I include the Underworld) runs squarely counter to the Neverborns agenda.

                                    If I were a Neverborn, I would be thinking "sending these guys out without some sort of mechanism to at least sort of guarantee they'll do as we want means that there's a good chance our tombs will eventually be hooked into some sort of awful, terrifying Death/Life Engine designed to turn our own power against ourselves and preserve their endless Empires of the Dead and Damned."
                                    "Let them go if they want to go; let them be free. Eventually, they will bring darkness to the world, as surely as we, the Solars once did."
                                    —The Bodhisattva Anointed by Dark Waters
                                    The Silver Prince is a pseudo-intellectual douchenozzle. But aside from that, his statement is only true in the context he made it, in which the Neverborn did, in fact, build in a mechanism to guarantee that the Abyssals would work that way.
                                    Unless they really want to die for real, and not just sort-of half die like what happened last time.
                                    Well, in fairness, they'd be thrilled if that happened. The Neverborn want to properly die. That's the whole point of their agenda, really. They would like to drop into Oblivion and end. If an Abyssal came to the Neverborn and told them he could kill them for real, he would be honored as no other before him, and bestowed with great blessings of power and gratitude as he ushered his patrons one by one into a true death.
