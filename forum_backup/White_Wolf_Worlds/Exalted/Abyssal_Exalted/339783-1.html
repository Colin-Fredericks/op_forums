I, for one, think it would be kind of cool to see a newly redeemed Solar kind of have to develop a new relationship with that which shackled him during his time as an Abyssal.

                    A redemption path should be a path of atonement with the UCS. Atonement means to make an individual -at one with- their god. So the biggest part of this will be finding a way make yourself at one with UCS.
                    Step 1: Save 10 lives for every life that you have taken in the service to the neverborn.
                    Step 2: Find your Lunar Mate (may be done while working on step 1)
                    Step 3: Take your soulsteel panoply and destroy it sending the souls trapped within to Lathe to be washed clean. Swear an oath to the sun not to use Soul Steel ever again.
                    Step 4: Undergo Spiritual Purification, Abstance Fasting from food, water, violence, sex, pleasure, for an extended period of time and look outward upon the world. (This changes your virtues from there Abysssal nature to the way the Living understand them). This takes a number of days equal to your highest virtue
                    Step 5: Spend 1 month in meditation, prayer and substistance fasting in a place a solar would meditate, and meditate upon the glory of the sun. Durring this time you can not prepare food, but can eat as much as 1 egg (with salt) per meal just enough to keep you going. You may have sex but only with your Lunar mate, if they are willing. The goal here to to focus your mind upon the glory of the sun and again away from you, to bring your soul and your exaltation into alignment with his.
                    Step 6: Endure agony, and overcome death. What? You thought the fasting was the hard part? Hell no. You just called in the fire of the UCS to reignite your exaltation, child your soul feel like it's on fire as your burned from being a Creature of Darkness back to child of light. Your exaltation is free of the Neverborn at the start of this step...you just need to survive this...without the use of your charms. This will require an endurance check with a difficulty of your essence. Fail and the death you dodged bends you over the barrel, your exaltation goes free and finds a new host. Succeed and enjoy being a solar.

                                    Abyssals as metaphors for fear, specifically fear of death:

                                    "I dislike death, but there are things I dislike more than death, therefore there are occasions on which I will not avoid danger." - Master Meng (Latinized as Mencius)
                                    The stereotypical phobia therapy would be exposure treatment - gradually learning to face your fears, rather than back down from them. Having the psychiatric qualifications of a small table, though, I can't say whether it works or not.
                                    In the overall metaphor, that would be renegade Abyssals repeatedly coming face to face with their own potential deaths, enduring the hunt of their former colleagues - running up against the forces of death time and again - and eventually they realise: they're not afraid of it any more. If it comes down to dying, or watching others die, they'll hurl themselves in the way of a blade, smiling as they do so.
                                    And that is the moment golden light flares, and the Neverborn tremble in the depths of the Labyrinth.

                                                To do this...
                                                The Abyssal unearths the secret history of Creation that was old when humankind was new, that it might write the incomprehensible name of the forgotten deity.

                                                It puts to rest the thousand shrieking, murdering Nephwrack's that colonize the Neverborn's skull.

                                                It brings the missive of strange devotion that was gasped out a dying primordial's maw, ten thousand years ago. It takes this message across impossible distances to deliver it to the ears of a Demon Prince who will weep to hear the impossible words, and swear eternal revenge on the Death Knight that dares to bring it to this humiliation.

                                                It burns in ritual a book of Necromancy born from the buried gods catacomb body; though Necromancy is lessened the world is perhaps made more safe.

                                                It travels to the chittering, clamorous, spiteful cities of the Aluan and brings a message of serenity to the Plasm-Creatures, and inspires them to take up rites and observances long forbidden since their time of death and exile.

                                                It gathers bits and pieces of a shattered being from these experiences, and a thousand-thousand more. It learns alien psychologies, it is twisted in body and mind a dozen times over and each slow recovery is achingly worse. It invokes names and prayers and benedictions that will mark it exile and Nemesis to a thousand heroes.

Where there was rage there is quiet, where there was horror there is rest. Perhaps even peace?

                                                    What I would go for in a redemption path would depend very heavily on the particular Abyssal. I feel an achievement like this should be very personally guided, reflecting the character's conviction, laying to rest the mortal and Abyssal lives once led, and affirming the new life yet to be lived. Not to say some of the big, grand ideas in other suggestions can't feature, but completing them wouldn't be necessary or even the point in this approach. It's that the character takes up the path upon deciding that the deathly entity he is is less than the person he could be, and that he is not deterred from walking it, even upon finding how truly difficult and painful the journey is. I do like the bit about a better understanding of the Virtues, but I'd go more for TheCountAlucard's take; the character doesn't need to actually increase Virtues to gain that deeper understanding,* though it may be an appropriate step on the way.

                                                    Whether a successful character manages to succeed and becomes a Solar, the Exaltation returns to the golden state it once held as the character passes into Lethe, or even the act creates or causes something entirely new... It's all down to the particulars of that story.

                                                    * If asked to choose who was braver out of two characters consistently standing to face some supernaturally frightening things, one character with 5 dots in Valor and the other with 1 dot, I think I'd pick the latter in that situation. It takes a certain kind of courage to have everything about you screaming at you to run away (represented by probably failing the thing's supernaturally enforced Valor check with your 1 die) and then force yourself to stand up to it anyway (represented by spending that Willpower).
