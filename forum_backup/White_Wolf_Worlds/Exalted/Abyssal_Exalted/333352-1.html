    I see a lot of the same comments so I'm going to post here in general and I apologize if your specific comments are not addressed.

    1: Abyssals are not Solars. They have and deserve themes beyond the Solar narrative of "infinite cosmic power and indefatigable pursuit of mastery." This is not a bad theme. Solars do this well, people love it, it's written well, it is NOT what every exalted story is about. Being an exalt should make you feel like a badass, yes, but that should not be the sum total of your experience as an Abyssal, as a Lunar, as an Infernal. These should be different stories. Abyssal narratives are very much the tragic hero narrative, where not every attempt to do good is thwarted but every step forward comes at a known price. Perhaps my idea is not the best way to represent this, if people have ideas on how to improve it then I'd love to hear it, but Resonance is a great mechanic that is every bit as essential to the Abyssal narrative as Limit Break is to the Solar. An Abyssal without a Dark Fate is not an Abyssal, period.

    2: There is a lot of valuable stuff in the underworld, both in the sense of epic loot and in the sense of narrative. Ghosts have plenty of story hooks and plot devices to use, a story set in the underworld can be very very engaging. It's just that no one ever pays ghosts any mind because they are "just ghosts" in much the same way that mortals are ignored for being "just humans." They only become scary after about a thousand years of down time. What I had hoped to bring to the table with my idea was the popular notion in Creation that some people come back as "super-ghosts" that are immediately terrifying to everyone, even the celestial exalted. That fear would make ghosts matter in much the same way that Solar exaltation makes mortals matter. That is the aim.

    (Sidenote: I actually really like the description of Deathlords as Kryptonian vampires. It's oximoronic but so are they sometimes.)

    3: Deathlords should not be undead Yozi. I don't mean to say that the Yozi are not good charm fodder, they are, but rather that the method and justification for the charmset establishes a theme I dislike being used more than once. It worked very well for Infernals in Ex2 because these were stories about people who had sold their soul to some flavor of "the devil" and paid the price in the form of eternal servitude and forced mutation into something altogether inhuman. This is Faustian, this is scary, this is very very on point for Infernals. This should be an Infernal theme, not an "evil solar" theme. Abyssals can be more than Infernals with soulsteel chasing.

    4: Adding more flavors to the Deathlords does not solve all the problems that plagued the Abyssals in Ex2. It is something that should be done and I'm glad they are working on it, but it is not a cure-all. The Deathlords serve the Neverborn in their eternal struggle to end all life. Having 36 flavors of "I kill a dude" was not good enough to make Abyssals distinct in Ex2, it won't be enough to make them distinct in Ex3. "Like Solars but more kill-y" is not a good enough splat concept. Everyone kills things in Exalted. Abyssals deserve to be infused with more than that and there is plenty of room to explore new concepts if we are given narrative license to make characters unbound from the Deathlord's ultimate goal of "undo Creation." Until we get that license Abyssal concepts will always be limited, that is my problem, that is what I want fixed.

                    Here's the thing about playing independent abyssals in Ex2: the amount of resources the deathlords can and would muster to pursue a traitorous abyssal would put the Wyld Hunt to shame. The deathlords know that a free abyssal could overpower them if they grew strong enough, they have had solaroid exaltations before so they know what to expect, and if they have your monstrance then they can freely kill you at any time they choose. I agree with Godjaw that it would be fun to allow this angle for players who want to pursue it or to take up the extra challenge, but making it the norm for all is putting a tall, tall hurdle in between you and playing a free abyssal in the setting who is still going to be alive at any time in the near future.

                    I think one thing that is missing from this discussion on resonance is that it doesn't punish you for using your powers, it punishes you for using your powers the wrong way. This is the core of the abyssal tragedy: that an external but innate force which cannot be ignored is forcing them to act a certain way, and acting against it will require sacrifices. This does not mean you have to abide by resonance rules, but breaking those rules is part of the drama, the conflict, the pathos of an abyssal exalt. The rules are there and we expect people to break them, that's why they are fun. If you want to talk about actual limitations talk about their charmset, which had fuck all besides murder charms going for it in Ex2.

                    And ghosts in Exalted already are interesting. Read the fluff on the lords of Stygia's districts sometime, those are some very well thought out characters. The whole Underworld book is chalk-full of interesting NPCs, and you'd be hard pressed to name even one of them that showed up in a campaign because if they did they'd be hammered into soul steel within seconds. Normal ghosts are not a threat to the exalted. Which means that compared to all the horrifying stuff that is they take a distant backseat in terms of importance. The problem is that the characters also feel this way, which is why there's basically no exchange between the Underworld and other realms besides the Deathlords. They simply don't matter. Until you build ghosts up to be significant somehow, they never will find major purchase in the exalted setting.
