    Abyssals as Champions of Oblivion

    03-31-2016, 12:48 AM
    This isn't quite as simple a topic as it might seem. First, this is a suggestion for one aspect of Abyssals, but not the only one. The question as I was thinking of it is, what's the problem with the concept in the past? Quite simply, the problem isn't the concept but how Oblivion has been presented both in and out of character as a horrific and malicious force. So to even make the concept viable, I think we have to get rid of that assumption.

    So, what can Oblivion be? One of the definitions in the dictionary is the state of being forgotten or disregarded. THIS is something that fits with some of the base ideas of something no one has come back from. That makes it very easy they're being forgotten or disregarded. In this case, the Abyssals are the champions of those who are forgotten and disregarded. Considering the nature of the Underworld, this could easily apply to the dead, but there are those among the living who would also count.

    This concept also implies what Whispers could be. The constant wishes and needs of people. Those things the people are either unable to do, or feel unable to do (for reasons of it being 'wasteful', pointless, or any number of other choices). The exact mechanics, I'm not sure of, but... if you go out of your way (Abyssals only) to fulfill their desires and wishes, somehow the world will pay you back. Maybe a living person feels a debt, even if they don't know you did it. Circumstances might make things fall into place that ease your plans forward. The idea is it makes smaller side stories and help keep the main ones moving.

    The one thing I like about this is... the Neverborn aren't "stuck" but resist the pull of Oblivion. If they give in, they will be forgotten. Their deaths will be disregarded. Everything was for naught. How can they give everything up, just so their suffering might end now?

    Does anyone have thoughts or feelings on this or ideas they want to add?

      One way to look at this:

      The Mouth of the Void is not the cause of the Underworld but it is a natural reaction to it. The broken dreams and regrets of the neverborn spun out to form a hungry shadow of Creation that saps away its vitality, draining it of souls, essence, and focus on the future. Ghosts are people in similar circumstances who add to the Underworld and weigh down Creation.

      Oblivion is a letting go of the past, forgiveness of old wrongs and the fading of old wounds. Without its slow entropy Creation would have suffocated under the sheer mass of the horrors in its past. The Neverborn clogged it up by being too big, too powerful, and too dynamic to pass into the past. They are in terrible agony sure, but they would rather their names be carved into all flesh and monuments vaster than mountains be raised to them than pass on. Now the Deathlords are perfectly loyal to the general cause of their masters while also being selfish and petty.

      So the constant work of the Abyssals is to keep things moving, resolving all those things that keep ghosts from moving on, destroying all the reminders of the past that strangle the future, and keeping what they can't get rid of buried so it doesn't restart old patterns of violence. So they might suppress the true history of a country built on genocide to prevent the same ethnic tensions from flaring up and leading to a new generation of ghosts with unresolved issues appearing in the Underworld. In some ways this also leads to stagnation because people can never learn from these things.

      The most "Enlightened" of such knights of Oblivion might advocate or practice something like Zen Buddhisms that advocates freedom from attachments and time.

              The idea that I had when I talked this over with Haren earlier was, basically, that "Oblivion" is not actually a thing.

              What is perceived in the Underworld as the Mouth of the Void, 'the Maw', is just Lethe. It's a letting go, a surrender, a forgetting,-- something that the Neverborn simply can't do, something that is forever denied to them by their very nature and their inability to forget what they were. Even when a Primordial goes through fetich death, after all, its own nature is carried on in some lesser fashion and that's the closest that Primordials come to 'death' unless you count the half-existence that the Neverborn currently enjoy. It's their own twisted perception of their fate that creates the twisted corruption of the Labryinth, that twists ghosts into Nephwracks and other forms of Spectres, that empowers Deathlords to tear down Creation as a giant scream into the Void proclaiming that they will not be forgotten. That they will scour the world rather than letting go of it.

              They like to imagine that if they destroy everything, if they destroy everything, then they'll be peace.

              That's not what would happen, though, even if they destroyed everything,-- they would still exist, as would Oblivion, eternally trying and eternally failing to consume them. All that remained of Creation would be the Neverborn's pain, their hatred, their twisted shadow memories of a world that no longer even exists... an eternal never-ending Labryinth, a nightmare reality not even populated with anything other than themselves, simply because there's nothing else left to wander the Labryinth and experience the Neverborn's nightmares. That is the goal that the Neverborn push forward to with their hatred and their rage, and deep in their hearts, they know it. So do the Deathlords, having once been Solars, and having thus known the ultimate futility of their slain enemies and the tragedy of their continued existence.

              Then... we have Abyssals...

              Lethe given a darkly regal form, the incarnate existence of a Solar given over to dark majesty by the very act that killed the Demiurges themselves, carved bloodily into the fabric of Creation and the Underworld with the murder of the Neverborn,-- twisted by those dark and undying gods, for sure, but also something more fundamental. An imperishable perfection every bit as stirring as that of their cousins that still dwell in the light: its no coincidence that Abyssal and Solar are mirror images of each other. It couldn't be any other way, because it wasn't the corruption of the Solar Shards that created the Abyssals or any "Monstrance of Celestial Portion", no. Abyssals simply are, inevitably so, and when the Deathlords first pried open the Jade Prison they found that some of the Shards had grown darkly majestic during their imprisonment.

              Waiting for their time to come, the Dowager of the Irreverent Vulgate (in Unrent Veils) thought, cooing lightly as she stroked the darklit spark that would be her Shoat. But, what exactly is it that they've been waiting for? What is their purpose and whose goals do they truly serve?

              That's why she never claimed more than one Abyssal, why she doesn't let her singular servant get too powerful: she doesn't trust these Champions of the Void.

              Is their goal truly to destroy Creation, she ponders, or are they merely the final fulfillment of what the Neverborn truly desire.

              A force that can finally end their torment; that which can kill those that cannot truly die.
