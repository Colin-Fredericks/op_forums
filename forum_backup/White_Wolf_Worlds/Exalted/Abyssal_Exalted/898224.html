                  What it means personally is for individual STs and players to decide. Thematically I think people like Isator Levi , Octopoid , and The Revenge of TV Head all have the right of it. By getting redeemed, an Abyssal affirms the ultimate power of life over death. Pretty huge slap in the face of entropy, there.

                  In my own campaign, Abyssal redemption, or "Taking Wing" takes two forms: Goldwings and Bonewings.

                  Goldwings are those Abyssals who forsake the Black Exaltation entirely and become Solar Exalted again.

                  Bonewings cleanse their Exaltations of the Void and become the Exalted of Death. Stewards of the underworld, protectors of the dead, bringers of relief from suffering. Nominally neutral, the Sepulchral Exalted are essentially dark heroes who defend the necessity of death and decay as a source of mercy, memory, and renewal.

                  Just under one third of all Abyssal Exalted will become Sepulchral Exalted, which is easier to do than becoming a Goldwing, of which there will be maybe 10. The remaining Loyalist Abyssal Exalted, perfectly happy to be life-negating shits, will go to war with the Bonewings to reclaim their Exaltations for the Void. The Bonewings will want to do the same to the Loyalists.

                                      I'm not sure we need the narrative of redemption for Abyssals anymore, tbh.

                                      Abyssal redemption existed in a world where Abyssals were sharply limited by Resonance and Monstrances, and where the purpose of Abyssal was straight forward (murder the universe). 3rd Edition Abyssals, who serve more complex Deathlords and follow the chivalry of death, are less narratively constrained. They likely don't have Monstrances or something similar to chain them in servitude. If an Abyssal can fundamentally do what they want without getting hit on the nose with a newspaper (or getting slapped with no-save auto-death), there is less need for giving them an "out."

                                      To put it another way, a Solar can do what they want with their power, good guy, bad girl, capitalist, whatever. The same is true for Lunars and Dragon-Blooded. If Abyssals too are that "free" then really we just gained another generally playable Exalt type, and why ruin that with the judgmental notion of "redemption?" Redemption says there is something wrong about Abyssals that needs to be changed, and implies a degree of failure if they choose to eschew that path.

                                      Redemption is an interesting narrative, of course, but it can be stifling for PC Abyssals. In this case, Abyssals narratives might become larger by actually removing the choice itself. If an Abyssal doesn't have the compulsion or the option of becoming something else, maybe they can focus on doing what they want with the power they do have.

                                      2nd Edition Abyssals was a choice between being Oblivion's bitch or slipping the leash by becoming Solar.
                                      3rd Edition Abyssals can be an infinitude of choices by simply leaving a Deathknight to their own conscience and ambition.
