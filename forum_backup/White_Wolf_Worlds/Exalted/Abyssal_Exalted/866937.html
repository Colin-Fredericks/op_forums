    I need suggestions for an Abyssal concept

    04-17-2016, 02:11 PM
    I'm conceptualizing an Abyssal Exalt who I think I can make into a rather deep, multifaceted character, but I'd like some outside input.

    This is what I have right now:

    This Abyssal Exalt is someone who was 'born in the wrong time'. He is someone who believes in morality, integrity and true romance. Unfortunately, he was born in an era of moral degredation, perversion, weakness; where civilization is failing slowly but surely. He can only see selfishness and hedonism where he wants to see chivalry and temperance. He wants to see true beauty instead of pornographic depictions of desire.

    All hope he had of reversing this dreadful malaise is gone and has driven him to suicidal depression. I'm not sure how he is to die, but I'm thinking of him throwing himself off a cliff, perhaps. It should be during the depths of a depressive fit, so as to make the offer of the Deathlord even more perfect.

    When he is Exalted, he'll be like a vengeful ghost (I was inspired by the vengeful ghosts of Japanese mythology), with the power to finally end the spiral of moral and civilized decay. In his mind, he'll be purifying the world; washing it clean of the filth it has fallen to.

    He's going to be of the Midnight Caste, but I'm unsure of who his Deathlord patron should be. I was thinking that The Lover Clad in the Raiment of Tears might be an interesting patron (perhaps out of a perverse sense of irony), but I'd like my character to be more or less loyal to his Deathlord patron. It'd be best if the Deathlord wasn't incredibly nihilistic, so I was thinking that the Black Heron might be a good patron, but I'm unsure.

    Any kind of critique would be appreciated.

          Okay, here's a few things I noticed that might be spots to check up on.

          One of the overriding themes of Exalted is a simultaneous use of Moral Relativity and Might Makes Right. In other words there is no one god or law defining morality, there are HUNDREDS...and the one we currently listen to is better than that tribe across the river's since we regularly kick their asses. This level of thinking goes all the way up to the current "King" of the gods who only got to his position by coop and usurpation. It is therefore very difficult to suffer true Righteous Indignation (TM) unless you A. have a large powerful organized religion with specific beliefs that encourage the pursuit of righteous purity, and B.have the society around it grow distant as it's needs turn away from the things it usually got out of religion (i.e. it's hard to follow the god of grain when you don't grow food any more). However, creation is one of those places where the gods throw rocks through atheists' windows, so you can't really move "beyond" the gods in any way to matter.

          It is however possible for your god to move beyond you.

          Consider this gradual darkening of people's morality to be the result of a change in the guard...the local deity (the one your Abyssal worshiped devoutly since he was young) has been transferred to another department and the new guy is a bit more hedonistic and laid back. And while most of the people accepted the new guy with gusto, your character misses the good old days when good was good and bad was bad. That should help refine his particular problem beyond "life sucks and I want to kill myself"

          I'd also consider throwing a bit of thwarted romance into this tale...you mentioned a lot of chivalric themes and I feel these aught to be explored. Give him a Dulcinea to pine after, have him wax poetic of her beauty and agonize over how to express his love for her. And have her offer to sleep with him within one conversation (why not, she didn't have anything more fun planned...) And that's what attracts the Deathlord du jour to him...the insistence upon the cold clarity of virtue over the lifegiving embrace of another actual person.

          While the Lover would be amusing, the greatest slap in the face would be the Silver Prince (aka he who manufactured his own messiah story and currently reaps the benefits of organized--and very obviously fake on this side of the curtain--religion). The Lord of Skullstone's other advantage is that he's not particularly nihilistic, focusing more on a hostile takeover of creation rather than feeding it to the maw of oblivion. This of course gives your Abyssal something slightly less evil to work towards while still coming to grips with the magnitude of his mistake. (and yes, saying my life is more important than the lives of EVERY SINGLE PERSON IN THE WORLD and I will now actively aide in killing them all--especially for a purportedly virtuous person--is a mistake)
