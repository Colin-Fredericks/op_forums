                                                11-19-2023, 04:53 PM
                                                Anyway, my thoughts on the first half of the first chapter.

                                                Okay, good introduction to the concept. I'm glad that it's not shying away from the fact that becoming Abyssal has eschatonic connotations, but still elaborates on the nuances to how and why one might embrace that. The comment about secrets is particularly compelling, that idea that one might see the offer as an opportunity to gain deeper insight into the nature of the cosmos.

                                                And lexicon providing a good hint at the new approach to the Underworld. I imagine the ritual afterlives will provide some basis for still having areas that bear a resemblance to one or more places in Creation, while the primeval ones seem like something that can make the ghostly experience very vivid for Abyssals to engage with. Something that sets potential empire building apart from the likes of the Realm in a big way.

                                                Hmm, intrigued by this line about the Deathlords being entrusted with the Abyssals. The implication that the Neverborn have their hands on the reins a bit more.

                                                Glad that the iconography of the Monstrance of Celestial Portion is still present, to distinguish these Exalted and provide something tangible for how the Deathlords engage with Choosing them.

                                                I find myself thinking about how a more nebulous Underworld will help allow the boundaries of shadowlands to finally make sense. I could never really get where the place one would normally cross into in the Underworld would go at night.

                                                I like what Revolutionary Girl Utena says about the tone and pageantry that will be evoked by Abyssals, a bit more of the surreal romanticism over just being bleak and grim. And yeah, Dark Souls has been creeping into my hopes for this for some time now.

                                                Ah, okay, so the basic history behind the Abyssal Exalted is still the same. I'll admit, the escape of the Solars still coming from the Deathlords getting hold of the Jade Prison is a bit more conventional than I would have liked, not least because I genuinely do feel it reduces some of the mythic impact of their returning to Creation at an hour of need. Still, I'll at least say that it paints a very vivid picture of the process utilised by the Deathlords to achieve that feat, the grandeur of their combined mystical efforts that would be necessary to acquire it. And I am ultimately glad that the Neverborn are presented as not really being able to function as articulate or calculating masters. The picture of what the Deathlords have done since the Usurpation is also nice, a good balance of being active but cautious in the face of who could oppose them. I do umabiguously still like the feature of the Yozis being involved, the kind of unbreakable deal behind how they'll get their own Exalted.

                                                I recall some people wanting to expand the parameters of the Bleak Exaltation, but personally I think the premise is vivid enough to stand on its own, that it would be distilled a bit if there was something like a metaphorical death it could go to. It's such a strong set piece, and it gets to be uniquely their own. I like how some of the snapshots get to convey a sense of the perspective the Deathlords might have on their potential Chosen, mortals that impress even them in the manner of their death. The one whose first thought goes to the duty to slay birds is oddly endearing.

                                                I think there's something worthwhile in the idea that the Abyssals are more at home in the Underworld, but a careful balancing act to make that premise diverse and ultimately playable; I've often thought about that point of how one doesn't want to discourage the wandering lethal swordsman over the person who builds missiles in shadowlands. I think presenting a tone of how the living world doesn't seem comfortable or natural to them would be an effective way to accomplish that, something where it's perfectly possible to play an individual whose adventures are primarily in Creation, and they're kind of grim and moody the whole time. On that note, I have a good feeling about presenting the obligation to the Neverborn by way of the Great Curse; something to feel less like a pressure that builds in them when they don't follow certain rules until it explodes and more like an uncanny weirdness that haunts them for trying to deviate from the dread oath they swore.

                                                And the thing about adorning themselves in trappings of death... I think that touches on how for all that it can tell serious stories with deep characters and real messages, there's still something a little bit silly about pulp. The idea that they're most comfortable if dressed in funeral garb or hanging out in mausoleums, it's like... it can be over the top in a way that ought to be embraced, trying hard while being unashamed of that. At the same time, I do think it creates a very visceral sense for what the Abyssals need to go through to function, with a nice sprinkle of the native mysticism of the setting to let it be something people can portray in-character with a straight face. On the other hand, I think one can get a lot out of how their Essence Fever isn't "destroy everything" so much as "be urged to your most impressive while your most intense emotions and darkest impulses are at work". The kind of thing that can let one play an Abyssal who isn't some all-encompassing monster, but has their personality flaws and something that empowers them along those lines.

                                                I am glad that the presentation of past lives does seem to emphasize some of what I wanted of Abyssals as the presentation of Solars returned from the grave, that sense that the memory of the Usurpation can be vivid enough to inspire the newly Chosen to embody that vengeance or reclamation. An organic way to embody the motif, while also being able to emphasize the character of the actual newly empowered person in question.

                                                The idea that the Deathlords see the Abyssals as people to instruct in their ways has been talked up for years now. The presentation of the Deathlords being enamoured of their Exalted as the most capable agents for achieving their own dreams is the sort of elaboration of that which I would have inferred from the similar modification to Sidereals. But the idea that they are motivated and struggle to form intimate relationships with them is the most surprising thing and feels like the major game changer. For one thing, it gets to me hard owing to a recent historical kick that has emphasized just how much being a liege required cultivating a personal relationship with vassals, so that awareness and using that to provide depth to the premise is pretty cool. And I'm somebody who's always thought that the Deathlords should be more ghostly than just people in scary costumes, so I think this presentation actually not only makes that stronger, but imposes an interesting and compelling challenge upon them. I think there's so much that can be done to make Deathlords a major and versatile presence in the experience of playing Abyssals that they recognise a need for cultivating a genuine sense of loyalty, and have their own challenges in accomplishing that.

                                                And indeed, if one has many different Deathknights, one might need to present a few different faces, or at least variations on the same face.

                                                I find the elaboration given to the debut of the Deathknight to make a very strong case for making their first meeting with their liege something to play out, rather than relegate to the backstory. The journey a unique adventure in its own right, that can culminate in something to let the Deathlord make a very strong first impression. Also seems like a great hook to bring circles together, to have multiple separate journeys that confer before arriving at the lord's citadel. A grand tour of the Underworld following training is all well and good as a device to start introducing ghostly society, but I like it most as something that gives a bit of nuance to the culture and methods of the Deathlords, makes them feel a bit more like thoughtful people, and even implies a bit of attachment to their domains.

                                                Overall, I think a very strong picture is given here for the manner in which Deathlords build a cohesion for their Exalted, to bind them into larger organisation and cultivate their most useful talents. It lends credibility to how and why Abyssals might be utilised in that manner, and a solid picture for what players might actually do in that position. Indeed, I already see an aspirational hook to being started out with the more minor assignments and wanting to prove oneself capable of the greater responsibilities or more autonomy.

                                                I hadn't expected the chivalry of death to be something so significant to the Deathlords themselves, but seeing it laid out like this almost makes me think its cooler on them than the Exalted. For a start, I really like the picture of how for all their power and terrible intent, there's something inescapably grim about being a Deathlord if you need to be occupied for ages trying to discern the nature of the commandments bestowed by gods who are dead and dreaming, especially when the punishments for failure are so horrific. I like the potential it lends to how Deathlords are occupied, the enigmas that can come from whether any given manoeuvre accomplishes their own goals, the directives of their masters, or is just about trying to deepen their understanding. I've always been a bit uncertain about the First and Forsaken Lion of limited commitment, but the notion that he still prioritises his own endeavours while being gloomily bound within the restrictions and obligations of his own liege is a great way to bring a lot of life to that. And yeah, I do think it's a great way to provide a foundation for their different characterisation as masters among the dead that remains distinct from something as obvious as patronising different forms of death.

                                                As for the substance of the chivalry of death, I'm all for something that pushes the ultimate culmination of Abyssal goals off into the furthest future, to give them more room to play around in. As somebody who was distinctly unimpressed by the whole "Chosen of Murder" thing because murdering the majority of people was super easy for the most basic powers they had, I love the notion that the Neverborn seem to have a specific preference for focusing on the most challenging targets that will have the biggest impact over indiscriminate slaughter. Mind, I think that itself can imply some more elaboration over merely killing, even after a challenging fight; to target somebody who is no great trouble physically, but has an impact on the lives of others that makes them worth gradually dismantling in order to create shockwaves through a community. I think that can go far darker for somebody that is into that, while providing for a lot of intricacy and investment over the single dice roll that could end them at once.

                                                Although sure, breaking down the boundaries between living and dead also provides a huge amount to work with that might be less bleak, and can instead emphasize the gothic beauty of the Underworld and the strange serenity that can come from embracing death, the motifs of accepting its universality.

                                                I think the end of describing the Abyssals on board with the deathknight role is a good breaking point, I'll get back to the rest of chapter one later.

                                                    I am so impressed with the Deathlords. The changes will take some getting used to, but the bulk of those "changes" is making their histories and true identities foggier and open to storyteller design. In particular, the ill-fated romance between Mask of Winters and Walker in Darkness was almost the extent of the characterization I remembered of either of them beyond Big Bad Evil Guy. But seeing them in context WITHOUT the history really made them unique. In particular, I love Walker as this seductive gothic romance paladin. To me, he really stands out as the beauty and chivalry of death, an impression I didn't get in previous editions.

                                                    The complete rework of Eye and Seven is expected. They are practically a new character, but honestly its for the best. Anyone overly attached to their.. questionable presentation in 2e can probably somehow fit that backstory in. I might reference it if somehow it becomes relevant in any way. But honestly, Eye does a much better job being an actual character this time around. The Deathlords absolutely NEEDED a cackling mad scientist.

                                                    Likewise, the alteration of the Black Heron makes her feel much more useful in play as an actual character rather than a subplot that serves to make two relatively boring Deathlords into a servicable idea. Her entry being the first really hammered home the idea that these Deathlords have been refreshed and remodelled in a good way. And the Deathlords NEEDED a serial killing harlequin.

                                                    All in all, much like the Dynastic Houses, I can actually get some mileage out of literally every Deathlord this time around. Choosing a liege for my Abyssal NPC is actually somewhat of a struggle, and isn't just defaulted to the nearest one. The only Deathlord I wasn't as pleased with was the Silver Prince, because his rather pointless and silly "Im going to pretend to die, then pretend to reincarnate and come back later" subplot was kind of neat, even if I couldnt for the life of me explain any reason for him to do so. Maybe it IS for the best that it was trimmed off. Can't wait for the Underworld writeup!
