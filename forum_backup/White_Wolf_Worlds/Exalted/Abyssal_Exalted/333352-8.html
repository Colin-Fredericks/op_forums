    Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
    I think my list of acceptable resonance eruption effects would include:

    non-damaging but very obvious stigmata; aura of the pariah where social actions besides intimidate fail or are heavily penalized; non-damaging scenery damage like bleeding walls, peeling paint, rotting food etc; implementation of interesting but non-lethal taboos such as "can only survive on blood" for a set period; temporary memory loss or memory loops similar to ghosts during a haunting; heightened emotional states either for you or for people around you; exaggerated aversion to sources of life such as Creation, children, sunlight etc
    These are good ideas. Mainly because they neither completely remove playa agency and shut down story (like 2e Limit) nor act as a pure punishment mechanic to direct the narrative in a narrow way (like current Resonance) nor punish you for using your divine-granted powers (like current Paradox).

    I see "normal" Limit Break, e.g. Solars and Lunars, being best described as expressions of the archetypal Greek hubris or other transcendent madness born in the heads of people who stand body-lengths above mortals in their deeds and capabilities.

    By contrast, Resonance seems to me like it works better as something that bleeds into the environment from the victim, rather than existing in the victim's own mind only. It taints the world with the All-Consuming All-Death that is the Abyssal's privilege to wield. Some of these suggestions open up story and plot ideas for both Abyssal PCs and NPCs, versus 2e Resonance being simply getting hit with an egg for having an individual personality or pursuing a goal in a way that plays to your strengths.

    Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
    Triggers: acting out of turn with your own desires, like a ghost trying to ignore it's fetters and passions. I like to imagine the exaltation super-charging your lower soul which freaks out if you don't do what it wants, hence the resonance effects. I'll admit that this mechanic is very very similar to the existing Great Curse though, and might need more to make it distinct. Ideas?
    I prefer not to get into the too-specific Exaltation mechanics that 2e insisted on exploring. But other than the "super charging your po" part I could get behind this as long as it does not require the Abyssal to be predictable or have a psyche that is mostly static (like a ghost).

    Originally posted by Rater202
    Resonance comes from neither the Death Lords, Nor the Neverborn, Nor the Abyssal's Exaltation. Rather, it is drawn from the paradox of a living-even immortal, being channeling the power of Death and perhaps Oblivion itself.

    If one preforms strong acts that affirm life over the dead without taking proper precautions, the resonance may build up and lash out at you. It should hurt you and you alone, or maybe cuase small plants to wilt. But that's it.
    Really cool ideas.

    Definitely can get behind the 2nd paragraph.

                    Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
                    Gayo: It's been a while since I read shards and I can't seem to find my copy, but I remember that it did at least address my core complaint which was that it removed the Neverborn/Deathlord's ability to push the resonance bad touch button any time they liked. Since I can't find my copy could you post here what you liked about it, personally?
                    OK, so the Doom of the Black Exaltation kept the general incentive structure, in terms of what adds Resonance and how you get rid of it, though the costs might have been retooled a bit. But you still get Resonance for having children, going too long without killing any sentient beings, etc, you still remove it by spending time in shadowlands or adopting the trappings of the dead, and so forth. I like these things, and I think they're important for distinguishing Abyssals from Solars with a focus on death. There's nothing about the agenda to kill the world, though, they're just "carrying the essence of death within you", which I find a lot more palatable than it being a chain your evil masters use to jerk you around. On reread I think it might still be a little too harsh a death spiral, given how serious the Dooms are -- the game shouldn't generally make a player feel obligated to spend three days of game time doing nothing just to chill out -- but it's a huge improvement.simply because it's about you.

                    If you get to Resonance 10, you go into a Limit Break that draws on abyssal themes. As with Solar Limit Breaks, there's a related condition that causes you to gain extra Resonance. These are serious (many of them involve a compulsion to kill), but while a few are just reskinned Solar Limit Breaks, many are really thematically excellent and are a vehicle for expressing your character's nature, rather than simply blighting the world. For instance, the Doom of the Grave Blade inspires you to kill those who do not fear of death, but if they survive you feel you owe them a favour out of respect. The Doom of the Killing Saint inspires you to kill those whose suffering you cannot otherwise alleviate. The Doom of the Black Sun causes you to compulsively avoid the light of the sun. These are pretty rough given that you can't take them as "partial" effects and it's a nuisance to lose Resonance, but it's a far more compelling vision of it than

                    In the meantime, your passive Resonance creates cool spooky effects based on how high it is. This is basically just a grab bag of neat occult effects which are mostly cosmetic but can be stunted in practical ways. Dogs howl as you approach. people think they see your shadow moving out of the corner of their eye, candles blow out in your presence, omens of death happen to occur as you pass, mirrors crack, ghostly figures seem to lurk in the fog, etc. At low resonance these seem coincidental and become more dramatic as you near the maximum, particularly when you're stressed out.

                    (Also, you have a regular key virtue instead of a fucking broken virtue. goddamn broken virtues!)

                            I dunno, does anyone else feel that way, that abyssal's being forced to kill people whether they want to or not is too much? I'd really like to be able to play like, a daybreak occult-spec who just wants to help ghosts pass into lethe or a Midnight social who campaigns for undead rights and a more visible underworld population. Affixing "and also they go nuts and kill people occasionally" to that is really not something I'm into.
                            I don't think Abyssals should be forced to kill people, but I think it's fine for them to suffer more than anyone else for not killing people, and for them to be more likely to kill people against their best intentions. If all the Dooms involved killing people that'd be bad, but I think it's appropriate that more of them do than Solar Limit Breaks, and that never killing anybody exacerbates Resonance. If you take the Doom of the Stabby Motherfucker rather than the Doom of the Runny Mascara, you're making a definite statement about the kind of person your character is and the kind of stories you want to tell about them.

                            The thing is, okay: I've seen a Solar PC who was deathly pale, came from a shadowland, adopted the trappings of death, and campaigned for undead rights and a more visible underworld population. (She also killed people a lot, but that's an aside.) That's off-type for a Solar, but it's not inappropriate, and the character was definitely a Solar and not an Abyssal. You can be a necromancer who spends most of your time in Shadowlands and the Underworld, is deeply concerned with death and the dead, and wears soulsteel, and be a Solar. The distinction between Abyssals and Solars runs deeper than that, and part of it is that Abyssals are cursed in a much more ostantatious and death-themed way than Solars (whose Great Curse, by comparison, is mostly just about extremeness of temperament). This is supposed to be kind of a cool thematic thing rather than a "fuck you" thing, but if you're not the kind of person who thinks that the conflict between the desires of a living human and the heavy mantle of a death god is an interesting thing to play, I think Abyssals are probably not your jam. Solars can dress like dead people, but Abyssals need to, because being able to play someone who dresses like a dead person but would rather not is part of the play experience Abyssals offer that other splats don't. The killing thing is similar, though since it's more extreme it needs to be handled with more care.

                            So I think that if you're specifically avoiding killing anybody -- which is unusual for any Exalted PC, let's face it -- there should be some pain associated with that. The "pain" should be calibrated to be playable, and you should have some latitude to decide how harsh you want it to be, but it shouldn't be purely cosmetic and trivial.
