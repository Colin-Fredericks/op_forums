            Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

            So one could avoid your concept of Resonance by staying in the Underworld?
            I suppose, but it's no different then current Resonance. The difference is that it doesn't make it impossible to go around Creation without breaking everything. But there is an assumption here that Underworld is cool enough that you'd actually want to be there for some reason. It has been problematic so far.

            However, I'm wondering if Resonance shouldn't rack up on it's own, even in the Underworld, just much, much slower. Because in the end, Abyssals are creatures of both Worlds. Unlike Liminals, who are somewehre between life and death, Abyssals stand fully on both sides. They're like Shrodinger's cat, both dead and alive at the same time and that's so paradoxical that the Universe rebels against it.

            There is an allure to life that lures them out of the Underworld eventually (best represented narratively, I'm not keen on solid mehcanics that would drag Abyssals out of their homes, seems too heavy-handed) and when that happen they will form bonds with denizens of Creation. It's inevitable, you'd have to be an amazing socipath not to form some bonds with the living. At that point Abyssal has to make a choice- try to contain their Resonance, through small sacrfices work towards making it into an acceptable condition... Or return to the Underworld and abbandon the living.

            It's good to make Underworld into a great setting in its own right, Creation is where all the shit is at. Abyssals need an incentive to actually interact with it and I feel like the current implemenation of this incentive (your boss tells you to, you either obey or you die) is horribly crude and uninteresting and it doesn't help that Resonance makes you not want to do that ever. There needs to be some form of middle ground here. On it's own, the idea of Resonance is sound, it's implementation that was butchered, as is with mostly everything 2e had to offer.

                I generally disapprove of Resonance being a thing that inevitably kills the world because I disapprove of the state of being an Abyssal as a thing that inevitably kills the world. Emphasis on inevitably. My logic is thus:
                • If you want to play an Abyssal, you should not have mechanics that urge or cause you to stop being an Abyssal.
                • If Abyssals cannot stop being Abyssals, and Abyssals will destroy Creation simply by existing, then Creation is doomed and there's nothing anyone can do about it.
                • You should be able to play Abyssals who are not destroying Creation.
                The thing about Abyssals is that solving problems through violence and death should be the easiest solution for them most of the time, they can apply murder and manipulation of the resulting ghosts to solve problems that Solars can't solve that way. But making it so they have to destroy everything and don't get a choice in the matter, to my mind, takes away the more interesting dilemma of making the Abyssal choose how much they want to embrace that method, especially when it's easy and especially when they've already been confronted with their moment of weakness. Not everyone would have said yes to the deal you were offered, and you know it. In that moment, you valued your own life - not even your life as a whole, your current incarnation out of thousands that have been and would have been still - over the lives of everyone else in the world. Now you have to decide if you're going to embrace that dark part of you, if you hadn't done so long before you became an Abyssal, or if you're going to try to correct that weakness and become better.

                The choice is more interesting when you're constantly tempted to embrace that destructive side than when you're forced to destroy in roughly equal amounts no matter what you do. There are times you'll be able to justify it with relative ease, like when you kill a monster or a hated enemy. Then there are times you'll want to do it, but at the same time it will push your boundaries if you do. How far do you think you can go before you start sliding?

                                    I'm going to keep to theme and follow up with another Gail Simone character, Jeanette the banshee from Secret Six.


                                    Jeanette is everything I’d want from a Moonshadow. For the most part she comes across as an eccentric rich woman with interesting taste in fashion rather than an undead monster (though her reasons for being attracted to the assassin Deadshot are kind of creepy).

                                    However she holds a particular hatred of Amazons that becomes apparent during her fight with Wonder Woman where she starts getting scary quickly.
                                    I've had to be selective with frames here due to the image limit and some pages not being online (at least not that I can find).








                                    More info:
                                    I should probably explain the reason she hates Amazons because I think it works in an Exalted context.
                                    Jeannette died the first time during the French Revolution when her husband betrayed her and she became a Banshee at least in part as a result of a the botched execution, she's angry at the Amazons because in her eyes they abandoned the women of the world to suffer at the hands of men for centuries but more importantly they didn't save her.

                                    It could be interesting to have an Abyssal realise that their life would've gone very differently had the Unconquered Sun not turned his back upon Creation and have a lot of misdirected anger towards him (and by extension the Solar Exalted) because in a world that he apparently left to rot they had the audacity to pick survival over death.

                                                            Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                                                            I always saw the Lasso of Truth as something of a finishing move given that you can go "tell me your secret weakness" with it.

                                                            Jeanette gets slapped around a bit but actually wins this fight with her Banshee Howl. Its not like Pants to be Darkened the unaired TV pilot where she tortures a guy in the hospital instead of using the lasso.
                                                            The Lasso of Truth is a classic example of a writer 'oops' - and one that RPG writers might even take informative insight from!

                                                            It produced like, how many good scenes before we were kinda... bored with it, and already knew what it did, and it wasn't a gimmick anymore?...

                                                            ... and in turn, it -eliminated- how many other classic dramatic scenes, like beating people up for information or otherwise coercing them?

                                                            Now replace 'Lasso of Truth' with 'Charm Of Your Choice' and you can see one of the unseen perils of charm design.

                                                            The solution in this case is to politely ignore the lasso of truth. Coincidentally, this approach will ALSO work if you accidentally allow a bad charm in your game, as long as everybody is on the same page.
