    Thoughts on Abyssal/Fair Folk PCs

    04-06-2019, 08:57 PM
    I have a new group forming and I'm excited to get a chronicle going. With the release of WFHW and the Lunars manuscript previews (I missed backing the former), I'm looking forward to finally be able to provide Exalted options beyond Solars to my players!

    ...naturally, one of them wants to play an Abyssal and another wants to try a Fair Folk.

    While both of these two players have also expressed interest in Lunars, I am wondering whether I can accommodate their original impulses. The three options that come to mind:

    1) Build some third edition homebrew, using existing material and second edition for inspiration. Probably the most difficult option for me, since I never know if my homebrew is balanced properly, but also the one that lets me stick with third edition, which I'd prefer!

    2) Make the entire game second edition, which would be quicker, but I've heard some not so great stuff about Fair Folk PC rules in second edition...which would seem to defeat the purpose of using it to begin with.

    3) Throw together a homebrew using something completely different (right now I have some ideas inspired by Dresden Files Accelerated), which would likely be the most time-consuming, but I feel much more comfortable with the nuts and bolts of something like Fate or Cypher.

    I'm wondering if anyone feels like weighing in based on any experience they might have with homebrewing or specifically accommodating these sorts of PCs not yet released in third edition.

      3rd edition Abyssal is potentially really easy.

      Take a Solar give them a black anima, potentially tweak a few of the anima powers (potentially the hardest part of this), make them only resonant with Soulsteel and give them a limit triger like "a character expresses lack of fear of death", "the character suffers rejection from the living" etc. It may seem lazy but the terrible sanity-shattering truth is that they're about as different from a Solar as a lot of characters made with MoEP:Abyssal were.

      The Fair Folk is a very different story, I really don't know where to begin there.

          You COULD make viable fair folk PC’s in 2nd ed. Mostly you’d just want to focus almost exclusively on the form-setting charms and their Creation-based effects.

          And they would be incredibly underpowered. Ludicrously so.

          Shaping rules are cool and I hold out a love for them, but they are very very not compatible with a mixed game.

          If I were doing it, I would make the Fair folk as a DB, no animal and limited merits. give the player [essence] excellencies in fulfilling their narrative roles, then let them have 2-3 “””artifacts””” aspects of their form or panoply from which they could have like, 10 Evocations.

          Of course you’d have to build the evocations, but thats the quickest and dirtiest homebrew method I could think of until Exigents hits.

                          Originally posted by memeala View Post
                          That does make sense given the lore background for Abyssals. I'm not familiar with the 2nd edition Manuals but I have noticed the sample Abyssal Charms in the core book are pretty familiar.
                          About two-thirds of the Abyssal charms in the 2nd ed Manual were just "see Charm X in the Corebook". They just had different names.

                          Some were different; Abyssal medicine was very different, for example, focusing on spreading plagues. There were also charms that were similar but weaker, except in the Underworld where they were stronger. That wasn't generally very interesting though.

                          2nd ed Fae were usable (after errata) but weak. They had a lot of powers that only worked in the Wyld (though errata gave those weak functions in Creation as well). So you'd be better off using Raksha charms from the antagonist section than stuff based on that book, though it does give some interesting ideas.

                                          I think Miss Fair Folk enjoys the idea of playing something ethereal and beautifully alien. If I knew more about the mechanical realities of Dream-Souled I could push her in that direction because they might meet some of those needs without being quite so...misanthropic? But that would still be a homebrew-heavy situation.

                                          Miss Abyssal loves melodrama and characters that are bound to dark fates. Which can certainly be accomplished by other means, but the death motif is a little irresistible to her. I could nudge her in the direction of a Lunar necromancer if it fits with the nature of the final group, although there's a lot of great stuff in Lanaya's homebrew.

                                          Both of them like the look of Lunars a lot (they love the shapeshifting and I think the Attribute-based Charms are giving them ideas), and depending on what the rest of the group decides that might be where they end up landing. But I like to accommodate what players want for their PCs, sometimes possibly to a fault ("Can I play a werewolf in Scum & Villainy?"), and my hope was this might give me more practice with homebrewing.

                                          So a Lunar pick for both of them isn't impossible, but I am trying to consider different options (and appreciate the thoughts others are sharing along those lines).
