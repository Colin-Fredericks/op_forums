                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                            Basically expecting companies to act in a moral manner of their own accord is hopelessly naive. That's why we have all kinds of laws to prevent businesses from abusing their workers or hurting the communities that they're based.
                                            Well that's why we are here, doin' our thang. :3

                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                            At the moment, it's not illegal to draw pictures of sexy women in America, and if businesses believe that by including pictures of sexy women with their product will improve profits or help them lock down market shares, that's what they're going to do. If you feel that drawing sexy pictures of women is morally wrong, then you should maybe contact your senator and try to pass a law making it illegal. Because that's the only way that businesses will ever stop doing it.
                                            Or, as you say, make them believe that it won't actually increase sales. As, according to Isator previously, has been implied by statistics since the 60s.

                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                            But that is at the core concept of "freedom" in western society. As long as something is legal, people will do it. Spitting in public might be gross and unsanitary, but unless it's illegal (and unless that law is enforced) people are going to spit in public. And ultimately I feel that it's better to have more individual freedom (like the ability to draw sexy women) than it is to make lots of things illegal because some people find it offensive or hurtful.

                                            For the record though, as an artist myself (who has drawn a fair share of sexy men and women in the course of my life) there's nothing morally wrong with drawing sexy women. I don't feel like a horrible person for doing so. In fact, I reject the idea that drawings directly lead to an increase amounts of rape or that those drawings lead to men hurting women or that I should feel or share the blame of any man that goes out and commits rape.
                                            No, man. Laws are ultimately expressions of the values of those who made the laws (and also local affairs; this isn't only about America). You have to start from the bottom. Trying to ban something that most people enjoys doesn't work that well. Before we can ever get any kind of law down, we must change the values those who are going to be affected by it.

                                            Because it's not about offence. It's about actual damage. There's undeniably more violence and oppression going on against women and members of ethnic minorities than against men and members of ethnic majorities. This is due to our culture, and culture is literally everything around us, from the abstract ideas of representation and thought processes to the physical realities of living conditions and economical situations. Representation in artwork is a part in all that. It's part of that "background noise" that was mentioned in a comic earlier in the thread, and I fully believe it has an effect. At least as much effect as any other factor.

                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                            That's because it's important to remember that it's not only guys drawing sexy women, it's also women drawing pictures of sexy women. Melissa Uran has drawn plenty of sexy or naked pictures of women in Exalted and other White Wolf products (and even posted some on the forums back in the day). Would you say that her drawings are encouraging rape? Should she stop drawing pictures of sexy women? When a man commits rape should she hold up her hand, take responsibility and apologize?
                                            Firstly, sexy isn't a problem. The same kind of sexy, all the time and in every context, is.

                                            Secondly, women and men both can draw problematic images of women. There is such a thing as internalized misogyny (and here I again use the term not as an instance of expressed "man I really hate those womenfolks" but rather as that "background noise" from earlier). There is some difference between the cases, in my mind, but not much.

                                            That said, I don't think there's much to be gained from looking at individual cases of rape and trying to find a cultural explanation, in much the same way as looking at individual cases of artwork and trying to get those revoked or changed is kind of missing the point. Sure, practical cases can be useful as examples and points of reference, but they aren't really the problem. The problem is the cultural structures behind them, that "background noise". That is what discussions like these seek to change.

                                            So, bottom line, I'm not trying to blame you for drawing stereotypically sexy people. I'm just trying to point out that there are effects, and also alternatives.
