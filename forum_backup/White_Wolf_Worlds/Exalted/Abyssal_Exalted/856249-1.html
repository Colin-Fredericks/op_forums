    Originally posted by haren View Post
    This isn't quite as simple a topic as it might seem. First, this is a suggestion for one aspect of Abyssals, but not the only one. The question as I was thinking of it is, what's the problem with the concept in the past? Quite simply, the problem isn't the concept but how Oblivion has been presented both in and out of character as a horrific and malicious force. So to even make the concept viable, I think we have to get rid of that assumption.

    So, what can Oblivion be? One of the definitions in the dictionary is the state of being forgotten or disregarded. THIS is something that fits with some of the base ideas of something no one has come back from. That makes it very easy they're being forgotten or disregarded. In this case, the Abyssals are the champions of those who are forgotten and disregarded. Considering the nature of the Underworld, this could easily apply to the dead, but there are those among the living who would also count.

    This concept also implies what Whispers could be. The constant wishes and needs of people. Those things the people are either unable to do, or feel unable to do (for reasons of it being 'wasteful', pointless, or any number of other choices). The exact mechanics, I'm not sure of, but... if you go out of your way (Abyssals only) to fulfill their desires and wishes, somehow the world will pay you back. Maybe a living person feels a debt, even if they don't know you did it. Circumstances might make things fall into place that ease your plans forward. The idea is it makes smaller side stories and help keep the main ones moving.
    I like the direction this idea goes, if only because it gives Abyssals more to do and more agency in the setting beyond being errand boys/girls for crazy nephwracks, but it sounds more like they are fighting Oblivion than championing it. Preventing ghosts from being forgotten and discarded is very much resisting the call of the void/lethe. Again, I like the idea, but the introduction is almost... misleading in a way?

    Also, I personally love the unsung heroes who show up, save the day, and take exactly zero credit. Solars, Lunars and Terrestrials all like to bask in the glory of a job well done but I really enjoy the fact that Abyssals and Sidereals traditionally do the same amount of work and get a tiny sliver of the acclaim, because Batman doesn't need to be loved to save a baby from a burning building. People may not even notice that you did anything, but you'll do it again and again because it's the right thing to do.

    Originally posted by haren View Post
    The one thing I like about this is... the Neverborn aren't "stuck" but resist the pull of Oblivion. If they give in, they will be forgotten. Their deaths will be disregarded. Everything was for naught. How can they give everything up, just so their suffering might end now?
    I can get behind this. Though there were a lot of problems with having "Oblivion" be a conscious thing the Neverborn influenced in the last edition, so my honest preference at this point is to have the Neverborn be as little involved as possible in the Underworld setting next time around.

            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
            I've actually always thought that Abyssals could be champions of the dead. One of my favourite concepts for an Abyssal is one who goes around taking vengeance for the unrightfully killed dead. He finds the ghosts of people who've been killed by an insane murderer? He uses knowledge only the victims have to track him down and stick a daiklave in his face. Find a villiage that had been burned by a mad king? Reap terrible justice for the restless dead. Horrible underworld spawned monster terrorizing the afterlife of some ghosts? Put that thing down! They spend a lot of time in the Underworld talking to the dead, unless they're heartless, which is totally an option, they'd probably start developing feelings like that.

            Or taken another way, sometimes death is a good thing. Old prejudices die when old men die, forests that burn to ash spring forth new life, a corrupt ministers grip on outdated policies vanishes with his death. Maybe your abyssal prays to Saturn, or defies her plan in favour of her own. Pruning the world of it's sickness so that the rest may blossom. Abyssals get to flip off the Neverborn just as much as Solars get to flip off the Sun.

            My sole Abyssal Character concept was a Midnight Caste doing just this. He meets his Lunar Mate when he was chopping down some healing trees. The gods of the trees had a racket going with some disease gods and a lot of villagers died of disease when they wouldn't cough up the worship (the villagers were ancestor worshipers). The Abyssal tried to play nice with the gods, but they wouldn't budge.

            Abyssal: Since you wouldn't play nice, I'm going to chop down all of your trees.
            Healing Gods: You wouldn't dare.
            Abyssal: *Points his ax at god* When I am done, you are going to be the god of tree stumps. *Starts cutting down trees using charms*
            Healing Gods: Oh, Shit! Help! Lunar! This Monster is cutting down the Sacred Healing Trees!

                    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post

                    Oblivion as Buddhist nirvana? Sounds like you've been listening to the Bishop and his Shining Path!
                    To be honest, I didn't remember his vocalizations of it were that. I used that in a game I ran in an AU of Exalted based on Escape from New York... except more like the Blessed Isle. Anyways, lots of Deathlords had their own views. Walker in Darkness was very much a religious leader/military leader, but focused on the Underworld. He thought that to transcend yourself, you had to give up your memories, that his own loss was a message. But he wasn't pushy about it. To him, trying to force someone into it was more harmful than good, and the living can't give up memories that way and live normally.

                    On the things Dr. LoveMonkey said, those sorts of things helped inform the original post idea. I'm really fond of imaging Abyssals as some sort of Revanant. I was super into Wraith, and the Shadow Players Guide actually had an amazingly good discussion of what the Shadow was and what it wasn't. It's not really classically evil. Yes, it wants to take over, but that's because deep down it wants what you want and won't admit to or work towards. The idea of Abyssals being sort of the "Shadows unleashed" of the world... making things happen that people want but don't get... it's scary and could be really awful, but could also be saintly.

                    And that's the point of all this, choices. You want to be an unapologetic wicked Abyssal. You can! Want to be one who is helping people. Sure!

                        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post

                        Yep, that's why I specifically included two of the ways they could interact with Saturn in my post. Saturn has a plan for things, when they must end, and when they must not. Abyssals tell Saturn and the plans of Heaven to go fuck themselves, the innocent slain demand retribution, their mournful cries echo in the halls of the dead. An Abyssal slices through the strands of fate and deliver the cold justice of the grave.

                        Or maybe they feel like, or even are, disciples of Saturn. Just because I'm a Lunar doesn't mean I can't worship death right? I do it in DnD all the time, with wizards who are devout followers of a religion, fighters who definitely aren't paladins but charge into battle with holy symbols in their armor anyway.

                        There's a million things that make Abyssals different from Endings castes already. They don't exalt the same, Heaven despises and fears one and wields the other, Endings castes want to send all ghosts to Lethe while Abyssals champion ghosts. I could go on but I don't want to ramble. If DBs can have the soldier angle without stepping on the toes of Battles castes, Abyssals can have the reaper thing without stepping on the toes of Endings castes.
                        The reason for my comment was that your last paragraph kind of read like a greatest-hits of core Endings themes. Just saying "ok but Saturn doesn't want you to do these things" doesn't really ameliorate that. Right now we have a nice, fruitful contrast between Endings as natural, productive death (though not necessarily good) and Abyssals as unnatural, destructive death (though not necessarily evil), and while I wouldn't want to see that enforced rigidly, I also think it would be very much to the detriment of Endings to have that separation broken down.

                        Abyssals are never just going to be a generalized "death/void Exalted", and that's probably a good thing, because they're far from the only Exalted working in that theme space. That means that there will always be things suitable for a death/void Exalted that aren't suitable for Abyssals except in against-type exceptions, and that's fine. Every Exalt type works that way.
