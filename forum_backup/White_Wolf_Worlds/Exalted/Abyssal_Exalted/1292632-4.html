    I'm suddenly picturing the path to becoming a Solar as fuelled less by an internal moral shortcoming of Abyssal Exaltation and more as an outgrowth of a kind of inherent romantic streak that might exist in the cross-section between the Essence and the kinds of people who get Chosen, possibly combined a bit with an affectation of melancholic gloom. It's just that the fact that it actually was originally a Solar Exaltation gives that sentiment somewhere to go.

    Something about Abyssals often makes me think of that episode of Blackadder where Shelly, Byron and Coleridge make a very big show over how they're all dying.

                        I believe that from 1st edition, one of the aspects of the Abyssal Exalted was to be grounded in the Byronic Hero. They didn't quite pull it off. It was more Romero bedecked in black roses. The British Romantic poets of the early 19th century make for great Abyssal inspiration, particularly the so-called "Satanic School": Lord Byron, Percy Shelley, John Keats - I think William Blake is a Sidereal or more likely a Getimian. Coleridge I suppose could represent a world-weary Abyssal addicted to the Guild's heroin. His poetry explores metaphysical aspects of the border of life and death, sure (after all, the Ancient Mariner is won in a dice game by Life-in-Death) but he lacks that savage spirit or romantic evil longing that can turn them into sexy slayers or aesthetes of carnage. Wordsworth is off wandering lonely as a cloud, so let's forget him. Keats wrote delicate poems in which the shadow of time seemed to lay on everything from the petals of lilacs in the morning to the tolling of the church bell at dawn. He also wrote Gothic inspired poem s, La belle dans sans merci, and the vampiric Lamia. He seemed presciently aware of his death at 25 of tuberculosis. Shelley, though wild and anti-Estalishment, was too high hearted and positive that Man would be bettered by knowledge and poetry; Shelley is a revolutionary bipolar Solar while his good friend Lord Byron is a brooding Abyssal aware of his evil deeds and the inevitability of more to come, however much he joys in his friend Shelley;s ideals. There's an inspiration for a couple of characters.

                        So, this turned out to be more of a lit lesson and its application to Abyssal role-playing themes than any application of these influences to the thematic design of Abyssal powers and charms, Sorry about that,

                            It was back in the early 2000's, in my group we were pitching each other different RPGs we owned. One friend talked us into playing 2e Exalted via explaining all the off the wall shit you could get up to by way of describing Invincible Sword Princess. We were figuring out names and, to sweeten the pot and probably show off a bit, he offered to write our character's names in Old Realm; all well and good but we didn't really know what an Old Realm name sounded or spelled like, he waved it off a bit and told us to go with something like Noun Verbing Noun/Adjective. "Just think of something that sounds right."
                            When time came to say mine he replied.
                            "That's an Abyssal name; I don't run Abyssals."
                            My fascination began there.
