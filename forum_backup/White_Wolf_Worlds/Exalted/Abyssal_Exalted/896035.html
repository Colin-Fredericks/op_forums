    Ideas on the use of Abyssals in Ex3

    05-22-2016, 08:58 AM
    In a game I'm running, the PCs have unintentionally inherited something of great value to a deathlord. Naturally, a circle of abyssals will be coming after them. I decided to go with Abyssals as antagonists because there is frankly more mechanical support for them in Ex3 than anything but other Solars. I don't have the time or the expertise to write entire Terrestrial, Lunar, Sidereal, or Spirit charm sets, but I can basically just tweak some solar charms for a different feel and I'm good to go with Abyssals.

    I don't want to run them as 2e Abyssals, however. That is, I don't want them to exist exclusively as extensions of a deathlord's will. I want them to be cool in their own right, but that got me wondering a bit. The devs have said that Abyssals will have a lot more options this time around, and that even those working for a deathlord will have not have such an obnoxious mechanic to deal with as resonance the way it worked before, or, "your deathlord snaps his fingers from worlds away and you die."

    I like the idea of these guys being beholden to a deathlord, but I'm having trouble coming up with a good way to handle that. Should deathlords still have monstrances that let them contain and offer exaltations to dying heroes? Should this still give them some measure of supernatural influence? Or should Abyssals come about like Solars (more spontaneously), and then many are drawn to the willing service of deathlords somehow?

          I would suggest keeping the scene where the Deathlord makes an offer to a dying person for Exaltation. It's very iconic and pretty cool. I would just as soon get rid of the idea of the monstrance however and the idea that the Deathlord has magical powers over the life and death of the Abyssal (though some Deathlords might lie and claim they do, and others might have Charms, Sorcery or are powerful enough that, if they wanted to, they could potentially kill their errant Deathknight, though it would take more work than a snap of the ).

          I think the specifics would be down to each Deathlord. So some, like the Lover or the Mask of Winters or the Silver Prince might rule more through giving their Deathknights praise, honours and rewards (basically enticing them through their greed and egos) others might use a bit more fear (like the FaFL or the Dowager) and some might keep their knights in line through other means (the Bishop likely uses a bit of brainwashing and religious indoctrination).

          Basically though it should be clear that a given deathknight has a personal relationship with her Deathlord and chooses to remain in service. And, importantly, if the Abyssal decides otherwise, there aren't any magical chains forcing her to stay.

                  Laws, was that an actual thing in 2E? Abyssals being mere extensions of their Deathlord's will? Deathknights dying when their liege snaps his fingers a continent away? Or some less hyperbolic version of the above? I recall plenty of Abyssals being "cool in their own right" in my 2E game. I ask because I tend to go through books as if editing a script for performance and just mentally black out stuff I think is dumb and never think of it again. So was this really how it was? And only in 2E? I recall Deathlords having power over their knights since 1E.

                  To answer the actual question: Yes, most Abyssals should be the willing vassals of the Deathlords, who act as the distributors of the Black Exaltation. Deathlords should be able to use monstrances to discipline their charges in some way, but not to effortlessly kill them. This is more compelling for several reasons.

                  1. Uniqueness. The more we can do to avoid "goth solars," the happier everyone shall be
                  2. Gives the Deathlords power and agency as arc villains and overlords
                  3. Creates a dynamic where the Deathords have a tiger by the tail
                  4. Makes rebel or unaffiliated Deathknights more special
                  5. Lets you play with the feudal lord/knight relationship which suits the Abyssals gothic themes

                  So, do give Deathlords some authority over most Abyssals, but not enough to eliminate the DKs’ individuality.

                                                          Originally posted by Andrew D View Post

                                                          I like the idea of these guys being beholden to a deathlord, but I'm having trouble coming up with a good way to handle that. Should deathlords still have monstrances that let them contain and offer exaltations to dying heroes?
                                                          Abyssals are Chosen by the Deathlords; the mortal just has to agree when they are on the brink of death.

                                                          As for the nature of the relationship, that's pretty simple. Why does any person in our world choose to "serve" another? Most people don't work for themselves, they work for other people in corporations. Most humans don't spend their time fighting for 100% independence from government, they either accept the government they have, or they try to reform it.

                                                          Deathknights will serve Deathlords for the same reasons people create hierarchies. Serving gains the Deathknight resources (arms and armor, armies, a Manse, etc). Serving gives the Deathknight protection in the form of their master's wrath. Finally, a Deathlord is a mentor for the Deathknight.

                                                          A standard part of Killfuck Soulshitter Syndrome is for all PCs to be 100% independent with nobody having any authority over them. That "feels" right to them, but it's not realistic. When you consciously move away from that construct (and discard the Monstrance nonsense), you remember that normal human behavior is working together and forming a hierarchy, and even Abyssals are still mentally human (even if they are dead).
