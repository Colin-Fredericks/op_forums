                Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                If you want to make the Deathlords sympathetic in a way that fits in Creation's venue, make them selfish actors with their own agendas who are maybe, in practice, only slightly more morally compromised than all of the other awful, awful people who've risen to the top of the setting's power heap -- and then make them personally sympathetic such that they maybe have a fondness for the PCs for a personal reason, that could lead to the PCs having a fondness for them for a personal reason. Like, I'm sure y'all have heard the many times that I point out that the Slug engages in and promotes trafficking in sex slavery on a massive scale, and Cathak Cainan has put whole satrapies to the lash and sword and crucifix just to make examples of them to other satrapies, but nobody has a problem with those two showing up on-screen as neutral NPCs or allies.
                The Deathlords as selfishly motivated at their core and any cosmological contribution incidental to their aspirations and methods, with the effects on Creation at best minor and unobservable in normal circumstances is where I was going with that, yes. Likewise, I was going with a similar stance for the Abyssals and the chivalry of death.

                I didn't intend to suggest any arbitrary metaphysical explanation serve as replacement for the complexity and dubious morality that naturally come with the wide-reaching actions of powerful individuals or as a justification for horrible acts against the living or the majority of the world as it matters. At most, I was suggesting a buy-in on the part of the Deathlords that is motivated by self-interest. One which, while not forgiving of anything, could also serve as an inherent note of positive meaning to be found in existence as an Abyssal (I mean, leaving aside their potential to change the setting and do good, which shouldn't be all that much different from Solars, even if differently focused).

                I don't think the idea of holding back oblivion in some way should excuse or make okay anything that the Deathlords or the Abyssals do, so much as interestingly inform edges of their actions and possible motivations. To put it the way TGUEIROS mentioned the ambiguity of the Sidereal and Dragonblooded:

                If the Abyssals cease their march to the chivalry of death and souls stop flowing to the Deathlords' kingdoms on drums of devastation, the Underworld may eventually lose cohesion and fall into oblivion, and Creation may one day follow. Or they may not, and the shadow of death one day fill the world.

                            I think the important thing here is you can do villainous things, even be a villain, but not be a bad or evil person. I mean, the Wyld Hunt kills Solars and Lunars who are innocent of the crime they're accused of (being really demonic monster in a human body), but does that make them evil? Not necessarily.

                            The fact you can be a heroic person who intends to do the right thing and BELIEVES you're doing the right thing, even as you are committing some wicked wrong act has nuance and depth. The thing to remember in the OP's idea is... they could be wrong. This might not be helping ANYTHING. They might be true believers trying to make the world a better place and doing something they think only SEEMS horrible to those who don't understand it.

                            To quote a character who betrayed humanity and companions for peace with monsters in a story, "Will it be hard? Will there be pains as things change? Of course. But I've determined only a few hundred thousand need die for this! Isn't this worth their sacrifice?" (I probably screwed up the wording, but the idea is there)

                            That character believed WITHOUT A DOUBT they were doing the right thing, and it didn't make it any less horrible.

                            Edit: It occurs to me, that can be a form of heroism... being a villain and doing something horrible, because you believe it NEEDS to be done. You'll probably be punished and suffer for it after the fact... and yes, you ARE a villain in doing that.

                                I for one think it would be great if both the abyssals and deathlords as a whole were morally ambiguous. The fact that we can, with this story idea, have a balanced discussion about whether the abyssals are justified in their actions is VERY encouraging to me!

                                "The greater good" is not an ironclad defense of virtue. That's a cornerstone of Exalted writing. It is, however, an excellent device for justifying morally complex characters, which are infinitely superior to flat, morally bankrupt antagonists.

                                As to the deathlords, I had not heard this idea that they might have stolen their powers in 3e but that does offer some good story options. Perhaps the neverborn can't control them directly anymore, but Whispers are still very much a thing and none of them walked away unchanged? A few might have the mental fortitude to preserve the core of their morality but I imagine most would be pretty cracked after swallowing a shard of pure screeching madness. Maybe one crafted the great contagion to be a permanent solution to the Underworld's ghost quota, but their madness caused them to carry a decimal point the wrong way, or another deathlord got ahold of the formula and decided to "help" by adding a secret ingredient that made it 100 times more deadly?

                                    I like the basic idea. What I'd handle it as is more...this is the easy way. Sacrificing ghosts by thousands, funneling death into the underworld, stabilizing the underworld, it's one way to calm the Neverborn.                                         Here's an interesting exercise: how much time does each ghost have to be "worth" in order to justify mass murder in Creation? If you knew that every fresh soul would extend the Underworld AND Creation's lifespan by a century, would you do it?

                                        What if it was just a decade? A year? A season? A month? A week? A day?

                                        Where do you draw the line? When does it stop being worth it?

                                        EDIT: Other considerations: since the final goal is a bigger underworld population, you are not just killing people, but "re-purposing" them. Maybe your character has a set of rules for which souls they will or will not take. Maybe they sleep better at night if they only kill the really bad eggs, or maybe they only want the best of the best in their little corner of the afterlife.
