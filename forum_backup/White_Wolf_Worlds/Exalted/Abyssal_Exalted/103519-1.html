        I'd really prefer it if Abyssals were not hopelessly crippling and depressing to play.

        Abyssals should have an element of tragedy and darkness within them, but that should be as much something you can struggle with as something you can accept. The problem with Abyssals used to be that either you were a nihilistic shithead who wanted to wipe out reality, or you redeemed yourself. Those things should be possible for Abyssal, but granularity is important. You should be able to play a whole gamut of Abyssal characters between those very far extremes, including characters with much the same motivations as Solars and Lunars and DBs and everyone else, who happened to Exalt as an Abyssal because you don't get to pick what colour the sun on your forehead shines.

        Death Knight in aesthetic, champion of virtue in reality is a character I always wanted to play, without having to sacrifice the Death Knight aesthetic to do it!

                                                My take on the "Doomed" theme and mechanics of Abyssals in 2e is that these themes were applied so heavily to the splat that they kind of backfire on themselves and become irrelevant. What is the point of being a bane against those you would love and protect if the application of resonance and its effects come up on you so fast that you cant really enter into these relationships in the first place. There are so many ways to gain resonance, that if a Abyssal tried to make any meaningful connections for their own purposes they would just be in a constant state of resonance break. If your storyteller doesn't rein themselves in on the application of these effects there really isn't any way of playing a Abyssal seeking redemption or even just a relatively normal life, the would kill all the crops, livestock, and unborn babies in the area in a couple of days, which i imagine would make it hard to form relationships. I would look to Michael Moorcock's Elric as a good example of how this could work better, every time he comes to care about someone they die because of the dark pacts and influences he relies on, but the key here is that he is allowed to make these relationships in the first place. Elric often spent month and even years with companions, allies, and lovers, before he caused their deaths. Being able to have periods of relative normalcy makes it so much more poignant when your true nature does surface to screw you over.

                                                The more story driven resonance effects and resonance breaks from "Shards" go a long way towards fixing this problem, and I hope some version of them will be used when the Abyssals get their EX3 splat book! The triggers that cause you to gain resonance are easier to work around, and the impact of your growing resonance though extremely ominous, is not necessarily physically threatening When your resonance finally breaks, the virtue based mind states are impactful and generally a major hindrance to your character, but still open enough for your character to retain some element of themselves throughout.
