        On the subject of clothing, Creation (just like our world) is a big place so there are lots of thoughts on, references to, and themes based around death, so you don't need to have all the skulls and chains and such all the time. I think it was a bit of a failure on the part of the authors that so many Abyssals came across as such. I mean, in Creation the color most closely associated with death is purple, while black is associated with water, yet Abyssals used the color black far more than purple. That's due to the author's western sensibilities that say that black is the color of death.

        Still, it's not hard to come up with other things. In the last Abyssal game I played in, one of the characters wore a white kimono covered with a cherry blossom design She fought with a magical branch (mechanically a wrackstaff) and when she would kill something, it would instantly leech out the corpse's nutrients and momentarily be covered with cherry blossoms which could quickly fall off and and gently waft to the ground. Cherry blossoms are often associated with the idea of dying young, transience and wistfulness; it's the whole concept of mono no aware.

        My own character was based around the idea of "rainbows" (they're beautiful, short lived, ephemeral and appear only when there is rain and sunshine - not a bad metaphor for life and death ). So this character came from a culture in the East that viewed rainbows as ill omens and symbols of death. His clothing was a riot of different colors and he wore lots of different colored bodypaint as in the culture he came from, when someone died their corpse would be dressed up to resemble a rainbow and then hung from a tree.

        I hope in 3rd edition when the Abyssals book comes out, the writers expand beyond the traditional western-gothic death imagery and maybe grab stuff from other cultures and nations, as well as come up with some things that are unique to Creation. Abyssals and their "all black all skulls all the time" just never felt as cool as they otherwise might have been.

                                            Keeping in mind that I'm actually more focused on the term "macabre" rather than Gothic, it's a combination of wanting it to feel more like a dead side than another country, so it needs to be widely infused with reminders and examinations of death, and something to build upon for the whole thing of Abyssals as Death's Lawgivers rather than psychopomps or whatever (or murderers, for that matter; it's something I think can give the Abyssals a strong thesis, and a powerful complement to the Solars, in the face of a Time of Tumult which is likely to start producing a lot of death). I mean, people want to talk about the amazing alternative of associating white with death, as though I didn't link to a picture of a European church fixture made from bones. Oh you can't have skulls, that excludes some of those cultures that have a different kind of bone surrounding their precious head meat. I'm all for the sense of the Underworld containing a variety of memories of cultures that no longer live to emphasize the universality of loss and memory, but still all existing under a pall of their dramas having finished, and that they endure grasping at what they are at the end. Things like "oh they've got actual cultures in there" is how we get people going on about how all you really need to bring somebody back to life is to stick their ghost inside of a meat suit. Macabre themes because they're a powerful real world contemplation on why your ghost would not be you, while also diversifying the Abyssals and the Underworld beyond "death is scary" and "death is murder" (note to self: Abyssal character concept who contemplates how their immortality really translates to having to watch everything else die forever). Even then, hardly just European; you can also get so much from the ancient Egyptian, the Aztec, whatever aspects of the African have wound up finding new expression in the American, Chinese ideas about the residence of the lower soul, all that jazz. I want bones, but dear God do I want people to do interesting things with them.

                                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                Not different enough. I can see how it's fitting to be jaded about Gothic fantasy considering how much the Gothic is informed by being jaded, but it's still a rich genre with a lot of pedigree. I think it will benefit more from invigoration than dilution in search of a broad appeal. Hehehehe
                                                Saying that the entire Underworld should be based around Gothic-fantasy/western themes and imagery is like saying that all of Creation should be based around Ming Dynasty-fantasy.

                                                While the Ming Dynasty plays a large role in inspiring Creation, there are still plenty of other places that have been influenced by the Middle East, by Mezo-America, by Nordic fantasy, by Polynesia, etc. Which is good. Creation wouldn't be nearly so interesting if the setting didn't have that sort of diversity.

                                                Limiting it so that the entire Underworld was solely or almost solely inspired by Gothic fantasy would feel practically stifling. Gothic fantasy is cool and all and it should be a powerful source, but it should be far from the only source, and it shouldn't be so overwhelming of a source that it swallows up everything else. There's a million other equally interesting depictions of ghosts, spooks and the afterlife out there apart from western Gothic imaginings which should be able to serve equally well for inspiring an entire world.

                                                    I take Loyalists seriously by reading the first edition STC instead of the subsequent works on deathknights. To be less flippant, think of the soulsteel caste Alchemicals. Souls only become worthy of supporting that variety of Champion by living several lifetimes wherein they place their goals above their lives, and die in the process of fulfilling those goals, or making an attempt with everything that they have. For Abyssals, however, this continues past the point of death, which is kept at bay by their Exaltation, and gives them the means to continue pursuing said goal while they tend to their duties under the (hopefully non-omnicidal in this edition) deathlords. Abyssals are determined beyond the bounds of mortality to change the world with their vision, or to wreak vengeance on a foe that wronged them, or make discoveries of applied scholarship.
