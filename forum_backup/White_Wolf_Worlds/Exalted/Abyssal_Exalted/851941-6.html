            Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
            "For the greater good" in that sense and embraced wholeheartedly is and always has been satire. There's a reason why it's best known from Warhammer 40k and Hot Fuzz. Also, moving the Deathlords from "Servants of the Neverborn" to "Free actors who stole power from the Neverborn" does not move them from selfish to altruistic without significantly more alteration necessary.
            Does this include Sidereals?

            I'm half-serious. Lately, the more I thought about the Usurpation and the way Exalted presents it, the more it bothers me. In-setting, it works; there's this prophecy and the Sidereals finally, sorrowfully decide that the only way to save the world is to nearly destroy it and take control of its future themselves. They don't want to do this -- ruling the world from behind the scenes is so very much work! Their books make clear just how much painful work it is to run the world from behind the scenes, how stretched-thin they are; surely they wouldn't have done this if they didn't absolutely have to! Why, Kejak cried for an entire day after nearly destroying civilization in order to seize power. An entire day!

            The problem I have with it is that this is exactly the sort of thing people who lead real-world coups say. "I didn't want to do it, but I had to to save the country, and look at how much painful work it is; I don't really enjoy leading, I've been forced into it..." is the kind of thing I'd have expected out of, say, Muammar Gaddafi.

            (Their continued inability to make any sort of peace with the Lunars is also extremely questionable when viewed from this light -- sure, the Lunars are really really angry, but at the end of the day the Sidereals have spent the last 1500 years killmurdering anyone who could oppose their grip on Creation's future, explicitly including people who have no particular place in their great prophecy. I'm increasingly uncomfortable with the way the book tries to justify that or to say how ruling Creation from behind the scenes is so hard.)

                    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
                    The problem with Oblivion is that it tries to reshift the focus of the entire game. It sends the message that the battles over the Realm and the like don't really matter compared to this inevitable world-destroying force at the root of the world that must be confronted.

                    ...

                    I've mentioned this with regards to Infernals, but it affected Abyssals, too... I think that if you look at, say, your typical Final Fantasy game, there's a moment when the game is done introducing you to the world and says "all right, now you're going to fight a superdemon." And from then on the story is all about killing a monster clown or an evil tree or a Sephiroth or whatever, and the other stuff stops mattering so much. And I think that because that kind of storytelling has become so widespread in videogames, people expect it out of Exalted. But I also think it's really really bad for a tabletop game, since it means you're discarding most of what matters to the main game.
                    This is something touched on in this video, "The Philosophy of Final Fantasy".

                    After watching that video, I immediately felt cheated by Squaresoft. I wondered what kind of stories could have been told if...


                    Final Fantasy plot-twists below:

                    ...in Final Fantasy 6, Emperor Gestahl had possessed enough sense to know Kefka was a mad dog and thus held him on a tight enough leash that Kefka DIDN'T murder General Leo, DIDN'T throw the Emperor off the floating continent, and DIDN'T destroy the world and become a god of destruction.

                    What if the rest of the story in Final Fantasy 6 was dealing with the fact that the Empire now had the powers of the goddesses, on top of controlling most of the planet? The Emperor and even the honorable General Leo would still be your enemies. Leo could be confronted about the evil he was agreeing to commit in the name of national loyalty. The Emperor would still be engaging in the story of nations, industrialization, invasion, occupation, and exploitation.

                    What if Sephiroth had never shown his whiny-ass face? What if the rest of the story of Final Fantasy 7 was about continuing the fight against the Shinra Corporation, quite possibly with the ORIGINAL Mr. Shinra in charge (since Sephiroth didn't show up to conveniently murder him for you) and his son Rufus agitating in the corner.

                    What if Kuja had never gotten the chance to throw his genocidal hissy-fit, so that the rest of Final Fantasy 9 would have been actually dealing with an entire other world of people that wanted to invade and conquer? OR, even earlier than them, what if the story was still dealing with that butt-ugly queen who wanted to conquer the world even when it meant destroying entire kingdoms and endangering whole sapient species of people?


                    All of these complex stories and their possible messages and lessons were always tossed out the window so that you could have a simple, purely evil villain to punch in the face until the world was saved. If that's what Final Fantasy was going to be about, then maybe they could have left all of the complexity out of the beginnings so that we could just cut to the fucking chase?

                    Like, I think Kingdom Hearts might pretty much be the clean distillation of a Final Fantasy game, in that light. "Simple and Clean", if you will.


                    I feel Chrono Trigger struck the best balance, actually. Even though you get briefly stuck into the historic past of the Human/Mystic war before learning about Lavos, there was never the sense that you personally needed to worry about that war. Because in the modern day, the war is long over, and the humans (which includes you) won. All you care about is rescuing Marle, which you do before learning about Lavos.

                    Even after learning about Lavos, you're continuously introduced to various smaller stories that have little or nothing to do with dealing with Lavos, and none of them feel meaningless! Because you're always engaged and bound up in these smaller stories.

                    I think, maybe, there's this sense of "sure, we've got to save the world from dying -- but we also care about making a world that's worth LIVING in".

                        Pretty much. One thing I really love about the Suikoden series (especially 1 and 2) is that it avoided the urge to replace its story about warfare and society and people with some bullshit magical supergod.

                        If Squaresoft had written Suikoden II, Luca Blight would have gotten some supergod power halfway through and the nations and wars would have stopped mattering. Instead, by having the war continue after his death, it made it clear that the nations and the conflict involved were bigger than any one person -- it made them the main focus. Even if Luca Blight was a fun character, it wouldn't have been as good of a game if they had made the last act focus on how evil and superpowerful he was.
