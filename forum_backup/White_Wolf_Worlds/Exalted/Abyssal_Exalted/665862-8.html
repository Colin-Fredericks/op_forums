    Originally posted by Root View Post
    No one is sitting around with an abacus or a spreadsheet or anything doing the math and saying, "Hey gang, some of you won't like this, but I calculated that we'd sell 3,000 more copies if we include silly fake looking boobs with chains and skulls and shit." There's a lot of cultural inertia or unconscious assumptions or whatever you want to call it that shapes the art we see. That's why it makes sense to be conscious of our choices and not just assume that things couldn't be different or must be the way they are for a solid reason.

    And the idea that tits and explosions sell isn't even that well founded. I can go on Netflix right now and find dozens of movies with plenty of violence, profanity, and gratuitous nudity that never were released to theaters. Half of them have Bruce Willis or some other recognizable star (actually I think he's in more than half). I can find vast swathes of drawings and fanfic packed with eye-rolling depictions of sex, and it's all free for anyone who can bother to consume it. I mean, I'd be nuts to say that no one has ever gotten rich by shoveling out bad art, just as I'd be naive to think that quality always leads to success. But pandering is far from a winning formula.
    I don't know about other companies, but nudity in White Wolf books was very carefully calculated. It's not like they accidentally had a bunch of nudity in their books or anything.

    White Wolf's goal was to use graphic pictures (including nudity) and adult themes in books to attract gamers who were tired of, or uninterested in White Wolf's other competitors. And remember that for awhile, White Wolf was the second most successful RPG company on the market right after TSR. So the use of nudity in their books was carefully calculated to bring in profits, and importantly, it actually worked. It gave White Wolf books more of an adult vibe, and helped them to grab a core audience composed mainly of edgy older teens, young adult guys and women(!) - White Wolf had the largest number of female gamers of any other RPG company during the 90s, and many were attracted to the more realistic and adult representations and themes in White Wolf books.

    I wouldn't say that they came because of the artwork, but it certainly did help to set certain moods, themes and expectations that were clearly popular with gamers (male and female) in the mid-90s.

    Now, of course, Exalted fans in 2015 are not the same ones who were buying Exalted products back during 2005, and they're definitely not the same people who were buying White Wolf games back in 1995. Times change, what was once good becomes bad, things that were good business decisions then might not be good today, etc

    But I don't think White Wolf/OPP should feel any need to offer any excuses or apologies for using nudity in their products. That was just a business decision and one that was actually pretty successful at the time. It's just not a policy that OPP should continue to use today, because hey, it's a different world and different audience.
