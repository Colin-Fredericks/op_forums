    Originally posted by Gayo View Post

    Well, previously the game was set up to cut off as many as your resources as possible just for being in the Underworld, which severely limited your ability to actually engage with the game's system.
    Maybe it should do that.

    The Underworld should not, in my opinion, be a place you can just walk into for a day trip. Even if they aren't going to go the full "the fact that the Underworld exists at all is a fucked-up mistake created by the Exalted murdering things that shouldn't have been capable of being murdered" on it, it shouldn't be the kind of place that the quick, even the Exalted, can easily penetrate deep into.

    Because the Underworld isn't for the quick. It's for the dead. There is no food for the quick. There is no water for the quick. The air and land itself oppresses them. The very essence of the place is dark and alien and doesn't nourish and sustain even an Exalt in the same way it does in Creation. If you're quick, and you want to go to, say, Stygia, that shouldn't be "it's a long trip, pack a lunch." That should be "this is a lengthy and terrifying journey through an alien realm that rejects you even as it tries to draw you in." It should be the kind of thing that even makes experienced Sijanese Funerists blanch away from you for suggesting it (that's the kind of journey they put off onto the dead members of their order, because trying it as one of the quick is a great way to become dead) and the sort of thing that even Lunars and Sidereals should react to by wanting to talk to one of the few specially trained experts in that kind of thing among their own ranks. (This is the reason dudes like Black Ice Shadow exist.)

    There are, of course, places where the realms of the quick and the dead come together. Thorns is such a place now! There are lots of little ancestor cults and shadowlands all across Creation. But the shadowlands are not the Underworld; they're actually considerably more hospitable to the quick and they're still pretty bad.

    I would really like the Underworld to not be a place you can just tool up and barge into. It isn't Creation's weird goth brother who you can go visit sometimes.

    And that's okay. The Underworld is for ghosts. And Abyssals. And Liminals. It doesn't also have to be for everyone else.

    Originally posted by Erinys
    I want Sidereals who have enough brain cells to seriously consider the Underworld a major threat, realize that the Contagion was connected to it, investigate the Underworld enough to notice that something was stirring, and have plans for how to help the Dragon-Blooded combat it, not being completely blindsided by Thorns because "Herp derp, it was outside of Fate, like, it never occured to me that anything could come from there!" In 2E and especially 1E this made them look very stupid.
    People keep saying this and I'm always baffled by it.

    You know who is aware the Great Contagion came out of the Underworld? The Neverborn and the Deathlords, and that's it. (Maybe some Abyssals.) Can you explain to me how the Sidereals are supposed to get intel out of those people? The Sidereals are pretty good at sussing out secrets, but sometimes things get past them, and "a Deathlord we barely know exists hauls a doom plague we didn't know was possible out of a hole in reality we also don't know exists, located in an inaccessible part of the death world where we rarely go, and she didn't tell anyone except her Deathlord buddies" seems like it should be one of those things.

    Prior to Thorns, why should they consider the Underworld a major threat, especially now in 3e with the Deathlords being significantly scaled back in power and reach? In the close to five thousand year history of Creation prior to Mask of Winters rolling in and being all "'sup fools, Scarlet's gone and it is my time to shine now!" can you name one significant threat the Underworld has actually thrown at Creation that the Sidereals would know about?

    Should the Sidereals have been keeping an eye on the place? Why, yes, they should... and they do! Black Ice Shadow exists because the Sidereals were like "hey, keeping an eye on the Underworld is one of the things we do, and we have a chance to get ourselves a Chosen of Saturn who is deeply steeped in its essence and mysteries. Sounds good, let's do it!"

    But there's a difference between keeping an eye on the place and devoting significant resources to it, especially given how overworked and overstretched the Sidereals are. They also keep an eye on Malfeas, but they don't invest significant resources into that either, because they're really very sure that the Yozi's cannot, in fact, escape. This is how the Ebon Dragon managed to run his wedding past them in 1e and 2e. The Sidereals cannot be everywhere at once.

    You are absolutely correct that the Sidereals should have spies and agents and webs of contacts in the Underworld. But there is a difference between that, and "we know secrets even the Deathlords hold close among their damned company." The Underworld is just not going to be that important to them unless it gives them a reason to be. "There are ghost-kings called Deathlords; they're pretty powerful in the context of the Underworld" isn't that reason. "They can roll up into Creation and fuck up a major city-state" is.

                                Perhaps I'm not writing clearly (this is not sarcasm). I don't think the Sidereals should know that the Deathlords, specifically, caused the Contagion. I think they should have enough external reasons and native intelligence to regard the Underworld as a genuine threat, pay some attention to it, and in time figure out that the Contagion came from there. Long before the Deathlords marched armies into Creation. Centuries before. They should really have paid at least some attention to it at least since the Usurpation, if not the Primordial War.

                                I've also always believed, as a basic setting axiom, that the different realms are different enough from each other that their essence, creations, denizens, and materials can be distinguished from each other with some educated research and investigation: that, for example, if a savant took some Malfean brass, some quintessence metal, some mundane brass, and some grave-goods brass, and compared them, they could tell they're not identical substances and with some time and effort, figure out which is which. I assume that those substances, like the essence of the different realms, are actually not identical. That an informed savant can tell a Celestial gorilla god apart from a Blood Ape. That Exalts in a shadowland can tell that they've entered a different place because the essence feels different, or just doesn't come to them. (In the real world scientists can tell apart the raw materials from different locations on the same exact planet, from isotope ratios. Here we're talking about separate universes. I can't imagine they wouldn't differ in noticeable ways, or that all the research and knowledge of the First Age would never have uncovered any of those ways.) Until just now, it had never occured to me that (outside my headcanon?) it could be truly impossible to determine that the Contagion (or any other Underworld thing/phenomenon/substance) came from the Underworld. That just doesn't fit my strong mental image of the worlds being fundamentally different from one another, down to their essence.

                                To me there are only 3 possible explanations:
                                Sidereal blindness to everything outside of Fate until after it personally attacks them;
                                the Underworld being so boring and empty that until the Deathlords showed themselves, everybody mostly just ignored it.
                                and/or the Underworld being so impossible to interact with that nobody alive, not even millennia-old First Age Exalts, are able to learn anything about it.
                                Whichever it is, I don't like the setting being that way.

                                The Underworld shouldn't be boring and empty, it ought to be filled with memories of everything that's ever been important to anybody who's still a ghost and many who aren't, in the entire history of Creation since at least the last part of the Primordial War. It should be packed with stuff superimposed on/mingled with other stuff, juxtaposing different eras of history, interacting in weird ways. Things should happen. The dead should do stuff. I also think they should definitely be people, even if they can't be called human anymore due to the lack of body and po and "life-force" (for lack of a better word).

                                I'd like reasons to care about the Underworld, other than "OMG The World Is Coming To An End!" Hopefully instead of that. I'm never likely to care as much as an Abyssals fan, but until now, other than the apparently immanent end of the world, I didn't feel a reason to care at all. It all looked so boring and empty to me.

                                        Sidereals have finite resources, made even more finite by their decision to promote Dragon-Blooded rule and suppress the Solars and Lunars. By definition, any time a Sid is assigned to something, that means another assignment is no longer tasked.

                                        So, how many of the 80-ish Sids that are active at any given time should be given the job of monitoring the Underworld, the Wyld, and Malfeas (all outside of Fate) instead of taking care of Creation and Yu-Shan? I can see the conversation now...

                                        Brindle, called the Worrier, Chosen of Battles: "Sifu Kejak, we need another two operatives to beef up our efforts in the Underworld. We suspect that the Contagion may have originated there."

                                        Chejop Kejak, called the Poor Overworked Bastard, Chosen of Secrets: "Look Brindle, we have been through this. The Fae have fallen to in-fighting, but they still occupy a good portion of Creation's borders. I've got people tied up keeping tabs on them WHILE avoiding the damned Lunars. And then of course I need to protect the infant "Realm" from the Lunars until they can breed their numbers back up.

                                        Meanwhile, this upjumped "Scarlet Empress" is my only way to control the Sword of Creation, and she has the temerity to demand my time, and the time of our best Sifus to teach her damnable brood. I have no choice but to honor her requests, because she could die at any moment, and we need replacements for her throne. The last six hundred years demonstrated that only a single strong ruler has any chance of chaining the Dragon's Blood.

                                        The gods that survived down there are angry and resentful. They are already forming cartels to demand worship and sandbag the proper working of Creation if they don't get what they want. Most of the Censors died fighting the Crusade. Those that didn't are now tacitly supporting the hubris of "Elemental Courts," to allow themselves to work less and get more power in the process. Meanwhile, dozens of gods are out of work, and a decent number of them are working for now working for Thulio. So, I need to send people to beg the Censors to their jobs, cow the gods, and hopefully head off Thulio's recruiting efforts.

                                        So, tell me, Brindle, which of those efforts would you short-change?"

                                        Brindle: "I apologize, Sifu. You are of course correct. I only wish to prevent another Contagion."

                                        Chejop: "And the Empress is our best hope for that. As long as we have her to guard Creation and wield the Sword, everything will be fine."

                                        ===Answer to OP===

                                        The most important thing I want from the Underworld is playability.

                                        I want the Neverborn in the background, stuck in their hole, with "I Suck and Die Prana" running full-tilt. The tail-end of 2E became so bogged down in the "doings" of the Neverborn, when they really shouldn't be "acting" at all. They are a source of trouble, yes, but they aren't supposed to be in the spotlight.

                                        I want the Deathlords to be varied and depowered. As others have said, when the Deathlords are E10 Super-Solars, all bent on the conquest and destruction of Creation, it's hard to just tell stories and have fun.

                                        I want Abyssals to have enough narrative freedom to be playable in a standard mixed group. I also want more narratives for all-Abyssal groups than "We gonna fuck Creation up, heh heh," and "OMG we ran away from the Deathlords keep running!"

                                        I want the Underworld itself to be an interesting, though formidable, place to explore for normal PCs. But, I also need, as a ST, to be able to push it into the background. Strange, occult planes of existence can siphon attention away from a core story pretty easily.
