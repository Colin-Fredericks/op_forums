    Abyssals, a new take

    12-09-2013, 03:22 PM
    I wrote up a rant about Abyssals, and why I don't like them.

    For ease, I'll put it here:


    I guess, since I said I would, I should talk a little bit about Abyssals.

    Whereas I don't like like Solars because they are a good concept that just doesn't work out quite right, devolving more into Mary Sue territory... Abyssals just start out bad and get worse.

    First, they're a corrupted Solar. Great, now they have the flaws that Solars have as characters but without any of the redeeming qualities. Everyone hates you, and it's for a good reason this time...because you're a horrible human being. But fine, if you want to play an unrepentant villain and just revel in your dark evilness of darkness, fine. That's not my playstyle, but okay have fun. Be a supervillain. Go. Have fun.

    But then you try to be an anti-hero. Oh gods. First off, anti-heroes aren't appealing to me in the first place, so you're going to have to be really good at pulling it off from the get-go. Exactly how am I supposed to sympathize with you when you go around murdering and looting with free abandon? You're a "designated hero." The only way I know that you're the hero of the story is because the story says you are. Usually by making the villain worse somehow, and with the bodycounts steadily rising from the "hero" and the villain....at this point I just want to stand up and walk away from the table.

    I suppose you could play a redemption Abyssal...but then it's kinda like just playing a Solar in emo makeup. They don't need more emo makeup.

    And on that thread, we've got that Abyssals used to be Solars. That just makes everything worse. What is trying to be said is that "In each Solar is this thread of darkness." Except what is actually said is "Here's the evil Solars, and there's the good Solars." This just makes the "good" Solars more insufferable, because they aren't Abyssals, QED they must be in the right, and that's what the story reinforces! The selection of Solar shards to become Abyssals being random would have been okay, if it seemed to have any sort of impact on the aesthetics and storylines at all. Instead, it just seems to be the "bad" Solars that spoiled the Solar barrel and gave them all a bad name, bringing us back to the "unfairly persecuted" Mary Sue Solars.


    I see Solars as a good idea that just needs a bit of tweaking to distance themselves from the Mary Sue territory they dug themselves into.

    Abyssals...I don't even know how to fix Abyssals. I can't even ignore them because even them existing taints the interesting aspects of the Solars. I guess the only way would be to make them their entire new thing, completely separate from Solars. But I don't see that happening.



    They can be a decent villain splat, (if a bit emo,) but really, they fail in any other aspect. And in an world trying to be complex and well-rounded in its views....this one-dimensional Exaltation really falls flat. (har har.)


    The main points are:

    1. They are a one-dimensional villain splat
    2. They are bleak darkness of dark darkitude.
    3. Their relationship with Solars detracts from Solar story, and does not add.

    Feel free to disagree with me, but that's not the point of this thread. Instead, it is inspired by my closing statement: "Abyssals...I don't even know how to fix Abyssals."


    Or in other words....

    Challenge Accepted.

    When the Sun first set his foot down upon Creation, his might and grace radiated from him, that even his formless shadow became a God in its own right.

    It is a terrible lesson that should be considered: The Sun, in his marvelous glory, is so overpowering that he even gives form and shape simply from his absence. Is it then, surprising, that such a thing would happen when he created his Chosen?

    When the Sun imparted just a piece of his glory into these glorious shards, the shadows he cast were also formed: everything the Solar was not. They fled his light into the darkest reaches they could to avoid his gaze, eventually finding the Void, that space of non-existence created when the Primordials forged Creation out the Wyld.

    These shadows of the Solar Exalted would have remained entrapped in this realm of non-existence, if not for the foolishness and pride of the Exalted host. They first trapped the hungering spirits of the dead Primordials--the Neverborn--into the Void to be disposed of and forgotten, bringing that faint spark of existence into the Void. And then, worse, they woke it up.

    The Neverborn hungered for revenge, to eat away at the world and reality so denied to them. They searched the formless Void and found these shadows of the Solar Exalted, and with their stolen secrets, forged their own Exalted from the shadows of the Sun's own designs. They toiled in their unreality, bringing each and every one to their designs (what did they care of the Law of Diminishment? How can you diminish something that no longer exists?) and then released them upon Creation with one singular goal: Consume.


    The Neverborn guide these Abyssal exaltations upon mortals, just as the Sun chooses his own Solars. However, as a hollow mockery they choose those that are weak, who seek power in greed, and who utterly fail in their moment of glory. Oh, those chosen are great, but they are also terrible and loathsome, antithetical to everything the Sun strives for.

    The power comes upon the mortal much as the Solar's does: unbidden and unasked for. The Shadows and the powers of the Void seep into her soul, empowering her darkest desires. For some, they feel a strong resonance with this unending emptiness, willing to consume and destroy all they wish, just as the Neverborn desired.

    However, most mortals quell at such power, shaken by the Void that now taints them. They are given new light upon their own destructive nature, and when faced with the sheer consumption of unreality--shirk. But many still fall into the base desires of the Void, enticed by the darkness and simply not strong enough to avoid its gnawing hunger. Often, these people will try to sate their thirst, targeting those that--in their eyes--deserve to die. Those that prey on the weak, those that slay as the first group of Abyssals would.

    And yet, there are those that, when looking into the mouth of the Void within them, realize how precious life truly is. They fight against the darkness in their hearts, clawing at what remains of their life, protecting others, teaching them to appreciate what already exists. Only they truly know what nothing is, and so will fight to keep anything alive.


    Still a few problems, but I think it makes them more well-rounded and less one-dimensional. Even though we've still got "loyalist, anti-hero, renagade," it feels more even in its execution. It keeps ties with Solars, but doesn't force them to be the "fallen" Solars. They were created this way from the beginning. They are starting from the darkness. They can move to the "light" just as a Solar can move into "darkness."

                      While I do agree with the above, that concern only matters when considering the current idea of the Neverborn, rather than what they could possibly be. What is a Primordial? The condensation of a concept into a spiritual form, from which tangential aspects cascade. What is a Neverborn? A dead concept. What is a dead concept? That is a trickier question.

                      Is it, perhaps, an idea which no one ever considers? Can such a completely abandoned idea even exist, when believers in a flat Earth still existed in our own world not so long ago? Is it instead like a dead language, an idea which is no longer used and does not change, but is only observed? Given the stupendous power of the Exalted, is it a gaping hole in reality where something used to be? Is is an inversion, the inside of a mask like the Underworld compared to Creation? That last one perhaps holds some value, as the death of one Primordial brought life to the region where its body fell. In the end, it doesn't really matter, does it? They all might work, so you might as well throw them in there as campaign hooks.

                                  I think a part of the problem is that the concept of an afterlife is a familiar, comfortable one to most of the Western world. Death is a transition to another state of existence, death is a natural thing, and so by extension, the transition to that other state of existence is also relatively natural. We have gods that act as guides to the recently dead, visitable afterlives, and the kingdom of the dead as a part of the natural order. From Greece's Elysian Fields, to the Maitlands and the Deetzes learning to get along, to Demi Moore and Patrick Swazye spinning a wheel post-mortem; we have a consistent refrain of the living and the dead finding a peaceful co-existence. Even with the counterexamples (often in the same media!) it's presented as a balance and a natural thing.

                                  A lot of us also have enough familiarity with Buddhism to understand Nirvana as the kind of complete ego annihilation that the Void seems to promise and which the Neverborn nominally seek. Surcease of suffering; the final balm in Gilead.

                                  This is also why you see a lot of people that try and soften Abyssals by pushing towards 'Solars of the Underworld'. To make them the psychopomps, the guardians of the good death, the destroyer of those things whose time has come. A turning of violence towards good purpose.

                                  Thing is, there *is* an Exalt type of psychopomps, guardians of the good death and the destroyer of things whose time has come. The Chosen of Endings.

                                  The Underworld in Exalted isn't meant to be part of the natural order- it's meant to be a *horrifying abberration* of that order. An awful and terrible thing. It's the antithesis of the good death- it's the thing that's lingered on past it's time. Nothing wholesome for the living of Creation should arise from the Underworld. This is why there are no natural gods of the Underworld, nor does Heaven try and extend it's domain thereupon. This is why everything that draws on necrotic essence is inherently a Creature of Darkness. The Underworld isn't where things go to die, or even where things go when they've died- it's where things go when they should be gone but *aren't*.

                                  The hardest part for making interesting Abyssals isn't in contrast with the Solars- they don't actually share that much conceptual space in my experience, save as people choose to play it up. The hard part is making reasonable and sympathetic Abyssals that don't end up looking like Reckoners with darker and edgier makeup.

                                  (Sidenote: A lot of this is the result of pondering some comments that Holden and Hatewheel made over time, which have caused me to re-consider my thinking on both the Underworld and the Abyssals. It still is likely not right!)

                                      Yeah, I think the write-up in the first post here doesn't give even the 2e Abyssals book enough credit. I'll try and put down what I see as core design conceits for an Abyssals splat in a poorly-worded and rambling form, as it's finals week for me. I agree with some of the issues; They should not be one-dimensional villains, and I really don't see the need for them to be emo, though I think just how dead you are inside should be something of an issue for many Abyssals. I've played Abyssals for a long time at this point with a big group of great players, and I think that I've got a handle on how I'd like the splat to go that isn't too terrible.

                                      1. Hardly anybody knows what you are.

                                      Solars existed historically and get spoken about by the Immaculate Order and the secret Cult of the Illuminated. Sidereals know all about Solars. Lunars probably have an oral history of Solars. Infernals have a peerage in Hell, and tend to be closely associated with certain types of demons, giving them at least a certain resemblance to the "Crazy Akuma Solars" that existed during history, and in my opinion, they should have more superficial/appearance based similarities to Solars.

                                      Abyssals are probably some weird form of Anathema to anyone who follows the Immaculate Faith, but outside of Lookshy, the splat's hardly had time to develop a reputation and history in a world where most of the population is illiterate. It may be pretty obvious after some time that you're a weird sort of Death Exalt, maybe the world's strongest Ghost-Blooded, but what exactly you do is largely a legend you create that relies on no existing precedent. Whether you're a bad guy or a scary good guy or a weird guy whose appearance tends to coincide with bad people spontaneously turning into mincemeat, well, you'll define that in your story.

                                      2. Liminals are Ghost Hunters now? Well, you're a Ghost Brother.

                                      What are Ghosts in Creation? They're the ones who didn't or couldn't pass into Lethe and reincarnate because they had unfinished business, wanted more out of life, or just weren't ready. Well, your Exaltation is just like that, except it's backed by the power of a Solar shard and done under the tutelage of the nigh-almighty Deathlords. Maybe, in 3E, Ghosts can be made to not be completely dull and weak and actually comprise an interesting group of millions to be involved with. This opens up tons of plot potential, as virtually everyone you know in your knew life as some form of unfinished business, or some axe to grind before they go, and nobody walks between the two worlds quite as casually or as dangerously as you do.

                                      On this note, I think Abyssals have a lot of room to develop "charms" that key off of the fact that they are pretty much the only beings in Creation that have died and come back to life, and this opens up space for tons of powers that aren't straight copy-paste jobs of Solar powers; While I love the connection to Solars, I hate the Mirror keyword with a passion 99% of the time. During the other 1%, there's an interesting and thematic difference between the Abyssal and Solar version of the Charm.

                                      3. You're a Ghost Sister, the Deathlords are Mom and Dad, and they're not so different from you.

                                      The Deathlords died after having what was, from their perspective, history's greatest injustice perpetrated against them. They refused to go away and made a pact with forces from beyond to become powerful enough to exact justice. That's probably a lot like your story, and at least one of the Deathlords probably has a story that matches up very closely to your reasons for sticking around and not just trying your luck next time as a squirrel. In addition to having some of the most powerful tutors in the setting teaching you how to be as much of a badass as possible, you're also learning about who some real assholes are, people who are responsible for the kind of things that make you angry. And, all of a sudden, you're very scary when you're angry.

                                      4. Avenging Angels aren't necessarily bad guys.

                                      When you're released into Creation, you have lots of room to do good things, and even the Deathlords are probably pushing you on that path to some extent. The people you take down are typically going to have done terrible things, the kind of things people can't forget even after millennia in Stygia. Your stance on collateral damage and whatever side activities you take are largely up to you.

                                      The nature of your powers and the neuroses you carry with you (see Shard's Dark Fate) will, though, ensure that the methods you use to take people down tend to be rather destructive. Exigent of Yo Ping, sadly, you aren't.

                                      5. There's more than one way to go from here.

                                      I see at least three clear and distinct options for an Abyssal at this point. One, usually reserved for NPCs but interesting in the hands of the right player, involves becoming so radicalized that they fully back the mission of the Neverborn and want to see the world end, not in the paltry sense of the Ebon Dragon overthrowing the ruling order, but truly bringing about an end to everything. Pulling an Abyssal like this back from the brink can serve for an amazing story, and in the process, the Deathlords themselves may even debate their long term purpose. The second option is to be an Abyssal, but try to walk the tightrope of humanity, remaking the world into what you view as a better, more just place with your destructive powers. Trying to be true to your identity both as an Abyssal and as a human. Lastly, you've got those who can't take the destructive methods any longer, those Compassion 5 types who seek to escape the Abyss entirely, or others who end up considering the state of themselves and their Deathlord masters to be the greatest trick the Primordials ever pulled. These characters may seek redemption for themselves, and they may seek to get their friends, and perhaps even their Deathlord, out of the grip of the Neverborn.

                                                  I think what bugs me about Abyssals can be summed up thusly:

                                                  1. Dark Fate

                                                  If the Neverborn can make people spontaneously up and die in Creation, then why the hell aren't they doing this all the time, all day, everyday? I get that the gameplay reason is to drive home a "damned if you do/damned if you don't" mentality, but it really limits the stories you can tell with an Abyssal to "emo" or "murderhobo".

                                                  Now as every use of Necromancy drags the Void into Creation, I'd much rather see every Abyssal get the Necromancy induction Charms for free (once they meet the minimum Essence requirements). I'd like to see Abyssals burdened by a more traditional Limit Break that drives them to Nihilism, making their Frenzy mechanic more personal ("what have I done!") and less about untouchable NPC characters ("rocks fall, all your loved ones die").

                                                  2. The ****ing Deathlords

                                                  Don't get me wrong. The Deathlords are interesting. Working for a Deathlord Liege is interesting. Sacrificing freedom for power is interesting. (And as far as 3rd Ed is concerned: learning the custom charms of a First Age superbeing is interesting.) What is not interesting, is if you have no choice but to play these stories. The Deathlords define the Deathknights: either your character is ruled by a Deathlord, or is ruled by their opposition of their Deathlord. (And as far as 3rd ed is concerned, if your only path to character development is becoming more like your Deathlord then that's 2nd Ed's Infernals-Yozi problem all over again.)

                                                  Now if Deathlords were powerful rulers among the dead, and Deathknights are the champions of the dead we have a more interesting dynamic than the Massa/Slave set up we have now. Remove the leash from the hands of the Deathlords and have Abyssals choose whether or not to deal with the Deathlords on their own terms. Now the mechanics of Exaltation can stay pretty much the same, great power offered to people on the verge of death. Maybe it's the Whispers of the Neverborn that offer Exaltation or Death. Maybe Deathlords can sense when a mortal is about to Exalt as an Abyssal and come to give a big speech and potentially pick up a new recruit... but it's all bluster and no substance. Monstrances can stay but their power needs to be neutered: they shouldn't be a shock collar or a kill switch, they should be a liability - a paper fetter holding down a god.

                                                  ****

                                                  With these changes, they're not "a one-dimensional villain splat": they are the Exalted of Death, capable of telling whatever stories they want to tell. They might still be the "bleak darkness of dark darkitude", as their themes will still be death and nothingness, but it's a darkness put firmly in the players hands - no more NPCs gimping your loved ones no more being press ganged into Team Kill-The-World. Their relationship with Solars is that of a dark mirror. Now you could argue that this doesn't add anything to the Solar mythos and screws over the design space of Evil Solars, but by putting agency in the players hands instead of setting in stone in a sourcebook, you can play any Solar concept as an Abyssal. There won't be a Redemption or Destruction dichotomy, and instead Abyssals will get to play whatever they want to be in whichever shade of grey they like... just like Solars. Which would actually let your explore the theme that Abyssals are !Solars.
