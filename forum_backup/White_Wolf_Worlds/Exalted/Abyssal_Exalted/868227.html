    What do you want out of the Underworld, Abyssals, etc...?

    04-19-2016, 02:46 AM
    Basically, all topics Underworld related that aren't completely new to this edition. What worked? What didn't? Any ideas on improvements?

    For me, I want an Underworld that matters. One that doesn't make sense for everyone to completely ignore except for the Deathlords, and those who later worry about the Deathlords. I think the Sidereals should have realized the problem there sometime after the Scarlet Empress took over and before Thorns, because it makes them look stupid if they didn't even consider the fact it might have some link to the Underworld. I want there to be a thousand kingdoms that had tinpot tyrants propped up by the Deathlords, as a way to hide themselves behind proxies as their power grew. I want a Stygia with smart rebels who don't try fighting face to face, but with trickery and hitting fast. Likely they should have some weapons and support from Sidereals funneling them weapons in the hopes of destabilizing some of the power of the Deathlords. Show us the different groups and give us reasons to care about the Dual Monarchy. Why have the Deathlords left them around? Is it because they're weak, or because they're a threat enough to even those who claim the title of Deathlord?

    And there's more, but at the heart of it, we need a better Underworld as a foundation for better Underworld related groups.

                              I recently went back and reread some of the 1ed Abyssal-book, and something that I didn't notice reading it some years ago, but that I really think that book does a good job at capturing, at parts, is the baseness of existence.

                              The occasional focus on, and/or description of bodily functions, ( /body horror, nakedness etc.) and coldness* (discomforts) of life, and the body/mortality as something unwanted, something less than fantastic, and maybe even a prison, really resonates with the anti-life kaos-gnosticistic satanism themes that I feel the Abyssals incorporate so well. I would really like to see that still present, in parts at least, of the Abyssal write down.

                              In the larger-than-life genre mainly incorporated by the Solars stories, where the dreams and aspirations of the characters soars high and the players symbolise the hope for a better future, where the possibilities for a brighter tomorrow is ever-present,
                              Focusing on the body, the baseness of life, the uncomfortable truths and inevitables of existence, becomes a questioning of whether life is actually at all that great, and a perfect contrasting genre to that of the Solars, further strengthening the Core Conflict of the Solars as symbols for the Highest, the highet hopes we all can strive for, and the Abyssals, as symbols for the Lowest, the darkest depths we all can sink to.

                                                      Before reading anything posted in this thread:

                                                      * Oblivion is a state of non-being. It is not a force that actively destroys anything, but rather the condition of having been completely destroyed. Oblivion is what what happens when you Ghost-Eating Technique a god; the god is obliterated.

                                                      * Oblivion does not whisper or drive creatures mad -- the Neverborn do.

                                                      * The Deathlords and Abyssal Exalted do not draw power from Oblivion, because Oblivion is not a creature or force. It is "nothing" to the extent that Elsewhere is "no where", and while Exalted have Charms that interact with Elsewhere, there will never be anything that draws power from "the state of being no where".

                                                      * Any power that needs to be begged, borrowed, or burgled should come from the Neverborn or powerful ghosts. This includes powerful ghosts who have Neverborn-derived power, such as the Deathlords.

                                                      * Ghosts struggle to avoid the loss of self which would plunge them into Lethe and reincarnation; they do not struggle against Oblivion unless assaulted by a force that seeks to obliterate them.

                                                      * Abyssal Exalted have the power to Obliterate creatures because all of the Exalted do; they were MEANT to kill things Deader Than Dead, in order to have a chance at killing things which could not die.

                                                      * Both the Abyssal Exalted AND the readers need to care about the denizens of the Underworld as if they were people, and this is rather hollow if ghosts just AREN'T people. If necessary, be unclear and ambiguous about whether ghosts are people or merely the mindless, endlessly-repeating echoes of dead people. If the only people in the Underworld are 100ish Deathknights, will many players or even many Deathknights care to dwell there for long?

                                                      * The Charms of the Abyssal Exalted should reflect undeath in general -- memory; the stubborn refusal to forget, forgive, forsake, or pass on; stagnation; repetition; leeching life and material goods from the living world and its creatures. They should also reflect undeath in variety -- Charms that would be at home in the most hellish or heavenly of afterlives, though seldom without an unhealthy twist to remind you that this is an unnatural state of being for Creation.

                                                      * As a sub-note to the above, the afterlives that Abyssals reflect should include the elemental environments -- hells of fire, hells of ice, hells of poisonous swamps or drowning depths or deserts of endless thirst.

                                                      * The Deathlords should be archetypical embodiments of the things the Abyssals represent. Where an Abyssal can mix and match to taste, each Deathlord should be a two-dimensional caricature of a particular field of power of which Abyssals can learn.
