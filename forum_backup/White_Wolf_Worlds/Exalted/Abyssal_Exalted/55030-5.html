                            Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                            That's the crux of an Abyssal player's conundrum. He wants to play an Abyssal, not a Solar. Otherwise, he would have skipped playing an Abyssal and played a Solar. So, from a playability perspective, Abyssals must be a splat that you can play without being forced to turn into another splat. A player should not feel bad or punished for playing an Abyssal instead of a Solar.

                            I think we need to change what we mean when we talk about "redemption" for Abyssals. The "sin" that an Abyssal needs redemption from is not being an Abyssal. Their "sin" is agreeing to kill the world. An Abyssal can be a tempted being, more able to solve problems with indiscriminate murder, but if he wants to recant on that promise without becoming a Solar, the game should let him. Sure, make him take the hard road. Deny him some of the most effective tools. Let his Deathlord see him as a traitor to the cause (or maybe not - some of the Deathlords really don't want Creation gone). Have him bear some pain for denying the purpose for which he was created. Just don't make him go gold instead of black.
                            I've heard this before, but I've never understood it. No, you shouldn't be forced to turn into a Solar. And you shouldn't feel punished for playing an Abyssal instead of a Solar. But why do you need moral validation of your character's decision to remain an Abyssal, in order to enjoy playing an Abyssal? I don't think you should get that.

                            The sin that an Abyssal needs redemption for is their assent to killing the world. But being an Abyssal is inextricable from that. If someone recants and says "I won't be a world-destroying weapon anymore" ...then if they walk that path to the end there's no way to continue to be an Abyssal, and they shouldn't even want to, because that's absolutely core to what an Abyssal is. Trying to "repent" without abandoning that path is like wanting to be a vegetarian without giving up bacon. When you say "Yes, give me the Second Breath and I will kill the world" the decision you make is not "enlisting as a soldier for the cause of Oblivion". Sure, the Neverborn would prefer if you were on task, you'd be more efficient that way, but ultimately what is asked of you, and what you agree to, is becoming an Abyssal. It's the nature of the Black Exaltation: Will ye or nill ye, death shall ye cause.

                            Originally posted by FromBeyond
                            Noooope they don't. I mean, we can be all metaphysical, but I fail to see what a "black spot on the soul" actually means. I remind you that Creation has no concept of "sin". Agreeing to kill the world might make you feel guilty. Or it might not. It depends entirely on your culture and upbringing. But as itself, it has no real wight to it whether you actually meant it or not.
                            The point isn't how you feel about it, or what you "meant". The point is that you agreed to become an Abyssal, and now you are one, and that means you bring death and entropy wherever you go. The guy who thinks "Ha, I totally played that Deathlord! I didn't mean it!" is like the fledgling vampire who thinks "Well, I'm not a sadistic psychopath like those other vampires! I'm not going to be a blood-drinking predator!" Good luck with that.

                                                            Attempts at subjective morality aside, there's nobody out there who's going to hear you agreed to commit omnicide and not think "Wow, you're an asshole.". There's no system of personal ethics outside maybe of total solipsism that's going to say that's okay. And if you're laughing in someone's face about it, well then you're really an asshole.

                                                            That said, it's self-evident. There's no need for the book to talk about it at length, or to punish you for it, because either you're sorry for it or you aren't. If you aren't, your "punishment" is how plotting omnicide rarely garners friends among the living. If you are, then your guilt is your punishment and ideally your reasons why give you some good characterization fodder. There's no need for some outside force to pass moral judgment on you, and doing so clouds the more useful point that how you feel afterward about your decision to become an Abyssal is foundational to what kind of character you're portraying.
