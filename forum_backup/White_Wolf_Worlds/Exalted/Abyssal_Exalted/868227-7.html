    On the subject of ghosts:

    ​I just watched a video of the last five or so minutes of My Dinner with Andre, in which the title character actually uses the term "ghost" to refer to how mysterious and unreachable other people are, especially as you become closer to them and thus have more opportunities to be aware of how little you really know them. It's actually part of a longer dialogue concerning awareness and experience of death, which may also be relevant but that I won't go into here, but it was interesting to me because it touches on a subject I'm fascinated by in general, and have sometimes considered as a basis for considering and portraying ghosts (and Fair Folk). The notion that what it might functionally mean for ghosts to be without one of their souls is that there might be very little of them there beyond what you see on the surface, but this doesn't necessarily detract from relating to them, because that might be the only level at which you ever relate to anybody; it might matter more as a basis for their actions independent of relating to others.

    There can be granularity to that, such that maybe only the very most insignificant and weak ghosts actually present it as being completely repetitive of some living daily routine and largely unaware of their surroundings. That most have a level of depth that could be sufficient to have some decent relationship with them for a while (with just how long depending on who you are as well; there are probably people who could get by their entire lives with a relationship in which they never really know the other person). That's short of the most advanced ghosts, the ones who could be said to have enough substance to realise what they're lacking and try to fill it in by maintaining connections with and the prominence of things from their memories, or retaining ties to living relatives and others who can provide them with fleeting substance of blood and memory, and ultimately live vicariously for them.

    The alternative being ghosts who, with the absence of such things (or having finally lost them all to time), fill themselves out with what they can draw from the other direction. The Deathlords being the most powerful and sophisticated of these.

    In some of those senses, I feel as though it's possible to treat Deathlords with some depth and nuance, even sympathy, even if they're all still characterised as ultimately hating the living, or at least having fearsome designs on them.

    I would be interested if these could be things significant to Abyssals regardless of their directions, regardless of how much personal investment and loyalty they can have towards the figures who have a lot of experience and teaching to provide them.

                            I think Abyssals should be more connected to ghosts, and Liminals more connected to corpses.

                            As to whether a ghost is a person or not: in Buddhism, a sentient being is composed of five Skandha, aggregate functions that arise as a result of our existence. Material form, feelings, perceptions, volitions, and senses continuously begin and end, and as such, there is no real self, no real "I."

                            So hypothetically, in Creation, when a person dies, their Skandha are disconnected. Some are housed in the Hun, in the Po, and in the corpse. And many of these are a components of a human, and to say that they aren't human is to say that our moonlight isn't also our sunlight. So a ghost is human, but not a complete human, possessing several but not all Skandha. If self is an illusion, then with a ghost the illusion grows even more dim.

                            However, there's a big difference between knowing something and really internalizing it. I have accepted that I have no self, that "I" does not exist, but it's very difficult to keep in mind at all times, as I have to use personal nominative pronouns like "I" to interact with others. As such, characters may academically act clinically to a ghost, but react very differently because it tugs at their intimacies.
