                I can explain it if you're interested. Not really much to do with Exalted though.
                Baptists don't allow Christenings, only adult baptism, while Anglican do Christenings and adult baptisms. A lot of stricter Baptists believe in predestination, but the Anglican church is firmly in the camp of Ariminianism (ie there's no predestination). Baptists believe that the bread and wine are only metaphorical representations of Christ's body and blood. Anglicans... have a very vague position in order to let people of both opinions fit into the Church.
                Anglicans believe in apostolic succession of bishops and priests, Baptists believe all Christians are priests. Anglicans have a hierarchy of vicars/bishops/archbishops, Baptists do not. Anglican vicars are appointed by the hierarchy, Baptists vote for their pastors. Anglicans didn't allow women to be vicars/bishops until relatively recently, Baptists have I think allowed it for longer, though I'm not sure (I've never seen/met a female Baptist pastor, but I don't remember it being explicitly banned). There are also stylistic differences, with Anglicans having a more formalised liturgy, but that's not a theological thing. Anglicans include a wider strand than Baptists, so an Anglican church could be evangelical or virtually Catholic, while Baptist churches are less so.
                So... yeah, in most ways the differences aren't that big. I guess the most significant things are probably the organisation of it. Certainly, as someone baptised in a Baptist church, I feel no issue going to an Anglican church the way I would a Mormon church, though Christenings make me a bit uncomfortable. Certainly that kind of thing is far less important to me than avoiding homophobic or Creationist preachers. Hey, I go to a Church under the iron contol of the Communist party (The Three-Self Patriotic Movement, formerly the Three-Self Support Korea Oppose America Patriotic Movement, the only legal Protestant Church in China), and there's not much difference with western Churches.

                                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                                    Pursuant to ghosts being treated as real people, making soulsteel out of ghosts should still be considered a heinous crime. Or, perhaps, MORE of a heinous crime than it was in previous Editions.

                                                    And a good way to do that would be if Abyssals are like Solars in that they can draw Evocations out of any magical material, so that they never use soulsteel except as a very deliberate choice.

                                                    This would also help move Abyssals away from "destroyers of the world or struggling to NOT be a destroyer of the world; no other options!"
                                                    I don't like this. Creation is a Bronze Age world where slavery and human sacrifice are not uncommon. Human lives aren't valued in the same way that they are in modern society and the idea of inherent human rights would be laughed at. Torture and mutilation are more common criminal punishments than long-term imprisonment and no one cares if a method of execution causes the condemned excessive pain - they'd probably count it as a bonus.

                                                    For Abyssals to look at a piece of black metal filled with the tortured souls of the dead and go "I, on moral grounds, will not use this" is incongruous with the rest of the setting. Exceptions might exist, but turning down incredible power just because it causes someone else suffering is not a Bronze Age attitude.

                                                    As a further point, how much humanity is left to ghosts is still up in the air and could potentially vary. From what I know of the Wraith setting, ghosts there are sometimes Drones, ghosts so broken that they go through the motions of their life and/or death and have essentially lost their ability of self-determination. In Wraith, Drones are often soulforged just to keep them from strengthening Oblivion in the end and because they are pretty much useless for anything else. If Drones become a thing in Exalted, you suddenly have a layer of moral ambiguity to soulsteel. You have these souls which are so broken it could be argued that they are no longer a person in any metaphysical sense. Would soulsteel forged from drones be considered something evil or would it be considered like a pound of hamburger at the grocery store?
