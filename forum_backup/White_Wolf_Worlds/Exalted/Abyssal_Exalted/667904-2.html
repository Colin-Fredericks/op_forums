            As a thought about how to approach Abyssals- in addition to the Castes, I would vote that each should symbolize a particular 'approach' to playing/portraying Abyssals:
            * The Loyalist (cheerfully serving their Deathlord)
            * The Malcontent (in service to their Deathlord, but questioning if it's the right thing to do)
            * The Rebel (someone actively rebelling against their Deathlord)
            * The Pentitent (someone actively seeking redemption as a Solar)
            * The Mad (someone outside the Deathlord structure entirely, enthralled in service to Oblivion)

            Rather than forcing the signatures to all be part of a single Circle.

                        I like this, but I think it leaves something out. Not sure where it would fit, but there should be at least one who is like the Harbinger of the Ghost-Cold Wind in the 2E core: I don't even know if he's a loyalist or not, but he represents the "third path" that is neither "destroy the world" nor "redemption." He's a Lawgiver of the Dead, who makes the living give to the ancestor cults like he believes they should, who protects the dead when nobody else will. I think he sees himself as a Robin Hood figure even when he kills the living in huge numbers -- taking wealth (life) from the rich so they know what it's like to be poor (dead). (Since he can't give actual life back to the dead, he compels the living to give them grave goods and veneration, which they owe to those who worked all their lives to leave their children something to inherit.)

                        I think The Mad could be somebody who wants to replace their Deathlord, not as a rebellion against their goals or methods but just because they're not good enough; this Abyssal wants to serve Oblivion or the Neverborn, not some Deathlord who's time is long over.

                        The Rebel role could be split between The Mad, and the Penitent. That leaves room for the Lawgiver of the Dead.

                            I have an idea for a Pro-Ghost, but Anti-Deathlord Abyssal, but was never clear on where they would stand on Oblivion. Ghost don't seem to like Agents of Oblivion but at the same time, a ghost wouldn't exist if it wasn't for Oblivion?

                            To add some background to my character concept, the Crimson Shaman was a shaman of an ancestor worshiping tribe in the East. His tribe has difficulties from some of the local gods who want the tribe to worship them not their ancestors. The gods try to assassinate the shaman and his apprentice, so in desperation Crimson accepts the Deathlord's offer and exalts as a Midnight Caste. Crimson exacts revenge on the gods and the ancestral ghosts of his tribe convince him to abandon his deathlord, leave the tribe, and head to the underworld to be a traveling ronin who helps ghosts out.

                            The Crimson Shaman has no desire to destroy Creation, but will violently defend the underworld and any Ancestor worshiping cults. I don't see him having any issues with Solars or wishing to become one. Yet his negative intimacy with the Gods of the Terrestrial Bureaucracy might cause conflict with the Creation based Exalts.

                                                        The Midnight who preaches that the living should be servants of the dead. The dead are immortal, they are your ancestors who gave you every thing you have, and they're your more experienced, wiser, more enlightened elders. And guess what! Unlike serving selfish Dragon-Blooded or rich mortals, you actually get to become one of the elite. Not in some theoretical future incarnation that might never happen even if it isn't a lie, but in actually verifiable fact -- you can watch your parents die and become ghosts, and when you speak to them it's clear that they remember their lives.

                                                        But you had better earn your place among the honored dead, or at least live a pious life and die a blameless death. Cowards, suicides, and impious youth who refuse to honor their ancestors become soulsteel.

                                                        Death is inevitable for all the living, so this character can have very much of what Isator Levie describes for the macabre. But they need not be gloomy. The living should not fear death, for rewards will come to those who were pious. The dead/undead are not repulsive, they are the next stage of each human's existence.


                                                        Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post

                                                        I can understand the point but... the signature characters exist to show players the sorts of play styles a splat can support. Just look at the new Solar sigs -- one of them is a Realm loyalist, which is rather bizarre IMO, but apparently a life that you can attempt as an Anathema.*

                                                        Redemption should not be the only option other than "destroy the world," but it should be shown, up-front, to be a viable story. The Penitent sig wouldn't necessarily to be on the verge of becoming a Solar, attempting explicitly to become one, or even aware it's possible. They could be seeking penitence and redemption for what they've become and what they've done, and on a path that could plausibly be leading them to Redemption.



                                                        * I'm imagining either an attitude of wishing the Realm would stop killing Celestials but supporting the Empire other than that (founded ultimately on hypocrisy, ignorance of what props up the Realm, ignorance of what Solar survival would truly mean for the Realm, or a desire to hugely reform the Realm); or a Reluctant Monster Solar who truly believes they are Anathema (at least to start with), but is determined to help the Realm and Immaculates as long as they can control the "demon" inside, and plans to die nobly and/or suicidally when they feel themselves losing control.
