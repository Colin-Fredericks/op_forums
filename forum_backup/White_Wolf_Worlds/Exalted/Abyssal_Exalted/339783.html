    What is your Abyssal Redemption regimen?

    12-14-2014, 03:05 AM
    Say you were in charge of an NPC Abyssal's path to redemption. What sort of training or deeds would you do to get the Abyssal redeemed. Myself, since the Errata said that an Abyssal's redemption was partially dependent on gaining a profound insight to all 4 Virtues and Solar Exaltation, I would start off by training the Abyssal up to the Form charm of each of the Arguments of Virtue Styles as well as Solar Hero. Part of the quest would be to go back, if possible, to people from the character's mortal life and make amends for past wrongs.

    These are just some ideas that I have, and of course much more would be required. What ideas would you have?

                      I would definitely require a meeting with the Unconquered Sun, complete with all the trouble that gaining entry into Heaven and access to the presence of the Most High should engender. Then, reciting your tale of woe in such a manner that he is moved, keeping in mind his ability to instantly know when you're telling the truth and when you've compromised your Virtues...

                      If your tale moves him, he agrees to help you, although even he can't just outright reverse your condition. He kisses your bleeding caste mark, Endowing you with a seed of his Essence, which it is now up to you to nurture. At this point, I admit that I'm basically ripping off Promethean: You must build up the Solar essence within you by roleplaying your sincere devotion to Redemption, preferably by resisting the temptation of using your destructive charms, especially when doing so would benefit you greatly. When you have complicated your situation through these sacrifices, as well as reached other milestones by exploring your Virtues through positive actions, let's say as many times as you have dots of Permanent Essence, you roll your Redemption Pool.

                      To make things really interesting, you may use your Whispers or current Resonance level as the Difficulty or as an Opposing Roll. If you succeed, then the Solar Essence blossoms within you (not unlike Doctor Who's regenerations), scouring away the taint of the Neverborn as you experience the agony and the ecstasy of rebirth into the Solar you always could have been. As an added consequence, not only are you a Solar untouched by the Great Curse, like canon says, but you also can "sense" the GC in others; you don't know what it is, but you can easily tell that *something* is seriously wrong with your fellow Exalted! Good luck convincing them of that, however...

                      If you fail your roll, then the rage and hatred of the Neverborn surge forth and drown the sun's seed in the flood of their spite, and you suffer an immediate Resonance outbreak, Agg damage and gain a level of Whispers. If your ST is harsh, then that's it, you had your chance and failed. You can either adjust to eternal life as a Deathknight or commit suicide, which Ignis Divine is likely to help you with if he ever lays eyes on you again, courtesy of a Godspear to the face. Or, if she's lenient, she may allow you to try again, starting from scratch...

                      That's pretty much how I'd do it. I also like the notion of the Abyssal going through the initiation Trials of Sorcery, specifically to learn Solar Circle Sorcery, and would roleplay that out as the "exploring Virtues" part above. But not every character would be that powerful or even sorcerously inclined, so that would be optional.

                          For starters, I throw out the "Must go on a quest per dot of Essence" thing outright.

                          Now granted, some ancient and blood soaked Abyssal Badass of Ultimate Darkness that's clocking in at Elder Essence would probably require considerably more effort to become a Solar than someone fresh off their Exaltation, but I really just plain don't like "Must do this number of deadly quests to redeem, also getting stronger to better handle those quest means we tack on another one."

                          Reminds me way too much of an MMO where you've got those endless fucking fetch quests.

                          Moving on.

                          The way I look at things, the way an Abyssal becomes a Solar is entirely too personal to each individual to truly set anything down.

                          That said, I've two guidelines for Abyssals, one looser than the other.

                          The looser one is Unconquered Hero's Faith. Not so much for the access to Solar Charms that it grants, but rather for what the Charm represents. That no matter how deep the darkness is, there's a spark of light that will never go out. That, blackened and stained as you are by the Neverborn, you will not break, will not bow, and will not die on bended knee for them.

                          The only thing that I really think is necessary though for an Abyssal, besides the desire to return to the light anyways*, is a reinvention of themselves, capped off with them choosing a new name for themselves. When they were chosen, when they took their Last Breath and Exalted, they cast their name in Oblivion. That name and who it represented? They're gone and they can't be reclaimed.

                          Between your Exaltation as an Abyssal, what you've done since then, the trials overcome to be redeemed, and finally succeeding and becoming a Solar again, you are not who you were before it all began and you frankly never will be again. You aren't someone dreaming of a distant past that you cannot return to, but you are someone new with a whole future ahead. The new name chosen by the former Abyssal represents that and their conviction to move ahead and live.



                          *I also apply the same in reverse. A Solar that becomes an Abyssal must truly desire it. They have to want, in their heart of hearts, to throw aside the Light of the Sun for the Darkness of the Underworld, and that can't be achieved by tossing a Solar into a magic torture box until they come out evil.

                                                      I'm not sure about the second name part. Sure, that's a great way to go about it, building a new name untainted by Oblivion, and the Neverborn will be sure to strike you as if this fresh title were your old, but reaching into the maw of the Void and reclaiming the name you cast there speaks of the Abyssal's intent to reclaim both his Exaltation and himself from that same fate, and I like the poetry of it. The Abyssal should dedicate himself to be as the Solar he will become should be, a task made easier by the fact that the Great Curse does not affect him periodically (old Resonance rules). If you're using the newer resonance from Shards of the Exalted Dream, Abyssal players should always try to go for partial control, as opposed to willingly allowing Death to dominate their minds and actions, even for a moment. That is not to say that an Abyssal cannot kill; rather, he should make his killings according to the will of the Unconquered Sun, defending the innocent, slaying Creatures of Darkness primarily (or exclusively, sparing non-Dark creatures entirely), and offering the chance of surrender and mercy before finishing blows are made.

                                                      There are several other aspects. Those who have had their appearance change (ex. the mummified husk guy earlier mentioned) might not heal from their wounds, the lingering 'wound' from their time in shadow. The Monstrance, if not destroyed, must at least be ritually severed from the Abyssal via painful thalmturgical rites or the Abyssal's offering of his own beating heart to the Unconquered Sun, freeing his next incarnation to pursue redemption in his name. Stopping a sizable threat originating from the Underworld using Solar-mirror charms from Unconquered Hero's Faith would certainly aid ny such quest. The only experience I have in the subject is my Dusk caste, Want of Nothing, adopting the pet name or his Sidereal love as his true name and willingly entering Heaven, knowing the Neverborn would more than likely strangle him to death on the steps of the Jade Pleasure Dome, to ask clemency from both Sol Invictus and said Sidereal. Confessing his love also seemed to help for some unspecified reason.
