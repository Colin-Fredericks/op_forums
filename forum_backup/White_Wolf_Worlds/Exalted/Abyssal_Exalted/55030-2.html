                                                    I'm going to take the time to register my disagreement with a quote in the OP...but surprisingly not the ones that have been focused on so far.

                                                    Originally posted by Ira the Squire
                                                    That's not the type of heroism that Exalted is going for: Exalted is about winning no matter the circumstances. It's not about standing up even when you are definitely going to lose: it's about winning even when you are sure you're going to lose. Thus things like the Primordial War where you do things that are impossible, Perfect defences which blocks things that are supposed to kill you 100%. It's not about heroism or courage during aHopeless War or Defiant to the End.
                                                    If you want that type of heroism or courage, that's a New World of Darkness thing, not Exalted.
                                                    Yeesh, I'm not a fan of these type of arguments for several reasons. For one, it tends to boil the game down to just a power fantasy and that bugs the crap out of me. It's not a game about averting tragedy through sheer over-the-top competence; in fact, a lot of the tragedy of the game happens despite the power the Exalted wield or indeed because of it.

                                                    And for another, it really bugs me on an irrational level when people say that exploring tragedy, defeat, etc. in Exalted doesn't work because it isn't about that; for me what made a lot of 2e's fluff and fanon so abysmally boring was that achieving your goals was treated like it was inevitable: if you were Exalted, you were going to win because that's just how it was. It was modeled in the vein of "yeah, taking over that empire was a breeze but -now- you have to run it" - and yeah, I dig the Conan-as-king quandry as much as the next guy, but once you start glossing over the obstacles in favor of the aftermath to the point that it almost seems guaranteed the whole shebang loses it's luster in my opinion.

                                                    In a Creation as big, mysterious, and dangerous as it should be an Exaltation should guarantee you a fighting chance to achieve your goals and not one thing more. Some times you win. Sometimes you barely scrape by. Sometimes you're one of the Spartans at Thermopylae or the Texans in the Alamo, and the fact that you can glow like the sun and swing a sword the size of a grown man doesn't change that fact.

                                                    Maybe it's a matter of semantics, and what Ira the Squire is talking about is how to be Exalted is to have that one shot to succeed, or at least the potential to change the world even in the face of utter defeat, and all I'm doing is splitting hairs. Regardless, I'm willing to pick this back up after I get some sleep.
