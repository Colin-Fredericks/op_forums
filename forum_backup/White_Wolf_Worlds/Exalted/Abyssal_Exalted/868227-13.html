                                                You're either not around to remember your life, or you just don't remember. Either way, for that particular go-round, you're done.
                                                What is "me"? Is it just my memories, or is it my soul? Because if it's my memories, does that mean I die a little bit every time I forget things? Did I even exist when I was 1, considering I have no memories of it? But if it's my soul, then amnesia is not death. It's just amnesia.

                                                When people have car accidents or whatever and get complete retrograde amnesia, as very occasionally happens, do their families consider them to have "died" or just suffer from a mental condition? I'd say very much the latter.

                                                I'm only aware of buddhism, wherein Oblivion is basically the goal. Beyond all desire and suffering and knowing and needing to know and needing to..need. I dunno, wulf could probably explain the whole thing better than I can.
                                                Yes, but. Your average Buddhist peasant in Thailand isn't thinking "one day I'll cease to exist entirely, like atheists believe they do", they're thinking "I'll reincarnate continually, and eventually I'll ascend to ultimate bliss, and while I'm waiting the tens of thousands of years that'll take I'll donate to temples to make merit and get a good reincarnation". Not to mention afterlives of suffering/joy that go between lives in most .

                                                And many other reincarnationist religions/philosophies don't view identity the way Buddhism does. In Hinduism, the aim isn't to cease existing, but rather unite with the ultimate existence of God itself.
                                                This is the whole point of karma. I mean, if your next life was an entirely different person, why even worry about your karma? And that's certainly how Immaculates in Exalted view it.

                                                Although on the other hand, the Bishop's followers do think in a more Buddhist* way: Oblivion, non-existence, is bliss, while existence is suffering, and thus Oblivion is the ultimate goal.
                                                *As I said, I think Nirvana=Oblivion is a bit of a simplification, though it can be viewed that way.

                                                Anyway. My point is that people in Exalted will have different views, but generally they're going to view Lethe as positive and Oblivion as negative. Occasionally it will be the other way round, or they'll view them as functionally the same. I could definitely see some philosophers arguing that Lethe is functionally identical to Lethe (before Immaculates beat them up), but as there are millions of people in Asia very concerned with what will happen to them in their next life, I'm pretty confident that it's not going to be a popular idea in Exalted.

                                                    Buddhist Nirvana isn't Oblivion at all.

                                                    Superficially, there are similarities: a soul that is destroyed is beyond suffering, and eliminating suffering is the goal.

                                                    But according to Skandha theory, even if you had a soul that could be touched, that could be manipulated, with every single memory encased within like a piece of amber, it still isn't proof that there is a self at all, because not even normal, breathing people have selves that are consistent from moment to moment. Impermanence affects everything.

                                                    Nirvana, then, is the state that is beyond life, beyond death, beyond joy and suffering, something that cannot be changed. "Nirvana" means "extinguish" and to some, it's the blowing out of the dim light of the illusion of self to enter the sun-like radiance of Nirvana. To me, Nirvana is the blowing out of the light to realize the darkness that has always been there, just out of reach. To some, it's a state of pure bliss, but to me, it's probably something beyond even that.

                                                    The thing is, Neverborn seek Oblivion because they want to be free only of suffering, and want to stop existing entirely; they want to annihilate themselves. Buddhists seek Awakening to attain Nirvana at some point, to be free of both suffering and joy, in a state beyond both annihilation and existence.

                                                    Also, another thing that states that Nirvana isn't Oblivion: the Buddha attained Nirvana, and gained multiple supernatural powers in the process. The Neverborn's power is not borne of Oblivion, as they have not entered it: those powers are just merely in service of it.
