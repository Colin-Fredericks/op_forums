                                        Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                                        Some of Morke's past quotes on Infernal Damnation Vs Abyssal Damnation might be enlightening to these questions, even if the backdrop regarding the Neverborn and the power of Oblivion likely no longer applies.

                                        Specifically, the idea that Abyssal Exaltation constitutes an inversion of Solar Exaltation, but the corruption of the latter was only possible because of its essential nature. That is to say, that Solar essence inherently had the potential to fall and become tainted in such a way as to create the Abyssal Exalted, which is the very thing that made their Redemption possible.
                                        This was a treasure trove of ideas, thank you for sharing this. To clarify, to what degree should these statements be taken as canon? A couple big points to draw attention to:

                                        Originally posted by John Morke
                                        Abyssals and Solars are a matter of transmission. They are two halves of the same coin, counterpart Exalted. In a word, the tragic fall to an Abyssal Exalted, or the heroic, arduous rise to a Solar Exalted, was something built into these Exalts as a matter of fact; of being able to tell the absolute pinnacle standby story of falling to the furthest depths of darkness or fighting your way back to light.
                                        This seems to mesh really well with what Haren suggested. Perhaps the Deathlords didn't directly corrupt the Solar exaltations, but found that they could corrupt the Sun's chosen themselves into accepting the dark half of Sol's power, something that had by natural law been there all along. Maybe something like a Monstrance torture chamber is still involved, or maybe we'll get tragic stories of Solars falling from grace in very personal ways. I believe its an Ex3 canon fact that there have been a handful of Solars in the world even as Sol has been turned away from it, maybe the Deathlords have been recruiting from that shallow pool over many centuries to gather their current forces?

                                        This reminds me of the story of the Ebon Dragon, and how none of his kin had taken notice of him until the Unconquered Sun took his true form for the first time. No one likes to talk about it, but maybe the USC creates as much darkness in the world as light?

                                        Originally posted by John Morke
                                        ...it would be doing Infernals a disservice if any path they take led them to becoming Solars. It sort of undercuts their mission and their thematic impact to even imply that they need to be Solars, in spite of whatever in-game reasoning there would be. It cuts the weight of the condition if, after some long transcendental quest, you could holy your way back into the Unconquered Sun's good graces and it resulted in you becoming a Solar Exalted, as if that were a superior state of being. Yes, for the purpose of being rid of your evil alien hellish connotations, it is, but the actual idea that you can fundamentally tear apart an Infernal Exalted and reach the Solar underneath goes against the entire metaphysical structure of what they are from top to bottom. It makes their Exaltation a bit less legitimate.
                                        And thank you, John, for clearly explaining everything I dislike about the Abyssal redemption arch. He's talking about Infernals here, obviously, but I've never understood why Abyssals got treated like the evil unwanted step children of second edition. Lest we forget, the way you make an Abyssal in Ex2 is by placing a normal Solar in a box and torturing them until their will breaks and they accepted the Black Exaltation in shame, which is not exactly an inspiring backstory for the splat. Having two sides to a coin is great, but clearly establishing one side as "good" and the other as "bad" does not leave us with equally appealing halves.

                                        In my ideal world, I hope that in Ex3 redemption continues to exist but that the choice to pursue it will not be so much presented as the only proper choice, and Abyssals will be appealing enough that Solar characters might want to make the switch. Perhaps the distinction would not be so much that Abyssals are "failed" Solars, but rather heroes of a variety that will cross any line to do what they think is right. So something like Captain America and the Punisher. People have strong preferences for one story over the other, but you could imagine a world where either one would want to be more like the other. Sorry, I'm ranting now.

                                                            Regarding being able to 'lock up' Solar exaltations...

                                                            I've come to see Exaltations as something more akin to a piece of reality than a thing; like a concept. Kind of like a title granted to you, like being president or the fastest man alive. And when you are bestowed that, it comes with boons. The Jade Prison did a hefty bit of reality shifting, and basically...folded that fragment of reality over and over again and bound it in metaphysical chains inside of a pandoras box equivalent.

                                                            The lock was there, physically anchored on the Jade Prison, and when it was breached, that was the point where it all expanded back out into the world. So for a moment, that bit of reality was physically localized, which allowed the Deathlords to physically 'capture' those shards of existence that contained the specific ability for someone to be chosen as a Solar.

                                                            That's my two cents.
