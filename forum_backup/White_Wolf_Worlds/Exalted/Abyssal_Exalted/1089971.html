    Rambling about the Deathlords and the Abyssals

    05-08-2017, 05:42 PM
    I was just thinking about how Third Edition has changed the Deathlords and what this means for Abyssals once we start to get more concrete information about them. To start with, the Deathlords oaths to the Neverborn are now the subject of rumor rather than being objective fact. ​This, of course, opens up the possibility of Deathlords who don't serve the Neverborn, with "Deathlord" being a state that a ghost can attain to, perhaps through a necromantic working. Also in an interesting twist, the Neverborn are no longer mentioned to be unified in their nihilistic urge to destroy Creation - no motive is attributed to them at all.

    ​This puts the Deathlords in a new standing in Creation. They don't have to be seen as inherent threats to life and existence. The Mask of Winters is a threat not because he wants to destroy the world, but because he wants to conquer your country and rule with an iron fist. Assisting a Deathlord in his ambitions is not necessarily helping to advance a villain's plot to end everything you hold dear. The idea of a sympathetic or even heroic Deathlord is now a possibility. It could well be that the reason that the Mask of Winters hasn't advanced his agenda of conquest in Creation is because another Deathlord is waging a war upon his holdings in the Underworld, a war motivated by a desire to protect beloved descendants living across the Scavenger Lands.

    This brings some pretty interesting nuances to the Abyssals who choose to serve the Deathlords. Now, a deathknight's efforts on behalf on his liege are not inherently antithetical and inimical to life and existence. Instead the why and the how of the Abyssal's service and the ends thereof must be brought into the picture. A deathknight can be a just crusader as easily as a monster.

    Of course, for this to matter, the Abyssal needs to be able to choose his or her liege lord and negotiate the terms of their service. After all of the feedback on Second Edition Abyssals and the Monstrances of Celestial Portion, I doubt we'll see the Monstrances return as a way to control or influence deathknights or their Exaltations. Abyssals will need a greater level of autonomy than they can achieve with a gun pointed to their head. I think that Abyssals will still be Exalted at the cusp of death, but I think for reasons similar to why I expect Monstrances to be going away that the old "choose between dying and being death" bit will be dropped. Abyssals might still face a choice, such as between dying and rejecting their place among the living, but it will be couched in terms that are less of a moral condemnation of the deathknight.

    Of course, with the Monstrances probably gone and the link between Neverborn and Deathlord in question, this brings up the question of whose Chosen the Abyssals are and where they came from. Who does the choosing? I find it doubtful that the Deathlords do the choosing if the Monstrances are gone and something feels off about making the Neverborn the hand behind the selection process, but the Underworld has no equivalent to the Unconquered Sun. The way the Underworld was left underdeveloped in Second Edition doesn't leave many alternative options beyond the Deathlords or the Neverborn, though. One theory that I had was that the Abyssal Exaltations were grave good copies of the Solar Exaltations, spawned when the Solars were entombed and freed by sympathetic response to the return of the Solars to Creation. This theory puts an explanation to where they came from, but it doesn't put a will behind the one choosing.

    Perhaps the Abyssals are the Chosen of Oblivion itself, picked out from their fellow men by a sort of resonance with the elemental force of entropy, stagnation, and dissolution, but this doesn't feel satisfying. All of the other Exalted can go find their patrons and ask "Why me? What about me made you pick me out of the millions of souls in the world?" Then again, the idea of shouting such existential questions into an empty void and hearing nothing back, not even an echo, is appropriate to some of the themes of the Abyssal Exalted.

    Whoever's Chosen they end up being, I do think the idea of "redemption" is going to be long gone. If your choice to become an Abyssal wasn't really a sin, then there's no redemption to seek.

    This is just something that I felt like putting down in writing. About the only thing I'm really certain about is that there's so much I'm looking forward to finding out about some of my favorite Exalts.

      Well, the aspects of the Abyssals seem to be fairly straightforward in 3e. Now, there could be Deathlords who are not sworn to the Malfeans, but they would not have been the Deathlords who received the 100 Solar Essences as part of the deal with the Yozis.       The independent Deathlords would also probably not be disgruntled Solar ghosts but could have been anything from the ghost of a Second Age dockside prostitute who died of syphilis centuries after the Great Contagion to the ghost of a First Age Lunar warlord who died of old age centuries before the Usurpation. Their only unifying factor would have been a terrible will when they first awakened in the Underworld to change the Underworld. They could be allies or enemies of anyone.

                      I'm attached to the idea that being a powerful ghost dedicated to the ultimate death of the world is basically the definition of the title "Deathlord", but also to the idea that the end of the world can be nuanced in a manner that lends itself to several variations that aren't all straightforwardly murderous.

                      ​I've got this image in which the Bodhisattva Anointed by Dark Waters evangelises his state ideology to the surrounding powers, and has long term desires to preach it to the furthest extent possible, and while the intent of his system is to ultimately facilitate the world's end, it's more of an ideological, even metaphysical thing, than a case of tricking people into providing themselves as raw materials to construct his fleet of superweapons intended to sail around blowing everything up.

                      ​Anything from the creation of a world in which people live just enough to leave sustainable impressions upon death, and since the dead require so much from the living to be fulfilled, life becomes a kind of commodity to be traded and exploited for the fleeting pleasures of the dead, to a kind of mysticism underlying his practices in which the accumulation of the willing dead in his corner of the world will act as a kind of anchor, weighing it down and ultimately dragging everything into the fathomless depths beneath the Underworld.

                      ​It's still about the end of the world and/or all life, just with a bit more complexity than just rolling around killing everybody, and based on a message that a lot of people can be expected to enthusiastically buy into, partially because there can be something very compelling about the idea of being granted high status by forfeiting your life, and partially because modern marketing teaches us that large numbers of people can be remarkably susceptible to being sold on shit they don't need that is bad for them.

                                      It was more nuanced then that; it was about using the Deathlords as the standard image of what deathly hero powers could actually look like, to give them a solid cosmetic basis.

                                      It's not the same as Sidereal Castes or Dragon Blooded Aspects, because we know what Abyssal Castes are, and they're not distinguished in any way like those other Exalted. The difference between a Dusk Caste and a Midnight Caste is not as dramatic as that between a Chosen of Journeys and a Chosen of Endings, so I can't see the basis of their being much clash from one's gothic warrior poet having a bit of exploitative relationship stuff in there if they favour Presence.

                                      ​Never mind that the people who developed the model pointed out that it would be more than just one Deathlord per Ability, and that not every Charm needed to be more than something that functioned about the same as a Solar one.

                                      ​Look at the list of associations that were given for the Bishop of the Chalcedony Thurible, and consider if any of those might seem like things that one would get in Presence as well.

                                      ​I really doubt that it would be any more intrusive than the fact that numerous Solar cascades go off in about three or four different directions, even when they don't call attention to it like Performance and Craft does. It would be like getting a preview of the Solar Presence Charms that led up to the more seductive or sexual ones, and assuming that was what that Ability would be all about.
