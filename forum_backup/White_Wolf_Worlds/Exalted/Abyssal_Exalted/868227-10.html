    Originally posted by Ferryman View Post

    I'm a huge fan of Wraith. It was deeply flawed as a game but had such an exquisitely baroque setting and tragic, heartbreaking themes that I can't help but have an abiding fondness for it. The old 2E hardback was the only RPG book that I would actually pick up and read for pleasure before I encountered Exalted back in First Edition.

    That said, I wouldn't draw direct parallels from Wraith for the Underworld of Exalted any more than I would want the Scavenger Lands to look like Greyhawk - it's always better for a setting to be its own thing rather than an imitation of something else. But that doesn't mean we can't borrow inspiration where it's available.
    • The Tempest need not be a separate dimension/layer, but the idea of Maelstrom-like storms sweeping across the new geography of the Underworld with howling, Oblivion-wracked specters carried on their winds is too breathtakingly awful not to include. It would also serve to help explain why the Underworld isn't just a boring landscape filled with aimless ghosts - the unquiet shades of the dead gather together in enclaves and cities for mutual protection and build bulwarks to protect them against the dangers of their world, just as the living do. Not just storms, but hekatonkhires, ghostly beasts like anglerfish that lurk to swallow the unwary soul entire, marauding bands of renegades and slaver-wraiths that prey on those too weak to oppose them, and other weirder things. The Underworld ought to be at least as strange and dangerous as Creation, but in a totally different way.
    • The Hierarchy might not map directly on to the Underworld as a whole, but it could make a beautifully oppressive framework for a given Domain - perhaps one ruled by a Deathlord, but not necessarily - where the unquiet shades must struggle to find productive work or demand burnt offerings from their own descendents so that they can pay their annual tithe to the patrolling Legionnaries. If they fail, they face a forfeit which can mean being maimed, enslaved, or even forged. These ghosts might struggle under the weight of their obligation, but at least they are safe from the dangers that prowl the untamed lands beyond their borders.
    • Ancient cities from the Shogunate or earlier that exist only as forgotten ruins in Creation might have fallen entirely into the Underworld. Some might have been consumed by the Labyrinth, but others might still persist in some strange fashion, even exerting their own mnemonic gravity on the malleable world around them to shape weird not-quite-right islands of being that almost resemble what they once were, before they were not any longer.
    • In a setting like this, the Abyssals can be so much more than just sad angry emo vampires. They can be heralds of Oblivion, sure. But they can also be the champions who rally the souls of the Dead to the wall, throwing back the hungry tide of night and lighting the lanterns that hold it at bay. They can be the emissaries who walk fearlessly between the lands of the dead and the living, or who cross the dark waters from one Domain to another to carry news and forge treaties and reunite the souls of loved ones thought lost forever. They can be the creators and arbiters of the Laws of the Dead which prevent a ghostly city from succumbing to corruption, despair and dissolution in the face of the insidious rot of the Nephwracks and their doppleganger spies. They can be the curators of knowledge that has gone from Creation but still echoes in the forgotten halls of fallen empires, delving into those secret places and carrying back what was thought lost. They can be the hunters of great beasts that even when slain refuse to die. They can do more than just play at heroism, they can actually be heroic.

    I certainly hope the Underworld delivers something like this, because that's what I want.

    That is EXACTLY what I was thinking. Like don't take things EXACTLY, but you can incorporate aspects that would work. I think Renegades work well, because let's be honest. There's always going to be SOMEONE not happy with the current status quo. So it's not so much a Wraith only thing. Like the only thing I think I'd use super closely is Doomslayers for the Labyrinth stuff (and some things about specters that adds more spice and variety). Yes, totally do ancient cities and such. In fact, why not have a library of lost knowledge? Stuff that was carelessly destroyed in Creation, preserved by ghosts who care.

    ​When noise turns to silence, when colors dull and pale, when reality no longer makes sense, there shall you find me. There, in the dreams of the River of Faceless Millions, do I dwell.

                                            Okay, just a thing that you particularly enjoy consuming because of its flavour.

                                            ​What is the basis of favoured tastes? The series of synaptic responses that provoke a positive association with consuming a particular thing. The conditioned or genetically coded circuitry that connects the relevant parts of your brain?

                                            Is it memory? Is there an event in your life that you remember that makes a thing desirable to taste, that compels one particularly towards it? If you like the taste of fudge above all other sweets, would that cease to be the case even if you were made to forget what fudge even was?

                                            Do you have habits? Do you have emotional patterns? Consistent cognitive processes? Muscle memory?

                                            Things that largely arise from how chemicals and electrical impulses are shunted around the particular architecture of your brain?

                                            If you had no memories, a lot of that would remain. A lot of your impulses, what attracts and repels, how one responds, would remain consistent. You might not recognise anybody else, but there can be a fair amount by which others can recognise you.

                                            I wonder if the reincarnated soul might not be a seat for those patterns. That a person who lives long enough to see the same soul over multiple lifetimes exhibit many of the same traits that have little or nothing to do with procedural memory. Maybe continuously make the same positive contributions. Maybe gradually transition away from bad habits as altered environments add new layers of conditioning. It seems like the kind of thing that would be a basis for how Autochthonian Castes and the Alchemical Exalted work; after all, aptitude is another thing that is part of that category. I certainly don't think there's any particular memory that serves as the basis for my attraction towards and aptitude for intellectual fields in general, and scientific ones in particular.

                                            A lot of what gives us a personality is emergent from things created by our genetics (not just neurology and muscle construction either, also the particulars of our hormone regulation). In other words, things we inherit from our parents. A lot of people notice similarities between parents and children, and that's not just confirmation bias. You don't have the actual memories of your parents, and yet there are evidently qualities that make you a continuation of that. There are people who draw meaning from that.

                                            I would think, in Creation, there could be people to draw meaning from a soul carrying traits between lives. Meaning from one those things are lost, seemingly forever, beneath the erasing darkness that seeps up from below the Underworld.
