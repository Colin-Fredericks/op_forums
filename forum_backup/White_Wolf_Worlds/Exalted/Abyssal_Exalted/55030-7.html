    If the character really is so full of hate and despair (or maybe just the serial killer type) that they really truly meant that they wanted to kill the world in exchange for their own life, sure. But if he/she was just feeling desperate at the time, or never really meant to follow through anyway, why should they be dealing with a moral dilemma like that? Not everyone in such a moment of desperation would agree, but a lot of normal people would. Yes, the Abyssal still has to deal with the fallout of the decision because other servants of Oblivion may be really angry and after him/her for breaking promises, but that doesn't necessarily mean the Abyssal should feel guilty about any of it.

                Isator, I feel like I'm connecting with you on this. You can see what I mean when I say that I dislike Exalted's tendency to overshadow the awesomeness of the exaltation with the dark fate.

                Originally posted by CoSaR View Post
                Not sure how I got that? I read your posts.
                Ok, let me clarify that. I don't think Exaltations are chumps, but it should probably be said that I don't think Deathlords are chumps, because you're not talking with the Exaltation, you're talking with the Deathlord, he or she is the one selling you the deal. Now, Deathlords being ages-old can totally see through you. they know exactly whether you're lying to them, whether you're just talking out of desperation and not really meaning what you're saying (which I posit to be the case among the majority of the people), or if you really mean that you're willing to destroy the World.

                Now. Do the Deathlords have a reason to give powers to people who don't really mean what they're saying, or are lying? That's the question we need to answer. And I think the answer should be "yes, obviously", if the Abyssals are to be playable without limitting the character concepts too much. But why should they? Well, first of all, Deathlords don't have as much leisure as the Incarnea. They only pick from a very limitted reasource and most people simply don't understand the deal. And on a rare occasion they actually find someone who is willing, fully and knowingly? There are high chances that person is insane. Deathlords don't need lunatics, they need smart agents. They need knights. Secondly, Deathlords have certain specifications for their servants. It's very probable that the virtues of the individual will outwieght her unwillingness to cooperate. They might exalt someone over traits that have nothing to do with the end goal of their mission, because they are too useful to pass up on, such as great willpoer, cunning or things such as social position, wealth or knowledge.

                All those things can be much more useful to the Deathlords then conviction to the end of their mission. And finally- Deathlords work against each other. If one can get a higher ground with a servant that already dislikes another Deathlord, whom they also hate? They're gonna take that chance and run wild with it, whether the servant is going to be problematic or not.

                        Originally posted by FromBeyond View Post
                        Oh, ok, I can certainly agree with that. However, I think the implementation so far has been horrible. I feel like if the Resonance is to stay, it should target things that are exactly opposite to what you, as a PC, care about. Because then Resonance seems more reasonable, to an egoistic person. It seems like a small price to pay for power and an ability to protect the things that you care about, at the cost of the things you don't care about. But bit by bit, eventually, there will be nothing left in the World except you, the things you cared about and Resonance. THAT's an acceptable vision of Resonance. I can understand Resonance that kills the World around you, but I just don't get Resonance that punishes you.
                        This makes sense to me too. If Resonance punishes every sin, then the Abyssal is trapped between Creation's rejection and the Neverborn's hatred. OK, so maybe she feels more allegiance to a Death Lord that way. But then the rebel Abyssals are totally alone in the world and the loyalists can be shocked with their pain collars every time they use the wrong fork. Great scheme for the bad guys, but terrible for anyone trying to tell a variety of stories.

                        So yeah, I'd much rather see Resonance as a corrupting force than as a destructive one. The puppy you pat on the head doesn't die in an agonizing fire - it follows you around, growing more protective, bigger, and meaner by the day. Your lover sees her rivals crushed by ill fortune and scarred by disease. Perhaps over time she craves your power or becomes obsessed with you, but she doesn't just die and turn into a zombie. Crops don't wither and die around you - in fact, fruit tastes sweeter and grain becomes more plentiful. Maybe the people who eat it change a little over time, or maybe they lose interest in fruit and grain, craving rare meat and strong drink instead.

                        It's not like 2e Death Lords are all trying to shovel the world into a furnace *right now* so it's odd that their knights go around killing everything they touch. The subtlety of, say, the Silver Prince is kind of wasted when his lieutenants are forced to reveal themselves as agents of undiluted hatred for Creation.

                                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                                I think I grok what you're saying here and I mostly agree, but I'm not sure how positive the default Abyssal state can be cast without losing the narrative power of the choice to become an Abyssal. One of the biggest driving forces in Abyssal stories is their reaction to their own choice to become an Abyssal. Whether they stay loyal to the Deathlords or eventually rebel really hinges on whether they decide to go through with their choice or if they decide to change their mind. Without the questionable morality of that choice, what's the practical difference between an Abyssal and a Solar with an evil boss?
                                The choice can be significant without being immediately and overwhelmingly awful. It could be like borrowing money from the mob to win an election or *cough* cooking meth to leave your family some money before you die of cancer. At first the new mayor can use her political power to improve local schools. Maybe her mob contacts even give her information that helps cut back on violent crime. At first the meth guy can pay his medical bills and make sure his soon-to-be-widow can pay the mortgage. Similarly, the Abyssal might be allowed to turn his new strength against whatever he hated in life. The Death Lord gives him some training and sets him loose against the bandits that plagued his old village or the slavers that dragged his family away in chains. The Death Lord's goal should be to set that new Abyssal on a slippery slope: let him use his powers "for good" at first, but ask for seemingly innocuous favors and shield the Abyssal from full knowledge of the change he is causing. Maniacal gloating and liberal use of the pain collar might come into play later, but they probably aren't great ways to convince a new death knight to serve.
