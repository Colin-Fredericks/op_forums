                    ...because somebody said trenchcoats and fedoras don't look right in a bronze/iron age world?

                    I don't believe anyone wants to remove all the "fun over-the-top-shit." It's just that the amount and kind of FOTTS you have in the game has to be moderated for the setting to stay coherent.

                    Have you ever tried to think about the Marvel (or DC) universe as one, collected thing? Comics present a world with all kinds of heroes, coming from all kinds of backgrounds, ostensibly existing in the same space but having curiously little impact on each other or on their world... most of the time. Generally, the root justification for almost everything that happens in those settings is "because it would be cool", and look what happens: humanoid aliens fighting against gorilla-men in alternate dimensions alongside honest-to-Copperfield wizards and genetically-enhanced supersoldiers. It's all very surreal when you step outside it and stop suspending disbelief for a moment. The worst part is, each individual character's story usually does make some amount of sense on its own, but when you put them all together the differences between them make the collective whole feel implausible.*

                    Exalted could be a Fantasy Superheroes game, where everyone just goes crazy with their appearance and abilities and there's no requirement for anything to make sense in the context of the setting around it... but that would be a selling the game a bit short, don't you think? Why would that be more compelling than a game that aspires to be cogent and consistent in its entirety? I don't know about you, but I feel more engaged by stories that I can believe, not just enjoy.

                    *
                    Disclaimer - this is why I dislike the Justice League. Too often, it feels like big groups of diverse heroes are constrained by the need to... continue being a big group of diverse heroes, which usually means that nobody contributes as much as they are able to or behaves according to the idioms they follow in their personal stories.


                    It's going to have none of the stuff which got me into Exalted in the first place (namely, playing as an over-the-top anime hero with badass powers in shit in a really insane world).
                    Idle curiousity - would you consider "Darker than Black" to be over-the-top? That show is my benchmark for what a coherent superhero anime looks like.
