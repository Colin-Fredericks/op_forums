                                          Look, "Dark Mirror Solars" is a viable vector for Abyssals, but it should represent the most basic elements of the splat at best. The Black Sun of Oblivion is one heck of a solid foundation. But it works best as only a foundation. I can see Essence 1 charms looking a lot like that. Just a few twists and tweaks, as this is the point where it is closest to being a Solar, only as nods to their new themes. The low level bread and butter charms that are already boring are arguably the best fit for "Mirror" charms

                                          Beyond Essence 1, (so 2-3+), they should be informed more by their own themes, as the Exaltation starts to grow beyond it's former existence. The seed of Deathly essence has begun to sprout. Filtering the Essence through the Deathlords means that the charms should start looking that way here. The 'Deathlords substituting for the Unconquered Sun' should be felt in their charm design at this point.

                                          At the capstones (Essence 5+) however, we should see this unholy combination grow beyond expectations. Not only not mirrors of Solars but even breaking away from the Deathlords here. This is where I would hope to see the Deathknights surpass their overlords. This should also be where their charms are the furthest from their Solar counterparts. Get weird, give us ghost stories and nightmare fuel. This is where I'd hope to see the nihilism really show itself for the first time, but it shouldn't be the only option.

                                          Heck questions like "how does this Ability deal with Death, interact with the Dead or even Die itself?" should be not only asked but even answered at various points of the charm tree and looking at how Deathlords themselves would answer those questions could be helpful at filling out the charm trees.

                                          Ok, so that is way too much about the Abyssal charmset, particularlysince I hope to get a better view of how the Underworld is ruled/'lived in' from this book, in addition to a nice big chonky set of descriptions about how Necromancy differs from Sorcery, even if i expect the rules to basically be a copy/paste kind of deal.

                                          Thoughts ripple out, birthing others
