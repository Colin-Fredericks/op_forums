                                    Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                    I wonder why Abyssal mechanics are so weird, I mean, they are hailed as the world-destroying weapon, but they can't regen mote in Creation, sunlight gives them penalty, they have the Spectral keyword, and being CoD make they vulnerable to Holy effects which everyone and their mother has in their arsenal. It's kinda like those aliens from Signs who melt like jelly when they meet water but decide to conquer Earth of all place......
                                    The basic idea is that Abyssals were designed more as antagonists. In order to use their full power they have to poison the world and spread shadowlands. This was the purpose of the Spectral keyword. Abyssal charms tended to be less expensive than Solar ones, but in exchange, they cost more when used outside of the Underworld. So, when confronted in Creation, an Abyssal will find himself at a disadvantage, so he will try to move back into the local Shadowland, forcing the protagonists to follow him, where he will then find himself at an advantage over them.

                                    Which is awesome if you tend to use Abyssals as antagonists, but it really sucks if you use them in a mixed party wandering around Creation, because then your Abyssal will almost always find himself at a disadvantage.

                                    It also didn't help that, even though Abyssals were based around spreading Shadowlands to the point that they had clear mechanical incentives to do so, we never got actual rules on how Shadowlands can be created or spread.

                                    Regardless though, as the Spectral keyword was errata'd into oblivion, and we saw new optional rules for replacing Resonance, it's clear that the current writers plan to take Abyssals in a very new direction come 3rd edition.
