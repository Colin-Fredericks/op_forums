            Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post
            Yeah, it went from a Hot War to a Cold War situation, that's a clear change, I'll admit. Would the setting be improved by sticking to the original version? Hard to say, since it all depends on the execution. The devs have said that they were making the Underworld more exciting, but I don't recall them mentioning bringing back open warfare among the DLs as a way of doing it...
            One of the biggest problems the Underworld faced is that it never felt like it mattered. The Deathlords conquered it, they controlled it, they won.

            So if you were playing an anti-Deathlord character, the Underworld was enemy territory. If you went there, you were taking a huge risk because it was under the control of your enemies. So your best bet was to avoid it, and instead, to try and stop them from conquering Creation (since that was their main focus anyway).

            If you were playing a pro-Deathlord character, then the Underworld didn't matter. Your master already conquered it. He or she already controlled it. You had little reason to deal with the Underworld because it was already your territory. Instead, your major focus was on Creation because that place was the heart of your master's plans and not somewhere under his or her control.

            Essentially with the current setup, there aren't any strong reasons for characters who are working against the Deathlords or for the Deathlords to really go and muck around in the Underworld. You'll spend much more time dealing with Creation because that's where the Deathlord's focus is.

            If you have an Underworld in which the Deathlords are in the process of conquering it, or in which Underworld trade routes are incredibly important, then that creates more interesting opportunities for players to engage with. Your character can go join an independent kingdom and help them fight off the Deathlord. Or your can play two Deathlords off against one another. Or you can cut trade routes and cause all manner or havoc for that Deathlord.

            And likewise, if you're playing a character who is working with the Deathlord, then just turn those above examples around and your character suddenly has reasons to deal with the Underworld.

            I agree though that having open battles between the Deathlords however is largely impossible when there are only 13 Deathlords as opposed to the hundreds that were originally envision. The distances between the 13 Deathlords are far to extreme for any kind of military action to be common. Even the closest Deathlords, the Mask of Winters and the Walker in Darkness are separated by a pretty long distance, and for many like the First and Forsaken Lion, the Silver Prince or the Dowager, they're simply too far away from other Deathlords to make open conflict anything other than a very, very, very rare exception.

                                            Looking back at the first post and recalling some things in illustrations, I feel a need to clarify that the values of the macabre are indeed undermined when they're presented in ways such as "drape chains haphazardly around a naked torso" (as opposed to something that might emphasize a sense of restraint or burden), and skulls used as little nipple adornments. Really, any kind of nipple covering is enough to undermine arguments that the bare-chested woman in question is being treated with an equivalence to men or should just be considered to have a values-neutral instance of being topless, because when you cover up just one part of the breast you're still doing the thing where the exposure of the breast is meant to titillate, with the hidden promise of some secret thing waiting to be uncovered. When you uncover everything but the nipples, you just reinforce the strange contemporary idea of women's breasts as a kind of secondary genitalia, making it both non-equivalent to a bare-chested man and inherently sexualized. One wants to talk about equivalences between and responses to nudity, but none of these characters are ever actually nude, because nudity is the point at which basically all capacity for titillation has been left behind, and it's just a human body. An actually nude Lady of Darkness would be preferable to me than one who has little fetish ornaments, because it would make several of those rhetoric points better. It's in a similar boat to how I've said that a Maiden of the Mirthless Smile wearing something like a ball gown would make the point better than one wearing a college Halloween costume.

                                                        I was going to make a post about this a few pages ago but got distracted. How the whole gothic thing is presented makes a huge, huge difference on whether or not it's a silly cartoon character or not. Part of what makes the dark gothic thing work is it's supposed to evoke feelings of sadness and loneliness, but like those feelings the the abyssal/death sort of motif has to be hidden and mysterious and dark. Abyssals shouldn't have bright white and purple bone armor that screams for your attention and to see how many skulls they can put all over it. For me an abyssal should be this guy sitting in this place. The creepiness doesn't come from the carnival of carved skulls everywhere, it comes from all the stuff you don't see. Why is he like that? Is he sad? Weary? Defeated? He could be thinking about all the people he's killed, and how it doesn't matter because they're all dead now, all the sadness he feels right now is something he's saved them from.

                                                        The place too wherever it was it was clearly once a giant place of worship, but now it's dead and abandoned. There's just something about a place of grandeur and life and history and triumph that becomes so sad when it's cold, alone and empty. Or nearly empty. Even if it was filled with ghosts, that could be just as bad. I'm not entirely sure, but I think we all know that feeling of what it's like to be lonely in a crowded room. It's the kind of feeling that you can imagine somebody sitting in for a long time, and maybe actually believing that since everything will turn to dust anyway, that nothing really matters. Especially if everything that was theirs is lost and can never be brought back, except in an echo.

                                                        That's actually one other, small, facet of it, is a kind of sad hope. An abyssal who's lost a lover might find some spark left inside her by sitting on a darkened balcony with his ghost. Knowing that this is the last vestige of her that he can't let go of. Like how some people who lose somebody in their real life keep watching videos, looking at pictures, holding on to any last scrap of them they can find, jealously guarding them. In the Abyssal's case, possible violently. For example a Deathlord might promise to keep his ghost safe and preserved for her, but she must go and kill the Solar exorcist who plans on destroying the Deathlord's shrines. Not a bad story for a loyalist Abyssal.

                                                        The problem is that it's a fundamentally internal and personal experience, so you can't just show everything and fetishize it and expect it to work at all.
