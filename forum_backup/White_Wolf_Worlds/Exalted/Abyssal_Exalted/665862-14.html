    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    I'm saying that I don't see the point of the Lady in Bloodstained Robes. What does she do? Badly evangelise, and screw her team-mates. How does this show what Abyssals are about (which is the point of signature characters)? She has no plans, and not really much in the way of competence or effective skill.
    Honestly, my impression is that the artist drew her, and then they made up the background later purely to explain why she looked like that, "oh, uh, why she's bare-breasted, because, uh, she was a prostitute? Like, a really scummy one? Sure, that'll do."
    Honestly, if they'd said "she's from Africa-land where they don't wear much, and also she's got this really interesting religion she's spreading, but it comes into conflict with her boss for X, Y and Z reason," I'd have accepted it fine.​
    This is kind of the thing. I mean, people can rag on how badly the Abyssal sigs were portrayed, but in terms of productive discussion (well, I suppose inciting laughter is productive, but we'll go with productive in 'how do I use these characters?'), I'd say that you're going to want to start changing the character. Now, of course, if you change the character too much, they just become a different character, so you want to stick to their basic traits. So what traits does the Lady have?

    1. She's an ex-prostitute
    2. She sleeps with various members of her circle
    3. She's jealous of Maiden of the Mirthless Smile for being prettier than her
    4. She's not very devout, and her cult is mostly her own creation that sounds profound, even though she doesn't hear the Whispers.
    5. She did something to attract the Mask of Winters' attention in order to get an Abyssal Exaltation.

    So, let's break this down. First off, we know the Lady was a prostitute originally. From the descriptions, she wasn't even a very upscale prostitute. These are the ones that get into the job because it's that or starve. She didn't have repeat clients that looked to her for skill. She was in the gutters, saw that her sexuality and body were her only tool, and proceeded to use them as much as she could.

    This ties into 2-3. Even after Exalting, the Lady isn't going to change much. Her life has taught her that sexuality is a weapon, and the one in her arsenal she's most familiar with. Using sex as a means to have leverage over her Circlemates is a common thing, because it demonstrates she has power, either through the simple method of leading them via their urges, or through having enough social Charms that she can persuade them anyway. It's a symbol, as much to herself as to the others, that she's not the disposable item she was before, and that what she handed out for a pittance before is now what people would die (literally, occasionally) to possess now.

    Similarly, her rivalry with the Circle's Dusk is because she's also grown to see prettier women as a threat. There's only so many customers to go around, so being the first choice was the difference between food on the table and starving. Similarly, the Maiden got the Mask's attention by basically walking up and being so crazy he stopped her heart and then Exalted her. The Lady's aware that the Mask is the whole reason she has power and status now, so him having another favorite for any reason is unacceptable, and a pretty favorite further undermines her own pro points. It's not petty jealousy so much as the survival instinct of making sure that you're the one to catch the customer's eye and thus be able to eat.

    Number 4 is pretty self-explanatory, even if it's a little contradictory. The Lady was never legitimately considered a person of influence in life, but persuading people by playing on the little hooks and quirks and insecurities was her bread and butter to convince them they wanted what she specifically could provide. Her cult benefits her in the sense of being in a position of authority over the rabble, rather than one of the scavengers begging the madame for scraps. But the Lady doesn't believe in the Oblivion-flavored tripe she's hocking. If she did, she wouldn't care about the fact her worshippers bring offerings and adoration she never had before. The status of being a Priestess of Oblivion is more important to her than the doctrine, and this is reflected in that she's not so close to Oblivion's nihlism to hear the whispers of her patrons slipping through the cracks in Creation. She'd have to be listening for them to do so.

    Number 5 is pretty much the big thing. The Lady of Bloodstained Robes did something to get the Mask's attention. He doesn't go around handing out shards to complete wastes of space, so something about her was so cruel and clever that he thought to recruit her into his service. What that was can vary from game to game, but it's the defining thing. She was personally picked to be an Abyssal. So obviously there's something that deserves it.

    Again, this basically boils down to having two choices. Either you ignore the character exists because they're bad and you don't want to use them, or you make them something you want to use.

        I think Isator, it's just a matter of players viewing the archetype of courtesan through the viewpoint of a player character in an RPG.

        What I mean is, for real life courtesans, their goal was generally to make enough money not to need to resort to prostitution, to find a sponsor who had money, and to eventually find a husband or paramour who was willing to support them for life. Their skills, and their refinement of their skills where essentially an ends, in and of themselves. Simply by improving their ability to play instruments or their ability at conversation (or even skills at sex though that wasn't really all that necessary), they would naturally find their goals coming true. Further, they traditionally didn't need to go seek people out, but rather people would seek them out.

        But players tend to have much larger goals (overthrow the evil king, destabilize the Realm, set up a major kingdom, etc). For these players, when they play a courtesan, they tend to have a much different goal in mind than just making a living. For them, their courtesan "tool set" is the means to an end - they're going to want to hook up with important individuals so they can destabilize the king, or get important information that their allies can use. Further, in many games, the Storyteller isn't going to have the player sit around idly while sending various clients to his or her character. Instead, the player is going to find him or herself in a position where they need to be more active.

        This means quite a few players aren't really looking to play a standard courtesan but rather a Mata Hari-type seductress (and many Storytellers expect that a player who makes a courtesan is looking to play this sort of character and design their games accordingly). They tend to unconsciously add that concept to their idea of courtesan or simply conflate the two together for the beginning. So for many people, when they say they want to play a courtesan or when they think about courtesans, they are in part thinking about spies who use seduction - someone who uses those courtesan skills as a means towards an end.

        Because of that, a lot of players tend to view the entire system of courtesans through the "player perspective" and imagine that seduction is a very important tool that courtesans need, even though that isn't really the case.
