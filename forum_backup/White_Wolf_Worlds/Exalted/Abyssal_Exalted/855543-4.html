    Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
    If I were to boil the themes of this down, I'd say the core difference here is adding a theme of consumption to a superman archetype. In many stories, especially the pulp fiction that heavily influences Exalted, Vampires are supermen equivalent to any other big bad in the setting, with the only drawback being that they have to kill or at least harm the living to survive. If Solars are pulp hero supermen archetypes, then would you take a deal to get those same powers knowing that you'd have to keep killing people to maintain them? What do you care about enough to make that worth it?

    It's a great foil and theme for Abyssals to have and maintain, but it does come with some challenges. For one thing, the player can just as easily choose to play a normal superman without this serious handicap. Why would you choose to play the inferior product, many would ask (I am infamous for loading up on flaws and disadvantages, so this is not a question that troubles me). For another, the core theme of the Solar charmset is "excellence" so taking that power also means copy-pasting a lot of those charms over in a frankly quite boring way.
    It probably isn't going to be a handicap, let alone a serious one. It wasn't really in 1e, and in 2e Abyssals were only nerfed due to carelessness. I'm actually surprised to see that you have a strong sense of Abyssals as inferior, handicapped Solars, because as far as I can tell that's never been their presentation or the intention underlying their design. You're not a V:tM vampire who has to go out and eat people to live or anything like that. Moreover, Abyssals will obviously never be so similar to Solars that there's functionally no difference between the two. That's never been the case -- Abyssals always brought a lot of appealing things that were their own to the table in spite of their link to the Solars.
