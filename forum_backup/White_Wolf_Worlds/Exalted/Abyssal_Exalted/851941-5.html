    As much as I love Oblivion as a narrative device, I will shed exactly zero gothic tears if the Neverborn and the Maw are written out of 3e. The fact that sometimes souls just disappear is a perfectly satisfying narrative device. Maybe no one knows why, maybe very few people even know that it happens at all. A final death which is little understood except that it happens, can target anyone, and possibly that it is happening more frequently over time, is much more scary than a giant hole in the ground that communicates its will through the corpse bodies of dead titans.

    I for one think it would be most effective to say that Oblivion does exist, but not in any physical sense unless you're in the labrynth. Outside of that, Deathlords simply know that sometimes things in the Underworld disappear without warning or passing into Lethe, and that they are losing more and more real estate to it over time. They posit that more ghosts will stabilize the area, which may or may not be objectively true, and the chivalry of death is born of their frantic desire to save themselves. The Abyssals are made as weapons with the greater purpose of combating this unknown force, possibly with charms that immunize them from Oblivion, and are tasked with bringing more ghosts into the fold because that's what their bosses think will save their world. And maybe a few of them want to try a different solution, or maybe it turns out that the Deathlords themselves are what is causing this loss, though they don't realize it. Options.

                Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                I think weird metaphysical justification for immoral action is a hack writing move, because the writer invents the metaphysics. You can justify a convoluted metaphysical reason for anything being "For the greater good," up to and including the actual historical Holocaust, and if you're the writer and thus get to decide the objective, from-an-omniscient-point-of-view truth of your metaphysics, who's to gainsay you? But the reader is fully aware that it's all just a bunch of arbitrary bullshit you made up, and that you can construct a set of supernatural universal rules to any specification you desire. I know that whenever I encounter a writer who wants to paint a mass murderer as "Not actually evil" because handwave handwave cosmic balance, I immediately think "This writer wants to incorporate mass murder into his stories but actually engaging with it in any way but the most superficial makes him uncomfortable."
                My hope is that 3e Sidereals rejects this line of thought as thoroughly as StephenLS does! The Usurpation (as discussed upthread) is one thing; coldly consequentialist murder of random persons "For the Greater Good" because otherwise Fate will cease turning according to The Plan, though, strikes me as exactly what is being talked about in the above quote...

                                    Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                    This Sidereal's Great Prophecy thing, is it like classical prophecy that will definitely and magically come true no matter what you do, or is it more like people gathering data and then make a prediction that can turn out to be full of shit due to unaccountable factors ?
                                    More the latter, really. The Great Prophecy is not intended to say "the Path of Bronze was the objectively correct utilitarian thing to do," it's to say, "the Sidereals largely saw two options, and one side won out."

                                    None of this was helped by the Sidereals' Great Curse of being myopic when working as a group.

                                    As to the former point, the accuracy of the Prophecy was always in question, and I think the point in myth is that the veracity of it coming true is always up in the air! Because prophecy is rarely described as absolute, and one has to wonder how often one makes a prophecy come true simply in taking actions to avoid it happening (see Oedipus - when you actively try to thwart a prophecy, such actions of avoidance are in fact the impetus for the prophecy actually being fulfilled).

                                        I feel like the last part of this is an often-overlooked factor in the Usurpation.

                                        Because the Great Prophecy didn't occur in a vacuum. The Sidereals didn't decide to all band together and preform a singular working never done before and which probably can't be replicated again, at least, not in the same way for fun. They did that in the context of realizing "holy shit, the Solars have turned into monsters, we didn't see this coming and weren't prepared for it. We're supposed to safeguard Creation from precisely this sort of bullshit, but usually how we react to crises of this nature is to tell the Solars and then help them deal with it, and that seems problematic for obvious reasons now. What the hell are we going to do?"

                                        And in that context there's going to be a lot of pressure for actually resolving the immediate problem. The Usurpation was just as much about solving the problem Creation had right then as it was about preventing an awful apocalyptic future. Indeed, it might been the more salient; a potential apocalyptic future you can deal with as an abstract problem; the people who wield the Mandate becoming manifestly unworthy of it isn't abstract, it is happening right in front of you right now.

                                                            During 2E there was a point some people who didn't like the Sids latched on to. Three things are notably outside of Fate and so not included in prophecy: Demons, Dead, and creatures of the Wyld. Given that those were also three for three in greatest threats to the world it seems like the Sids appeared to have ignored the most glaringly obvious reason their prophecy was worthless in the long term. This has the side effect of making the Usurpation have some of the worst possible justification while not making it entirely unnecessary since in the short term the Solars really were a problem.
