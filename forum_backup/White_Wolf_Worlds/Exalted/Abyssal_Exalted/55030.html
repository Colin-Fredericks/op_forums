    3e Abyssals Are Dark Solars (A Conversation From The TVTropes Forum) (Warning: LOONG)

    01-21-2014, 07:37 PM
    Originally posted by LMage
    I've been browsing the dev commentary on 3E and I have to say....I am...underwhelmed...with the look of Abysaals so far.
    The changes to Resonance bother me, I can understand why it is often put upon/dissed, but personally speaking I like it the way it is, since it helps to quantify the Abysaal condition- which is one of utter despair. The whole point of being a Death Knight is that you accept complete death in exchange for a half life- a half life that is enforced both by that acceptance and by the control exerted by the Death Lords, the Neverborn, and beyond that the Void itself. The Abysaals are all about hopelessness, about having to face mortality and the inevitability of one's own demise, of the demise of all things, and of the nature of death. In many ways- making them the most human Exalted. They are confronted with an inescapable reality, and unlike Solars who can just ignore it and Infernals who can just give it the bird, they must face it- sooner or later, either through succumbing to despair and embracing it becoming murder happy nhillist psychos, finding solace and purpose in other areas of death (like the Exalted who acts a minster/diplomat from ghosts to the living), or struggling with it- every day to find some sort of way to cope with these realities while subject to the whims and terrors of the Death Lords.
    Or, maybe, ever once and so rarely....Turning on those self same Deathlords- and fighting against all odds to break free of their shackles, to find a true meaning and understanding from existence, wrest from the throws of death a life: such a quest that can take them to only one place at the end. The maw of Oblivion, to confront the Neverborn themselves.
    I don't know...It feels like the death and hopeless angle is being...abandoned in favor of drifting back to "Dark Solars" more and more- which is....less interesting to me. I feel like Infernals fill the slot of dark/twisted Solars a lot better (but then I also feel like the state of Infernal and Abysaal end games should be reversed- and Infernals should have the chance for redemption while Abysaals should be unable to switch back in their life times) and that Abysaals have a lot of potential to be the mirror of the Solars in a different way then just "Solars that glow black". The Solars are all about life and living- about seizing every moment and taking every challenge, climbing ever mountain, the Abysaals should be all about death, about facing grim reality and tragedy, about being confronted with hopelessness and despair and a dark fate, and maybe- just maybe- finding hope there, and telling the Neverborn to blow it out their undead asses.
    I also, am not a fan of the idea of Deathlords being responsible for the creation of Abysaal Exaltation, VS the Neverborn- since it really does sort of neuter the whole narrative strength of Abysaals. The Deathlords as they are- glorified inreaibly powerful insane Dragons to the Big Bad of the Neverborn makes the most sense, and a big point of the Abysaals is that they are made painfully clearly aware that the Neverborn insane and lost in nightmares of the void as they are are the end game. They stand at the precipitate of the void, the edge of nothingness, they are ancient and twisted and impossible and they want everything to die, and they are using you to do it. Shifting the focus to the Deathlords serves as another narrative barrier, takes away a little bit (A lot) of the punch of a Abysaal endgame- going to the maw, facing this long dead ghosts of their makers, the very symbolic source of this hopelessness and malice attached to their condition- to death, and beating them, beating it.
    I just...I feel like Abysaals are going to loose a lot of their draw- for me personally- when we see The Void and the Darkness fall into the background, while focus on the Death Lords, and being "Dark Solars" comes to the fore.
    Originally posted by rikalous
    As their Storytelling chapter notes, Infernals don't really need redemption, because they don't have Resonance slapping them down every time they try to do straightforward heroics outside of the Underworld.
    Originally posted by LMage
    I really don't want to derail into another Infernals discussion, but I will state, to me, that Infernals and Abysaal represent very different things.
    Abysaals are opposite of Solars their dark mirror, while Infernals are Solars twisted. The endgame for Abysaals shouldn't be redemption, because they are the ones who don't need it (And it always felt bitter in my mouth, especially if you run with the Abysaals-as-metaphor-for-depression, in which case "Redemption" defeats the whole point- which is learning to live with death and inevitability of it, learning to live with their demons and push forwards anyways- and instead just being like "flip a switch and suddenly everything is awesome and better") while Infernals, who represents twisted Solars rather then their opposite, who's whole arc is based around and beings with Fucking Up Royaly (TM) and cutting a deal to make things better, hell who had they done differently would have been glowing gold (Remember- Abysaals are chosen not by fate but by the insane death lords, who Exalt almost at random) and really, their end game should be moving past that, or overcoming it in someway, which narratively would be best concluded with becoming a Solar- earning the Exaltation that should have been theirs to begin with etc.
    (If our local Infernal fanboy wouldn't fry the living hell out of me- I would so house rule Abysaals as non-redemptive and Infernals as redemptive. But I might actually be the first person to be punched through an internet screen if I did.)
    Originally posted by Ira The Squire
    And here's the problem: one of the shticks of Exalted is about being awesome and heroism, which makes this kind of take really out of place.
    If you want despair, play Vampire
    Originally posted by LMage
    Who says you can't be awesome and heroic while facing utter despair? (To me- that's the best kind of heroism, courage in the face of unquestionable, unrelenting doom. Going in, without a chance, without hope and still fighting anyways- and maybe just maybe...If all the winds blow fair all the Stygia stars line up right...winning.)
    Originally posted by Ira The Squire
    That's not the type of heroism that Exalted is going for: Exalted is about winning no matter the circumstances. It's not about standing up even when you are definitely going to lose: it's about winning even when you are sure you're going to lose. Thus things like the Primordial War where you do things that are impossible, Perfect defences which blocks things that are supposed to kill you 100%. It's not about heroism or courage during aHopeless War or Defiant to the End.
    If you want that type of heroism or courage, that's a New World of Darkness thing, not Exalted.
    Originally posted by LMage
    Meh. I am of the opinion that the majority of mechanics should be treated as a strong suggestions anyways.
    That having been said- simply abandoning that angle all together by completely ditching Resonance and overhauling all the themes and ideas of the Abysaals isn't the answer. The primary theme of the Abysaal splat was dealing with death and it's inevitability, just like the central theme of the Core Solars is living life against all odds- the Abysaals all show a myriad of ways (and the splat allows for a ton more) to deal with this condition- and that's fascinating and interesting. But with by just ditching Resonance, completely reformatting the Deathlord/Abysaal relationship, hell even altering the source of the Exaltation, they are tossing out the baby with the bathwater.
    EDIT: And why not? Why can't that be the Abysaal's specific flavor of heroism? Every other Exalted gets their own specific style of the heroic and their own themes to build off of- and I'm not saying the Abysaal's shouldn't be able to accomplish the impossible- in fact I firmly believe that the Abysaal's story leads to once place- overthrowing the Neverborn and closing the Maw of the Void- so the Underworld can be liberated from the Abyss and they can turn it into something good.
    Finding hope and joy and new rebirth in death- that's where the Abysaal story should lead. "Dark Solars" feels like a cop out- and more over feels like doubling down on what we already have for that- the Infernals.
    Originally posted by Ira The Squire
    Because it goes against a theme that is common with every other splat (There's nothing mechancially stopping other splats except Infernals from fixing things, and Infernals have their own mechanics to get them out from the mechanics that stops them from fixing things)? And against the themes of the rest of the game? There is a reason why the backstory have the Primordial War, after all. Also, if Abyssals are supposed to be about Defiant to the End why let them have Perfects at all? Point of Perfects is to show that even against odds that seem like suicidal the Exalted will still win.
    Also, Resonance mechanically make what you suggest about fighting the Neverborn impossible, simply because it just undoes everything that you do. It is the worst of Reset Button.
    Originally posted by rikalous
    The impression I got from the dev commentary is that the changes to Resonance and the deathlord/Abyssal relationship were about providing greater freedom within the splat. Without Resonance slapping you down every time you defend or become friends with someone living, there's now room for Abyssal characters who aren't grimloner or evil to go forth and adventure through all the cool stuff in Creation. Without the default assumption that Abyssals are slaves, runaway or otherwise, to the deathlords Abyssals are free to have adventures where their deathlord isn't a major overhanging threat. To analogize across to other splats, they've also talked about how even though barbarianism is a major theme for Lunars a socialite Lunar will be totally viable, and even though Getimians are apparently linked to this rogue Sidereal warring against heaven, one of the sigs is apparently working as a special consultant for them.
    And the thing is, if you want to have the old stories, there's really nothing stopping you. Want your deathlord to keep sending dudes to kill you? You're free to include backstory where you pissed them off and now they dearly want your skull for a drinking cup. Want to do a story where hopelessness is the Abyssal condition? You can still have their attempts at heroics lead to awfulness and failure, you just have to do it with the story instead of the mechanic.



    And I firmly believe that none of the splats should only have one place their story can lead.
    Originally posted by LMage
    Only it doesn't? Each Exalted has it's specific central themes that have very little to do with each other- and while there are a lot "core" themes, what I've said about Abysaals.
    Exalted's central theme is- you can do the impossible. But no where does it say that you will do the impossible, or that the impossible will be easy. (And yes- the game does have serious problems with power scale at times, but I honestly don't get the impression, especially from Dev comments that these epic feats of awesome are supposed to be easy.) More over, Exalted is a pointless exercise if everything is easy- no victory has meaning if it's just simply won.
    As for Perfects- those come with flaws recall? And even if they didn't- they are more a mechanical balancing agents against insane damage or D Vs then they are serious thematic aspect.
    As for Resonance, dealing with it isn't impossible: and any prereq story to confronting the Neverborn will undoubtedly have to feature an arc where the Abysaals learn to temper it- which makes for an interesting challenge and chance to develop the characters, as well as a good story on it's own.
    I want say the Resonance is without problems- I think with a GM in the right mind set not exactly insurmountable problems- but there are problems. But the idea of Resonance is vital to creating the "all odds against" "hopeless despair" element that features into such a grim and macabre Exalt. That isn't to say hopelessness and despair can't be overcome- but to me at least, the overcoming of that, and the making peace with death itself, through overthrowing the Neverborn and their agenda, or finding a sense of purpose in a different cause, or even whatever, that should be the end game of the Abysaals. Coming to terms with Death, and even changing it's very nature if they wish to try.

    For the sake of clarification: When I say "one place", I mean metaphorically- that is, peace with Death- in whatever form that takes. To me ideally that form would be overthrowing the Neverborn and repairing the Maw, so the Death Knights can transition into rulers/protectors of the Underworld. To some it might be fostering relations between dead and living, or trying to reconnect with family and friends in the after life, or maybe just being left alone to do whatever it is they do.
    Unless you want to play a murder happy psychopath- in which case more power to you.

    Originally posted by Ira The Squire
    I am of the opinion that White Wolf have a habit of making their systems that is the opposite of what the game they want needs. A narrative-focused game should not have mechanics getting in the way of story progression to where it is supposed to go.
    As written, you cannot foster good relations between the living and the dead as an Abyssal. At some point you will have to compromise to the living and technically that counts as defending the living from death. Then Resonance erupts and the living dies. And also as written the Abyssal cannot rebel against the Neverborn at all without almost immediate Resonance eruption.
    Originally posted by Count Dorku
    If your GM is splitting hairs that finely, it is not the fault of the mechanics.
    (This is not saying that I like standard Resonance - I vastly prefer the one in Shards - but that sounds more like a GM issue than anything inherent to the rules.)
    Originally posted by LMage
    The thing is- when you change the landscape so utterly, along side the build it just becomes a different beast. The Death Lord/Abysaal relationship becoming more Master/Apprentice and less antagonistic wouldn't be terrible on it's own- it would still shake things up- but they are completely ditching Resonance, hell even changing the source of Abysaal Exaltation's construction from Neverborn to Deathlord. They are stripping down the things that contain the themes and ideas central to the Abysaal story and condition- in favor of moving them away from the void and that story into being "Dark Solars"- which is both incredibly boring as an idea and really annoying since the game has so much Solar focus already.

    That's a bit of a dick move on the G Ms part and less a fault with the mechanics.
    Originally posted by Count Dorku
    I love the way that literally every single complaint I have heard about every single change that is being made to every single splat has involved a comparison to Solars.
    I'm not surprised, because this has been going since 3e was announced, but I am getting incredibly tired of it.
    Originally posted by LMage
    Well....IT's hard to deny, that the game is becoming vastly more Solar-centric. I'm not fan of Infernals but even I raise an eyebrow in changing them to be "commentary on the Solar's decadence" rather then the trans-humanist whatevers they where before. (Which given that I am kind of extremely allergic to their whole tranhumanism combined with unabashed objective [still hate that word] evil, should say how worrying the change should be.)
    But seriously, this:

    ""Dark Solars" continues to be a concept that fires my imagination, far more than "Exalt of the [Oblivion/Void/other generic concept]" ever will, and that the game has pushed them in that direction in 2e is one of the number one reasons why they went from arguably the most popular splat in 1e to the hands-down least popular splat in 2e.""

    Is not okay.
    Originally posted by Count Dorku
    The Abyssals were always "Dark Solars", dude. Their original conception...well, okay, their original conception was Vampire: The Masquerade, but when translated into the Exalted setting it was "Dark Solars". 1e was Dark Solars. 2e was Dark Solars Who Suck And Get Set On Fire When They Try To Not Suck. 3e may actually be making them less Dark-Solars, because for a change their Charmset is not going to be loaded with "this works just like Solar Charm X" and "Solars have Charm Y which works exactly like that".
    Also, I will note that everyone warned you that chapters 1 and 2 of MOXP: Infernals did not represent any view of the subject matter that led to good gameplay or the ability to relate to them, and you read them anyway.
    Originally posted by God of Awesome
    Mechanically, they are becoming less Dark Solar.
    Thematically? I also think the same as with mechanics. Solars start off almost practically on their own. Abyssals will be Princes Of The Underworld, given what I understand, with more positive relationships with fuck motherin' Deathlords.
    Honestly, Abyssals are starting to sound like Goth Infernals.
    While Infernals are starting to sound like Hell Solars.
    Originally posted by LMage
    I don't get why everyone is so freakin down on 2e Abysaals- the game as a whole has a serious problem of entitlement and silver-spoon syndrome among the majority of the Exalted. Abysaals where the ones trying to actively subvert that- there is no special criteria to become one, they are stacked with odds that just might posse a real conflict driven threat, and they are written with such amazing imagery and themes in regards to death and dying, particularly in the Exalted setting where the potential exists not only for them to change the entire game re: The Nature of Death but it is actively assumed they will do so- at the same time the obstacles they face aren't ones that can just be crushed with some cunning minimaxing and rules lawyering. They require a real story- an epic world spanning earth shattering story that is hard and full of struggle and loss and pain- but the glorious ending is still within reach, and always a possibility if you are simply brave enough and stubborn enough to keep trying.
    And, I feel like all that great potential is just going to get tossed out, or at the very least left by the way side and completely neutered in favor of making them more generic "Dark Solars". I actually don't mind the mirrored Charm sets, and honestly I would take their being a near mechanic carbon copy as long as their unique flavor was preserved. What bothers me is and will always be the story of it.
    As for Infernals- It's not just the first few chapters that make me decidedly un-interested in them. (Though make no mistake- they are truly disgusting...And I will note still have the glowing endorsement of the Devs.) It's also the kind of completely disjointed transhumanism themes that pushes an already over the top setting so far over the top that it's insane into completely unreasonable territory. (Though in the spirit of fairness- I can understand the draw of that and the logic behind it I just....I am not a fan.)

    RE: GoA: I can't even begin to express the sheer annoyance this fills me with.

                              I think that whatever happens to 3E Abyssals, I hope they have more flexibility. They are traditionally the only Exalts of the Underworld: A spiritual realm with a population that eclipses that of Creation. In such a setting, having them shackled to one singular purpose (murder Creation) is doing the very potential of their existence a massive disservice. I think the "murder Creation" thing should be part of who they are, but not to the exclusion of everything else. "Princes of the Underworld" makes a great deal more sense and is a better reflection of their relationship to the Deathlords. The Deathlords are all technically supposed to be working towards the end of all things and, sure, some of them are. But some of them are content to preserve the status quo because they enjoy their position of power and privilege. Who wouldn't? Abyssal students of these figures would in all likelihood have some similar attitudes towards the fate of the Underworld. It makes a lot of sense for most Abyssals to not be obsessed with destroying all things; maybe they're preoccupied with murdering entire cities to bring them into the Underworld and thereby enhancing its wealth and prestige, but they know to draw a line lest they accidentally suck all of the Underworld down the drain.
