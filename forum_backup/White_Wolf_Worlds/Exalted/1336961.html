    OC and Character repositery

    09-19-2019, 07:45 PM
    Here, for people you write, exalted or no. Mind you, I'm mainly going to be writing exalted.

    The Healer

    Pacifica Davis was a girl with big dreams, and low standing. Born to two working class parents in debt, and living in a housing project, her early life was one of hard work, stress, and poverty. Her parents struggled to put food on the table, and yet was willing to work for their child. Since an early age, she had always been interested in medicine and doctoring, reading the anatomy charts on the hospital walls and the nurses' office. But there was never enough money to send her to anything resembling support in this ambition. Even so, she forged on, native genius, an eidetic memory, and immense determination making up for unfortunate circumstances. By the time she was 15, she could, at the theoretical level, match professional trained general practitioners. And upon the completing of an online course in medicine, she exalted, granting her a supernal in medicine and granting her the means to complete her work. She now focuses on the poor and destitute, leveraging healing powers to obtain the resources to help them.

    Veda, no last name.

    Since the beginning she had wanted to be a teacher. Born in a poor village, she self-taught herself, learning from passing merchants and practising and reading till to the dead of night. Taking the entrance exam at 18 years of age, she passed, and became part of the kingdom's new education program, returning home so that other children could be like her and improve their lot. It was not to be, as the enemy attacked. The program was cut short, the village razed, and she fled with the survivors.

    There in the refugee camp, she made a decision. The enemy was advancing, there were numerous war orphans and those who had been left destitute to the enemy. The food was running out, and safety lay several thousand miles away, and between them were forests, rocky mountain passes, and wastelands. And so she took them on a fast march, taking all the food and equipment they could go. It was difficult. Bandits and soldiers from both sides were their enemy, and they had to take significant detours to avoid enemy encampments. Wild animals were a danger. People got into accidents, and had to be carried or left behind. A disease came, ravaging their ranks. Help was few and far between, as all they passed by were subsistence villages who could barely feed themselves.

    Ravaged by disease and hunger, exhausted and healing from numerous minor injuries, Veda wept as she heard the children around her crying of hunger. There was nothing in the wasteland around them to eat. But still, ignoring the screaming pain in her muscles, she got up. She would not let her children starve.

    She could have left. She could have run. She could have arrived far faster and easier without them, not needing to wait for slow children or having to detour around enemy camps she could slip past. She could be a camp follower, sleeping with men in exchange for safe passage. She could have joined the enemy, for a teacher and other educators are always welcome in the help of administration. She could have simply lied down, and given up. But she refused.

    In reward of her compassion, her selflessness, her hard work, and her integrity, she was exalted by the sun, the light burning away the plague and tearing away the injuries that besot her. Her first action was to take the survival charms, and gather food for her flock. Her second, was to summon demons to heal injuries and cure the sick, calling her children to stay still and not be afraid. Her third, was to take the rest of the journey; two months worth of slow walking, and finish it in a week. And her fourth, was to start up a training program in the stronghold, starting up and training men. She was no warrior, but she was a scholar. And war was more than fighting. She trained armies of bureaucrats, sorcerers, investigators to root out spies, administrators, and whatnot. She exercised the minds of men, making them cleverer and quick-witted. She created new weapons. And in the end, the enemy was pushed back, surprised by the newfound strength of their victims.

    After the war, she was elected to the throne on popular acclaim.

    Veda still has no last name, still keeping the memory of her lowly origins. A woman who valued mind and thought, she emphasised universal education and literacy, making all education in her lands free of charge for both education and the livelihood needed while learning it (this means free dorm rooms). Her administration is well run, and legions of sorcerers march in her name, repairing the damage done and building vital infrastructure. Her police are efficient, clever, and root out crimes on a daily basis, while savants and scholars advance both technology and the arts.

    Time will tell on what will be the fate of the kingdom of knowledge.

      It is said that all men were equal.

      Some said nay.

      A nation was discovered the powers of DNA, and began using them. A racial creed took over, and they believed that they were greater than all men, destined for greatness. They attacked another nation, stealing its resources, using its population as slaves. The victim fought back, tooth and nail. But they were smaller. Less militarised. Others, not wishing to risk themselves, stood back, and turned their faces from the atrocities being carried out.

      One woman was amongst the thousands that fought against the invaders. Having lost her home, her husband, her children, and her friends, she vowed to return everything a thousandfold. Every rape. Every theft. Every murder. Every piece of abuse, she burnt it into memory, and every night, she vowed vengeance.

      Life as a rebel against a greater enemy was no easy task. They were betrayed, surrounded. And so the woman stayed behind to man a machine gun nest to give her friends a chance to escape. It was at this moment, in reward for her courage, compassion, and valour, she was granted power by the Unconquered Sun. Slaughtering the battalion sent to kill her, she rushed out to rejoin her comrades. Only to find them killed by the betrayer. Cutting off his limbs and leaving him to die, she went off the match another cell, all the while exercising her powers.

      The enemy outnumbered her a thousand to one. She was a woman with a pistol and a sword, reaching 35 years of age. The enemy had factory lines, tanks, entire cities producing ammunition, iron mines, airfields. The enemy had an army, and enough power to level cities. The enemy had thousands of trained scientists, the best scientific minds in the world (no matter how depraved) and had legions of guards.

      They all died. Some perished as colleagues they thought could be trusted stabbed them in the back. Entire factories and cities were put to the torch, infiltrators walking within and then detonating as one, a golden figure slaughtering any who walked in the city. The enemy had their armies attacked by trained, determined, and well-coordinated men, each attack doing just enough damage to leave them open to the next, letting them die to a death by a thousand cuts. Spies and saboteurs were trained up, and sent on suicide missions, detonating explosives within public squares and other vulnerabilities, driving them mad with paranoia and desperation. And then, finally, their war machine broken to pieces, their army demoralized, their people weeping, and their economy in shambles, the woman walked in, slaughtered their capital city after making sure none could escape, and cut off their leader's head, broadcasting the proceedings to the rest of the country.

      The enemy nation outnumbered hers by a thousand. But she swore to return each death ten thousand fold. And she kept her promises.

          Silver Snows of Winter

          A girl born to a mining family, that ruled the Snowy Mountain Company, Silver Snow was born to a life of comfort and ease. She wanted for nothing, and needed nothing. Her father was a man in charge of mining Ether Powder. A coalescing of elemental essence into solid form, at the intersections of Dragon Lines. A product that was constantly in high demand, the work was dangerous and deadly, the elemental imbalance killing any without strong protections. The family was on a mid-rim world, and Ether Powder was sold both on- and off-world for their various uses. A childhood calm and without problems, she excelled in everything she wished to do. She had no ideas on what her family's business was like.

          One day, out of childish precociousness, she decided to follow her father to his work. Using her quickness, small size, and a lot of planning, she evaded the guards, and entered the mining town.

          There, what she saw, shocked her. Starvation. Malnutrition. Disease. Injury. Workers who were too injured or sick to be of use, were either 'disappeared', or sent into the worst of the conditions. Many of the workers showed signs of elemental overload, a sign of too little protection. And by 'too little', it was 'nothing at all'. They were sent into mines, without protection whatsoever, dying to elemental imbalances, or killed by falling rocks when the unstable conditions caused avalanches and collapses.

          She continued investigating. The things that she had simply dismissed as slander, warranted greater scrutiny. What had once been viewed as worthless gossip, was looked at further. The screams and chants of a terrorist group that had been hounding her family, was now looked at.

          All true. All very true. And worse. Her father had unsafe conditions. Her father used children, sending them to their deaths in hazardous work spaces or simply deadly machines. Her father took the downtrodden, put them in contracts, and then sent them to places worse than prisons. Her father specialised in taking beastmen, taking advantage of the kingdom's racism and prejudice against them, and offered them lower benefits and pay, using their desperation. The lack of care for them, meant that their deaths were not too closely looked at, and anyone wishing to look at the suspiciously high death rate were either bought off, or simply ruined with charges of 'slander' and 'malicious rumour-mongering'.

          That had to stop.

          She trained herself. Sword, body, mind. Before, she had never quite cared for anything enough to completely master it, just learn enough to impress someone with her skills as a 9 year old. But now, that had to change. She learnt subterfuge. She learnt how to forge signatures. She learnt how to sing, and started out in high society as a beautiful singer. She hired men, from her own allowance, and staged several prison breaks and escapes. She brought in cameras, to take photographs of the inequality and oppression within the mines.
          ;
          Of course, all good things came to an end. And after 5 years of this, she was found out. She was brought to her father, and told to recant. To claim those were all fakes. To give up. To tell the world that he had done no wrong. To give up the names of her companions.

          She spat in his face.

          For that, she was thrown onto the street. With nothing but the clothes on her back, her sword, and her skills. But her father underestimated her, thinking that she was just some spoilt girl with pangs of conscience. She knew. She knew what was happening, and she knew that something like this might happen. Dead drops of cash and equipment, to wage her war. Transferred off shore accounts. Friends and allies. She moonlighted as a singer, singing performances. She took her money, and started up her own company, in direct competition to her father, using everything she learnt from him.

          It was still hard, though. For a girl who once lived in mansions and palaces and who once had legions of servants to tend to her whim, it was a surprise to do things herself. For someone who once had the resources of the Snowy Mountain Company at her access, realizing that she had to choose between eating and having a heated house, was an unwelcome epiphany. Realizing that she might never win over her father, looking at the numbers slowly go down, knowing that if she failed, she would either go and swallow her pride and beg her father for mercy, or die in the gutter... that was the worst.

          It was fortunate for her that the video call came. A video, not a conference call, to showcase her father's lack of respect for her. A condescending, sneering and mocking call, meant to shake her confidence and break her spirit.

          It did the opposite. The biting words hardened her will like earth melting and resolidifying into solid granite. The insults cooled her passion into a cold fury. The dire pronounciations and predictions of her future flared her ambition like a raging fire. The offers of mercy and reinstitution of the family name only gave her boundless reserves of will, like the depths of the sea. And the imprecations and condemnations of her, pronouncing her an ungrateful daughter for turning against him, she simply turned and bent, letting them blow over her like a tree bending to the wind to prevent breaking.

          The anima flux destroyed her computer, but it was worth it. She walked out of the dingy environment of her one-room flat, her mind burning with Essence Fever. She was a Terrestrial Exalted, a Prince of the Earth. Her words and writings came in quicker and faster, now. Her management and organisation streamlined, and she began to write new plans for her fledgeling company. By the next 6 months, they had grown by 500%, and she had made several vital inroads with politicians and rich men of all stripes. She sang for charity, and credits surged towards causes of equality and poverty-alleviation. And by the time 4 years had passed, she ousted her father, and took over Snowy Mountain Company, taking over the board of directors.

          Taking over the company, she began to branch out. Labour and company laws were lax, as seen by her father's freedom, and she took advantage of them. She extended her reach. The Snowy Mountain Company paid money for the injuries and wrongs it had commited. They fired managers, and hired new ones. They reorganised and reshaped themselves, becoming more efficient and effective than before. They branched out, taking on new roles. Pharmaceuticals, hospitals, R&D, military weaponry, Engineering, Electronics, programming, Transport.... Before half a decade had passed, she ruled the planet, with barely no opposition, having more money than most countries. She sang, and became an example of philanthropy, hiding just how much power she had, pouring money into causes of equality between baseline human and beastmen. She sang at gala events, wowing the world with her singing and poetry.

          And then, she began to branch off world.

          Her fleets now number in the thousands, her captains plying the trade routes to bring exotic spices and goods from around the manifold worlds to their destinations. Entire governments moved to her command. Armies marched under her banner of a snowflake. And entire planets were transformed into hubs of industry, to pump out goods and plants that she required for her company.

              Haboru, the unlikely King.

              A boy born in a poor village in a outer-rim world, Haboru's youth was one of ordinary poverty. Working as an assistant to the blacksmith, and his mother being a widow who was also one of the village's few healers, his life nonetheless was fulfilling. For the most part. And yet, somehow, there was a... sense of emptiness. A lack of direction. Haboru had a desire for greater things. But what could an illiterate boy from a village which barely had a well do?

              The answer came on his 16th birthday, as he exalted as Chosen of the Dragon Mela. The air essence freezing away the ground around him, Haboru realised that he could be much, much more. Bidding a tearful farewell to his family and friends, he forged a great sword of solid steel, and went on his way. Mercenary work came to him, his sword arm cutting down his foes like the wind. There, he fought, a lone dragonblooded amongst mortals, cutting a bloody swathe through the Open Kingdoms, wiping out his foes.

              And as he fought, men and women gathered about him, soldiers who he further enhanced with his Exalted power. They were the Band of the Eagle, a great mercenary warband that promised victory. Haboru was not a talker, but he was the one that stood in the back of the room, the icy cold created by his presence enough to intimidate the negotiators, while his spokesman, a deposed noble scion, wrote contracts and discussed terms.

              Money was good, he made a name for himself, and as he grew in power and repute, his influence grew.

              Some decided, it grew too much. A contract was made, without his presence, the negotiators fearing that he would smell the trap. An assault onto a fortress, held by some rebels. The Band of the Hawk smashed their way in, cutting them down with gunfire and sword. And then, at the heart of it, they found the bomb. The entire thing was a trap. The rebels were deliberately provoked, and double-agents within had obtained the bomb and set it to a wireless detonator. The bomb detonated... slaying every single member, save for Haboru.

              He lay there, buried in the rubble, smelling the cooking corpses of his companions. His enemies celebrated, but he did not die. Exalted never died easily, their native hardiness and accelerated healing letting them recover from the most grievous of injuries. He survived, drinking down the moisture that dripped into the cavern that he was pinned in, until a month later, he developed an Earth Aspect charm that let him swim through stone as if it was water, and he exited the place.

              He trained, attempting to replace the half of his body that was vaporised in the blast. Growing a prosthetic of clay and wood, he stalked back into the palace of the man who hired them, the winds hiding his approach. he came into the banquet hall to hear him boast of his trapping and killing of the mercenary band. That was it. With a gesture, the earth bent, closing the doors and entrances. He took down the cloak, drew his sword, and began killing.

              Now, he stalked the earth. Still smarting from the loss of his comrades. The bomb that detonated and killed his brothers-in-arms was something from the Old Ages, a weapon which nearly killed and whose inflicted wounds still smarted and burnt beneath the salves and lotions. He wonders now, what to do. He searches for a healer, to restore his destroyed body, or to at least soothe the pain.
