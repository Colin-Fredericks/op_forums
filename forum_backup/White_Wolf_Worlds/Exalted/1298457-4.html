                    Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                    Do the cultural factors involved mean they're not considered transgender?
                    As far as I understand, no, they would not be.

                    Depending on how much they're doing it as an expression of a more loose personal sense of gender identity, they might be genderfluid, but changing your gender expression based on culturally ingrained ideas about what gender roles are is not equivalent to having a gender identity (defined as the deeply ingrained sense of what one's gender is) that does not conform to one's assigned at birth sex.

                    I think a lot of Dereth are really more like transvestites, a category that I feel gets unduly left out of some of these discussions; there are people who will dress and express in a manner conforming to a particular gender, and employ the pronouns utilized for the same, who still do not identify as that gender.

                    The matter of how it's a response to rigid gender roles also complicates the subject; I find much of the Dereth akin to historically documented instances of women in more rigidly patriarchal societies who dressed and expressed as men for the apparent sake of getting into roles otherwise denied outright to them (often the military). It's hard to effectively assess whether or not they would have been trans men, as the society makes it hard to determine the distinction between that and women who are performing or disguising as men for the sake of doing something that, today, they could have effectively done as women.

                    These details inform the complexities and the nuances of the gender spectrum. But the thing that I derive from that spectrum for the sake of this discussion is that, within that spectrum, transgender still means a particular thing, and it's not something that would encompass all of the Dereth.

                                            I really like Isator levi's arguments, but I just don't know that I'd apply them here. I tend to side on the magic can help but doesn't fix everything side of the discussion in most cases, but I think if I work it out in my mind, it boils down to:

                                            "Terrestrial Circle Sorcery can change your appearance, but it can't make you capable of full reproduction."

                                            "Well why not?"

                                            "Because it only changes your appearance, like mundane surgery and hormones."

                                            "Mundane surgery and hormones being limited the way they are, is a product of the method being used to create the change. If you're using sorcery, it doesn't actually make sense for those limitations to exist. You're not sending hormone signals to the brain and then speeding things way up - or else you'd also have to claim that terrestrial circle sorcery is less effective the older the person is, and it definitely doesn't involve using advanced surgical techniques. You are reaching into someone and manipulating the nature of their body and appearance. So the only possible reason I can think of as to why a transitioned-through-sorcery individual can't reproduce, is in order to maintain some Doylist imperative that says in order to complicate a transsexual individual's life, the sorcery has to be a Celestial level working."

                                            And at that point it starts to stretch my credulity to the breaking point. I'm fine with expressing and representing the struggles of 'the real world' in game, but not at the expense of the coherency of the setting.

                                                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

                                                            Vance and I are just going to have to agree to disagree on that score. The wording is incredibly silly (all sorcery is supernatural). If you want to equate 'any' form of magic in Exalted to 'modern medicine', it would be Thaumaturgy, not Sorcery. To be blunt, if you're changing someone's bodies to the point where they have breasts, feminine bone structure - and continue to have that feminine appearance long after - it is fundamentally *no different* than just making them biologically female, with a working uterus and everything.

                                                            Which brings us to:

                                                            If I had to come up with a phrasing in line with the rulings, it would be something like...

                                                            Terrestrial workings operate by manipulation of the crude matter from which you're already composed. The results can be quite sophisticated, but still a bit limited by the base form being worked with, and significant modifications to the internal anatomy are tricky.

                                                            Getting a result indistinguishable from if that had been how you were born requires starting from the base Essence pattern and building up. That requires being up a level.

                                                            It might not be to all tastes, but I think it can be read consistently.
                                                            This wording still acts like the external 'just cosmetic' appearance of a person is just that - crude cosmetics. Anything 'beneath that' would have to be (or approach) the raw power of the Celestial Circle. But somehow this only extends to the act of reproduction, which all of this rather problematically places on some kind of holy pedestal, as if it weren't just a fundamental part of the body's anatomy, a process carried out so long as you have all the right parts, and fundamentally no different than shaping breasts that can produce milk.

                                                            Exalted is a game of demigods of incredible power. The difficulty of transition is not an appropriate hurdle for them. If it's a game about heroic mortals either learning or employing Thaumaturgy to transition, that's a completely different beast, but there is no way to have any circle of sorcery be capable of a complete transformation, while also denying the ability to reproduce, and not just come off as incredibly problematic, to say nothing of inconsistent to the themes of the game.
