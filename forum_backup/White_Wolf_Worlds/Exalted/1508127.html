                  A working to keep rodents and barnacles away would be reasonable. Low ambition, but possibly needing a bit of finesse.

                  A working to slow down the decay of the wood and male it somewhat harder might be good, once again I can see it messed up with low finesse (now your ship is heavy as stone or can propagate cracks like stone).

                  While not a working, creating an elemental to help get your ship to its destination seems to be one of the most straightforward uses for the summon elemental spell.

                  A working to reduce the resistance of ship moving forward in the water would be useful, but if the working reduced the resistance in all directions, then the ship would be blown about by the wind unable to control its movement.

                  A working to repel arrows and stones.

                  A working so that the bilge of the ship constantly pumps itself dry would be appreciated.

                  A working to desalinate water, or collect rainwater, so the ship could make those long trips to and from the west without having to haul so much fresh water with it would be useful, although this might be more appropriate as an artifact.

                  A working to make the inside of the ship bigger than the outside, ideally allowing the ship to hold more cargo without losing the ship's buoyancy, (or even not adjusting for buoyancy - just giving everyone more space without allowing more weight).

                                          Here are some of my ideas for sorcerous workings that don't seem to be posted above:

                                          (1) Improved stability for less rocking and swaying on the ship, making it safer for the crew and causes less cargo damage

                                          (2) A working to seal the hull so it doesn't leak

                                          (3) A warning against intruders of some sort

                                          (4) A working for the cargo hold so that the contents are preserved (so they don't rot, waste away etc) and the containers remain stable.

                                          (5) A working to reduce or eliminate the transmission of diseases
