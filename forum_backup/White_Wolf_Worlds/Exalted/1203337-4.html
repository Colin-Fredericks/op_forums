                    Originally posted by Lion Hamster View Post

                                    Originally posted by Lion Hamster View Post

                                    You're right about one thing dnd wizards are nothing like exalted sorcerers, dnd wizards are actually good

                                    This is an entirely ridiculous statement. The level of difference between Exalted fighters and sorcerers mechanically speaking is nothing like the difference between you're 3rd level D&D fighter and a 3rd level wizard who knows how to use Rope Trick in 3.5. D&D spellcasters are perhaps the worst example to draw from within any TTRPG because of how stupidly abusive their capabilities are compared to character classes focused on "mundane" abilities.

                                    Instead, why not take a look at the GURPS spellcasting rules in their 4th edition core set, which is another crunchy system including magic. Not only did your character have to buy an advantage to have magical aptitude, you also had to purchase each individual magic spell as a difficult-to-learn skill. Without some serious setting work by a GM, GURPS spellcasters often end up being more like skill monkeys with very strange abilities that require player creativity to effectively employ. While still distant from Exalted's treatment of sorcery, it's much closer than the wizards and clerics of D&D with mechanical abilities that invalidate the rest of the system and most intuitive game premises.

                                    I agree on the the fact that there aren't very many charms out there that improve sorcery in the Solar charmset (perhaps because their is an emphasis on making Solar characters unique based on their charm trees instead of a mechanic shared with other exalts). There's also nothing in the rules that says you can't make custom charms adding dice tricks to shape sorcery actions like many melee charms do. There's also nothing out there in the rules that says you can't have an artifact with evocations that aid in sorcery. If you want your game to have sorcery work more like melee (or even better than it), than try your hand at writing those charms, evocations, and maybe even new spells. Exalted seems to have always been a game that essentially relies on the players to write up mechanics, for better or for worse.

By investing in one other charm which also aids Occult rolls, you can grab a 1/scene full free occult excellency, and if that isn't designed for sorcery I don't know what is. That said, while there is sorcery support in Occult, it's kinda all over the place and requires a character to probably grab a lot of charms involving spirits they might not otherwise want.

                                                    Also, the way mechanics have been made for Ex3 means that it isn't all that hard to fill out a charm tree at E1 and E2 with dice tricks. At around E3 is where the really big and resounding effects occur. Writing exactly one or two charms for your sorcerer (who already is supposed to be rather unique via shaping rituals) isn't that ridiculous. Charms at E4 and E5 tend to focus on improving the real flashy stuff at E3 in different ways.

                                                    Do I wish there was better sorcery support in Occult? Oh yeah.
                                                    Is sorcery pointless without it? Hell no.

                                                    Is it annoying, and in some ways disappointing, to be expected to write mechanics for a game? Of course.
                                                    Am I angry that Melee has some cool subtrees over more sorcery-related charms? No, writing up some basic charms for sorcery akin to Excellent Strike or Rising Sun Slash doesn't seem overly difficult. Writing something like Peony-Blossom Technique seems really hard for someone without a lot of design thought and play-testing.
