    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post

    It’s a consideration. Really everything here is something to consider, including “giving Alchemicals reconfigurable charms is actually a bad idea and we shouldn’t do it again.”

    Honestly, it might be worth going back and asking “why are we giving Alchemicals reconfiguable charms in the first place?”
    Absolutely worth considering in any 3rd edition port.

    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
    The answers I’ve come up with so far for that are as follows:

    * It serves as an external power check on Alchemicals, so that they cannot use all their charms at once, which helps set their rough position on the power scale without having to give them entirely useless charms.
    * It feels techy and cool, so it helps set the atmosphere and make Alchemicals distinctive from other Exalted.
    * It helps tie Alchemicals into the industrial infrastructure and society of the Eight Nations, making everything more cohesive.
    I'd also add that properly implemented it may give some of the Batman feel to Alchemicals. IE: Given time to prepare for every situation I'm unstopable.

    Your cons I'm going to examine one at a time.

    ​
    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post

    And here are some fundamental issues with it:

    * On the other hand, it can make characters feel less distinctive, since they don’t necessarily have iconic charms anymore.
    I don't think this is a problem if you treat alchemical charms the way 3E treats artifacts. Every Champion who installs Piston-Driven Megaton Hammer is going to have a slightly different take on it, flavored by what caste and personality they are. It does raise questions of how can more than one champion even have PDMH since artifacts aren't normally replicable but I'm working around that.

    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
    * Or a character who does have a favorite set of charms might just skip using the mechanic entirely, which will either make their character much less effective overall or simply point out that the mechanic is unnecessary.
    Just because one player chooses to specialize does not negate the advantages of charm swaps for other players. A lunar can choose to stay in one form forever (including human) and that's not a problem. Charm design should encourage different playstyles, not enforce them.

    I'm perfectly fine with a player installing a single charm and then building an evocation tree off of it if that's the only thing they want to be able to do. Alchemicals can't dip into charm trees to round out a build the way solars who focus on evocations can.

    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
    * Unless abilities and attributes are equally discounted as charms, it might be difficult to get enough dice together to really make use of the swapping mechanic.
    I'm not sure what you mean by this? Are you suggesting that the charms be mostly divorced from attribute/ability combos or that alchemicals should be able to swap around their attributes/abilities at need? Or do you mean something else that I'm not getting?

    Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
    * Most players are used to creating a team of specialists who each have a particular skill that enables the party as a whole to handle a wide range of tasks. Suddenly being able to have an entire team of diplomancers might cause problems and complaints of “you’re getting into my niche” without explicitly examining the idea.

    For that last one, designing in fairly obvious and somewhat narrow builds that work well in concert might be a good idea. Like a good build for Reading Intentions that can give ammo to the Persuader build, while an Inspirer build helps set the mood in the background to give extra bonuses to the others. Since it’s difficult to do three things at once, it can be useful to have those as three different people, so setting things up like that in the charms might help bring that out.
    I see what you mean here, but Alchemicals are the splat most useful for bypassing niches and niche protection entirely. I find in the 3E game I'm playing in currently most scenes have one or two players come to the forefront and the rest don't have much to do. The bruiser is the combat monkey, while the Twilight has almost no combat skills at all. To me, the ability to swap out competencies should allow most characters to have something to do in most situations. At least that's what I'm hoping... The trick is balancing attunement costs and charm purchases.

                    Something else to consider is xp costs vs. how easy you want to enable going from “middling mortal” to “skilled mortal/basic exalt” to “compentent exalt” to “best exalt ever.”

                    The example I listed above with Mechanic, assuming she already had 15 charm slots, ends up costing 90 xp for 15 social charms and gets you effective Cha 4, Manip 3, App 4, 1st Cha and Manip Excellencies, effective Presence and Socialize values of 5, and 5 misc social charms. This should be enough to put her in the high side of “skilled mortal/basic exalt” or the low end of “comptent exalt” depending.

                    Now, iirc, going from 1 to 3 in an Attribute costs 12 points and going from 3 to 4 costs 12 points and going from 0 to 5 in an ability costs 33 points. So doing the above normally costs 102xp just for the stat bonuses, which cost the Alchemical 60xp. So there’s a significant advantage there.

                    I will also grant that it’s something of an extreme case where a character decides that they want to go from “this is my dump stat” to “actually pretty good at this,” and in most cases it’ll usually be someone picking up a few extra charms to round out a secondary competency or just not suck at their third (say buying 4th Manip twice and a Guile booster so they can stand in the back and not give the game away while the actual diplomats talk) which can be done pretty cheaply and thus fairly easily, though it still takes a bit of advance planning (or keeping 18 xp banked for emergencies).

                    But... Well, if you really wanna enable the “I can deal with anything at all, provided I can get a day to prepare” thing, you might wanna include a few emergency options (with significant downsides) that let an Alchemical install a big chunk of charms they haven’t actually purchased yet. Maybe they can borrow the unused charms of other Alchemicals, but it creates a bunch of issues and rapidly increasing penalties if they keep them installed for more than a day. Maybe they can take out a loan from the state and pay it back later.

                    Something to consider anyway.

                                                I had an idea for arrays that basically changed their purpose entirely.

                                                Actually it was to help with the "nobody switches charms" problem.

                                                Basically it worked like this: An array lets you put more than one charm into a single slot. Bigger arrays would take up more slots, with my completely untested value being charms/3, rounded up. The downside was that activating an array also deactivated an equal number of slots to what it took up, and you could only have one active at a time.

                                                The idea was that this would incentivize carrying two different arrays you could swap between. Expanding your overall charm capacity while restricting which ones you could use at the same time.

                                                    Ahh, the problem with slots. I can tell you this was a pretty huge problem in my 2e game, people almost never switched out charms. People almost never bought charms they didn't have slots for either, it simply wasn't worth the XP in most cases. You did see charms getting re-configured, changing out specialties and such, but people pretty much stuck to a core set of abilities that defined them and didn't play around much beyond that. There might be an exception for one or two players where, for one of their slots, they'd shift between the one or two spare charms until they ended up buying additional slots.

                                                    There's a good reason for this too, your character's potency in crisis situations stems from their invested XP. You can diversify and have a lot of options, but that means you just spend most of your time without access to a sizable chunk of your invested XP. Or you can continue to invest in submodules or say martial arts/protocol weaving where you don't have to sacrifice personal potential for the illusion of versatility.

                                                    All of that went into consideration when I did my 3e rebuild: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...mical-re-write

                                                    Here's what we did: Alchemicals buy "core" charms that becomes part of them, these don't get the same kinds of mild discount that previous versions did. Their slots could be filled with anything at no XP cost, though each charm had a much heftier install cost against your personal motes, up to 4 motes each that only cost more with sub modules included. This encouraged players to try out new things and determine if it was a situational need or they wanted to establish that capability as part of their core identity down the road. It also let them practice with new submodules and gain the versatility that they were intended to from the design perspective. Even still, some players hardly swapped things out but that lack of preperation came back to bite them later on. There's a bit of a learning curve too, overzealous players would load up and discover that only having 3 motes of personal essence lead to a lot of problems with constantly flaring animas, so it was always sort of a balancing act to find that right sweet spot for whatever loadout they wanted to go with: Trying to be social or stealthy kept the installed charms lite while going to war where there wasn't social/circumstantial consequences to glowing like a beacon would have people armed to the teeth. I don't see this as a problem though, rather a design feature that encompasses what we thought players really wanted !

                                                    Anyway, I spent WAY too much time doing charm design and balance, feel free to utilize http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...mical-re-write wholesale or adapt the charms to suit your purposes. These were developed to be celestial tier without a bunch of time consuming dice tricks. Some mechanics were lifted wholesale from the 3e core book because interesting mechanics are always more fun than dice adders. Plus we went with the adage of: There should be more meaningful options available than one player could ever take, that way two players can take the same things while still being distinct. The Essence levels were scaled to: 1-3, typical alchemical champion; 4-5 is an alchemical colossus and anything beyond that gets into the municipal level.
