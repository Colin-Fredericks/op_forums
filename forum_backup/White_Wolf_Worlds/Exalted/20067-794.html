                This exchange happened in the Ask the Devs thread a while back:





                So, I'm curious what the folks here think. (I seem to recall that in older editions, "the Anathema stole power from the sun and moon" was the general belief, but it sounds like it's not that simple anymore.) If a young Immaculate asks his sifu what the deal is with the sun god, which of these is more likely?

                1) "The Unconquered Sun is one of the Dragons' foremost lieutenants -- as you'd imagine, since the sun is second only to the elemental poles themselves as a pillar of Creation. The foul Anathema waylaid him for his power long ago, but he managed to escape with enough of his power to continue illuminating the world. He has long stood as an exemplar of virtue, and has never shirked his duty to seek worship from the mortals (although some fools have been known to pray to him regardless)."

                2) "The sun god is a fool whose power was stolen long ago. If he'd been more vigilant, we wouldn't have to deal with Solar Anathema! Still, he performs his actual duty well enough, so I suppose the Dragons must have forgiven his lapse eventually."

                3) "The Unconquered Sun is a damnable traitor. The Solar Anathema are his so-called 'Chosen!' Someday he will pay the price for his perfidy. Still, the Dragons must have their reasons for tolerating him now."

                4) Something else (obviously there could be a lot more nuance to any of these angles)

                (Eventually Immaculates do learn that the Solar and Lunar Anathema are Exalted, but I'm not sure how much context they get for that information...)
                To the uninitiated (including the vast majority of Dynasts and young monks), I picture a hard 1. Peasants might get a spiel about how asking questions about the gods is heresy, but vigilance against the Anathema (and given the raw power of the Unconquered Sun it) probably makes it prudent for Immaculates to give a sermon on the nature of the Unconquered Sun every Sunday. And that's going to lean hard into interpretation 1.

                For the higher ups who know the Anathema are Exalted, something half-way between 2 and 3 with plenty of theological debate on the Sun's culpability and monks who swing between the extremes.

                I imagine there are some Immaculates who believe that wayward gods are simply misguided beings trying to help mortals, and they might extend this belief to the Sun (and perhaps they dream of travelling to Yu Shen to teach the Unconquered Sun his proper place in the Perfected Hierarchy).

                        Originally posted by Jefepato View Post
                        Huh. I just noticed, there's a sidebar on page 90 of The Realm that mentions that while the Immaculate Order acknowledges the Celestial Incarnae as the highest gods and second only to the Dragons, it doesn't worship them at all. You'd think they'd have the most prominent positions on the prayer calendars. But I guess the Sidereals backing this plan decided their top bosses really aren't paying attention.
                        Going by 2e, I'm not sure the Incarnae would even notice the loss - they receive something like half of all all prayer devoted to gods in the Celestial Bureaucracy anyway (from which the Unconquered Sun pays the "ambrosia dole" that indigent gods live on), and I believe they get basically all of the generic prayers that someone sends up to Heaven in general.

                        3e hasn't really described anything quite like the prayer-based economic system of Heaven, and personally, I rather suspect it won't, as I think it rather goes against the generally more numinous, mysterious feel that the gods are supposed to have now. Also note that Cult doesn't give Essence any more, just willpower recovery. I think that one of the following situations are pretty plausible:
                        • Even without active religious organizations, the Celestial Incarnae have more than enough people worshiping them through various means that they don't even notice the difference. When it takes 1,000,000 people to give you Cult NA, the fact that you went down from 100,000,000 people doing so during the 1st Age down to 2,000,000 doing so now doesn't really signify.
                        • Maybe the Incarnae never had Cult of very high rating! In the First Age, the Incarnae gave control over Creation to the Exalted Host, and retired to play the Games of Creation. Maybe serious cults of them were never really set up by any of their Exalted, from a combination of not thinking it necessary (why tell people to be grateful for the literal sun in the sky, when it's up there all the time anyway) and possibly a bit of contempt (when you've met the Moon, and found out they are kind of done with the whole "ruling the world" thing, it takes some of the shine off the devotion). So perhaps they didn't really notice that they had dropped a dot of Cult or two, because it wasn't providing much in the first place.
                        • Possibly, while they've dropped enough levels in Cult that they'd notice, they've never actually needed to spend enough wp to notice. Playing the Games of Divinity and lounging in the heavenly pleasure dome doesn't sound like it burns all that many wp, all things considered. If Mars used to have Cult NA and has now dropped to Cult 3, but she hasn't had to spend more than 3 wp in a story in the last few centuries, she might not have noticed the lack.

                        My own interpretation would be that the default assumption is that gods shouldn't talk to non-monk mortals at all, except in emergencies (and it better be a big emergency). In fact, the Immaculate Order would probably prefer if gods didn't casually talk to anyone, including monks. Actual Dragon-Blooded monks would be more okay, but probably still discouraged to form casual relationships with, at least unless it was initiated by the Dragon-Blood. Now, all these rules can probably be relaxed a bit for a spirit that's done long, faithful service to the Immaculate Order. If the God of Orderly Social Relationships has done everything the Order wants since she signed up when the Empress first declared the Realm, and wants to preach to mortals every Water-day about the benefits of the Perfected Hierarchy, the monks are probably going to give that the okay. But it will always be an exception, permitted by special dispensation, and everyone involved knows it can be rescinded if the god steps out of line.

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                                    I don't think Kelly was suggesting someone would take the Sun away. Just that people might not consider it an entity to worship -- like how people generally don't offer prayers to gravity.

                                                    In real life, Sun cults are pretty common. In Exalted where you literally might meet a god walking down the street, and every being in heaven apart from the Sun has a dedicated priesthood actively trying to recruit you for their Cult, it literally might not occur to people that the Sun is a god. And recognising that the Sun rises if you pray to it or not, people might wonder what the Sun is actually the god of, and what benefit (if any) is derrived from praying to it.

                                                    Of course some might offer the Sun prayers of gratitude. But for what?

                                                    If the Sun shines well and your crops are bountiful... you thank the field god.
                                                    If the Sun is wasting and the river dries up... you pray to the river god.
                                                    If you want to darken your complexion with a tan, you pray to Venus.

                                                    Because these are all real gods. If you haven't personally met them, then you have met their priests, who extoll the value of worshiping them.

                                                    Now I'm not saying that no aiministic faith on Earth places importance on a Sun god (far from it). But in the context of Exalted, where learned scholars insist the Sun doesn't need prayers, and you can actually meet the gods you sincerely believe in... I can see the absentee king of heaven falling from prominence.

                                                    Sun worship is going to be viewed by the average denizen of the Creation the say way modern humans view worship of Viracocha. At best there's going to be some cultures whose ancestors used to worship the Sun generations ago, and a handful of resurgent worshippers who either cling to the old faith or who have rediscovered it as a kind of counter culture.
