        Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
        In a lot of western rpgs, there's a far off land of Martial Artists with exotic and powerful techniques. Is there the opposite equivalent in Exalted? Is there place/school/tribe of heavily armored, shield bearing, knightly style warriors who are unusual and different to the standard Martial Artists and Military Kung Fu style of the Realm/ most PCs?

        There is a hell of a lot of other cultures present in Creation. General East Asian just tends to be Creations "Rome", in the sense that it influences some aspects of the cultures that surround the Realm. Most of my campaigns have had very little, if at all esoteric martial arts and suchlike.

        There are Vikings and other European cultures and such in the North, African and Mediterranean stuff going on the South, Native American stuff in the East, and Oceanic stuff happening in the West. And those are just general trends. You could feasibly fit any culture in Creation, up to and including generic feudal fantasy.

        Plus ....... heavy cavalry is going to be pretty similar, no matter what your culture is. A heavily armored guy on a horse with a lance isn't going to turn many heads in most settled parts of Creation.

            Originally posted by Shaddar View Post
            Would it be too op to make the eclipse oath to last for generations. So the son of a oathtaker? (is this the word) or oathbreaker would also be subjected to the oath or curse.
            It's probably fine as long as it's one person at a time, like a "passed-on responsibility/burden" kind of thing. Stylistically I would make it literally a contract signed in blood, though. The person making and signing the deal/contract has to be well aware of what they're signing away.

            There were examples of this in the prior editions, but I can't think of any off the top of my head beyond the generic "oaths of hospitality" Deathlords, Demons and Fairfolk are bound to. (Doesn't help that Changeling: The Lost also has fae that broke an oath with Iron, which made them forever vulnerable to it because of its spite. I get those two confused often.)

            IMO it's the kind of story detail that is almost entirely the ST's call, as it's purely a narrative device, so if it fits the story you or they want to tell, go for it.
