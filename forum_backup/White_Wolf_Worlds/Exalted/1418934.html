    Help understanding the Raksha

    10-19-2020, 12:42 AM
    I came in around very late 2e and am most familiar with 3e's take on the setting. Going back through 1e and looking over their depictions later on I've more or less wrapped my head around the various spirits and creatures in Exalted, where they come from, and what they do.

    However, the Raksha have always been weird to me, not in terms of their origin or nature which I get, but more what they can do and how they affect Creation. What is their role and why are they so feared? The 3e isn't super clear and I've found the information gleaned from reading about them just reinforces the idea that they seem to be a thing that has no place outside the wyld, their abilities seem weak in creation, am I missing something? I actually have 1e fair folk but I keep putting it down because I can't seem to figure them out, seeming more focused on being player characters who go around wyld shaping.

    I'm hoping I can get a few questions answered, I'm not trying to get their 3e version (which I know isn't out yet), more just trying to figure out what to do with them as NPCs:
    -Can they naturally change shape?

    -Do they die when killed or need extra work like spirits do?

    -Are they considered 'spirits'?

    -When a raksha is born do they decide their station or is it based on strength when they take form?

    -Do grace's exist in any physical sense like in CtL or are they just weird virtues?

    -Is there a consistancy to their appearance (ears, elemental or beast traits, etc.) that makes them recognizable? 1e player's guide stated this but I don't know if it stuck around, and I'm uncertain how they would be distinguished from elementals if so.

    -Can they shed their shape for power at the expense of their identity (question inspired by kill 6 billion demons)?

    -I know they are very good at shaping in the wyld but what kind of power would they have outside of it? I've seen mentions that they're rather weak but that seems odd considering the fear around them.

                  Originally posted by Darkfoxdev View Post
                  Thanks.Aside from wyld shaping and feeding on virtues/intimicies what are some notable traits about them that would be seen in creation or the bordermarches?
                  Well they're beings born out of a sea of infinite potential and change, so trying to pin down commonalities there might be a bad road to go down. If anything the thing that joins them all together is that they're so different. As the book says about their cataphracts:

                  "Cataphracts vary in appearance, though coteries
                  and courts tend to share similar forms. They may be leonine
                  desert riders with eyes of fire, queenly warriors astride
                  winged nightmare-beasts, or owl-faced knights driving
                  chariots pulled by naked mortals"

                  Basically just make them freaking weird and you should be good. You won't find any Raksha that just look like normal people hanging out with normal animals.

                  They're also kind of fond of stories and stuff usually. Like rather than have goals and wishes of their own necessarily they prefer to play roles in stories. That's not a 100% always thing, it's just something they do sometimes. So like a Raksha might go on a crusade into Creation not because he wants to kill people, but because he wants to play the role of a crusader.

                          Here's my take, which I think is mostly canon as of 3rd edition:

                          Raksha are a type of being that emerged from the Wyld an attacked Creation at the end of the 1st age, when it was weakened by the Great Contagion. There are other types of creature in the Wyld, many of which the Raksha have enslaved as servants or warriors.

                          The Raksha that currently live in Creation are exiles. Having failed to destroy Creation, they are now trapped here. This may be because, having taken on fixed shape in order to attack Creation, they found it impossible to go back. Or it may be because a new type of being has arisen within the Wyld which preys on them.

                          They vary considerably from one another in terms of appearance and behaviour but there are a few consistencies: They are usually humanoid but with inhuman (animal or elemental) features, they are almost always incredibly beautiful, and they usually put on the airs of aristocracy - organizing themselves into courts and surrounding themselves with servants. Their servants and warmonsters follow a similar theme, but tend to be more phantasmagorical - beasts and elements blended together in a nightmare, religious vision, an acid trip.

                          Raksha sustain themselves by feeding on emotions. Typically, they provoke those emotions in mortals by raiding to create terror or seduction to create love or lust, but other feelings are equally sustaining so long as they are passionate. They often kidnap mortals to more easily feed on them. Having one's emotions fed on by a Raksha repeatedly over a long period is damaging to the mind and/or soul, and eventually results in a mortal which lacks the capacity for emotion and therefore volition, known as a Dream-Eaten. This kind of thing gives Raksha a bad reputation, and most people try to avoid them, with mixed success.

                          Raksha can demonstrate a wide variety of different powers, but they tend to be either inhuman beauty, skill, and grace (representated by massive dice pools), power over emotions (social effects, particularly to inspire emotions and create intimacies), and power to create things out of ambient emotion and wyld energy (for example, a Raksha knight might be able to create swarms of warrior-creatures born from nightmares). Other powers are very possible.

                          They are vulnerable to iron but apparently not steel, for some reason. They extent of this vulnerability varies.

                          Thematically, they are a blend of the most epic European fairy stories (my go to is the elves from Poul Anderson's Broken Sword), decadent exiled nobility, and the most dramatic cape-swooshy kind of vampire.

                          The art usually depicts them as having gigantic Record of Lodoss War-style elf ears.

                          To use them as NPCs, figure out an interesting method of provoking strong emotions (maybe this one traps mortals and makes them play games for their lives, feeding on the terror, elation and despair elicited by rolls of the dice) and then come up with a couple of strange aesthetic features. Then use the stats for the Cataphract or Lorelei from the corebook, depending on whether they are more social or more combat oriented.

                                  Humans are scared of sharks. Very few humans are harmed by sharks each year, but sharks can harm humans. They're (or at least can be) bigger, stronger and faster than us.

                                  Fae are bigger, stronger, faster... smarter. And they actively predate humans.

                                  They are the terrifying stuff of myth and nightmare.

                                  The hag in the bog. The troll under the bridge. The knights of thunder and shadows who steal the breath of little children. The evil spirit that stalks the winter feasts. The giants who live in the wild places.

                                  The 3E corebook gives some sample creatures of the wyld, complete with powers. And HDNP gives a great raksha noble.

                                  Exalted can usually beat the snot out of them one-on-one.

                                  So, to challenge a circle, you'll need several. Or something the fae is good at that the circle is bad at.

When you run an encounter with The Knights of Leodine and describe them as men with the heads of lions, the PCs aren't supposed to know if they're fae or beastmen or gods or demons.
