            I agree with aluminiumtrioxid that the fact that xp to BP ratios incentivize you to take 5s at character creation, and this isn't ideal. But in my current (and first) game we used XP-based character creation and I still had multiple 5s (I forget how many - I have tons now as I am Essence 4). In fact I wonder if one could go in the other direction, with some house rule where getting 5s costs extra XP at character creation - sort of going in the reverse direction.

            I definitely think experience in other games is a big factor, as JohnDoe244 suggested. But I don't think "build to hit the absolute limit of your combat ability" is a good description of the problem. In games like D&D, maxing out your combat ability requires carefully weighing different choices against each other and looking for powerful combinations. That helps a lot in Exalted too, but the problem we're talking about here is more like "That ability looks useful - I guess I'll take a four or five in it." That habit is a lot harder to break!

            It's certainly true, though, that it would help to pay closer attention to the "5 = best in the world" idea at character creation. But if your character concept involves combat in any way, it seems quite easy to spend a few points in character creation, end up with 4s in key abilities and attributes, and be very powerful, when you have so many to start with. Most core concepts require you to take, at most, a 5 in one or two abilities, some high attributes, and a pile of Charms - you'll have plenty left over, and the (perhaps) trap of shoring up your combat skills is easy to fall into.

            The fact Chausse mentioned that combat protects from death is also very relevant. I would add that a starting player can be a lot more sure that, if they put points in combat, those points will help them out, even if they don't understand and master the combat system, then they can be for abilities relevant to other systems like social abilities or Lore.
