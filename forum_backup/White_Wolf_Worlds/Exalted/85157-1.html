        Great art, but the writing - especially the below-comic extras - seems like the religious/spiritual bits are tacked on. In particular, many of the flourishes seem to advertise "this is from the age when people didn't know how to tell stories that well" rather than "this is mystical".

        One illustrative example is when the peace keeper speaks of knowing "of" the fourth syllable: that literally just means it knows that there are at least four syllables. If it meant that it knew the fourth syllable, then it should have said so; if it meant to state some sort of half-knowledge, then it should have said that. Saying that it knows "of" the fourth syllable as if that is meaningful implies, among other things, not immediately knowing "of" the first, second, and third syllables - i.e., that this character is incapable of basic logical deduction, which is a mental limitation inconsistent with the rest of the character's portrayal.

            "have knowledge of" is a fairly well established (if baroque) way of saying "comprehend/know". See, e.g. Proverbs 30: 3 "I have not learned wisdom, nor have I knowledge of the Holy One."

            The writer is not saying "I don't know that the Holy One exists" he's saying "I don't know *who/what" the Holy One is".

            Likewise, "having knowledge of the fourth syllable" does in fact mean "knowing what the fourth syllable is" not just "knowing that there are at least four syllables".

            It's an appropriate phrasing giving that it's supposed to be from a religious text.
