    Can a circle of dynasts "win?"

    06-15-2018, 06:31 PM

    Of course, the title of the thread is meant to be a bit provocative

    Let us imagine, arguendo, that a classic sort of conclusion to a dynast campaign is to put one of the PC's on the throne, the rest of the circle as the most powerful dynasts in the Realm, and to reform the Realm into the greatest civilization on the face of Creation, a beacon of justice and virtue that protects Creation from those things that would threaten it.

    The question, then, is this: is that even a plausible goal for a group of terrestrial dynasts in 3E?

    Allow me to play devil's advocate and answer in the negative:
    • Terrestrial exalts are individually quite weak in comparison to all of the other powers in Creation; they simply lack the metaphysical power to overcome the obstacles.
    • The Realm is a baby-eating rape machine of aristocratic decadence coupled with engineered (for Reasons) incompetent dysfunction and a myopic focus on short-term self-interest (when pursuing short-term self-interest is not reasonably calculated to achieve long-term success).
    • The Realm is being smacked around by Lunars, secretly manipulated by Sidereals, and any newly-exalted Solar can punch even the most powerful dragon-blooded elder in half without breaking a sweat.
    • Etc.
    Now, I don't really want to debate the merits of my devil's advocate argument; these views are not consistent with the forum orthodoxy, and I don't want to generate a thread that simply reiterates that orthodoxy. Further, a YCMV argument may work for some people, but it's both an uninteresting response and an unsatisfying one from the perspective of a game that includes both rules and fluff (both of which I quite like).

    So, can a circle of dynasts win, or are they helpless in the face of more powerful actors in Creation with their own designs?

      Let us imagine, arguendo, that a classic sort of conclusion to a dynast campaign is to put one of the PC's on the throne, the rest of the circle as the most powerful dynasts in the Realm, and to reform the Realm into the greatest civilization on the face of Creation, a beacon of justice and virtue that protects Creation from those things that would threaten it.

      I think, tbh, that the first is very achievable in game (my group did it... I only ended up as ambassador to Autocthon, but other PCs became Empress, Prime Minister, Head of the All-Seeing Eye, etc) though probably not in a short game.

      The second part, reforming into a beacon of justice...
      Well, reform, cultural and economic changes take time. A lot of time. And you're probably not going to be able to do it until after winning a civil war against reactionaries and counter-revolutionaries anyway.
      So, I don't really see it as viable in the short or medium-term. The long-term though? Yeah, it's possible. In fact, making it a beacon of justice and virtue probably makes protecting Creation much easier, because you may not have to fight Lunars and Solars quite so much.

          Originally posted by Equitable Remedy View Post
          So, can a circle of dynasts win, or are they helpless in the face of more powerful actors in Creation with their own designs?
          Keep in mind that any answers we provide you are subjective, and amount to little more then personal opinions. That said, my opinion is that; yes Dynasts can "win" at least in part. Terrestrial PCs can absolutely win the throne. With that accomplished, they could definitely set about reforming the Empire into something a more efficient (though as the Wizard above said, this takes quite a bit of time to accomplish). They can, and should, use the Realm's military might to protect Creation from those that would see it destroyed! So the answer to your question is "yes," a circle of Dynasts can win in this regard.

          However, I do not believe Dragonbloods have the power to save Creation. One of the big themes of Creation's current timeline is that it's slowly dying. The number of Shadowlands appearing and/or growing in Creation continues to rise. The number of Wyld-tainted lands and those completely reclaimed by the Wyld along the borders continue to rise. Enemies that want to see Creation destroyed or transformed into something else, have been gained considerable strength. Personally, I don't believe the Dragonbloods have the strength to turn it around at this point. They've passed the metaphorical point of no return for terrestrial powers to stop it. This doesn't mean though that Dragonbloods aren't integral to "fixing" Creation. Not even the Solar's have the power to be everywhere and do everything. Dragonbloods are vital to Creation's safety and well-being, but I believe they're going to need considerable help to pull Creation back from it's downward spiral!

                          I'll split my answer:

                          Thematically, well, yes. They'll have to be the most exceptional people in their society, and they'll have to eat the elephant one bite at a time to co-opt the rest of their society into social changes... but thematically yes.

                          (Or at least, so I'd argue on grounds including allowing us to imagine a more open and diverse set of futures for the game setting, and play out the game as if it's high stakes and all of them are up for grabs. Other people would argue, and this was a common argument at least back in late 2e era and early 3e era that, thematically no, Dragonblooded can't do much to "save the world", because "It's the Solars' show", which is a reference to the idea that everyone has to be insufficient in order that the Solars can come to save the day, and on the grounds that Sidereal prophecy is otherwise undermined.).

                          Now, mechanically, I wouldn't even begin to know how you'd answer that question. You certainly can't white room simulate it. The play rules don't really allow to abstract what the advantages are of masses of Dragonblooded against, say, 300 strong Lunar Exalted (or however many there are).

                                                  What do you mean by "win?" I feel that you're losing the thread of things by defining it as this sort of all-compassing eternal victory that nobody (with the possible exception of loyalist Abyssals) is capable of achieving.

                                                  If you're playing a group of Dynasts, and you maneuver your families into positions of power, stabilize the Realm, beat back challenges from the prominent Anathema of the time and lead into an era of peace and prosperity under Dragon-Blooded rule, then you've won. Solars and Lunars will still be out there and may one day overthrow the new era of Dragon-Blooded hegemony you created; one day the Realm may fall. But not today.

                                                  And that's... basically how it always is, regardless of who you're playing. With the possible exception of Abyssals, nobody ever gets to say that they won in the end, because nothing ever ends. If your goal for winning is "achieve eternal victory forever in a way that can never be reversed by events in the future", you're dooming yourself to disappointment.

                                                  (Even the most gonzo-crazy loyalist Abyssals just sort of have to take it on faith that there won't be another universe created after they destroy the current one.)
