                                    11-01-2016, 04:32 PM
                                    Try to Help People: 3
                                    Loot the Bodies: 4
                                    Attempt to get the Emissary of Nexus to be your Mentor?! 1
                                    Watch and Learn: 2


                                    As screaming starts from the middle of the river, the crowd watching from the shore decides it’s probably time to be somewhere else. Anywhere else. The boats and boatmen whose vessels are still intact are skirting the middle of the river, giving the Emissary and the wreckage a wide berth. The people in the middle of the river scream and thrash…but by the time you’ve managed to get out of your boat and grab a big bundle of rope you could maybe throw to somebody…the screaming has stopped.

                                    Bodies bob in the water, the pale of their clothes little more than daubs of color against the blackness of the water. Here and there you see a dead fish bobbing white through the heavy rain…and the vast white bulk of the River Dragon’s belly slowly surfaces along with great gouts of white steam rising up from the canal. The cold and heavy rain pounds the steam down to a few feet over the water. It would be pretty, if dozens of people weren’t floating dead in the water.

                                    Eel is staring, muttering a quiet prayer. You look at him…and back to the river. It looks like everybody is too scared to do anything…but that’s not going to last long.

                                    “Eel!”

                                    The old man ignores you and stares out at the river.

                                    “Eel!” This time you throw the bundle of rope at his head, and he clumsily ducks and catches it.

                                    “WHAT?!”

                                    “Eel. This is our chance. While everybody else is too scared!”

                                    “Are you daft, child?” Eel glowers at you. “Our chance for WHAT, exactly? To get boiled like a couple of duck eggs?”

                                    You sigh. “Eel. How much is a River Dragon tooth worth over at the Alchemists’ market?”

                                    Eel raises an eyebrow. “…I see your point. But I gave you your ride and I’ve got the fish you promised me in the bottom of the boat. Why should I cut you in?”

                                    “You need an extra pair of hands. And, um. Do you really want to be the first person to put your hands in that River Dragon’s mouth? Maybe it’s tougher than the Emissary thought.”

                                    The old man stares at you. “Gods. You’re your father’s kid, alright…fine. Get back in.”

                                    Moments later, you are poling back out into the river. The bottom of the boat is hot against your bare feet, and the steam rising from the river is scalding. Normally you’d be frightened…but after a day trudging through freezing rain, it actually feels pretty nice!

                                    You near the River Dragon’s corpse. The Emissary is floating nearby, standing on a roiling bank of steaming fog as though it were solid ground. It cocks its masked head, watching the two of you approach the monster. Eel tries hard not to look. You give the Emissary a nervous wave. It…waves back, seeming faintly amused, then turns and sails away across the canal and out into the rain-swept darkness.

                                    Eel takes his pole and gingerly pokes the River Dragon in the head. When it continues to float upside-down, he gets a bit more confident and pokes it harder, in the eye. Nothing happens.
                                    “…I think it’s dead,” he tells you, poling closer.

                                    “Good. Alright, I’m gonna see if I can cut out some teeth.”

                                    “Get its eyes too, if you can,” Eel nods. “Dunno what they’re worth, but alchemists always have pickled eyes floating in glass bottles.”

                                    “Eyes.” You nod.

                                    Eel brings the boat up right alongside the monster’s head. Its huge snout is half the size of the canal boat, and twice as wide. This close, the River Dragon smells…delicious. A little bit like hot turtle soup from the stand outside Aunt Dwi's brothel. You reach out and poke it with your knife. No reaction…

                                    “Hurry,” Eel tells you. “I can see folks on shore getting in their boats. We’re about to have some company!”

                                    You grab the River Dragon’s huge snout and, straining, lift it so it leans on the boat, which tips dangerously.

                                    “Ho! Careful there, kid!”

                                    Armed with your Nighthammer-forged spear blade, you lift the creature’s front lip to reveal fangs the size of your forearm. You stab deep into its gums and begin sawing. It’s…slow, hard work. Once you’ve gotten the base of the fang revealed, it’s still hard to pry the tooth loose…but you manage it. The long spearpoint is definitely better at stabbing and prying than at cutting.

                                    “One fang down,” you tell Eel.

                                    “Great! We’re lucky it’s such a shitty night out. Looks like our competition’s only two or three boats, kid!”

                                    As you saw out fangs, Eel greets your fellow scavengers with a wave and a big grin, telling them there’s plenty for everybody and asking if they want to grab the other side and help pole the big fucker to shore or if they just want to harvest out here to cut out the folks who don’t have boats. It’s agreed to do the butchering out in the middle of the canal, and men with hooks and large knives quickly set to work cutting the River Dragon and divvying up the pieces. One of the boats ignores the monster completely and starts rounding up the dead bodies to search them for silver teeth or jewelry, and maybe to sell any recognizable corpses back to their families for funerary rites.

                                    After an hour of finger-numbing sawing and hacking and yelling in the cold rain (for the canal cools rapidly), you’re done.

                                    Eel finishes your ride home and drops you off in Nighthammer with a tough, rubbery, stinking spleen stuffed full of what he has deemed “your cut.” Which pretty much means everything he was able to get hold of but didn’t have any room for in the boat or in his net. He promises to give you some more lessons on being a boatman and lets you keep your fish, telling you he made plenty of profit off you tonight!

                                    Nobody’s out in the rain to question you staggering down the street covered in blood and gore carrying a large and bloody “sack” over your shoulder. You managed to get back to your nest, and with some difficulty you manage to get your loot up onto the roof with you and curl up under the eaves.

                                    Food, a fish to eat tomorrow, a whole silver dinar, and a bunch of stuff you can sell at the market for more coin! And on top of that, Eel really likes you and referred to you as his apprentice talking to a couple of the other boatmen. All in all, a pretty good day!

                                    It’s pretty late now-the bell over at the Foundry just rang for eight o clock a few minutes ago. What now?



                                    -Sleep! You’re safe and well fed and warm and dry, but you’re exhausted. If you’re going to haggle River Dragon teeth tomorrow, you should do it with a clear head and probably after you’ve changed into clothes that aren’t covered in blood and eaten your fish for breakfast.

                                    -Get Clean. You’re covered in blood and bits of River Dragon organs. Strip down on the slippery roof tiles and stand in the freezing rain and scrub out your hair until most of it washes off, or else you’re gonna be covered in flies if the rain lets up before tomorrow morning. Dry out under the warm eaves of the Blackhand Company’s foundry before you put on dry rags, so you don’t catch a flu during the night.

                                    -No time to rest-go sell your loot NOW, before anybody else gets to the Alchemist’s market with River Dragon bits. If you’re lucky and you can get somebody out of bed, you might be able to offer to connect an alchemist with Eel and the other boatmen, so he can have a monopoly on the goods tomorrow. That could be real profitable…if he doesn’t just have his guards rough you up and take your merchandise and go to haggle with the boatmen on his own.

                                    -You want to hit the market first, but nobody’s going to haggle in good faith with a kid. You need some muscle that looks old enough to barter with. Perhaps you could cut Aunt Dwi in, she knows a few alchemists and healers who might be good buyers…or perhaps you should get a hold of Rat. He’s been a jerk lately, but he’s got some connections with local drug dealers and might be able to get you in touch with an alchemist who won’t ask too many questions.

                                    -Selling the stuff is dumb. You should put the meat over one of the foundry’s smokestacks to make sure it’ll keep-that’s enough meat to eat for weeks! Maybe a month if you can get a pot of stew going...That patch of skin would make a great purse, or maybe armor plating to put under your shirt in case somebody stabs you, if you can boil off the fat under it and sew it up to some leather with some twine and get rid of the stink…and you bet those River Dragon fangs would sell better if they were carved, like the carved mammoth tusks you’ve seen at the stands for the merchants who trade up North. Worked goods sell for more than raw materials!

                                    -Selling the stuff is dumb. You've spent enough time around the monks and generally wandering the Temple District learning about spirits from their stories, to know that River Dragons are spiritually potent. Drinking its blood, prepared properly, could let you breathe underwater. Eating its tongue (which you have a sizeable chunk of in your bundle) could let you speak in the languages of birds. Those fangs could be carved and inlaid with powdered jade or molten moonsilver to make potent magical talismans! That's why the alchemists would pay for the ingredients! Why sell this stuff when, if you can hold onto it long enough to figure out how to USE it, you might be able to give yourself magic that could make you rich?

                                    -Something Else?

                                    Resources You Have Available:
                                    • Tattered Rags
                                    • Fishhooks (2)
                                    • Rough twine (1 roll)
                                    • 3 Large Earthworms
                                    • Your Lucky Pebble
                                    • Twine Armband
                                    • Steel spearhead (six-inch knife blade with a clumsy handle)
                                    • A medium sized fish
                                    • One silver Dinar!
                                    • Six River Dragon Fangs (two big ones the size of your forearm, four smaller ones a little longer than your hand)
                                    • A four-by-four swatch of thick, scaly River Dragon hide
                                    • A River Dragon Spleen? You think it’s a spleen? Tied in a bundle
                                    • A Spleenful of River Dragon Parts, including one eyeball

                                    ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                    You Have Gained:
                                    Attributes: (12/13 Allocated)*
                                    Dexterity +1: You just spent an hour climbing all over a giant dead lizard and cutting it up with hands numbed from the cold using a tool not intended for that purpose without cutting off any of your own fingers.
                                    Intelligence +1: Instead of thinking in the moment and running away from danger, you quickly came up with a plan for long-term profit.

                                    *You may spend no more points on Physical Attributes, which are primary for you. How you spend your last Attribute point will determine whether you are Social or Mental Secondary.

                                    Abilities: (23/28 Allocated)
                                    Larceny +1: You spotted an opportunity for profit in spite of danger and went for it!
                                    Medicine +1: You can learn a lot about anatomy dissecting a giant corpse in the pouring rain!
                                    Melee +1: You can learn a lot about knifework by dissecting a giant corpse in the pouring rain!
                                    Athletics +1: You can learn a lot about climbing and balance by clambering around on a giant floating corpse trying not to fall off (the main thing you learned is that having a good stabbing implement to anchor yourself with really helps!). That climb to and from your nest on the roof doesn’t seem so bad now at all!

                                    Specialties: (0/4 Allocated)

                                    Merits: (1/7 Allocated)

                                                            Since most Exalted folks are used to Resources, thought I'd give you an idea of what a Dinar looks like.

                                                            One Koku, which is the realm paper scrip equal to half a Bit of Jade, is equal to 0.96 Dinar. I'm gonna round it to A Koku equals a Dinar for the purposes of keeping it simple, since we only have one.

                                                            That's one month at peasant wages working in the Realm, or three months of unskilled day-to-day grunt labor in Nexus. It's enough money for a family of four to eat meat for a weak (chicken or fish) or rice and cheap vegetables for six weeks. It's enough money to buy yourself a dozen goats or a couple of dairy cows, though not land for them to graze on, for that you'd need to pay a grazing fee. It is not enough to purchase a quality riding animal like a horse or a camel, nor is it enough to buy you a working animal like an ox or a mule. A Dinar is enough to buy yourself eight new sets of simple peasant or workman's clothing, or a single nice outfit you could wear to deal with the professional artisans in the city. It is not enough to afford any decent drugs or medicines, nor is it enough to afford a sword. It's enough to easily afford a small quantity of simple tools like chisels, hammers, awls, shovels, crow bars, knives, axes, or hatchets, or enough to afford inexpensive weapons like javelins, spears, tridents. It's enough to afford quite a bit of raw ingredients you could use to put your Craft: Weaving skill to use-needles and thread, or wicker, are cheap. It's enough to afford any Thrown weapon (knives, hatchets, needles, slings, lassos, nets, bolas...) but not enough to afford any Archery Weapons (bows, flamepieces) or any form of armor. It's not enough money for anything occult, or at least not anything with real power-for the donation of a Dinar you might be able to afford to have a priest at the Temple District inscribe your name on a prayer strip to hang in the temple for good fortune, but it's not enough for them to make a true offering on your behalf.

                                                            It is enough to open a bank account with the Guild, who would be happy to store the money for you (and invest it and keep their returns on your Dinar). It is not enough for the Guild to issue you cheques drawn on your account, you'd need to visit the bank in person to withdraw cash if you wanted to use your money. The Guild requires a minimum of ten Dinars in an account before they begin to pay interest based on the Salt Rate.
