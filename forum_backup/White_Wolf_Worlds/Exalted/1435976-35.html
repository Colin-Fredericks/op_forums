    Originally posted by Voyd211 View Post
    How would you handle artifacts if one of your PCs has the Giant merit?

    Most armor and weapons from the First Age just wouldn’t fit a character who’s tall enough to bonk their head on any doorway not built for a horse and rider. Plus, I’ve heard the Craft system is a hellish nightmare that nobody likes trying to use so making something just for your giant is also no bueno. Does the character have to just rely on their innate strength and toughness?
    From a realism standpoint:
    Weapons, who cares. Unless the handle is tiny the size of the wielder probably doesn't matter unless the wielder is just absurdly huge, beyond the bounds of the Giant merit. A really big fellow might have trouble with small or short weapons, but artifact weapons aren't small or short.
    Now realistically speaking, every single set of armor that we recognize as armor, especially anything involves greaves, gauntlets, or other such bits would probably fit absolutely nobody in the party, Giant or no. Armor needs precision. Those are carefully made pieces that need to be neither too short nor too long, neither too wide nor too narrow, or they don't fit and you have gaps in your armor or it prevents you from moving. In the real world, people buying armor went and tried on every piece they could until they found a suit that fit or had to go to another place - nobody just grabbed something off the rack because there was no "generic armor size". So you're already ignoring realism for armor.

    But lets set realism aside entirely. The Giant merit is a merit. It's a good thing. Good sir Dawny Wagonass paid a ton of character creation resources to be staggeringly huge and frankly it is not providing a value-to-price ratio that deserves any sort of further costs or complications unless you're also houseruling significant secondary benefits. Especially not any complications that shut down access to other merits that the PC might be paying for.

            And, going back to narrative solutions, it's actually not that improbable that giant-sized artifact armor exists. The First Age will have had gigantism, as well, in addition to non-human or quasi-human heroes (Dragon Kings, beastfolk, shapeshifters, etc.).

            Now, obviously, it won't be as common as normal-sized armor, but you can easily gin up a justification using destiny, Sidereal interference, past life memories, Lore research and any number of other influences that will have gotten Just The Right Armor into your PC's grasp. Heck, make "Find the Lost Panoply of Solar Duncan the Excessively Large" a quest/adventure/personal goal. Or hunt down a set of moonsilver armor with shapeshifting properties that allows it to fit anyone who wears it.

            And, for all the complaints and reworkings it's received, I've recently re-read the crafting rules, and they're not actually as bad as I had come to believe. Definitely excessively complex and baroque, but if you want to build a character around them it can be managed. Or just find an NPC artificer to throw fistfuls of jade at, and hand-wave the mechanics.

                I used to headcanon that something special about Artifacts and Hearthstones caused them to adjust size slightly when Attuned. An artifact ring would usually fit at least one finger (or as a bracelet, at least, heh). An artifact sword wasn't just able to ne lifted, but also balanced to be used regardless of arm length and strength level. Armor would adjust itself to fit the user. Hearthstones, no matter how oddly shaped or uncut looking, would adjust themselves to fit into a socket. I would even extend that to armor donned using Resistance charms. While mostly there for game convenience, it did slightly set apart artifacts from an in-game lore perspective, valued due to not needing to be taken to a very advanced smith and adjusted for the user. Grab and go, unlike bronze or steel.
