                                        Originally posted by Neall
                                        It's also a Tolkien reference in a game deliberately devoid of them, which makes me think it's not a deliberate Tolkien reference: You can make something greater than yourself, but you forever diminish yourself in doing so. Sauron's creation of the Rings forever changes and diminishes him.
                                        To add to it probably not being a deliberate Tolkien reference, Tolkien didn't write that as an inherent property of forging Rings; Sauron needed to specifically add some oomph to the One to make it strong enough to enslave the others, and the only ingredient he had was himself.

                                        Originally posted by Neall
                                        With the Ring, and those it Chooses, comes great power, but without the Ring Sauron's but a shadow.
                                        Nah, that was just how the movies played it. In the books, even without the Ring, Sauron is politically and mystically the most powerful being in Middle Earth. For all Gandalf's "he needs this to cover the world in a second darkness", the council of Elrond went hard on emphasizing that the Ring was important because destroying it was their only means of stopping that from happening.

                                        One of Tolkien's letters had it that nobody besides Gandalf wielding the Ring would be capable of harnessing its power in a manner that would truly render Sauron subservient; in a military contest, he'd still have a good shot, and in a direct confrontation there would be no question.

                                        Tolkien certainly held that, if Gollum hadn't intervened and the Nazgul had caught up to Frodo, they wouldn't really be able to harm him anymore, and Sauron would have personally gone to get the Ring himself.
