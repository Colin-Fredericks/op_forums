                                The game is mathematically balanced around the fact that an optimized non charm DV maxes at 6 for dodge and 8 for Parry. Your change would raise both of those by 1, which would introduce more missed hits than the game is assumed to have. This will also make Decisive attacks harder to land.

                                It also increases the gap between average dice pools and maxed dice pools, which was a problem of the previous edition. It probably won't cause your game to light on fire or anything, but the math is pretty tight in the base rules this time around.

                                        Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post
                                        In 3E, Specialties only add one die, as opposed to up to 3, as in previous editions. Does anything break if one houserules Specialties back to the previous editions way?
                                        Aside from the effect it has on DB pools and the (relatively) modest effect it has on the overall curve, the main result is that this substantially widens the skill gap between people who are built to excel in an ability and those who aren't, which increases the amount of tiering and reduces the effective ability of someone with a modest investment. However, while it will steer the game in particular directions (and you should do it only if you want that effect), I don't think it will actually break anything so long as you limit DB adder caps to one die's worth of "specialty bonus". If it were me, I'd probably also require that people have specialties in at least three separate abilities, because the "4 free specialties" in chargen is probably a bit too generous in a system where Specialties stack.

                                            Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                            What does the "Grappling" tag do?

                                            Page 586 says it can be used for Grapple Gambits. But Gambits, as Decisive Attacks, don't use weapon stats. "Unarmed" has both the "Grappling" and "Natural" tags -- meaning you don't need a Draw/Ready action to launch a Grapple Gambit even if you're wielding a two-handed weapon without the Grappling tag.
                                            The main mechanical benefit is that it lets you grapple with Melee, I think, which is useful if you aren't kitted out as a dedicated grappler but happen to want to grapple somebody now and then. Personally, though, I'd definitely apply a dice penalty to the hit roll on a Grapple gambit if the PC didn't have any hands free and wasn't willing to drop what they were holding to use it (notwithstanding a really good stunt, tentacles, etc.).
