                                        I'll harp on the "2e and 3e's Ability prerequisites are too high" thing forever, but I also want to note that, like, it is absolutely not the case in 3e that if you build like the game tells you to, taking into account what the traits mean, then it won't work.

                                        Like, if you want a character whose concept is "the group's main combatant" and is not good at fighting then yes, you've set yourself up for a spot of trouble, but if there's another primary combatant then you can easily make it through the entire game contributing significantly to fights with nothing but Dex 3, Melee 2, a straight sword, and Dipping Swallow Defense and Excellent Strike. You could even do that as the group's primary combatant, it'd just make for much tenser combats. In 3e, at least, this works fine. If you take twos and threes in your combat stats and all the Charms those permit, then especially you will be in good shape, so long as your Attributes are complementary.

                                        But if you want to be the group's primary fighter, and you want the group to be able to fight enemies that would easily defeat elite mortal armies, then you do actually need to be that good at fighting. Part of the premise of the game is that your power doesn't just come from magic spells, so it's not like Vampire where you can play someone with zero combat background, take Protean and Celerity, and wreck shit left and right.

                                        In practice, the way I usually see this squared off is to assume that people get better rapidly once they exalt, and that PCs tend to be prodigies even among the exalted. You almost have to assume that anyway, given how many games involve you earning 200 XP over the course of an in-game month. So your starting ratings would reflect things that happened since your Exaltation as well, and you'll be able to train those things quickly during the game using Solar XP, and if you need to rationalize this you just throw in some training montages and say like "Wow, what a natural! I see now what the Unconquered Sun saw in you!" You don't have to do that, but if you want to, it's not really any sillier than "what luck that I found this giant magic sword in that tomb!"

                                        Edit: One last thought -- when evaluating how essential something like War Lion Stance is, do consider that, you know who else doesn't get War Lion Stance? Anyone whose combat Ability isn't Melee.

                                                The crux of the issue is the dissonance between Supposition 1 and Supposition 2, and its effect on character creation.

                                                We all know that if you want to be The Warrior of the Group, you are going to want The Charms.
                                                You know what I mean – I mean you want to have on your character sheet the abilities to deflect punishment and dish the hurt. You want the power to face a dozen brigands and be able to fight them. You want to be able to stand and fight against monsters, ancient constructs and a Sidereal assassin that stands between you and cancelling your membership at the gym you haven't gone to in 14 years (…not since The Incident).

                                                It's also important to remember that odds are one thing, but as any gambler or veteran roleplayer can bitterly attest: dice fall where they may – and they may just fall as a row of middle fingers.

                                                Now, yes, you are not wrong in your calculations and it is possible that I'm still carrying "trauma" from the previous edition which colors my expectations of lower dice pools, but it is important to stress this:
                                                Dice fall where they may – and they may just fuck you.

                                                This is obviously still true whether you're rolling a dozen dice or just a couple, but it's far, far truer the lower the dice pool is.

                                                The problem, however, is not that you can't fight decently with a low dice pool in most situations against casual opponents such as elite soldiers or First Age padlocks.
                                                The problem is that in order to be The Warrior, you do need to qualify for certain things.

                                                You see, elite soldiers are far from the worst thing you're going to face as a warrior exalt.
                                                You might get pitted against a Fair Folk Cataphract, for instance, and have to defend against an attack of a humble 16 dice and pass its Parry of 7 when you attack.

                                                If your dice pool is 4 (you wanted Dex 2, Melee 2, remember?) before enhancements, it's safe to say you will, at the very least, be blowing a full Excellency on every turn you make an attack – and you're probably going to do that to bolster your Defense every time you're attacked, as well.
                                                This means you will be spending a minimum of 8 motes every single round, before factoring in the occasional Excellent Strike.

                                                If you're wielding a mundane medium weapon, your Parry is exactly 3 before enhancements (and even with full Excellency and a good stunt, you're looking at a total of 5-6 versus 16 attack dice).

                                                I just rolled 16 dice for the hell of it and garnered 13 successes.
                                                I think it's safe to say our hero is in trouble.

                                                Yes, you won't always be facing the Fair Folk Cataphracts of the world… What do you say your odds of getting hunted down by the Wyld Hunt are? We're talking at least one to three Dragon-Blooded Immaculate monks, a legionnaire and an assassin, accompanied by a modest Size 3 battle group and you're telling me that…

                                                4-5 dice is fine
                                                No, no, it's not.

                                                I say again: you cannot just put a dot or two in Melee or Brawl and expect to be the warrior of anything.

                                                In other words, your ability to contend with the challenges the game throws at you will always come down to rolling a bunch of dice and adding effects to those dice from Charms, Stunts and whatever else.

                                                As we've already established, this brings us back to the dissonance between Supposition 1 and Supposition 2.
                                                If you want to get on the Fire and Stones Strike ride you need to be at least Melee 3 tall and if you want to survive the Fair Folk Cataphract and the shikari, you're gonna need a lot more than just that.

                                                Now, you can say that the daring young farm girl is a starting character and should be facing challenges appropriate to her level and get stronger and more experienced over time, raising her stats and acquiring Charms accordingly.
                                                However, what counts as a starting character in Exalted, especially when we're talking about Solar Exalted, isn't really equivalent to most other games such as D&D.

                                                The daring young farm girl is a Solar, rats and drunken brigands are not worthy opponents for her. She is Exalted, she is supposed to be able to contend with elite soldiers as if they were mostly a distraction.

                                                Once again, the problem is not "how can I justify taking a 5?", the problem is not "how can I make an effective character without min-maxing?", the problem is not "4-5 dice is fine against a single drunken brigand sometimes".

                                                The problem is that when you sit down to create a character and you try to stick to Supposition 1, you get slapped in the face by Supposition 2. So you want to account for Supposition 2, which means you have to break Supposition 1.

                                                You can, of course, pick a concept that suggests more accommodating stats – but that's not what we're talking about, and where does that leave our daring young farm girl?

                                                If putting 2 dots in Melee is stretching it, you are not going to be an efficient fighter.

                                                Supposition 1: I want to make a fun, interesting character and be loyal to her concept.
                                                Supposition 2: must be this tall to ride the fun train.

                                                            Well, the book says that you can be "simply a person of enormous potential who has never had the opportunity to exercise it, or who is possessed by greatness of spirit", but those sound like cases where the "why are my stats so good" explanation writes itself, doesn't it? If feeling like your character hasn't yet transcended her origins is important to you, the thing to do there is just to start with decent attributes but crap Abilities, take a bunch of charms to offset the crap abilities, and make growing into the fullness of your power part of your character's arc. I've done that even in 1e; in 3e it's virtually painless due to having Solar XP and not needing to spend XP to raise Essence.

                                                            Edit: Also, just to cover the math part a bit, so as not to make too many separate posts:

                                                            Your minimum spend of 8 motes per round will often be a maximum spend as well; assuming you defend twice it gets up to 12. If you always ES, call it 9 or 13. You get 5 motes per round, so you can last quite a while. The character who has five attack dice before Accuracy, Solar Excellencies, and a couple Charms will totally wreck most mortal opposition, as long as they take them seriously enough to spend. Solar Excellencies and Charms, even the cheap root Charms, are practically win buttons against most things in the setting. Only comparable classes of enemy, which are rare, even have enough power to make mote efficiency an issue. And do keep in mind when looking at the numbers that players basically always stunt, but STs typically do not, or at least have full control over how much they want to stunt for the purposes of calibrating battles.

                                                            Now, it's true, this character can only get her Parry up to 8. She can do that easily, but not every time someone attacks her, so if she's fighting people who can regularly hit Parry 8, she's probably going to want to do something other than just have a knock-down fight with them. Needless to say, those kind of adversaries are uncommon, so this doesn't really preclude getting into fights in general so much as being an application of the principle that you shouldn't fight vastly stronger enemies cavalierly.

                                                            But...I don't see the problem with that? Are you saying you want to make a character who's not a world-class fighter, but you want her to be able to do the things world-class fighters do? Because, like, it's definitely not the case that you need to do those things, and your GM will just TPK the party if you don't pass the arbitrary Kung Fu Master check. But it does mean you need to plan around your actual abilities. The "Wyld Hunt might kill me" thing isn't an issue that only comes up with weak fighters; the Realm can muster a Hunt capable of wrecking pretty much any Circle below Essence 5, and in practice they don't generally do that because it makes for a bad game, and we sort of assume there's a behind-the-scenes political reason for it that makes sense. The corebook shikari aren't just on-call waiting for your party to show up; whether a Hunt is mustered, and whether you're still there when it arrives, are things you have control over as PCs.
