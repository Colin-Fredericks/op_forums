                                Originally posted by Fata-Ku View Post
                                Also, I do recall the devs talking about how DBT is going to be treated as "that tool you relied on when you were young and inexperienced" more than a must-have for any and all Lunars (with it hopefully being very upgradeable for those that do want to keep using that tool).
                                From the quote itself, that feels like the intent to me:
                                Originally posted by John Mørke
                                If you don't want to be a DBT Lunar, I'd rather you think of DBT / spirit shape as the tool you relied on when your Lunar was still young, inexperienced, and less powerful, rather than ignoring it entirely. I want to help people build away from a DBT-based character through powers that work when you are in your human form and don't work when you are in DBT.
                                I think of DBT as an extension on the philosophy suggested by more typical shapeshifting. By which I mean, a Lunar has a plethora of shapes at his disposal, but no shape is better in all situations. A Lunar's likely going to pick something like a sparrow when he wants something that can fly discretely, and something bigger and more dangerous when he wants something that can fly and fight. Even in the realm of "creatures that can fight," there's variety in the options each has available.* In various situations, all have their individual upsides and downsides. A Lunar looks at the situation and picks a shape that's going to get plenty of the one and relatively few of the other. Picking the right shape for a situation feels like a sizeable piece of playing a Lunar, so there's value in designing things such that no particular shape feels overwhelmingly powerful. From there comes the idea of giving DBT the minus that a Lunar can't utilize Evocations (which are potent) when it's active, and the plus that it's designed such that a Lunar can still find it to be a legitimately good option to consider even if they've spent time and effort learning Evocations.

                                * By the sound of it, EX3's combat system and animal stats alone will be varied and context-sensitive enough to actually reflect this, so woo.

                                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                Wouldn't the rarity of the Sideral Evocation wielder be adequately addressed by the rarity of Star Metal? I don't see why Sid's should get the thin end of the Evocation wedge.
                                I think the issue here is that you're looking at things in a straight Evocation-to-Evocation comparison in a vacuum. That would only be valid if Lunars and Sids had equal capabilities outside Evocations, but Lunars just happened to be better with them. So two points I'd make for how Sids not doing so hot with Evocations can work out:

                                1) A general statement is all we've got. We don't have reason to think this is going to be like 2e's "These power levels are only for Solars, these are only for Celestials, these are only for Terrestrials, and ne'er shall a being from a lower tier see power from a higher," nor do we have reason to think there are, say, Solar Circle Evocations, with further Circles extending on down, with Siddies getting the shaft. Basically, we don't know this is a hard delineation. There could be some Artifacts where Solars and Lunars have equal access to its Evocations, Artifacts where Sidereals and Lunars have equal Evocation access (with Solars above them), some Artifacts where Sidereals have better Evocation access than Lunars, etc. In fact, since we've heard talk about how "Dragon-Blooded have a special dispensation which will be explained in future books" regarding Evocations, that suggests it may not be so clear-cut.

                                2) Characters get access to particular Evocations as an option in the context of all their other splat-specific options and capabilities. Let's take a hypothetical Starmetal daiklave. Let's say Solars can use all the weapon's Evocations, while a Sidereal can only use some or most of them. In a direct comparison like that, of course the Solar comes out on top. But that would be silly, because Sidereals have a ton of capabilities that are hard or impossible for the vast majority of other non-Sidereals to pull off. We know the devs are keeping an eye on synergies -- John said mid-tree Solar Charms aren't meant to look so impressive on their own, but are meant to really buff or expand options a Solar purchased earlier in the tree. So perhaps the Evocations in the Starmetal daiklave that a Sidereal can use are ones that become especially dangerous or impressive when viewed in the context of native Sidereal Charms, Sidereal Martial Arts, or Sidereal Astrology. On that last note, it's even conceivable there may be Evocations that spell out such synergies: maybe that daiklave's Evocations are handy enough for a Solar, but clearly have special effects written in for a user with access to Sidereal Astrology, elevating it to being straight-up amazing. These kinds of things can make it an open question whether a Solar or Sidereal is more powerful with a given Artifact. Or even where, in terms of raw combat potency, the Solar's definitely on top, but raw combat potency isn't really the benefit the Sidereal is trying to derive from it: in the end, it might feel like an apples-to-oranges comparison.
