    Originally posted by Irked View Post
    This sounds to me like an invalid-but-inductive argument, i.e., "Given that someone is good, it's overwhelmingly likely that they'll be a Solar, even if not necessarily guaranteed." Which is a workable approach, except that I have no idea where you're getting that as a premise. I haven't seen anyone argue that Creation has only a few hundred good people, and several people arguing the converse, so the induction's pretty weak - and I definitely haven't seen anyone arguing "Solar if and only if good."

    Again, not agreeing with hippokrene in general here, but I think you're stepping beyond solid ground on this line of critique.
    It stems mostly from this idea that Solars specifically need to be good people some how. Those people through the thread arguing that seem to cite Solars as good heroes, Solars changing the world, and in one case, a historically exemplary Solar being said to "Not really be a Solar, probably an Infernal instead" in persuit for an idea for Solar Exalted specifically being good.

    The issue I take is that why Solars. Why can this not be spread to other Exalts? No one has presented why only Solars are brought into this discussion. If I were to grant it, however, the setting breaks. Dragon-Blooded, Lunar, Abyssals and Infernals outright cannot be "good guys at start" in order ofr a lot of the stories we expect for the setting to work. So we then find ourselves back to Solars and good being a criteron for their Exaltation. This in turn leads to problems I keep hammering at (introduction of objective good, confusing of where unlikable characters stand as Solars, and so on). Among them is the induction as you summarize it.

    I admit it's deliberately hyperbolic. I bring it up in part because there seems to be this want of Solars to be good guys, but just as easily an argument can be made to make all other Exalts or supernaturals bad guys. And while this seems unneded, I've seen enough times hwere this is brought up (in this thread even, where Sinbad being bad means he's obviously an Infernal, not a Solar). I am only trying to show how this imbalance of presentation between Solar Exalted and other beings can lead to pretty awful assumptions that or justifications for awful baseline to make the black/white presentation starker and reasonably so. It leads to either the goodness of the Solars being pointless (as it's no different from the general population and so a waste of words that tries to elimiante subtext) or it can be brandied as a justification to villainize any Exalt you personally don't like (whcih I have seen done enough to not want to give any fodder to it).

                                                            Why does it matter if there's an objective good in the setting? It matters in the real world, because it what we should be doing. Exalted is just a game, a role-playing game. All that matters is what the PCs and NPCs believe is good.

                                                            Or, to phrase it an entirely different way:
                                                            Of course there is an objective good in the setting, because some things are objectively good and some are bad. And these things are in the setting. Serial killers are still evil, charitable doctors are still good. But nothing in the setting is enforcing that. There's no all-knowing God who knows exactly what's objectively good and evil and rewards or punishes appropriately.* So while PCs can and should argue about it, it's not DnD where Pelor is good and Vecna is bad. And such, I see no reason why the setting should say "Solars are good" even if it's only just most rather than all of them, when nothing else in the setting works like that, everything is nuanced. In fact I see no reason why the setting should say anyone else is good, or evil. You don't need to be told "guys, Mask of Winters is evil!" because you can judge for yourself. The point is you think about what's good and bad, rather than just reading "goblins are Chaotic Evil, but Elves are Chaotic Good".
                                                            So when your Solar is a good guy/girl, it's because he/she chose to be a good guy/girl (man, sometimes I wish English worked like Mandarin and lacked gender pronouns).

                                                            *And of course, this is what a lot of people believe is the case in the real world. But just because there is no universal believed standard for good, and even if there is no spiritual entity or karmic system based on it, that doesn't mean that objective good doesn't exist in the real world. I don't really understand why people are saying that because there's no objective good promoted or enforced by the setting, this makes it a horrific crapsack doomed world with no hope. Unless you believe in God/Karma, etc, surely that's the same in the real world? Nothing's enforcing, deciding, or even perfectly understanding objective good, but that doesn't mean it doesn't exist.
