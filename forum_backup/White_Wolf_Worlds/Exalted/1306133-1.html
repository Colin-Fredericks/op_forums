    I'd go similar with LeTipix. There are mortal sorcerers in the Realm, but they often were from backgrounds of privelege and luxury to do so, or were individuals with enough weight as sorcerers to be recruited. The Realm doesn't mass-produce them since it well, can't. The handful of known sorcerers in the Realm are notable because they're sorcerers. So it's not like there's this imperial service that has sorcerers specially trained and has procedures. It's a range of very unique, powerful, and valuable individuals the Realm has drawn from folks who might be allowed to do so and doesn't consdier a threat. There's no like, department they work in or standard use in the Imperial government. Instead, they're indivdiuals who probably got to particualr notable places of power in the Great Houses or Thousand Scales in part because being a sorcerer gives you a leg-up, but also puts you in a position that siding wiht the power is better than being against it.

    Unaccounted for sorcerers probably operate underground, doing weird personal projects and all that. A good example of this is Bomanz in the Black Company series. He was a sorcerer who as it turns out more or less did his stuff under the radar, had his big project he was working on and eventually it exploded and released the Lady and Ten Who Were Taken. I think he's a good illustration of what non-registered sorcerers in the Realm are like, as well as the sort of reason the Realm likes to keep a clamp down on unaccounted sorcerers in general.

    Remember that in general, with a few workings, sorcerers techncially don't count as "mortal" save in an academic sense. Sorcery does a lot ot open up individual power, but it also allow syou to break a lot of the norms of the Realm, a society very much built on enforcing those norms.

                            On Blaque and LeTipex's comments, I think maybe this certainly comes down to your taste about playing out and emphasizing the Realm as governed by oppressive norms through the Immaculate Philosophy vs the stance that the Immaculate Philosophy is actually shaped by the Sidereals for the Realm's hegemonic advantage and to support the society that they find safest and most amenable to control, and that they have latitude to manipulate "purity" status within its hierachy to serve their ends.

                            (Certainly, I think, either stance taken, definitely include consideration the latter - if we don't, we maybe get the things like the Sidereals writing themselves into the Immaculate Philosophy as anathema from 1e which didn't really make the most sense!)

                            That is - Is this the sort of thing that your vision of the Realm+Bronze alliance is going to co-opt and subordinate, without detracting from its oppressive character, or suppress totally as a threat? In my vision, the Realm+Bronze would tend to only suppress totally as a threat the declared Anathema and threats from outside Creation (fae, dead, demons), with a lot more latitude to coopt with accommodations much else (co-opting, without actively cultivating). Within this framework, I wouldn't tend to view scattered mortal sorcerers as much of a threat to the Realm+bronze and totally the sort of thing that they wouldn't worry too much about and would co-opt, and not in any case as saying too much about how oppressive for mortals the IP is (a few scattered mortals winning the lottery of the potential for sorcery attaining the heights doesn't say much at all about the general condition of Realm society!). This totally depends on your vision of the Realm, though, which has some space to vary.

                            I agree about Blaque's thing on there being likely no sorcery "department" as I'd tend to envision it, and that they'd be more likely to be consulting adjuncts with some institutional relationship to the Heptagram and the formal institutions of the Realm. But if you were considering that, caveat with considering that a lot of historical bureaucracies and ministries before the modern 19th-20th century era of pretty large governments still existed without tending to have many employees (the kind of bureaucracy that you had in medieval China / Raj India etc was often pretty fairly minimal in terms of actual headcount), so rarity of mortal sorcerers is not quite a total argument against it.

                                A lot of it to me is less actually the Bronze Faction and more the Empress. She liked having a lot of control of the reigns of power, and some dumbass doing a big sorcery project ot summon a big demon or rewrite the leylines in an area is something she would rather not have to spend resrouces dealing with the cosnequences of. My views on the Realm trying to co-opt sorcerers to serve it when possible is purely power-play, with the societal norms being part of that. I tend to see the Bronze Faction as while an important cabal, still a cabal trying to affect a giant nation with complex insittutions that look more like the British Empire at times than some of the clasical ones the Realm draws on (it's navy does as well). The Realm is pretty enormous and well-run, which indicates at least a fairly intricate bureacracy and has been described as such in the books for the game's lifetime.

                                Mind, this goes wiht my general view that states are greater than one person and the Realm having a super-strong central power base doesn't preclude having to have institutions devleoped to extend that beyond direct control. The norms of the Philosophy plus the complicated checks and systems fo the bureaucracy serve this, but indivdiual sorcercers can be disruptive, regardless of what they do. Mortals doing it probably are at risk of becoming Anathema or causing issues in general by getting too big for their britches. Folks who break the norms of a regulated society often hve to be made to fit in the society. Those that don't well, go underground. There's good story to be had by those ones, which is why I think it's soemthing I would emphasize wiht mortal sorcerers in the Realm who choose not to become part of the government in some fashion.

                                    To expand on my indoctrination comment.

                                    Indoctrination makes people loyal to the image you've cultivated, the person you want to be which reality often falls short of, especially when your will is experienced through institutions under your control. So a peasant-born mortal sorcerer might condemn an uprising as a perversion of the natural order or they might participate in it because they see injustice done in the name of the Scarlet Empress by corrupt officials.

                                    People often misuse indoctrination when they’re world building in order to hand-wave the possibility of a group or individual using their power in a way that’s not in line with the greater good. The idea that indoctrination produces effective results bug gosh darn it we love freedom too much is one of those insidious pro-authoritarian myths that’s perpetuated through fiction.
