    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
    I'm hesitating between 2 options :
    - Either the boat is a merit in itself (like in Arms of The Chosen)
    - Either possessing the boat is represented through merits like manse, influence, ressources, etc ... (for example the main boat is controlled through a manse by a NPC)
    I'd definitely go with the second option. Here's how I'd break down the various merits:
    • Influence is the most obvious - at the higher levels, it even specifies that the character with it could be the ruler of a city or larger state. I'd peg "ruler of a town, not the largest in the region but not tiny, either, at Influence 3.
    • Backing means the character has a formal position in an organization. If there's an actual town council that runs a ship-town's affairs, Backing could represent a position on it. Backing says that you're not in charge of the organization, but I'm generally willing to allow characters to buy a combination of Backing and Influence to represent actual leadership of a formal organization. I'd probably put "one of the councilors of a ship-town" as 3-dot Backing, and then charge only a single dot of Influence to be the mayor and head of the council as well.
    • Command could be appropriate if the character is a strongwoman, ruling through military force. Without any dots of Influence, though, she's going to be very unpopular, basically a dictator with no legitimacy in the eyes of the people.
    • Followers and Retainers covers the people on the town-ship being personally loyal to the character. Followers alone won't be otherwise politically significant, but buying enough Followers can overcome this - if she commands the personal loyalty of 500 people, and there's only 2000 people on the ship total, her words will carry a lot of weight, even if none of her followers are highly ranked. Retainers, on the other hand, could be actually politically-powerful people, and thus control the town-ship's actions more directly.
    • Other merits, like Manse, as you mentioned, could represent other forms of control. If not combined with Influence, though, such means will always lack legitimacy in the eyes of the populace, meaning people might be resentful or even openly rebellious.

                            I think Kelly is onto something with resources not being infinite - Projects are known to have complications if you don't go about them the optimal way. Now Speed the Wheel ensures you'll get what you want, but it does not in any way ensure that the task will be conducted in the best way possible. And that goes double when your project involves going so fast that you break all laws of physics beyond even the believable limits of an over-the-top setting like Exalted's.

                            Originally posted by Core p.226
                            Another good rule of thumb is that the more the ruler stands to gain from a project’s successful resolution, the more likely it is that a failure condition will be tense and interesting, and thus should happen.
                            If the player decides they raise a navy in mere minutes, then saying they stand to gain from the project is a serious understatement. Therefore by the quote above they just opened themselves to an epic failure condition. Maybe they harvested an entire forest in seconds... by making a deal with a powerful demon. Or maybe they recruited their sailors in seconds... by conscripting citizens at spearpoint, leading to political unrest and a navy that is not fully loyal to them.

                            So I guess what I'd do is I'd inform the player that while Speed the Wheel really does let them go that fast, that past a certain point there will unavoidably be severe consequences for going that fast. And then the player can decide just how much they're willing to speed things up.

                                                        Yeah, that's definitely what I see as the main limitation on things like Speed the Wheels. Failure conditions should be used liberally, because every one not only puts some brakes on the whole "build everything in an instant", but they're also useful adventure hooks.

                                                        Originally posted by Epitome
                                                        If the player decides they raise a navy in mere minutes, then saying they stand to gain from the project is a serious understatement. Therefore by the quote above they just opened themselves to an epic failure condition. Maybe they harvested an entire forest in seconds... by making a deal with a powerful demon. Or maybe they recruited their sailors in seconds... by conscripting citizens at spearpoint, leading to political unrest and a navy that is not fully loyal to them.
                                                        The way I read the project rules, the failure conditions are even more extreme than that - until you resolve a failure condition, your project just doesn't proceed. If supply problems are an issue, if you don't resolve that, you don't get a navy at all, not even one you made a bad bargain to get. Making that deal with a demon, in fact, would be the resolution of the failure condition, and should be played out.


                                                        To illustrate how I think Speed the Wheels should work, by being useful without necessarily overwhelming everything instantaneously, I figured I'd write up a campaign outline of how the "build a navy from a single town" arc might go. For these purposes, I'm assuming an all-Essence 5 party, a perfect Circle, with a War-focused Dawn, Presence-focused Zenith, Craft-focused Twilight, Investigation-focused Night, and a Bureaucracy-focused Eclipse.

                                                        The party has picked a small village (about 200 people) at the western end of the Cinder Isles as their starting point. For Reasons of Plot, they've got decent personal resources (artifacts, wealth, etc.) but don't have massive infrastructure or organizational resources - those will have to be built up. The Twilight has had a brilliant idea of how to build ships really quickly - create a ship assembly line, that effectively means that producing a ship, assuming all the raw materials and personnel are there, only takes a day, and isn't a significant Craft project. However, building each such shipyard is a Superior Craft project.

                                                        The party's first task, upon arriving at the village, is getting its population on board. This is easily accomplished by the Zenith, who uses her Presence to make an inspiring speech or two, promising fame, wealth, etc. to the villagers as the nascent heart of the new Solar Empire (and, not incidentally, protection from the various dangers of the Southwest). With the villagers on board, the Eclipse can start setting up. However, it's not really possible to start setting up the shipyards yet - the village lacks both the population needed to work on them, and the resources to build them. The Eclipse declares she is working on two concurrent projects, one to recruit population from around the region, and a second to set up some supply chains for the resources needed. Since she has Speed the Wheels and is Essence 5, both these projects (which would normally each take a season) could be accomplished in minutes. This is stunted as a matter of the Eclipse having known that this would be required, and having made arrangements ahead of time, off-screen. The party has multiple members with personal Resources 5, so the ST decides they have enough money to pay for this out of their personal funds, and allows the resources project to go ahead without a hitch. Minutes after the villagers have cheered the Zenith's speech welcoming them to the New Solar Empire, the first wagonloads of timber, stone, tools, and food begin pulling up at the gates.

                                                        However, the ST declares that there is a potential failure condition plaguing the second project, the one to acquire the extra workers - the nominal ruler of the region, who styles himself a king, doesn't want a bunch of his subjects moving about without permission. Convincing or removing him is necessary to allow the project to continue. The Zenith must spend a few days travelling to his palace and getting him on board with Project Solar Navy (the party has reasons not to just overthrow him and declare themselves co-queens, connected with a Sidereal astrological curse). Once the King is on board, however, the project of recruiting more workers can proceed. It takes the time required for the Zenith to travel to the King's palace and convince him, for the King to send out messages to his subjects that it's okay, and for the people to travel to the village, delaying things by perhaps two weeks.

                                                        Once the workforce arrives, the Twilight can begin building the first shipyard. This is a Superior project, but she has Thousand Forge Hands, so with the necessary resources (which includes a trained workforce, hence why she couldn't start until the workforce project was complete), she can make her first roll after only a week. She achieves the full number of successes the ST calls for on the first roll, so the first shipyard is now complete. Note that Speed the Wheels was not capable of speeding this up, because the work to be done was all in Craft, not Bureaucracy.

                                                        The first shipyard now complete, the party wants to get it started pumping out ships. The workforce required can be the people who built the shipyard, the ST agrees that the skills involved overlap reasonably. However, each ship will require more resources: wood, nails, etc. Of course, those are mostly the same resources that were required for the shipyard, and the ST declares that the easily-purchased ones in the area have already been purchased. The Eclipse makes plans to set up a couple of operations to log local forests and produce more nails, rope, etc. locally (in minutes, of course), but all that will take considerable time to come to fruition (the organization may be instantaneous, but lumber still takes weeks or months to season, ropes take time to braid, etc.). Since time is of the essence, the Eclipse in the meantime makes plans to buy from more distant suppliers. However, the ST imposes another failure condition on this - they don't have enough resources available to buy the resources they want. The Eclipse scans the village, and turns up a local art product that Insightful Buyer Technique tells her is sufficiently valuable in a market a few days sail away. She uses Speed the Wheels to have a trading concern established, and minutes later, the first boat (again, stunted as being sent out a few days ago) pulls up at the docks with the first load of silver in exchange for the art they've already sold. With the silver, the extra resources can be purchased, and ship-building can begin . However, since the resources to overcome this issue only just arrived, the resources will have to take time to arrive, again a few days or more.

                                                        This goes on, but each ship built in the shipyard requires more resources, the cost of which continue to increase, both from reduced supply, and the increased demand. More commercial ventures can be set up, but in fairly short order, the ST declares that the economic potential of the village is basically tapped out - that's a resource too, and Speed the Wheels can't create potential commercial opportunities from nothing, any more than it can create wood or nails or rope. As the resources get more and more costly, the party will have to start branching out and finding new ways to make money, whether that means hiring even more people to create other industries, offering to sell some of the output of their shipyards to others, or going out and conquering or otherwise controlling new areas. All of those, of course, take time to accomplish, and have potential consequences. People will become aware of their presence and actions, even if this is all accomplished super-humanly quickly. In fact, doing so will probably spread the Circle's new legend even faster, because it's an amazing story. They should expect visits from spirits and other Exalted in fairly short order, and that will take time itself.

                                                        Anyway, I hope I've illustrated how I think Speed the Wheels works to improve, even vastly improve, the speed of large-scale projects, without necessarily reducing all timeframes to a matter of minutes. The above sequence could easily take weeks or even months to fully create a navy, which is plenty of time for interesting, exciting things to happen to the Circle, while still being incredibly fast compared to how long it would take a typical mortal nation, starting with far more resources.
