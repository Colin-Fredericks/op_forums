                                    As I said earlier, there's a huge gap between "not really part of your central thing" and "not something you can do at all."

                                    eg. teleporting, flying, or dropping meteors on people are definitely not central to the Solar theme, but they're stuff you can reach from inside the Solar charmset if you approach them from the right angle and build up to them. (And conversely, while Solars have charms that can do all those things, you couldn't really build a Solar who does those as their primary shtick, not without building them very unintuitively or homebrewing a massive amount.)

                                    Some of the stuff people are requesting for Lunars is probably like that -- not a core thing, and not something you're likely to be able to build your entire Lunar around, but you could probably squeeze them in somewhere as a supplemental trick, especially if you can explain how you're building your way towards them out of their more central core themes.

I mean that's what concerns me the most about Third Edition's direction for Lunars - throwing hundreds-to-thousands of expendable dupes into pointless meat-grinder conflicts for purely-selfish reasons. At least Sidereals have the excuse of "*mumblemumble* Destiny, *mumblemumble* greater good of all Creation," and - this is the crazy part - the books never call them out on this. Only for the Usurpation itself, and for their failures to act, as in the case of the Great Contagion; at no point is it ever seriously suggested that the Bureau of Destiny itself is the problem.

                                                            I'll probably make a longer effortpost on this later, but a big part of the issue with W: tA as Lunar inspiration is that removing the Garou from the context of the World of Darkness also removes a lot of their justifications for existing. They don't fight to defend nature, because nature can defend itself just fine in Creation. They don't champion or defend the dispossessed, but instead sacrifice them in droves to buy a few more years for themselves. They don't represent any tribal societies or lineages because they're Celestial Exalted, set above and apart from the rest of their peoples, and the only marks of their heritage are beastfolk, born as living weapons for their forebears' sakes and basically as wronged by their progenitors as anyone they're sent to fight.
