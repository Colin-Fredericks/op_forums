    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

    I find it odd that any part of Luthe or its society still endured when the call for the edition was to cut back on that kind of thing, but the whale's swam.
    Okay, so a thing to understand about Sunken Luthe in Third Edition is that it is almost entirely flooded. There's almost nothing of big airtight chambers in which typical humans can survive (barring a few tiny holdouts, whose existence is almost akin to a disability). The majority of its inhabitants are aquatic beastfolk (of a large variety of animals), who live freely in the open water, that arrived in the aftermath. They possess their own distinct culture that grew up in the meantime under the circumstances.

    Luthe is not a surviving First Age city, it is a new community that grew in the remains of a destroyed First Age city. Much of the existing architecture has been grown into it from things like coral.

    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist
    I'm going to call particular attention to this because if he were actually acting like his icon beast he'd be even more social. Orcas don't usually survive alone, act alone, or get along alone; they, along with many other toothed whales, are extremely keen on organizing themselves into peer alliance systems, family units, and family units grouped into alliance systems. Having all Lunars be loners because they're "living like an animal" is a lot like the continued existence of Siakal as presented.
    Well he's not Lilith, he didn't transform his mind as well as his body.

    If "living like an animal" was an unsuitable shorthand for living in the wilderness largely detached from human mores and social structures then fair, but the latter point is still the thing he was doing (that a Lunar is empowered to do in ways distinct from other Exalted) that presents an obstacle to him getting back in the game.

    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist

I feel that's metal in a way that subsequent editions can't match by saying "Voila! First Age underwater city, still partially intact, with some of its old culture!", especially when you add on how he wasn't actually idle in 1e, but was solo-ing monstrous behemoths.

                    I won't say they're improvements, but I do think they fulfil that purpose in a way that validates the intention behind doing so.

                    That being said, I can understand that if a person felt a strong attachment to the characterisation of any given Lunar in prior Editions, it can be a disappointment to see them rewritten like that.

                    Likewise I can sympathise with anybody who might have read OG Leviathan as aroace representation and feel like it's a loss to shift him away from that*, particularly if there's no apparent character to fit in the slot left behind (even if there are aromantic and/or asexual characters in the Edition as a whole).

                                        Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                                        So, Leviathan is presented as having a solar mate in FATG and I'm calling shenanigans on the necessity of this
                                        I think the Solar Bond occupies a tense place in the state of Exalted lore when composing a Lunars book. It's apparent that writers agree that there's not only an enduring sense of relevance to it, but to even some of what it took on in Second Edition. From that position, one might not want it to be neglected, lest it seem an irrelevant background detail (especially since a lot of its heft is still being excised from that Second Edition version). But on the other hand, yeah, you do want to not make it feel as though it's the major defining force of motivation for Lunar Exalted.

                                        (That in combination with the fact that you get very few First Age Lunars to have actually experienced the Bond, or at least at the level of intensity that might have come from a relationship spanning centuries.)

                                        For Leviathan, I think it could be as simple as somebody just thinking that such melancholy was neat.

                                        And whether or not scuttling a fleet would have been a suitable alternative source of trauma (I think it would have been fine), that seems to be a casualty of apparently removing that detail from his backstory altogether in favour of giving him a history as a hunter that is more consistent with his portrayal in the Second Age. I think there's a valid basis for that on the grounds of a bit more conservation of detail (it would seem to go along with a point of how we're otherwise told little of First Age history that has little bearing on the playable setting), that it doesn't give him a skill set that is conspicuously absent from his familiar mode of activity, and because the overall presentation of the First Age makes the idea of a Lunar as the highest ranking admiral stand out less, both on grounds of "the Old Realm might not have even had a singular navy" and more general Exalted distribution of authority.

                                        On top of which, well... the Lunar Charm set that has been decided upon doesn't exactly empower them in the best way to command ships in battle. Personally, I think this is a preferable thing; there's a point at which you have to decide what capabilities don't really translate well into your Attribute based Charm set, and I think an Ability as specific as "Sail" is an obvious choice for that. Given the state of Lunar Charms in the past, it's not as if they're losing anything that they were particularly attached to in the first place.

                                        (Really, between the Charms and the surrounding statement of "Lunars can develop outside the lines defined by their Charms, but they have to rely on mundane skill to do it", I question what exactly the basis for Leviathan to have been a great admiral amongst other Exalted exactly was in First Edition)

                                        Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist
                                        And if you really wanted to drag the bond into it, it could be a demonstration of the Terrestrial Bond from Heirs to the Shogunate.
                                        Perhaps. The publication dates are a bit apart, but I see they have enough authors in common that people working on Lunars may well have been aware of such a thing being part of the other book. Possibly it would have been a tricky place to introduce and then explain it.

                                        Particularly given the writing in Heirs that seems to want to frame it as an exceptional plot hook for the future, rather than a reasonably established part of Lunar and Terrestrial lore. That kind of makes the only reason to back trace it into Leviathan to be looking for an alternative to the Solar Bond just to be an alternative to the Solar Bond, but I don't think they were proceeding from the assumption that their depiction of Leviathan would draw antipathy.

                                        On the other hand, sure, he doesn't need to be mystically bonded to somebody for their loss to have that impact on him. But I suppose that kind of rolls back around to "there will be plenty of Lunars who never even met a Solar that can have such backstories".
