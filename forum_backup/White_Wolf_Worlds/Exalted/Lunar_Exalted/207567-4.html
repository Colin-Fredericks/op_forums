                            Hmm, at least one advantage I can see in Breeding being a fixed-cost Merit is that it disassociates the idea of its mechanical scale matching precisely to how good it's supposed to be in-character. So having the Merit means you have some nebulous quality of "good breeding" which is not only fairly complicated from inside the setting, but also means different things for different characters.

                            I'm okay with the idea of Celestial Exalted having that Merit, and thus being able to have Dragon Blooded children, albeit only with any kind of reliability if they get involved in the usual Dynastic matchmaking, and not with assumptions that their greater lifespans or whatever necessarily make them superior at having large numbers of Exalted children, at least on any meaningful time scale (like, if you have a dozen or so Exalted children in one century, and repeat it several centuries later, by about a time when the prior batch have passed on, it doesn't make much difference). I don't think they should be able to game probabilities with anything like "sure my bloodline is weak, but if only one percent of my children Exalt and I have 10,000 children, I'll get a hundred Exalts". I think the heredity should be a bit more mystical.
