                                I'm not exactly thrilled with either the tone or the direction of this thread. But I feel like its an important topic, and I want to throw in my 2 cents. A range of thoughts and concerns have been brought up with various levels of legitimacy, and I think they should be addressed. In part, because several important factors have been brought up and have started to cross with each other namely the pure mechanical question of how balanced that Lunars being able to combo martial arts charms with their own native charms and whether that will break these charms, the second is the question of whether it is balanced relative to the solars (and consequently, what is the proper relationship between the splats in terms of power level), and lastly how the balance relates to the setting as a whole (which is frankly, a far removed discussion from the purpose of this thread, but still a discussion worth having).

                                So first, are Full-Moon combos (as I choose to call them) a serious problem balance wise? I'm not that worried about it. However, the reason that I am not particularly concerned is that I suspect that with the ever-expanding list of combos available to martial-artists the game will produce some 'unstoppable' combos or bizarre interactions as a matter of course. This is not an invitation to throw all such concerns to the wind, especially for highly idiosyncratic (and thus hard to combo) styles like Righteous Devil Style getting access to the accuracy and action economy of Lunar trees. As a matter of principle, I agree with Chung's perspective on exception-based rule-systems, but that should not be a paralyzing fear from designing a few more tools for everyone with each splat. We should measure the risks of breakage against the positive good it provides. It seems to me that without being allowed to make such combos martial arts would be a cripplingly poor option for most Lunars, and that would really suck for a great number of players.

                                Second, is this balanced against the Solars in such a way that it allows said Solars to still be the masters of martial arts? This I confess, I do not know. I don't doubt that overall the solars are overall still more powerful combatants, though whether they are specifically the best martial artists is difficult to say. It will probably definitively not once Solars have Sidereal Martial Arts theoretically available. I will say that as a matter of pragmatism, the desire to narrow the gaps between the Exalt tiers will result in some combinations of what other Exalts are capable of to 'spike' above Solars in particular areas, even if Solars remain on a higher tier in general. It's worth remembering that with their broader Evocations, SMA combos, and mastery effects it strikes me as unlikely that Solars remain worse martial artists for long, even if we stipulate that Lunars are better at the moment.

                                I will say I feel like a lot of people are forgetting the power of Supernal advantage combined with Mastery effects. People have correctly pointed out that until the advent of SMAs, Supernal Martial arts isn't super appealing and I tend to agree. A Martial Artist Solar taking the long view should probably be Resistance Supernal instead, since its highly tuned for combat already and combines well with MAs since it covers some of their main weaknesses. Plus Essence 1 gets you to the crucial form-charm for many builds as well as a nice little kit of combat powers, to begin with. I don't know that this makes them better 'martial artists' necessarily, but it assuages and concerns I have about the Solars as combatants more generally.

                                The last big point of contention is how powerful Solars should be compared to the other splats as a matter of setting. I will confess that this is where I am most opinionated, even if its the most removed from the topic at hand. However, it strikes me that a lot of the people concerned that the Solars, and particularly the Dawn caste, being considered unimportant because they can't casually puree the other splats have a somewhat stilted view of what makes the solars such a force to be reckoned with, even at their limited numbers. The key factor is that, even if they are only marginally the best Exalt at something, they are marginally better at all such things, and thus they can act as a force-multiplier for one another.

                                Consider if the solars were only Twilights and Zeniths, while they would be able to gather an enormous number of supporters or launch nukes at-will they would be assassinated almost immediately by their rival factions. However, in a circle the World's Greatest Sorceror (with nukes) is being backed up by the toughest bodyguard, his enemies being scouted and intercepted by an unrivaled spy, their Zenith rallies gods and men alike to their cause, and an unparalleled adventurer is helping them unearth powerful occult knowledge and spells. This is a Twilight-centric example of the same idea. You might imagine that a No-moon in some sort of Wizard-duel with a Twilight is a similarly close affair to how we talk about Full-moon v Dawn, with the Twilight having more raw power, but the Lunar having some additional charm-synergies and flexibility that they could potentially leverage for a win in the right situation.

                                The Dawn-centric version of the same thought experiment is likely similar. When attempting to white-room a Full-moon it might be a 60-40 split favoring the Solar. But when their respective allies pour their resources to advantage their side (assuming a 'white world' with similar availability and risks to both sides), you end up with the better warrior with better morale and tutelage (Zenith), the better equipment (Twilight), better info and sabotage on their opponents (Night), and better Eclipse! (More seriously, the eclipse could also adventure for lost treasure or provide support for the others in a bunch of ways). In the 'white world duel,' the Dawn will likely smash the Full-moon, maybe even 2 Full moons that the circle supported. This goes for any endeavor.

                                Note that this isn't like the cooperation of the Dragonblooded, where cooperation allows them to collectively create effects they aren't capable of individually. Solars each raise each other's abilities permanently in their core-competency the aid of a Twilight helps the Dawn solo a behemoth, and the materials the dawn provides access to do something similar to the Twilight. You might be able to get away with replacing a Dawn with a Full-Moon in a circle without too much disruption, but you can't replace all the Solars to get a similar effect.

                                        Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
                                        They don't need (and shouldn't get) a power boost relative to similarly top-tier Solar Brawl; but they can't fall too far behind.

                                        This is a problem if people want SMA-using Solars to be noticeably worse at SMA than Sidereals, who themselves are supposed to be slightly weaker than Brawl Solars - at that point, even accounting for the versatility of SMA, it becomes tricky to avoid a situation where the SMA using Solar is actually noticeably weaker in a fight than a Brawl one, which goes against the idea that SMA is this big valuable deal for Solar martial artists.

                                        There are various ways around this, but most of them require sacrificing some ground on the three points I mentioned above. Historically... Epimetheus is sort of right in the sense that SMA was always "Solars are flatly worse than Sideraels at it and also pay bazillion XP, so if SMA was remotely balanced no Solar would seriously consider learning it except for flavor reasons." Of course, the corollary to this is that there has always been unbalanced SMA (so the answer to "why would a Solar want to learn this" has historically been "well, these are actually notably stronger than any published Solar charms.")

                                        That latter part isn't going to carry over into 3e (obviously, since it was an error), but without it, if you want the story of a Solar finding an SMA tutor to make sense, you need to grapple with these questions more seriously.
                                        It is a tricky issue - I have to admit, my personal preference is that I just really don't care about the image of Sidereals being martial arts sifus to the Solars who make them stronger at MA than they would otherwise be.

                                        I'd prefer just to go the route of having Solars with Celestial MA just be on a level with Brawl anyway, which frankly IMO they should be anyway, without any of the kind of complexities SMA being important to the development of Solar Exalted martial arts specialists introduces. I'm not really a fan of the idea that Solars who use MA should be weaker without a Sidereal sifu than Solars who Brawl so that they can have a story of finding a Sidereal to be their sifu and become as powerful as a Solar who uses Brawl. (And if Solars with Celestial styles are already as strong as Brawl Solars, then there's no room for that "Finding a sifu to learn SMA and this is a valuable big deal" story to even make sense because you literally can't offer them any more power without putting them above Brawl Solars, which would be a really bad move. So it becomes a kind of thing of being able to pursue flavourful out of character magic which doesn't leverage a lot of strength.).

                                        When I look back at the 1e Sidereals version of SMA, the idea that Solars use them does not feel to me like it was ever really been a core part of what SMA was originally written to be, rather than more of an allowed thing, but without mechanical thought about making it work, and even then as an afterthought in the Player's Guide*. Sidereals as sifus to Solars, rather than more general mysterious advisers, hasn't really ever felt, an important part of their relationship. (The most iconic expression of that Sidereal-Solar sifu-student relationship is probably a few words written by Mike Goodwin on our message boards ages ago, which sold it really well... but it never had any serious mechanical support for how it would work within a solid rules environment.).

                                        So that's my bias. If they can find a way to make it work, then great for the folks who want to see it work, I'm just not too attached to it.

                                        *Specific section for historical reference stuff!:

                                        p239: "Solar Initiation Into the Blossoming Lotus: By dint of practice and meditation, Solar and Abyssal Exalted can learn Charms of the Blossoming of the Perfected Lotus, the Sidereal initiation, the ultimate circle of martial arts. However, they do not do so easily. As normal, they cannot learn without a tutor. Even with a tutor, their strong connection with forces in Creation slows their learning, and the study time is quadrupled. In addition, the pay 15 experience points per Charm or 13 if they favor the MA Ability. As form, these Exalted cannot learn the Form Charms or above of any Sidereal MA tree until they have mastered a Celestial style entirely.

                                        If this seems extreme, consider that it is easy compared to the difficulties the Dragon-Blooded face learning the Celestial-level martial arts. Celestials need not learn special Charms or undergo years of grueling tutelage. The greatest hurdle is the fact that they must find Sidereals willing to tutor them. There are practically none of these, for the Sidereals guard the secrets of their techniques jealously. Any Sidereal who taught techniques to a Celestial would be under the gravest legal risk and generally seen as a trait to the Five-Score Fellowship's innermost secrets. Non-Sidereal individuals who have learned Sidereal martial arts will be marked for death by the Sidereals as a group. The Gold Faction has not yet taught its Solar proteges in the Cult of the Illuminated's training camps Charms of this type, but they are always weighing the option                                        ".

                                                    Sure, but what many people are effectively advocating is that a Solar who learns SMA should be, overall, less powerful than a Solar who just learns non-SMA martial arts. Pre-errata 2e went this route - obviously SMA in 2e was broken and mechanically dysfunctional, but 13 / 15 XP a charm wasn't a serious offer, it was "haha, fuck you for wanting this." The second part of the relevant sidebar you quoted even half-heartedly acknowledges this (though it's flat-out wrong, of course, when comparing it to Terrestrials learning CMA, which didn't require nearly as exclusive a tutor, didn't charge remotely such absurd XP, and required some level of barriers for balance reasons because it was supposed to be more powerful than DB native charms rather than less.) Like most of the screwups in 2e, it was probably written by someone who only vaguely understood the underlying mechanics; and it was later errataed to sane XP cost.

                                                    Anyway, my point is: If you're going to allow Solars to learn SMA from a Sidereal tutor, it should at least be a sane, reasonable option for it. Spending XP through the nose for "versatility" doesn't make any sense at all (especially in a world with both sorcery and evocations, not to mention Sorcerous Workings and regular martial arts to diversify yourself with using the same XP.) Obviously SMA can't actually make a Solar stronger than a, well, Solar, that's a given, but if it's going to be an option then it can't be an actively bad place to spend your XP.

                                                    Regarding your other quote, I don't see how you could interpret that to mean Solars learning SMA wasn't a big deal. It implies that Sidereals were willing to murder over it. (Granted, Sidereals have sometimes been portrayed as perhaps a bit too murder-happy.)

                                                    I may be in the minority, but I think that a Solar who learns and masters an SMA should be distinctly more powerful than the solar who uses brawl, mostly because the level of investment is that much higher.
                                                    This is also an option (sort of), provided the "investment" balances out overall. But in practice it's pretty unworkable - first, SMA has to primarily be designed for use by Sidereals, limiting what you can do to fine-tune its balance for Solars to the extent that this would require. Second, what the "investment" means varies a lot from game to game depending on how long the game goes on and so forth, which could lead to weird issues. Third, you have to ask if the investment part is fun.

                                                    The reality is that balance isn't some precise science or anything like that - as long as the wheels aren't falling off the game, it's the feel that matters as much as anything else. You can make SMA feel powerful for both Sidereals and Solars without really having it break the game or be a strictly-mechanically-superior option to Solar Brawl. My objection is that the mechanical design does send a message in terms of how the game "feels" and its overall thematics - if the message is "Solars shouldn't be learning SMA", then you've lost the thread of why Sidereals were able to teach it to them in the first place.

                                                    (And it's also bad design because it amounts to ugly back-and-forth between different writers and developers - ie. one person said Solars can learn SMA, and another plainly didn't like that, that so they made it possible but with costs or limitations that would make it a punishing experience in actual play. This was almost certainly the story of the 13/15 XP cost, say. Aaand I'm sure it's how some people in this thread feel. But the mechanics should try and make the stories the game offers play out smoothly and in a fun fashion - if the game says "Solars can learn SMA, and it's a big deal for them", then it has to at least feel like a triumph for a Solar to learn it. If it's as heavy-handed as costing 13 XP a pop for charms that are weaker than your native ones, players are going to notice and not have much fun following that route.)

                                                    As long as SMA used by Solars is roughly within eyeball distance of going with native Solar charms, though, the exact differences won't matter, especially if eg. SMA has a lot of pop-off-the-page powerful feeling stuff that's actually more brittle than it looks at first.
