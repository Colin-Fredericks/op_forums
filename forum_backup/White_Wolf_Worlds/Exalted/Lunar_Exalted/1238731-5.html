    Originally posted by TGUEIROS View Post
    You were the one who made dice pools comparisons, but then when they don't serve your purpose they are irrelevant?
    I made a comparison for a specific reason. That doesn't actually mean all numerical comparisons are relevant.

    2 dice is a great advantage, and could easily determine success or failure.
    It's a significant advantage in the system, sure, but if my character's life was on the line, I wouldn't trust two dice to carry the day by itself.

    That point that system is full of abstractions is what invalidates this notion that a hit must connect due to dexterous accuracy.
    Except it doesn't. Abstractions only mean that the way Exalted does isn't the only way to abstract it, not that Exalted is broken/wrong for abstracting it the way it does. Whether or not Exalted should abstract things differently requires a stronger case than, "you can do it differently."

    We are simply making the argument that a hit could also connect due to raw power.
    Well not really. The case for Strength as the default close combat attack Attribute (that goes back to D&D and all) isn't that raw power is accurate, but that in abstracting what "strength" is, things like muscle speed - and hence accuracy in hand-to-hand combat - are covered by the abstract concept of "strength."

    It's, as I've said, where you put something in the abstraction. Strength based close combat defaults aren't about "accurate" raw power. It's about putting accuracy and raw power in the same abstract trait, just like foot-speed and hand-eye coordination are both in the same abstract trait; not inherently saying the two are the same thing.

    It is not as ludicrous as what people try to paint it as.
    Having Strength be the default close combat Attribute isn't ludicrous.

    Insisting that your accuracy derives from raw power is actually ludicrous, even if it isn't ludicrous to abstract raw power and accuracy from the same trait.

    Insisting that Exalted is broken for not allowing Strength to be a close combat accuracy trait (which is the whole point of the thread) for free instead of gated behind Charms is ludicrous.

    Originally posted by Fenrir666 View Post
    Asking for an alternative to be baked into the rules means you don't have to argue every game with stubborn RAW STs, or people who insist that it's not broken, don't fix it, you can't attack with strength so you'd better have Dex 5.
    Or be a Grapple Solar, or a [whatever] Lunar, since you can make more Strength based attacks with your primary attacks as either Exalt.

    The thing is, it isn't broken. And you don't need Dex 5 to have fun with the game a a combat character.

    Also, for reals? I was in a game with a character with Dex 3, Strength 5, Stamina 5 and a heavy weapon. They were a joke compared to the PCs with Dex 5 and medium weapons.
    As these forums have discussed, a lot, heavy vs medium weapons is a significant contributor here that can't be discounted. A normal medium weapons +2 accuracy and +2 Defense advantage over a normal heavy weapons +2 raw damage advantage makes heavy weapons a joke outside of very specific circumstances (esp. since most are two-handed).

    The weapon blocks are totally something you'll see plenty of support for something that should be fixed; and something that doesn't even screw with anything other than NPC stat blocks when it comes to the rest of the system. Because they're actually broken in that weapon choice is not up to style while heavy weapons are objectively horrible choices unless you're fighting low DV high soak enemies that aren't too low DV that the higher damage doesn't matter (esp. with the right tags) and/or too high soak for it to not matter anyway.

    And, bluntly, there had better be at least some monsters written to challenge people who optimize their characters for greater effect.
    Eh.

    Better to continue focusing on non-optimized antagonists. Lots of people don't play Exalted for optimization, so TPK NPC stat blocks are useless to them, and it's far easier to buff NPC stat blocks if your players are steamrolling them and not enjoying being so badass, than it is to nerf them if they're too strong.
