    Narrative Logistics for a Lunar Impersonating a DB?

    02-19-2016, 12:41 PM
    So I'm looking to have a Lunar NPC show up in my campaign soon, who's masquerading as a known DB. By which I mean she has stolen a DB's identity and has been living it long-term. What are the sorts of narrative hurdles she might have to face, and possible solutions for them? I'm not looking for mechanics exactly, as I am willing to hand-wave them for the sake of story. Any help with the idea would be greatly appreciated.

    So what I have so far is that she probably has artifact weapons with elemental flavor. Probably the volcano cutter.

    The sorcerous tell for Stormwind Rider would be helpful, if there was a flame version for it. Does Magma Kraken have a flamey tell? I'll look that up later.

    Continuing down the sorcerous route, I'm thinking she'll have Workings tied into a couple items that help her emulate anima flux. She'll still have to control her emotions, since outbursts should lead to more fluxing, but having things randomly burst into flames should be doable.

    How might she emulate some iconic DB charms? More artifacts? What would be the ones that she absolutely must be able to approximate to pull this off?

                                              Things are gonna be tough. I think you'll need to restrict the character cleverly to situations where Charms are internal or you plausibly wouldn't want to use them. Sorcerous workings and borrowed magic from spirits seem like the least blatant way to fake Dragonblooded elemental power, but I think people will know if they look close (audience dependent: faking a DB to Solars - probably not as hard; to DBs - well, IMO not much easier than faking being a Lunar as a Dragonblooded or Sidereal to another Lunar). If the character can have allies who've helped her prepare all this in the background (and are maybe now out of the picture) that will make it easier without the character herself needing that much sorcerous capability.

                                              Edit: In general, I think Lunars have it easier pretending to be some Exigent or strange god or behemoth or god blooded. Faking being a standard Exalt is hard, because they're relatively standardised as sub splats (so it's weird if they can't do some particular capability universal to the Exalt) and have a lot of separate ways to express their underlying power.

                                                  Get a Hearthstone that gives you effects to mimic the anima. That's the hardest part. If you're in 3E, get the ST to let you use one of the hearthstones that gives you an ice-aura or a fire aura or lets you breathe water.

                                                  Social stuff is easy if you're a social kind of Lunar. Learn some Immaculate martial arts just to be on the safe side-DBs won't call you out for not using DB charms in a pinch if you're using those, because DBs use them in a pinch!

                                                  If you're claiming to be a DB you ate, and you look like her and you sound like her and you act like her, nobody is going to ASSUME she's been eaten by a face-stealing demon-monster since last time she was over for tea. You don't think that about your friends! If you're acting a little off, the default assumption isn't going to be "IT MUST BE AN ANATHEMA," it's going to be "I wonder if she's feeling stressed," or "is she ill," or "did I do something to offend her?"

                                                  We don't assume people we know have been replaced by horrible monsters wearing their skins all the time in real life, after all!

                                                          People have covered anima banners and charms pretty well, so I'm going to focus on what makes one a dynast, rather than a Dragon Blooded, which, IMO, is the immensely harder and more interesting con to pull off. These are all potential complications - how well the lunar handled them, how much they came up, etc, are obviously up to you.

                                                          Retainers. Dynasts are never alone. You know all those personally loyal close friends of your victims? Are you good enough to fool them? Are you sure? It might be best to arrange their accidental deaths at the same time you take over your new role - excellent excuse to be in mourning for a while, and changed afterwards.

                                                          Family.Did you remember to get your nephew an exaltation gift? Shame you killed off your retainer (who was getting too suspicious) before he remembered to remind you. And your Grand Aunt's 150th coming up - do you recall that she doesn't like red jade, reminds her too much of that incident fifteen years ago, and also that you can't get her another dress, because you did that last year? No one will tell you these things - seriously, they won't. You're supposed to already know.

                                                          Responsibility. Every dynast has something they were doing before you replaced them. Sure you're going into seclusion, but before you go, we could reaaaaly use a rundown on how to keep things going while you're gone. Like, you ordered three months of beer for the troops but nothing else - I assume you had a deal lined up, a trade with someone to cover that, really, I'm sure it's brilliant, but you need to tell me before you leave or we'll have soldiers with beer and nothing to eat, and that sounds perfectly stable.
