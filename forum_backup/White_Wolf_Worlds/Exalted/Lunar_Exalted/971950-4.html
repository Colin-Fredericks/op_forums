        @ Piff, mostly close to things I would agree with, and a good post to make, though as a grace note, I guess would personally articulate this a little differently, as I feel like this is a bit more hype that could overestimate the "barbarians"* or that people could dismiss though.

        I would point out the barbarian peoples who made big splashes tended not to be 10-1 in actual soldiers (though I don't think you're implying they were), because of quite high mobilisation of men, and tended to have more in the cavalry in particular, and at the same time people from the steppe basically tended to have an interest in conquering China that wasn't as much in the reverse direction (because people on the steppe basically had nothing by the standards of wealth). Then they seized upon the opportunity to invade mainly China (but also the Middle East and South Asia), when internal factionalisation provided it and when the people in the settled world were also weak at funding and supporting the military and at using their technology and resources to its maximum.

        (In our history the advantages of the steppe cultures being able to mobilise high %s and the technological advantages of lots of cavalry tended to drop pretty fast by the time you had guys like the (mostly sedentary and "civilized") Manchu and Russians and the Gunpowder Empires on the scene, and then certainly with later Western European colonialisation. But there was a time between the fall of Rome and the rise of Gunpowder Empires and early modern empires who could leverage their economies' superior wealth and infrastructure, when the technology was right for cultures from the steppe to be in zenith as conquerors and imperialists, even with no real settled base of wealth and power).

        I point this because, though we would say it differently, you can have the Lunars being leaders of barbarian forces who have really great mobilisation of their cultures and they have pretty good gear and cooperate well and the Realm doesn't really want to take a fight to them because they're losing money every time they do, and they're great at picking when and where the Realm is internally divided. Then with all these factors so they've got a good chance. And I think a lot of the older stuff did say this, but just said it in kind of a lame way that was easy to dismiss as hyping up "dumb" strategies (basically undercut the ideas the Lunars could be effective with a kind of "They have no strategy and no gear and specifically field fewer warriors in battles and also have a huge cultural arrogance" vibe). (Though I don't know if Creation really has the right geography for anything like the importance for the specific steppe barbarians from our history.)

        * I haven't scare-quoted "barbarians" for the rest of the post because it's pretty boring to do and read, but assume I have if you'd like.

                                    I have only played 3E exalted and do not carry any knowledge of the previous editions or of W:tA either although I know what it is. Just from the 3E book I get the impression the Lunars honestly have several big clues into who/what they are/do. I think they currently are the main reason the Wyld is not lapping at the shores of the blessed isle right now. I just get the impression they are battling the fae back making kingdoms in the Middlemarches to keep them from going full Wyld. In this respect I think their shape changing powers are most likely the best defense against being horribly warped or consumed by the Wyld.

                                    In addition to this it seems like they have a complex relationship with the Solars. Like they used to be favored consorts/lovers/confidants as well as in some cases treated as slaves and fodder like Dragon Blooded. I am just guessing but I think the disconnect between Lunars and Solars most likely starting occurring due to depredations Solars did during limit breaks in which they were lashed out at or their hearts were broken watching their loved one or admired one literally turn into a monster.

                                    The information in the 3E core book seems to imply the Lunars were the most trusted confidants of the Solars and often acted on behalf or for them. Although I do not personally know anything about Gaia/Luna just from reading the forums I have picked up some understanding of them. I think Lunars stepped into the the role of Guarding creation as well as a strong desire to destroy those who slew the Solars and ruined the world with their actions and selfishness. Honestly it seems like the Lunars got the Anathema tag due to loyalty or love not due to them being monstrous destroyers or kings of excess. The book seems to imply the Lunars as a whole fared a lot better then the Solars did in surviving purges overall. Which means there are probably still grieving lovers, bitter idealists, vengeance seeking friends out there that remember and possibly personally know or even commanded the leaders of the houses. Knowing the faces and personalities of the traitors who destroyed so much of the world has to be a potent reason for revenge and hate.

                                    So to stop meandering I think Lunars will be the balances and defenders. The explorers and sages. I think their role in creation was and will be the ones who explored the edges of the world, keep and kept it safe, and tempered the Solars in their excesses to keep them from becoming the monsters they could devolve into when they limit break. I am willing to bet limit breaks would have been much much more common without Lunars. I think the role of defender became much more prominent after the Solars fell.

                                    Anyway as an ignorant of previous settings gamer that was my impression of my read through of the 3E core book in relation to Lunars.

For me this gives them a very strong and not always antagonistic relationship with it.

                                        Even a Blessed Isle/Inner Threshold centric Lunar could be able to Wyld Quest for their development arc and it gives them something unique and in keeping thematically. for their Splat. The Full moon who battles in the Cataphractoi in the Wyld for a new weapon or to achieve a permanent physical mutation by surviving and addictive and toxic landscape that seeks his corruption, the No moon who spirit quests to induct themselves into a sorcerous path or who crafts a minor fay into a familiar or bind it in a moon-like orb that floats around the Lunar, A changing moon who is the only Creation bound creature capable of playing in the intricate and illogical webs of the great Raksha Courts, who is bestowed a powerful love token from his otherworldly paramour or tricks an Unshaped who takes the form of a giant Goblin market and vexes it for a boon it can take back to creation.

                                        I don' think this breaks their new interpretation as the bestial, barbaric other on the fringes of society rageing against the Scarlet Empire. I think it compliments this feel. The questing warrior, the spirit shaman and the primal trickster are all very much tropes of the barbarian, primitive, animalistic culture they are trying to evoke.
