    Invisibility isn't that impressive - it's what, -2 external penalty to attack or defend against? Really, the biggest problem is that it makes it very easy to reestablish surprise, and if a fighter doesn't have a surprise negator he's screwed anyway. I'd be okay with it as an Abomination. True, there are Charms that grant that power, blah-blah, but really, just because there are Charms that let you do it doesn't mean it can't be done without an equivalent Charm. See, for instance, the ability to fly.

    I hope to see more of the Companion aspect of Lunars. I feel that Lunars should be at their strongest when aiding those they care for - my Head-Canon has the Breaking of the Castes as a consequence of losing their Solar Mates, because the whole bond was made for the purpose of anchoring their identity, like how Luna's is anchored in her Intimacy to Gaia. So, more Argent Muse, more Ferocious Guardian Beast, and more Bond powers.

    Dragon-Blooded are the best Soldiers because they can put aside their differences and work together; Sidereals are the best Advisors because they don't need you to listen, Solars are the best Leaders because they can overrule any objections - and Lunars should be the best Companions, because they are always what their loved ones need.

    In a fight, a Lunar should boost his Mate in every way; make her faster and stronger, defend her so that she can focus on the offence, weaken the enemy with underhanded tactics, distract them at critical moments, and when she needs rest take up the slack and carry on. Outside of a fight, the Lunar should be the best assistant his Mate could ever ask for; the Squire who always has what his Knight needs, the fearless and tireless Steed she rides, the Hawk who finds her enemies, the Wolf who runs them down, the Spy who knows what they think, Seducer who turns them against themselves, the Advisor who tells her what she needs to hear, the Scholar who knows what she needs to know, the Courtier who leads her supporters and the Loyal Opposition who pushes her to do her best...

    A Lunar should be a Solar's Best Friend, and even a single Bonded pair should give an experienced Wyld Hunt nightmares.

    Aretii: "As one of the players described it, 'After initiation, it was actually a relief seeing who had eyepatches or bandaged hands - you know what they sacrificed, instead of wondering what bits people cut out of their own souls to gain power.'"

                                Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                I have some reservations about that concept.
                                Mm, I dunno. I don't feel using hypothetical Solar archetypes to explore a counter-example is appropriate. With the structure of the game, Charms fall under various buckets depending on the splat. Solars have 25 Abilities, but Lunars have only 9 Attributes. Solars can thus get a lot more granular in the types of concepts they can create between Caste and Favored in that structure, where some Lunar concepts are going to fall through the cracks there. Particularly as Solars really have one end toward which they're working, representing the skills of the human character and the archetype in Favored/Caste Abilities: the two mostly go hand in glove, and cases where they don't feel like big exceptions. Lunars, by contrast, need to represent both the human attributes of the character and the particular capabilities/associations of the totem. I feel involving some form of relocating and discounting particular Charms is meant to help with this. Cases will arise in which Lunars with totems that don't lend themselves to particular Attributes (i.e. it doesn't suit the character concept for the character to have that in Caste or Favored) will still find particular stuff within those Attributes that fit the totem like a glove.

                                Here's an example of where I see it helping. Consider the Scorpion & Toad stuff, since I consider it among the least controversial Charm effects Lunars have; I don't think I've ever heard a complaint or argument about them in concept. Nor about their placement in Stamina. However, let's think about toads and scorpions themselves. They're not very tough or durable. There's really not much of anything about them that screams, "Stamina!" So Lunars who take such small poisonous creatures would probably be interested in Scorpion & Toad stuff, but their precious Caste/Favored Attribute choices may have more important places to go for the concept than Stamina -- say we're talking swift and nimble Lunar assassins, where durability really isn't the aim at all.

                                Despite it being so appropriate and important to their concept to pick up Scorpion & Toad stuff (possibly a big reason they'd picked that totem at all) and not to pick up Stamina, they'd have to make the unpleasant choice to buy those Charms -- and buy up to them -- for out-of-Caste/-Favored cost or make Stamina one of their Favored/Caste Attributes after all and pay out-of-Caste/-Favored cost on whatever they'd wanted from the abandoned Attribute. With the totem playing a role, though, that player has the opportunity to find a more convenient place for the Scorpion & Toad stuff in their own trees and -- even if they can't -- they still get to buy it at a discounted rate. This can take some or all of the sting out of running into such a situation.

                                To respond to a point I don't think you quite made, but which I see arising, I would also disagree with the idea that such cases coming up at all is a result of a poorly executed/designed or otherwise undesirable Charm set. It seems a natural consequence of having such a relatively small number of broad Attribute buckets into which Charms can fall (relative to Abilities) and trying to capture an array of totem possibilities that spans the entirety of the animal kingdom, possibly including folkloric and symbolic associations. Certain effects appear in the Attributes they do based on a certain logic, but the possible totems are so varied that not all of their qualities will adhere to that logic, creating little hiccups which this totem solution is meant to address.

                                That said, there is the possibility that this totem thing may not benefit every concept. If the only totemic Charms one can get are ensconced in one's Favored/Caste Attributes anyway, it may help with rejiggering where those Charms fall in the cascade, if that's even an option, but clearly isn't as big a benefit. This was the incentive to sequence-break with totems you mentioned. It could be a possible concern, but I also have three possible counter-arguments, and I suspect it's one or both of the latter two:
                                1. Depending on one's design goals, there may not actually be a problem. The dev attitude in retaining BP and XP was (very broadly) explained as wanting to help people play the concepts they want, with less prioritization for on-paper equivalency. Following this logic, if the end goal is just "Lunar characters should get easy access to powers that suit their totems," then characters who can't get Charms that are normally outside their Favored/Caste choices don't need them, so it doesn't matter for the purposes of that goal. Makes me feel kind of uncomfortable, since I'm not totally sold on that, but eh. Fortunately, I also don't think this is it. Or at least not all of it.
                                2. It was said to be a system of keywords and associations. Perhaps the plan is to spread those keywords widely enough (and in a sensible fashion) that it isn't actually possible for your Caste-and-Favored choices to fall on all of them. In this case, you will definitely have Charms that can benefit from it. Some of these Charms may not be ones you want for that concept, but I don't see how that's different from not wanting to use everything in a particular Ability/Attribute for your concept. If the actual Charms in question are bad, that's a different issue.
                                3. We probably don't have a complete picture, here. Aside from further development thoughts in the interim, what we got from John a ways back might have just been a taste. Maybe totems do other things, too, that make it kind of irrelevant -- or at least much less important -- that some may get more Charms out of this than others. Or, as I've recently been inclined to think, perhaps the keywords he mentioned serve important purposes beyond just giving certain Lunars easier access to those Charms.* Sunder, you spelled this out at the end of your post so I'm under no delusion that this is something you haven't considered. Just stating it here for completeness.

                                * My present pet theory runs to the tune of, "The keywords he's talking about are the legacy of the Protean keyword: wearing physically/thematically relevant shapes when using those keyworded Charms gives an additional benefit." I wouldn't consider this a complete explanation,* but I do see it playing a heavy role.
                                ** For one thing, characters usually have few reasons to dip into animal shapes for traditionally social pursuits and even fewer for traditionally mental ones. I mean, this is unless some of those Charms play up mystical associations more than has been previewed or the keyword-linked benefits really make up for it. I kinda hope they will (if my idea is even in the ballpark), but we can't yet say.

                                                    Originally posted by Nocte ex Mortis View Post
                                                    The Primordials invented Sorcery. Seriously, since the core rulebook of 1E
                                                    That's not even remotely true. The 1e core (and most of 1e's books on Sorcery) just said that Sorcery's origins were mysterious; the story of Brigid (which was explicitly denoted as mythological and not-necessarily-true) very ambiguously implied that a demon was involved in her becoming the First Sorcerer (for what that may or may not be worth and for whatever accuracy-value of 'first' may or may not apply), but it also implied that the Fair Folk were involved, that the Gods were involved, and so on, and it put them all on equal footing as supernatural aspects of the world she encountered in her journey that ultimately made her a Sorcerer. To the extent that 1e talked about "Infernal Sorcery", it presented it as a distinct sort of magic from what Brigid used. (This is not necessarily a good way to go either, for a variety of reasons, but the implication I always got from 1e was that Brigid -- to the extent that she's a historical figure and not a general mythological account of something done by the Exalted -- really was the first character in the setting to use what we now call Sorcery, fullstop, which was assembled from aspects of all the existing magics in Creation, one in which the supernatural secrets of the Demon Princes were just one in a very long list of secrets she was exposed to in her travels.)

                                                    This did not become "the Yozis invented Sorcery" until 2e in Infernals, when everything became about the Yozis 24-7. I would not expect it to remain canon in 3e, because it is dumb dumb dumb. I did not sign on to play a Twilight to use Yozi Magic; it was especially dumb to make Sorcery 'Yozi magic' while introducing a splat for people who want to draw on the powers of the Yozis. Every book on Sorcery has gone out of its way to imply that it was thematically neutral and to express the idea that Sorcery draws on the entire breadth of the setting.

                                                    The story of Brigid, again (while I'm not sure it was ever necessary) went out of its way to show how she drew on every aspect of the setting to become the First Sorcerer, including Fair Folk and the power of the Wyld, which predates the Yozis. I'm not saying that out of a nit-picking desire to say 'well yes but when Infernals retconned that to "the Yozis invented Sorcery lol" they were contradicting line X of page Y of this book" -- I'm not that fond of Brigid's story anyway, like I said -- but my point is that the fact that they had to contradict the fact that Brigid's journey touched on things outside the Yozis is a symptom of how aspects of late-2e narrowed the scope of the setting by making everything about the Yozis. Turning "Mara was involved in Brigid's creation of Exalted Sorcery" into "Mara handed Brigid Sorcery, which is Yozi Magic, fully-formed as a plot by the Yozis, and oh yeah the rest of the story is unimportant" belittles Sorcery and cheapens the Exalted in order to burnish some dumb handwavy NPCs. The core of the story of Brigid -- in my opinion, the only part of it that's really important or worthwhile -- is that Sorcery is derived from everything (a category which is not, despite what 2e would have you believe, limited to the conceptions or creations of the Yozi, as, again, the fact that Brigid had to study under one of the princes of the chaos beyond Creation along the way.) 2e narrowed and collapsed that grand mythology down to one line vaguely-implying Mara's involvement in the same way that it narrowed and collapsed large portions of the setting.

                                                    Let me put it like this -- suppose you had a superhero, I don't know, Doctor Strange. And then one day you say "Doctor Strange's magic is actually derived from GALACTUS! He is using the POWER COSMIC and never knew it!" Do you think that fans of Doctor Strange would enjoy this revelation? Especially if they were people, like me, who thought Galactus was a pretty dumb kind of character in the first place and thought Doctor Strange was really cool? No, they would break out the torches-and-pitchforks. And if you told them that they should be happy that Doctor Strange was retconned to actually be using a facet of the Power Cosmic because the Power Cosmic is awesome and anyway why aren't they glad Doctor Strange is now connected to a much more powerful character -- someone who predates the universe! -- they would probably go murder-eyes.

                                                    That is exactly what the retcon about the Yozis being the sole-direct-one-true-source-of-Sorcery did to Twilights. I am not exaggerating or speaking euphemistically or using it as an example, I am saying is exactly, down to a T, like saying that Doctor Strange's powers are actually derived from Galactus. It is that terrible.

                                                            Also, shifting mutations through Hybrid Body Rearrangement already lets Lunars do lots of things other Exalts need specific charms for, such as growing gills to breathe underwater... enhancing natural armor... producing weapons... parrying lethal unarmed... buffing parry DVs (quills mutation)... developing extra senses to see in the dark or detect invisible things... enhancing existing senses... converting their bodies to stone, fire etc... walking on ceilings... becoming invulnerable to heat and cold... instantly regrowing a severed limb (though without regaining health levels)... and... oh yeah... flying. "A different kind of Exalt needs a charm to do that" just seems like a strange reason not to let a player accomplish something using HBR. I mean there are things HBR enables that some Exalts can't do even with a charm. It's the one Knack that epitomizes Lunar awesomeness.
