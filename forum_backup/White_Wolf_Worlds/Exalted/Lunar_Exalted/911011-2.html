            I would like to clarify that I never said INDIVIDUAL Lunars would be terrible at creating order or leading nations.

            I was talking about the Lunar Host as a body, compared to the families within the Dragon-Blooded Host.

            Which also reflects the fact that, if the Lunar Exalted were in power, it would be a bunch of scattered kingdoms (perhaps as many as 300) at best, and a hostile Terrestrial-led force of mortals could take out most such kingdoms one-by-one.

                        Originally posted by The Revenge of TV Head View Post
                        How does one look for tells when damn near everyone is some variety of beastman? What sense is there in looking for tattoos when anyone who's anyone wears them?
                        ​Hmm, that raises the question; in an alternative setting where the Lunars won out in the Usurpation, did they ever need the tattoos?

                        We've not yet been given rules for the mark of Luna, but I think the sidebar is going in a direction of it often being fairly subtle, and something that can be hidden even further when desired, possibly without requiring additional powers.

                        And given the provisional rules for how to spot a shapeshifted Lunar, well, it might be that if a Lunar ever goes to the effort to never be seen with their mark at its full capacity, that nobody would ever be able to identify them.

                        I'm not saying they're bad ideas, just that the final product might render them unnecessary.

                        After all, Lunars are already supposed to be operating in a context in which they're keeping themselves hidden, so their marks and tattoos might be designed around enabling that against casual observation.

                        Whatever the case, I think it might be that the mark of Luna isn't something that generically gives somebody away as a Lunar; I mean, it's something that's common to Lunars and adds to their uncanny quality, but there can be other explanations for why somebody has oddly yellow eyes. I think its main story purpose is to provide a means of recognising a familiar Lunar whatever shape they're in, a logical basis and a bit of flavour.

                        Although that does make me wonder if, say, something in the anima rules they wind up getting might include qualities that make the mark apparent as the mark. Otherwise, I get the image of a Lunar who goes to lengths to give themselves as many distinguishing features as possible so that somebody familiar wouldn't really know which one they're supposed to be looking for.

                        But perhaps I'm overthinking it; it might be that if a person takes in your distinguishing characteristics, including the mystical one, then the experience that corresponds to succeeding on the roll is a case of dawning comprehension that you know those eyes, those scars, that shadow.

                                                            I had an image that I think could match up nicely with my current speculations on the complicated relationships between Solars and Lunars.

                                                            ​The Sidereals attempt to bring many of the Lunars in on their planning, thinking that the situation of the people closest to the increasingly tyrannical Solars will be a strong motivation to rebel.

                                                            Almost all of these Lunars, loyal and outraged, attack the admitted traitors and inform their mates. Everything kicks off a bit earlier, and is very messy.

                                                            Most Lunars, seeing an opportunity in the new vulnerability of their Solars and with a lot of frustrations boiling beneath the surface, strike them down. The bewildered Sidereals retain the wherewithal to capture almost all of the Essences, or maybe even actually all of them. This causes the Castes of the Lunars to break in an entirely different way than the familiar setting, and the new Castes they eventually build for themselves are focused on different subjects.

                                                            Since the Usurpation was forced to start too early, before all of the preparations could be effectively made, there is chaos amongst the ranks of the Dragon Blooded, and even if the Sidereals aren't gutted, they could be considered in a state equivalent to rout. They're forced to make terms with many Lunars from a position of significant weakness. For most Lunars, what they want is not really to govern, but have a series of measures instituted that allows them to live ungoverned.

                                                            A lot of Dragon Blooded do actually get organised into institutions that actually govern, so in a way the prophecies of the Sidereals were actually fulfilled; it's just that they're many, fractured, and often weak. In a very figurative sense, the Lunars did not become the kings, but the dragons at the border that the king regularly needs to send tribute to, just that these are dragons with enough political awareness to keep an eye out for any kings who try to do things like heavily centralize government and develop effective finances, because they're the type of dragon that is a lot more vulnerable to large, effectively trained forces than the occasional band of uppity knights.
