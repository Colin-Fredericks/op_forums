    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
    Not really *want*, so much as that it's an expected cost of the strategy and everyone within the Pact is fairly away of this. The idea that they tend to define victory purely in taking down Dynast numbers, at whatever cost, because they can replace themselves faster. Seems like that implies that they are intending losses, not quite kamikaze like but certainly with the idea that heroic deaths are expected?
    Lunar replacement process sucks compared to Dynastic Dragonblooded. The Silver Pact trades in one trained Lunar for a complete unknown newbie. There is only the vague 'passed life' connection between each excarnation, and only Luna knows where or when the successor will appear.

    Meanwhile every adventurous Dynastic Dragonblood that leaves the Blessed Isle already has replacements being raised among their cousins or siblings if not children. New Dynasts have a connection of familial emotion and traditions to the old, and may well know their elder. This steady rate of trained replacements only encounters glitches with exceptional circumstances -- such as the Tepet expedition. And they can draw on Lost Eggs to replenish.

    The Sidereal replacement process is a bit worse than Lunars, in that they have to wait until the candidate matures; but at least they have the ability to track and train them while the wait.

    So, no, Lunars don't win when they trade exalted lives. Lunars win when they erode the Realm's influence, exhast its more irreplacable resources, and erode faith in the Immaculate traditions.

                                        I think I was the very first person to mention Lunar attrition in relation to Dragonblood attrition on this thread, when I was talking about the Contagion, and how it was a much bigger problem for the Dragonblood (who are harder to replace the less of them there are) than the Lunars.

                                        Partly because, unlike just after the Shogunate, they're massively outnumbered by Dragonblood, partly because it's a bad tactic (because of the loss of skills*), partly because who'd want to join up with that strategy.

                                        *In WW2, Britain and America's air forces improved over time. The Japanese air force got worse. The reason is that the Japanese used their best pilots to fly missions, where they died. The Americans and British retired a lot of good pilots after a certain amount of missions and used them as trainers for new pilots. So over time, the Japanese air force lost its skills and ended up with badly-trained pilots. This is one of the reasons for the increasing use of Kamikaze. Which, you'll note, did not win them the war.


                                        Saying that, I'm sure the Lunar elders are aware that, over the long term, a lot of Lunars are going to die, and get replaced.                                                             Re; big barbarian armies, this is probably well verging into personal headcanon, but I would tend to see them perhaps in the light that they are things which happen among mortals and which Lunars infiltrate and use, perhaps more than, y'know, large hordes of beastmen and things like that.

                                                            To explain: It seems to me that barbarian armies are going to be a fact of life for the Realm, whether the Lunars are involved or not - there are going to be a lot of regions that border somewhere rich but don't couldn't pay for themselves in taxes, extraction and trade but can support fairly militarized populations (large shares of the population who spend a lot of time fighting each other), and afford to import weapons and such enough to be a threat to the richer places the Realm dominates.

                                                            The Realm can make such place border marches under garrisons, but this imposes costs of paying all those soldiers, and also the costs of "No one can pay me enough to be posted to Hadrian's Wall" where soldiers really want to be back in the capital and part of civilization, so you have to really pay to recreate "civilized" life out there, and get the locals to buy into this all as well. You don't want a gaggle of bored soldiers who pile a litany of abuses on the locals and inflame rebellion and conflict, before marching back themselves in rebellion against the core empire or its rich provinces!

                                                            Often it's just cheaper to let these places be, and even pay them off.... but in the longer term that inspires flareups of opportunistic hordes, particularly when the pay offs become tight, and this coincides with general reductions in how much jade the Realm has about on the whole and so the legions it can pay. Then you get barbarian armies.

                                                            That is, I expect, often when the Lunars get involved. They infiltrate silently, or if they can, join publicly, taking control or "advising" in various degrees of overtness (depending on how open the cultural beliefs are to them, how much they can engineer it, the competency of the mortal leaders in play). They have the supernatural skills and the access to supernatural allies (perhaps including their own elite beastman child heroes?) enough to give mortals a complement of magical forces that can make them more vital threats against the Realm and the magic it can bring to bear, and offer a "partnership" that's hard for barbarian leaders to refuse.

                                                            I'd see a fair amount of groundwork building before that; building the base of skepticism that allows them to operate without troublesome Immaculate beliefs and building the conditions that make these forces likely to happen together, and smaller forces that the Realm seems as unconnected join together to make a larger horde.

                                                            Anyway, that seems to me like a reasonable compromise on the idea that Lunars build barbarian hordes from scratch, in the knowledge they'll be sacrificed (like "donkeys leading lions"), which seems rather callous and makes them committed, particularly in ways that are perhaps boring to play, with the opposing idea that they're rarely involved in barbarian armies. There might be something very wrong with it though!
