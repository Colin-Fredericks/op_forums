        Before reading the thread beyond the first post:


        The Lunar Host cannot possibly control Creation without the cooperation of the Dragon-Blooded and Sidereal Hosts.

                    From one of a half-dozen or so "Lore Zero" alternate backstories of Creation:

                    The Lunars usurped their cruel Solar mates, somehow using their Bonds to restrain them. They then create Dragon-Blooded (which didn’t exist for the Great War) to be their enforcers. The Sidereals swear semi-coerced loyalty.

                    But there’s a problem. When Lunars die, the elders don’t trust their new Incarnations to not betray the conspiracy. This didn’t happen much for centuries, but in the Great Contagion, more Elders died than survived. The Solar Shards stayed bound, but those Bonds were now being maintained by Exalts who weren’t consciously aware of them, and might have objected to them had they known. This, of course, was a serious threat, since there is almost nothing an Exalt cannot change, if driven to do so.

                    And there were other problems - with the Lunars distracted by the Contagion, the Sidereals made their power play. They broke the Mask, to stay one step ahead of the Lunars, killed as many Lunars as they could, and Ayesha Ura, leader of the rebellion, took on the Resplendent Destiny of The Scarlet Empress, telling the Dragon-Blooded masses that the Lunarsth ey had once revered had become Anathema, and had to be slain. The Lunars were forced back, to try to bring down this new Realm. Naturally, they lied to the new generation of Lunars, claiming to have been betrayed in the beginning (as well as claiming those Lunars - like Leviathan - who opposed the Usurpation were dangerous and insane).

                    The Sidereals, of course, did a Grand Prediction, which told them it was impossible to free the Solars, so they got to ruling and fighting off the Lunars as best they could. This state of affairs continued interminably, until somehow, the Solars were freed.

                    ... so, the short version is, I can picture a Lunar hegemony looking a lot more like the warring states of the Shogunate than like the Imperialist might of The Realm.

                        Originally posted by Xerxes View Post
                        But how & why would a Lunar hegemony want to shut away the Solars? Without an explicit Sidereal alliance, would they even be able to? The removal of the Dragon-blooded as a power bloc leaves the question of what happens to the Solars that little bit more open.

                        They would do it for exactly the same reason the Sids and the Dragon-Blooded did it: The Solar's were no longer the glorious heroes of the Primordial War. They were cruel powermongers who recognized no-one as equals save themselves. Small powerblocks in the Deliberative could have devastating effects on millions. Like their glory, their power and cruelty no longer knew any limits.

                        In this version of the Usurption, the Sidereals invite the Lunars to join the rebellion.With the extra aid, intelligence, combat power, and the tactical benefit of position, the Usurption probably goes MUCH more smoothly. The Lunars take over the First Age, and begin carefully dismantling the infrastructure they consider too dangerous to remain, or no longer know how to maintain. The greatest secrets of the First Age are lost.

                        With the Deliberative shattered, Lunar territoriality becomes the dominant form of politics. The greatest of the elders form the Silver Pact, and rule from the pole of Earth as the Solars did before them, and send the younger Lunars out to the Threshold with Dragon-Blooded armies to pacify resistance. What each Lunar conquers, they keep as their own territory. Politics mostly relies on favors traded through the pact. No Lunar is so powerful as to threaten all he others, and the Silver Pact destroys those that try. Wise Lunars petition to join the Silver Pact when they reach a certain age or threshold of power.

                        Creation becomes more wild.. and more Wyld, but the borders never collapse. Lunar treaties prevent something as grand as the Balorian Crusade, but these same treaties do not have the powerful negotiation magic of the Eclipses and so the Lunars give ground as well. Individual Fair Folk are permitted within Creation for a variety of reasons. Treaties similar to those of Halta become more or less common in the far Threshold, and more and more rare as you approach the centre.

                        Lunars recognize the flaw that the First Age needed the Solar's to maintain the deeply sorcerous infrastructure that supported Creation. Lunars progress along a path designed around mortal limitations... and forcing mortals to exceed them. Cities become strongholds against outside threats, with Lunars testing each others societies, and greater supernatural threats moving between isolated city-states in the far distances of each direction. The might of the Silver Pact prunes those that become too dangerous, or too unacceptable.

                        OR

                        After the Usurption, the Lunars were pushed to the edges of Creation. When they learned of the Balorian Crusade, they saw an opportunity, and co-opted it. They feigned that they would see Creation burn in revenge for the Usurption, and acted as a hidden vanguard for the Fair Folk armies. They replaced Shogunate leaders and led their Dragon-Blooded followers into war with each other, leaving vast areas open for easy conquest, while they sent the eldest No-Moons amongst themselves to seize the Imperial Manse. With the Shogunate distracted by Fair Folk armies that moved faster than anyone thought possible, they thought they had a real opportunity to finally seize the Sword of Creation or themselves. Imagine their surprise when they were confronted by a young Dragon-Blooded officer with rather pitiable remains of her forces who had discovered the secret to gain access.

                        They struck an uneasy truce, and together faced the dangers of the interior, and directed the Sword against the Fair Folk armies. At this, the Lunars who had been working ahead of the Fair Folk sprung the traps they had laid behind enemy lines, and the invaders were slaughtered. To this day, the greatest hatred in all of Creation lies between the Fair Folk and the Lunars for this betrayal that cost them their greatest leaders.

                        The Lunars had to give a number of concessions to the Dragon-Blooded officer, including naming her empress, and supporting her rule... but they became her most powerful agents and advisors. The empress would have little real authority under this arrangement, but the Lunars did not count on being countered by the Sidereals, the masterminds of the Usurption, and a group that would never accept the Lunars as rulers over a Creation they had maimed. The tripod is the least stable of political structures, and this would prove no different. The empress would rule, and did everything she could to direct the Sidereals and Lunars against each other, while positioning her children in a regime that would reduce their influence as much as possible, considering their status as Celestial exalts. It became a reasonably common event to see a Sidereal assassin team eliminating an upstart Lunar disrupting events, only to have that team disappear completely with every Lunar elder observably in other locations at the time of their disappearance.

                        The Realm is a dying beast, hanging on by sheer tenacity of her ruler. Her children are prepared for fight aware against both the Sidereals with Heaven's support, and the Lunars with those Fair Folk still enslaved by ancient oaths during the Crusade. On their side, they have turned to the powers of hell, those Fair Folk who declare themselves enemies of the Lunars, and any ancient power left behind in Solar tombs or armories. The empress' disappearance can only mean the first strike of the civil war has been struck, but by who? By one of the empress' own children, seeking to replace her? Mnemon in particular is distrusted or her ambition. The Lunars? Their skill at removing obstacles from their path without detection. The Sidereals? The fact that they cannot determine who is at fault suggests strongly that they must be guilty of this crime. Why would they hide the actions of either the Dragon-Blooded or the Lunars? They could easily ally with those innocent of the crime and crush the guilty party. The maneuvers begin, and all fear that it shall be the last war Creation ever sees.

                                        Just shuffle the roles around a bit.

                                        The Dragon-Blooded then vanished from Creation, working from behind the scenes to manage its elements, though work was often stretched thin with a mere ten-thousand of them (the five-hundred-score fellowship, as they're more commonly called among those who can still remember them.) The Lunars now rule Creation, though its fate steadily declined without the wise Sidereals overseeing it.
