                        Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
                        Having read this I still have some reservations about it as a direction because it's sort of unclear to me still what parts of the "barbarian" theme Holden is wanting to preserve and which he want excised or watered down. Also, a lot of these examples of "barbarian" cultures like the Mongols were extremely advanced in a cultural and technological sense in their own time. The recurve bow and saddle stirrups were the six-gun and iron horse of their age..
                        Basically, the notion of ideological barbarians is kind of ridiculous and ahistorical and only works in fiction that you don't look at too closely. Maybe the Mongols thought there was great virtue to living on horseback versus in cities, but given the chance they definitely took over cities (and destroyed the ones who refused to pay a tithe to them), amassed incredible riches, and held court.

                        Most groups that are "barbarian" are really political and, often, stylistically barbarian. They're in certain circumstances but they don't hold their have-not position as an inherently good thing. I think in Creation, groups that are considered "barbarian" are going to be political and to a degree stylistically barbarian. Ideological barbarism doesn't make a lot of sense, ever, unless you have really really good reasons to hate civilization (see: Garou beliefs in WtA, where cities and industrialization are literally killing Gaia). And that was a big problem with 1e Lunars: borrowing heavily from WtA as an inspiration for character aesthetic, while not really getting that the Garou environmentalist/anti-industry themes make sense when you view the natural world and spirit world as precious living things being deliberately killed by pollution and stasis. But those are not themes in Creation, for a number of reasons (Crusaders of the Machine God tried to bring this in a bit as well, with Autochthonian industrial pollution being super-toxic to Creation's spirits, and that bit was was fucking terrible not least because how out of place it was).

                        Sine those major WtA themes are not present in Creation, it was and is inappropriate to port them into Lunars and really added nothing to their themes - it just made them sort of scattered (and we got it a bit in one of the 2e Silver Pact camps, and it made no fucking sense there either).

                        Basically, I expect ideological barbarism to be super culture-specific if it exists in Creation at all and political and stylistic are going to be the things played up.

                        Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
                        So to what meaningful degree are these cultures "barbaric?" Barbarian in the "political barbarian" sense just means the "other" it seems.
                        The Realm is the best example of the notion of Empire in Exalted. It's Rome at its height (both as a Republic and as an Empire); it's China during the Han and Tang Golden ages; it's (in different ways) both Tokugawa and Meiji Japan; it's Bismarckian Prussia. This is important to note because what is "barbarian" is often defined by the culture that views itself as the most civilized.

                        Thus what is "barbarian" is basically "the people the Realm dominates by its presence, versus dominating directly." The satrapies are not really barbarian in a strict sense; they're more analogous to the Roman Provinces, where Germany is the barbarian. The Realm is America, where the Native Americans are barbarian. The Realm is Saxon England, where the Vikings are barbarian. Etc.

                                            I think that was a consequence of a different problem. I mean, throughout most of 2nd edition, there were lots of Abilities/Attributes that were pretty bereft of Charms. In the corebook, Solar Larceny had exactly six Charms going for it. Solar Dodge had four. Part of this owed to the core systems and rules themselves,* part of this may have owed to developmental priorities,** and part of this probably owed to a number of different factors and circumstances. For comparison, take a gander at this KS update. There are eighteen Dodge Charms in there. We know the set's gone through a rewrite, granted, but they're definitely going for a hefty number of options in all areas. Given what they've mentioned elsewhere (see my second footnote), I don't foresee them withholding this philosophy from other splats. Mental -- and Social, to a slightly lesser degree*** -- Attributes shouldn't be the red-headed step-children**** of the Lunar Charmset in EX3.

                                            * When writing Charms, you need to refer to those core systems/mechanics: you will either hang the effects off of hooks the system gives you or invent your own new bit of mechanics. If those systems aren't robust enough to give you plenty of hooks -- and the 2e systems often weren't -- then that's going to mean either writing Charms that very quickly exhaust everything one can possibly do in that mechanical area, or more Charms in which you have to invent your own bits of mechanics to get an effect you want. This means they're going to tend to be wordier, which means fewer Charms the writers can squeeze out of a given block of word count. Word count basically always being at a premium, this generally means the Charm writer either bites the bullet and goes with those wordier Charms or kind of avoids that mechanical area. Where the writer does bite the bullet, there might still be further complexity: writers on future splat books need to either repeat those mechanics because it's not fair to refer players to mechanics from outside of the core book or come up with a different way of mechanically representing it. It also probably means more errata and/or screwy circumstances, as it may mean you now have a bunch of different mechanics across several splats for doing what is basically the same thing.*****

                                            ** This is not really a shot at Mr. Chambers, just that John and Holden are certainly placing a higher priority on having oodles and oodles of Charms. Like, I remember some thought they had about fitting in multiple elements within each Ability for the Terrestrial Charmset, despaired at how much word count that would use, and then delighted on remembering that they get to decide word count allocations. That's encouraging.

                                            *** Also not counting broken stuff, like Pretty Kitty Revolution.
