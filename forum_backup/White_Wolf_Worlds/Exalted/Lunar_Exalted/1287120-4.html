            If an empire wasn’t a baby eating machine, then it wouldn’t be an empire.

            You’re effectively complaining that we’re calling a system of hereditary rulership with just one ruler at a time a “monarchy”. That is what the word means. Words mean things.

            Grass is grass; it is a plant that consumes light and water to grow. That is how plants work, and Creation is not meant to be so fantastic that plants work any differently.

            So too empires. An empire which does not operate or behave like an empire would need to be called something else, appropriate to how it operates and behaves.

            Imperialism is all about conquering one’s neighbors to serve oneself. Nazism was imperial.

            There is no particular word, so far as I know, for “leave your neighbors to manage their own affairs so that you could each concentrate on building up your own assets and then seek trade with each other.”

                        Um, why are empires essentially 'baby eating machines'? This is asserted without any discussion. I won't dispute that many if not most empires have been so; is that an essential part of them? This is quite a poor argument.

                        As far as the larger concern, the Realm is full of moral dilemmas. Of all the Exalt types, Dragon-Bloods are the ones least affected by the Great Curse, and likely if the Great Curse deals with the Exalts as type, the larger the number of DBs, the less impact the Curse has on any given member. This translates to the Dragon-Bloods being the Exalt who can, actually, create the most stable Creation-spanning government. It is very likely the case that the ideal state structure for Creation as it stands in the wake of the Great Curse would invert the First Age's structure: the Dragon-Bloods are on top, and the Lunars/Solars/Sidereals/others are all troubleshooters for issues too large for local DBs to deal with.

                        That is the true glory of the Host. The problem with the DBs is that it requires unity of a much larger group than the other Exalts. The Scarlet Empress's Realm IS an evil empire; that is basically uncontested. It is very possibly a pragmatic 'this is what I had to do' evil, but its excesses are unnecessary and surrender the moral high ground.

                        But the Empress is gone, and the Realm is going to implode without extraordinary measures. What part of this limits meaningful decisions on the part of the DBs? If anything, the Empress's control was what limited true discussions of moral character.

And that involves killing babies (I don't know why people keep focusing on the babies, it's not okay to kill adults either).

                                That's why the British Empire was, well, an Empire, whereas the USA is a federation.


                                Anyway, that doesn't mean you can't have stories of the Realm defending territories from rampaging beastmen or Fair Folk or anathema. But... is that not mentioned in WFHW?
                                Either way, I kind of feel that WFHW isn't the one to judge it on, but rather The Realm.

                                That'll have examples of a number of Satrapies, so that's the one to look at to see if they present it as "The Realm conquered this satrapy, and many people would like to be independent again due to the taxes and religious oppression, but the Immaculate Monastery did at least turf out the volcano god who demanded human sacrifice," or as "100% of people in this Satrapy hates the Realm because Realm soldiers murdered their babies."

                                I very much doubt it'll be a case of "everyone loves the Realm in this satrapy," but that would be unrealistic.*

                                *There are of course, many places that, generally not initially but eventually, quite liked being part of an Empire. Britain, for example, ended up as one of Rome's most loyal and best-assimilated far provinces.
                                But these are places that are either colonies (Canada, New Zealand, etc) or which are integrated and assimilated, so the people see themselves as part of the Empire, unlike their barbarian ancestors.
                                The Realm doesn't colonise much, and nor does it really try and integrate foreign lands into it. The people in Satrapies are not taught to be Realm citizens, and they do not gain the rights of Realm citizens. They are forever second-class, and that's the kind of thing that makes conquered lands unhappy. And unhappy lands rebel, and so can only be controlled via violence.
