    Exalted fans have contradictory desires for and ideas about Lunar shapeshifting

    11-28-2015, 04:12 PM
    I've seen this a lot. Some people argue that the default method of Heart's Blood Hunting is too bloody and murderous and combat-oriented, and other people argue that Changing Moons should absolutely keep the same disguise-oriented anima effect they've always had. I'm pretty sure these arguments even come from the SAME people, though never at the same time.

    Well, it's time to put these two impulses up face-to-face and show the contradictions between and underneath them.



    "We Don't Want To Be Animals."
    In part, the desire for the Changing Moons to keep their disguise power is a blind reaction against the aversion to the basic Heart's Blood Hunt. It lets the player avoid the combat and the killing and the blood. It lets them play X-Men's Mystique or Morph, especially if they ignore the character's totem and spirit shape and pick a Tell that isn't an animal feature.

    Let me say upfront that I don't condemn this sort of character build. I myself argue for the existence of Lunar disguise Charms on par with Alchemical disguise Charms (save for a Tell which the Lunar perhaps can't so easily suppress as the Alchemical can disguise her clay-like flesh and Soulgem), which would make this sort of character build available to Lunars of any Caste, not just Changing Moons. But more on that later.

    However, while I applaud exploring characters who want to avoid their more beastly aspects out of distaste or self-loathing, I think that players who want to avoid animal associations are making a mistake if they choose to play Lunar Exalted. At least half of the drama inherent to the concept of the self-hating Lunar is that the Lunar must eventually confront that side of herself -- even consistently choosing to reject it means being confronted by it on a recurring basis. Is that really what the player wants out of the character?

    Certainly, the Exigent book is going to be released before the next Lunars manual, and the developers have made sure to include the Chosen of Masks, a woman who can look like other people without any bent towards animals. Far more like Mystique. Even right now in the corebook, Solars have disguise Charms so thorough that they're practically person-to-person shapeshifting, without any need to hunt, and the Socialize Charms let you create alternate personae with their own, different strengths and talents. Choices like this let a player avoid hunting and killing and blood entirely, without having to cut off entire limbs from the Exalt's splat and Charmset.



    "We Love Animals, But Must We Be Red In Tooth And Claw?"
    But there are people who very much love Lunar animal associations and wish to embrace them while still keeping the default Hunt at arm's length. There are two different but often inclusive reasons for this: (1) the player doesn't want to play a murderer, or perhaps wants to play a vegetarian who is horrified by the idea of eating meat or drinking hot arterial blood, or (2) the player simply wants to play a socially-specialized Changing Moon and doesn't want to invest into combat capability.

    Again, let me say upfront that I applaud these character concepts! Even further, I don't think trying to play a vegetarian Lunar is missing the point of the splat, though it still touches in a positive way upon the drama of rejecting the path of least resistance and most reward (or at least, the quicker reward).

    I certainly want non-lethal methods of Heart's Blood Hunting to return as paths of mastery that provide bloodless alternatives. And yes, not just having sex with the target, for the same reason that the writers included the Red Rule. Not every player or gaming group is comfortable with bringing sex into the gaming session, and this should be respected.

    But some people have taken the argument that if non-lethal methods are going to be allowed at all, why can't killing be a matter of choice? In fact, why not stick the killing method on those brutish Full Moons as a Caste feature, and let us social Changing Moons have the more humane option, and us clever No Moons have the more intellectual option?

    There are multiple reasons why.


    First, this goes back to the reason why anyone should be playing a Lunar Exalted in the first place. Even the least combat-oriented Changing Moon or No Moon can turn into a bear, or an elephant, or a shark, or any beast with considerable muscle and killing power, without the least bit of Ability or Background or Charm investment in combat. True, not all of these Lunars will, but ANY of them can. Additionally, Third Edition has lowered the bar on the minimum level of combat investment required to kick ass in a fight, or at the very least to survive and flee a fight gone bad. A bear or shark or elephant often has the advantage in running away from a losing situation -- they're not just large, they're fast!


    Second, Second Edition screwed up the Hunt. No, I'm not talking about the minimum length of time needed to hunt something, nor the Willpower cost, nor the inability to take and store Heart's Blood you couldn't yet use. I mean, all of those things sucked, but there was a much bigger mistake. Second Edition emphasized the image of a single Lunar hunting alone, personally chasing a beast down, and killing it in mortal combat. Obviously, Full Moons have the advantage in this specific range of scenarios, and other concepts get the shaft.

    But First Edition had a much more open, flexible, and forgiving emphasis. It insisted only that the Lunar be the one to strike the killing blow and taste the hot life's blood. It explicitly mentioned that several other characters could participate in the hunt, which naturally meant that the Lunar who takes the Heart's Blood didn't need to participate beyond marking off the final health level.

    Naturally, Full Moons would often be able to take any Heart's Blood they wanted personally and without help, but excelling in physical situations is why players choose Full Moons to begin with. Naturally, Changing Moons and No Moons would have to either rely on their Full Moon party member to help them with killing something, or else they would need to use their social powers to persuade NPCs to corner and cripple the beast for them, or else they would have to construct traps or cast spells to get the job done... and using methods like those are exactly why players chose to play Changing Moons and No Moons in the first place. The game was working as intended, and it can work that way again.


    Third, if killing something and taking its blood becomes no easier than any other method, it loses its dramatic power. Making it the most basic way for all Lunar Exalted to steal the forms of others presents a moral dilemma for those characters concepts which would care about such a moral dilemma at all. How can you have a social or intellectual Lunar who struggles to reject her beastly side if by default she has a bloodless way to steal forms? How can your pacifist Full Moon have a moral victory unless there's a temptation to reject?

    This is also why there should never be a Hunt that involves merely beating someone unconscious to take their form. That's just an objectively more convenient form of the default method. It would be like an upgrade to Blazing Solar Bolt that lets you use the Charm without spending the Willpower cost and without needing to substitute any other cost in its place; why would you ever spend the Willpower cost again? The choice becomes meaningless.

    It's not like killing the target in the basic Hunt was ever an advantage compared to a KO. Sure, you could eat the body to dispose of the evidence, or toss it off a cliff, but you could also do the same to a victim you'd beaten unconscious; it's not like they could fight back. This alternative method is thus just a pure upgrade that kills one of the most interesting conflicts of the hunt without introducing another conflict to take its place.

    Now, let me say upfront that I am eagerly hoping for a Charm that lets you challenge someone to a contest of some kind to steal their shape without killing them; that would allow you to beat someone unconscious in a martial arts duel and take their face while leaving them alive. But that's different from the carte blanche to ambush someone in a dark alley at night. Unlike the basic Hunt or the proposed "Hunt without killing", THIS kind of hunt requires you to warn your target that you want to confront them, gives them the option of rejecting your challenge, and might even give them the option of choosing the specific form or place of the contest. Which offers a completely different set of advantages and disadvantages to the basic hunt in a way that "just beat them unconscious" does not.


    To answer the question of this section, "No, you don't have to be 'red in tooth and claw' to be a Lunar. But that doesn't mean it should be just as easy. Abyssals face similar problems with avoiding incidental killing of people they don't even want to kill, and Sidereals face problems with wearing as much armor as they might like or finding as much starmetal as they would need, and Alchemicals have this Clarity business as well as the limitations of their physical Charms. All splats face challenges that reinforce their core themes and which lend weight to their decisions."



    Why Bother Making Disguise Into A Charm?
    Okay, now it is later. Let's return to the conflict between the people who want to keep the old Changing Moon anima power, and the people who want to see that turned into a Charm.

    I've already addressed most of the reasons why people are adverse to this proposed change. There are certainly other arguments as well.

    For instance, Changing Moon players don't want to spend a Charm investment on something they've been able to take for granted. However, not every Changing Moon concept involves wearing other people's faces; Changing Moons can be entirely centered around training and commanding beasts and men in battle, or the ever-popular Pretty Kitty Revolution. To cover all three of these Changing Moon concepts equally, it would make sense to give the Caste a new anima ability that enhances their social persuasion (and intimidation) abilities in much the same way that the Full Moon anima effects enhance their athletic prowess. Which means one less social Attribute Charm that a face-stealing Lunar has to buy, which offsets the cost of needing to buy one disguise Charm.

    However, the most frequent and heart-felt argument is that wearing other people's faces is somehow more appropriate and useful to Changing Moons than it is to Full Moons and No Moons. This is also the most BASELESS argument I've ever heard on the matter.

    No Moons can benefit very much from looking exactly like the owner of a library when there are other scholars present; he might be able to sneak in as a mouse, but a mouse turning the pages of a book to read it is rather conspicuous! A No Moon also has much more use for using human disguises to spy upon others in a social setting and overhear their dangerous gossip and secret confessions -- a Changing Moon is persuasive enough to manipulate people without this knowledge, but using knowledge to leverage the world to their desires is what a No Moon is all about. Sharp ears and blackmail cover all sorts of shortcomings in persuasive talent.

    And what about Full Moon assassins? While it is a mistake to think of all assassination methods as involving direct combat and brute force rather than poisons or traps, it is a similar mistake to assume that all Full Moons must always choose to fight openly on the battlefield. The ability to assume anyone's face is an invaluable tool for getting close enough to a target for a private explosion of deadly violence, without having to defeat all of the target's army and bodyguards first.

    People play Lunar Exalted to be werewolves, skinwalkers, and facestealers. All Lunar Exalted are capable of becoming werewolves with a single Charm purchase. Why should one Caste have better talent at being skinwalkers and facestealers than the other two?



    Disguise Charms versus Heart's Blood
    Finally, some may ask what the point of having Lunar disguise Charms for wearing other peoples faces would be, when it would seem to invalidate any point in stealing the Heart's Blood of other people.

    Except following this rejection to its logical conclusion would mean getting rid of the old Changing Moon anima power anyway.

    But it also ignores, forgets, or refuses to imagine the advantages to having a person's Heart's Blood beyond taking their face. Perhaps they were larger or stronger or more graceful than you -- the disguise Charm should not cover this. Perhaps they had a functional, physical mutation or two which you would find useful for purposes other than convincing people you have them and thus obviously are who you appear to be -- a disguise Charm should not cover this.

    Second Edition offered Flickering Star Infusion, for a way to keep your victim's death from being detected by most elements within Heaven and its Bureau of Destiny, by keeping the victim's thread of Destiny alive within the Loom of Fate. If Sidereals tried to ascertain the validity of your assumed identity by placing an Astrological Curse or Blessing upon your stolen name, you could bear the Curse to keep your cover, or enjoy the Blessing as much as possible. There's also receiving Infallible Messengers intended for your victim, where NOT receiving them would give you away, or where the utter failure of the spell would reveal your victim's death.

    There are also potential Heart's Blood Charms for taking advantage of skills the victim had which you do not. Treating your victim as a mentor for anything they knew in life, to shorten your training times, or to learn Martial Arts or Spells which would normally require lots of searching for living sifus or investigation of forbidden libraries. To say nothing of Lunar versions of the Solar personae Charms, which create personae based on the personalities and skills of the people you killed.

    Finally, the option to steal Heart's Blood without killing anyone opens up SO MANY possible Charms to use against other people. Bonuses to track them down and locate them no matter where they hide. Charms to better understand them, piercing their masks and lies to see the true motivations underneath. Charms to undermine their Resolve against the hypnotic sway your social influence can exert through the bond of the blood the two of you share in your veins.



    Conclusions
    So, every side of these issues I can think of come down on this:

    1) Changing Moons need a different anima power, and their old anima power, in so far as it returns at all, needs to become a Charm available to all Lunar Exalted.

    2) The basic Heart's Blood Hunt (or at least the First Edition version) is good, and doesn't need to be fixed, replaced, or somehow made "equal" to other options.

                                                      Re: overlapping Lunar and Solar mechanics which this thread touched on, Lunars are a specific thing, and I would think they should get mechanics which most simply implement that thing, not guided by any sort of "Well, the way that makes sense to implement this would be as Solars do it.... but we can't have them do it the way Solars do it, so here's this sort of weird and arbitrarily restricted way of doing it which no one actually wants, just so we can say it's different".

                                                      I think you have to trust in a combination of the paths being through attributes rather than abilities, and the Charms being rooted in transformation into animal forms (of sorts - including beastmen and others who are animalish but not exactly any real animal), and the different natures of Luna and the Unconquered Sun (and Maidens, etc.) to naturally differentiate Lunars vs others, combined with solid execution. This seems like the "No single Charm from splat X should overlap another single Charm from splat Y" thing again.

                                                      My feeling on Lunars is that really I want this solidly executed, through all three Castes, plus a strong emphasis on the Lunars as transforming and animalistic heroes with many selves who perform great deeds and who grow in their different selves through their deeds: rediscovering and fixing ancient magical treasure, protecting people under their command, slaying and taming mighty Raksha and oppressive gods, exploring Wyld kingdoms and the far parts of Creation no one can reach through supernatural animal and animalistic forms that can go further than man, bringing Luna's justice and enlightenment to unjust places and times, building peace between and a better future for peoples at war. At the same time, I want them to have the greater than human flaws expected of Exalted and generate interesting drama- petty jealousy, anger, hubris and arrogance, rivalry, harsh and unbending moralism, impractical excesses of compassion, hunger for power, indecision.

                                                      I don't really care too much about the concept of playing out guerilla warfare vs the Realm (a la Maoist / Islamist / separatist rebels, etc), really, or the internal politics of guerilla war insurrection. Nor about playing a person who eats people and animals to steal their form and face. That's just my take!

                                                      So it'll be interesting to see what the Lunars actually offer here, and also what the Lunars' fanbase actually wants in regards of these different elements. What would people actually prefer to see the Lunars do? Not assuming that guerrilla warfare and hunting and stealing forms versus what I guess I would label heroic fantasy drama are mutually exclusive or in contradiction, but there's a balance, and a question of emphasis, here.

                                                      For Lunars generally, on your post StG, although I think you're smart enough to know this already, and the implication may already be there, I think there's a big gap between both a vegetarian character and a character who wishes to avoid murder generally, and players wanting to play a character who can murder, but for human reasons, not connected with hunting, of a literal or metaphorical sort. I don't know how much support there should be among Lunars for characters who are not interested in being a hunter of any sort. I personally am not so interested in assuming forms by hunting and the stealing of forms, so much as find assuming animal forms as a pathway for enabling mighty heroism to be an interesting concept to explore.

                                                          I think you have to trust in a combination of the paths being through attributes rather than abilities, and the Charms being rooted in transformation into animal forms (of sorts - including beastmen and others who are animalish but not exactly any real animal), and the different natures of Luna and the Unconquered Sun (and Maidens, etc.) to naturally differentiate Lunars vs others, combined with solid execution. This seems like the "No single Charm from splat X should overlap another single Charm from splat Y" thing again.
                                                          That's how I feel.

                                                          A lot of Alchemical Charm effects worked exactly like Solar Charm effects, but there were so many other differences in their other effects and in how their Charms worked on a basic level that they still came off as very unique.

                                                          More unique than the Lunar Exalted would be, but that's because no other Exalted relates to their Charms the way the Alchemicals do.



                                                          I don't really care too much about the concept of playing out guerilla warfare vs the Realm (a la Maoist / Islamist / separatist rebels, etc), really, or the internal politics of guerilla war insurrection. Nor about playing a person who eats people and animals to steal their form and face. That's just my take!
                                                          I'm personally hoping for support for those characters who want to bloodlessly prey upon people to steal their forms, if only to use the Heart's Blood of those victims against them later in various "sympathetic magic" ways. Especially when the Lunar would find killing these people virtually impossible. Lunars should be able to change the rules of the game and come at problems from new angles.

                                                          But I am certain that there will be room to tell Lunar stories which don't involve the war against the Realm at all. The Threshold is a big place, and it's not impossible for Lunars, Dragon-Blooded, and Sidereals to make strange bedfellows in one particular region against a common foe.

                                                          Which is the sort of story I'm personally more interested in playing. The Dragon Uncle, the Godfather of Dragons, a Lunar who wanders the Threshold looking for Lost Eggs and giving them a step up in the world which doesn't involve leaving their struggling communities or imperiled kingdoms for the empty promises of the Realm. The Threshold of Creation needs all the heroes it can get, and those heroes need all the training and connections that a Lunar (and his secret Sidereal partner) can provide.

                                                          It's not that I object to the main story of the Silver Pact waging guerrilla war against the Realm, though. That absolutely should be the main story of the splat.



                                                          For Lunars generally, on your post StG, although I think you're smart enough to know this already, and the implication may already be there, I think there's a big gap between both a vegetarian character and a character who wishes to avoid murder generally, and players wanting to play a character who can murder, but for human reasons, not connected with hunting, of a literal or metaphorical sort.
                                                          Your question is: "What about characters who want to murder someone just because that person needs to die, rather than for Heart's Blood or other material or magical rewards?"

                                                          If it has absolutely nothing to do with Heart's Blood, then it has absolutely nothing to do with my points in this thread. Whether or not your character has the motivation and the resolve to murder people for any other reason is up to you. Nothing in the basic system of the game prevents it, and the question is no different for Lunars than any other character, save that a Lunar might be able to get Heart's Blood out of the murder as an incidental bonus if she so chooses.


                                                          I don't know how much support there should be among Lunars for characters who are not interested in being a hunter of any sort.
                                                          How broadly are you thinking of "any sort"?

                                                          Some Lunars literally hunt in the fields, but others hunt metaphorically. They hunt in ballrooms and bedrooms, in ball-courts and law-courts, through oaths fulfilled and vows broken.

                                                          In plainer language, Robert Vance wrote a Charm where a Lunar could steal your Heart's Blood by successfully suing you in a court of law; he not only takes your money, but your face. If you want to play a Lunar who seeks to bring justice to the world, this seems like a fitting method.

                                                          There was Defeated Technique Mastery which allowed a Lunar to steal another splat's Charms by beating them at some mutually-agreed-upon contest. Obviously that sort of thing is hideously unbalanced, but it should have been a method of Heart's Blood acquisition from the beginning. It also doesn't need to be a martial arts duel -- it could be a footrace, a cooking contest, a children's card game, or a Rap Battle of Epic History (the Vikings called it "Flyting").

                                                          The corebook's Changing Moon has a way to gain forms through deals. I have envisioned a Charisma-based Charm where the Lunar sincerely swears an oath so hard, it creates in him a Major or Defining Intimacy towards the promise. With an upgrade, he can use this power to swear a mutual oath with someone, and if the Lunar fulfills the oath or if the other party breaks their side of the bargain, the Lunar magically and non-lethally gains their Heart's Blood.

                                                          Even without illusions of light and sound, there is plenty of room for Lunars to filter "hunter" into "trickster who is always looking to get the better of his enemies and rivals by being clever".
