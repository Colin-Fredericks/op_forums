    As i've posted before on past threads, the cosmos does not hand out cool kids clubhouses as a matter of course. They're a rebellion hiding in the enemy's midst, not a fully organized hierarchy roughly symmetrical to the Realm or the Bureau of Destiny. Insisting that they be one is grand scale missing the point, which prior editions unfortunately ended up doing, with squatting out in the Wyld or trying to be Silver Sidereals with the TSR.     I agree with you a great deal - and I guess I'll have to wait to see their charms.

    The "lesser Solar" charms were problematic, because any threat that could kill a Solar would (by extension) seem capable of killing a Lunar. If everyone on the map has "clubhouses" (and armies and patrons and etc), it just seems like a gross disadvantage to have inferior charms AND the least resources.

    Maybe 3E charms. I never had a chance to play Alchemicals, so I only have vague familiarity with their mechanics - but Infernals were amazing at the "disrupt areas" schtick without needing heavy patronage. Beastmen are dope - but if Lunars had By Rage Recast, you could quickly twist an entire town into monsters. I always liked the idea of some Immaculates visiting a town - only to realize the "noble families" are all werewolves or something - but mechanically, this would involve Beastmen (with a mutation to hide other mutations; and then you have to raise them, educate them, build a town or steal one, etc). Infernals also had some She Who Lives In Her Name charms that were amazing for sweeping in and reorganizing human behavior.

    But without the mechanical "umpf", yeah, cool kids clubhouses.

    Being the "eternally on the run" Exalts isn't bad, but has other issues (like how do you find peers - and why would you stick together). The "cool kid clubhouses" weren't just about power - they were ways to short-hand expectations. It wasn't "the Deathlords give us stuff!", it was "the Deathlord we all serve kind of gives us a unifying theme for a circle" (whether it be the FaFL's conquest of the South; the Silver Prince's naval adventures & politics; etc). Which Dynastic House, which Bureau you worked for, etc all helped orient games. By not having this, I feel like you end up with a generic Silver Pact - followed by "every Lunar pulling in different directions because we all join with different expectations".

    In the Abyssal circle I played with, most of us served The Lover Clad. That - alone - was great "short-hand" for what kind of adventures we sought (which are wildly different from say, the Dowager's use for Exalts.

    Answering these altogether:
    • Totem:
      Yes, these mutations may be common - but my point is, it's not the obvious "I look like Ursula" or "I want to be a wolf-dude". A lot of Lunar players seem to love their werewolves, centaurs, harpies, etc - I specifically wanted to not be obviously half-animal.
    • Tell:
      I agree with you on 1E > 2E, although I'm not complaining - again, my example is simply "I'm not obviously half-animal".
    • Heartsblood & Corpse-Like:
      You're correct that corpses are "food, not friends" - but I'd take anything that emulate Abyssal body-horror. I don't remember my old list, but off the top-of-my-head, here are some I recall and some I'd consider (I forget if they were on the old list).
      • Fangs - easy fit alongside Abyssals.
      • Poison - subtle, easy fit
      • Corpse-like skin - Camouflage (via octopus) or just a thick hide (with the appearance of the giant maggots who are described as being grotesquely pale, veiny, etc.
      • Lamprey-hands - If "bird-Lunars" don't automatically lose their arms in order to grow wings - and "medusa" Lunars can grow multiple snake-heads in their hair - then why should I suffer similar constraints? I forget if it was the giant-maggots, the lamprey or both - but at one point, I asked to turn my fists into maws full of teeth so I could bite targets via punching (mechanically, just claws). One ST was cool with Lunar body-horror (and I wooed a couple Abyssals who saw), another ST was less open to horrific-Lunars (so I avoided shapeshifting with them).
      • Black Chitin-Armor - Giant centipedes provide segmented armor - but then I photoshopped a mock-up, depicting my pre-existing clothing - to show how I covered the joints, etc. The goal was to make it look like "polished black armor" or even "chitin armor from a dead giant-centipede", not something I grew.
      • Bone Claws - Central African Hairy Frogs break their own bones - to pierce their toes - to create claws.
      • Ribcage Spikes - Spanish ribbed newts push their ribs through their skin as defensive weapons
      • Internal Organs - Sea Cucumbers can vomit up their (stick & toxic) organs and regenerate them. Maybe I take damage, but a few spare guts would be worth the stunt.
      • Death scent - I rad that opossum's (when afraid) emit corpse-like smells (to enhance their act).
      • Blood - Texas Horned Lizards emit blood from their eyes. Two snake species (Natrix Natrix & Tropidophis) both secrete blood from their mouth, nose and eyes when disturbed (to play dead).
      • Human Teeth - The sheepshead fish has humanoid teeth.... but no lips. Grab this (instead of fangs) just for a smile to make the Joker cringe.
    • 2E Writing to be Desired:
      Agreed! But this wasn't about the writing. This was player decisions (even though they had no in-character knowledge of what my Exalt was). Ergo, this is not "bad writing led characters to behave badly", it was "bad players - who like the writing - using out-of-character knowledge to pursue a "deviant" they want to convert".
    • Unwashed Savages:
      I'm being hyperbolic, but reminiscing on how pushy they were is just infuriating.

    pg 203 " While individual members of the Silver Pact may view a Casteless Lunar with excessive Wyld mutations as a chimera and try to kill her, a true chimera is a Casteless Lunar who accumulated 10 points of permanent Limit and fell into a state of permanent Limit Break."

    These are just a handful - but as written - it all adds up to Silver Pact Lunars:
    • send retrieval pacts for the Casteless
      • wont mention the possibility of staying Casteless
        • may use charms to manipulate you into not being Casteless
          • may use brute force to tattoo you anyways
    Remain casteless despite this? Silver Pact Lunars see you as...
    • doomed eventually
    • unfortunate
    • weaker (mechanically enforced)
    • temporary
    • arrogant
    • not respectable
    And as a result, they will...
    • spy on you
      • regret respecting your decision
        • kill you sooner (who cares about the "narrow majority" who spares you - you just need 1 motivated Lunar from the other side who disagrees and quietly kills you)
        • kill you later (for looking too mutated, even though you're not Chimera)
    Altogether, Castelessness is not a "peer" (like Vert) to be respected. You're more an "ignorant threat to yourself", and an opportunity for renown-seeking Lunars to "recruit a novice" (thereby establishing their steps to no longer being at the bottom of the social hierarchy).
