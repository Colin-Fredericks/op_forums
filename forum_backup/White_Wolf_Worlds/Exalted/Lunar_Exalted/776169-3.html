                    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                    How much does this even matter compared to Lunars being appealing and likeable as playable characters?
                    As someone who's been a Lunars fan since 1e, I find that Lunars are likable as trangressive, visceral counterpoints to idealised-nigh-angelic Solars. A part of that transgressiveness includes heart-eating and cannibalism, and I think it goes a long way to showing the kinds of things a Lunar will do to succeed. There's a deep stomach churning fear, that Solars don't really create, in having to beg for your life to a shape-shifting monster that you know, deep within your trembling bones, will eat you (of course, this being Fantasy, the people getting eaten usually have it coming to them through being callous and cruel in a way only possible in a world where everything is turned to 11).

                    I (personally, and this goes to reinforce the thread's thesis) see Lunars as, basically, anarchists. Not in a teenage-rebellion way, mind you, but in a real "cooperation without leadership, compassion without societal obligation, decentralised resistence"* way; which makes them fickle and dangerous from the point of view of those raised in more structured/stratified society.

                    Or sometimes you just want to play a legendary monster and don't want to deal with the angst and Humanity of world of darkness.

                    *I'm not writing an anarchist manifesto, just some thematics to get the idea across

                                                    The devs seem pretty set on Lunars as monsters this time around, and I think that will drive the stories you can tell with them. Earlier someone brought up the inconvenience of taking on a child's form without doing something monstrous. That looks deliberate to me. Maybe your character will take on a child's form by finding someone who is sick beyond saving and convincing herself that death is merciful. She's gonna have a time with the actual tasting of the Heart's Blood, assuming no charms, but hey, that's what a monster would do. That doesn't mean every Lunar is a wanton child murderer by design. It just means they will sometimes have a reason to do something monstrous, and perhaps think about how to mitigate it or make up for it. Or in other cases how to rationalize, pretend nothing happened, and move on. Or in yet other cases to slide into more horrifying habits. Seems like the point of all this is to examine those themes, not ditch them when they get in the way of overcoming challenges as the player sees fit.
