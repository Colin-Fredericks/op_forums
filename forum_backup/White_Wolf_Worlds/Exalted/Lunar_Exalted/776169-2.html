    Were I writing the Lunars from the ground up, I would probably have each of the three Castes approach the Hunt - and their default benefits from shapeshifting - differently.

    First, I'd have three means of "hunting," each represented by a Charm (or, if we're keeping them, a Knack; for purposes of this discussion, I will use Charm, however, because I suspect we're not keeping the distinction). Each Caste would get a specific Hunting Charm for free, reflecting their method and the benefits they get from it.

    Tasting the Heart's Blood would be the Full Moon Charm, and would do more or less exactly what we're used to: grant physical shape, powers (e.g. wings->flight and claws->weapons) and Attributes. This version may require skinning the creature to wear its pelt as the form of shapeshifting, or maybe not. I'd have to think about it and look at the pros and cons.

    Name-Stealing Practice, the Charm of the Changing Moons, would involve getting to know the target, gaining their trust, and stepping in to fill their social shoes. It would gain their social Attributes and steal their very identity, while donning the appearance without, necessarily, changing species. A little awkward that Tepet Marcelus is keeping a young dog-boy as his pet, perhaps, but nobody questions it more than they did when it was his actual loyal hound, because all their Intimacies and experiences towards it are the same. The victim is still slain, left to die an empty husk wherever things utterly forgotten go.

    No Moons would have the Trickster's Bargain. Through artifice, trickery, and con artistry, the No Moon would trade and trick the target into yielding up his knowledge and wisdom, trading it away for metaphysical barter until he had nothing left in his head but pointless grey meat. This would permit the character to take on the mental guise of the victim, learning his knowledge, gaining his mental Attributes, and perhaps even stealing from his soul the mystical properties and authorities he once had (allowing him to learn Charms they might have had as Evocations, to some degree). Their mannerisms would remind people of the being they're emulating, though their form may not change at all. It is the mind and soul, not the body or the social ties, they have stolen.


    In all cases, learning another Caste's Charm enables you to assume those aspects of the creatures in your Library, as well, so a Lunar who knows all three would be able to perfectly step into the life, knowledge, and body of any creature in their Library. Knowing more than one also lets you choose the best way for you to perform your hunts.

                    I was thinking about the Lunar castes, and it seems like while Full-Moons are great, that the No-Moons and Changing-Moons are reversed.
                    Shamans, as I understand them, rely almost entirely upon having a personal relationship with the spirits/monsters/things that they interact with. They need to be able to make just about anyone feel comfortable with them, because they will frequently deal with powerful things that can have rather short tempers or odd perspectives. That suggests really impressive interpersonal skills and thus Changing-Moons.
                    Tricksters, on the other hand, are generally widely disliked. They generally influence people and situations by finding an opportunity to be a manipulative bastard and taking full advantage of it. They are almost always clever and perceptive, and often quite intelligent. To me that suggests No-Moon.

                                                        Originally posted by Eurhetemec View Post
                                                        Assuming that's canon in Ex3, that's a very good point, actually, and changes my perspective a bit.

                                                        In that case, we could have all Lunars have Heart's Blood (as you suggested), and then all Lunars gain a further non-Charm method when they get their Caste. That seems like a win/win to me.
                                                        I have much fewer problems with this.

                                                        The biggest problem is "what do you consider appropriate Heart's Blood Hunting methods for each Caste?"

                                                        (I'm not talking about Segev's "emulate the victim socially or mentally" idea, I'm talking about taking the HEART'S BLOOD, complete with the entire physical form, with any social or mental traits being accessed through social and mental Charms.)

                                                        Not every Changing Moon is going to (or should have to) sleep around for forms. Other Changing Moons could form bonds with familiars and mortals, gaining their Heart's Blood as they recognize him as their lord and master; the beastmaster Lunar might even be able to gain Heart's Blood from creatures that his familiars kill. Still other Changing Moons could gain forms through deals and exchanges backed by magical oaths -- acquiring Heart's Blood by fulfilling a Major or Defining task as a service, or stealing the Heart's Blood if the second party breaks the agreement.

                                                        Even the "agree to a contest of skill or luck" idea for gaining Heart's Blood seems like a Changing Moon Charm, based on how similar it is to the oath-related Charm. The Lunar is trying to convince someone to consent to a contest of skill or luck, with agreed-upon rules of play and conditions for victory.

                                                        There's no single "Changing Moon hunt" that I can see; each of these applies to one archetypical character concept and not necessarily any of the others.


                                                        What would be a non-lethal method of Heart's Blood Hunting rooted in the Physical Attributes? Killing is the default. Rape instead of killing is right out; it's simultaneously too "easy" and too skeevy. Beating someone unconscious instead of killing is weak-sauce and is more interesting as an application of the "agree to a contest" Hunt.

                                                        At least for Mental Attributes, Robert Vance's "I steal your face by suing you" is Intelligence-based.


                                                        There's also the problem that not all of these Charms are entry-level. Vance's Charm requires you have the Charm for using human Heart's Blood. The oath Charms probably need to start with a Charm that creates an Intimacy to the terms of the oath in the Lunar before the Lunar can learn Charms to derive other benefits from the oaths. Gaining Heart's Blood from familiars and human vassals might follow after the oath Charm as well.
