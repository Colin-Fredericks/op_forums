                            07-31-2018, 06:13 PM
                            A few of the DB ones actually do (I checked with Vance). And a lot of them make Instill or Inspire attempts require a Decision Point to break. That's actually really powerful, because that means they're going to need 2 different intimacies to resist (since one gets used to pump their Resolve). And one of them has to be strong enough to oppose the level of intimacy you're instilling.
                            Normally, Decision Points are only for Persuade, but Persuade requires them to already have an intimacy that supports your intimacy.

                            Whereas, with a DB using Dragon Warlord's Convocation, I walk past some random fisherman, tell him "Come and follow me!" and make an instill roll to give him a Major Tie of loyalty. I make my social roll, and if he doesn't have two intimacies that oppose him getting a Tie of loyalty to me (one of which needs to be major), then if I beat their Resolve there's no way to resist. (And even if he does have 2 opposing intimacies, including a Major one, it's 2wp to resist instead of 1). He'll become my loyal follower, probably abandoning his fishingboat to become my disciple (but it's only Major, so he might abandon me if I get captured and crucified. But up to that he's loyal. Also it's best to use on childless widowers so they don't have Defining Intimacies to their families).

                            Outside of mind-control effects, Solars are actually not great at being able to make social attacks that can't just be ignored for 1wp*. If they've found a way to make such a social attack, they're amazing at it. But they're not that great at getting the opportunity to do so.
                            This is the problem our Zenith has; sure, he can regularly roll 25 successes on his social attacks, but people can just spend 1wp and bounce off the intimacy he's trying to inflict,** and he can't persuade people without them already having an intimacy that supports what he wants.

                            *Fulminating Word lets you raise it to 2. It's one of the best charms in Solar Presence. But it doesn't work on Instill, so doesn't help you Persuade someone who lacks a strong enough intimacy already. And it's expensive if you fail. And for non-mind control, you might be able to use Mastery of Small Manners to treat people as having a minor intimacy to you, but that's pretty much the only way to persuade people when they don't already have appropriate intimacies.

                            **Obviously regular mortals tend not to do this, but at persuading people who are significantly opposing us, it's an issue. Also, trying to convince other PCs to make peace.

                                                Originally posted by prototype00 View Post
                                                Memory-Drinking Mediation
                                                Looking back at this Charm, I realised something interesting and kind of messed up about how it works.

                                                So, you don't only get the memories, you get the Tie that came with them. That becomes a part of your personality, and something that you can be influenced through. There's no reference to it only being when you're in the shape; as long as you're carrying the memories, you'll feel how the person felt about them. You have to, because if you act in a manner inconsistent with that (i.e. allow the Intimacy to decay), you'll lose the memories.

                                                So if you want to pass yourself off as a person that you've just killed, you won't only know about their spouse, children and friends, you'll have the same feelings about them. Apart from what that might do to a Lunar's personality, there's also the moral effect that it might have upon them; it's got to be kind of weird to suddenly fall in love with somebody, have the memory of having loved them for a while, and consider that you've actually murdered somebody that your loved one loved.

                                                And then, what, you're going to abandon him when your interests take you elsewhere? But you love him! I wouldn't find it unbelievable that there are a few Lunars that stole a person's shape and life for some pragmatic or ruthless reason, and then kind of abandoned that reason and spent the length of a mortal lifetime in that position because of the feelings that came with it. How's that for mercurial!

                                                And then from the other side, well, it depends on how strong the Intimacy was natively. It may be that the spouse or children notice a certain cooling in the affections. Perhaps that will improve over time.

                                                This is more horrible than the Solar persona Charms, and I love it. This is the kind of thing that I was talking about wanting when I abandoned the playtest thread.

                                                    I was more focused on the human-only target limitation. I'm inherently not a fan of mortal-target-only social Charms, because it creates an incentive to choose a less-limited alternative. A PC's player likes the idea, hates it only works on mortals, so here comes the Custom Charm for 15m, 1WP that does the same thing without the restriction, Prereq'd by this Charm, and this one becomes naught but a speedbump that's only utility is as a -5m discount when the target is human.

                                                    I'd much rather the developers simply hike the price, and ditch the human-only tag. Taking human forms generally falls into two broad categories during play. 1) Not You: The point isn't who you are, it's simply not being recognized as yourself. Most PCs will keep two, maybe three such everymen/everywomen, and reuse as needed. 2) Specific impersonation: While there is a very significant minority of times the target-to-be-impersonated is human, IME it's a minority compared to the number of times a Dragon Blooded got eaten and replaced, or a Raksha. Supernatural entities tend to be at or near the top of any organization, and they're the ones tending to create the incentive to impersonate. The Guild WOULD be a major exception to this, except for the fact they've spent centuries creating security procedures precisely to prevent this kind of thing.
