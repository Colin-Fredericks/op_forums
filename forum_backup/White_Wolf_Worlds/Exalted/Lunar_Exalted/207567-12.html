The problem is that nobody has actually written about many of them doing anything of note, unless you count retconning "oh these civilizations were actually Lunar projects."         The thing is, your assumptions don't matter to the line as a whole. What matters to the line is good portrayals in accessible, published material. And you want to say "sure the Lunars had several Halta-like places with DB beastmen running around." Let's say this gets picked up and is now a core piece of canon that a writer wants to build something off of. There's things that need to be worked through if that statement becomes canon, the largest being "how does that change history, if at all?" The obvious questions to ask are (1) where is that society now; (2) what happened to it if it's gone; (3) how did it affect the setting in any term of time while it existed; (4) how did the relevant Great Powers at the time the society was formed and grew to power relate to or deal with this place. You can't just say "oh Lunars made a Holy Roman Empire in the Hundred Kingdoms" and leave it at that in the setting we have, because it leaves too many questions unanswered. You can;t just insert giant things into the setting without context; not answering those questions basically means you made assumptions in a white room without considering anything outside the bubble of Lunar Holy Roman Empire, and that itself creates setting consistency issues. Working through those questions shows that the setting has to be tweaked a little, or else all the cool shit Lunars might have done was so far away from the game's main theater that it effectively is irrelevant fluff. Yes, if all that shit happens in the borders of Creation, no matter how cool it is...if it doesn't interact with the rest of the setting, at least its immediate neighbors in some ways...it's not relevant, because it doesn't plug in and try to connect to the world.

        There has yet to be a nation or thing whose base design concept stemmed from "Lunars did this" until the teasers for the Caul in 3e came out. The Lunar NPCs are whiny, uninspiring, and pathetic. Most of the Lunar NPCs that get word count are elders, making them even more unrelatable to the basic player looking for inspiration for a chargen character. Hell most of them just sit around for decades or more just plotting and/or moping. How is that inspiring compared to the NPCs of other splats who are being active instead of sitting in passive stasis? Your assumptions aside, there's nothing about Lunars that makes a player think "this concept should be a Lunar and not something else" unless that concept really revolves around shapeshifting. Because, outside of shapeshifting, Lunars have no theme and nobody has tried to give them a unique theme instead of pulling themes from other splats. Which isn't good for either splat.

                                                Okay, so, back to Outcastes for a bit. There are a few things going on with Outcastes. One is that, yes, there's typically an assumption that they have really poor breeding, partially because this is the connective tissue between what breeding is supposed to mean for Terrestrials and the usual background portrayed for Outcastes, and partially because it's kind of necessary for maintaining consistency in how the setting doesn't look like it would if more Outcaste Dragon Blooded were able to maintain good bloodlines.

                                                I mean, forget about Lunars, or even the Realm for a moment; if more Outcastes were able to build up Exalted numbers from their own descendents, they'd be doing it for themselves; for their own power and position. Whatever one might think is the big Lunar advantage for locating and uniting them across distances of hundreds of miles, I don't think it's so unsurmountable an obstacle that no Outcastes ever encounter one another without it. And yet, by and large, such encounters do not seem to widely produce new miniature dynasties of Exalted; if they did, the setting would look significantly different. Never mind dozens of Haslanti Leagues, there has to be a reason that several hundred miniature Scarlet Dynasties aren't crowding the Realm off the map.

                                                If we assume, as things like the preview of the Wyld Hunt imply, that even in places where the grip of the Realm is weaker, the Immaculate dogma of Dragon Blooded spiritual superiority is a pretty far-reaching concept, that would seem to be the thing that most Terrestrials would be more inclined to turn their own families to; not serving the interests of a Lunar, no matter how polite and respectful the Lunar is. Hell, association with a Lunar probably makes their prospects actively terrible, considering that alliance with the Anathema is the one, singular thing upon which the Realm will never budge in its conditions for satrapies, and general diplomatic relations. They'll tolerate failure to pay your taxes in full before they will the idea that you're doing stuff with a Lunar.

                                                That actually, that's probably worth putting ahead of the idea that the Realm has stronger incentives to recruit Dragon Blooded than Lunars do; like, it's one thing to offer them opportunities and riches, people can accept or reject that as suits them, many people in Creation will dislike the Realm such that they won't accept offers. But throwing in your lot with Lunars will lead to the Realm actively making war on and trying to destroy you, and that's a whole 'nother thing besides. If a Lunar's going to try and recruit some Outcastes, they'll need to make an offer that greatly outweighs what you're sacrificing to become an active target of the Realm.

                                                Still, one way or another, some Outcastes are likely to end up with Lunars; they're a pretty diverse, but fairly mercenary lot. But if we're going for the idea that a Lunar wants to take these Outcastes and breed a force sufficient to challenge the Realm with them... let's just assume that Outcaste breeding is ever good enough to manage this on a reasonable time scale, does the Realm just... not notice this? Is bringing up a sizable number of Dragon Blooded, especially in service to an agenda based on opposing the most powerful nation in the world, an easy thing to hide? Even if we assume that the Lunar stays around to guard the children, is the Realm not up to overwhelming a priority threat who has to stay in one place? Or is the Lunar continuously on the move to stay ahead of the legions and the Wyld Hunt, and hoping that these are that extremely durable kind of human baby? How do you get away with something like this long enough to pull it off?

                                                I don't even know what "breeding Charms" Lunars are supposed to bring to the mix to make any kind of difference.
