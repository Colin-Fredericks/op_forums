                        So, reading some of the earlier 5 pages of this thread, someone suggested a re-write of the Lunar's fluff got me to thinking to a re-write to the whole setting of my own:

                        So, changes:

                        Both the Lunars and Solars absolutely vanish from Creation come the Usurpation. [2e: Not only do the Sidereals manage to lock up most of the Solars shards into the Jade Prison, but they contrive a means to get the Lunar shards to dick off into the Wyld, for like, ever. / 3e: When Sol Invictus turned his face from Creation in shame, Luna lost interest herself, a bit fed up with this bullshit too.] [2e: When the Jade Prison broke, it lit up like a beacon across Everything, and led the Lunar shards slowly back to Creation. / 3e: When Sol finally turned his favor back to Creation, Luna's attention began to wax back as well.]

                        The Solars returned all in one go. It was just bam, Solars are back. [2e-ish: Not only that, their return reawakened a few Eldar Solars from suspended animation, who fled into a deep sleep hidden from the gaze of Fate. With the Solars returning, they're interested in getting the world back on track with the help of all these younglings. To aid them, they've reactivated old dormant infrastructures that they had left behind, but remain functioning, such as cults or somesuch, after countless generations, awaiting the return of their lord, ancient arsenals hidden beneath the earth or old pacts that even now hold water.] Lunars have been trickling in, one by one, their coming unnoticed, unheralded. Unlike the Solars, they've been largely forgotten, not even remembered in twisted tales of Immaculate propaganda. They're god-blooded or something as far as anyone else is concerned. [2e-ish: And Elder Lunars have followed the beacon back as well after their long banishment to the Wyld. They don't have any infrastructure, but they're singular power houses all on their own.]

                            Reposting some thoughts I had on Lunar Exalted before the release of 3rd Edition:



                            I've been thinking that the Lunar Exalted may be intended to cater to a specific human fantasy: To let loose.

                            But true to the themes of the game, it's also about the consequences others have to face when you have the power to let loose and get away with it.


                            Lunars don't have Socialize or Bureaucracy magic. Nothing which allows them to exert fine control over a society or organization of great size and complicated structure. They can make big impacts, but those impacts are rough and destabilizing.

                            The more that the Moon-Beasts find themselves surrounded by such things, the more they need to act with care, and the more power other Exalted can bring to bear against them.

                            But if Lunar Exalted can cause trouble carelessly, they can cause even more damage deliberately. They are perhaps uniquely suited for tearing apart order and introducing chaos, as well as being uniquely suited for thriving within sudden chaos.

                            The question is: Do you care for all of the little people who can't handle it?

                            A Lunar within a Solar's kingdom must decide if he cares whether or not the waves he makes will capsize the mortals around him.


                            Those were my thoughts before learning about the new Lunar focus on "freedom", but I think that "to let loose (destructively)" certainly fits.

                            At any time, a Lunar trapped in a net and sinking into the waters can transform into an enormous whale. He breaks free of the ropes and becomes at home in the sea, but the sudden displacement of water caused by his new mass threatens to capsize the mortals around him. Is that what he wants, or does he need to restrain his full might because he cares about them?

                            If Lunar Charms capitalize on that idea of "my full power is dangerous for those around me", then it brings an internal tension to the Lunar narrative of freedom. The Lunar must always be asking himself how much his personal freedom is worth, and how many restrictions he is willing to accept for infrastructural support and the assistance of allies.

                                            Having looked back at the core write-up for the Dragon Blooded, I wonder if they might build off of Anima Flux now and make the idea that their Elemental Essence is a wild, dangerous thing that needs to be tamed evident throughout more of their powers.

                                            ​The idea that there can be something dangerously unrestrained about Lunars feels as though it may be fitting to how they're currently being written and how the developers have referenced them for years, but I think it would need to rest on something more substantive than the volume displacement incurred by turning into something large.

                                            ​It might be something that comes through in their version of the Great Curse. Wyld Taint and Second Edition chimerism had issues, but I think there could have still been potential in the idea of getting Limit from your own powers.

                                            ​I know First Edition didn't have the same kinds of chimeras, but did it still have something to the tattoos besides fixing one's Caste? I think that even without chimerism, there might be something to the tattoos helping to stabilise their tumultuous personalities this run around, given a couple of things Holden has said.

                                            ​Part of what I thought was wasted potential in Wyld Taint was that it was wholly irrelevant to most playable characters, so I think and would like the results of a similar thing to be muted even with the tattoos. Something like the untattooed Lunar risking Limit for a broader use of powers and perhaps indulgences in Intimacies, whereas the Lunar with tattoos restricts this to only particular uses of power or other Intimate activity. You get caught in a net and have a number of choices for how to escape, but choosing the big, flashy, violent one nets you a bit of Limit, all building up to a Break which is less refined than that of Solars. Possibly Bonding themselves to something (Solars, the destruction of the Realm, some personal cause) could interact with this, although I couldn't say if it would be to create an avenue safe from ever building up Limit or that provides a means of losing it or even just being the focused target of it (i.e. whatever might cause you to Limit Break, your fury is directed at the Realm).

                                                            Lunars are my favorite splat as well, setting and story wise at least.

                                                            I think that Lunar storytelling can benefit from two ideas.

                                                            First, is that of the deuteragonist role in a story. Not to say second-fiddle (second-fiddle storytelling really chaps my backside, no matter the setting), but as the heroes whose heroic journey takes them into a variety of set-piece situations. They resolve the situation and the story arc by becoming part of it - making it also their own - and finding the solution from within.

                                                            For example, a Lunar Pack might travel to an area beset by strife among several factions. Where a Solar Circle might find direct opposition to themselves and their own goals, the Pack would use Charms and Influence to adapt into the conflict and it's participants. The Pack's resolution of the conflict would have them as the ones who move the story and are the focus of the story, but the story arc remains "about" the set-piece conflict.

                                                            I suspect a lot of stories take this shape naturally, anyway! The trick would be to keep from turning the heroes into mere spectators or sidekicks.

                                                            The other idea that seems very Lunar to me is to focus on being rather than doing. (See this page , about 1/3 down, regarding personal style)

                                                            The Lunar adapts to the environment, to the scene, to the story, to the conflict. At each story arc, the Lunar *agonist becomes something more than he had been before.

                                                            In a story arc where a Solar would go to discover a secret of world-shaking sorcery to complete the story (perhaps to destroy an army or to lift a vast curse), the Lunar would go to hunt and eat that secret. The Solar's climactic scene would be to cast the spell, while the Lunar's climactic scene would be to become the spell, hurling themselves into the army or into the dark heart of the curse.

                                                            The Solar hero rides away (sometimes literally), leaving behind tales of their glory. The Lunar hero carries forever the tales and glories that they've accumulated.
