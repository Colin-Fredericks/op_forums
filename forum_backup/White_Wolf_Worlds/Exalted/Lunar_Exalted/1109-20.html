                                                12-10-2013, 09:53 PM
                                                This begins to verge into 'Assumptions of Competence' and 'Assumptions of Incomptence' territory for me.

                                                I prefer to see competence and incompetence more evenly distributed.

                                                The Sidereals have had issues with Assumptions of Competence for some time. Part of this is due to the way the archetype of the 'Gandalf' character is constructed ; later, we got elegant mechanics that tried to explain how such a character could exist and still be... fallible, or interesting.

                                                Unfortunately, when the history of Exalted was written, those mechanics did not exist.

                                                So we get Sidereals as strangely infallible masterminds, the 'Illuminati' of the First Age, always one step ahead and never caught unawares. Gandalf is just too awesome for that!

                                                The problem is, that 'short hand' didn't really mesh when the rest of the game was described... PARTICULARLY, when we saw the Sidereals more in depth and understood exactly how they functioned. The Great Prophesy was written in a time before the 'Player Agency vs Fate' question was even seriously considered. Contemplate that. Back then, it literally was just, 'They tell the future somehow. It works.'

                                                In order for the Usurpation to work, you need to assume the Sidereals were always exceptionally competent, and those they slaughtered were always exceptionally incompetent.
