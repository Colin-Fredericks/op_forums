    Also, aside, if I were redesigning Sorcery I would probably change a number of things that would help with this. Specifically:

    1. Instead of each tier of Sorcery being limited to a specific Essence level, they start at E1, E2, and E3 respectively; but individual spells can then have higher Essence. This allows SCS to come up in an actual game more often, but it also allows the Lunar to keep learning more powerful spells past E3 (since you can now make E4 and E5 Celestial spells.) The bulk of SCS would still be at E5. Granted, coming up with high-essence Celestial or Terrestrial spells could be extremely difficult; generally they wouldn't increase in power on their own quite the way that charms do. Instead, high essence on non-Solar Circle Sorcery would be used for...

    2. Allow Sorcery to, like Evocations, have prerequisite charms (as well as spells.) This allows the creation of spells that can only be used by a specific splat (as well as spells that can only be used if you've mastered a specific martial art, or even spells that require specific evocations of a specific artifact.) This would be what high-essence Celestial and Terrestrial spells would be for - you don't get an inherent power boost for an E5 Terrestrial Spell like you do with a charm, but you could use an E5 DB charm as a prerequisite, which would let you bend the limitations of Terrestrial-tier Sorcery slightly by piggybacking on whatever that charm does.

    Then the answer of what unique thematic / mechanical ground a Lunar Sorcerer gets becomes easy, since they can make unique Lunar spells branching off of Lunar charms.

            Originally posted by Verzio View Post
            The following is one part my impression of what the 3e devs/writers/editors (former and current) have been saying, nine parts my opinion on how they should execute things:

            Human greatness (and the pitfalls thereof) is the Solar theme. If the core of your character's identity is a profession or a D&D character class or the like, your character concept is a Solar, and trying to do it with a Lunar/Sidereal/ won't work as well. At the moment in Creation the Solars are only newly-returned, so they aren't currently sitting at the top of the heap, but rest assured that unless they get put back in the bottle, they will. If you're playing a non-Solar whose current identity is "the best sorcerer", part of the (canon) story of Exalted is that you've been served notice that your days on top are numbered. If you're playing a non-Solar whose ambition is to be "the best sorcerer", part of your (canon) character's concept is that character will not realize that ambition.

            Other types of Exalted (in context of the line) have identities that are something other than their profession or "character class". So, "Lunar sorcerer", per se, isn't a valid character concept (in the line as a whole). The reason you play a Lunar sorcerer is that you've got a Lunar character concept that is supported by being an initiated sorcerer (or becaue you're playing a Lunars game, of course; again "in the line as a whole"). Lunars might have some unique bits to how they interact with sorcery, but that wouldn't be there to make them better at sorcery, but to make their sorcery better at supporting a Lunar character concept.
            The thing is, though, that both Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded do have unique features that aren't really reproducible under the auspices of "Human Greatness", and for a Solar to even attempt them would require a maximum-Ambition, Solar-Circle Working and a very permissive ST. Sidereals can manipulate the underlying rules of existence, allowing them to work at a remove that even the mightiest sorcerer would be hard-pressed to imitate. A lot of their charms in previous editions let them declare an outcome by fiat, and while a Solar could accomplish the same thing through hard work and perseverance, they'd have to be physically-present and doing hard work, exposing them to risk and requiring them to invest a lot of time and effort that the Siderereal can spend doing other things. A Solar can learn Sidereal Martial Arts, but they cannot teach them, meaning that if a Solar wants access they have to actually come before a Sid as an inferior. On a narrative level, only Sidereals can maintain the Loom of Fate, without which we've been assured everything would go to shit. Sidereals have a niche.

            Dragon-Blooded are individually-weaker than Solars, and still can't access the highest circle of Sorcery, but they absolutely can cheat their way up a tier in both Sorcery and MA (yes, both Mnemon and the Empress used powerful artifacts to accomplish this, but the fact they managed it at all is a powerful precedent). In prior editions, the Immaculate styles were really fucking powerful, to the point of being straight upgrades over their native charms and frankly over a lot of other Celestial styles. Neither of these put them on a level with Solars, but that didn't matter because they had something else that very much did - their ability to make more Dragon-Blooded. NONE of the other Exalted types can manage something like this, and it has huge ramifications for the rest of the setting, starting with the fact that all the Dragon-Blooded working together managed to kill the Solars and conquer the world. A Dragon-Blooded Sorcerer who graduates from the Heptagram may be overworked and underappreciated by the rest of Dynastic society, but they have access to resources that the returned Solars can only dream of. If a Dragon-Blooded Sorcerer wants to put together a really ambitious project, they have a vast social network to call on, including lots of other sorcerers - hello, extra Means! A Twilight is lucky to run across one other Sorcerer who isn't actively trying to kill them.

            In Second Edition, No-Moons also absolutely had something that Solars couldn't replicate (until Glories came out, but eh, the supplement treadmill is a harsh mistress). They could cast spells more-cheaply, which enabled them to spam battle-magic like nobody else. They couldn't cast the strongest spells, no, but they had something unique. And honestly, that's at the heart of this issue, which is really less about Lunar Sorcerers than Lunars in general. Their signature ability, shapechanging, is ultimately limited to facilitating things that they do personally, and so they are always going to fall short of the Solars in just about every respect. They're alone in lacking anything that sets them apart from Solars beyond aesthetics. Maybe 3E will change that! I really hope so! But as the situation stands, I've seen more cases where Solar PCs decide to side with the Dragon-Blooded against the Lunars than the other way around, which suggests to me that something has gone seriously wrong with the execution.

                Originally posted by Thesaurasaurus View Post
                In Second Edition, No-Moons also absolutely had something that Solars couldn't replicate (until Glories came out, but eh, the supplement treadmill is a harsh mistress). They could cast spells more-cheaply, which enabled them to spam battle-magic like nobody else. They couldn't cast the strongest spells, no, but they had something unique.
                Ok, here's why that was a terrible idea and why Glories was absolutely 100% correct to give it to Solars (although it's a path that shouldn't have been started down in the first place and was really only begun because 2e Sorcery had so many problems that you could pretty much give people whatever you want.)

                1. Solars only get SCS access at E5. If you go that route, Twilights - supposed to be the greatest Sorcerers in Creation - are worse at Sorcery until E5.

                2. In practice, "meta-magic" charms are difficult to balance around. They're something that has to be balanced into the spell system from the start.

                3. The charms did absolutely nothing uniquely Lunar about it. They were just "generically better at Sorcery." This would be perhaps appropriate for a Solar charm, but unless your premise is "Lunars are better at all Sorcery except the Solar tier" or "Lunars are better at combat sorcery", it doesn't work.

                4. "Lunars are flatly better at combat sorcery" is not an acceptable statement. Combat makes up a huge portion of the game and, yes, massive swaths of Sorcery itself. Beyond that, the usual problems with "better at Solars at [narrow thing within the Solar purview" applies here - once you start peeling off aspects of Solar excellence like that and doling them out to other splats, Solars very, very rapidly become the "jack-of-all-trades, master-of-none" Exalted. (Why shouldn't Sidereals then say "all right, we claim preeminence at subtle, manipulative Sorcery", and DBs say "ok, we'll be the best at Elemental Sorcery", etc?) There are clearly-defined limits to the Solar theme; if you want to do stuff Solars don't, you can go there. If I want to be the very best, like no one ever was is absolutely, completely central to your character concept, play a Solar. That's part of what they're there for.

                The primary advantage a No-Moon has over a Solar, in terms of unique stuff to explore, is that they've got an innate capability for shapeshifting. Their charmset might find some other interesting directions to go in or synergies with their other stuff (and one problem that Lunars have - the reason we keep coming to this argument with Lunars and not with other splats - is that they really don't have much outside of shapeshifting), but they absolutely shouldn't get their own special "generic" Sorcery boosts.

                (Of course, an additional thing to consider is that in 3e, Sorcery-boosting is generally occupied by initiations and sorcerous evocations. I would expect Lunars to have initiations they tend to use and teach each other - but those could also be learned by a Solar if someone was willing to teach them, because that's how Sorcery works. And they're not generally going to be uniquely superior to the initiations found elsewhere, although they might be better at eg. working well with shapeshifting or exploiting other unique aspects of the Lunar existence.)

                Giving Lunars uniquely Lunar stuff that interacts with Sorcery is fine; making them best at a broad swath of Sorcery is not. And the corebook very pointedly didn't have any charms to directly enhance Sorcery, so I'll be pissed if we start seeing them in other splats (and would expect it to be an oversight that would be rectified by adding Solar sorcery-enhancing charms in a later book. Because, again, SCS only shows up at E5, and doesn't even appear in most games, so if everyone else starts getting broad sorcery-boosting charms and Solars don't, the result is, most of the time, in most games, that Twilights will be comparatively bad at Sorcery.)

                An E1 Twilight needs to be able to express the thematic of "master of Sorcery" every bit as thoroughly and uniquely as an E1 Dawn expresses the thematic of "master of swordsmanship." Right now they're already struggling to do that. Giving everyone else Sorcery-boosting charms would turn the situation into a joke.

                                Yeah, but my point is, when it comes to Sorcery in particular, it's very hard to give Lunars anything at all that makes them better at it in some unique way, when Solars have... basically nothing that makes them better at spellcasting in any way, outside of SCS access (which, while awesome, is only available at E5), occasional free Occult excellencies, and some indirect benefit from spirit interaction stuff. I think that that sucks for the reasons I outlined above and was a general mistake that makes it hard to feel like you're playing a dedicated Sorcerer, but I'll also be mildly pissed if it's corrected by adding Sorcery-boosting charms to other splats (even well-thought out, carefully-made ones that benefit Lunars in specifically Lunar ways) without eventually going back and giving Solar Sorcerers something they can use to feel like they're focused on Sorcery outside of having a bunch of spells.

                                That's also why I'd prefer that such Sorcery stuff be only "soft"-bound to Lunars (eg. via initiations that can theoretically be taught to outsiders, or moonsilver sorcerer-focused artifacts that could also be used by others). Because given the near-absence of charms that enhance spellcasting in the core, the gap between "giving Lunars some stuff that makes their Sorcery unique in an interesting way" and "Lunars are now mostly better spellcasters than Solars up until E5" is vanishingly slim. (Unless you make the charms E5, but then... what's the point? Like SCS, they'd be mostly unavailable.) The charms would have to be really weak or really specific, and it's pretty hard to make spellcasting-booster charms in a balanced fashion in the first place (probably why the core lacks them, of course.)

                                This could be avoided if Solars had a handful of charms to boost spellcasting in their own way, which would then create wriggle room to give other splats things that range up to that point or which provide their own thematic take in different directions. But that's not where we are right now. And yeah, I know what you meant, but the reason I had to point it out is because we are still pretty close to where we were in 2e in that respect (ie. a potential failing of the Solar charmset that might get fixed in other places without getting fixed for Solars.)

                                This isn't a problem with, like, Lunar Melee, because Solars have completely awesome unique Melee charms, which lets you give Lunars ridiculously powerful combat charms of their own (including ones applicable to Melee) without much risk of disturbing the Solar narrative; or even with Martial Arts, since that has much more synergy with things like Solar Athletics or Solar Resistance.

                                I mean I mostly agree with your analysis of what the Lunar problem is. But with Sorcery in particular, it's compounded by the fact that at least as presented in the core, 3e Sorcery leaves very little wriggle room for unique splat-specific advantages to spellcasting unless we want to conclude that Solars are uniquely bad at the act of casting spells.
