                                    Originally posted by Kleptomania View Post
                                    I think you broke Holden.

                                    To be honest, it kind of makes a small bit of sense, but only if you squint -really- hard.
                                    Even then, the Solars would probably still be the 'superiors' anyway as far as military rank is concerned, as they would likely still be the ones in command, but the Lunars might have been the ones at the forefront of their armies. The Solars lead, and the Lunars champion their cause as the most indispensable weapon in their arsenal.
                                    Or something like that anyway.
                                    In the past, we've sometimes discussed how if you take things the Solars are supposed to be good at and say "but XYZ is universally better, because they're ultra-focused on it!", you end up turning Solars into jack-of-all-trades, master-of-none Exalted, which is certainly not what they're intended to be. This sometimes comes up when people say "well, Lunars should be better at stealth or disguise" or "Alchemicals should be better at Craft" or "Sidereals should be better scholars."

                                    But this the first time I've seen someone put "wrecking shit" in that category.

                                    (Not really; people suggest it all the time for Abyssals.)

                                    Exalted's Exalt types aren't character classes. They're all intended to be complete and enough to approach the game on their own, especially Solars, without feeling like you're missing something important. I think that this cuts to the heart of what people are sometimes asking for when they compare types of Exalted and when they ask loaded questions like "but was XYZ really needed in the Primordial War?" Because what they're really asking -- the important question, anyway -- is "are Lunars really needed to play the game? Do they serve a necessary role in a circle?" And the answer to that is a definite and resounding no; that's not how the game is made at all. If you have a Lunar in your circle, it means that someone wants to tell a Lunar story, not that you needed them for some hypothetical Lunar Niche that only they can fill best, like a cleric in D&D.

                                    Of course this is also true for everyone else; you don't need Solars in your circle unless you're aiming to tell Solar stories. But it's sort of an undercurrent I feel is present in a lot of the questions people ask about Lunars.

                                    (This is also part of why Lunars can't be absolutely everything, because you have to be able to answer "what is a Lunar story?" in some way -- it can be really really broad, but it needs a useful answer. And you have to answer it in a way which fits into the setting, which means "be better than the Dawn at swording people" can't be your story. You might do it, with an individual Lunar -- there's no rule saying the best swordsman in the world can't be a Lunar, though it might be an uphill fight -- but you can't give Lunars as a whole a core story incompatible with the Solar narrative like that, for the same reason you can't call absolutely everyone the Greatest Martial Artist Ever. The setting has to hold together as a whole, and some things people want don't work for that without eg. changing Solars.)

                                    I mean, while 3e seems to be giving them something, you wouldn't take "Lunars breed beastmen" and say that Lunars have used it to breed a mighty empire that is more powerful than the Realm; that would be dumb and wouldn't fit into the setting. Saying "Lunars are simply better at murdering shit than Dawns, as part of the core definition of what a Lunar is" runs into the same problem.

                                    Anyway, that aside, TAW does try to answer "what is a Lunar story?" to an extent... but I think that part of the reason people call them Silver Infernals is because they feel that that overarching story (even more than the individual charms) resembles the Infernal one.

                                                I disagree that's an apt analogy, although I can see how you got that impression. Mea culpa.

                                                Although a certain amount of insight is involved, in that sometimes a good chef just knows that adding a pinch of this or that will make a dish even better, I would prefer to view it as a professional chef knowing how many seasonings can fit into one dish, and when they want to make larger dishes, or a larger spread of dishes, to accommodate more ingredients.                                                 Originally posted by Kleptomania View Post
                                                I think you broke Holden.
                                                That broke me when I first read it, and I helped write TAW. I did a double-take when I saw that.

                                                Seriously, where the hell did that come from? TAW backstory doesn't compare Lunars and Solars much at all, honestly, I'm baffled where the idea that it implies they were better than Solars comes from, and I'm curious where God_of_Awesome got that idea.

                                                What I've always understood TAW's to be aiming for, and what I've tried to cleave to whenever I'm at the helm, is as people who are distinctly lagging behind in certain grand-scale areas like nation-shaping and crafting, in exchange for being just about equal to Solars in the arena of wrecking shit on a personal scale - which is a huge freaking deal, because equaling Solars at anything is a pretty big deal.

                                                Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
                                                Anyway, that aside, TAW does try to answer "what is a Lunar story?" to an extent... but I think that part of the reason people call them Silver Infernals is because they feel that that overarching story (even more than the individual charms) resembles the Infernal one.
                                                I'm also curious about this, because I don't see it.

                                                The Infernal story in 2e, as I understand it, is heavily bound up in irrevocable transformation of the self and of rejecting the alien masters who recruited you with honeyed lies, then rolling around in Creation bending strange and disturbing cosmic power towards good ends.

                                                There's some overlap with TAW's there, yes, in that TAW's also deal in bending strange and frightening powers towards hopefully good ends, but that's hardly an Infernal only thing. Abyssal heroes have been doing it even longer, and heck, Sidereals would probably deal in it a lot more if their powers were less subtle. The notion of struggling with inhuman and not entirely benign power is a piece of storycraft with fairly deep roots, all told.

                                                Meanwhile, TAW's don't have any alien masters to reject - their closest analogues would be the Deliberative-era elders, but those are written up as antagonists from the word go, and while TAW's do have a transformation angle, it's much more like putting on a new suit - it's a fluid, mercurial change that can be discarded, in contrast to the irreversible mutation of the self that Infernals deal in.
