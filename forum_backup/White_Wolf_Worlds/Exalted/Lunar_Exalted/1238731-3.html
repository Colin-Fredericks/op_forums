                        The game runs without the ability to select with Attributes you use for attacks as a default thing everyone can do, instead of a special thing only Exalts can do with Charms. It doesn't need a band-aid.

                        Don't confuse, "I'd rather things worked this way, so I want to house rule it," with, "there is an objective flaw in the system."

                        Secondly it doesn't matter if you're using Storypath or not.
                        Storypath is built from the ground up with the expectations that characters will be allowed flexibility in Attribute assignment. That's why Storypath generally avoids "Attribute" or "Skill" powers, and even things like Epic Strength in Scion 2e has applications when you're not actually rolling Strength.

                        Exalted is built from the ground up with the expectations that characters do not have much flexibility with Attribute assignment. Given that Exalted uses Attributes and Abilities as the basis for Charm structures, there's a solid reason to maintain it.

                        Nothing will break.
                        Great. That still hasn't demonstrated that there's a flaw in the first place; just a design decision you don't like, and you can get around with house rules.

But this also applies to every Attribute. Strength 5 lets you do Strength based stuff without the appropriate Ability on par with what most towns best people can do. Appearance 5 will let you out social 99% of mortals in Creation even if you don't take any social Abilities (worse here, because you'll get the high App bonus, not just a good base dice poll by normal folk standards).

                                                            Not really... and we're talking a mighty 2 dice difference here. This doesn't account for damage, soak (or lasting power in general for boxing which doesn't really work well under the base combat rules), footwork, etc.

                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            Having watched some footage of George Foreman fighting, I'll admit that to my extremely layman's eye, he does make something of a case for throwing punches that don't look like the fastest/least telegraphed that I've ever seen, but appear to have a lot of weight behind them.

                                                            Irrespective of this subject, I actually want to go and look for anything that might analyse his style.
                                                            Foreman was a KO artist for the majority of his career (he adapted later during his comebacks as his age forced him to adjust his approach).

                                                            His general style involved hitting people as hard as possible as soon as possible. While Foreman could take hits, he didn't have the best endurance, and his loses tended to involve longer fights.

                                                            He tended to struggle with two types of opponents: good counter-punchers that could take his hits and return them to wear him down (basically how Ali beat him in at the Rumble in the Jungle), and aggressive more nimble fighters that could get hits in to throw his offense off kilter.

                                                            It's important, when watching boxing, to remember the sport aspect of it. Weight classes skew perceptions because members of the same weight class look very close in terms of things like speed and all, but it's because you're seeing them in comparison to each other, not compared to everyday folks. As well, there are how scoring influences how boxers are trained. Boxers need to throw a bunch of "showy" hits that might not actually do much to impress the judges. Boxers throw a lot of punches in a bout, and scoring if the fight goes the distance is largely about perception of control over the fight, and landing 'big' hits. You want to throw a good number of "slow" punches so the judges see them, otherwise your ten body shots that are really wearing your opponent down might not mean anything.
