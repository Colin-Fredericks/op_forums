    Originally posted by Omicron View Post
    The Lunars are good at building stuff. I mean, they don't particularly have a big overarching theme of "great builders," but neither do Dragon-Bloods or Sidereals.

    They don't particularly want to build stuff most of the time, because it usually ends in them being ousted by Dragon-Blooded forces who then turn this infastructure to their advantage. So they favor installations that can be quickly destroyed or taken with them when they need to leave.

    They also have the issue that they don't have access to nearly as much 'prerequisite groundwork' - for instance, they tend to be unable to create the stable, entrenched societies that give rise to cultural elites from whom they could draw elite engineers, architects, or scientists that they could then put to work on building Manses or creating Artifacts, or to durably secure large deposits of jade.

    What's important to remember is that none of this is due to an inherent brutisheness inherent to the Lunars' natures - it is due to being constantly hounded and persecuted by the most powerful empire in the world.

    Lunars would certainly love to stop and build their Lunar version of the Heptagram. The Wyld Hunt won't let them.

    Though note, this portrayal is getting caveats and nuances in Third Edition. The Caul, people!
    Actually, both the Dragon-Blooded and Sidereal are regularly portrayed as being the builders of organizations, and the caretakers of organizations. Lunars in 1E were all about tearing stuff down. In 2E, they were a little more nuanced, in which they tear stuff down, and then teach people to be free and live independently. A few try to build up societies, but some are incompetent like that Tiger in the West, and others are extremely competent, and manipulate from the Shadows, like Sidereals. Others make beastmen tribes and lead them.

    I really wish it was different, but the general Lunar theme is tearing stuff down, and breaking stuff down, and hiding mystic secrets away so people can't get them. There are a couple of exceptions... but they aren't exactly filling. Lunars need some work. Lunars need to have been doing something other than building barbarian tribes since the 1st Age.

                        The 3e direction for Lunars will be what it will be, reading the last few pages, with the Lunars, I feel like my preference for how the operate, and why they don't accumulate as much stuff (manses, weapons, libraries), generally, *could* potentially be about mobility and flexibility, rather than sabotage or terrorism or an eventual advantage in a stone age world or persecution, as such.

                        Mobility is a major advantage, whole peoples being able to migrate to where the enemy is weakest, to migrate and disperse in the face of threats. Mobility makes sense as well, when I think about how the Lunars could operate in the Threshold, moving around as patterns of Fair Folk assault on the world change, as new resources to gather and trade back into the world emerge from the Wyld, Creation's ecology and human culture.

                        I'd also say that, when comparing the Dragonblooded and Lunars, I feel like it might be worth saying that the majority of people in the Dragonblooded and Lunar cultures are not productive. They mostly produce what they themselves use, or they extract from others what they want. It is only a fraction within the Dragonblooded society that really build, and that fraction only because they are not as mobile as the Lunars are.

                        Like if you think about the settled world vs the Eurasian steppe, in history, only a vanishly small fraction of the settled world indulged in philosophy, learning, moving the world's understanding of the sciences forward. Those small fraction of people could not easily exist in the Eurasian steppe, yet not only that, the Eurasian steppe still proved to be a conduit for knowledge and trade that moved the world forwards.

                        (In our world, of course, the "barbarians" were in the centre and "civilization" at the periphery, whatever the Chinese or Romans thought about it, while Exalted inverts this!).

                        I do feel like you need an element where the "Lunar strategy" (whatever we might thing about how tight / loose the Lunars are as a people) is working in a way that doesn't purely "diminish" (in some sense) the grandeur and sophistication of the world, and where that isn't the aim of their strategy. Just a preference.

                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                            I played a Haltan character traveling in the Scavvy Lands once. He found the cities unfortunately kind of grubby, bleak, uncivilized places. And also strangely two-dimensional.
                            I think that's awesome! That's certainly a great way of looking at things.

                            Here's the thing: Halta always came across like an unbelievable twee hippie paradise, a place where people have Weird Hair Just Because and are as dextrous with their feet as with their hands Just Because, kind of like someone took a bucket of Changeling: the Dreaming and dumped it all over a region and turned it into kind of a Disney Princess fantasy setting where animals are on the same level as people and work jobs and make friends with people and can vote. You have a gigantic-beyond-all-scale nation out in the boonies that's a constitutional monarchy (already something very weird in an age of despots, juntas, and absolute monarchs). The size is a problem because it brings up all kinds of logistical issues; you might say "well, only part of that is Halta proper, the rest are protectorates and regions occupied by allied tribes" and that's a cool explanation but that is trying to explain away the pre-existing problem that someone did a bad job on a map. You're saying it makes sense for the greatest military power in Creation, organized in a fashion that is meant to evoke the Han Chinese or Japan, is the same size as something that seems to draw imagery from the Iroquois League With Animals and it doesn't really make that much sense when you look at it closely.

                            This was all its own thing well before someone came along and said "you know what, Lunars really haven't done enough, so...Lunars founded Halta." So anything you found cool about Halta (and there was cool stuff there, don't get me wrong) was probably an established Thing about it before Silver Python and Rain were tacked on. And they were tacked on kind of pointlessly!

                            Back in 1e, the Lunar book map (which was kind of a depiction of territories and song lines) had "Tamuz Walks Here" in the area near Chiaroscuro and it's not a huge leap thinking the legendary Ka-Khan of the Delzahn was some kind of Exalt; that retcon wasn't a huge stretch. The Haslanti League was not implied to be a Lunar thing, but Gerd's backstory and taking on the Haslanti as a "project" always seemed better integrated than Halta did. The Lunar parts of Haltsa just seemed like a response to the fandom's "the Lunars never do anything" complaint.

                                    OK, it's seriously only too big because someone did not grok the map scale at all. The fluff doesn't even support its size.

                                    Maybe that's one of the problems with it. It's a weird, progressive culture in a world that is varying degrees of the Bronze Age. As you put it, it's supposed to be as cosmopolitan as the Realm. It feels a lot like the Tau in the 40k setting: in a universe where the factions include "humans who don't really understand their technology and have ritualized it religiously," "aliens whose civilization went through apocalypse," "amoral space bugs drawn to the thing that makes human space travel possible," "traitors to humanity who consider themselves free but are just as backwards," and "ancient robots who awaken to find their empire gone and lesser races squabbling over the spoils," the Tau are a Great White Hope, an up-and-coming race that is progressing technologically rather than regressing, that comes across as Good Socialists who will conquer a human world and let them keep worshiping the Emperor, in a universe of morally-grey autocrats (if the Imperium conquered a Tau world it would torture and then publicly execute anyone professing loyalty to the Greater Good). It feels kind of out of place in the setting milieu, and that's why (I think) the authors started breaking that perfect facade and giving them problems, breakaway factions, and secret morally grey things they do (like secret forced sterilization of Imperial populations) - to make them fit better in the universe they exist in.

                                    There's something like that in Halta. It's a constitutional republic in a world where the closest thing to that is a confederation of otherwise-autocratic or military-controlled nations (or Nexus, which is basically quasi-anarchic in a way that works). It's cosmopolitan and enlightened, but it's not located on trade routes so the question becomes "why, other than for its own sake?" It has trained sciences without really any explanation for the breakthroughs other than A Lunar Did It: by this I mean, harvesting naturally-existing bioluminescent mushrooms or whatever for light makes sense, as does creating colonies of them for your needs, but it seems like Halta straight-up engineered them from non-glowly fungus or whatever.

                                    The tree-city conceit is indeed admittedly cool, as is the alliance with the Fair Folk (and the local Fair Folk having their own little internecine issues). The "evergreen versus deciduous" is a great concept for an eternal religious war. But to me an enjoyable Halta should evoke more...I dunno really. You have opportunities for Iroquois vs. Algonquin narratives with the Linowan except it does not really play out that way. The Linowan are made to look particularly brutish and savage compared to the Haltans (in other words, the depiction of the Haltans seems to be written to give them the moral high ground in that war because they're more advanced socially and therefore more worthy of existence).

                                    Originally posted by TSk View Post

                                    Maybe that's what bothers me about it. The Delzahn are clearly inspired by Arabian stories, with a cool twist to the modern culture public modesty that is kind of the inversion of Islamic interpretations. The Haslanti make me think about the Vendel/Vesten from 7th Sea - a Norse culture, considered barbarians but with a rather technological twist (hot-air balloon airships reminding me a lot of the dirigibles in His Dark Materials). Halta doesn't have a good real-world parallel for its themes, so it seems alien, but in a kitschy Disney Princess way.

                                    From the start, Halta had notions that "this couldn't happen on its own, a Lunar Had To Do It" but it never out-and-out said that. And then it made it explicit. Adding Lunars to Chiaroscuro and Haslanti adds a little bit to them but you could see those societies existing as purely mortal, without Exalted interference. Halta doesn't. It doesn't really pan to any real-world society for influence (which is OK I guess). If any Eastern nation feels kind of Lunar-ish in the same way that the Haslanti and Delzahn do, to me it's the Linowan (because it's feasible but doesn't whack you over the head with the implication).

                                    The Realm is considered cosmopolitan and enlightened because it's like Rome or China or Japan. It's still a brutal military state, but it controls and influences so much that it sees itself as better, and the world kind of has to agree to an extent. It has an internal organization that the places it conquered (which it would consider more primitive) could not match in long-term opposition. The Realm is cosmopolitan because it has the money and the armies and domination. Halta has none of that, but it's still enlightened and cosmopolitan, Just Because? It doesn't really make sense to me.
