                        All right, so, long answer (as best as I understand it):

                        At the end of the First Age, if there was any sizable number of Dragon Blooded who retained loyalty to the Solars and Lunars (considering that grievances are supposed to have been widespread, and the Sidereals had been working for some time to set the course of their attitudes), they would have been basically fully destroyed in the establishment of the Shogunate; under the circumstances, it seems as though an end to fighting that would signify a transition of authority wouldn't be possible until they were.

                        So then you've got the Shogunate itself, where the Dragon Blooded have established their hegemony, and endeavor to incorporate all of their number into it; there isn't really a social category comparable to Lost Eggs or Outcastes around at the time, everybody is part of the widespread institutions of the Shogunate. At the same time, the Terrestrials are developing their idea of their own inherent spiritual superiority and demonising the Lunars, at the same time as the failing infrastructure is causing increasingly oppressive policies; a lot of Lunars are going to experience the Dragon Blooded first in terms of a life of subjugation beneath them, and of being brutally hunted and ostracised following their Second Breath, and pretty much any Dragon Blood is going to possess a position greater than what Lunars could give them by default, even without viewing Lunars in terms of terrifying monsters who threaten their dominion. There's a lack of opprtunities for Lunars to use Dragon Blooded there, and a lot of bad blood that might make the prospect undesirable besides. This does not mean that there aren't occasional clandestine (and maybe even the extremely rare overt) occasions of alliance between Terrestrials and Lunars; the Shogunate is heavily factionalised, and that makes for marriages of opportunity. It's still a distinct thing from the idea of Lunars raising their own competing groups of Terrestrials.

                        Then you get to the Age of Sorrows, and that's likely the point where things are most possible but least useful. The numbers of Dragon Blooded and their assets have been gutted; that means that the majority of remaining ones of power all close ranks in the centre (partially for the sake of defence against the scary Lunar Anathema), while the ones who are within reach are scattered and comparatively weak. Centuries of stories about real or imagined atrocities on both sides will have accumulated a lot of weight; a lot of Outcaste Dragon Blooded are still coming up with the social perspective that Lunars are terrifying things and a danger to the safety and stability of the world in general, as well as the sanctity of their enlightened souls, while many Lunars still have issues with the existence of the Terrestrials as a general thing, or at least with the actions that the current crop have perpetrated against them and theirs (or at least form expectations of there being low viability in using them based on such experiences). That's not to say that no Lunars have some measure of power over (or other relationship) with any Outcastes; part of the point of Outcastes is that they're so widespread they find their way into virtually any line of employment (just look at Minton's latest blog post). But it's a bit of an uphill battle, on both sides, not just for overcoming practical barriers or biases between the involved parties, but in the responses of their peer groups (for the sake of argument, imagine how a Lunar who's trying to use Terrestrials loses the support of Leviathan; more concretely how Outcastes associated with Anathema are rather problematic for the Realm).

                        Building your own little competitive dynasty of Terrestrials as a Lunar? Virtually impossible; Outcastes are too widely distributed (incidentally, the most common cause of Outcastes grouping together is when a bunch unite around fulfilling the social expectation that they kill Lunars) and their bloodlines are too weak. You'd probably have to gather up a sizable portion of the Outcastes in an entire Direction just to have a good base to start with, and it would still likely take decades or centuries of careful cultivation to start meaningfully increasing their numbers; the Scarlet Dynasty has far superior breeding, and even they seem to carry assumptions that this generally requires carefully selected matches based on collating and comparing numerous factors going several generations back through the family line. If it was as easy as all that, more Dragon Blooded would be trying it for their own benefits, as well as certain management resources like spiritual regiments, dietary information, gynecological knowledge and staff, as well as a sophisticated infrastructure for raising the offspring. Some Lunars might be willing to invest the effort, resources and time required to build up a consistently viable bloodline to reign over, but that's likely to be further compounded by the negative attention it will accumulate, not just from the Realm and other Lunars, but other provincial powers who dislike the idea of a local sept of expanding and self-perpetuating Exalts taking up space. I'd work in an idea for a Lunar who's coming to the fruition of a loooong term project to produce a local bloodline capable of birthing larger numbers of Exalted, but where that part is still on the horizon.

                        Besides, such a group still wouldn't quite be competitive with the Scarlet Dynasty; the Exalted of the Realm are richer, more deeply embedded in a highly sophisticated economic, political and military apparatus, and still have better equipment than your local crop does. The value of whatever such subject Dragon Blooded could damage in the Realm is probably not often commensurate with what it would have cost you to build them up; not compared to much cheaper, more easily replenished mortals whom you can clog the Realm's gears up with over a long period of time.

                        Beastfolk, in comparison, are super easy; you get (or be) a person and animal to have sex in a suitable area, which then grows like any population of people. Beastfolk aren't even especially useful; I really think the main value they have to Lunars is being a populace they know Realm-run society will marginalize, as well as having cultural aesthetic values that makes it really easy for Lunars to form cults of personality. Tactically speaking, they're not much more useful than any other group of mortals the Lunars might use to tie up Realm military resources while all their cool shit gets broken.

                        They're certainly not an indication that Lunars have any particular distinctive or impressive capacity to breed human beings, because again, it's super easy.

                        I have approximate knowledge of many things.
                        Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.
