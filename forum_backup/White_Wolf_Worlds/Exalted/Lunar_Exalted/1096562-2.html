                                                    05-29-2017, 05:44 AM
                                                    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                    it's not that you become a Chimerae and thus can access the special super-gated Chimerae charms, like in 2nd ed; but rather, you become a Chimerae by taking the Chimerae charms
                                                    The idea that the sole function of chimera Charms would be to characterise oneself as a chimera would be better, sure. If they entail such a transformation, then the best case scenario is one in which people who like their spirit shape the way it is aren't missing out on anything, I think.

                                                    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
                                                    just as you become a Lunar able to turn into a giant monster by taking the giant monster charms, or you become a Lunar prophet by taking Eagle-and-Fish Prophecy, or you become a super-strong Lunar by taking the super-strong-dude charm tree)
                                                    ​Those aren't equivalent, because they don't entail something like a permanent, somewhat intrusive cosmetic change to the character.

                                                    ​Their functions are also a lot more apparent.

                                                    ​I suppose that does lead back around to the question of why one should want to be a chimera in the first place. It's my understanding that in First Edition, it represented a kind of failure state; one had attempted to change their spirit shape from one to another, and got stuck in between. It's just that somewhere along the way, the in-between state became desirable in its own right.

                                                    ​Which might fold back into the concern I have about chimera Charms. Charms that branch away from the state feel like they're at least flirting with the idea of chimerism being a special form of Lunar superpower, which I think sells the default Lunar concept a bit short. But if it didn't have much in the way of extra powers, it would raise the question of why one would want to add wolf features to their goat totem in the first place.

                                                    ​If it was all a purely cosmetic thing, then I would think it would be a lot less circuitous to let mutant animals be a possible spirit shape from the start.

                                                    ​But when it gets given a particular name, it lends it the assumption of a bit of weight and significance, and I alternately see a struggle to live up to that assumption and issues that arise should there be actual success.

                                                    ​I remember ages ago, there was developer discussion about the idea of spirit shape having a possible impact on how one learned Lunar Charms in general; nothing as dividing as "Lunar with snake totem can only learn snake Charms", more a case that the totem created a theme that could change some of the progression of a cascade to make accessing some things easier. In that model, I would have thought that the benefit of a hybridized spirit shape was being able to expand into such rearrangement following two or more animal themes, while giving up the highest benefits of having a singular totem. I feel as though that would have been a decent way to give chimerism a point, without handing it something exclusive.
