    Originally posted by Lashet View Post
    Usually got criticized. Like, it's broken and barely useful.

    >And how the Solar relationship works.
    Also afraid of this.

    Other things (experience and skills focus) being equal, can Lunars fight on equals with Solars at 2(L)-to-1(S)? And with Abyssals at 3-to-1?

    P.S. How can you work with this, since the book isn't there yet

    An excellent question! I'm working with it because the book isn't there yet.

    There's a set of homebrew charms, which I've browsed a bit. And I have the 1st and 2nd Ed books for ideas. My main line of scribble-scrabble right now is in a non-Attribute-based on how I'd like things to work, plus the (very interesting and open-ended) idea of Evocations, as deeper magics that build upon basic ones. Basically, breaking out as much as I can from the existing ideas of the Charm trees.

    In the past, we had a 2nd Ed game where the Lunars kept up with the Solaroids very well. (Disclosure: I portray Solaroids, Lunars, Sidereals all as co-equal, co-awesome, but different-niche Celestials.) Some Charms in Ink Monkeys had to be excluded (I punch you 50 miles away!) but not too many.

    Lunars did need some careful Charm-choosing thought, while the Solar players could be pretty carefree in picking them.

    The worst issues we had in that game were our Infernal character running away with the show, and our Sidereal character who was completely buried in broken speedbumpy Charms he had to buy. The latter became an NPC.



    So, keeping in mind that this is coming from my own scribbles that I've barely gotten out of my head and into OneNote...

    Shapeshifting is the intended default mode for Lunars to Get Things Done. A basic Shapeshift costs 1 mote - this puts it in line with Excellent Strike. I'm picking on Excellent Strike because it's a presumably automatic effect that Solars do - better economics than Excellency, and priced to encourage you to do so often. I anticipate that a proper Shapeshift should add dice for the duration of the Scene or the Shapeshift, as a persistent effect. It would be really nice if I had a guideline ready, but I don't.

    Buying into more Shapeshifting Knacks gives you more access to different things, which can also drive the cost up.

    Shapeshifting comes with free Elsewhere storage for your Stuff. There ought to be a Knack for Big Stuff.

    DBT will (I'm hoping) be an optional combat style for Lunars who prefer to build a damn awesome combat mode and stick with it, instead of constantly shifting around.

    Shapeshifting in general is harmonious with Changing Moon Caste. DBT and its Gifts are harmonious with Full Moon Caste as well. Ghosts, demons, Raksha, gods, and the aspects thereof are also harmonious with No Moon.



    All of the Territory stuff goes into Lair. I'm totally copying my homework from Beast, here. If you have a Lair, you have a Heart. Unless you expand on it, it's the Elsewhere zone where all of your shapes and Stuff are present. You can go and visit them if you have the proper Charms. Sometimes they'll talk to you and it will be really creepy.

    If you do expand on it, each expansion lets you take over a Lair in a territory somewhere. To do so, you become the apex predator/monster/master in the area. This may be social or craft or occult. Or it may be a Proper Lunar Beatdown.

    When you're the apex, the territory is yours. You can appear and vanish from the Lair entrance there, and use your burrows to quickly travel around. You can gain Intimacies, empower Charms, and fast-travel this way. This is the Lunarity equivalent of high-speed Solar travel and political dominance.

    Also note that there's a hint of the Solar-Lunar dynamic here. The Solar would be the natural king or queen of a territory, while the Lunar could be the legendary monster nearby. This could be a spousal relationship like was expected in 1st and 2nd Ed. It could be hostile, or mentoring, or protective. Or it could be entirely out of whack and Limit-whammied. To the mortals nearby, the Solar & Lunar could be the hero-monster pair of myth, just as easily as they could be partners.

    You can link up your Lair Chambers to other Lunars' (or other gods, etc, if they agree), and the Lunars themselves have a project to build Moon Paths all around Creation and beyond. The project is going poorly, but it gets them around the worst of the Wyld Hunt and the Loom of Fate's predictions.

    Territory in general is harmonious with Full Moon Caste. Territory in a civilized area is also harmonious with Changing Moon Caste. Territory Way Out There is also harmonious with No Moon Caste.

    (Rumors that an ancient and cruel Solar forged the first Anathema Dagger to use against his Lunar mate in her Heart chamber, for fear that she could retreat there out of his grasp, are certainly no more than cautionary tales told to young Lunars. Yep. Definitely.)

    (There's more hiding in here too. A Lunar Lair can easily be a "magical world" in a Campbell-style hero's journey. Also, I'm pondering a sort of Hurry Home as an emergency escape, in the style of the Sidereal ceasing to exist Charm.)



    Lunar understanding and manipulation of mysteries, lore, occult, crafting, and so on, I'm putting in a tree for Lunar Circle Sorcery. Though that name is grossly inaccurate and just a placeholder. I pretty much picked that name with the intent that it will lead to a capstone power of sorts.

    This will include all of the related Charms that don't really map to a particular Attribute. Plus it will have all of the ways to travel to faraway places and survive there.

    I'm anticipating a high-level type of working, which is what inspired the temporary name. This will cover all of the great Lunar miracles, such as saving the Castes from further dissolving, creating the tattoos, creating the Charms and Knacks other Lunars use for Territory and Lair, and so on. The main difference is that the Lunar, themselves, becomes what they are creating - temporarily or permanently; or they otherwise breed it or inspire it as a legend until it takes root and becomes true.

    A Lunar who wants to create a Daiklaive, for instance, might start by talking up the legend of the terrible monster wielding it. It might draw some interest as mortals and Exalted go to find it, only to go missing. As the legend starts to take shape, the intended weapon might appear in his hand, long enough for the Lunar himself to appear (as that terrible monster) and defeat a Circle come to claim it. Finally, it takes shape permanently, when the working is complete.

    Creating a Manse might involve becoming the various gods of the area, enough to adjust some dragon lines. Then taking on the shapes of the land, buildings, and other features, a bit at a time, until the Essence of the area starts to hold the shape on its own.

    The greatest miracles would be the final workings of Lunars who devote themselves entirely into their completion. The mystery of tattoo creation, repairing the Castes, and so on, each has the soul and Heart of a Lunar hidden inside them.

    This is by far the least-drafted bit of what I have in mind. I just started this week.

    This Sorcery in general is harmonious with No Moon Caste. Something in here would be harmonious with each of Full Moon and Changing Moon Castes.



    Caste will absorb all the ideas of renown, as well as Anima and other non-Charm benefits.

    As a Lunar grows and excels in each of the three areas above (Sorcery, Lair, Shapeshifting) they gain benefits relating to that Caste.

    [Edit to add: The intent is that a Full Moon warrior will be your straightforward Werewolf or Were-X , with emphasis on shifting into a defined and ever-growing War Form to Get Stuff Done - the War Form would be something greater than its parts, unique, powerful and terrifying. A Changing Moon trickster will be the face-borrowing, skinchanging, Beast Boy, Zan and Zane type master shapeshifter - they wouldn't be able to impersonate a Destiny (that's for Sids) but they could build fake lives and steal faces and bodies. A No Moon witch will be the monster under your bed, ethereal, shadowed, and knowing of all things hidden - and who create magic of and through themselves. This post is a horrendous scrambled mess of the ideas I'm going for!]

    Un-tattooed Lunars would have the full benefits of the Caste matching the phase of the moon, and including all of the rewnown-like progress within that Caste. They wouldn't get much else that tattooed Lunars get (as below) but also wouldn't have to sink XP into it either. Each time they see the moon for the first time in a night, they may choose three of the (about five) perks in that phase's Caste.

    Tattoed Lunars gain three perks (out of maybe five to choose from) in their fixed Caste. Those are also fixed.

    The Lunar Tattoos however, can gain Evocations of their own, which interact with Caste, Anima, and other things.

    Tattoed Artifacts (armor, for instance) may be Evoked as needed, which is rather expensive. They may be evoked permanently, which is less expensive but committed forever. Or they may be evoked as part of DBT Gifts, which is relatively cheap and obvious and becomes a built-in part of their DBT. Each one will be only Evoked one way, unless maybe you alter that later by buying in to further Evocations of that Artifact.

    At about Essence 2, you can start gaining the other perks of your assigned Caste. Only three can be active at a time, but you can choose them each time you see the moon (in any phase) for the first time in a night. Each additional one you gain is an Evocation of the tattoos.

    At about Essence 3, you can start gaining perks from other Castes. They are also Evocations. You can only have one Caste's perks at a time, even if you only have the one.

    At about Essence 5, the Lunar begins to realize the totality of the Changing Gift, and may start to mix-n-match perks that they know.

                I very much agree with you about the first point, but not the second point.

                I find Lunars tend to have very high attributes: unlike Solars, they put a fair amount of XP into them, but also because of freebies.

                Without much need to boost abilities, favoured attributes traditionally at 3 points a dot, and now tertiary attributes also at 3pts a dot, it's very easy to go "hmmm... what to do with my 15 freebie points. I guess I'll buy 5 extra dots of attributes."
                Or at least 4 and a few dots of backgrounds.

                Also, Lunars have always started with slightly more attribute dots and slightly less ability dots than Solars, which I imagine will continue.

                My Essence 4 No Moon had 7 attributes at 5 (and one at 4, and one at 3).
                Obviously that's at essence 4, not essence 1, but I think starting Lunars tend to have quite a lot of dots too.

                Certainly, "Attribute+Attribute" for an Attribute-based Exalt seems more advantageous to me than "Attribute+Ability" for an ability-based Exalt.

                    Originally posted by Tikor View Post
                    I like the idea of having it be a Flaw, as I've written out here.

                    What mechanic are you looking at?
                    I was thinking of giving it some Merit points or even a Charm-like tree of suggested benefits. Know how to find each other, instant communication, perfect combat coordination if you really go all-in on it. That kind of thing.

                    Each partner would have to buy in the same amount and in the same benefits, or it simply doesn't happen.

                    The Great Curse lurks in this relationship though. At some point in the story (If and only if the players are into the idea), one or the other of them would discover ways to unbalance the relationship. Perhaps taking extra dots or Charm-likes, when the other hasn't taken ones to match. The more unbalanced it goes, the more troubled the relationship becomes. Maybe that should be Limit, or maybe Curse-twisted Intimacies if we need mechanics for it. Maybe it only manifests if one partner reincarnates, and re-Exalts without enough Relationship Stuff to balance it.

                    There are a lot of stories that can come out of this, so I like having ideas and options. But, it flirts with the Red Rule so I hesitate to make it mandatory.

                    Just thinking it through with my own group...
                    ... I have a married couple who play, who would very likely jump on an in-game partnership. They always make up partnered characters anyway.
                    ... I have a player who likes stories with relationship or familial status. Things like being a noble and dealing with noble courtship and politics.
                    ... I have a player who enjoys challenging and difficult situations, and would probably like finding out that a reincarnated mate has gone evil this time.
                    ... and I have a player who enjoys playing sarcastic troublemakers (in character) just outside the idea-space of the rest of the group, who would probably be disinterested in doing the mate thing.


                    [Edit to add] I really like how you lay it out as None/Minor/Major/Defining and use the Flaw mechanic to provide XP. I might build up on my idea based on following the Intimacy levels like you did, with a Flaw coming out of it for going off-balance. Awesome food for thought, thank you!

                                            Originally posted by Simon Darkstep View Post

                                            What are some Lunar story ideas or themes you particularly like?

                                            Change on a deeply personal and arguably petty level. Lunars make the best dramatic heroes, because change is fundamentally tied to their core identity. A Solar sorcerer comprehends the magics that flow through the world; a Lunar feels them. Also, the intersection of sorcery and the Lunar aesthetics of primal physicality: haruspicy, bloody rituals, and the like.

                                                        Generally, I've found that Exalted, more than other games, relies on good-faith agreements between the ST and the players to throttle the action and allow for pacing and tension, especially since it's very possible to max out the stats you care about at chargen, such that the only form of advancement left is charm buys.
