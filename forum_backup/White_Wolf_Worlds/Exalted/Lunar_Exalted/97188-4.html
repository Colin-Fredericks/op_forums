                I'm sorry to rain on everyone's parade, but the best philosophers in Creation are the Sidereals.

                'Wait, Mercury!', I hear you cry, 'How can that be? They've got bad Excellencies, and a very limited charmset and they're neither as intelligent as Lunars nor as skillful as Solars!'

                That's all true, but what you're forgetting is the starting advantage. See, if you put a Lunar in a room and tell them to philosophize, they'll think long and hard before coming up with something simple and beautiful and raw, although they won't write it down so nobody will ever see it*. If you tell a Solar to philosophize, they will begin writing the Grand Treatise of Virtuous Wisdom, which will be very convincing but also suffer from the fatal flaw of being a book on virtue ethics.** If you tell a Terrestrial to philosophize... well, honestly we all know that Terrestrials don't count, so they'd probably produce some sort of Aristotelian nonsense and call it good.***

                If you tell the Sidereal to philosophize, she'll look at you all funny and then pull out her earmarked copy of Everything The Maidens Say Is Objectively Correct and ask what it is you want to know. You might think you can defeat this stratagem by asking things which aren't in the book, but Sidereals actually have charms to intuit what we'd think about something!

                Now, since our wisdom is objectively correct and other philosophies are not, it is superior to other philosophies. Ergo, the ability to intuit what we'd think about something makes one the supreme philosopher.

                Q.E.D.




                *Bam! Take that, prehistoric philoso-phytes!
                **Sick burn, virtue ethicists! I got you!
                ***And our Aristotelian contingent! Bam! I'm on fire!

                        Originally posted by TSk View Post
                        You might have to fill me in here if I'm missing an important charm, but I don't think Solars have anything comparable to a Strength Excellency, or can use Strength specialties and whatnot, on top of having lower Strength to begin with. The Solar charmset is very diverse and powerful, but it all works off of abilities. I think that's for thematic reasons, because power in terms of attributes is seen as more of a Lunar thing. I didn't say Solars are weak - obviously, they're the greatest among the Exalted, but their greatness isn't expressed as raw attributes like strength, charisma and intelligence. At least not to the extent that Lunars' is.
                        Strength-Increasing Exercise, in Athletics.

                        Both true. The most eloquent writer (linguistics) and the greatest salesman (socialize?) are both Solars. I only give Lunars a theoretical edge in philosophy because it seems to be a consensus that developing philosophies isn't something that falls under abilities. But whatever rolls you would make to develop a philosophy in the game, I'm pretty sure having greater intelligence will make it easier.
                        Now you've lost me. What does Socialize have to do with making correct choices? To me, philosophy really seems like more of an (Intelligence + Highest Virtue) thing.
                        Philosophy isn't simply about making correct choices for yourself; it's about the correct choices everyone should make. If you want to play Confucius, for instance, you will need extremely high Socialize, and to the extent that charms can help, Socialize charms will probably be the most useful ones (possibly also Bureaucracy, although that feels like a bit of a cheat.) Linguistics is necessary to convey this philosophy to others so it will have a practical effect (which is more than just eloquence.)

                        Of course, being the best philosopher in terms of being "right" in a cosmic or moral sense is not something the mechanics can govern; socialize doesn't make your choices cosmically 'correct', because nothing can do that. But it can help you ensure that they achieve specific broad purposes -- in terms of, for instance, producing a philosophy that reduces crime or corruption or causes other specific effects when implemented across a society? Solar Socialize is probably your go-to ability, and as a result, if you wanted to play Confucius, a Solar would probably fit best.

                        (Or a Sidereal, but the focus would be a bit different.)

                        Lunar Confucius... I mean, is it a cool concept? Sure, partially because offbeat concepts can be cool. But is it the first combination you think of when someone mentions Confucius, or an idea that naturally seems to come to mind when reading the Lunar charmset? No.

                        Charm flavor text probably isn't the most reliable thing to go on. It's handier just to talk about what the charm actually does. Lunars, of course, don't have as many published charms as Solars because Solars are the default flavor of Exalt for the game. But they actually do have charms to enhance their intelligence, whereas I don't think Twilight charms are supposed to work that way. With Solars it's more like, "You guide your mind unerringly to the right conclusion," rather than just, "You quadruple your intelligence."
                        Charm flavor text is absolutely what you want to go on. The purpose of that charm text is to tell the story of each type of Exalted -- to tell you what they are. To me, both the text and mechanics of Solar charms says "philosopher-prince" more than any of the Lunar charms.

                        In general, I would say that Lunars tend to have a bit more of a thematic of down-to-earth practicality to them; while individual Lunars and Solars differ, if I picture a Lunar-Solar mate pairing I have a much easier time picturing the Solar as a high-minded philosopher and the Lunar as a down-to-earth confidante rather than the other way around. This is a loose thing and doing it either way is obviously fine, but "figure out the way everyone should live and convey it to them" is more a Solar thing than a Lunar thing.

                        (I'm deliberately ignoring the Thousand-Streams River here. Partially because we know it is going to be specifically excised in the new edition, and partially because it always had the problem that it was something the Lunars were defined as doing without being something that their intrinsic Exalted themes or charmset had any real support for.)

                        It's an interesting question. I think it would need custom charms either way, and/or an ST ruling on what, mechanically, is involved in creating a philosophy (if anything).
                        Yes, but my point is that I don't think I'd need any custom charms or ST rulings to play a Solar who comes across as a philosopher. I am very good at giving lessons (including spiritual or moral lessons) and producing texts to raise people's abilities and attributes -- I can convey intimacies, virtues, and heroic motivations related to my philosophy as good as or better than anyone else. Just with published Solar charms, I am very good at reforming organizations by implementing my principles, or getting an entire society to accept my ideas. These are, practically speaking, the only vectors where I feel the game is likely to provide much mechanical support for "philosopher" as a concept; and for Solars, these things are clearly the focus of entire charm trees, sometimes noticeable focuses for entire abilities.

                        Solars also have a noticeable number of charms that express the idea of being focused on a high-minded concept; whereas Lunars tend to be more defined by personal relationships. For instance, Solars and Lunars both have Righteous Lion Defense, but the Solar one requires a commitment to an ideal, while the Lunar one requires a commitment to a person, place, or thing. Which one do you think better expresses the concept of a philosophically-minded character archetype? I can play a Solar with Righteous Lion Defense focused on pacifism or justice or some philosophical concept; you can't play a Lunar that way, because the "default" assumption about Lunars tends to be that they're more grounded and less focused on abstract philosophical ideals.

                                                    Originally posted by Nocte ex Mortis View Post

                                                            Lunars are very intelligent. They have enormously sharp wits. They are frighteningly perceptive.

                                                            But what might help make them distinctive as great philosophers is their capacity for a broad wide experience.

                                                            Lunars, fundamentally are shapeshifters. This gives them experience of people and different lives (they can drink and eat of others hearts, perspectives and memories), different ways of being a living being that could give them a wider and more general take on life and existence, and even being a human, than would be easy or possible for one of the other Celestials.

                                                            For instance, a Lunar who could live as Tiresias did, in a sense, might have the advantage in thinking about how humans should relate to one another. It also might be an enormous advantage in terms of being able to persuade other people, who can know that you have been in their place.

                                                            I can't imagine how it would be appropriate to express this in a Charm (or whether that's even necessary)!
