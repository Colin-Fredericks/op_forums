                                                Originally posted by Exthalion View Post
                                                To comment on the actual question: No I do not think Lunars make philosophers for the same reason that no one else does, its too important to the setting that no one has a strong handle on it.

                                                As Holden has said, there is no magic to rule with grace and wisdom. There is no charm to make a perfect world even though there are charms to create worlds. The questions about what Creation should look like and what the place of anything in it should be are ones everyone has to try to answer for themselves. Just because the Solar's utopian vision would be very shiny does not make it a good thing or better then the Sidereal's safe, stable, and soul-crushing plan. They have to settle it with their fists or with their after effects, and everything will constantly be in motion as they attempt to deal with each other. No easy answers to be found.

                                                At best, charms should make you better at picking holes in other people's ideas by improving your ability to consider consequences and structural problems. Those same charms would also make you better at refuting the flaws you or others find and to prop up a bad system.
                                                Yeah, I agree with this, in a sense, "The Greatest Philosopher in the World" can't reeeeaaaly be a Solar concept.

                                                Because if philosophy to the moral domain, it does seem to essentially be "I am the wisest" and Solars don't get that. Wisdom, is formed strongly by intuition, character and experience, which aren't skills and aren't particularly nourished by the Solar Exaltation (they're part of what contributes to skills, but not actually a skill), or really any other Exaltation.

                                                Solars would get to be the philosophers who philosophize and argue with the clearest clarity and persuasiveness though, all things equal (which of course they never are, thus plausibly mortals who've had a particular insight, etc and thus end up being viable as "the greatest philosopher" etc like Isator suggests).
