            It's an absurd idea. The Exalted were not created to be slaves, to be led about and bred for good teeth and strong Essence by whatever wanker has the shiniest Caste Mark. They will fucking murder you.

It feels a lot like the Tau in the 40k setting: in a universe where the factions include "humans who don't really understand their technology and have ritualized it religiously," "aliens whose civilization went through apocalypse," "amoral space bugs drawn to the thing that makes human space travel possible," "traitors to humanity who consider themselves free but are just as backwards," and "ancient robots who awaken to find their empire gone and lesser races squabbling over the spoils," the Tau are a Great White Hope, an up-and-coming race that is progressing technologically rather than regressing, that comes across as Good Socialists who will conquer a human world and let them keep worshiping the Emperor, in a universe of morally-grey autocrats (if the Imperium conquered a Tau world it would torture and then publicly execute anyone professing loyalty to the Greater Good). It feels kind of out of place in the setting milieu, and that's why (I think) the authors started breaking that perfect facade and giving them problems, breakaway factions, and secret morally grey things they do (like secret forced sterilization of Imperial populations) - to make them fit better in the universe they exist in.

                        There's something like that in Halta. It's a constitutional republic in a world where the closest thing to that is a confederation of otherwise-autocratic or military-controlled nations (or Nexus, which is basically quasi-anarchic in a way that works). It's cosmopolitan and enlightened, but it's not located on trade routes so the question becomes "why, other than for its own sake?" It has trained sciences without really any explanation for the breakthroughs other than A Lunar Did It: by this I mean, harvesting naturally-existing bioluminescent mushrooms or whatever for light makes sense, as does creating colonies of them for your needs, but it seems like Halta straight-up engineered them from non-glowly fungus or whatever.

                        The tree-city conceit is indeed admittedly cool, as is the alliance with the Fair Folk (and the local Fair Folk having their own little internecine issues). The "evergreen versus deciduous" is a great concept for an eternal religious war. But to me an enjoyable Halta should evoke more...I dunno really. You have opportunities for Iroquois vs. Algonquin narratives with the Linowan except it does not really play out that way. The Linowan are made to look particularly brutish and savage compared to the Haltans (in other words, the depiction of the Haltans seems to be written to give them the moral high ground in that war because they're more advanced socially and therefore more worthy of existence).


                        Maybe that's what bothers me about it. The Delzahn are clearly inspired by Arabian stories, with a cool twist to the modern culture public modesty that is kind of the inversion of Islamic interpretations. The Haslanti make me think about the Vendel/Vesten from 7th Sea - a Norse culture, considered barbarians but with a rather technological twist (hot-air balloon airships reminding me a lot of the dirigibles in His Dark Materials). Halta doesn't have a good real-world parallel for its themes, so it seems alien, but in a kitschy Disney Princess way.

                        From the start, Halta had notions that "this couldn't happen on its own, a Lunar Had To Do It" but it never out-and-out said that. And then it made it explicit. Adding Lunars to Chiaroscuro and Haslanti adds a little bit to them but you could see those societies existing as purely mortal, without Exalted interference. Halta doesn't. It doesn't really pan to any real-world society for influence (which is OK I guess). If any Eastern nation feels kind of Lunar-ish in the same way that the Haslanti and Delzahn do, to me it's the Linowan (because it's feasible but doesn't whack you over the head with the implication).

                        The Realm is considered cosmopolitan and enlightened because it's like Rome or China or Japan. It's still a brutal military state, but it controls and influences so much that it sees itself as better, and the world kind of has to agree to an extent. It has an internal organization that the places it conquered (which it would consider more primitive) could not match in long-term opposition. The Realm is cosmopolitan because it has the money and the armies and domination. Halta has none of that, but it's still enlightened and cosmopolitan, Just Because? It doesn't really make sense to me.
                        The super magic plants are because of their proximity to the Eastern Wyld. In the Far East, any kind of plant imaginable already exists. Haltan botanists go out into the wilderness for a few months, explore, then come back with several specimens of a newly discovered flower that sings when touched, playing back music it hears. They get sold to specialist thaumaturges, who (working with gods, god-bloods and wood elementals) figure out how to make it grow and breed in Creation. Then when they've got a few nurseries set up, they bring the first cartload of singing vines to a Guild factor, and his beady little eyes pop out of their sockets when he realizes how many dinars they'll be worth in the Scavvy Lands. And that's how Haltans get rich. Pharmaceuticals and plants are a big industry, one of their major exports. And it's how Haltans end up with plants that grow superabundant food, plants that light their streets like lamps, plants that circulate fresh water and carry waste away, etc. It's a bit optimistic, sure, the degree to which they've succeeded in utilizing all of them, but they have had the protection of Lunars (since late first edition) so it makes some sense, and it's nice to have one place in Creation that actually takes the Wyld into account and gives us a look at what a Far Eastern kingdom might be.

                        They're not really all that democratic, also, as MG pointed out. I actually think the Haslanesh are more democratic; if memory serves, they're a federal republic. Halta is pretty much a monarchy. Candidates for Queen, IIRC, are chosen from the family of the previous Queen. Is that really such an unusual system, for a people whose laws originated in tribal customs? That's a real question; I'm not sure myself.

                        As for the rest, though, I don't disagree. Halta is probably the best place to live in Creation outside the Realm itself. It's pretty much the Tau Empire of Creation. But there are plenty of 40k players who actually like the Tau, may the Emperor shine his blessings upon them.

                                                            I never liked Lunars as the "breeding Exalted" because it seemed like this unintended side-effect of several factors rather than a specific design decision, and it took them to a weird place.

                                                            Like, okay, the general idea was -- initially where things stood was that Lunars lacked much connection to the world or a strong sense of purpose, but one of the points that was established was that they often used armies of beastmen, and could breed beastmen by accident. Not touching on the whole "beastmen as a metaphor for savage natives from safari stories" thing, because that's a whole kettle of fish, the idea that Lunar Exalted could breed beastmen seemed to be based on their inspirations -- Lunars draw on mythical monsters, and one of the common origins of such characters is weird or transgressive conceptions. So allowing Lunars to breed beastmen sometimes, even accidentally, is a nod to that, and it seems like the kind of thing the Wyld could do, and that's where they live. Okay! Beastmen were mostly an aesthetic thing early on, rather than "super-soldiers".

                                                            At this point the game was roused to attention about Lunars not having a coherent presentation or role in the setting, and in general not being attractive to players, so it set about working on that. 2e had to find a way to sell Lunars -- to make them feel like they belonged in the setting and also to make them appealing to people who didn't have their money out the moment they saw a loincloth. The whole deal where Lunars are playing a 4X game with experimental puppet nations at the edge of the world grew out of that. Personally I think this was a mistake, because it made them feel a little too much like Sidereals, but it was done under two constraints: it had to be a calculated refutation of 1e Lunars, and it had to be something they could sneak in the back door. (3e's big advantage is that it's tossed those constraints, giving it room to breathe.) So, okay, the Lunars like to engineer experimental societies, and we know they have a bunch of beastmen which are produced in part from people having sex with Lunars, so that makes them into extremely hands-on eugenicists, right? This part was not hugely emphasized in the Lunar book, but it was there.

                                                            Now, 2e Lunars was a huge improvement and made everyone happy initially, but fast forwarding a few years people became dissatisfied with it, because its failings were more obvious when it wasn't being compared directly to 1e Lunars (which, in addition to being genuinely bad, had been blown up into an icon of towering evil by the fandom by that point). At this point people were making an effort to polish up the things that made Lunars feel unique and distinctive, so the "breeding beastmen" bit rose to increased prominence. This happened both in the fandom and in the official line -- if you compare the Lunar charms in Glories: Luna to the ones published in the Lunars manual, you can see that the Glories charms are really trying to push the handful of things that 2e established as "distinctively Lunar" that they're allowed to have Charms for. I don't think this was so much that everyone thought at that point that yeah, Lunars should totally be about this, so much as it was them making the most of what they had. But it further established the idea, and now here we are.
