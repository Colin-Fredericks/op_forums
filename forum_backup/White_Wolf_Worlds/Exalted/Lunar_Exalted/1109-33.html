        Originally posted by TSk View Post

        I've never yet asked a player to leave a game, for that or any reason.

        Generally speaking, when you ask a player something during chargen, you're not saying, "Answer the way I like or you're out of the game." The worst that would happen in the case of mammoth Lunar player, if he had no explanation of why his character had Dexterity 5 and Strength 3 other than "god stat", is that I might ask him to choose a different totem animal, one that actually had a high Dexterity score and would therefore seem more appropriate for him.
        Mammoths would have a size variance in the species. That character could be a skinny, relatively lithe mammoth that's clever about using the old snoot proboscis.

        The reason is because the whole point of the spirit shape is that it's the animal that best reflects your nature. It's the animal you. It's not some other thing you change into. It's your true form. Is nature is your nature, its instincts are your instincts. You can't separate them. And just as your spirit shape is an animal repreesentation of you, so you, in human form, are a human representation of your spirit shape, because the nature of both is the same.
        The spirit shape comes to a Lunar due to some symbolic reason. Hence why so many of the entries don't just mention the animal, either in the anima banner entry or the spirit shape entry. Tamuz isn't just a gazelle hound - he's a sleek, fit gazelle hound. Ma-Ha-Suchi isn't just a wolf, he's an oversized wolf with goat features. Anja Silverclaws has an anima banner that forms a housecat out of shadows. What the totem represents varies from character to character, as you can see with Ma-Ha-Suchi's oversized brute compared to Red Jaws's shadowy, skulking lupine manner.

        It seems the consensus on this thread is that your totem animal only reflects your nature as far as your personality is concerned - but I've never read it that way, because you can't really separate, say, the instincts of a fox, with the Dexterity and Perception a fox relies on to hunt. Physical and social attributes are a large part of an animal's nature, and if your spirit shape reflects your nature then it should reflect your attributes too.
        And again, those animals will vary. There are larger and smaller examples of an animal within the same species. There are beasts of the same type that nonetheless differ from each other in a wide variety of other ways, too - coloration, dietary preference, dietary requirements, etc. Those can serve as markers of the totem, not just one ideal view of the species.,

        And in my experience, it's always more enjoyable in a Lunar game the more in common you have with your spirit shape, because you get to play on that in a variety of ways. If the fox Lunar is the one who, even in human form, has the best hearing, the one who always notices a trap, the one with the quickest reflexes, well, it makes you feel like you're actually playing a fox Lunar, someone whose spirit shape actually fits. And it makes the character feel more like it actually works, and the game experience is more enjoyable. Or at least that's how it's always been for me.
        Superlatives are risky business for Lunars, recall. That's why personal symbolism plays a factor.

                                                    Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                                    anyone who says something like, "It's a Knack" will make me laugh at them. We don't know if Knacks will even be a thing in EX3, much less if DBT will be among their number.
                                                    I doubt there will be Knacks. The whole concept of the "Knack" arose out of (1) the belief that Eclipse Charmshare should allow an Eclipse to acquire any and all things-called-Charms possessed by any supernatural creature in the game; (2) Lunar-style shapeshifting and Charm effects like DBT are completely out of theme for Solars, even through Charmshare, and should be completely impossible for Eclipses to learn with Charmshare. The result was creating a new non-Charm term of art for the category of effects, since not calling them Charms locks them out of being acquired with Charmshare. It was a distinction created as a workaround for a specific problem, instead of just addressing the problem itself.

                                                    2 is as true as it ever was, but 1 is no longer a problem, since 3e Eclipse Charmshare does not allow Eclipses to learn the Charms of other Exalt types, "only" specific kinds of spirit Charms.
