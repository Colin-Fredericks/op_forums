    Really though, it is more about a) are Lunars effective and fun characters to play (on a game level) and b) have Lunars been and are Lunars effective and cool characters to have in the wider Age of Sorrows and history of Creation (the setting level).

    Craft is kind of just one route to this ends, and piece in that puzzle, and if it ultimately, really, really, really whatever the writers try just doesn't fit their style, it isn't necessarily a failure state for Lunars.

    (To some degree I think late 2e made it feel like a failure state to not have much Craft, because the vibe there was a bit like "Creation of the Age of Sorrows is a post apocalyptic magical sci-fi setting. The game is about rebuilding a technologically better world from the ruins of the apocalypse. So if you matter, you *need* to be doing serious technology". Craft as the route to technology became this big thing and this contentious thing.

    3e's world, to the extent it carries that on (and it doesn't seem to as much), seems to have moved on to more emphasise sorcery over technology, and that as a space is more welcoming to character types that don't have a clear "in" to technology.)

        Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
        Does it?

        ​I mean, just going with the base rules, occasionally producing wonders can be done with some combination of time, the right ingredients, and high dice pools, no fistful of Craft Charms or unique infrastructure required.

        ​People have done the math on this, after all. The largest single obstacle to mortals making Artifacts is that it's just not an action they're permitted by the rules to take. (The second and third obstacles being the limited Terminus and the high difficulty, respectively.)
        I just ran the numbers. To create an artifact, you need between 60 to 65 gxp, 5 to 10 sxp, the necessary materials, and an effective dice pool that depends on the artifact rating as follows:

        Rating Effective Dice Pool
        2. 18
        3. 25
        4. 33
        5. 42

        Based on the dice adder described in the back of the 3e book, a Lunar could hit the average number of dice for a level 2 artifact relatively easily (crafting usually requires either Dex or Int, so stunting to use both with your dice adder should be fairly simple) and could build a level 3 artifact if they spend WP and stunt well, but anything above that probably requires additional charms. While one could assert that anything Lunars have built that would be classed as Artifact 5+ was them attempting the same thing for a couple of decades until they got lucky, I'm pretty sure most players aren't going to even attempt something like that without a better chance of success.

        ....Well, ok, so they might attempt it, but when they fail after putting in two years of work, I'm betting they'll run the numbers and be discouraged and disappointed.

        Thus, while I don't know that forms Lunar Craft charms will take, it's fairly clear to me that they will have something more than just the dice adder.

        Edit: for anyone who wants to view my analysis in full, here's a link to the Google docs file: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1...AsOY3RsOM_mRzw

                            Okay, Meianno Yuurei , please take these preconceptions of my point that you have in your head, and dump them out, because they really aren't helping. Nowhere did I say that Lunars can't make artifacts. In fact...

                            ​I don't think it's a bad idea for Lunars to make artifacts. I feel that they should be capable, in the best of circumstances, of pulling off some truly miraculous works of artifice. Is that clear?

                            Now, with that out of the way, Lunars have not been dealt the best of circumstances. At all. Between the scarcity of necessary materials (and not just magical ones like behemoth fangs, but even stuff like bronze), the threat of the Wyld Hunt, the lack of First Age laboratories and such, Lunars are in a bad way when it comes to crafting. They might be particularly well-suited among their Exalted peers to thrive in those circumstances, but it's still a poor position to be thrust into.

                            ​As for the subject of how the Caul was made, I don't think it's necessary to pick apart the story of its creation any more than folks need to sit down and calculate how Rakan Thulio parried the Mark of Saturn, so let's pretend we're talking about just making a wonder of similar power.

                            Now, like Isator Levi said: playing with the craft XP or altering the terminus is probably outside their wheelhouse. But that hardly removes every possibility! Consider dice tricks. Or stunt enhancers. Or reroll effects. Or any number of other options. These will not be given a similar cost as a Solar artisan's Charms, but they hardly need to be for a five-dot artifact to be within the realm of possibility (albeit hardly incentivized).
