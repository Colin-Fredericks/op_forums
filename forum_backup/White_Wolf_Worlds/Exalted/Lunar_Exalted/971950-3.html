        The Lunars role in Creation is actually very simple and I thought pretty well defined in the First Edition which got swept aside in all of the noise. The Lunars role is to survive, look at who Luna chooses: The slave who seeks to escape, there is an easy way to escape-kill yourself, Luna does not choose that person. Instead she chooses the slave that uses his/her looks or charm as currency and then while the Master lays sleeping pulls the tent stake and uses a hammer to nail him/her to the ground. Luna chooses the ship-wrecked survivor that uses his wits to escape from the visiting pirates while rigging traps to defend his new home. Luna's choosen are the ultimate in adapt and survive, the Wyld Hunt has tracked you to a small valley, but you have taken the heart-blood of the elderly hermit that rarely visits town but whom everyone will vouch was born there. The Death Knight has driven you to the edge of a cliff, you shift into a bird and flee.
        I may very well be wrong and I firmly expect to hear how wrong I am, but this is my take.

            I'm all for adaptability and flexibility, but not so keen on the portrayal of Lunars as cowards who callously throw away human lives - individually, for their Heart's Blood, or by the hundreds or thousands as expendable shock-troops - all so that they can survive one more precious day. I prefer that moral conflict arise organically from difficult and relatable situations within the context of the gameworld, irrespective of splat, instead of pigeonholing one group as "The Bad-Guy Splat" by giving them Skill Focus: Being A Bastard. Sociopathic parasitism isn't necessarily out of genre for Exalted in general and Lunars in particular, but I'd rather it be something they do rather than something they are. The World of Darkness, this ain't.

                                    Mmm. There's a few things I want Lunars to be capable of.
                                    Warning: Many analogies ahead, and I have no idea what anyone is planning for Lunar 3E

                                    First off, survival like some folks mentioned. Solars tame the Wyld, lock it down and turn it into whatever part of Creation they need. Lunars use/inhabit the Wyld by adapting to it. Which definitely means they need to be able to resist the mental effects as easily as the physical. Another part of that is Lunars shouldn't need infrastructure. Solars lead and get groups together to do big things, where a Lunar would do it themselves or do without. (Solar crafter in the wilderness: use 'need no tools' type charms to build better and better tools, and eventually make/acquire minions to handle the non-crafting stuff. A Lunar goes nekkid and uses claws, or makes a flint axe and uses Lunar charms to make it a great weapon for that Lunar.)

                                    Second would be infiltration. A Solar would use a disguise, or might go as far as a Cylon (brainwash self to think you are the role). A Lunar routinely does a Kimball Kinnison - replace some minion on the inside, then the minion 'suddenly' shows 'more potential' and quickly goes up the ranks. Solar would be better at a short con, the Lunar does the lifelong mole thing. Especially if, say, you want to infiltrate someone's Blood Ape army. Also, a Solar taking over a town is all 'glorious skilled human who you want to follow'. The Lunar way would be 'I'm a new, strange looking god or elemental spirit. Worship me and accept some taboos."

                                    As far as the Solar thing, that type of Lunar would be a balance for a specific Solar. In D&D, one of them is the backstabbing rogue, the other is the fighter who flanks to let the rogue do his thing. Lunars would be big on synergy. That also means that a all-Lunar group tries to synergize with each other. (and there are some things a Solar just can't do well because they only have one body. Having that second pair of hands can help)

                                    Assuming Lunars are still attribute-based, and the Ex3 take on excellencies, I would guess that Lunars start with all 9. To me that means better than mortal level at everything, but still needs charms to do non-mortal or much much better than mortal stuff. Any Lunar can code a 3D shooter, but it takes a specialist to make the graphics engine.

                                        Yu-Shan is as much of a weakness as any medieval castle was when facing the steppe hordes. It shouldn't be looked upon as an advantage. It should be seen as an albatross.

                                        Let me discuss the problems with the geopolitical perspective of 1e/2e and how they relate to the Lunar Exalted. Owing to the influence of work like 'Conan', the barbarian cliche of 2e was very western, very European in character. It proposed primitives that were attacking in 'screaming hordes' only to be cut down by the Realm Legions, rarely gaining ground except when given aid by a Lunar Anathema. It proposed such attacks as a nuisance, kept at bay by the ever-expanding 'Roman/Chinese' Realm.

                                        This is utterly unlike history tells us the 'barbarian hordes' that ACTUALLY ravaged the world were.

                                        The reality of the 'barbarian cliche' is that these were not primitive people. In fact, they were (by far) the most sophisticated military tacticians of the era, who understood not only the conventional siege-based warfare of the west but also the value of mobility and the critical nature of supply. The Exalted cliche is a horde attacking with immense numbers and being turned back by the noble shields of civilized society. The reality, far different, has groups of 'barbarians' defeated forces with more than 10 times their number.

                                        That's right. With a 10-1 advantage, with siege fortification in place, with extensive military traditions going back centuries... China AND European forces, including some of the most well provisioned and prepared in that era, were defeated. Over and over. And not just defeated - absolutely crushed.

                                        Exalted borrows from the European 'barbarian cliche' even more when it suggests a society based upon rubbing twigs together and shamanic magic. In truth, the actual barbarian hordes that people like Ma-Ha-Suchi are based on were ruthless about absorbing, maintaining and USING the technology of every culture they came into contact with. Contrary to the expectation of many modern readers, the barbarians showed up with SUPERIOR siege technology and SUPERIOR siege engineers.

                                        Exalted has always had a major issue with pushing barbarians into the cliche box, and never was this more profoundly demonstrated than with Lunar Exalted. They should be portrayed as a force engaged in 'Neo-Warfare', just like the steppe barbarians were, with none of the condescension or implied cultural superiority that Exalted has discussed these people with over the last decade.

                                        To the people who understood what 'barbarians' were in the real world in the context of actually living in the period, they were... the end of the world. An unstoppable force. And this happened MULTIPLE times in history. In fact, much of Chinese military policy and political theory was based upon the idea of preventing it from ever happening again, because they knew once these people started fighting?... they were not only 'to be respected', but they were essentially invincible.
