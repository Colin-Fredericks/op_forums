                Originally posted by TSk View Post
                Well, you can have a rough idea of the demographic information.
                Sure! Then we're using numerical ranges rather than specific numbers. It's still math!

                (I don't think knowing, you know, the exact singular number of Dragon-Blooded in the Realm is particularly helpful - but having workable approximations is nice! And it's really handy to have workable approximations that make sense with each other, because it avoids the problem where you realize that two different numbers you've pulled out of the air conflict. So, for instance: I don't much care whether there are 30 or 50 new Dragon-Blooded born in a given year, but knowing whether the mortality rate is more like 0.5% or 5% - which necessarily depends on the general answer I give to "How many kids?" - does matter to me, quite a bit. I don't want to describe the setting with 500 new kids a year and then realize I've written myself into a corner.)

                But in terms of population, I don't think you can really make a science out of it, considering the numbers given in various books are contradictory and often kind of ridiculous, with nations populated by millions fighting with armies of thousands in bronze-age style warfare. How far would you have to go to make it all add up? I really don't know.
                You can definitely make a science of it - you just can't do so while also honoring all the numbers in the books. So, again, it comes down to "Which parts of the books do you want to ignore for your particular vision of the Realm?" I recommend ignoring Exaltation rates and the number of new DBs per year (both way too high), at which point the rest of the world mostly falls into place, but there are lots of workable answers. It's just important not to take things like the Exaltation rate table as meaning anything.)

                        I think it is a very rare thing when a Great House of the Dynasty falls. Mortal families, we know, come and go with regularity and are hardly noticed. Patrician families in whom one finds thin traces of the Blood Terrestrial multiply and divide. But when a Great Family falls, nations change hands in the balance, lines are redrawn across Creation. And I don't think any dynasts enjoy the thought of a Great House descending into the status of a patrician family, even if it should be their enemies, any more than they like the thought of a patrician family being elevated to the status of a Great House. Because either event subtly erodes the class solidarity on which Realm society depends. So from that point of view, it's preferable to the Empress and to all dynasts that a Great Family is destroyed utterly, than allowed to sink into the status of patricians.
