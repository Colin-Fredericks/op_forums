    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    There's a reason No Moons are known as Ogres in popular imagination (the way Dawns are known as Forsaken, Changing Moons are known as Tricksters, etc).
    Remember the Ogre from the original Puss-in-boots story? He's a powerful sorcerer, lives in a castle, turns into a lion, and turns into a mouse (and then Puss eats him).
    The giants of Welsh legend probably fit too, with all their magical stuff... to a limit.
    I'd say the more humanoid monsters should probably be less of an influence. I certainly wouldn't mind seeing Lunars being able to make themselves bigger though.
    There are plenty of things that kind of skirt the border between divine beast and monstrous humanoid. On the one hand, you have things like the supposed connection between cyclopes and elephants, and on the other you have oni, who are bestial despite being humanoid and serve double duty as monstrous bringers of calamity and fierce guardian spirits.

    As for "primal gods," there is Set, god of storms, violence, and foreigners, and guardian of the sun; Tezcatlipoca, teotl of sorcery and beauty and night and chaos, among so many other things; Dionysus, outsider god of wine and ecstatic revelation; Kali, terrible, beloved mother goddess, and destroyer of evil; Gozu-Tennō, fierce lord of disease and benevolent protector from disease; et cetera, et cetera.
    And these are just the famous ones.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
        Anansi, like Loki, is another shapeshifting trickster God, so a good inspiration for Changing Moons.
        Studying how he tricked others led me to deconstruct the various skills that tricksters use to fool people (even the people who should know better), but the stories themselves provided no particular feat that seemed like a Lunar Charm to me. Or perhaps I should say, none of his feats seemed to inspire a spectacular Lunar Charm.

        Not in the way that, for instance, Hanuman growing into a giant could be a Charm or Knack, or how having physical representations of Rama and Sita (the couple whom he loved above all) inside of his flesh-and-blood heart could be a Charm.


        You'll notice most of these are gods, rather than the mythical beasts that others were suggesting.

        The issue with mythical beasts is that they're fine for Full Moons, but rather too physical to cover all bases. Full Moons had the best charms in 2nd and 1st ed, followed by Changing Moons, with No Moons a rather paltry 3rd (actually, in 1st ed I don't think Changing Moons were that great either, but I only read the 1st ed Lunar charms briefly, as I never played 1st ed).
        I don't want to see that situation repeated, with the physical attributes getting all the best charms.
