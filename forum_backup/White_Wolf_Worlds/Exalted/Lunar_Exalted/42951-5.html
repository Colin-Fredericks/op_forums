    Theme is a factor of fiction, really ; the botch on Lunar fiction muddled thematic connotations a bit. Without a clear unifying Ubermyth to hang character traits off of, developers for Lunar Exalted sometimes would borrow Werewolfisms or other such things. I believe a fiction pass should fix this issue; we're getting that, so, I'm not worried about it. Ultimately, the 'Animal Man' myth is sufficiently deep and well represented enough so that it shouldn't be an issue.

    As for borrowing powers... as I've said elsewhere, so what? Yes, cool and distinctive powers are interesting, but when you really get down to it?... the Monkey King -is- kinda a silver Solar. It's just another flavor of martial arts superhuman. Some crossover is gonna exist. It's not the end of the world.

    The purpose is to make Lunars fun to play and to make them fit in well, which doesn't require every aspect of what Lunars are to be completely NEW, TURBO, HYPER, EXCITING and unique.

    It pretty much worked before ; I played many games with them, so did many others, we had then on our MUSH for many years.

    My impression has always been 'Lunars could be improved and need love' rather than 'Lunars are a hopeless mess of trash'.

    So when I say be wary of overfixing, that's what I mean. We're tinkering here, really -- doing a big fiction pass, a mechanics pass, and then we ought to be more or less ready to go. No need to make a mountain out of a molehill.

            All this discussion made me review the various aspects of Luna to see if there was anything to be gained from them. I don't think we need to go as far as having a "Bloody Huntress Aspect" or some-such, largely for the reasons Maddy stated above, but a few interesting ideas came out of it that I would like to share.

            Each of Luna's Aspects is influenced both by one of the "alternate-moons" and by Luna's relationship with that alternate. Thus an Aspect related to that may be based on the Exalt's relationship with Luna herself, (or perhaps with Gaia, or their Solar mate, or whatever) and that would form the basis for their powers in some way. Rather than the Solar charms which reach a pinnacle that brushes against the Unconquered Sun, Lunar charms would spring from their relationship with their patron and lead to what such emotions might push them towards.

            So, yeah. Random thoughts that may or may not be useful.

                                Originally posted by hatewheel
                                Aspect suggests a physical association, where Caste only suggests a predilection for certain kinds of action. It can be more complex than this, but this answer is the simplest version.
                                Reposting this from Isator Levi, upthread - this was John's simplified definition of caste versus aspect.

                                Given that there is also an intent to make Lunars more physical in their approach to things, I can see why the novelty of exploring caste vs. aspect would be interesting.

                                I like Omicron's idea that Lunar's choose their aspect to become something, and then it empowers them going forward - it both merges the inherent properties associated with the Aspect with the flexibility and freedom that is still part of Luna.

                                To Sunder's idea about using totems as a jumping off point, it is actually tricky, mostly because of adjectives. For example, if my totem is Wolf, I probably am Full Moon, under your schema, right? But my concept of wolf is actually the wolf from O-kami, who is the goddess responsible for healing the world through knowledge and exploration - so maybe the adjective I need for my totem is Mystic Wolf, and now that totem makes me a No-Moon. Or if I apply Charming, it becomes a Changing Moon, etc. In the same way, a Bull is an herbivore, but I can bull examples where it is appropriate for all three extant castes. Totems are too subjective, and frankly too wide-open, to serve the sort of role that game-designers use Castes/Aspects/Auspices/Clans/Classes/Traditions/Kiths/Whatever to fill. Though, I welcome John et al. to include a kakapo-totemed Lunar somewhere in Creation

                                I am deeply ambivalent about whether Lunars should have Castes or Aspects - given that I feel one of the defining features of Celestial Exalts in past editions has been that they transcend the limitations they find imposed upon them, I would be reluctant to give them something else as a limiter. However, I have not actually felt that Terrestrial Aspects were particularly constraining - and I note many people felt that there was no functional difference between caste and aspect.

                                Using John's definition, I am not sure that the current Caste scheme does not mechanically have the same force as Aspects - a Full Moon has almost no way to use her 1 favored attribute to sufficiently break out of her defined role, unlike a Dawn, who could be a Sailing-Sorcerer-Diplomatic-Scholarly-Street Musician, who reluctantly is the greatest warrior alive.

                                In this case, Caste/Favored Ability mechanics, coupled with out-of-caste point costs and even some of the Lunar societal fluff (for example, No-Moons not teaching other castes Celestial Circle Sorcery has cropped up in both editions) has also made it so that the Lunar bins are less 'predilections' and are more hard-coded...

                                So, if we keep the current paradigm, perhaps reformatting to Aspects would give the Lunar structure the conceptual weight it needs.

                                    As I note in my previous post, the current Lunar paradigm does not really function mechanically like a Caste, where it provides a predilection which can be over-ruled through the use of Favored Attributes (in this case). I previously suggest that redefining the bins as Aspects, perhaps along the lines Omicron proposed, or something equally cool, would add a conceptual weight to the tight fit that binning currently gives Lunars.

                                    The alternative is to keep Castes for Lunars, which would necessitate reworking the mechanics of the Caste/Favored framework to allow for the flexibility Castes are supposed to provide. Thus, a "Full-Moon" should have an inclination toward physical stuff, but should be able to sort of nod in its general direction while grabbing goodies from elsewhere.

                                    This has not happened before because of a concern over the breadth covered by the Attributes - that somehow favoring 1 Attribute (other than Dexterity) was equal to favoring 3-4 Abilities. Or maybe it is that Favoring Dexterity is equal to favoring at least 8 Abilities*, and therefore everything else gets thrown out of proportion. While the base mechanic may make that true, the result still seemed to be a less flexible character - perhaps because of how the charm trees evolved, with inconsistent requirements within a tree (switching from Charisma to Manipulation to Appearance, in 1e; in 2e it was more the huge jumps in Essence minimums, and the narrow purviews of some of the shrubs).

                                    The final challenge is that, even with a Caste/Favored discount, raising an Attribute is much more expensive that raising an Ability, and so progress feels slower, and it is harder to meet new requirements via in-game advancement. Thus, you have a strong incentive to stick to a narrow range of proficiency - which is again more in keeping with the feel of an Aspect than a Caste.

                                    *Archery, Athletics, Craft (Air), Dodge, Larceny, Martial Arts, Melee, Stealth, Thrown
