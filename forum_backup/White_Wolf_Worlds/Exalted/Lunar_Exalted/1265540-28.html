                            Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                            This is part of why I'm a little wary about them existing. Giving the Bronze Faction their own Dragon-Blooded Gens is probably the worst thing you could do with the concept.
                            Now I'm wondering... I'm thinking of Heaven's Dragons engaging in activity primarily as functionaries of the Celestial Order as a whole, of which Sidereals are a particular and significant part, but not the only one or even the most important.

                            Sidereals, in this Edition more than ever, have duties and agendas besides those that concern Solars and Lunars. There are probably things in an official capacity that they can send directives, or at least requisitions, to Yu-Shan's other Exalted about.

                            So it becomes a question of whether or not this Edition will approach Sidereal factions as something that they can reasonably formulate official policy to prosecute, or if it's something that they're kind of expected to do on their own time.

                            That's a Bronze Faction thing, but it also leaves a hanging question about the Lunars; for however long there have been Sidereals actively advocating for the Solars, I feel as though the hostility from Lunars is going to be a thing separate from that, and one that crosses factional boundaries. So whether or not dealing with the Lunars can be a matter of policy is an entirely distinct hanging question.

                            Still, I do believe that Thesasaurus had the right of it when he said that the previous line was in fact about the gods not wanting to be too involved, because they were afraid of being marked as enemies of the Silver Pact as well. So it may indeed come down to the Sidereals being limited in how much they can dedicate Bureau resources in that opposition.

                            Which would also possibly handle the question of whether or not Sidereals have impunity to unleash highly destructive sorcery on Lunars, if doing so is going far beyond the boundaries of their mandate.

                            Laying all of those hanging questions aside, I will say that I've been convinced that seeing Heaven's Dragons as a source for Wyld Hunts issued directly from Yu-Shan is a bad idea on several counts.

Though that somewhat depends on the nature of Arcane Fate this edition (in 2nd ed, certain divine employees were immune to it, but I forget how extensive that was).

                                And then other Sidereals who thought the whole thing was ridiculous, and they should be concentrating on their day jobs of stopping demonic and Wyld incursions, stopping Rakan Thulio and his faction of rogue Sidereals from destroying Fate itself*, and making sure Fate goes as the Maidens decreed (however the whole Samsara, Fate, and committees things ends up in this edition).

                                Though my point wasn't that Independents never make friends with Dragonblood in Heaven. They probably do. Some sidereals and some dragonblood have probably been friends in Heaven throughout the millenia.

                                Possibly I should have just said Sidereals and Dragonblood invite each other to parties, but I could well imagine that Sidereals who say things like "Why should Dragonblood rule Creation? Wouldn't it be better if the Solars were in charge again?" might not get invited to many Dragonblood parties, nor invite Dragonblood to their parties (or perhaps they do invite them, but the Dragonblood don't go to Gold Faction parties, because no-one likes to stand there while people sit around talking about how people like you are responsible for most of the world's problems. It's pretty awkward, I know that from experience).


                                Originally posted by Isator Levi
                                The Second Edition factions were nonsensical for reasons including that they weren't really factions (schools would have actually been a term that made sense for them), and the actual content of most of their activities. They were things that were either dull, redundant, or didn't really make sense as things for Lunars to do.

                                The basic concept of being divided along lines of philosophy and methodology isn't a problem in its own right, so long as they're functional.
                                I agree with this about 50%.

                                You are right that they were problematic in terms of their philosophies. The Winding Path and Swords of Luna were redundant, the Sun King Seneschals and Wardens of Gaia didn't make much sense, and while the Crossroads Society probably made the most sense to me, it was still rather redundant (and was more a mutual teaching club than a political movement).

                                But, while that was my main objection, a lot of people also complained about how uniform they seemed across an entire Creation.

                                If you're a group of Lunars in the far East who think Solars are pretty awesome, and want to team up with them and help them recreate the First Age... how are you really in a faction led by Leviathan? I doubt he sends any instructions to you. He's probably never heard of you.

                                Similarly, if you're a group of Lunars in the South-East who believe protecting the Cinder Coast from Raksha is more important than expelling the Realm from An-Teng, are you really part of the same organisation as a group of Lunars in the North-East who help the Realm's ally Linowan to fight Halta's Raksha allies? Yes, you all have the same philosophy, but you're not really in the same school. The first group is probably much more closely linked with the local Cinder Coast pro-Solar Lunar, Lunar aiming to empower ordinary mortals, nature-loving Lunar and Lunar sorcerer than they are with some people on the other side of the world.


                                Essentially, I think there's two ways of doing schools. I think the first runs into the issues of 400 iconoclasts across an entire world, but here's two examples, see what you think.

                                The Children of the Crow
                                Every weapon must be used against the Realm, even black magicks and the restless dead. The Children of the Crow believe that necromancy is a valuable tool against the Realm, which other Lunars scorn unfairly. Their first Shahan-Ya, Smiling Rat, was killed by other Lunars for opening Shadowlands in Realm Satrapies, and today they are led by Sharvara, the great four-eyed black hound. Crow's Children often dwell in Shadowlands, and have webs of contacts and favours amongst the dead. At their urging, the angry dead command their descendants to resist the Immaculate Philosophy, and the Realm's garrisons find themselves assailed with hauntings and disposable hordes of shambling corpses.
                                Many other Lunars dislike the Children of the Crow. In defeating the Realm, they leave haunted hellscapes and shadowlands. It's effective, but many in the Silver Pact feel that freeing humanity from the Dragonblood will be of little good when the world is one giant Shadowland. Most Children of the Crow however, are not quite so extreme as their original Shahan-Ya, and they can be a powerful ally.
                                In the north, Crow's Children empowered the Karnese to resist the Realm, but now find them subjugated to the Bull. In the Scavenger Lands, their centre of power is Sijan, while in the South they they meet in hidden shadowlands in the deep desert. In the West, Skullstone was once a centre of the Children, but when the Silver Prince returned he slew the elder Beak of Black Night, and the other Lunars fled. Now they plot their revenge, but wonder how the ghost of a dead prince can be so powerful as to defeat an elder necromancer.


                                Sharvara
                                Visitors to An-Teng often remark at the beauty of its capital. But this is not the original capital; the City of Dead Flowers once stood as shining capital of An-Teng, but is now a realm of ghosts and fear. And in the midst of it dwells Sharvara, the four-eyed black hound. In his youth, Sharvara learnt sorcery from the demoness Mara, but he realised that if was to avenge his dead parents upon the Realm, he would need a magic they had little experience with. And so he entered the Underworld, swimming through its black waters and into the Labyrinth. A year and a day later, he returned with the dark secrets of Necromancy. In his century-old crusade to rid An-Teng of the hated Realm, Sharvara has gathered a group of younger Lunars to him, teaching them his dread secrets.
                                The Vulture-totem graverobber Beak of Black Night has embraced this power, and recently drove the Realm's garrison out of the mountains between An-Teng and Gem using an army of zombie wolves and lions.
                                The ferocious White Tiger Lu and mischievous baboon-girl Grin have little interest or aptitude for the more esoteric secrets Sharvara has to offer, but are happy to have ghostly spies report to them and friendly ancestors bless their troops in their fight to expel the Realm from the Cinder Coast.
                                With the secrets of the Labyrinth Circle, the exorcist Red-eyed Ngo now commands not one familiar ancestor, like most An-Tengian exorcists, but fifty. She ensures those who resist the Realm suffer no predations from restless ghosts, while the Realm's loyal tax-collectors are haunted and cursed. Her dead relatives have attained many prominent positions amongst An-Teng's dead.

                                However, not all Lunars of the region around An-Teng approve of its most prominent Shahan-Ya. Once Sharvara's most promising student, Radiant Moth turned her back on her teacher, believing that his power would lead only to ruin. She believes that Sharvara's hate of the Realm has blinded him to the threat of the black-armoured lord of the Thousand, a dark fortress a month's voyage from the City of Dead Flowers. Radiant Moth damned Sharvara's stupidity, and journeyed west in search of different allies. She has created her own school, which though smaller, now commands a pirate fleet amongst the Cinder Isles. But so far they've had not had great success against the Realm, and one of her students, Lintha Ng Zukarios, suggests alliance with the Lintha. But Radiant Moth fears she'd be making the same mistake she damned Sharvara for.
