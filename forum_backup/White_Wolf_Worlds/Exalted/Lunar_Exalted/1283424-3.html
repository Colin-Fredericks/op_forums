                                                            Originally posted by marin View Post

                                                            That's a big question, and I'm not sure what it is you want to know specifically.

                                                            We've had a few Lunar previews already: http://theonyxpath.com/lunars-preview-exalted/&quot;]a selection of Lunar Charms[/URL], http://theonyxpath.com/solar-bonds-exalted-3rd-edition/&quot;]3e's Solar Bond[/URL] and some of the Charms that benefit from it, and http://theonyxpath.com/the-lunar-exalted-exalted/&quot;]an overview of Lunar history[/URL] and the modern Silver Pact.

                                                            We know Lunars get two new Charm keywords: Protean, for Charms that gain benefits with particular animal forms, and Totemic, for Charms that gain benefits with particular spirit shapes. Knacks are done and gone, with shapeshifting being integrated into the Charmset.
                                                            Thanks, that’s a useful answer. Vance talked a little about how the tone was shifting away from barbarian pulp fantasy and more into god/monster dichotomy a couple years ago, and I wanted to know how they were implementing that.

                                                            The material you've linked looks all right. I don’t recall reading the word ‘barbarian’ even once in any of it, which already seems like a huge step in the right direction in tone. Another of the failures of Lunars up to now is that they've focused a lot on animal shapeshifting, which isn't very interesting. It feels mundane, and is simply not a very useful tool compared to the rest of the supernatural abilities on display. The fact that they're explicitly going for the walking gods + monster in myth angle is good: those thematics are broad enough that they should have a lot of things to play with for the Charmset, and an interesting direction to take shapeshifting in. Obviously, building shapeshifting into the Charmset (in any way) is a good thing. My preferred angle would have been "protean shapeshifter" and simply make all Lunar magic themed around optimizing your form (and potentially your mind) for the task at hand, but this can work.

                                                            It's a shame that they went with the same warrior/trickster shaman divide of old: the role of the shaman in particular is unclear and has never been well supported as an archetype. I really don't see how having an archetype that's mainly mental-based while having large chunks of the mental abilities' domains restricted ("Lunars are the most self-sufficient Exalt type because when everyone's limited to fighting with clubs they come out on top, and they're not good at building things or countries in the first place") is going to be a good idea. It hasn't tied into shapeshifting at all either—as opposed to warrior and trickster. Rethinking this would have been better. If they're going in this direction again, I hope they at least have useful things for mental Lunars to do. Fortunately, ditching the whole barbarian thing opens some doors here.

                                                            The attribute-based Charmset seems more like a sacred cow than a mechanical innovation that had any point in previous editions. Most of the subsystems in the game were tied to specific abilities, which meant that the premise of having broad attribute-based magic that applied across multiple abilities was false for basically everything but the weapon-based combat abilities and some presence/performance social stuff. At best, it was a combo package in which you had prerequisites in many different somewhat-related areas, and it wasn't a very appealing deal. 2/2 is a massive improvement over 3/1, at least, considering how redundant some attribute pairs ended up being. I'm not happy to see it come back, but maybe they'll do something interesting with it this time around.

                                                            The war v. the Realm and the Bronze faction reads well. These Lunars don't give off the vibe of being losers and victims with impractical strategies, which is a nice change of pace.

                                                            Somehow, I'm cautiously optimistic.
