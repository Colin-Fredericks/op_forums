The conquests of the now-satrapies are DB victories. I wouldn't read Sidereal interference into any of those victories (unless that's part of the story you're telling - in which case go nuts!)

    Originally posted by The Despot View Post
    Zel, I have a question. Been on these forums for years, and I have to wonder, why can people support every faction but Lunars. I mean really, if Lunars are so worthless and destined to fail - to have no meaningful connections to anything in the game (go play in the Wyld) why even include them. If they are not meant to fight their enemies the Sidereals (but be victims forever), if they are not meant to make something or sway Dragonbloods (who have been swayed by Solars and Sidereals over thousands of years), if they are not meant to be the strong second who questions and even overturns his commanders words when he is wrong (rather than a bonded suplicant) - what do you want them to do? Do you really want them to sit in the Boardermarches playing with Fairfolk, having no influence on the game, till the PC Circle goes off and kills some Eldar Lunar in a raid and takes his loot?
    I guess the big problem with Lunars is they no more belong to a relevant unified faction than Solars do, but people want to insist that they do. This stems from their last-minute inclusion as a PC splat in 1e when they were intended to be barbarian antagonists, but that narrowness doesn't make for a lot of PC options. The 1e Silver Pact was trying to evoke the Garou Nation from OWoD Werewolf. So in 2e they tried to distance themselves from the "boring barbarians playing boring barbarian stories" vibe explicitly supported in the 1e splatbook ST chapter, and instead got "Stewardship" as a theme, paired with fluff saying "yeah they all ran and hid in the Wyld for 2000 years and do nothing else." Which is an inherent dumb contradiction. So now you have "shapeshifting Stewards," except the question becomes "how can you steward the things you should care about if you can slip off into the night at any time as a mouse and abandon them?"

    For some reason, Solars = Lords worked well in their themes and charms. Just like Sidereals = Viziers and Dragon-Blooded = Soldiers. Lunars = Stewards never really did, because on the most important level, the Lord is also the Steward of his domain and people. Hence Stewards were redundant with Lords, and didn't explore that different a thematic space, so we got Silver Solars instead. Silver Solars, with worse mechanics and stuck in a boring organizational faction structure (seriously, there's a "fight the Wyld" faction, which is something all Lunars would do so they have a faction inside the organization that does the same jobs as the organization?)

    I don't support Lunars as a united faction because the Silver Pact doesn't make that much sense, and the 2e factions inside of it make even less sense aside from the Crossroads Society (and the TSR comes across as a stupid hobby that will never really do anything big and cool that actually affects the setting).I would support the stories of individual Lunars in a heartbeat, just like the stories of individual Solars, Sidereals, etc. The difference being that Solars have no overarching faction, while (most) Sidereals, DBs, Abyssals, Alchemicals, and (in 2e) GSPs belong to a large faction (Bureau of Destiny, Hell, the Realm, Loosky) or a small faction that shares general overarching goals with other small factions (the Deathlords, the Divisions of the Bureau of Destiny). Lunars have a large faction with no overarching policy except "survive," composed of smaller factions that are frequently both irrelevant or ineffectual. There's no fun in actually being a part of the Silver Pact. This is a separate issue from 2 editions portraying the Lunars as uselessly sitting around forever or masturbating to societies they set up and ultimately abandon that don't seem to make any big waves ever.

    I had thought about a Lunar-Sidereal War during mid-2e as a way to spice up the terrible Lunar fluff but hadn't thought much about implementation, but it seems someone on 3e also had these ideas and is running with them in a cool way. I like the idea of a 700-year cold war (or 2000 year cold war since the end of the First Age, whichever). Now the TSR societies could actually serve as catspaws to the Lunars in the same ways that the DBs have their satrapies and the Sidereals use some of the DBs. i'm hoping this is how it shapes out for 3e because as it stands the TSR has no purpose, where it might have a cool niche role to fill with the Caul and a more active Silver Pact.

    The problem with Lunars is that they're thematically muddled. "Animal Exalted" is very restrictive and evokes both thoughts of furries and a specific play style hearkening to Changing Breeds in OWoD. "Steward Exalted" turned into "Silver Solar," i.e. "similar effects as Solars, but more expensive and with higher prereqs as a cheap way to balance them against Solars."

    Originally posted by The Despot View Post
    Also, the Scarlet Empress was created by the Sidereals. She has no influence or interest in Heaven. She wants to bring Dragonblooded rule to the edges of Creation (not that she made any gains there). She brings stability to the Realm, just by being who she is, she has served the Sidereals. Why would they change anything. Even then, they council her and manipulate others in the Dynastic structure. The Sidereals very much control the Realm. Whenever she steps away for vacation, they could easily move in under a destiny and become her, killing the "usurper" when it/she returned.
    I don't like this idea nor do I agree with it. She doesn't serve the Sidereals. She's not Chejop's little proxy, even if he could kill her pretty easy. If Chejop had killed the Empress after the end of the Contagion, what then? He couldn't rally the DBs - they followed the Empress in part because she was now a huge heroic figure, and in part because she had the power of the Sword of Creation. Chejop in an RD couldn't rally them to him, nor would he inherit the Sword, so he struck a deal that was good for both of them. A Sidereal advisor controls the Realm only insofar as they influence an actual Dynast of high standing, because the Realm respects hierarchy and high standing. The Sidereals will be relatively powerless to stop a Realm Civil War, no matter which side they throw their weight behind, in part because the Sidereals are not unified and some of them would benefit from a Civil War even as others freak out about it, and in part because Sidereal powers make them better advisors than god-kings. Also insofar as she has "not made any gains" to control Creation - I'd say controlling the Blessed Isle and the shores of most of the Inner Sea is pretty good. Maybe not 700 years good, but not terrible.

    I find it interesting you're looking for someone to go to bat for Lunars while trying to minimize everything DBs have done and could do as "just a byproduct of Sidereal puppetry." Am I misinterpreting that or missing something?

The white-room setting, however, does not provide a plausible reason why they haven't done more, because the mechanical system (in which the Deathlord stats were written) undermines the setting that the system is supposed to facilitate playing in. So you either have bullshit paper-thin excuses (problematic) or no valid reason to support your thesis (problematic).

                                                Originally posted by The Despot View Post
                                                Command is a better word, sorry. However, Sidereals *do* control them. So if anyone makes Dragonbloods irrelevant, its the Sidereals, where in they are just cat's-paws and unwitting lackeys.
                                                In Exalted: the Dragon-Blooded, the relationship between Chejop Kejak and the Mouth of Peace is presented in an ambivalent manner: Kejak influences the successive Mouths, but sometimes they prove resistant or stubborn and take decisions he does not want them to. When this happens, he tries to outmanoeuvre them, but sometimes this proves impossible. When a Mouth of Peace is too out of his control... he shrugs and waits for the next one to come along, hoping they will be more malleable.

                                                This is an interesting portrayal. It shows that while Sidereals influence Dragon-Blooded society, they do not control it - individual people and discrete moments in history can fall out of their control. They work with that, because they don't really have a choice and because they can live thousand of years, so they can afford to take a long-term view and accept losses and setbacks along the way.

                                                Then came Exalted: the Sidereals. In this book's intro fiction, the Mouth of Peace is described as a pitiable, "poor creature" "addled" by the "sorceries worked on her mind," basically a puppet for Chejop Kejak with no free will. That was a change in tone - and perhaps it set a new vision for the Sidereal/Dragon-Blooded relationship across the game line.

                                                I think the first depiction was more interesting. There are too few Sidereals - too few Gold Faction members - too few elders. They can't control Dynastic society, much less "the Dragon-Blooded" as a whole. For most of the time following the Usurpation, the Dragon-Blooded where in a state of constant warfare against both themselves and the enemies of Creation; there was nothing to control there. And when the Terrestrial Host was finally (partly) unified... it was under the control of a single Dragon-Blooded woman who could and would afford to tell off the Sidereals if they got delusions of controlling her.

                                                The Sidereals have been trying to influence and guide along Dragon-Blooded societies for centuries. They have met success and failure, but they've never been fully in control. Hell - some Dragon-Bloods are even aware of their existence, Arcane Fate notwithstanding. What they have most control over is their religion; the Immaculate Order is their tool, but even there they have issues (see the Mouth of Peace above). Dynastic society as a whole? Yeah, right.

                                                And that's not even getting into Outcastes.

                                                Mnemon's undercover Sidereal mentor almost died when she Exalted in a fit of anger against him, and she was a child. These are not people you can manipulate at your leisure.
