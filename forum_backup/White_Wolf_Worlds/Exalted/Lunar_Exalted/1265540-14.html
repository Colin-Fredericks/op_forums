                Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post

                I don't really see the link there, could you say more?
                I may have been slightly mixing up some things there, but...

                Okay, so as has been brought up in this thread, the corebook, and the preview about the Solar Bond, part of the significance that it has to the Lunars is in their overall mindset and stability. Establishing the Bond with Solars is part of what allowed Lunars to adapt to the First Age, and definitively losing it by means of most of the Solars being removed indefinitely (and quite possibly still for those Lunars whose mates' Exaltations remained free, if circumstances in which they ever met again became vanishingly rare) has destabilised them a bit, and is a driving force in their vendetta against the Realm.

                These are supposed to be important parts of the Lunar experience, but they can't really be represented mechanically. They even go beyond concrete or emergent elements of the connection between Lunars and Solars, because they're about something that it does for Lunars internally, rather than examining the nature of the relationship itself.

                Even if that's not explicitly part of Lunar Essence Fever, developer comment has clarified that it would function along the same principles of being a description of the inner experience of being that kind of Exalt, that the onus is on the players to fulfil (and which is hopefully written in a compelling enough manner to warrant it).

                So there are factors to it other than the actual strength of the Intimacy that can give it a mythic weight, and set it apart from Ties to other people.

                It will be interesting to see how the book portrays the manner in which the Silver Pact has talked about the Bond in the interim. Presuming that it's not some big secret kept from initiate Lunars (which seems like it would be hard to sustain and counterproductive), there's the matter of how it might be a thing that a few Lunars have spent a fair amount of time thinking about, and in the event that it actually comes to them, might be something that they're intrigued to pursue even if they're not yet at the level where they're willing to risk significant sacrifices or inconveniences for it.

And the Bond will ensure that, even if the two are separated for an extended period of time, the Lunar's impression never just fades away to irrelevance.

                                    Yeah, this is a slightly tricky one.
                                    On the one hand, the whole point about the Bond is that it is, you know, a special bond.
                                    On the other hand, you don't really want a PC to be much more connected to one PC than the others.

                                    With my own Lunar, his Solar Bond 5 stuck him to the Night Caste, Prince Meek. And this stuck him to the other PCs too, because the other Night worked with/for Prince Meek, the Changing Moon is married to Prince Meek, etc. And if I play another Lunar in the future, I could do the same (though that'd mean my old retired character had died, which'd probably really upset Meek and his wife, my adopted daughter).
                                    Occasionally, he did side with Prince Meek over other PCs, but as Meek is basically the party's leader it didn't really matter. And the PCs all got on well, so they didn't have big arguments or anything.

                                    But that's not always the case.


                                    As I've previously mentioned, I ran a long-running game that I eventually stopped because I thought it would become unrunnable. But by popular demand, another ST took over.
                                    Anyway, we have the big problem that most of the PCs just... don't like each other very much.
                                    One is a semi-righteous bumbling priest, one is a radical theocratic revolutionary, one is a transhumanist absolute monarch, one is an anarchist atheist (she believes in Gods, she just dislikes them), and one is a fundamentalist zealot and ultra-nationalist warlord.
                                    It wasn't too bad before, but now several of them are actually in power in nations that border each other and thus have tensions over resources, their radically different approaches to rule have caused relations to break down, to the extent that despite the threat of Realm invasion, the warlord and queen went to war for a couple of sessions.

                                    I made a Solar that was a member of the Cult, since 2 PCs are members, so I thought it'd be a good way to build links with the party.
                                    But it turned out that by having more links with some PCs than others, he ended up (despite resisting) on one side against others, leading to his death.
                                    The Cult and the Queen were almost at war, as she refused to withdraw a garrison from a Cult-aligned city. The Changing Moon, who doesn't trust or like the Queen at all, asked me to sneak in to the Queen's palace and listen to the peace negotiations, because if they went to war the Queen would Death Ray the Cult's base, so I should kill her before the war could begin. The negotiations ended inconclusively but acrimoniously. I didn't actually want to kill her, as I wanted to work together with her against the Realm, and she generally tried to be friends with me, so I tried kidnapping her, so I could take her to the Cult and force people to sit down in person. I failed, was captured, and chose death over magical slavery.


                                    So, for the last 2 sessions I've been playing a Lunar, Dr Soma Vaidya, a Snake-totem kung-fu doctor. Poison charms and a lot of martial arts.

                                    I'm not really sure what to do about having a Solar Mate. On the one hand, it seems a good way to have an excuse to like one of the PCs, even if they're not very likeable people. Just go "yep, I've got a magical positive intimacy to that PC, and even if they come off as a pretty mean person it'll keep coming back so Soma would keep working with them, rather than leaving the party."
                                    But the problem is that, if they keep arguing, then I might end up siding with my Solar Mate rather than trying to get them all to work together.

                                    I did actually ask the guy playing the character I considered least likely to fight others, but he didn't want a Lunar Mate.

                                    The ST's hopeful that his next session's plot will bring most of the PCs together, away from their nations, to work together in Varang and start a revolution. So ideally I'd see how that goes, and pick accordingly (probably pick the character who my PC would be least likely to like). But, in theory, you're supposed to know your Solar Mate as soon as you meet them, not after a couple of sessions of hanging out together.

                                    I can sympathise with the poster (I forget who) on this forum who was complaining that you can't have more than one Solar Mate! Just having the entire party as Mates would solve the issue.

                                    I am considering asking the least likeable character's (the Dawn) player if I can be his Mate, and asking the other players (who unlike the Dawn have very good social stats) to just throw a lot of social influence at me so I have equal positive intimacies to everyone.
