                            Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post

                            Aren't you paying attention? The 'Team : Sidereal' shirts clearly outline the ultimate goals of the Usurpation in bold type along the back side, along with a complete description of the Great Prophesy.

                            I mean, I don't know what game YOU'VE been playing, but typically Sidereal Exalted are really honest about what they want you to do. They don't really so much manipulate or subtly guide people, they just kinda show up and tell you what they want (usually, by pointing at the T-shirt and exclaiming 'Duh' really loudly).
                            Not that I don't agree with your analysis of the Usurpation in the context of historical revolutions - I do, and I'd really like to see it get a more nuanced treatment, given how amazing Exalted tends to be when it applies modern conceptions of nuance to myth and legend - but your post makes me unironically want to see a Sidereal Charm that lets them foster teamwork and cooperation towards an agenda by passing out T-shirts with cheaply-printed motivational logos or slogans like you'd get at a corporate seminar.

                            Regarding the role of Lunars in the Usurpation, I personally favor the explanation that much of the groundwork was done by sowing as much mistrust as possible beforehand using every dirty trick in the book, so that fewer Sids and Deebs would get pureed by their primary targets' mates when the time came. When Solar lie-detection runs up against Lunar perfect lying and vice-versa, the safe option for both of them is to just assume the other is lying (Prisoner's Dilemma, ho!). That said, I'd be leery of spelling out TOO much about how everything went down for one, simple reason: for many players, the Usurpation is as much a part of their character's backstory as their mortal, pre-Exaltation life, if not moreso. Locking in too many details locks out a lot of cool background possibilities, especially when there's a pronounced tendency for player and ST alike to see anything written in a book as ironclad and immutable.

                                    I don't mind a Usurpation where not much is specified, but that doesn't really work with a 'not much wil specified EXCEPT for the lame splat story'.

                                    Personally, I take the High First Age at face value. This is what I know about it from what I have read; which is why I have issues, because many of the things printed about the High First Age seem to contradict one another.

                                    In the High First Age, the dominant political entity is the Circle, not the splat. Multiple circles command immense influence. Many of those who are in command of these circles are having struggles with the Great Curse ; comrades accept these difficulties based upon long-standing relationships (and the fact they endure the same thing), and generally speaking Circles look out for each other. Mixed circles of multiple Exalt types are extremely common. They are, in fact, the rule... not the exception.

                                    Absent any real conflict or pressing world-ending threats, the Exalted have turned to insular pursuits. Pet projects... either personal, or circle-based... are the order of the day. Deliberative control is growing more and more tenuous as more and more Elder Solar Exalted declare themselves autonomous tributaries. 'Everybody knows' those guys are doing stuff they shouldn't be, but nobody really does anything about it. It's institutionalized and the Exalted are so august they aren't about to be challenged on this.

                                    Because they don't have so many REAL battles to fight, the Exalted tend to get very emotional and passionate about various ideological and political issues - let's take a real-world modern example that doesn't translate into Exalted as a hypothetical placeholder example : drug policy. Exalted fight duels over drug policy. They talk shit about drug policy endlessly. They describe people who don't agree with the policy THEY agree with in hyperbolic, inflammatory terms.

                                    When speaking with Dragonblooded... or anybody else, for that matter... objectively speaking, a description of the Great Prophesy comes across as culty madness. Only Sidereals are going to understand the magnitude and importance of the prophesy; nobody else really appreciates what they do as much. One must also view this in the realm of the established metaphysics, and how silly the prophesy is -to begin with- in a setting that repeatadly emphasis the importance of free will. Destiny is already proven to not-really-apply to Exalted, so forming a coalition based upon a prophesy that the Exalted will do something in some nebulous future is a ridiculous idea.

                                    Therefore, the far more effective strategy would seem to be to exploit already existing political divisions and bring the Realm down using the same tools and methods the Sidereals have -always- used.

                                    Instead, we get the 'Red Wedding'. In Game of Thrones, this worked because we were describing a pre-industrial society of far reduced magnitude and complexity... and, we were discussing -people- who were unified behind clan banners, not God-Kings plucked from various eras/locations in Creation of various age half-maddened by limit break.

                                    It's honestly pretty silly to suggest the Solars would -ever- get in the same room at this point, without serious defenses and paranoia involved that would have made the Usurpation kinda a wash.

                                    We describe this world experiencing fundamental political angst due to these god-kings going semi-crazy and becoming more and more irrational, but then we have them all act like a unified entity when confronted with other 'splats'.

                                    I would rather see all of this sillyness completely disregarded in favor of 'A coup happened'. I think the stupid dinner should be gone ; I think the stupid 'over in a day more or less' war should be gone; I think the stupid declarations of Lunar and Dragonblooded motivations on the macro level should be gone.

                                    It should be declared a mess that the Sidereals came out on top of (probably due more to Machiavellian maneuvers than martial pussiance) and left at that.

                                    This is Lunars thread, so I'll point out that while I'm focusing on the Usurpation here, that's only because the Lunar 'identity issue' in the first age is a symptom of the greater disease.
