    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
    Hey, remember when Lunars had shapeshifting powers?

    By all means, let's get rid of the extraneous crap that doesn't really make sense with "zoanthropic shapeshifting demigod". But when people start arguing that a Lunar can't become a pack of wolves despite the power to become a swarm of bees, I'm going to get pissy.

    "Turning into animals" is what they do, and there's really not very far to GO with that idea. Cutting out "become multiple animals at once" seems unnecessary.

    What would this turning into a pack of wolves entail? Are those wolves benefiting from all the Lunars self-enhancement magic? Do they all have a wolf's health track? Does killing one kill the Lunar?

    If each of those wolves is a copy of the Lunar in wolf form with all associated abilities then this leads don the path of madness. If all but one wolf if just a meatpuppet the Lunar happened to have made from their body then it is not mechanically different from letting the Lunar call up/bond/familiarize a pack of wolves. Heck, if battle groups work anything like 2E where they get bonuses based on size, this is a one charm/knack supermode or some flat/boring bonuses.

    "Turning into animals" is a thing they can do, it is not what they do. There are socialite lunars who might occasionally turn into a cat when they need to run away or mystic lunars who are too busy with their astral projection or summoning demons to ever bother getting a new form. Heck, there are even warrior lunars who are too busy hitting you with evocations to want to change into animals.

    Edit: I know you like Lunars and that they have consistently gotten the short stick. That doesn't mean they need to burrow into one mechanic and inflate it beyond hat it was designed to do. They shouldn't need to, and if the devs do as good a job as we hope they wont have to.

                        It would be better for Lunars to not be able to turn into a swarm of bees than for Lunars to turn into a swarm of bees and a pack of wolves, IMO, and the fact that you feel this way is a strong argument for taking any swarm-form ability from them. That said, I think the current setup -- where they can become a swarm of bees but not a pack of wolves -- is probably fine.

                        I wouldn't want Lunars to canonically be able to turn into mycelium, either, despite the character concept I mentioned earlier. It warps what Lunars actually are if they go too far in that direction. "Turns into different types of creature" is not the wellspring of all Lunar thematics, so you can't just blindly extrapolate from that or you end up with things like that guy who thinks the Great Contagion is horning in on Lunars' turf.

                        Lunars do a hell of a lot of things besides turning into animals, just like Solars do a hell of a lot of things besides glowing and leadership and Green Lantern shenanigans. Trying to buff them up by expanding the breadth of "turning into animals" is likely to get silly fast. Like, Hungry All-Consuming Cloud is already pushing it.

                                                    Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                                    EDIT: Hmm... I'm curious. I don't expect answers on these from the EX3 Team, but they're things I may look up when the Core is in my hands. If they're statted up in there, do wolves get any special options/tricks/benefits for operating as a pack? If so, do these come up when wolves are part of a battle group (on top of anything normal battle groups get) or only for wolves as individual actors working together? I guess an underlying question concerns how static a battle group must be.
                                                    I would wager the point of a battle group is to easily represent a larger number of combatants than is feasible to run as individuals, combining that with notions of how groups of people fight together and react to fighting together.

                                                    If an ST runs a mob of 20 angry peasants as a battle group, simply being a battle group shouldn't necessarily provide those 20 peasants or the Exalted they're fighting a super huge advantage or disadvantage over fighting 20 peasants as individuals. The point is to make it OOC easier to run such a combat (and end it in ways other than killing every discrete living thing inside the battle group) by having only one "unit" to interact with (as opposed to 20), with its own internal rules like "for every X damage taken, suffer Y stat degradation and Z morale effect."

                                                    I would imagine that a battle group possesses the normal "special" powers of its constituent entities, plus specific group-only stats like Drill, Morale, and Might to represent certain group functions. A pack of wolves would likely have a higher Drill than a random peasant mob simply because a wolf pack is a family that lives and hunts together, versus a bunch of angry people who grab pitchforks and coalesce into a mob for safety and intimidation purposes.

                                                    Think about how swarms are often represented in RPG video games like Final Fantasy: when you fight a swarm of hell-wasps it's visually a blob of things but mechanically it's got one health pool and you target the swarm as a whole rather than individual wasps. I would say that is the model to reference.

                                                            Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                                            I would wager the point of a battle group is to easily represent a larger number of combatants than is feasible to run as individuals, combining that with notions of how groups of people fight together and react to fighting together.

                                                            If an ST runs a mob of 20 angry peasants as a battle group, simply being a battle group shouldn't necessarily provide those 20 peasants or the Exalted they're fighting a super huge advantage or disadvantage over fighting 20 peasants as individuals. The point is to make it OOC easier to run such a combat (and end it in ways other than killing every discrete living thing inside the battle group) by having only one "unit" to interact with (as opposed to 20), with its own internal rules like "for every X damage taken, suffer Y stat degradation and Z morale effect."
                                                            Simplification is indeed part of the reason, though story significance is another component. Some random wolf pack may become a battle group, meanwhile the Solar who was literally raised by wolves might find the members of his family important enough to be treated as individuals. It's possible there have been system revisions, but talk we got a while ago suggests there can be a difference in the difficulty of those fights, even if the actual number of wolves involved is the same.

                                                            Originally posted by Holden
                                                            To be clear, assuming we're fishing our ninjas out of clone tanks, a battle group of 100 ninjas is going to be stronger in an absolute sense than a single ninja, insofar as that it has bonuses the single guy doesn't. On the other hand, when you engage the single ninja, the system is going to process your conflict as a high-energy cinematic battle between two actors worth paying attention to; whereas when you engage the 100 ninja katamari, it's going to interpret that as you laying waste to a bunch of them with every attack, Bruce Lee style; so the one guy is going to seem tougher. If that one ninja is the ninja that killed your brother, then he's never going to get rolled into the katamari because he's story-significant; if he's one of the three ninjas that killed your brother, all three of them are going to always be rendered individually (and in this sense may be more dangerous than the ninja katamari simply because the nature of the system favors gang-up tactics over single big attacks in many situations). This isn't considered a problem, because a battle against the three ninjas that killed your brother should be more dramatic and memorable than chopping your way through the break room at 1-800-Rent-A-Ninja.
                                                            They sound like they're trying to build in factors here that we've not fully seen to avoid perverse incentives. One of the factors we have is quoted below (which also answers part of my unexpressed-but-kinda-underlying question about what level of distance or being "unattached" from one another would cover battle grouping characters who aren't distinct enough to be individuals).

                                                            Originally posted by EX3 What We Know Wiki
                                                            Q: (Mizu005)
                                                            Hmm, I'm going to go out on a limb and assume that you are free to divide a group into separate blobs instead of one blob as it suits you.

                                                            A: (Holden)
                                                            You're not, actually. You're only allowed to split a group if it's geographically divided WAY the fuck apart-- like, at opposite ends of a valley doing a pincer maneuver. Otherwise, you keep stacking on until you hit the maximum size for a BG, and only then start a new one.
