    Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post
    Of course, once a Solar makes the effort to truly apply themselves, the cap on their Excellencies means that they can temporarily throw more dice at it, which must be acknowledged, so Solar Socrates can likely win a philosophical debate with Lunar Aristotle, which may lead some to argue that he cheated...which may well open up a philosophical question itself!
    To be fair, in Exalted 2nd edition, Lunars can get higher dice pools than Solars.

    A Lunar with Essence 5, Intelligence 6 (Thanks to the Impossible [Attribute] Improvement general charm) and 5 dice in Lore with a 3 dot specialty.

    This gives the Lunar a base pool of 14 dice. She can add an additional 6 dice thanks to the First Excellency for a total of 20. Then activating Inevitable Genius Insight allows her to increase the cap on Excellencies by her Essence (5) for a total of 25 dice; further Inevitable Genius Insight adds an automatic success, which is roughly equal in value to 2 rolled dice.

    So essentially the Lunar can potentially get an effective dice pool of 27 dice on a mental roll dealing with a very specific subject.

    Solars are usually sitting at a dice cap of 23 (5 stat, 5 Ability, 3 specialty + 10 dice from an Excellency).

    The discrepancy gets higher when you compare physical dice pools as Devastating Ogre Enhancement adds 2 to the Lunar's physical Attributes, potentially giving her say, 8 Dex, 5 Melee, 3 specialty for a total of 16 base, and with Relentless Lunar Fury allows her to add an addition (8 + 5) dice for a total of 29. And of course the automatic success from RLF essentially acts as 2 rolled dice, so effectively the Lunar is rolling a dicepool of 31 to a Solar's 23.

    On the downside, a Lunar can't sustain that high of a dicepool for very long at all; Instinctive Attribute Excellency only allows a Lunar to add 5 dice for free.So they;'ll burn out of Essence pretty quickly trying to keep those dice pools up for long.

                                        According to the 2e core book and all subsequent books, attributes, in Exalted, represent potential. It makes perfect sense, mechanically and thematically, for Lunars as the embodiment of infinite potential to embody attributes the way Solars embody abilities. Abilities are about skill.                                         It seems that a lot of players never really grasped that concept, and so any discussion about Lunars as what they are in the setting results in a lot of people calling them "Silver Solars" based on the assumption that Solars (using your "power level" example) are supposed to be inherently better at things, whatever things you're talking about. Which, of course, they are not, never have been, and (I hope) never will be. There are lots of things Solars aren't the best at, and a large part happen to be embodied by their metaphysical mates, the Lunar Exalted.

                                                General capability is a Solar thing. Lunars are monster saints. All of their Attribute-based Charms should be keyed around being monster saints. General high capability and effects provide nothing interesting on this front. This is why the second go at Lunars is cited as having failed, though nowhere near as badly as the first go at it.

                                                It seems that a lot of players never really grasped that concept, and so any discussion about Lunars as what they are in the setting results in a lot of people calling them "Silver Solars" based on the assumption that Solars (using your "power level" example) are supposed to be inherently better at things, whatever things you're talking about. Which, of course, they are not, never have been, and (I hope) never will be. There are lots of things Solars aren't the best at, and a large part happen to be embodied by their metaphysical mates, the Lunar Exalted.
                                                The position that you're championing is one that I formerly found myself in back when this material was new and shiny and everyone was just getting out of the first edition Lunar torture chamber. After a number of years of digging deeper into everything, however, all the flaws began to reveal themselves. The organization was ungainly, with pointless prerequisites, fillers and of course, not much in the way of shapeshifting. There was no celebration of the game that Lunars had been born of. The meaning of the Spirit Shape and Tell was still left unstated. And, of course, there was Relentless Lunar Fury, which you had to take in order to get your "real" dice pool. Which was also so brief in duration that it hobbled the capabilities of the Lunars beyond belief.

                                                We all perfectly well understand what the book was trying to accomplish. And most everyone now realizes that it was far from the best way to go about accomplishing the task of making Lunars fun. It was an exercise in tick counting and using a repainted God Hand to temporarily be important. It was the system shrugging its shoulders about other potential forms and just not caring about how fauna was statted out, even though turning into animals is a rather noticeable feature of being a Lunar. And it also chained the Lunars to the Wyld even more viciously than first edition did, since most of their interesting, non-copied Charms had the rider of "must be used outside of where everything important and interesting is happening."

                                                Which is why many people in this thread are rejecting most of the theses of your posts. They simply just don't work as a comparison between Solars and Lunars. Even if there are certain small numbers of Charms that can just be shifted around a bit, like Righteous Lion Defense and Ox-Body Technique, by no means does it make it okay to make all Charms according to this scheme.
