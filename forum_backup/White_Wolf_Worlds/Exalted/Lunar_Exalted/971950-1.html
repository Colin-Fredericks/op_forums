                            I can see a couple different possible 'roles' for the Lunar, both based on their mechanical function (i.e. what they were 'designed' to be capable of) and the personality of their patron.

                            1: The Guardians of Creation. Like Luna and his/her/its/whatevers deep and enduring love for Gaia (and deep love for trolling/devastating the Fair Folk)...the Lunars were created to safeguard Creation. The Solars were intended to rule, to lead--The Sidereals to advise--and the Lunars to protect. They were intended to be out in the world, using their unique capabilities to blend in...to go where no one else could...and to crush the enemies of Creation wherever they might be.

                            Honestly, this one feels a little narratively weak to me, because technically all of the Exalted are intended to protect Creation...but it's an interpretation I've seen before.

                            2: The "Bottom-Up" equivalent to the Solars' "Top-Down" approach. Solars lead from the front...larger than life heroes that say "Follow Me!" and don't need to look back to make sure you are coming. Lunars lead from within...they mix into the crowd and say "Come on, you can do this," and inspire action from those around them.

                            Again, this one feels a bit weak, because it pigeonholes Lunars quite a lot...though I do like how it has the sort of 'counterpart' feel that Luna and Sol already share.

                            3: A balance to the Solars. A Lunar may not be able to match a Solar power for power...but they are (or rather, should be) adaptable enough to stand against them anyway if the need arose. The Solars draw on the Perfection of the Sun. The problem is that this can distance them from their humanity, even without the Great Curse. Lunars also run the risk of distancing themselves from humanity...but have a tendency to go in the opposite direction...towards the feral, instinctive side. And besides that...Lunars know exactly what it is like to hit rock bottom, they know what it's like to suffer and want to give in...because that is exactly where they were then Luna came to them. Solars may not have had such an experience...their Exaltation likely occurred during one of their finest moments as a mortal and (barring the Great Curse, which wasn't in the plan when they were designed) it basically would have been constantly getting better from there, and thus have a harder time relating to it...something the Lunars would give them.

                            Still doesn't feel quite like a definite 'role,' honestly.

                            4: "I don't know, dammit, figure out your own destiny...I gave you all the tools you need."

                            This....sounds a lot like Luna, actually. Luna is very big on freedom, endurance, and forging your own path. It honestly wouldn't surprise me if Luna didn't pick out some concrete 'role' for her chosen and, instead, was like "Here, I gave you a Swiss Army Knife set of powers...use them as you best see fit!"

                            Honestly, of these so far...I'm liking #4, with perhaps a few glimmers of the other ones stuck in as the sorts of things Lunars 'tended' to do.

                                                            This is what I've been trying to say, but you've said it better.

                                                            The first step of Exalted splats is "Solars let you play really powerful but still human-esque heroes, Dragon-blood let you play aristocratic elemental heroes, Lunars let you play bestial god-monster heroes, Sidereals let you play Secret Old Kung-Fu Masters, etc".

                                                            The "what is this Exalt for" is a setting justification made up later to give the splat some backstory.

                                                            The writers should be going "Lunars are awesome bestial god-monster heroes who steal faces and turn into badass beats of legend!...
                                                            I guess we should come up with a reason the Gods made them. Uh, because that's what you get when Luna makes Exalts I guess? Or maybe it was useful to take on bestial demon-monsters by becoming badass monsters too? Or something?"

                                                            Rather than "Lunars were made to, uh, be jack-of-all-trades I guess? To cover the holes other Exalts didn't cover and adapt to whatever Third-Circle Demon they were fighting that day? So, how can we make a splat with powers based on that? I guess, make kind of good at everything but not really good at anything?"
                                                            That's really the wrong way to design them.

                                                            People should pick up the Lunar book and go "Wow Lunars are really cool! I want to play a Lunar so I can play a bestial anti-hero who steals the face of a Dragonblood's servant and then sneaks into his room and turns into a giant bear-monster and mauls his face off! That'll be awesome!"
                                                            Or, alternatively, "I enjoy problem-solving by having a toolbox of different forms and working out how to leverage them to solve particular problems, rather than just rolling a bucket of dice at them, and Lunar mechanics seem to do this, but in a less wacky way than Sidereal mechanics."
                                                            Or, alternatively, "I want to play a champion of the barbarian peoples who takes the form of their patron animal to lead them to victory."
                                                            Or even "I want to play a werewolf in this fantasy game. Werewolves are cool."

                                                            Because if they pick up the book and go "So, the point of these guys is the gods made them to kind of do a bit of everything because the other Exalts were more specialised?" Well, that's not very inspiring. It's probably going to make you want to play one of these other more interesting-sounding splats, which is a pity, because IMO Lunars are one of the more interesting splats, they've just been written badly both mechanically* and in terms of the fluff (because the game is obsessed with Solars vs Dragonblood/Sidereals vs Abyssals and forgets about the fact that the Solars are the new kids on the block and the Lunars have been fighting the Realm and ruling barbarian kingdoms for 1500 years, then tries to slot Lunars into a setting that's not really designed for them to exist.
                                                            But I think the Devs know this, and what they've said about Lunars seems to be generally going on the right track. What they want Lunars to be like seems much better, frankly, than what some "fans" seem to want, which is not actually Lunars at all.)

                                                            *Well, No Moons. Changing Moons and Full Moons weren't perfect, but I wouldn't say they were written worse mechanically than Solars or Abyssals or Dragonblood. Possibly better than Dragonblood. And obviously they were written better mechanically than Sidereals. I'd much rather have run a Lunar game than a Sidereal game, in terms of mechanics.
