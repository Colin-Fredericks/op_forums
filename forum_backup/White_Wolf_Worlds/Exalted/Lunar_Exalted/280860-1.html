        Several reasons really, lets see:

        1. You know that guy in the band who's not the lead singer but is just as cool (the Slash for every Axl)? Well, in a setting plagued by unending lines about how awesomely awesome, important, significant, powerful are the solars, lunars occupy that place in my perception of the setting. Sure solars get most of the spotlight but... there's always these guys in the background that don't give a damn if writers make them justice, cause we all know they are cool as hell.

        2. Animals. I'm a biologist and an illustrator, so lunars give me both a chance to implement strange biological aesthetics and themes to characters: the alligator shaman in the swamp, the photosynthetic mollusk undersea sage, the sparrow ninja with the poisonous feathers, etc.

        3. Rivalry. Yep, lunars above other exalts hold this special potential to become a solars best buddy or biggest rival. Sure you can have an abyssal playing that part, but he's more like a bizarro or shadow rival. This is why I've always liked the idea of pushing lunar power level above sidereals, and let them have their own strange way to rival the absolute power of the solar. I wanna read about how when the old golden king of the first age went mad, it was his silver comrade that took him down for the sake of the kingdom, and not just how impossible or unlikely this would'be been given the stats and systems.

                Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                I don't get how this is a Lunar-specific game theme. Any splat could do this; it's more of a general game premise and storytelling model than anything else.
                It's the idea that people, regardless how open minded or innovative they are, see splat types in certain terms.

                If a player goes with a solar, you kind of automatically expect them to:
                1. go to their old tomb to gear up.
                2. Bring their circle together
                3. Be the best at their niche "thing"
                4. Change the course of history
                5. Reinvent the First Age or go off into the Wyld and build a new world.

                If a player chooses to play a Lunar, they can do all of the above or they are equally likely to turn into a cat, curl up into a ball and sleep through the adventure.

                Because Lunars have so few storyline requirements nearly any form of behavior is appropriate, add in the fact that they turn into animals and basically any internet vid of animals doing goofy things becomes a role play guide.

                So Lunars basically become the perfect character splat for people with ADD or commitment issues since what ever random thing takes their fancy is thematically correct for the exalt type. Obviously Lunars are good for other player types, but they really have the least back story to know, the simplest of reasons to come together and have the least on-rails expectations.

                    I have rolled this question around in my head for a very long time. I'm no longer completely sure I do like them; I may have just been in "I want to like them" territory for a while. We'll see how they shape up in EX3.

                    Regardless, reasons for liking or wanting to like them may include:
                    - The Lancer: I've never had a lot of drive or attraction toward being the star of the show (or at least a traditional one). But I do get a lot of personal satisfaction from supporting something or someone I care about. Even if the implementation was rocky, there's an element of that in Lunars that speaks to me.
                    - Animal Symbolism: What can I say? I've always been a sucker for this stuff. Whether playing it straight or playing with expectations, I enjoy myself when it presents itself.
                    - Shapeshifting: Just a very fun, flexible, and powerful capability they've got, straight out of the box. Could do with more options for changing others, but still, cool stuff I rather enjoy.

                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                    I don't get how this is a Lunar-specific game theme. Any splat could do this; it's more of a general game premise and storytelling model than anything else.
                    That was my take, too. Almost every splat can just go in for general exploration and game play just fine, or they can go in for their respective splat-specific narratives. Lunars don't have a meaningfully distinct splat-specific narrative in 2e, so this comes across to me as fewer options, not more.

                        The Thousand Streams River has been confirmed as not coming back in 3e (in fact, it was confirmed as axed back in 2e, as in "will never be mentioned again.")
                        And that still depresses me.

                        Thousand Streams River.... whilst not exactly the main thing I liked about Lunars was very evocative of it. They had options. Well, all splats have those to some degree, but Lunars had existed as a society largely apart from Creation and the Realm. And that meant they could interact with those things in ways other groups couldn't. More than any other splat they felt like they had potentail for individuality. Sidereals are bound to make sure Fate works, DBs aren't bound to anything specifically but usually have very close ties to a specific nation. Infernals and Abyssals have their masters to serve or rail against, and Solars have kinda a preordained path to a point.

                        But Lunars?

                        A Crossroads Society member could try to institute a program of education amongst the slaves of Deshan. Another could create a nation of illiterate monkey slaves to serve her every whim as she continued her personal studies into Solar Circle Sorcery. You could get a Lunar Guildsman in Nexus selling slaves to Malfeas, or a Lunar Tribesman breaking up those same caravans. All of that's consistant with the Silver Pact. They're all the same people. They just don't have a narrative they're bound to.

                        Lunars had infinite variety. They could be tricksters, or monsters, heroes or villains. They could stand for anything or anyone or they could tear down whatever part of the world offended them. And that's what I loved about them.

                        (Sorry if this is negative. Trying not to start a flame war, but... 3E Lunars just depress me thus far.)

                                The main problem with the Silver Pact and TSR is they were trying to port the Garou Nation (or the Ahabi, or the Beast Courts...same concept) into Creation. The Garou Nation and those other groups have specific purposes in OWoD and their constituent Tribes and customs reinforce and justify their existence. The goal of the Garou Nation is pretty clear: prevent the Apocalypse. It's straightforward, it's fundamental to the nature of werewolf society, it's easy to use and implement and create intra-group drama for because of unique Tribal identity and history. That purpose and those constituent entities do not transplant well into Creation and a society of only a few hundred Lunars which should, logically, be spread across the face of the world. That and how most of the Silver Pact's constituent groups made no sense for why they would be separate entities within the umbrella...

                                The TSR may have had some potential but it just never made sense why the Lunars as a whole chose to do that, and there was no viable long-term plan for the 2e TSR. It was basically "hey everyone do your own thing." I much prefer 3e's concept of maybe creating or guiding a culture to weaponize it against the Realm, with the goal of deploying several such cultures against Realm holdings simultaneously across Creation, but then again the 3e concept of a more unified Lunar culture makes a lot more sense than the Silver Pact or 2e TSR ever did. Like, TSR-esque projects are perfectly in theme for some Lunars, and would be pretty different depending on what cultural value they wanted to emphasize, but by making it a universal thing that all Lunars do just made no sense (kind of like the 2e concept of Lunars = Stewards to begin with).

                                So, like...to me it's just a huge morass that I won't miss it. 2e did this thing where it painted splats with way-too-broad brushes at a baseline and later books built on that to destroy any sense of nuance. Basically everyone but the Solars and Alchemicals suffered from this.

                                                    Well, there were a bunch of reasons they called my attention at first.

                                                    First, the silver motif caught my eye. Yes, a bit shallow, but gold is super tacky while silver tends to look much better on everything :P

                                                    Then there is the TSR. I actually liked the idea of people trying to make it so that societies didn't need Exalted for basic every day survival. It seemed smart and actually rather constructive.

                                                    Then there is the fact that they aren't LOOK AT ME I'M SO AWESOME READ AS THE ENTIRE SETTING REVOLVES AROUND ME like the Solars were. They're strong (theorethically) without being THE most powerful actors in the setting assumed to eventually triumph at anything given enough time like Solaroids felt. That struck a nice balance in my scale of interests.

                                                    I liked the whole intimacy focus idea that people posited, too. See, here's a thing. Exalted enshrines ambition, wanting to be the very best like no one ever was, having great huge ambitions to change the world in your image, living for great big ideals good or bad, all that jazz. These are really hard for me to get into. I can do it, but it's a definite roleplaying challenge. If I was given the world in a platter I'd trade it in for a little home at the mountain with a good internet connection and close to my family. But Exalting because you simply refuse to go down no matter how much shit the universe drops on you, I can get that. Dedicating yourself to people, and to protecting that which you love, I can get that. That is satisfying to me. I leave being the king of the pirates or whatever for whoever wants it.

                                                    On that note, I LIKE being support. I like seeing others succeed thanks to me practically more than I like succeeding myself. I don't mind being the backbone that gets no face time.

                                                    And on the note of the Exaltation, the whole perseverance thing. I like the heroes who go on and on and on even as they bleed and die, refusing to fall down until they fulfill their purpose.

                                                    Then there was the whole illusionary and fluidity motif, the whole stalker in the night business. I am a wellknown player of illusionists and other such types, and so the Lunars sounded great. Then, of course, I discovered that the Lunars had almost fuck all for proper illusions, which made me sad and annoyed.

                                                    Probably a handful of things more.

                                                    EDIT: And yeah, on the 3rd Edition Lunars comments... the dev comments and Masters of Jade notes have made my group basically expect them to be a villain splat for them, so, you know, I expect I'll probably be playing less Lunars in the future.
