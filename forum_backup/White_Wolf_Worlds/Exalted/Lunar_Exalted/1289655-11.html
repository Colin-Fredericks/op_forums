                    I like the angle of 'The Solar may gain a tie when they see the Lunar's true nature'. And for roleplaying it... Well, I remember a good friend of mine making a bit of advice for characters.

                    Your characters don't necessarily have the rules book in front of them.

                    You, the player, may know of this Solar Lunar Bond. You, the Solar's Player, and the ST or the Lunar's Player, will know about this Solar Bond. It's possible that the Lunar, from their mentor in the Pact, may know that some Lunars are Bonded to Solars.

                    However, it's entirely reasonable that the Solar, in-character, has no idea what this is, if you play it as 'Receives a mutual Tie'. Could be as simple as "Who was that other person in the battlefield? And why did he have such sad eyes?"

                    Disclaimer: In favor of fun and enjoyment, but may speak up to warn you that you're gonna step on a metaphorical land mine

                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post


                        I feel as though you're getting too hung up on the origin story, and disregarding the way that multi-faceted relationships across a number of lifetimes are both inherent to what is enduring about the Bond, and will form the substance of any First Age memories. A newly reacquainted Solar and Lunar seem to me to be a lot more likely to remember the last time their Essences were together than they are to a few decades after the War of the Gods.
                        What I'm getting hung up on is the precedent in usurpation of control: As written, a decision is made by the lore on behalf of the character that the player has to accept. Romantic or not, enthusiastic or not, you are being told that your character made this particular life/lives changing decision to get married. When a GM/ST/DM does it, we call that railroading.

                        Railroading can be done not-terribly, but you should only try if you have a good reason to need to. What is the reason here?

                        I am not talking about mechanical benefits or equality, I'm talking about 'Why is a player being told what their character decided to do back then, as opposed to being asked to make up something cool themselves?'

                        In a game that wants to embrace 'choices matter' as a core principle, there needs to be a good reason to restrict such an important choice to 'you got married' or 'you don't get to play with this story element'. What is that reason?

                        And this is different from the Usurpation, for example, because it dictates a choice rather than a fate. The ST is expected to inflict horrible fates on the PCs, but they are also expected to not touch their agency, their freedom to make choices.



                        I thank the Devs for the great game of Exalted!
