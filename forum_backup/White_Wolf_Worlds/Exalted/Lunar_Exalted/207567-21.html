                    300 dying each year out of 10,000 is 3% mortality, which as I said is about the mortality rate of World War I and considerably higher than the worst countries in the entire world to live in from a longevity standpoint. It's considerably higher than the mortality rate of most wars. So every single Dragonblooded is partaking in trench warfare for their entire lives.

                    3% mortality rate means that by the time you're twenty-four, half the people you grew up with are dead. By forty-seven, another half. Five Dragonblooded out of every 10,000 will make it to retirement age and only one will die of old age.

                    Numbers that "feel" right almost never are when it comes to statistics. You have to actually go and do the math. The math comes out to a society of long-lived people needing to have very low birth rates, not the typical Dragonblooded woman putting out a baby every twelve years. That in turn means you can't have the kinds of schools described where Dragonblooded are most or all of the students. It's simply not plausible. Either the Dragonblooded population is undergoing growth over time, Dragonblooded die at a rate equivalent to the bloodiest wars in human history and thus have shorter lifespans than their mortal peasants, or Dragonblooded are comparably sex-averse to modern Japan and only a tiny fraction of them end up actually bearing children at all. All of these require rewriting chunks of Realm culture and politics, because the current arrangement is impossible.

To go personal, I'd for darn sure sit up and take notice if 7.4% of, say, my graduating college class were dying every year.

                        And again, these are worse than Black Death rates: these are rates as if all those who died of the bubonic plague were compressed into a period of seven years.

                        That and considerations for the other circumstances, like the difference between everyday society in general and what's basically a subculture of a dangerous lifestyle. How does that percentage compare to gang related murder rates in one of the more violent American cities?
                        A quick bit of Googling doesn't turn up any definite numbers, but let's go with this as a benchmark: 782,500 gang members, with ~2000 gang-related homicides reported. If all of those deaths are gang members, that's, uh, a 0.25% death rate for actually being in a gang, so.

                        Or perhaps you meant "what percent of people are dying to gang violence, in violent cities." Again, hard to say, but let's overestimate: the city with the highest murder rate in America is (surprising no one) Detroit. Suppose all of those murders are gang-related; that's still only 54 out of every 100,000 people, or a 0.054% murder rate.

                        Or let me give another perspective for a "high risk" population: the Soviet Union took pretty appalling losses during World War 2, right? They lost something like 14% of their population during that time, which is horrific. Of course, that was the total over six years; on average during WW2, the Soviets only suffered about 2.5% mortality rates due to war, famine, and disease. To get the kinds of numbers we're talking about in this thread, you have to triple those rates.

                        Stop defending this number. Its only function in this thread is to make everyone go, "Well, that can't be right." Find an assumption to relax that doesn't require 90% of the fifteen-year-olds who Exalt as Dragon-Blooded to be dead by 45.

                                    The Lost Eggs don't come into consideration here because, as we've established, they overwhelmingly don't breed and more importantly, aren't counted as part of the 10,000 we're trying to keep constant. 300 births among Dynasts requires 300 deaths among Dynasts, which gives you World War I death tolls.
