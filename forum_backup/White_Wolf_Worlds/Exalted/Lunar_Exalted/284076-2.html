                Yeah, this is a pretty fantastic idea that needs developing.

                The only consistent theme I've seen about Lunars is that they change. They change their shape over the short term. They changed their Exaltations over the long term. They changed who they were. If you were to ask 10 Lunars what they do, you would get 10 different answers. And if you were to ask them again later, you would get 10 entirely new different answers. It kinda makes it hard to answer what the Lunars are all about, if the only thing they are is changing and adapting. Also, it's pretty reactive, rather than proactive, and I like my characters to be proactive.

                I like the idea of the Exaltation taking a while to really settle in on a Lunar, and some of that happening before the Lunar Exalts, and some of it after. I also like the idea that the Exaltation might decide to leave if the Lunar changes to become unfit for it. I like the idea that Lunars change everything. I just want someone who is more clever than me to integrate these themes with an aesthetic that I can understand, and make sure Lunars are as interesting in all regards as the other Exalted. That includes PCs, Elders, and the works.

                A Celtic aesthetic would actually work pretty well for them, I think. Celtic stories go beyond the summer of the story and into the winter of their characters. Modern day tales like the 'And they lived happily ever after.' In Celtic stories, that's the summer, and that's about the middle of the story. I think that works well for Lunars too. Here's an example:

                Celtic Hero:
                Spring: Hears seer say 'A child who picks up his weapons this day will die young, but be remembered forever.' Character picks up his weapons that day.
                Summer: Has fought many glorious battles, married, and is raising young children.
                Autumn: Children take up weapons and join the wars, just like their father, but too young. They are taken captive. Character picks up his weapons again, joins his old warband, and they go to rescue the children.
                Winter: The old warriors rescue the children, but many fall in battle, destroying their enemies. The hero is one of the last to fall, and he passes his weapons into the hands of his children, who fight their way to freedom.

                There's a lot more to Celtic stories, like a smaller scale (Lunars care for those close to them, or about nations, but not about concepts). Characters are often darker than elsewhere, but not less heroic: Revenge is a common theme, and is seen as honorable. Characters are reasonable, however: If an offense is given, they pay back what is due. Revenge only enters into it if the price is so steep the offender refuses to pay it or the offender is a real jerk and refuses to pay a reasonable price. And both sides will have supporters deciding the other guy is wrong. Same with duels, unless both sides really want to fight. That's ok then. And that is WAY better than Lunars and their debt-bond thing with silver tattooes. Very similar, but way better.

                Of course, the barbarian aesthetic sticks here too: To a Celt, if you had 25 men who followed you into battle, you were a king (or a chieftain, depending on how you want to translate the word). Also, arms and equipment were less important than your heroism.

                Oaths and geasa are awesome. That kind of became a Fair Folk thing, but Lunars should touch on it. I'd love to see that both sides claim the other side stole it from them. The four Celtic Geasa also are very appropriate for Lunars: Not sure if I can remember them all right now, but its something like Hospitality, honor, valour, and luck. I don't really want to go try to look these up, and I'm sure someone here can explain them much better than me (and correct me, because I know I'm wrong on those). That's WAY better than the Lunar thing of Obedience, Paranoia, Anger, Make a society.

                Lunars are not Werewolves. Give them something new. Please?

                    This is a brilliant idea and most assuredly in thematic harmony with the Lunars as they are presented. The offer of Exaltation is placed before the would-be hero, but they must survive a vision-quest and endure hardship and testing along the way. Very much in line with Lunar mindset. I also really like that the potential Lunar will meet possible allies/rivals and possibly a mentor along the way. This mimics the elements of the heroes journey as seen in many mythic traditions (which is what I'm sure the TC was going for).

                    An element that I think could be included is that the Caste of the Lunar is decided by how the potential Lunar decides to circumvent the obstacles placed within her path. A Lunar who prefers a direct, physical approach would more than likely Exalt as a Full Moon. The Exalt who uses cunning would likely Exalt as a Changing moon, and those who use their intellect or wits would likely exalt as a No-moon.

The titanic barbarian post Holden made definitely indicates they're definitely putting in a lot of thought here (in case that was in doubt). However, I don't think Lunars are ultimately going to be as comprehensively dissimilar from what they are as you might think. Spoilering a quote from John for space:

                                        Originally posted by John Mørke
                                        It isn't as hard as people think it is to make Lunars good. They have all the ingredients for a great design. It's the presentation that has always been the problem. A good presentation breaks down biases and eliminates misconceptions about your ideas. This is one of the fundamental concepts of good design.

                                        People turn away from classic Lunars and change them into something else because they aren't good at design.

                                        It's not a habit I foster with anyone I work with. I push them to understand the reason why something failed, to get to the closest statement of a problem in order to supply the best solution.

                                        It all goes back to my first axiom: You can never produce the best solution until you make the truest statement about what the problem is.

                                        Over time, that has grown to encompass another statement: You may not find the best solution your first try, but the one you find will lead you to the one you need.

                                        I think that people don't understand what was wrong with Lunars, so they avoid the problem by just getting rid of whatever they can't explain.

                                        This has caused Lunar fans to ask me to remove the following things:

                                        -Tattoos
                                        -Tells
                                        -Shapeshifting
                                        -Shapeshifting into animals
                                        -Solar bond
                                        -Association with barbarians
                                        -Elder Lunars
                                        -The Silver Pact
                                        -The lost castes

                                        Given the tremendous ingredients above, when what you have left isn't identifiable as what you started out with, you're doing it wrong.

                                        Naturally, the presentation on which they decide -- and, hell, what many of those things even mean in the new approach and context-- is hard to predict at this point. I just get the sense that Lunars are going to look both more different in some ways and more similar in others than most might expect. Or it may be some manner of optical illusion, where it looks mostly the same as what we've seen, but casting the right light on it causes something genuinely interesting and compelling to jump out. We shall see!

                                            I love what Lunars are, and what they CAN do. I like their powers in general (though they could use some clean up). What I don't love, are the little things that form the (very flimsy and loose) foundation. Their foundation seems to be what they don't like and haven't killed yet. They hate the Realm, but haven't torn it down yet. They hate the Sidereals, but are too afraid of them to strike directly. They are bonded to their Solar mates, except that mostly they hate them. They love Luna. They love Gaia because Luna does, and so they do too. Sometimes they love each other. And that's it. Sometimes they do things, but they either do it out of anger, or to screw with you, or they are super clinical about it. Killing it when they are done with it, or ignoring it and leaving it alone.

                                            This has started to change, a little bit, in some of the most recent stories. But not much, because there hasn't been much published since the changes started.

                                            An entire line of characters focused exclusively on their darker emotions is... depressing and dull. Even the Abyssals have a great deal more love in them, even if it is twisted into something dark. Even the Infernals love themselves. Lunars are dripping in self-loathing. They don't even have what little the worst of the other lines have. A line of characters who have nothing they really care about occupies a space more appropriately reserved for NPCs. Villains, anti-heroes, obstacles, people to be redeemed... but in a game like Exalted, which is all about hope and building and human potential, Lunars don't share that.

                                            What do Lunars love? What do Lunars want to do? Can a Lunar exist who isn't based on feeling either hatred or nothing?
