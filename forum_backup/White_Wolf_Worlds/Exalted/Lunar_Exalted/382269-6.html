    Second Edition Luna was fantastic. I loved her every bit as much as I hate the Silver Pact.

    Luna was glorious. Her aspects were varied such that her beastial side was only a fragment of the whole ; such that rather than 1 psuedo-monolithic "culture", we had 10 or 11 or so archetypes from which to "validate" our concepts, depending on which one we might emulate. She was a huntress and a seducer, not an animal guarding territory but a witch beckoning the very waves and storms with her allure. Many of the charms in that book became my favorites (be it customizing offspring or almost all the No Moon charms). Glories of Luna made me want play Lunars (and without exaggeration, when I did role another Lunar, I used that book (including its charms) more than the actual Lunar book.

    The Silver Pact is a flat faux-culture emulating WtA with a tiny fraction of the population (300 or less god-kings scattered on the extreme and opposite ends of Creation -- not an entire species of Beastman-like werewolf tribes). And the way it was written, some STs could (and would) use it like a mallet to smack you over the head, since one way or another you were expected to leave home and join them ; do their silly shamanistic rites ; embrace the tattooed deformity they pushed with their kool-aid ; and then owe them for it. Only Lunars & Sidereal Exalted have to worry about ancient 1st Age Elders who could easily show up to curbstomp you into line --- and only Lunars had the numbers and culture that encouraged conformity to a bunch of furries.

    .

    Much as the Abyssal Exalted have their Deathlords (and a few cities in Shadowlands), Infernals have their Yozis (and a few unique cities in Creation), Dragon-Blooded have Houses and Gens, Sidereals have their Bureaus ---- I'd nosebleed if Lunars had something as varied and rich as Luna's aspects as inspiration (such that if they have a sense of "cultural kinship", it be one that fosters and embraces their diversity). I loved that the charms in that book also drew upon some of those aspects (World-Walkers and Mother-of-Monsters in particular).

                Originally posted by Yishten View Post

                Pre-Glories, Luna seemed Mundane. Lack of detail doesn't automatically make one mysterious or fantastic --- it can also just result in "this NPC is not used at all" or "the ST defaults to the most generic, average idea they have" or "the ST copies some other lore".

                Infernals are a good comparison. I loved them as a concept since 1E -- but we never got a write-up so I never saw an ST use them (unless it was as super-Satanic antagonists in the most cliche villain roles ever).

                So

                The theory doesn't amount to practice.

                And as aforementioned, Glories of Luna revitalized the splat for me (such that I was able to get concepts approved that - going just off the Lunar book - STs previously thought were too weird). The word count spent on her only enriched Lunars, validating those who didn't want to be the 235th "generic glorified beastman trickster-warrior in silver ink".

                        In regards to Luna, whatever else, I do NOT want wordcount spent detailing five faces (out of easily hundreds) by which people worship Luna, as in the Second Edition book. None of that truly helped inform what it was like to play or be a Lunar Exalt.

                        If a culture worships Luna in any form, it should be something written in the description of that culture, whether in an official text or a player's character backstory or a storyteller's notes.

                        It also kind of implicitly constricted the sorts of faces Luna would wear when appearing before your Lunar PC at Exaltation. There should be a lot more variety, there, and no one should need to explain why she doesn't appear as one of those five.
