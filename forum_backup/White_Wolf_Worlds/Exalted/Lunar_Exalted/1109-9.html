                    Originally posted by God_of_Awesome View Post
                    Now, on to a suggestion: Lunar did not all pick one side in the Usurpation. The Silver Pact was a pipe dream, Lunar have always been individualistic. Some of them sided with the Solars, some of them opted out, some sided with the Sidereal. These Lunars are not Wyld Hunted, living it up in Heaven or walking around in Creation with a 'I'm not an Anathema' card. Lunars are not organized, not like Deliberative was and definitely not like the Fellowship. Each one is his own guy, doing his own thing.
                    I enjoy this notion. Honestly, some of the best takes on the Lunars looked similar to this. Heck, I even did a variation where there was an ArgentOort Coud of Wyld kingdoms, lots of individualistic personages running around in Creation during the Shogunate, and a faction that actually conspired with the Sidereals. But ultimately, I feel that 'everybody just did whatever they wanted' can be just as boring as 'everybody line up for tattooes'. I actually enjoyed the notion of a Silver Pact, of some sort of organization dedicated to gathering Lunar strength in the Threshold, doing social things there, and/or being a counterbalance to the Dragonblooded Realm-centric political mapping. But that should be a result of the emergence of the Scarlet Empress, of centuries of slow, growing antagonism between factions of Terestrials growing increasingly paranoid of Lunar influence and pushing them towards the Threshold (and the Lunars pushing back) from a homogenous Shogunate, starting with an uneasy, public truce with those Moonchosen who sided with the Uprising (with big names like Ma-Ha-Suchi and/or Leviathan being amongst that number) to Gens treating having a Lunar like the modern world treats having nuclear armaments now in the middle Shogunate (sanctions, embargoes, nuclear deproliferation movements) to outright hostility only really emerging by the end. As opposed to the Solar revolution, which was a whispered conspiracy up until the sudden Calibration Feast slaughter, it should be more of a 700-year slide. Only with the coming of the Realm, and the Immaculate faith post-Contagion, do you get the Silver Pact. Which is perhaps at most half the total Lunars.

                            Originally posted by The MG View Post
                            wearing the trappings of civilisation over a savage heart.
                            This brings to mind something I thought recently, as regards "civilization".


                            I've been reading a story in which the population of an entire MMORPG was incarnated and transported to a fantasy-alternate counterpart of Earth. (But no, I'm not talking about the excellent new series, Log Horizon.)

                            Not only the various player races, but also the nonplayable races like Fox People. While the playable races have adapted better to the situation and have had the numbers to be both visible and strong enough to require and secure a treaty of basic human rights with the nation where they found themselves, the smaller, weaker and less visible Fox People have remained secluded... and thus they will not benefit from legal protection should anyone find them. This is an issue that came up with the Pixie NPC race when they were discovered, with the players pushing to have their "mascots" added to the treaty.

                            Recently, I imagined a player finding the Fox People and wanting to extend them the same protection. But I wondered if he might run into a problem trying to explain the point of such a legal agreement.

                            Ultimately, I envisioned him explaining the whole matter as it really was -- a game of posturing and growling and showing teeth. It's two packs of beasts wanting to plunder, kill and destroy each other out of fear and greed, but recognizing that open warfare would be too costly. The "laws" and "rules" are merely a mutual language of posturing and dominance by which the opposing forces can compete with each other without open warfare, and perhaps even strike a balance of peaceful co-existence.

                            That is, yes, in one sense the treaty is only worth the paper it's written on, and yes, the agreement by itself offers no protection, and unscrupulous elements within the local nation WILL try to plunder, kill and destroy them. But it means that these efforts need to be individual and clandestine, and that it's not an act of war to retaliate against such efforts.

                            Of course, the treaty is also a promise of mutual protection with the player races; a recognition that they are all in the same dangerous boat, so one defends the other in return for being defended later, and presenting their opponents a larger, more intimidating target. Safety in numbers.


                            Because really, how civil IS "civilization", when you get right down to it?
