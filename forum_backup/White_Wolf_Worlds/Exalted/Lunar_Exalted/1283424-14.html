            Originally posted by Leliel View Post
            Oh yeah, and I'm guessing Essence Fever is what they're calling Lunar Limit now.
            Not to rain on your parade, but no, Lunar Limit is separate, and still called that as far as I know. "Essence fever" is a term used for most Exalted, it represents that tendency of new Exalts to go out and get things done, trample jeweled thrones beneath their sandled feet, shakes the pillars of Heaven, etc. It's referenced in the Core book as something most Exalts of all types go through (pp. 35-36).

                            Originally posted by Cactuar View Post
                            I feel like the Assassination tidbit is odd given how several of the mentioned NPCs are described / implied as having done just that (though, I guess not to Dynasts?) Is the point of it to answer the question of why DBs aren't constantly worried about shapeshifting assassins?
                            I actually brought this up exactly in a thread a couple of months ago. Lunar shapeshifting, combined with the fact that they're second only to Solars in raw power, makes living when they don't want you to a seemingly insurmountable task. In theory a Lunar could circle around the Threshold as a bird just picking off vulnerable Dynasts at their leisure. In their palanquin as they parade through the city, in their bed while they're sleeping, or even when they're sitting on the good old chamberpot. Even if you have half a dozen mortal guards with you at all times they're just chaff to be winnowed before celestial might.

                            So you either need to have decent reasons why Lunars are reluctant to do that to an enemy that they positively drip with hatred for, have them constantly do it and wonder how the Realm functions when it's constantly having high ranking leaders killed, or have to start writing Dynasts like death awaits outside every open window and make them hide their identity from the public and live in fortified bunkers ect.

                                                            The boundary of monstrosity and humanity that Lunars can cross seems to apply to the perceptions of others, too.

                                                            Raksi puts on a show of being a monster, while being a much more sane and companiable creature in truth.                                                             While people can misunderstand others all on their own, Ma-Ha-Suchi might be employing deliberate subterfuge with his own followers, at least to prevent Kathaka from noticing his condescending sentiments.
