        Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post

        To explain: It seems to me that barbarian armies are going to be a fact of life for the Realm, whether the Lunars are involved or not - there are going to be a lot of regions that border somewhere rich but don't couldn't pay for themselves in taxes, extraction and trade but can support fairly militarized populations (large shares of the population who spend a lot of time fighting each other), and afford to import weapons and such enough to be a threat to the richer places the Realm dominates.

        The Realm can make such place border marches under garrisons, but this imposes costs of paying all those soldiers, and also the costs of "No one can pay me enough to be posted to Hadrian's Wall" where soldiers really want to be back in the capital and part of civilization, so you have to really pay to recreate "civilized" life out there, and get the locals to buy into this all as well. You don't want a gaggle of bored soldiers who pile a litany of abuses on the locals and inflame rebellion and conflict, before marching back themselves in rebellion against the core empire or its rich provinces!

        Often it's just cheaper to let these places be, and even pay them off.... but in the longer term that inspires flareups of opportunistic hordes, particularly when the pay offs become tight, and this coincides with general reductions in how much jade the Realm has about on the whole and so the legions it can pay. Then you get barbarian armies.
        Going by descriptions of the businesses of Cathak, Tepet and Sesus, and some historical examples of how several Chinese dynasties engaged with northern pastoral nomads (other than the times that they did indeed pay them off), I kind of expect that the Realm finds a much preferable option to maintaining permanent garrisons to be periodically sending in legions to kill and enslave large numbers of people.

                                                            Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                                                            Fair enough - not a number I've ever seen or internalised I guess, and not one I could find when checking through the source I'd expect it. 1e didn't bother giving a numbers of Dragonblooded per legion in its legion writeup in core / Dragonblooded book, while 2e offered
                                                            The Second Edition corebook contains what might be my favourite write-up of the Realm published thus far, but I find its description of Exalted in the officer ranks of a legion to be significantly understated. I was probably overshooting it a little with eighty, but it's still likely to be several score. That's how certain First Edition writers described it, I recall that's how developers at several stages have said it would be put in The Realm.

                                                            Originally posted by Ghosthead
                                                            However, I still don't think it's good strategy for them to always respond in the way described and that it "lacks nuance" as some would say.
                                                            The nuanced view is that the ones doing it are recognisable people, people whom one might even like under different circumstances, rather than orcs.

                                                            Look at our shiny new signature Hearth of Dragon Blooded. I like all of those characters, I think some of them are outright adorable, and I find them all to be quite well-rounded and sympathetic.

                                                            They're not written as disabusing the notion that they'd wipe out villages that were harbouring Anathema. The greatest objection that River has to the point is "bristling".

                                                            You say "not a good strategy", but what's your value judgement on good here? One that achieves their goals? One that preserves their integrity? One that makes a priority out of not radicalising the local population such that, ten years later, they widely support an extremely severe theocracy?

                                                            What I said before about authoritarians often being intrinsically bad at assessing the strength of enemies, that can apply here, and there's also a case where they can be bad at assessing the strength of themselves.

                                                            And otherwise, whenever I bring something like this up, it's primarily because I think that it's sometimes not well remembered that wars whose express purpose is to kill large numbers of people, break down social institutions, as a pre-emptive measure. From the perspective of people on the ground, and with the mindset of a different era, it might not seem wholly unjustified; the idea that it's just silly to wait until somebody whom you're reasonably certain can be an aggressor based on past events or their current lifestyle, attacks you first once their numbers are devastating, before you pull in forces to defend yourself. People like that might think that it's not wrong to attack them first, and in a pre-modern context, your strategic targets to reduce their capacity to wage war upon you in future can't really be anything but human bodies.

                                                            Like, I'm not suggesting the Realm periodically attacks peaceful people that were just minding their own business; you talked about "barbarian armies", and I presume that in most cases the precursors to these armies are bands of semi-nomadic people who either have lifestyles based on raiding their neighbours, or are otherwise liable to get into personal or territorial disputes that can spill over into neighbouring regions valuable to Realm interests. Hence the idea that the Realm might see a threat that needs to be dealt with pre-emptively.

                                                            (Although given the way that financial concerns intersect with military power in the Realm, there's probably also the occasional instance of "Julius Caesar needs to enslave a million Gauls and steal gold from the rest, because his debts are gargantuan)

                                                            And in the context of how the Realm isn't really much of a colonising power, so in a lot of cases garrisons (particularly the ones on a model of providing border defence, rather than being a nexus of military power over an area already under control) might be stretching their resources a bit. You talked about Hadrian's Wall, but that's an exceptional example in the overall Roman milieu. In a lot of cases with the Realm, where would they be building a wall that an enemy couldn't just walk around?

                                                            Still, I'm not saying that it's something they would always do, just that they'd probably do a lot, and it might bear repeating if it's a model of war that many people won't readily intuit.

                                                            ...Although I was probably too quick to dismiss the point about the victims' retaliation, since I'm also imagining this kind of thing as being where a lot of inhabitants of a Lunar Dominion come from. I maintain that if the argument is "the Realm wouldn't do something that would make people into their enemies", than I think in the setting as written that ship has already sailed*, and it's a charge that the Realm might frequently respond to with a challenge to bring it on.

                                                            There are definitely circumstances such as Chiroscuro and Fajad where it's seen as more productive to make concessions in the interests of stability, but these are surely the exceptions (especially with the current developmental mandate that having focused too much on the exceptions in the past made the Realm seem either toothless or reasonable, in contrast to the point it was intended to make).

                                                            * Perhaps to the port city of Nansha.
