                    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post

                    Hey, did everyone forget the bit where Vance popped into the thread on PAGE TWO to confirm they're going to put something about the bond being more mutual into the final draft?

                    Personally I don't see anything breaking either way. I'm much more concerned about the unnecessary restriction of describing every bond as originating with a "political marriage" when it would take one line to broaden this up to include other possibilities without disrupting the rest of the lore.

                    It's just so sad and frustrating that this part of my character's story has been taken out of my hands for no reason. Yes, the current incarnations can relate to each other on whatever terms now, but telling the story of who your character and their mate were to each other when they first bonded so long ago (lovers, comrades, teacher and student, parent and adoptive child, enemies who became best friends) is cool and thematic and mythic and I just ... can't? Unless it's this one thing? If I want to give context to the bitter rivalry between a Lunar and her mate by hearkening back to what brought them together in the first place, I only have this one story to play with, and I don't see why.

                    And don't come at me with that "political marriages are political arrangements so you can define how the pair actually relate to each other" guff. If that's the case, why are they called marriages? Historically, political marriages created/strengthened alliances that were affirmed by producing children. If that expectation isn't present and the two aren't romantically involved, why do I have to call it a marriage? It's not like there aren't other spiritually significant relationships you can swear an oath before the gods on. What's wrong with one line allowing those?

                    And no, thank you, I don't want to be patted on the head and told "Well if the ST says so you can have the one speshul bond that wasn't a marriage if that will keep you happy." I just want every other option that should be a perfectly valid (and would have been in previous editions, where, for all the other faults in the presentation of Solar Bond, there was no requirement that a bond-pair had been spouses at any point) to not be invalidated in the text of the book!

                    "Measure of Hope is right about everything." - Wise Old Guru

                                More than none*.

                                Mind, I don't think that there can be any version of those stories that can transcribe very directly to a game like Exalted, because even within the consideration that it's something for players to opt in to, whatever form the Bond takes can't constitute a really major infringement on story and character.

                                I think there are game systems that can more directly model some of the most overt forms of reincarnation romance, but I don't think Exalted is one of them.

                                That's all on a different side where I personally care about reincarnation romance only insofar as it can have elements extracted to make something that I find distinct and enjoyable to this Edition of Exalted (founded in biases formed from exposure to prior Editions of Exalted), and all of the rest discarded.

                                Although now it's raising questions to me arising from complications related to the manner in which the books have frequently depicted reincarnation romances that had nothing to do with Lunars. Solars with other Solars. Sidereals with other Sidereals. The ghost of a Solar who keeps being frustrated that none of the reincarnations of his Sidereal wife leave behind ghosts.

                                EDIT: * For one thing, among the range of possibilities and realisations of the trope would be stories in which two people remember a past life relationship, one of them is somewhere between "yeah, it would be totally great to resume that" and "welp, I guess we have an obligation written in the stars to resume that", and the other is like "no, that's dumb; maybe I have these memories, even that soul, but that's not me, I don't actually have or want any such feelings, and I resent the idea of some nebulous force mandating my relationships".

                                There are a few implications for what that means for my preferences towards Lunars, but if it's a matter of comparing pedigree of sources, it's something among the contenders that could inform an element in which at least one side of that equation practices dedicated indifference towards it.

                                            Honestly, if I was going to go for stories in which something compelling comes from the experience not being felt in the same way on both sides, I'd be inclined to first look at the Second Edition portrayal of Swan.

                                            Just, given his overall demeanour, I think there's something interesting and a little bit cute in the idea that the slightly naïve young man and fresh new Exalt is here faced with a legendary 2,000 year-old Lunar who is focused very intently on him because of a tumultuous relationship to his past life, and he just doesn't have any idea what to make of that. I always enjoyed seeing little allusions to that story.

                                            I kind of roll it into an overall liking of stories in which some inherited mantle of power includes some people that will hold you accountable for the actions of a past life, and you don't actually disagree with them, but you don't feel or remember the nature of that past and needing to find a way to handle it is a bit of a balancing act. Avatar: the Last Airbender sometimes did some stuff with that.

                                            … Oh snap, Steven Universe consistently does huge things not only with the matter of Steven struggling with the question of whether or not he's accountable for things his mother (who is basically also his prior incarnation) did, and how to handle any accountability he does accept, but also has a major thing of characters projecting relationships with her onto him, of which he has no memory and struggles how to handle.

                                            So, there's at least one solid example I've got to back up my somewhat more nebulous "more than none".

                                            (Gems wouldn't feel that relationship with Steven through something as direct as a metaphysical bond, but it's more a matter of how Gems perceive identity and the fact that Steven is an entirely new category of being; there are characters that know he has Rose Quartz's gemstone who simply don't have the frame-of-reference for processing Steven as a distinct person from her)
