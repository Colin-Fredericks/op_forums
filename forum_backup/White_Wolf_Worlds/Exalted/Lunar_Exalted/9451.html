    Lunar Craft Themes

    11-13-2013, 12:22 AM
    As a bridge to 3E for Lunars (which is looking to be 2-3 years out from the time I write this) what *are* the themes of Lunars? I've become so lost over the course of 2E.

    I feel each Exalt should have a path to the different mechanics of the game. Sidereals follow visions scoured from the Loom itself to aid in crafting. Dragon Bloods are able to create great wonders that resonate with their elemental property. I could think of several charms for each of those splats....

    However, when I come to Lunars, I am lost. Making things is about permanance, a lasting mark. This can match up with the survival and warden themes to a degree...but the mecurial and ever changing nature of the Moonkin not so much. Appealing to the Wyld themes - I feel that both 3E is going to abandon them to the Fay and that it does not lend itself well to direct crafting (vision quest to your hearts content). Lunars are neither the Animal Exalted nor would those themes aid in any way. Autochthon choose man because of their capability for Faith, Dogma, and *Tools*. Animals had no tools and pretty much suck at building things compared to man kind.

    I don't even feel I grasp the theme of the Lunar any more. All we know thus far is Rage? That they can shapeshift? These are not themes - at least ones that I could adapt to the mechanics of Sorcery, Crafting, or most (but not all) social interactions?

    I'm just curious and a bit flustered.

          The way I see it, Solars make the BEST of whatever they design. The best sword, the most beautiful robes, the manse with the coolest hearthstone, etc.

                  Lunars improvise, yes. They make temporary things that are perfect for the moment, and they adapt existing things to unintended uses. Wonders forged by the magics of the Lunars are multi-purpose, inobvious or cleverly outside-the-box obvious in their application, or made simple but strong.                                   Just for fun and discussion aid, I'mma drop in the substantive dev quotes I've pulled together on EX3 Lunars and Crafting (spoilered for space) :
                                  Originally posted by Wiki
                                  Q: (volkmar)
                                  I was merely curious how does [shapeshifting] help in making daiklaves or repairing the weather controls in Rathess or in any other thing that requires a bit more specialized knowledge than "hammer, nail, hit" assuming a Lunar would not be out of place doing any of those things.
                                  I'm also curious if shapeshifting would come into this somewhere. Ie: could you have a "smart" form, maybe taking on demon or spiritual shapes?

                                  A: (Holden)
                                  Shapeshifting doesn't help you with those things. (Well, I mean, maybe you could turn into a mouse to crawl inside a sky mantis tower and jigger some delicate parts back into alignment by hand? I dunno. Mostly it doesn't.)

                                  It's not like Lunars have some kind of special brain damage that makes them less-able to learn things than other Exalts. They can study Lore, Craft, Occult, sure. Their magic isn't the best in the world at building stuff, although they have stuff to help with it. They're certainly not the peers of the Solars in that category or anything (but then, who is?).

                                  Generally speaking, as a group, Lunars are less dependent on external props than anyone else-- from artifacts to infrastructure-- and this is one reason why they tend to favor simple, robust tools that are either very difficult to break, or which are easy to replace.

                                  That said, keep in mind-- they have Intelligence Charms. You will, from time to time, see Lunar Tony Stark building amazing shit in a cave, out of scraps. But as a whole they don't trend toward sophisticated big-scale manufacturing projects, in large part because the majority of Lunars don't like doing anything that requires them to set up an immobile and semi-permanent base of operations, like a fully-stocked-and-kitted artificer's workshop. It's more common to see a Lunar set up a temporary work site, build a daiklave or some armor or some similar piece of personal kit for himself or for someone else, and then break down the site, hide any of the components he can't easily replace, and then move on until he needs it again some years later. Most Lunars operate on wartime footing at all times, and they are all priority targets.*

                                  *There are exceptions to all these things, and they tend to be interesting subjects in their own right.
                                  Originally posted by Wiki
                                  (Kitsunemimi-Maiden)
                                  This is the first thing I've heard of 3e Lunars that truly excites me, as 1e and 2e both liked to pretend Lunars were brain-damaged (since the crafting system was entirely dependent on either uncommonly long downtimes given the 10-year span most games are expected to never breach, or charms to reduce those downtime requirements). I hope this remains a true part of the plan all the way to the 3e Lunar book and beyond, and Sidereals are also capable [Serenity does favor Craft, after all].

                                  (Holden)
                                  Mmm. Just keep in mind that I said "amazing shit" rather than "power armor" or "warstriders" intentionally. When you go off the grid, you don't get the benefits of the grid, so to speak. That still leaves the ability to make moonsilver plate, personal weapons, mystic veils, and other such magical wonders well within your capabilities-- and frankly, those things are generally better-suited to Lunar endeavors than something that needs specialist personnel to maintain and repair and a manse to keep it running anyway.

                                  If you're looking for someone that can outperform a maxed out Solar artifact-crafter-- you're not going to find one. If you're looking to play a Lunar that can make cool artifacts and enhance the story, sure. 3e crafting should be fun, whichever kind of Exalt you're playing.

                                      So point:

                                      Art is all about creation. The fundamentals of technique will only carry one so far - after which it becomes a matter of the innate insanity of ones mind and the brilliance of your vision. Both of which rely upon (attributes) rather than (abilities). While I agree some other splats will be better at making sounder armor and sharper swords - it is my *firm* belief that Lunars are better at distilling artistic sensibility into the raw technique of painting/sculpture/so on. While I see Solars as the primary engineers and architects of the First Age roads/bridges/forts and factories - I believe the greatest artists of the First Age had many/mostly Lunars among their number. Who else can see, better, the infinite shapes a block of stone might take or distill the flitting images of a dream onto canvass?

                                      I think art resonates with the concept of Lunars better than any other splat honestly.

                                      If you all think turning to yourself for tools is the Lunar plan, and crafting is mostly mental, what kinds of changes to your own mind could you invoke?

                                      Further, if Lunars are typecast as too insular, such as not making things (that last), it still drives them away from making an impact on the setting. I know this is not the case so I expect the writers have other plans for Lunar craft than just MacGyver. That box would be far too narrow.

                                                      Originally posted by The Despot View Post
                                                      I feel each Exalt should have a path to the different mechanics of the game. Sidereals follow visions scoured from the Loom itself to aid in crafting. Dragon Bloods are able to create great wonders that resonate with their elemental property. I could think of several charms for each of those splats....
                                                      Shapeshifting is also one of "the different mechanics of the game!" If you believe that all Exalted should have paths to all the different mechanics of the game, then you also believe that the game needs a Solar take on shapeshifting or a Dragon-Blooded take on weaving Fate.

                                                      Lunars may have some charms that incidentally help them at crafting, but I don't think that there should be any "Lunar craft charms" -- attempts to shoehorn them in in previous editions have been part of the thinking that led to the bland sameness that infected so much of the 2e Lunar charmset. Different charmsets follow different types of logic; the logic of Lunar magic doesn't (or shouldn't) focus on those kinds of competencies or skills in the way that, say, Solar or Lunar magic does.

                                                      It isn't something that any Lunar fan should want wordcount wasted on in the Lunars book. If you line up the Exalted we know about according to how close crafting fits into their thematics, Lunars are -- literally, no question -- at the very bottom of the list. Solars, Sidereals, DBs, Abyssals, and, in 3e, Infernals are all Ability-based to some extent or another and therefore have a dedicated Craft tree; Solars are explicitly based on skill and human endeavor to an extent, while Abyssals and Infernals are altered Solar shards; Infernals inherit the powers of the makers of the universe; Alchemicals are Attribute-based, but are the chosen of the Great Maker.

                                                      Lunars have nothing. Luna is not a famous crafter, and their charmset is not structured in a way that leads to any particular focus on crafting themes. The reason you can't think of how Lunar craft could work is because it doesn't really fit into their themes at all. Sure, you could force the issue and give Lunars crappy bottom-rung dedicated crafting powers that are, no question, strictly worse than everyone else's (because, again, everyone else has at least some route to crafting in their themes, and Lunars do not), but why? Why do you want that? People happily make numerous Solars who never learn to Craft, and DBs who never learn to craft, and Sidereals and Infernals and Abyssals and Alchemicals who never learn to craft. I don't understand why some Lunar fans feel that access to crafting is important. Nothing in Lunar themes or concepts makes it particularly relevant to being a Lunar; nothing inherent in the Exalted requires that you have access to it (because, again, people happily play numerous Exalted of the other types who never learn it -- in fact, I would say the vast majority of Exalted never learn to craft, even among types like Solars and Alchemicals where the potential is a big deal.)

                                                      I don't think that the Lunar manual should contain a single specific 'crafting charm'; I don't see how it benefits Lunars to include it. Devote the wordcount to something (anything!) else, to developing some other aspect of Lunar power that actually fits their core themes rather than trying to mindlessly ape the themes that were appropriate for other types of Exalted. The different splats are meant to be fundimentally distinct -- they support different types of stories and are focused in different areas. Some of this includes growing in directions or ways that other splats don't; some of this includes, yeah, lacking magical access to areas of expertise and types of charms that other splats have.

                                                      This doesn't mean that Lunars are unable to craft -- it doesn't even mean they're bad at crafting, as long as you're not comparing them to other Exalted or similar beings. There will be artifacts crafted by Lunars. They can craft better than a mortal, definitely, and better than most gods or spirits, because if nothing else they probably have ways to add dice to the process, and there are probably some ways they can shoehorn their powers into providing some benefit for it, like growing extra arms to work a bit faster or somesuch. In short Lunars can craft the way any superhumanly smart, strong, fast, dexterous magical being with vast control over their physical form could craft, which is still very very good. But these will all be incidental applications for abilities with other focuses as well -- there is no "crafting path" for Lunars inherent in their charmset, and I don't think that Lunars get many, if any, charms specifically dedicated to crafting alone. They can't put their Lunar magic directly into the crafting process itself the way most other Exalted can, because that's not how Lunar magic works.

                                                      Picture a blacksmith who Exalts as a Solar, say. The magic of his Solar Exaltation flows through the paths set by his skill at crafting, making him a supernaturally-skilled crafter, because that's the specific way Solar magic works. That's likely the main focus of his character! He can also expand into a few other sets of skills.

                                                      Now picture him Exalting as a Lunar. Lunar magic follows different paths; he will gain magic flowing from his great strength and toughness, as well as an animal form that's likely thematically connected to his arms-like-iron-bands, and magic related to that (plus the ability to expand into other forms, as well as other aspects of his physical form.) Does this make him a better blacksmith? Well, yes, compared to where he was as a mortal. He can hit the iron harder -- mechanically, he's throwing more dice at the task. And he's a magical being, so he has the basic ability to craft magical things that all magical beings have. (And while I might sound dismissive, both of these are big deals.) But his story as a supernatural being (the epic legend inherent in the Lunar Exaltation that Exalted him) does not care, particularly, that he was using his great strength as a smith or that he honed it as a smith; that's not going to be the direction his charms develop in. Playing this character and expecting to become a legendary smith among the Exalted makes you this guy, basically -- if you wanted to tell a story about magic flowing through your talent at crafting and enhancing your talent at crafting, why are you playing a type of Exalted that doesn't really work that way?
