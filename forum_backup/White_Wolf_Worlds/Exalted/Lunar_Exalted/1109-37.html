            I think the discussion of hidden potential is odd.

            One dot in Lore means you're literate and can read a map. I'm rather sure that anyone who is old enough to exalt has the potential to learn to read. There's nothing 'hidden' about it.             When we talk about hidden potential, we mean someone who appears unlikely to master something can actually become great at it. Someone who is clumsy and out of shape becoming a fabulous dancer. That's not Grace, who has Dex 5 and Performance 0, getting Performance 2. That's Grace, who has Dex 2 and Performance 0, getting Performance 4.

            If Grace were already dexterous and had good balance, no one would be surprised if she became a great dancer. If Grace is strong and in good shape, no one is going to be surprised that she's a good athlete. If Grace is good-looking and pleasant to be around, no one is going to be surprised that she excels at public speaking.

            High attributes are obvious potential.

                            Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
                            When we talk about hidden potential, we mean someone who appears unlikely to master something can actually become great at it. Someone who is clumsy and out of shape becoming a fabulous dancer. That's not Grace, who has Dex 5 and Performance 0, getting Performance 2. That's Grace, who has Dex 2 and Performance 0, getting Performance 4.

                            If Grace were already dexterous and had good balance, no one would be surprised that if she became a great dancer.
                            This is sort of how I'm seeing it, too. I don't generally take a high Attribute to mean a character has strong potential in something; I generally take it to mean a character has a lot of her broad capability in that area. Fox may have Intelligence 2 and nothing in Lore, Occult, or other thinky Abilities, but he may still have a natural way of thinking and approaching matters that might make a learned sage say, "Study hard and train your mind, boy; with your natural approach, you have potential to be a brilliant scholar." Sara, meanwhile, may have only Strength and Dexterity 2 and Melee 1, but someone who knows what to look for sees she has the right build, bone structure, attitude, foundational instruction, or whatever else to become an impressive master of the blade. To me, it feels natural that the term "you have potential" implies the "you" there has yet to realize it, whether in terms of skill (Ability), general capability (Attribute), or both.

                            I can kinda see how someone with Dex 5 and Melee 0 could be told he has potential to be amazing with an axe, because, well, yeah, he's deft and nimble with 5 whole dots of space to grow into that Ability. But I would raise an eyebrow if he was told, "You have potential to be deft and nimble," because having Dex 5 generally means one already is deft and nimble. The other eyebrow would also go up if his potential skill with an axe was described as "hidden potential," because, no, he's plainly very dextrous unless he deliberately plays it down, so the term there would be "obvious potential." But yeah, the phrase "potential" in a vacuum? I generally presume it speaks of someone with neither high Abilities nor high Attributes in the area, but with some trait that isn't necessarily reflected anywhere on the character sheet.

                            I feel like I'm missing something with the "Attributes as hidden potential" idea.

                                It's also difficult to define anything that can be measured by numbers on a character sheet as "hidden potential" because...well, yes, a Cha 2 guy can spend the XP to get up to Performance 4, and it's not any harder for him than it is for the Cha 4 guy. It does take less training for the Cha 4 guy to get the same results before considering Charm prereqs, that is to say he only needs Performance 1 to have five dice and the Cha 2 guy needs Performance 3.

                                But then, raw stats aren't potential either because the Cha 2 guy can also spend XP to get up to Cha 4. In the real world there are things like genetics that put limits on how strong/fast/tough/intelligent/handsome someone can be, and while there are ways to get around some of these limits they're largely unchangeable through training. In Exalted, the only thing stopping your character from being just as strong as any other strongman is that you don't have them spend XP on it. That's not even a function of being an Exalt, you can do it as a heroic mortal too. So clearly either everyone in Creation has equal levels of "hidden potential", or stats aren't hidden potential. I tend to go with the latter.

                                As for what is...I'm not sure there really is such a thing, mechanically speaking. You can see all the values on the character sheet, you can tell exactly what someone's capabilities and remaining potential in a given field are. Hidden potential is more about narrative, a character starting weak in something and then growing stronger in it. We're having a lovely thread about how Exalted character creation makes this a bad idea for a story to tell, but you can do it, and I think this is the closest you'll get to Gohan displaying lots of untapped power.

                                                Well, look. I quoted the 2E core book exactly. The words and concepts I'm using are the same the game system uses. In 2E, your Attributes are your innate talents and potential - whether you know you have them or not - and your Abilities are your learned skills. That is how they're defined. It may not make the most sense all the time, but that's the system in the most recent published form.

                                                If you need more quotes, here's the section from the 2E book that describes the game's core mechanic:
                                                POOLS AND DICE
                                                Whenever a character attempts an action where success and failure are both possible, her player rolls a number of dice equal to the dots the character has in the appropriate trait or traits. Most rolls couple an Attribute with an Ability, representing the combination of innate potential and learned skill that best applies to the situation.
                                                Here's a quote from the Lunars book, about charms:
                                                Lunars are naturally instinctive creatures. This is part of why their Charms focus on Attributes, their natural potential, instead of the training represented by Abilities.
