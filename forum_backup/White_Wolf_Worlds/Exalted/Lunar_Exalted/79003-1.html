                Ah, so what you're getting at is "Given the substantial opportunity cost of getting mechanically-useful tools instead of more intrinsic power, and that tools can be lost, broken, or taken away while intrinsic powers can't, why would a Lunar invest in the former and not the latter?" In 2E and 2.5E, the answers would be:
                1. Maintaining a cover ID
                2. Doing something intrinsic powers don't cover, or
                3. Aesthetics
                1 and 2 are things that, in theory, can be fun and interesting but, the way the rules handled it in prior editions, were generally not. 3 is something I don't really care to have players pay for. Depending on how pervasive the system conceit of 'Low buy-in to competence, high buy-in to mastery' is, I'm hoping 3E will add to that list:

                4. Because branching out is now just as valid a choice as hyper-specializing, and the game engine will no longer go out of its way to punish you for Spending XP Wrong, and
                5. There are distinct strengths and weaknesses to both shapeshifting and external tools, such that you could justify a focus (or lack thereof) on either

                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                    I wasn't so much curious about why a Lunar would use any tools at all, but why he would max out his Evocations with an artifact, or go to any trouble to get special tools like artifact boats or godblooded horses.
                    I kinda feel like a lot of the examples and reasons for the one will be true of the other. Lunars could still gladly master all the Evocations on an artifact weapon even with a mutually exclusive DBT as an option. The situation probably analogues to a Solar maxing out the Evocations on multiple mutually exclusive artifact weapons. Evocations don't sound like they're meant to be pushover effects:* having multiple awesome options that wouldn't otherwise be available is reason enough.

                    Besides, from a more gamist perspective, all Exalts will have regular XP as well as Solar XP,** the latter of which can be spent on basically anything except native Charms. Without competition from Charms, Evocations might look a sight more attractive to a Lunar for that Solar XP. The competition for Solar XP is thus mostly between the relatively boring -- but serviceable and reliable -- Ability/Attribute advancement, and the flashier and potent -- albeit perhaps situational -- trio of Evocations, Martial Arts, and Sorcery.*** It doesn't feel like a given choice need be better than any other.

                    * Remember, we've only got one real example so far: Stormcaller initiates a hurricane when drawn. Holden talked about it with us at Aggiecon, and gave the impression that's not just a visual effect. Even if they can't get up to the apex Evocation's massive stormblast, Lunars keen on breaking the Realm's shit would eat that up.

                    ** Would still go for a different name, but whatevs.

                    *** Evocations as situational things with DBT right there goes without saying. Each Martial Arts style**** has a kind of paper-rock-scissors-ish relationship with other styles/obstacles, so a given style won't always be a good idea. We don't know the new build of Sorcery, but the last word ages ago spoke of special actions and tools to gather motes for a spell; whatever the case, powerful though it may be, it doesn't sound like Sorcery is always easy or convenient to use.

                    **** Have I mentioned my interest in a Lunar Martial Artist who switches from style to style as well as from shape to shape in combat? Because that interest exists.

                        Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                        I wasn't so much curious about why a Lunar would use any tools at all, but why he would max out his Evocations with an artifact, or go to any trouble to get special tools like artifact boats or godblooded horses.
                        Because the Lunar wants to reap the considerable benefits of having maxed-out Evocations on top of his natural puissance. Or to have a boat that is better than mundane boats, for purposes of transporting people other than himself across the sea quicker and more comfortably than he could by going orca-form and letting them ride in his mouth. Or because he is a legendary horseman, and seeks a steed worth of him in its saddle. Those are all pretty natural ambitions, if you're an Exalt who wants to gain power, and there's no compelling reason not for them to apply to Lunars. Now, a Lunar could shun the legendary sword and instead dedicate himself to perfecting his body as a living weapon, or just take some aquatic forms and not worry about having a crew/cargo, or just turn into a horse himself. But just because they can pick option B doesn't mean they should be disallowed or disincentivized from taking option A.

                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                    My curiosity is more "are these options incentivized enough for players to care."

                                    Well, no. Obviously, you guys are doing your best on that front. So my curiosity is "how will these options be incentivized enough", and I created this topic (however poorly) with the intent of brainstorming together with the forum on such methods.
                                    My question would be:

                                    If tools are so f***ing bloody awesome, then why would a Lunar ever want to use shapeshifting? If Evocations are so useful I'm going to want to pursue them even if I can't use them with DBT, then why would I want to learn DBT and lock myself out of all the XP (or other character resource) I spent learning those amazing Evocations? If the party's artifact airship can fly faster than my Mospid form, then what use is it? If Moonsilver Armour is going to protect me more than Elephant Hide, then why shouldn't I spend my Merit points on Artifact instead of Heart's Blood?

                                    Of course the person who invests in both tools and native powers is going to be more flexible, able to use tools where she can and falling back on her body when she can't. But will the structure of the game support this flexibility? Because every iteration of Exalted so far (and WW games in general... and RPGs in general) has gone the opposite route and has incentivised specialisation over generalism - the character with Martial Arts 0, Melee 4, Dex 5, Strength 1, is always going to beat the character with Martial Arts 2, Melee 2, Dex 3, Strength 2. If the rules are unbalanced, you can pass the buck to the ST, but it's still going to boil down to the permissive ST (under which tools will always be better because he won't take them away) or the evil ST (under whom native powers will always be better because he'll steal your tools at every opportunity).

                                    tl;dr

                                    If Lunars are supposed to use tools and their own powers, how will this be balanced?

                                    Hi, I'm JohnDoe244. My posts represent my opinions, not facts.

                                                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                                If Lunars are supposed to use tools and their own powers, how will this be balanced?
                                                How is a Solar supposed to use his barehands and a bow?

                                                "Supposed to" is the wrong phrase. A Solar can choose to specialize in Brawl or Melee; he is not forced to take either, much less to generalize with both. Likewise, a Lunar can choose to specialize in the Martial Arts Ability and combat animal forms, or he can choose to specialize in the Melee Ability, acquire an artifact weapon, and invest in Evocations; he can also choose both or neither.

                                                Can the Solar fight with a sword in one hand and the other hand empty? Yes, and Lunars will more than likely have the option of switching forms between one attack and another, so that they can stomp someone as an elephant and then stab the broken heap with a spear.

                                                Remember that Holden and John were experimenting with a new Protean keyword in Exalted 2.5, which would allow a Lunar to reflexively assume a form appropriate to that Charm. So a Lunar could freely shapeshift into various combat-appropriate beasts just by using his combat Charms like he always would. If the Protean keyword doesn't return in Third Edition, it will be because they found a more elegant solution.


                                                the character with Martial Arts 0, Melee 4, Dex 5, Strength 1, is always going to beat the character with Martial Arts 2, Melee 2, Dex 3, Strength 2
                                                That might matter in Burn Legend story-free tournaments, but not so much in Exalted.

                                                "Player versus player" combat is the central thesis of Burn Legend, but Exalted is about telling a story which often happens to involve combat, usually between players and non-player characters, and where "beating the other guy soundly" is not always the necessary win condition.

                                                In Exalted, generalization should be rewarded with additional scenes in which the character can contribute, because Exalted is a storytelling game.

                                                If a generalist PC is losing a fight to a special NPC, that's not the fault of the system. That's either the Storyteller's fault for not understanding (or caring) about a proper difficult threshold, or your fault for trying to fight an opponent on his own terms when you should be trying to assert your own terms.
