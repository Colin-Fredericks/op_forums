    Originally posted by Valles View Post
    I can't deny that the Thousand Streams' details were in need of work, or that this execution makes more sense than that one. It's a moral one-eighty, though, and I am not okay with that.

    It's like if we were still being told that the only things for Sidereals to do was Hunt Solars and that that dominated all they had become in the modern age. It's not just limiting, it's limiting to playing, at best, someone well-intentioned but horrifically destructive, and more likely an outright villain.
    Couple things.

    1. I don't think that's described as something ALL Lunars do, or that all Lunars go about it in the extreme realpolitik way I described. Certainly some do, but other Lunars probably go about their goals in other ways. The Silver Pact is not the monolith of the Bureau of Destiny, and making it less like the Garou Nation is great for all the reasons that making it the Garou Nation to begin with didn't make sense.
    2. I'm not sure I see the problem with a moral 180 when the past portrayal was not just derpy, but actively immersion-breakingly stupid.
    3. I'm not sure the about face as to "what are Lunars, as a whole, trying to do" being centered on "destroy the Realm" is bad in light of having no solid focus in past eds except "something something barbarians something fighting fair folk something forced breeding beastman army"
    4. In a way I like the solid greying of morality in the Realm vs Lunar war thing. Lunars are not just some ancient good guys who the current government won't leave alone; they're doing terrible things to support their goals, which is a strategy that human beings employ and always have employed.

    Why was their 2e "morality," which to me seemed to be "farcically ignore the actual state of Creation to stick our heads in the sand re mortal societies that will not be able to last without us," a better state?

    I mean, is this the true ideal version of the Lunar narrative. I dunno. Probably not? I'm really not sure, because we never have had a good alternative to compare it to. We can't look at it like Yozis and say "I prefer the GoD portrayal to the MOEP Infernals and CoCD Malfeas one," because 1e Lunars was an example of total bedshitting and 2e Lunars was narratively incoherent. I'm not sure TAW even answered the narrative question, so much as its goal was to make the mechanics not "Solar Charms, but overcosted and silver-colored."

    Are they now filling a niche that was unfilled, and better than the sloppy lack of direction, set on the boring edges of the world, that they had as a splat in the past? I say absolutely.

        Originally posted by *End View Post
        I think that what struck me most about the Lunars this time around is how both Lunars and Luna are defined as "iconoclasts."
        Not actually new, that came up in Second Edition.

        I still don't like it, because it would mean that Lunars would always be defining themselves in opposition to something stronger than them, which would either prevent them from ever being a part of the dominant side of the equation or else leave them confused and directionless should they ever become the dominant power. Like the Ebon Dragon of Second Edition, they would lose all personal identity and become shallow and reactionary. A rebel always looking for a cause, any cause, every cause, just 'cause.


        The other major thing that struck me about Lunars is very first sentence of their description page: "The Chosen of the moon were forged in the chains of bondage but Luna only chooses those with the power to free themselves."
        Now THIS has me excited.

        Out of many attempts to find a "shared narrative" for the Lunar Exalted which wasn't just "like the Solar Exalted, but silver" and which was thematically-specific such as enjoyed by every non-Solar Exalted Host, few have been as powerful and appropriate as this:

        "I was once a slave -- to my owner, to my passions, to my debt, to my culture, to my parents, to my ancestor ghost, to my god, to my delusions of grandeur -- until I choose to break free and escape -- to flee by the cover of night, to join a monastery, to declare bankruptcy and accept my jail sentence, to marry Juliet, to salt the ghoul's grave-shrine, to eat the god's sacrifice for myself, to accept that I was worthless...

        "And in midst of casting off my chains, the Moon gave me Exaltation, so that the freedom I had grasped for would not end immediately in the face of the consequences.

        "But as I grow into my power, I begin to feel the chains of a forgotten past trying to tie me down once more. If I am to truly attain the freedom promised to me, I must continue to destroy every fetter that everyone ever attempts to impose upon me."

                        Originally posted by auroradragonkaya View Post
                        I admit the hints at new lunars make me really wary, especially that emphasis on Rage. I don't want lunars to be more like WoD werewolves. And having something where "all of this caste has this personality trait" (e.g. all angry) is a roleplaying cardinal sin, in my book.
                        Hopefully, my concerns are groundless.
                        I think they probably are. I honestly don't see the emphasis on rage that others do, but that's because I'm probably looking at it in a rather unique way. Rather than rage, I characterize what the Lunars are experiencing as loss, or seeking, or something like that. Think about it this way - the Lunars apparently lost something when the Solars vanished, and it may have been more than simply emotional (in that some magical connection existed between the Solars and Lunars). Since then, the elders who lived in the First Age have slowly been decreasing in number, but they still recall what it was to live in a world so much better than the Second Age, to live in a place where they weren't hunted like animals. From this arises their anger.

                        The majority of the Lunars are probably not quite so emotionally invested in dethroning the Realm... except inasmuch as the Realm has basically demonized them, kicked them out of their homes, and probably tried to kill them many times over. When everything you've known has been taken from you, and suddenly there's a Silver Pact Lunar talking about how the evil Realm is awful and needs to fall, telling you about the age-old truth that you are, in fact, Chosen, and of a far greater sort than the Dragon-blooded who you have always been taught are the Princes of Earth, you may find yourself grasping this direction, because you need that direction...

                        Then, as you carry out your actions, you may find yourself questioning why you are doing this rather than something else - this is what I see as the fangs of the past, still clinging to your Lunar, and freedom shall not be yours until you realize how to free yourself from them. But you can do so, because of who you are - that is why Luna Chose you.

                        All that I write but don't cite is simply my perspective, colored by my experiences and beliefs. I extrapolate a lot, too, so don't take it too seriously. :P

                                                            About the "Raising up barbarian hordes to fight the realm, even though they're going to be wiped out"...
                                                            ...Those barbarians they recruit? They live right at the edge of Realm territory. It even calls out that Lunars rarely have to lie for their propaganda - these barbarians often are driven away and hunted by the realm.
