    Lunar Rage

    05-14-2017, 01:04 AM
    Originally posted by Exalted, Third Edition, page 46
    Broken Bonds

    The Lunar Exalted were created to be the Solars’ seconds, their spouses, and the sacred guardians of the Old Realm. The bond defined the Lunars; it sang within their very Essence.

    So too did the bond’s destruction. The Old Realm fell; the Solars were murdered, their spirits banished beyond Lunar ken. The Lunars changed. They’re still changing. A deep rage lives in their Essence, and a deep wound. Their sleep is tormented by visions of loss, of murder, of the past. The Lunars are compelled to shatter the chains binding them to the past—to tear down the stolen remnants of the First Age, to raze the Sidereal and Terrestrial hegemony that dares to wear the ancient name of the Realm. The Silver Pact exists to facilitate this.

    Some Lunars resist the primal howl that shivers in their Essence. They harness their rage to fight for that which they cherish, or bend their powerful instincts to their own benefit as they walk another road. There is tension between these Lunars and the Silver Pact traditionalists, but also common bonds; the two trade favors and mark debts more often than they shed one another’s blood.

    The return of the Solars changes everything for Luna’s Chosen. Neither Lunar camp seeks to turn back the clock, nor wishes to return to defining themselves as the shadows cast by the Chosen of the sun. Something stirs in the Lunars’ hearts, but none can yet say what it is or what it means.
    Originally posted by Exalted, Third Edition, page 46
    By unleashing the rage deep inside, Lunars may evoke monstrous battle-forms.
    Originally posted by Exalted, Third Edition, page 47
    Play one of the Lunar Exalted if you want... to be the inheritor of an ancient vendetta, empowered by rage to be nearly unstoppable.
    Originally posted by Exalted, Third Edition, page 106
    ...each Lunar Exaltation brings with it a hungry turmoil of the soul.
    Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
    I am intrigued by the mention of Rage in the Essence of the Lunars in their blurb in the core. Especially under the Broken Bonds header, it gives an intriguing and excitingly distinct view of their relationship with the First Age, the Age of Sorrows and their opposition to the Realm. It also brings something else to mind...

    Will Lunars still use Limit in this Edition, or will they have a new thing to track, a la Resonance or Paradox? If they are still using Limit, will their experience of it be distinct from Solars, and will it involve this Rage in their Essence at all?
    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
    Lunar Limit isn't something we've gotten around to discussing fully. I can say that I don't want to tie the Lunar's rage entirely to Limit, something that many players perceive as a drawback or ignore altogether. Rage something they drawn on with their Charms, one of the sources of their power. They're predators, but they aren't driven by hunger—rather, it's their rage that sustains their will to hunt.
    What does this tell us?

    1) Lunar Rage seems akin to Sidereal Arcane Fate. Breaking the Constellation of the Mask afflicted Sidereals with the mixed curse of Arcane Fate, and breaking of the Solar Bonds afflicted the Lunar Exalted with Rage.

    2) Lunar Rage is something distinct from their Great Curse. Comparatively, it is a new problem

    3) It is also a new source of strength. I remember Holden once writing a short story in which Chejop Kejak is getting stitched back together by a god of medicine after an encounter with a Lunar, and the Sidereal is talking about how he predicted that the Lunar Exalted would become the monsters he painted them to be, and lamenting that they exceeded his expectations.

    4) The wounds left by the broken Solar Bonds, and the resulting Rage, have not only changed the Essence of the Lunar Exalted permanently, but the potential depths of the changes haven't yet been fully explored. The Lunar Exalted might experiment with their altered nature, and perhaps find a way to change even more.

    5) Vance declined to comment on whether Rage would be represented with points, like Sidereal Paradox or the Rage points of the Classical World of Darkness' Garou werewolves. So, that possibility is still on the table.

    6) This Rage is not something that the Lunar Exalted choose; rather, they are unavoidably angry, and every one of them has to decide how to deal with that. Many of them choose to harness that anger towards personal ends which have nothing to do with the injustices suffered by their previous incarnations.

    7) Few Lunars welcome the idea of fixing the Solar Bonds that proved so disastrously unreliable and painful. Why invite that same heartache again?

    8) The language surrounding Rage leaves little doubt about its intended emotional tenor. "Broken, wound, torment, loss, shatter, tear, raze, howl, shiver, vendetta, hungry turmoil"... Lunar Rage stems from pain. Lunar Rage is angry and violent. It's not "passion" or "obsession" or "focus", it's pain and the anger that comes from being hurt. Insofar as it resembles hunger, it is starvation to the point of torture.


    What does this NOT tell us?

    1) It does NOT tell us that every Lunar Exalted is always seeing red, all of the time.

    2) It does NOT tell us that the Lunar Exalted have no control over their Rage at all. Indeed, it explicitly tells us that Lunars find it possible to harness that anger towards their own purposes, and seems to imply that a Lunar can disassociate themselves from that Rage -- seeing it as something imposed upon them rather than rightfully belonging to them.

    3) It does NOT tell us that Rage is the only source of Lunar power. On the first page of the Ask The Devs thread, Robert Vance waxed lyrical about the themes of Hunting, Predation, and Symbiosis. Also, shapeshifting.

    4) It does NOT tell us that any Lunar has found a way to Chill Out. Maybe they can appease or simply exhaust the Rage (especially if it is represented with points), but it apparently never really goes away.

      Originally posted by Exalted, Third Edition, page 46
      ...they remain caught on the fangs of the past, and until they escape it, they cannot truly claim the freedom Luna has promised them.
      Rage is the antithesis of freedom.

      Freedom comes in many forms, and one of the most important is your state of mind. A home becomes a prison when you are placed under house arrest, and a prison can become a home if you have no desire to leave. Being given too many options can paralyze someone with indecision, whereas being restricted by rules can free someone to explore the remaining possibilities.


      When someone detains you against your will, you can respond with anger, and use that anger to smash away the barrier that restricts your physical freedom to leave. When someone blackmails you, you can respond with anger, and use that anger to fuel you to out-scheme your nemesis.

      But in choosing to be angry, you choose to make your freedom conditional. You choose to care so much about something that you cannot feel free without it. In a sense, you enslave yourself to this thing.

      Further, if your rage proves insufficient to secure the condition you've placed on your freedom, then you still lack your conditional freedom while also being enslaved to rage.


      Of course, it's good that the Lunar Exalted do not find simply giving up to be any more natural to accomplish than other human beings. It's hard!

      What's more, the logical extreme of this approach isn't healthy, either, as a life is not worth living if you refuse to care about anything enough to live for it. This sort of hollowness is what the soulless Raksha have to carefully balance, and what makes mortals vulnerable to that controversial Charm, Cup Boils Over.


      Still, it remains ironic that the Exalted most capable of simply avoiding, escaping, and hiding from trouble are the ones most motivated to stay and fight.

      But it also makes a lot of sense, because if the Lunar Exalted didn't have a strong motivation to fight, they would be rather boring. They'd largely just run away and hide from the Realm and Sidereals.

      If they spent all of their time hiding in the Threshold and Wyld and cutting away every passion that might tie them down, they would be indistinguishable from the Raksha.

                                  In the "Ask the Devs" thread, BrilliantRain expressed curiosity about how Rage could be reflected in all nine Attributes, as Robert Vance said it could.


                                  Wits is the Attribute of Resolve and Join Battle. Rage can be used as a shield against persuasion and fear, and also to lower one's brain into the hyper-reactionary "flight or fight" mode.

                                  Appearance can be used for intimidating Threaten actions when it measures ugliness rather than beauty, and Rage can make you terrifying to behold. On the other hand, we've heard the expression "you're beautiful when you're mad", and a Lunar's allies might take inspiration for battle in which she leads as a furious goddess of sex and violence.

                                  Seeing red can narrow your Perception to the point of blindness, but only regarding everything except the one you want to kill. Your focus on them is heightened.

                                  Charisma is excellent for infecting others with your Rage, or for leveraging your Rage to intimidate.

                                  Manipulation might use smaller, colder Rages as fuel for delivering the most infuriating of cutting remarks, or for hiding your other feelings behind a mask of anger.

                                  Dexterity could use your Rage to drive your rush actions, attacks, and counterattacks.

                                  Rage easily lends itself to feats of strength and destruction.

                                  Stamina was the source of Relentless Lunar Fury, and their healing and injury-survival powers likely improve when they see red.

                                  Intelligence... well, I suppose you can better devise and create things that you can use to kill someone you hate.
