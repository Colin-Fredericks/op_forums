    Opinions on 3e Lunars

    09-17-2024, 02:40 PM
    So, Lunars have been around for a long while now. We've had time to play them or use them in our games. What do you think about them? What do you like or dislike about them? How do they fit into your vision of Creation?

    Personally I'm mostly happy about them. They're fun to play or to insert in a story, they're useful in a mixed group without stealing the other Exalted's spotlight.
    Their new focus on playing intermediaries between spirits, fae and mortals is also really interesting to explore and it gives Lunars are peace time function that use the liminality of their nature.
    Lunar witches have become especially fun with their expended ability to transform people other than themselves and create all kind of crazy situations.
    The different Lunar factions and their politicking are a very nice addition, as is the War for the Caul which make for a nice little sandbox, although the Caul itself could do with a little more fleshing out, but we might see that in a book on the West with any luck. I love the concept of Shayan-yas and how it helps make Elder Lunars relevant and helps with creating rivalries or alliances.

    I'm a bit disappointed about how shapeshifted Lunars can't use Martial Arts, even those MA created by Lunars, it feels kinda random and arbitrary, but it's nothing a house rule can't easily fix.
    I also wonder why they lost the ability to take the shape of supernatural creatures. It's a bit weird that the shapeshifters made to fight demons, dark gods and behemoths can't turn into one of those beings. That's plenty of lost story potential.
    Lastly I believe Lunars still feel more like powerful Terrestrial Exalted than like Celestials. They're strong and versatile, yes, but they don't have the cosmic scope Solars, Abyssals, Infernals, Sidereals or even Getimians have. They don't shape fate, reality or the Chaos at the edge of the world, they don't shake the fundations of Heaven or shatter constellations, nor do they herald the advent of the end times or the fall of the gods.

    All in all, it's a net positive for me and I've enjoyed using and playing Lunars a lot.

    When will you rage?

          My opening salvo: Protean is a kludge that should have been left in 2e. If you keep it, it'll just give you more and more Silver Solar Charms, again and again, forever.

          And from what I scanned from reading through the cascade notes and from the Inkless Pen review, you have an awful lot of Silver Solar when it isn't Silver something else that isn't a Lunar. You have an animal shapeshifting Celestial Exalt that expressed power through said shapeshifting as a concept, and... you abandon that, wholesale, to play Wyrd Sisters for some reason. You write in psychic surgery instead of versatile silk Charms and mother of pearl* production. I clearly do not have the same set of priorities, here.

          If you're perplexed about how to fit shapeshifting into, say, Intelligence or Wits, 1) consider the results and aims of the Charm, and 2) consult the Alchemicals Charm sets (2e preferred, 1e still good, even Time of Tumult's limited samples would have done E:tL a solid), because it has a lot of useful insights on the topic of making the abstract physical. Not all of them translate (brain implants are obviously a no go), but Autochthonians/Alchemicals gets remembered fondly because it tried to plot out a path here. Lunar Charms have a history of just charging headlong into a Solar-esque lack of flavor text, which only works for Solars because they're the fill in the blank heroes, and so Lunars don't usually get beyond "okay, I guess." That's for 2e, of course; 1e is some shade of "burn it down and start again".

          That said, the sidebar from 1e, "The Narrow Path"? It wasn't completely off target. I just happen to disagree about how limited in scope relying on shapeshifting actually is. It's like the Exalted equivalent of the guy at the gym fallacy, only contorted around so that people will accept steam!magic implant bullshit doing literally anything, but not that Lunars can use animal shapeshifting to overcome cosmic threats, even though both are just arbitrary effects with a visual hook. Never mind that back in the first mention of magical material armor, design flourishes like fins and spikes on armor were called out as specifically having a function in channeling Essence through the item in question; if it works for armor, why not for armor formed from skin?

          All in all, I would rearrange a lot of where effects show up and would prefer a set where almost everything relies on shapeshifting and anything that Lunars need to have that delves into dreams, the unconscious, etc. could probably get farmed out as a function of moonsilver artifacts.

          I'm not particularly hung up on the lack of shapeshifting into spirits. Not like I'm angry about there being, apparently, lots of Charms to key into Solar bond and none about shocking people with your electric fish-themed bioelectric buildup. Or the absence of Argent Weaver Art. Or that the natural weapons that Charms form aren't made out of moonsilver and capable of striking dematerialized spirits without the need of an additional Charm for it.

          I also understand that they gave Leviathan another Solar mate-related plot tumor to weigh him to the bottom of the ocean. I miss the elements of Leviathan from 1e where he was allowed to not be involved with Solar mate romance plots.

The Martial Arts are specific codified fighting styles, and they're channelled through the motions of a human body. A literal snake can't use Snake Style because they don't have any arms.

                          Originally posted by Anteros
                          I also wonder why they lost the ability to take the shape of supernatural creatures.

                          Besides, there's also the matter of what the utility of it is. Part of the point of the animal shapeshifting is that you gain the special capabilities of the animal you turn into. If you turn into a demon, are you supposed to gain all of its powers? Or is it purely cosmetic? If the latter, a lot of the value of it would be lost anyway.

                          They were all made to fight monsters of the ancients, but they're still primarily the champions of their patron gods, and have powers expressing that more than being custom made to perform a function.
