                                                This portrayal grates on me for several reasons, but the strongest one, and the one I can most clearly articulate, is that violence against and exploitation of the periphery for the benefit of the center is what empire is. There is no forgive-and-forget - your option are 'kneel', 'fight back', or 'die' (the latter of which does not preclude the former two). An empire that can no longer expand is an empire that's about to implode. If there's peace (and not make-a-desert-and-call-it-peace), it's because you've cost them enough blood and treasure that they decided to cut their losses.

I don't know personally. Theories about why, when and where empires exist and expand (imperiogenesis) are beyond the scope of what it's worth going on a derail about here (and there are other threads on it anyways). I def. agree that, on the whole, empires don't have any internal binding nation state logic like "The state is only legitimate if it governs over our nation", so powerful imperial states are going to over time tend to accrue any bordering territories which are economically worth conquering for imperial elites (the carrot), and which seem to pose potential military threat (the stick). That's particularly the case if they encounter peoples at the border who have no concept of unified government or diplomacy (diplomacy can't beat out war if you're making worthless treaties with people who either don't really hold the concept as important or don't hold any real power).
