                    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                    Wakanda typically has to be double-secret-hidden, else a Sworn Brotherhood happens upon it, rolls it for everything valuable, and returns to the Blessed Isle.
                    I think the notion that a Sworn Brotherhood can simply roll an entire kingdom or civilization no questions asked (even without Lunars or artifact goodies entering the equation) is an incredibly strange notion that is not borne out in the DB stats we have so far, nor will it be when we get a DB book.

                    And that the idea that you can't have little undiscovered Lunar kingdoms (maybe even with a higher standard of living than their surroundings, or with unique goodies that the ST wants to introduce) because Dragon-Bloods are 100% guaranteed to have found them and kicked them over is a strange (and insidiously reductive) idea that vastly underestimates the size of Creation, and overestimates the power of the Realm.

                    There's a sort of literalism that renders Lookshy the only dragon-blooded state opposing the Realm, Ejava the only Scarlet Pretender opposing Mnemon, and now, the Caul the only place where Lunars can set up shop. ("Well okay you can have your PC be a claimant for the throne, but only if you play Ejava" rather than "you can build your PC to be a claimant for the throne, Ejava might help you model what that might look like.") It denies the essential vitality of a world that is supposed to be vibrant with legends and heroes. It denies the exploration of a world that is, frankly, mostly blank space.

                    Are there reasons why secret Lunar kingdoms aren't on every corner store? Sure. Are there reasons why every Lunar doesn't have a death ray? Sure. Might the Lunar kingdom be sort of doomed in the long run, or the death ray a target for the Dragon-Blooded? Sure. Do you need a reason to give a particular Lunar you encounter a hidden kingdom or a death ray? Not at all.

                                                    Based in part on one of Incbill's posts here are three attempts (I have no crunch abilities, though) :


                                                    Moonsilver Mine
                                                    In its dormant state a Moonsilver Mine is a disc about a foot across and 1 inch thick, which can be concealed under a thin layer of dirt, grass, or leaves. When stepped upon it rapidly flows upwards, consuming mundane weapons and armor and pouring itself down throats into lungs. It also severely damages artifacts, which require repair before they can be used again. Moonsilver mines also must be reset with a repair roll before reuse.
                                                    In the Second Age, the Lunars retain many of these mines, which they carefully deploy to defend their holdings.

                                                    Ring of the Hidden Beast
                                                    A moonsilver ring which resizes itself to fit the attuned user. It hides a Lunar’s Tell from all observation, taking the place of the Tell as a white ring around whatever appendage it's attached to. It can fit on a finger, toe, wrist, ankle, tail, or neck. A Ring of the Hidden Beast can be implanted as a tattoo artifact, if desired.

                                                    Moonlight-on-the-Water Amulet
                                                    With this potent wonder, a Lunar gains a great measure of protection from astrology and the Loom of Fate. Using the principles first applied to the creation of complex power armor such as the Most Excellent Armor of the Elemental Dragons, two elder Lunar survivors of the First Age designed a much simpler artifact to fool the Sidereals. They completed their first Amulets during the first centuries of the Shogunate, and spread the methods of crafting more among the Lunar Host.
                                                    The most common version of the Moonlight-on-the-Water Amulet blurs a Lunar's fate while they wear any shape that is not one of their true forms. Their activities are still visible to the Loom and astrology, but only indicate the presence of the animal, human, or other creature whose shape they stole. This requires a daily expenditure of [motes].
                                                    Amulets worth +2 dots actually apply a continual astrology-fooling effect equal to that of Dragon Armor and even benefits non-Lunars, but very few elders remain who possess the skill to craft such a wonder.
                                                    (From 1E Dragon Armor: “The armor can also protect against detection by divination and astronomy — add +2 difficulty to all attempts to locate or determine the fate of the wearer, his armor and anything it is touching. This is proof against Sidereal astronomy and the foresight of the little gods. (This ability costs 5 motes and one Willpower per day).”)

                                                            An obvious thing for the Moonsilver Mine might be shapeshifting itself to be better concealed than an ordinary trap, and/or that the Lunar can find it with better intuition than they could an ordinary mine, through intuition. I think its a devilishly horrible trap though. It is kind of techy, but its easy enough to just not call it a mine.

                                                            I do think the problem with it, if there is one, is more that Moonsilver is a treasured rare and precious substance of Luna. It's like if you had an Orichalcum Mine or a Starmetal Mine. Is that really something that makes sense as what you'd do with one of the Five Magical Materials? In the sense of a mine as something that you sort of scatter around a domain and then just abandon and forget about, less than something you've placed in quite a specific place for an ambush and reuse.

                                                            I like the idea of a ring that harbors and contains the Lunars' bestial aspects. I think there could be more that could be done with that theme in addition to hiding the tell, if someone wanted to.

                                                            Moonlight-on-the-Water Amulet is a cool idea. I think I'd find it more fun to encounter as a Sidereal PC as a challenge (where is that Sidereal?). It seems like it'd be very dependent for utility on how innocuous what the Lunar is doing is, how well matched their activity is to the shape, and how observant the person watching the Loom is. "The presence of One Lucky Shepherdess in Mnemon's bathing chambers came as some surprise to Falling Leaf, of the Chosen of Endings. Not least because 1) her Fate had been never to leave Incas Prefecture and furthermore 2) because she was dead.". However, I imagine there is quite a bit of flex there in the lazy gods and overworked Sidereals and in doing rather sneaky stuff.
