        Originally posted by Eolirin View Post

        If I wanted to topple the Bureau of Destiny, and I had the same advisory position that the Sidereals had with the Solars, I wouldn't try to do it overnight. I'd work to strengthen the inherent bureaucratic turf conflicts, making their system as dysfunctional and backbitting as possible, keeping them weak and distracted, so that when I did have all my pieces assembled, the strike would be at it's most devastating and hardest to respond to. You don't need to directly recruit Solars or Lunars into things to effectively gain their aid in your plans. Creating a cold war between groups of Solars and Lunars at the height of their insanity probably wouldn't be that hard for the Sidereals to pull off.

        So you don't frame it as "Hey wanna help take out the Solars?", you phrase it as "this small group of your peers are up to some crazy stuff, you should stop them!" while convincing the other groups that the group you're talking to is also crazy, and you do all of this in such a way as to make it seem like they're discovering this on their own. Nothing ties back to you, you rely on existing fault lines and distrust, and nothing hints at the final hammer blow.
        The point of the comparison is that you are trying to recruit Sidereals to help you destroy their reason for existing. Sure, there's at least one who'll do it. The chances of fucking up your one shot at executing the plan trying to recruit the right one are astronomically high, and in the case of the Usurpation, the risk is not worth the benefit five, ten, hell, a dozen Lunar traitors bring to the table. Not when you already have several armies of Dragon-Blooded.

                                Originally posted by Eolirin View Post
                                Consider what it'd mean to the setting if some of the worst excesses of the First Age were actually prompted by some of the Sidereals as part of their plans leading up to the Usurpation, plans that had existed for decades or centuries before fully coming to fruition. Think of what it'd do to the Bronze and Gold faction conflicts if Kejak was implicated in being part of making the Solars so much worse than they would have been because that's what was needed to make sure they fell.
                                I could imagine seeing some people in the fanon seize on this to exaggerate that the Sidereals mainly engineered the circumstances to justify the Usurpation and combine it with

                                - "The Solars were never that bad, rather there were just some disquietening elements of their rule, quiet atrocities"

                                - "The Sidereals and Dragonblooded were really just frightened about Solar powers making them obsolete, or threatening their OCD vision of an orderly world. They weren't driven by any concern for humanity"

                                to make the perfect storm of returning kings who are, in someone's mind, somehow meant to be likeable or rooted for, but are so eternally wronged and betrayed, perfect and never ever in error that they're completely and utterly dislikeable. Maybe that's someone's idea of a good game though, hopefully it's not the norm.

                                On attempting to recruit the Solars, yeah, note of the paths of prophecy was "Attempt to reform the Solars and fail" is the one that would leads to the civil war then apocalypse (while doing nothing was what would just lead to continual decline under Solar misrule, a Dying Earth scenario and quieter dying of the world).

                                Recruiting the Solars or working with them in any form, in terms of fate mojo, might come too close to putting the world on the path of Gold, which, in its worst case scenario, is about an Exalted civil war and then conquest by the Fair Folk, and which the Sidereals didn't plump for.

                                        I'm incidentally reading some stuff about the Iroquois League right now, and I'm wondering at the idea of the Silver Pact adopting apparent tribalistic terminology and hierarchy, not because they being Lunars are intrinsically inclined towards it, but as a form of appropriation from certain extant lifestyles that, both in resulting organizational principles and somewhat political statement, served their purposes.

                                        I'm just thinking, given the lifestyle they'd been forced into and the people they wound up moving amongst, it might have been a useful model for a group that's internally disagreeable but wishes to present an image of external cohesion.

                                        Just, another thing that might help the Silver Pact is if it was viewed in terms of the numerous, very different Lunars, independently looking for ways to make use of one another (with an understanding that the strategies employed by any given Lunar will take a form that is most readily helped by the talents of another Lunar), while also having a means to make them look more unified than they really are (helpful for being a terrifying Other to the Dragon Blooded), and maybe have a nebulous overforce to reference in place of their own goals and methods.
