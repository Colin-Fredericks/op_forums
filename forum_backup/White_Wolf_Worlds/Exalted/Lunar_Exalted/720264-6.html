    Originally posted by Taliesin View Post
    I think what Smiles is saying is that "I can reshape the world with my third circle sorcery" of the Solars and the "my day to day job is to keep the laws of nature and causality working and I know all loopholes to exploit them" of the Sidereals is much more goal-oriented than the Lunars "well, I can shapechange and ... uh ... was around in the last age too. Yay me?". The Lunars have a unique tool but the only thing they seem to use it for is totally reactive - destroy the Realm.
    I know what Smiles was saying. It still comes down to preconceptions and assumptions lowballing what could potentially be much more grand. You say that shapeshifting is limited, and that Lunars are reactive; I say that Lunars make living next to a Dominion like a horror movie for the Dynasts with the misfortune to be assigned to such a post. Lights get snuffed out, communication with your fellows is cut off, and you're so very, very alone. You'll feel it even harder, given where the true might of the Dragon-Blooded comes from.

    Lunars are desperately in need of something they can actively do. All human societies, without any exception, and in fact all forms of life are hardwired to preserve and better themselves. Making Lunars to mere rage-fulled warriors makes them kind of unrealistic. They need to be able to build and enhance stuff, both literally and metaphorically - and just because they are animal-inspired doesn't change that. Over at rpg.net I once suggested a charm called "Million Souls Home" which allows them to instinctively build working homes and infrastructure for lots of people ... as do termites.
    That's not quite on par with the animal legendry which their Charms use as a bedrock. It's about the awe, especially terrified awe, of people coming in from the wilderness with lurid tales of weird and frightening feats which the beasts performed. Cats and owls that stood and watched just outside of a village by someone who drops dead of illness or infirmity, suggesting that they know who's going to die. Armored terrors that can snap a coaster in half in one bite and turn steel with their hide. Tigers that seem to emerge from locales that couldn't hide them. Long-necked, whip-tailed and more elephantine than an elephant dinosaurs, their hide fearsome and barbed, their tails capable of tearing the divine ichor from gods and demons and resounding louder than thunder when they strike. While I like to work in a lot of real life nuances myself, Lunars revel in the spirit of unsettling and frightening others.

    Lunars should be able to create a highly effective but subconsciously working learning-by-imitation/play strategy for people, like a cat-mother bringing half-dead mice to her kittens. They should be able to whalesing helpful or manipulative memes into existence which pop up at the same time in multiple, far away places. They should be able to slowly, passively but irreversibely make their surrounding more supportive to life - not like a Solar plopping down a palace via sorcerous working, but like the roots of a tree slowly breaking and eroding stone to fertile earth.

    And besides something they can actively do, Lunars need something great they have actively DONE. Mate and companion to Solars is ok and fine - but at the moment they look more like trophy wives. There is no problem at all with being the minor half of a legendary pair, if it's done right: Enkidu was never as protagonist-y as Gilgamesh, but the story had enough points to make Enkidu a vital part of the story. Lunars are missing something like that when the game talks about the age when the celestial exalted still ruled Creation. The Solar ruled and created wonders, the Sidereals kept the world running and do so today. And the Lunars ... well ... most likely they helped to rule the world? Or built some smaller wonders? Maybe?
    They have done something great - they locked horns with a civilization that had those vaunted masters of fate and the children of the five dragons, who had all those warstriders and flying ships, and they've made that civilization bleed pale and clear. Grand tales of overwhelming victory might get included in the Lunar book, to show how the undying hope and rage of the Lunars is putting them ahead in the long game.

            @ Saur Ops Specialist

            I never said that shapeshifting is limited either. I just said that shapeshifting is a tool, but not a goal. It doesn't matter if shapeshifting is werewhatever-only or something that includes other states of being and the most fantastical chimerical creatures, there is still the question "what are you going to do with that?".

            And that's what I mean with "reactive". The Lunars want to tear down the Realm and fight the Dragonblooded and Sidereals.
            Fine. Ok. And then? What are they going to do when they succeed? Run to the countryside, hide in bushes and jump at mortals, screaming loud "BOOOO!" when one comes along - if making people afraid and creeping them out is so important to them as you say, this sounds like a reasonable endgame.

            For me this sounds just lacking. Currently the Lunars seem to be defined almost completely by their opposition to something else, but they have no clear goal or even options about what they want to do on their own, no higher goals or own aspirations (contrary to their current state of "we don't want that these guys over there reach their goals of ruling the world").
            The Lunars should be the strongest Exalted in the setting after the Solars disappeared - but strangely the thought of "we are the top dog, so we should rule creation" never seemed to pop up and all they got is "well, let's find some barbarian tribe so they can worship us".

            That's what I wanted to say with my charm ideas: generative or protective charms and powers work on their own, destructive effects work just when there is someone or something that needs to be destroyed. If Lunars don't get the first type of charm, their stories are limited to "defeat enemy" but they won't be able to tell stories about "create and manage kingdom" because they don't have the means for this (or just the means a normal human would have for that).
            That's why I liked the 1000 Stream Rivers so much. Ok, I admit, the execution of the idea was not really overwhelmingly good in Ex2, but the theory behind it was awesome. The Lunars just need more abilities that interact with such a goal.

            Luckily the devs said in the corebook and in some older forum posts something like "the Lunars of today are no longer the Lunars of the First Age and they changed themselves". Which is at least for me and my group enough reason to allow our Lunar to completely rewrite his charmset from rage-filled warform-smash warrior to something much more subtle, creative and less destruction-focused.

                                                I don't quite think that blatant PHENOMENAL COSMIC POWER quite suits the Lunars - the same way Luna doesn't have a singular thing that can be said to be exceedingly standout the way the Unconquered Sun has his undefeated perfection or the Maidens have Fate -, which doesn't make them any less suitable of being considered Celestial Exalts - or Luna any less of an Incarna. In my view, they are the power of the heavens on Creation incarnate in its most primal, yet most subtle form.

                                                They have unmatched flexibility and guile among the celestial exalted, and wield both sorcery and martial arts that reach the height of divine power, even if they may never breach their highest secrets. Their bodies and raw capabilities express incredible power beyond the Solars and Sidereals, even if their respective world-shifting prowess and reality-ordering finesse are beyond Luna's Chosen. Through their shapeshifting and their link to animals, they are the closest to the natural world, and through their keen sensitivity and their ancient discenment, may understand it and its cycles better than even the dragonblood, attuned to the elements themselves, ever could.

                                                I agree that they could use a lasting mark on Creation or an impressive constructive proof of their feats, but at the same time that might be the sort of thing that would lessen their narrative and take away from their role in Creation now - their vicious underdog role as defined by the Usurpation and their bitter, centuries long inherited grudge that they nonetheless have the potential to break free from. But I don't think the writers are going to ONLY use that for the Lunars, and I certainly don't think they're going to be reduced to mere rage filled warriors; their conflict, even individually, is more nuanced than that and has a lot more meaning for them and for the rest of Creation.

                                                And there was something they actively did. They did have an important, powerful place in the First Age: where the Solars ruled and created wonders, and the Sidereals ordained and maintained the world, the Lunars guarded it. They were the wardens of the Old Realm, and the protectors of the world, but as the Moon seems faint by comparison when the Sun is still in the sky, so too does the significance of the Lunars of then seem small. But when your duty leaves you outside the center of the greatest accomplishments of your time and you are by designation placed as a complimentary second to another, of course your achievements enhance the brilliance of those who have occupied the stage rather than be recognized on their own. The Lunars worked to support the Solars in all things and labored away from sight in a way different from the Sidereals, and a consequence of that is that their shine wouldn't get through all else that was more evident of the essence of that era. Without them, the Old Realm may well not have been near as successful, and the amazing creations of the Solars would not have come into being so easily, but the Lunars kept at their functions at a distance which made them easy to overlook.

                                                            I really think that rather than downplaying Luunars we're merely pointing out what appears to be obvious flaws or weakness in the way they're shown.
                                                            Most Exalted had a clear reason for being, a role that only they could fill, a specific purpose nobody was as qualified to accomplish, except for Lunars. When I say this, I'm not downplaying them, just pointing that they weren't given their own niche to fill.

                                                            We get a description of the Silver Pact raising up barbarian armies to harass the Realm forces, getting the tribes defeated, then moving on to start again, eroding the Realm's strength. What other group is given such a narrow focus and fixed modus operandi?
                                                            We're told not all Lunars get on this train, but no other major Lunar faction is mentioned, when even the Siddies being divided between Bronze, Gold, followers of the Sleepwalker is explicitely stated. And they're three times less numerous! We're not back to the point of being told to pick different weapons to be able to differentiate between Lunars, but the narrow focus is clearly felt nonetheless.
                                                            Pointing his out is not downplaying Lunars, just attracting notice on the perception I have of this fact.

                                                            As for the shapeshifting thing, in earlier editions Lunar shapeshifting was designed to be "primal" or whatever, rather than esoteric. They didn't turn into strange creatures with wondrous abilities, they became animals, sometimes in giant or miniature versions. It's a design choice. It's not a bad one, but there is no way turning into Zilla (not even Godzilla) would rival SCS or SMA.
                                                            3e looks like it will go even more into the animal thing, what with Luna making her Chosen bestial because of Gaia. There are ways to make this more than just about turning into giant critters, so I hope we'll see something less limited this time.
