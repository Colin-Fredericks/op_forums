                                        Militarily, there are numerous examples of a for that is stronger on paper ultimately losing or withdrawing because the conditions for victory are sufficiently frustrating or inconveniencing that they'd prefer to make a settlement, or give up entirely.

                                        The United States is making peace overtures to the Taliban. The Taliban didn't suddenly develop their own drones.

                                        Socially and economically, negotiation from a position of weakness is a thing. It's a thing based on accepting that you're constrained to give up something, but through clever presentation, a bit of cunning, maybe harnessing conflicts or divisions in the other side, and appeals to their better nature (or at least their vanity), it's possible to give up more manageable quantities. That's when the stronger party doesn't enter with an intent to be more reasonable.

                                        These systems are only binary if one believes solely in zero-sum games, which is ludicrous.

                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead

                                        The Immaculate narrative about the nature of gods is not completely inaccurate, but it's still a bad idea to take the justifications of an authoritarian society at face value. Not all gods require the constant threat of force to not continuously exploit mortals, not least because sometimes the threat only needs to be made once and it sticks. Some cultures would prefer the authority of their traditional gods to that of the Realm, and not even just because it's lighter; people can be averse to an alien and intrusive culture as a matter of principle.

                                        And for all the Immaculate posturing, the Realm still negotiates with many gods. Salt gods may have constraints in negotiation, but at the end of the day they're still getting a percentage of the value of an important commodity. After enough generations, it would be a bad idea for Immaculates to enforce their compliance through brute force even if they were able; too much of Realm finances is valued based on the rate of taxes for the sacrifices to the salt gods.

                                                        I can't speak for other writers (and certainly not the devs), but "Gods are full-on assholes, but a few are okay" is not an assumption I write from. I write from the assumption that gods are venal and self-centered by human standards, because their nature gives them little capacity to relate to many human struggles. They tend toward shocking short-sightedness and mind-boggling far-sightedness, which may make them seem more callous,but on average they aren't innately more or less cruel or violent than humans are. Even gods out in the middle of nowhere (by human standards) have relationships, want ways to make their jobs/lives easier or more rewarding, and can respond to other overtures with generosity or hostility, as they feel is warranted. Even corrupt gods of a fallen age have some incentive to think cooperatively because, to borrow a popular phrase, they live in a society. The horror of gods in Exalted is not that they are hateful or unrelatable, but that their motivations are banal and relatable. Their natures make them no more or less monstrous than any powerful person who's had the privilege not to experience certain hardships, or who thought that their problems were more important than anyone else's.

                                                        Put another way, a god is the sort of person that might hear you talk about having lost your entire family to a wasting plague, and respond that their favorite tree died thirty years ago, because they think those losses are similar in scope. Depending on their attitude, that might lead them to try to empathize, or to tell you to shrug it off, and they might become gravely offended if you object either way. Conversely, if you show appreciation for their condescending wisdom, they might favor you with a blessing or even their personal attention.
