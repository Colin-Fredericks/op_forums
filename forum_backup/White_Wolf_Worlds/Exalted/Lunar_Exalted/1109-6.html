    Anyway, Alex Mercer was dead on a slab and then woke up with hardly any memories from the brain he inheritted (though with some muscle memories from the parkour enthusiasts who got absorbed into his body) and with a completely different personality than he had in life. He's something very much not natural or human, though he's human enough to understand and interact with people. He also improves himself by incorporating the flesh of other people into his own body.

    So, I figure he's very much a Liminal Exalt. Born from a dead man's body as a new, abominable creature that will never really fit in among natural human beings. He's also the quintessential Flesh Aspect, being a figure of violence and rage and revenge, whose powers revolve around superhuman physique, using his flesh as a weapon, and more efficiently incorporating the bodies of his victims into himself (ie: absorbing them whole and directly, without the need for surgery).

                                            The direction of previous editions and the setting info for Exalted 3 gives enough information to answer a few of the questions above.

                                            Originally posted by Arrakiz
                                            Something to consider. I don't remember there ever being an incentive to give Lunars an ability to transform parts of their bodies, on the spot, acording to the situation.

                                            As far as I remember, when it comes to transformation, it was always complete. The only "partial transformation" is the hybrid form, but arguably that's just the third, complete and integral form Lunars can assume.
                                            Don't count on anything like Hybrid Body Rearrangement. There are several approaches to shapeshifting, and the one that allows you to customize your limbs / organs whatever on the fly to perform a particular task is not what Lunars have ever been about. It's not what any of the mythic shapeshifters (e.g., Loki) that Lunars are supposedly drawing inspiration from were about. If tools for this appear in the Lunar manual, they'll be a minor and comparatively-unimportant part of shapeshifting. What you have to look forward to should be clear enough.


                                            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold
                                            But that utter failure to even harm the enemy, followed by Shinji's semi-ascension and one-man victory, apparently followed by someone else (who is apparently a Solar?) using Unit 02 better than Mari... It just stinks so damn much of Second Edition's "only Solars matter".
                                            Going in with this attitude I can guarantee that Exalted 3 Lunars will feel like a disappointment to you. The facts of the matter are that they were left out of the cozy arrangement that Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded brewed up, were hunted down like animals by the Wyld Hunt, and have not succeeded in bringing their enemy down. The writing team will write this to sound good ("it is only by the constant erosion of Lunar sabotage that the Realm has been brought down this low"), but it does not change that the defining narrative of Lunars is about their failure. The narrative in the standard Exalted game will be "Lunars fought the Realm constantly for hundreds of years, but it was only when the Solar Exalted arrived that the tide turned and the Empire fell".

                                            I encourage you to read what Holden has revealed about Lunars again. In his essay, he describes "barbarians" as being defined by oppression from an empire: their infrastructure is broken, their treasures are looted, and they are chased off to live in the ruins of their former cities and make their living in resource-poor lands that the empire doesn't want. That is what Lunars are. Using your perspective as detailed in that previous post, Lunars are the beast-mode Eva making a show and buying time until the Solar Exalted return and punch the Realm down.

                                            If you don't find this appealing (I don't find anything about the new direction of Lunars appealing), I suggest that you drop Lunars. Your attachment is to your own memories and conception of them, which will never come to pass in official material. Lunars are not being written for you, but for some audience that is a fan of pulp fantasy and will hopefully want to play leather-clad, shapeshifting barbarians on a warpath. So, leave it to them. With the new Exalted and the Exigent Exalted being introduced this edition, you don't have to care: you can tailor what you want in a way that's consistent with the setting. Please believe me when I say that doing this will do wonders for your mood. The amount of worry, care and hope-seasoned-by-despair that I've read in this thread horrifies me, not least because I used to share it.

                                                            Originally posted by Faller3 View Post
                                                            I don't know much as to what's up with New Lunars, but I am certainly excited about Spirit Shape being more important.
                                                            Definitely with ya, there. ^_^ Here's the big blurb where John talked about his plans for the Spirit Shape. That was many months ago, so things may have changed, as he notes. Still, it's very promising.
                                                            Originally posted by John Mørke
                                                            It is still the animal you choose to be the basis for your DBT. How much I force it into your character's appearance remains to be seen. If you don't want to be a DBT Lunar, I'd rather you think of DBT / spirit shape as the tool you relied on when your Lunar was still young, inexperienced, and less powerful, rather than ignoring it entirely. I want to help people build away from a DBT-based character through powers that work when you are in your human form and don't work when you are in DBT.

                                                            Once you reach that point, it is my intention that the spirit shape, through a clever use of keywords and associations, provide you a favored discount on Charms whose Attributes you don't favor, going so far as to change the Charm's prerequisites and shift it to a logical place in your current Charm trees (if one exists). So even if you ignore your spirit shape in roleplay, its themes are there helping you by providing a framework to power up your character.

                                                            I also realize that one of the reasons people search so furiously for meaning beyond DBT is because a single animal is so constraining, so I am looking at completely redoing Chimeraism so that it is an accepted state, so that players can make cool-looking hybrid monsters with lots of different traits.

                                                            Note that everything I've said here is "Maybe" not "I promise" and is subject to change. This is about as far as I am willing to reach into my giant vault of future noise to show people, but I feel like the general tone of acceptance and readiness for big new ideas has reached a level where such a classified view won't confuse and upset anyone.
