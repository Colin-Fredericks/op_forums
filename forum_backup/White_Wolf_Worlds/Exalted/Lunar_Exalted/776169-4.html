    Originally posted by Root View Post
    The devs seem pretty set on Lunars as monsters this time around, and I think that will drive the stories you can tell with them. Earlier someone brought up the inconvenience of taking on a child's form without doing something monstrous. That looks deliberate to me. Maybe your character will take on a child's form by finding someone who is sick beyond saving and convincing herself that death is merciful. She's gonna have a time with the actual tasting of the Heart's Blood, assuming no charms, but hey, that's what a monster would do. That doesn't mean every Lunar is a wanton child murderer by design. It just means they will sometimes have a reason to do something monstrous, and perhaps think about how to mitigate it or make up for it. Or in other cases how to rationalize, pretend nothing happened, and move on. Or in yet other cases to slide into more horrifying habits. Seems like the point of all this is to examine those themes, not ditch them when they get in the way of overcoming challenges as the player sees fit.
    I agree. The Lunar is an outsider to human culture, breaking taboos (cannibalism, bestiality). But, at the same time, the setting sets them up as "heroes" against the Realm's "villains" which I find is interesting.
    And certainly, looking at the Lunar PCs in the games I run/play, they do engage with these themes with reluctance in quite an interesting way.
    It's why I dislike the knack for gaining forms by just knocking people out. You're playing a monster-hero, for goodness' sake!

    I would say that polyamory is not a great example though Alucard, because it's something that some societies do accept (perhaps in the more formal form of polygamy or polyandry), and which is semi-acceptable in our society (I mean, I know a whole bunch of people who do it, or have done it; which makes me wonder, what is the link between roleplayers and polyamory? There's got to be a sociology paper in that).

As impressive as those characters can be, they're also guided by someone else whose glory they live for, and who points them in a good direction.             Lunars have a special problem; the community doesn't know what we want from them. Almost everything about them to date is considered divisive. The only thing we are united on is that the previous iterations have problems, though which elements are problematic and how...terminal they are have been the center of many debates.

            It is made harder because the many conceptions of Lunars have led to a plethora of compelling concepts, often mutually exclusive. The ideal Lunar splat would let you play; the Monster, the Outsider, the Barbarian, the Infiltrator, the Power behind the Throne, the Dionysian Witch-Queen, Wolverine, Spiderman, the (fake) tribal spirit, the Frontier Lord, the Warden of Worlds, the Inhuman Genius as well as Animal Man & Changeling/Beast Boy.

            Until we get (official) Lunars that are fantastic enough to unite the fandom, we are not going to be happy with any Lunars...we don't know how a 'good' Lunar works, let alone looks like.

            Additionally a lot of prior baggage means Lunars are perceived to be lesser actors, with less agency than most other Exalted. Which is almost universally decried as awful but certain relationships (such as the Solar Bond) have been portrayed as fundamental to the concept. Being defined by a relationship is a highly unhealthy in any case.

            Thoughts ripple out, birthing others

                                        I wrote an essay, once, on Lunars as the Other, which was on the old forums and on the wiki, but sadly, I don't think I had a personal copy of it that survived the computer-swaps from then until now.

                                        The idea, though, was that Lunars can be ally or enemy, rival or coconspirator, friend or nemesis, even to those whom they support or undermine. They can do the more traditional forms of allegiance and opposition, being an open and honest friend to bolster and aid, or a clear and present danger to rip apart those they oppose. But they have a tremendous capacity to aid those for whom they care by being the enemy who sharpens them, the rival who keeps them on their toes, or the terror that constrains them from self-destructive behavior. They also have a horrifying facility to destroy by being close, cloying, trusted aids who cut all support and undermine and encourage their hated ones to destroy themselves, with their despised victim thanking them for it all the way.

                                        My favorite example that I can remember is a Lunar-as-Monster who protects a village. Taboos about going out at night, about entering certain parts of the surrounding woods, and about how and when to greet visitors all spring up from the fact that there is a horrifying Monster that dwells in the dark. It lurks just on the edge of vision, and attacks those who transgress the taboos the village has pieced together. But it is also the ONLY threat to the village; nothing else that would threaten it dares draw near. Bandits break the taboos and are destroyed before they can so much as harm a wayward child. That wayward child is chased, even wounded, to the edge of the village for having gone into the forbidden parts of the forrest...but he survived, when he might otherwise have been slain. And those things which the Monster tacitly forbids? All are things which endanger those who do them, endanger the town as a whole if ignored, or are otherwise harmful.

                                        The Lunar is a horrifying beast and predator that everybody in town is afraid of. A force of nature that they all are only partially comfortable with, and then only because they have the taboos figured out. But it keeps them safe, gives them room to prosper and grow, while other villages without such a terrorizing beast have floundered and even been wiped out.
