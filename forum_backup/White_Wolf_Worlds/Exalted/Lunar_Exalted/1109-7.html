    I am currently playing a Lunar in a long-term mixed-group Heaven's Reach game (passed 150XP not too long ago). He's the group's pilot, but per Lunar norms is actually fairly proficient at a wide variety of things. I think this is the niche Lunars are meant to occupy in mixed groups: the best at moving between places quickly and competent-to-great at nearly everything else.

    He's about 50% custom Charms at this point, excluding excellencies, simply because they're necessary to realize the character fully. To be entirely blunt, this character is an iteration of Lunar that exceeds even 2.5 in liberal application of the rules.

    Our imagining of HR has all but 9 Lunar exaltations under the thumb of the Emperor. So almost every Lunar you encounter is or was an Imperial operative (this character included). Lunars frequently work with Sidereals to stabilize problems inside the Empire and occasionally outside the Empire, depending on the Lunar in question. They can work in opposition, of course, if the Emperor's will doesn't match what the Grand Central Mountain foresees/cultivates as necessary.

    So we opted to throw out basically all of the form-related Knacks. They don't seem relevant in the least to any of the Lunar characters and it means Lunars have much more interesting and varied totems. (We have: a legendary giant bird (that can fly in vacuum because magic), a space wyrm, a colossal predatory weasel, and a swarm of insects.) It also means Lunars change forms at their leisure, and change their clothes like other characters change their expressions, and move freely through society, making them ideal in their role as embedded agents of the Emperor. The Knacks we did keep are: form changing speed Knacks, ritual form acquisition Knacks, and Fate-related Knacks. This means a Lunar at outset is pretty robust from a shapeshifting perspective, but one who invests deeply in it doesn't leave a trail of bodies, digital detritus, and snarled threads of Fate behind them. They're literally ghosts in the machine. Which seems a fair trade for 50+ XP in my estimation.

    Hopefully Knacks will conform to this dynamic, where you're basically buying an ability that is expressed through shapeshifting, rather than the opportunity to break game mechanics if you can convince everyone it's worth their time to let you.

If tools for this appear in the Lunar manual, they'll be a minor and comparatively-unimportant part of shapeshifting. What you have to look forward to should be clear enough.
                    Interesting.

                    So, do you also feel that Wasp of the Labyrinth Trick will be abandoned, at least in its current form? If HBR is unthematic, I don't see how briefly growing an ovipositor would be appropriate. If Lunars are going to be primal barbarian beast-gods, then perhaps the Charm would invoke a Voudoun ritual, with chants, gris-gris and other fetishes and ritual trappings?


                    Going in with this attitude I can guarantee that Exalted 3 Lunars will feel like a disappointment to you. The facts of the matter are that they were left out of the cozy arrangement that Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded brewed up, were hunted down like animals by the Wyld Hunt, and have not succeeded in bringing their enemy down.
                    Don't care. That's fine. That's not my issue.

                    Morke said he intends Lunars to be able to throw down with Infernals on equal ground in combat. If an Infernal Shinji shows up a Lunar Mari that badly, it's going to be because of an XP differential, a character build more effective in that particular situation, or just plain having the better Warstrider courtesy of the Storyteller.

                    It's not going to be because Lunars are inherently chumps. Not that they were in Second Edition, mind. Not mechanically.

                    But I'm still remembering that one poster, where each of the signature Solar circle is facing off against one other Exalted opponent. Except for Panther, who was fighting two beastmen.

                    Not one obvious Exalt, like anyone else in the picture. Two naked, unarmed beastmen who might have been Lunars in warform, except neither one of them featured any anima display or Castemark at all, so they could have been just mortal beastmen.

                    So either the Lunar Exalted got no representation at all in a picture that was otherwise exclusively casted with Exalts, or they were deemed the only Exalted that needed to double-team a Solar (or bring along a mortal helper) to provide a challenge. Despite Mnemon, of the Terrestrial Exalted designed specifically to rely on numbers, dueling Ariana by her lonesome. Mnemon was also clothed and displaying obvious signs of supernatural power.

                    That poster has nothing to do with the Lunar history of disenfranchisement and exile. The poster is meant to say, "Being an Exalt is awesome!" Unless you're a Lunar.

                    And Lunars don't really appear anywhere but their own book in Second Edition (excepting Shards), and their own book whines too much about History Is What Was Done To Us to make it sound awesome to be a Lunar.


                    As an aside, I consider the cover of Scroll of Exalts to be more of an unfortunate coincidence rather than "more of the same" (and certainly not as part of a non-existent conspiracy). Sure, it could have been a Dragon-King filling in for "lizardperson laying slain on the altar", but that would have broken the pattern of only Exalted being the characters on the cover, and they were already stretching the visual homage by adding a second thief going for the idol's gemstone eyes. There really wasn't much room left on the page for yet another not-dead character.

                    I also thought Madame Vert was a fool and would have felt no great loss.


                    If you don't find this appealing (I don't find anything about the new direction of Lunars appealing), I suggest that you drop Lunars. Your attachment is to your own memories and conception of them, which will never come to pass in official material.
                    My conception of Lunars has always been as fluid as the execution of them. I've only ever been searching and searching for "what Lunars SHOULD be" without any particular attachment to one concept or another, so long as that concept was strong, well-executed, and made sense in the context of the setting.

                    I've proposed a various different versions of them, since my First Edition days.


                    Lunars are not being written for you, but for some audience that is a fan of pulp fantasy and will hopefully want to play leather-clad, shapeshifting barbarians on a warpath.
                    And I'm interested to see how that plays out!


                    With the new Exalted and the Exigent Exalted being introduced this edition, you don't have to care: you can tailor what you want in a way that's consistent with the setting.
                    I definitely am looking forward to making my Changeling Exalted a reality, once I get my hands on the new rulebook.

                    formerly Tornado Wolf, formerly Inugami

                            The Lunar Exalted are playing the long game, pretending at being the wild and savage barbarians, but they've been playing chess all along. It's a war of attrition the Lunar Exalted are better-equipped for, and they probably aren't losing.

                            The return of the Solars isn't turning the tide, it's knocking over the chessboard at a time when things are getting unpredictable. How would the Lunars handle the sudden civil war in the Realm, and its pouring-out into their satrapies, in the absence of the returning Lawgivers?
