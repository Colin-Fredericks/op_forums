            Originally posted by Imrix View Post

            Like, the Lunar Exalted are four hundred individuals scattered across the length and breadth of the entire world.
            I don't think it's as bad as all that, actually. There are certain areas where Lunars are going to cluster more, just given the circumstances. They're going to avoid the Realm proper, and its satrapies, for obvious reasons. Likewise, they're probably not going to be living out in Creation's true wilderness areas, because most of them will have goals that require some humans to accomplish. That leaves out the deep forests of the East, the deep deserts of the south, most of the open water of the West, and the frozen wastelands of the North.

            So, looking at this handy map of 3e Creation, I'd estimate that the areas the Lunars are packed into is probably around 50,000,000, or maybe 55,000,000, square miles. Now, that sure sounds like a lot, but if you start doing the math, it gets less intimidating. It suggests each Lunar could have about 140,000 square miles of territory to handle personally, which, in turn, is only about 375 miles on a side. So, two Lunars will be on average only ~400 miles apart, which is actually not a long journey for a being that can fairly trivially assume the form of a bird.

            And Lunars are likely to cluster even more than that - a young Lunar is likely to stay near an elder who's willing to teach and protect them, and elders might accumulate several younger Lunars at once while doing so. That, in turn, ups the likelyhood that someone in the group has communication magic that can reach long distances, so not every Lunar needs to have it to keep groups connected.

            All of this is to say, I think the "loosely connected group of mutual allies and friends" model (or the "mailing list" model, as you described it) isn't that implausible as a description of how the Lunars organize themselves. And Lunars may come from a very diverse set of backgrounds, but the enmity of the Dragon-Blooded host and the Sidereals is going to be a strong motivator to overcome that and get organized, at least to the point of the loose mutual aid and information setup they have right now.

                                                Personally, I've generally liked the way that the game has always presented the idea that, between similarity in nature and purpose and a historical continuity from the time in which they were most directly uplifted in common by the gods to go to war against the terrible monsters that created the world, each group of Exalted has had a sense of solidarity, if not outright community. Obviously the setting and the game presents these as strongest with the Sidereal and Terrestrial Exalted, but it still persisted as at least an underlying detail for Solars and Lunars (both among themselves and intertwined); even if they weren't getting along, there was still a common forum in which they could ultimately reconvene. I like it for the sense of ancient world mythical caste system it projects.

                                                The Solars were always distinct in how their existence in the world came after a period of that continuity being destroyed completely; they were the only Exalted who ever returned to the world after a lengthy absence. The Terrestrial and Sidereal Exalted didn't have that; the central hinge of the setting is on how their colleges took over the world by destroying the Solar one, with the latter group having living members who remember that instance. The Lunar Exalted aren't like that; their college was bloodied up a bit and forced underground, but there was never a case in which 98% of them disappeared completely from the world for a thousand years and returned piecemeal all of a sudden, and they too have living members who remember the circumstances of their status changing.

                                                Heck, the Abyssal and Infernal Exalted came about under circumstances in which fresh new communities tailor made for them were ready to accept and condition and gird them, before setting them loose on the world, substitutions for the thing that the Solars lost when they were almost completely wiped out and gone for centuries; they have solidarity (as distinct from organisation) provided for them for the sake of malefic agendas.

                                                And the Solars are quite distinct in the idea that they're returning to the world after that foundation has long since fallen away and been forgotten, with nothing waiting to take its place, almost all on an even footing, giving them a completely fresh slate such that no Exalted have had ever, meaning they're a lot more free to be themselves rather than being part of an overarching structure of Solar Exalted.

                                                I think it's neat, and while I respect the idea to overturn the rest of that elsewhere, I do think that some of the core narrative of the return of the Solars would be diminished if there was no sense of contrast between their situation and that of the Exalted that have actually been alive for the past thousand years.

                                                That and I'm just attached to the other Exalted communities.

                                                It's fine to not like them, but I think it's overlooking a lot to say that they don't make sense.

                                                            So, I've just recently started playing a new character, Dr Soma, a snake-totem Lunar. She's a martial artist and doctor who lived in a big city so has good knowledge of other lands (Lore 3)). She exalted fighting some corrupt Realm soldiers.

                                                            So, what to do on her first day as an Anathema? Run. Before the famous Wyld Hunt arrives to kill her.

                                                            So she ran to the woods. She did some Sacred Hunting (I asked Vance, he said it's instinctual knowledge) and picked up a bird form. She then flew west.

                                                            But where to go next? Well, she doesn't know much about being an Anathema. Maybe she can find an experienced Anathema sifu to teach her.
                                                            Folktales, merchant's tales, and Immaculate propaganda have told her of 4 possible candidates in distant lands (but reachable as a bird): Deep in the jungle to the east is the monstrous barbarian warlord Ma-Ha-Suchi, whose beastmen are the reason you never go deep into the jungle. Closer, west, is the demon sorceress Enhenduanna, who apparently sacrifices thousands to feed her dark god. South-East is a desert city recently seized by the debauched sorcerer Babar. A bit more West is the Tangle, an impenetrable thorn-forest of barbarians led by a giant demon-bear. (Who is canon.)
                                                            But it's hard to tell what's propaganda and what's real, and they didn't all sound like Lunars. So she went for the demon bear. It was hundreds of miles, but that's not far as a bird. She had to stop and ask directions a lot though.
