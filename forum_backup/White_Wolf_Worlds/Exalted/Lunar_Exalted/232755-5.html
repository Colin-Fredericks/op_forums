                                        I'm pretty sure the default assumption is that if something opposes your dice pool or static value, you get to know about it as a player in order to use powers that enhance those things.                                                     Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                                    Well yes, generally, but you don't need to know everything. For instance, when you meet someone who's disguised, you normally roll (Perception + Awareness). But you don't need to be told that this is a disguise roll. You're just rolling (Perception + Awareness) to see what you notice about this character.
                                                    If you're told to roll Perception + Awareness, it's implicit that you could use an Awareness Excellency or other Charms to improve your roll. There's no division in this case between "actively looking for something" and "instinctively spotting something of interest"; either way when the roll is called for, you can use Charms on it. Meaning that the declarative Charm use is purely an OOC act at least some of the time, versus a deliberate IC act.

                                                    The notion that the Tell's social attack is "undetectable" seems to imply to some that you cannot augment your "notice this thing" pool, in this case the MDV, because you are not aware of the attack IC. Maybe because the MDV is seen as a passive thing, when really it's an averaged result stand-in for a roll that is designed to speed processes up by applying a flat value rather than requiring a roll. But even when applied to other non-deliberate rolls (e.g. Awareness, most Resistance rolls) it's clear that simply OOC being told about something prompts the ability to spend motes to augment it. Hence why the problem arises if there's an implicit "you don't even get an OOC prompt" rule in the Tell.
