                    Was reading a book by one of my favorite Zen authors and came across a few quote-worthy lines that immediately made me think of the Lunars:

                    When you identify rigidly with certain aspects of yourself you're actively suppressing those aspects that don't fit that identity.
                    I could see an elder teaching this to a new Lunar.

                    Daigan told me, "People in some forms of Buddhism say you have to get rid of your identity. But for me, I spent a lot of time getting molested over my identity. What do you mean get rid of it? This is what we got. But later on I saw that it's not the identity we get rid of. We change our relationship to it. We try not reify it or lock it into some place. It's seeing identity as empty. It's just there. It doesn't have any intrinsic value. it's not good, and it's not bad."
                    Ignoring that this is specifically related to Buddhism, the part about changing your relationship with your identity strikes me as being very much a Lunar ideal.

                    Defining oneself as straight, gay, bisexual, kinky or whatever designation you prefer is one way for the ego to try and delineate itself. Such a definition may be provisionally true and may have some use. But ultimately it's not who you are. Buddhist practice is about trying to uncover something fare more fundamental.
                    Ignoring that this is specifically related to sexuality, the idea of using self-identifiers as the way the ego tries to delineate itself, and how while that may be the way you provisionally perceive yourself in a given context, it's not who you really are you. You are not your identity; you have an identity.

                                        I've long had the desire to make a Lunar who from time to time chooses to humiliate the hell out of his adversaries. He does so by assuming the form of a mouse. And then commencing the ass-kicking of a lifetime. With or without brandishing the giant moonsilver daiklave. I don't recall that being especially viable in 2nd edition, but I'm hopeful for EX3.

                                        Honestly, I'd be psyched for a much greater emphasis on the trickster side of Lunars in general. I mean, that's the archetype that stands out to me when I think of a lot of shapeshifter myths Lunars draw upon. I'm hopeful they work off of that at least as much as the warrior archetype.

                                        Which I actually feel fairly confident and excited about; I remember Holden spelling out that the Lunar castes embody Warrior, Trickster, and Shaman for a reason. Like, all in the same breath. As though the latter two aren't going to feel like throwaway afterthoughts this time!

                                        I'd be happier still with more examples of stuff in those two wheelhouses -- I admit I still don't feel I have a solid grasp of the Lunar Charm paradigm, altogether -- but I'm happy enough with the reassurance they won't be neglected.
