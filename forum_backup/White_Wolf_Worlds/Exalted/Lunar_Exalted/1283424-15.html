    So, Favor trading is complicated, but it sounds pretty similar to a lot of the ways pre-industrial societies and/or small villages handled things.

    The Shahan-ya Councils are where they ratify the decisions of the executive officer of the week I see. I assume most need to be ratified by a simple majority in the case of purely internal affairs, but by a two thirds majority in the case of more major- "Be Quiet! I order you to be quiet!"

    So, she murders a dude, takes over their wealth, and uses it to hold a banquet? That's hella creepy, right? Right?

    I like the prohibition against murder, but I imagine that it could be difficult to prove at times. ....Though, now I'm imagining a newly Exalted Lunar who has called council to accuse another Lunar of the murder of their previous life. That could be interesting.

    I do like that they don't just exile people and hope that it solves the problem.

    The Realm is horrible. However, there are times when it is horribly useful, because they do actually solve problems with rogue gods, bandits, Fae, and other such problems. But the system is still terrible and uncaring and, worst of all, makes other national leaders go "Hey, I wanna be like THAT!" and perpetuates itself.

    Good points on why assassination is a relatively rare thing. I enjoy the idea of Dynasts using Doppleganger Codes.

    ...Lunars undermine attempts at reform, presumably on the basis that it'd be "too little, too late," because none of the Dynasts really want to give up their ability to do lines of jade infused coke off a pleasure slave's belly. But, that got me wondering about what Creation might look like today if the remnants of the Lunars and Sids had come together during the Fae invasion and/or the bit just after when Her Redness claimed Creation and they all tried to rebuild everything? Can you imagine a Realm where the DBs serve as monster hunters and basically do what they do now, but the Lunars are there to counter balance things, so the Realm doesn't take too much? Where neither side has to pour all this extra energy into fighting each other? Where the Wyld Hunt doesn't exist (so, yes, there's like ten living Solars. If one of them really does go crazy, we thin we can probably handle that.)

    I get that all the bad blood made trust impossible and so that could never be ...and that makes me very sad.

    Anyway, back to the best ways to kill the Realm

    Herding cats again.

    Guerilla road workers, who show up and rip up roads in the middle of the night. Ninja accountants who sneak in and alter tax records.

    Also, now I'm thinking about Fight Club and a group of Lunars and humans who attempt to burn down the main records building in the Realm. I could actually see some great houses helping with that, since you could just say "Debt? What debt? No-one has any way to prove I haven't paid you, especially since I have records here that say I did. So are we done here?"

    "....Wait, let me get this straight. We're fighting imperialism and your main plan for combating this is, basically, to force them to make the imperialism worse?" "Yup!" "Really?!?"

    So, house cat vs. Mouse!Lunar who really doesn't want to give anything away is totally a thing that should happen in games and more Guild Factors should be carrying insect eating animals on their shoulders, got it.

    Someone acting weird and then vanishing is a pretty solid sign of Lunar activity. It wouldn't surprise me if some Lunars didn't arrange to do that periodically just to heighten paranoia. Or even get some mortals to fake it for them.

    Oooh, yeah. I have always loved the idea that you can sneak into a manse, rearrange the furniture, and then it explodes a week later, and I will be looking forward to rules for that in 3e. But, yeah, tricking the Realm into walking their forces through an ancient Working that even you barely understand also sounds hella fun.

    I keep thinking about how, if you had half the hearthstones in the corebook together, they could enrich and protect a small city state. You might need to move them around a bit sometimes, but it could be pretty idyllic there. I could see a Lunar doing this.

    Yeah I could see Lunars faced with the prospect of trying to assemble a professional, open field army balking because they know exactly how vulnerable such large and slow targets are and how expensive they are to maintain.
