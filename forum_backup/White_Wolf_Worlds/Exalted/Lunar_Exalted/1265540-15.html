                        Um about Charm sets, just reminding everyone that they aren't actually a thing in setting outside for like Sidereals and Alchemicals. They are abstractions, expressions of what they can do with their Essence. Whether they were changed from how it was in the First is a little bit irrelevant since it actually isnt a thing they would be aware of. Now I'm not saying that their powers didn't change, it most likely did. But let me ask why would it?

                        About them changing their Castes, you know from the sounds of I don't think their First Age Castes are any different from their post Ursurptation Castes. Going by the implications in the preview. It pretty much sounds like they created those Castes for themselves as well. From the looks of it, Lunars natural state is Casteless. So if that's the case why create Castes in the first place? That is because giving themselves Castes is giving themselves Roles they want to fulfill. All Lunars have a choice on what Castes they want, so it's a way of expressing exactly what they wish to do. During the First Age, they probably created Castes for themselves to nurture and protect the era, to be its guardians and stewards. That was their duty and the way they want about expressing and enforcing that was through their Castes. So what exactly happens when such duties is torn away from you and the world you knew was gone. What would these Castes mean now? If they were created to enforce a duty would it be even possible now if that duty is dead? Thus they changed, they took new Castes to give themselves new purpose and a way of accomplishing it. So did their Charms changed in a way? Maybe it did. Why? Because Charms is simply a way for an Exalted to express their powers under the lens of their Exaltation. They aren't literal. Their extrapolation of what you can do. If your First Age Caste was about being a teacher, you abilities would most likely revolved around that, would it be useful in the current situation where you were facing literally the whole world against you? No so you change, you take the mark of the Full Moon so you could fight down these oppressors and take about what was yours. Think less on what Castes can do, think on why they can do what they can do and why is that important on what they want do with those powers. Full Moons are warriors and warlords, Changing Moons are infiltrators and manipulators, No Moons are witches and tricksters. These Castes all have a firm idea of what the specific Lunar wants to do, that is why they were made. They abandoned the First Age for the same reason, under those they weren't able to do what they want to now. And that is one simple thing, fight back the Realm. Well that's how I think of it anyway.

                            I'm primarily going by that being an example that Holden once used, and secondarily from a notion that it's a role that kind of requires them to be welcome in polite society.

                            That contrast is also apparent in the way that the core book describes the old castes ("They were priests and courtiers, spies, observers, knives in the night. The Lunars shed those narrow definitions long ago, leaving them behind in the fallen ruin of the First Age."), and the new ones that are both broader and more focused, framed in terms of survival in a hostile world and using power to overcome enemies.

                            The Changing Moon Caste is most socially oriented, but still from a perspective that Lunars have to hide who they are, using wit and guile and magnetism to conceal and overwhelm and take what they please or need.

                            The powers are presumably not altogether very different, but the context in which they're used is, and I'd say that Lunar XP can help to make that feel like it matters. A First Age Lunar would grow as a person through a different set of priorities and achievements than a Changing Moon would, and the latter one is probably more optimal to the position that they're in.

                            Really, given the basic element of how all Editions portray Castes, I would imagine that one could run with a notion in Third Edition that the Lunars experienced their own form of them breaking, not because of being warped by continuous exposure to the Wyld, but because the circumstances in which one could effectively be tested or conduct themselves to settle into one of those Castes just didn't exist anymore.

                            Or perhaps were actively abandoned... wait, no, the idea that they stopped being able to settle into the old Castes doesn't really cover it, since you have First Age Lunars adopting the new Castes... no, lean more into the idea that the Lunars were abandoning and outright breaking their Castes as a mission statement. Your courtier Lunar isn't becoming whichever of the new three best suits them just from an extra form of power, but as a declaration of intent in the new world, a statement to themselves, one another and their new enemies that it's not going to be a clean war. They leave the old ways behind and embrace the new ones developed by the mystics, rougher, leaner, dangerous.

                                Originally posted by Iceblade44 View Post
                                Full Moons are warriors and warlords, Changing Moons are infiltrators and manipulators, No Moons are witches and tricksters.
                                You know, while it's popular and straightforward to describe the Lunar Castes in terms of these roles (past and present developers have gone with the same), and even if they're referenced in that preview*, I actually think the core takes the right approach with describing them less in terms of these specific roles and more in terms of the method and mindset in approaching the universal Lunar issues. This is because, unlike the other Celestial Castes, or even the Dragon Blooded Aspects, there's less space to differentiate them due to the smaller pool of Attributes, and with both the system of "two out of three Caste, two Favoured" and the Totem keyword, there's more room for crossover than ever.

                                Take warlords for instance; Charms for commanding troops belongs to one of the Attributes that is not covered by the Full Moon, I can't remember precisely which, but it means that Caste will also have equal claim to the image, while a Full Moon will be able to take them as Strength Charms if they've got the Totem of a pack hunter.

                                And as infiltration and sabotage go, well, there's at least one Strength Charm with which to psychically assault from a distance, being able to inflict terror and acquire shapes, so that also goes into the Full Moon wheelhouse by default (and it has a certain witchy element to it and all).

                                I think that's a thing worth emphasizing, both as a way of conveying that sense of Lunar diversity and really making the Castes feel rough and mean... crude, if that could be applied without being pejorative.

                                That and how much is going to need to fall under each Attribute's umbrella; it doesn't really make sense to play a Dawn Caste if you don't want to focus on at least one fighting Ability, and all of the Terrestrial Aspects have qualities of fighters/warriors, but you can probably legitimately make a Full Moon Caste who doesn't take any of their Caste Attributes Charms for fighting, and end up with somebody who bears a closer resemblance to a Solar's Zenith, Night or even Eclipse Castes.

                                                Witch derives from the Old English Wicca (m) and Wicce (f) meaning a spellcaster of some kind.

                                                They themselves are probably related to old Germanic words for divining, "divide sacredly" (probably something about divination via drawing lots), soothsaying, and possibly to a word for making mysterious gestures. One theory is it related to a Proto-Germanic word for "one who wakes the dead".
                                                Another theory that fell out of favour, but may be true, is that it relates to an Indo-Germanic word for "to know".
                                                There was also a theory that it was related to the word for willow, but that's discredited now.

                                                "Witch" (wiccan) was often used by early medieval English writers to translate Latin words for augurs and soothsayers. You can also see it in an English translation of a Scandinavian source that I think is quite interesting:
                                                Some men are so blind that they bring their offering to earth-fast stone and also to trees and to wellsprings, as the witches teach, and are unwilling to understand how stupidly they do or how that dead stone or that dumb tree might help them or give forth health when they themselves are never able to stir from their place.
                                                Not all, but the majority of early medieval usage of the word is in relation to soothsaying; specifically, the kind not approved of by the government and main religion.
                                                From a 10th century homily:
                                                "                                                Ne sceal se cristena befrinan tha fulan wiccan be his gesundfulnysse."
                                                (Christians should not consult Witches to find out their future)

                                                Of course, medieval people were into magic all the time, at all levels of society. As BrilliantRain said, this word is mostly used for people doing the "wrong kind" of magic. It's not the only kind: the laws of King Alfred banned the following kind of magics: "                                                gealdorcraeftigan and scinlaecan and wiccan". But that's the word we maintained.
                                                The other thing about medieval views of "good" and "bad" magic, is that it's often simply about whether it's a blessing or a curse. A monk or cunning man/woman might use magic to bless people; a witch would use it to curse people.

                                                So we've got associates with:
                                                sooth-saying,
                                                divination (via runes, lots, etc, not astrology),
                                                necromancy,
                                                healing and blessing through sacred spots in nature, trees, etc,
                                                curses,
                                                being considered "unclean magic" by the main governmental and religious structures.


                                                I think this gives quite a different feel to Lunar magic than Dragonblood magic.

                                                    I think that there's a definite narrative space for a synthesis of what both BrilliantRain and The Wizard of Oz have said. Notably, all of those magics that TWoZ lists as "unclean" are in fact things that Dragon-Blooded Sorcerers do. These things are permitted, but tightly-controlled - the Realm doesn't like necromancy because traffic with ghosts is the first step down the path of heretical ancestor-worship. Gods and demons are acknowledged, but dealings with them are forbidden to all but the Dragon-Blooded - ostensibly for the sake of mortals' physical and spiritual safety, but also because spirits can grant mortals power that the laws say they should not have. Divination is restricted because it's knowledge that the authorities can't censure or control.

                                                    These laws, these proscriptions against witchcraft, are all about the authorities' fear of the wrong people gaining any kind of power - the slave, the foreigner, the dissident. It is not merely that they seek power through forbidden avenues, but that any avenue that might grant them power is forbidden. The Lunar witch embodies the realization of these fears. They reject the "legitimate" constraints of society because they are acutely-aware that legitimacy has been socially-constructed around them as a prison.
