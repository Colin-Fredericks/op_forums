          There's a strong mythological association between animals and the Moon that's not quite reflected with the Sun, mostly because the sun has a strong association with humans; fire and light are associated with human civilization (cooking and forging), while darkness is associated with the wilds. Humans are diurnal, while many animals are nocturnal. The full moon drives people mad, robbing them of their human consciousness and reducing them to the level of beasts. Criminals act during the night, but criminals are generally considered subhuman.

          The problem with second edition Lunars is that they don't filter the association with animals through the mythological symbolism of the Moon. From what I gather, first edition Lunars did filter the association with animals through the mythological symbolism of the Moon, but completely failed at subtlety.

          What you want is a Lunar who presents a veneer of civilization to mask her beastial desires, much like the Moon reflects the Sun to provide a pale imitation of light. The Lunar slowly sheds the false light to reveal her true nature, just as the Moon reflects less and less light as it wanes, until the mask of civilization is completely gone and there remains only the hidden things in the dark, the beastial warform, and the hidden desires and vices that had thus far been suppressed. Once the Lunar's instincts are satisfied, she dons her disguise again and pretends everything was always perfectly normal. The Lunar does not shapeshift into animals; she shapeshifts into the darkness of the New Moon, symbolized by animals.

          Take, for example, Cersei Lannister, who acts like a perfectly regal queen most of the time, then turns around and fucks her brother when nobody's looking.

                                  A good way to avoid being a reskinned Solar is to figure out what their limitations are.

                                  To quote Holden on the difference between a Solar physician and a Chosen of Healing.
                                  Originally posted by Holden
                                  The Solar is not 'trying to be' anything. He is a physician-- since he's a Solar renowned for his medical acumen, let's say he's one of Creation's greatest physicians, in terms of skill. Because he's a Solar, that means he's going to be quite possibly the greatest doctor in the world, or certainly will stand among their ranks. That's what Solars are.

                                  Then let's say you have Argent Arrow, Chosen of Nala Ku, God of Healing and the Healer's Art. Let's say the Healer is an Ability-based Exalt. What this means is that he's going to be excellent in every aspect of healing within Medicine--as you'd expect! It also means that his Craft Charms are going to focus on mending what's broken, his Socialize Charms will focus on mending broken pacts and alliances, his Presence Charms will focus on restoring lapsed Intimacies or mending broken hearts, his Performance Charms will promote good health or the recollection of lost memories in those who hear them, his Resistance Charms will focus on rapid healing, his combat Charms may suck health out of enemies and give them to himself, or disarm an enemy's weapons the way a surgeon's scalpel might cut away infection, etc.

                                  The Solar's competence maxes out at "can probably cure any ill." So will the Healer, possibly, because you don't uh, really get any better than that? This doesn't say anything about his mote costs or prereqs, of course, because we don't know how strong of an Exalt he is.

                                                  Well the Moon was often a time telling piece, lunar calendars and so on so perhaps something to do with time and patterns, tides, and so on.

                                                  From Wikipedia we've got (going for some of the less common Gods as Artemis has been name dropped already) Ataegina associated with spring and the ideas of rebirth, Jarilo again with life-death-rebirth as a cycle - with Jar as a root for Spring or Summer. Arianrhod takes part in myth to place three curses/geas on a boy (her son via humiliation) preventing him from the three things required of a man (name, arms, wife) so you;ve got a link between moon and curses. Heading east we get Chang'e who bestows beauty on her worshippers, Anumati is all over 'granting permission' which could be stretched again to link with the geas of Arianrhod as forbidding certain actions meaning the Moon could be associated with rules and permission to act. Chandra is known as 'one who illuminates the night' or 'bright drop' so we've got a light source connection as well as dew, fertility, plants and vegetation. Ay Ata is anotgher link to cyclic rebirth and death but also witchcraft, magic, evil spirits and demons only being consorted with at night. Africa gives us Khonsu associated with youth, life and travels as well as the passage of time as well as other names meaning embracer, pathfinder and defender. Mesoptamia gives us Sin with wisdom and astrology, Nikkal is associated with fruit. Mayan Xbalanque is a hero with many tales of bravery who eventually becomes the moon - their tales are often filled with trickery and skill overcoming monsters.

                                                  So...plenty of options but common ones seem to be trickery, mystery, life-death-rebirth, time, travels, cycles, plants and fertility, cruses and magic.
