                Okay, time to finish off Intelligence.


                You know, prior to looking at it, I had been thinking about the matter of Lunar relation to Abilities and what it might mean for a category to be focused on a singular one. Thus, Many-Phase Insight is a very good one to start with, as well as being an interesting concept in its own right; being able to generate craft points with success in tasks related to learning and knowing things concerned with one’s Craft categories, or employing self-made tools for matters of medicine or navigation (as well as still giving the Attribute a nice hint of tattooing, with that being valid as an objective for points).

                Similar with Brilliance-Drinking Approach, that idea of harnessing inspiration from the blood of other crafters (possibly including the likes of beavers and spiders and such). Apart from the general picture of the nature of their power, I think it’s interesting to consider the context in which things like this would be applied; Lunars in circumstances where they don’t really have the resources or opportunities to keep up a continuous crafting practice, so you build towards important projects by means of survival and learning and hunting.

                Hmm, I knew there would still be sorcery, but… Demon-Drinking Fang, that was mentioned before and hasn’t been seen yet… maybe crafting won’t be very long. Faster!

                Protean Creator Discipline just covers a simplistic image of being a multi-faceted crafter by being able to double up on their Ability points for applying across numerous fields. And now that they’ve got all of this build-up for working on things, Wonder-Weaving Art starts to provide their actual capacity to craft, although it’s dependent on calling up the full scope of their Lunar intellect, with full Excellencies on craft rolls providing the additional benefit of double 9s, which at higher Essences can be improved to 8s or even 7s with craft point and Willpower surcharges.

                Pattern-Realizing Genius might turn out to be their closest equivalent to Craftsman Needs No Tools; rather, the craftsman definitely needs all of the tools, and raw materials, and the Lunar’s supreme insight into how they fit together allows them to complete a project with great rapidity (the image of somebody assembling things with whirring hands is one I like, but didn’t fully fit with Solar Charms, I think it works better here). Also nice to be able to get a little discount on the point cost for major projects.

                Beast-Slayer’s Art… is not one of the previewed Charms, just an earlier version of the concept; using the parts of a slain animal (or similar spirit) in a major project provides additional silver points towards the endeavour; I think the limit of one animal per project and one project per animal is appropriate for keeping the Lunar from making everything out of a dozen voles or arming a battle group with the remains of a yeddim. For Essence 2, Endless Ingenuity Approach lets a character reap gold and silver points from upholding Intimacies with the use of knowledge and cunning, nice bit of expansion from prior concepts and characterisation there (and I think the per-story reset condition of fulfilling objectives with at least a major project is very fitting). And Implicit Design Epiphany is one where I like the description of reaping insight from their own inherent multiple possibilities, and the basic mechanical function of improving craft point rewards (particularly how it can apply to Charms to get them as well).

                Inchoate Wonder Realized is the first directly for making Artifacts… I had been wondering how they might realise that idea of Lunars working effectively in constrained and limited circumstances, and the idea of being able to skip ahead a bit when something is being made or repaired to keep it going for a short period seems like an interesting way of managing that. You’re building the thing, you reach the limit of your means or patience, you just hold the rest together for a story with your Essence and go straight out with it. If you get the chance to finish it towards the end, great. If not, just keep it together for use and then put it aside until you can work on it again. Quenched in Legend can enhance that Charm towards furthering the Artifact’s development by providing gold points to complete it as rewards for various feats achieved using it, and awakening Evocations out of it, with a particularly significant bonus at the Charm’s end if an especially significant goal was accomplished using the device, so that’s another one for Charms shaping interesting stories.

                Ever Mutable Prowess provides further help, as the Lunar can meditate to convert their craft points upwards. And Shifting Skin Raiment… wonder what a gold point cost might be good for… ahhh, they get to create clothing and jewellery from animal parts and imbue it with the power to bestow mutations. Not as powerful as true Artifacts, but don’t have Essence costs or commitments, and at up to five points per item… maybe this would be the way to let a character give themselves functional wings. And for the Lunar who wants to share, treating the object as the basis for Moon-Marking somebody lets them invoke the transformation without cost. It is expensive, though, so probably only used sparingly.

                Silver Crucible Refinement helps towards the work by getting to spend craft points to add non-Charm dice to a roll, although I’d say it really comes into its own at Essence 3 when those points also get to count towards the crafting cost. Introducing Essence 3, Ecstatic Inspiration Satori provides even better craft point rewards when Defining Intimacies are upheld through mental prowess. And Manifest Miracle Forging allows a Lunar to act more rapidly in just about any magical crafting project (plus general large-scale crafting) besides First Age Artifice, making them intervals shorter and discounting the gold point costs.

                God-Skinning Hunter Art was previewed, make Artifacts from powerful creatures. Hmm, Inexplicable Lunar Wonders… it’s a curious kind of thing. Doesn’t cost any Essence, and the player gets to defer stating what the intended function and character of the Artifact (or manse) is until it’s actually completed… I was kind of anticipating it to be something that would allow one to hold that definition off until encountering a circumstance in which declaring what it was retroactively would be useful. I guess that the value of this is being able to work on a project without other characters (such as spying enemies) being able to tell what you’re working towards, and not needing to designate something that will be cut off forever if the project fails. It’s interesting, if nothing else.

                It skips straight over 4 and into ending on Essence 5 with Unbound Demiurge’s Dream… wait, wasn’t there a preview of a Charm to share Artifacts with one’s Solar mate… was that in a different Attribute? Hmm… {checking} ahh, it’s in Wits, it will literally be one of the last Charms. Okay, moving on, Lunar gets to spend white points to make a craft roll for a superior or legendary project not count towards the terminus (although in addition to the expense, the number of times it can be used on a project is… twice, really, for just about any playable Lunar). Fair enough it’s powerful, if not quite as sexy as the others.



                So I ultimately think that as much of Lunar crafting as is shown here strikes a nice balance between the particularly Lunar (emotions, multiple approaches, including animal parts, cunning) and some more general crafting enhancement (which still includes hints of their distinct nature). So what’s left…



                Ahh, of course, warfare Charms. Back when I was thinking straight, I had anticipated these, because of course, it’s one of the command Attributes. There clearly aren’t many of them, so I’ll come back in just a little bit.

                    Come on, quickly, warfare.


                    Predator’s Deadly Cunning lets them finally get to do something with strategic maneuvers, starting off with the basic adding dice and rerolling 1s, with pack hunter Lunars getting to take it as a Stamina Charm. Argent Pack Formation adds a success and ignores penalties depending on the battle group’s Might. Raiton Follows After adds non-Charm dice to Intelligence command rolls for every 10 initially rolled, as well as what is basically an Excellency when the order is to go for an enemy that is crashed. Silver General Foresight shakes it up a little with the image of employing the Attributes prophetic quality, by giving the attack rolls of a battle group following one of the Lunar’s strategems a bonus success, and allowing command rolls to reroll 1s.

                    Goes into Essence 2 with Dauntless Tactician’s Reversal allows one to exchange Initiative for dice on an allied battle group’s rout check, and provides a very brief bonus to Defence and soak for the group that successfully holds together. Triumph-Directing Insight allows a Lunar to transfer the bonus for crashing an enemy or reducing an enemy battle group’s Size from the group that got the key attack in to another one in medium range, in addition to even more Initiative if the crashed enemy was a battle group. Silver Lion Supremacy allows a general to add non-Charm dice to Strategic Maneuver rolls based on the Might of allied battle groups (which plays into the value of beastfolk troops), with a stunt that directly incorporates their supernatural abilities into the Maneuver turning that bonus into successes instead. And Quicksilver Strategy Meditation models the image of their flexibility and unpredictability by letting a character roll to benefit two Strategic Maneuver rolls simultaneously, dividing successes between the two, and being able to active both at once with enough successes.

                    Finally, for Essence 3, there’s Sparks-to-Fireflies Reversal: Lunar gets to make a Strategic Maneuver roll, that an enemy is allowed the opportunity to make an opposing roll against (needing to pay Willpower if their armies aren’t already in battle). If the Lunar wins, all enemy strategems are negated. Can only be done once per story, and reset by suffering a major combat setback that puts a Major or more Intimacy at risk.


                    The Intelligence warfare Charms help round out their capability in that area, but does come across as the least invested and valuable branch of that, or at least not the most interesting one; generally utilitarian. Makes enough sense, that the others might be forms of warfare aligning more closely with the Lunar Essence, and it’s good that they still don’t end up being bad Charms.


                    Okay, all that’s left is the brevity of sorcery.


                    Okay, Terrestrial Circle Sorcery requires four Charms in any Mental Attributes to learn. Probably a bit of a non-problem for No Moons, and for the rest… I don’t really mind the characterisation of sufficient investment into those mental enhancements being necessary as a substitute for proper occult knowledge. Cloaked in Moonfire… wow, being able to hold onto sorcerous motes while not shaping a spell just for having your anima glowing? That’s really good. They might just make No Moons premier combat sorcerers yet.

                    Essence 2 gives Shadow-Hands Invocation… they can flurry shaping sorcery with other actions? I mean, fair enough they can’t be attacks, but still. Getting to make it last for a scene just by committing, what, four motes, six motes? Wow, good for them. The image of having your detached shadow going around shaping the sorcery is cool and all.

                    Up to Essence 3 in order to initiate into Celestial Circle. It takes knowing five spells to learn, and honestly, I kind of feel that such a thing should be the case for Solars as well. I mean, I know it’s unlikely to have the second circle without having learned any of the first beyond initiation (and its automatic spell), but the possibility still grates a little.

                    And finally, an Essence 5 Charm in Cosmos-Rending Fury, for the real battle sorcerer. Winning Join Battle allows a Lunar to make a reflexive shape sorcery action with a free full Excellency; starting something with the wrong Lunar is just inviting a nasty spell to be blasted off at you.



                    So Intelligence has done very well. Very effectively realises a lot of the fluff attached to the No Moons in this Edition, expands on some old ideas and very heavily fills in old gaps, overall gives Intelligence a lot of interesting and useful things to do in comparison to an Edition in which it… mostly just gave some craft and combat stuff, and not even a lot. Speaking personally, I’m also very happy that it never went for anything like expanding brains. It did end up going to some very different places than some preliminary ideas I had in the past, but I’m currently thinking those might ultimately be better suited to Wits, maybe Perception.

                                    Hmm… okay, I can fit in one Martial Art before sleep.

                                    Centipede Style. Described as being all about speed in the form of rapid barrages of attacks and nimble footwork to get around enemies; even apart from the direct animal symbolism, I can see how this would be very complementary to a Dexterity focused Lunar. It’s all intended to build to a point where the worm down enemy is grappled, to either hit an opened weak point or poison them. Said to be similar enough to Snake Style that in the East, schools have a tendency to either form rivalries with the other style, or syncretize with them. The weapons are fighting chains, hook swords, the newly introduced iron boots, seven-section staves and tiger claws, it can be used in light armour, and benefits from skill in Athletics and Dodge.

                                    For the Charms, first is Blurring Scurry Prana, which adds dice to a movement action, improves Evasion if that movement is successful, and if used to enhance a rush that succeeded to bring the martial artist into close range with an enemy, gives an extra die to follow up attacks. Motion of Myriad Legs gets into being able to launch a multi-attack really early on, no less than three of them, which can ignore Hardness (although they are limited to a single opponent). It’s also limited in a distinct way by mandating a minimum of Initiative that must be dedicated to each attack, but in exchange gets the benefit of at least one attack connecting and hurting making an onslaught penalty last for an extra turn. Terrestrials get fewer attacks, and their divided Initiative is rounded down instead of up. I’d overall say that this one’s limitations make it reasonable for being such a low level.

                                    Chitinous Centipede Shell allows one to add the better of their Athletics or Dodge to soak, or use them as Hardness, which Mastery makes function for the length of a whole tick rather than against a single attack; I find it a neat picture of synthesizing the emphasis on speed with being able to ape the creature’s armour.

                                    The Centipede Form conveys the neat image of moving with a kind of speed to create afterimages giving the impression of dozens of limbs, giving a persistent +1 to Evasion, and allowing decisive attacks against enemies with an onslaught penalty (nicely building from Myriad Legs effect if the stylist fights alone) to get double 10s on the damage roll, or reroll 1s on grapple control rolls, as well as being able to flurry an attack and movement without penalty. It can be reflexively activated when attacking or grappling an enemy with a more severe onslaught penalty. I’d have to look back at prior Form Charms, but I vaguely feel as though those are often about setup, where this looks more about payoff, so I’ll be interested in seeing where the later Charms go.

                                    Hundred-Leg Embrace (which curiously has a health level cost) allows greater control of a grapple, getting a non-Charm die for every 10 made on the control roll, and forcing an opponent to subtract their 1s from successes. Ah right, the health level cost is optional, made only if they oppose an attacker’s control roll, to represent dislocating a grabbed limb and reposition to reflexively turn the grapple gambit back on the attacker, with bonus dice to attack and control roll based on their initial successes. I like the name for this, The Ninety-Nine More, and what it conveys about the Centipede approach to grappling; Terrestrials can’t use it.

                                    Tenacious Centipede Technique… going to put a pin for the moment in that lore, and concentrate on it being used as the basis for an effect of ignoring penalties of injuries and fatigue equivalent to Athletics for rolls for attack, grapple controls, movement and Evasion, for the length of a tick. The Charm is not limited to characters that have taken injury (or worthless for those who have other powers to relieve penalties), as they’ll instead get half their Athletics in extra dice for withering attacks and grapple controls; that feels to me like the kind of need that might have come up in playtesting. Terrestrials are limited by being unable to relieve penalties to their Evasion.

                                    And it finally brings the poison with Venom-Dripping Mandible, which just basically envenoms a decisive attack, although with the local benefit of being able to add an enemy’s onslaught penalty at the time to its duration, or expend rounds of control while conveying it through a grapple to also extend it.

                                    Agony-of-One-Hundred-Hells Technique looks particularly nasty, a decisive attack that makes an enemy add onslaught penalty to wound penalty, although given the limit to -4, it feels as though this might be an attack more intended to open a fight than end one. Although, it also allows a poison to discharge multiple rounds of its duration in one go, the number depending on onslaught or wound penalty (or, if grappling, a much higher total for expensing control rounds), so perhaps it can serve both functions. Not often in the same fight, though, with the per-scene use that requires raising an onslaught penalty very high with decisive attack… unless that earlier Charm can, if used repeatedly, keep rolling the onslaught penalty to the next round over and over again to keep building it up? That might make sense with some of the other effects.

                                    And last is Numberless Blows Technique, which serves as a permanent enhancement to Motion of Myriad Legs, allowing a large mote surcharge and anima payment to be made for that Charm, both adding one to the number of attacks that it can make and adding half of one’s Essence as damage dice to all of its attacks (which I think might greatly increase its capital as a multi-attack Charm). On top of that, two attacks successfully dealing damage makes the last of them unblockable, three makes it undodgeable as well, and four makes it function as an ambush. If the multi-attack takes the form of a grapple savaging, rounds of control can also be surrendered to add even more dice to the damage roll. Yeah, I’m pretty sure that this is the nastiest Charm for multiple attacks in the whole game… possibly a little bit too good. Sure, the motes are costly, but it doesn’t exactly rely upon something set up earlier in the style, I think the conditions for piling damage into each of the attacks are very generous, and you can even use it on more than one opponent in a scene. And jeez, Mastery allows Charms besides Excellencies to only be paid once to apply to all of the attacks? Sure the Solar is limited in what can be done with their own Charm, but anything they might fold in with other Martial Arts… even the Terrestrial limit of only being used on crashed enemies is fairly accommodating, given what the venom can do to an enemy’s Initiative, and if they can’t inflict ambushes (and still one less than others in how many attacks they get) they still have a shot on all the rest for some major attacks. I wouldn’t characterize myself as somebody with deep insight into mechanics, but I wonder if there’s something that I’m missing behind this, and if it possibly might need a bit of tweaking.


                                    In any event, I think that Centipede presents itself well as a Martial Arts Style; it's got an evocative flavour that it consistently realises mechanically, there's good internal synergy, I'd say that it holds together effectively. And it is nice to keep having Martial Arts focused a bit more on stylized methods of hitting people.
