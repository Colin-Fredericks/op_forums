    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    ​I'd say that depends a bit on how that story is framed; more emphasis on the idea that ancient reincarnated kings is something sinister and disruptive, in other words where the primary story is not being those kings, and it would be serviceable to put them later, if at all.

    But that's really the question for a different game with a different tone; perhaps not quite World of Darkness, but showing those roots a bit more.

    Hmm, now I want to check the Making Of book again and see if Grabowski said that the decision to refocus the game on the Solars came before or after the realisation that there would be several practical difficulties with effectively realising the idea of them as the strongest if they weren't also the yardstick.
    If I may offer my own perspective on why the Solars need to come first (assuming that we can't put more than one Exalt type out immediately): there's a few different reasons.

    1) Solars need to take a prominent role - The current age of Exalted is defined as one of history's major turning points. The old guard (the Realm) is starting to fade as all empires do, and are leaving a power vaccuum. The major change for this age is caused by not just the Solars but their immediate mirror opposites; the Abyssals. They are the great movers and shakers of this world. The Sidereals, DBs and Lunars are all to varying degrees trying to protect their status quo.

    Now, I'm not saying Exalted can't be a game where the DB are the main protagonists or even that it shouldn't (in fact it would actually fit better with east asia's culture given its emphasis on group power) but if you do do that how do you frame the Solars? If they're the big defining moment of the age, where does that leave the PCs? You would need to either remove the Solars and Abyssals (likely by setting the main "timeline" of the game BEFORE they begin to regain prominence). The Solars' return is meant to be a huge deal, something that no one can really ignore. But if you take them out of the game... well then you can just ignore them. :P

    "You could make their return sinister and make them the bad guys"
    Then what do you do with the Abyssals? The guys who are literally deathknights? It's hard to frame the Solars as the bad guys while having Literally Their Opposites running around as basically the same bad guy after a trip to Hot Topic. If you have both Solars and Abyssals running around as the bad guys, they quickly become redundant, since their power sets are so similar. It could create a very interesting scenario to have one set of bad guys be there to build things up (Solars) and the other to tear them down (Abyssals), to be sure, but you'd need to get deep into the campaign/setting to see that.

    And that's ignoring that the Anathema's nature is supposed to be a secret :P

    2) They fit new players better than DBs or Lunars. - This may be a bit of bias on my part, but I feel that when you have an established setting your players' characters should know about what the players themselves do. Whatever you throw new players into should be as undefined as possible, so they have little chance to mess with the setting too much. A Solar's backstory can be basically anything, and the memories they get from their first age incarnation happened so long ago that they might as well be hallucinations. If a Solar wants to be a farmer that's never even heard of the Realm, it's a bit of a stretch, but Creation is so vast you can do that.

    A Dragon-blooded, otoh, is a noble in a vast empire and has been training to be one ever since he/she exalted (around 10-12, iirc). There's only so much you can get away with this character getting wrong or not knowing. Even an outcaste (probably the best option for a new player who wants to go DB) is going to have issues. As such, you shouldn't be giving players DB characters until they're more familiar with the setting and system, or playing with other players who are. Granted, that has its own problems, but I feel throwing newbies into a fully-established imperial court creates bigger ones.

    3) Solar arcs mirror heroic arcs - Let's think for a second about how a typical solar's journey may go. He lives a normal life until it's not normal, then Exalts. For simplicity's sake let's say he's a Dawn and that we're looking at this story from his perspective. He now has an Evil Empire (again, from his perspective) on his heels wherever he goes, so he needs to maintain a low-ish profile. He's strong enough in a fight that he can handle himself, but not so strong that he can afford to be reckless/careless. He can have wise old mentors in the form of gold-faction Sidereals or Lunars. His presence will shape whatever area he's in to his desires as main characters usually do. His journey will be about making his own way in the world, achieving his desires and potentially even ruling the entire world.

    This is the hero's journey at its most basic, and the Solars present it in a way that's easy to understand.

    A Dragon-blooded, otoh, is a servant of the current status quo. Theirs is a story of noblesse oblige, of imperial majesty and dynasties. They're in the setting to try and restore the Realm to its former glory, and to unite the world under one banner. Also a cool story, but it's a very different sort of conflict, and this game is primarily about being a badass hero (see #4).

    4) What Aquillion said earlier about them being the strongest and thematics and blah blah blah.

    ...

    Of course none of that would matter if more than one exalted type were fully statted in the corebook. *twitch* I get that you can't throw 5 essence worth of charms for all characters into a single book but you could do it for E1 or E2 for two or three Exalt types (Solars, DBs and Lunars plz) and entirely avoid these problems!

    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post

    This is actually the problem my players have with Solars... the ones who take it seriously can't buy into the idea of Solars as returning god-kings.
    (Others enjoy it ironically, so it's fine)

    But yeah, Isator is right. Everyone wants certain splats in certain orders, because that's the ones that are useful to them. If that's different for different people, not much you can do about it.
    Since Solars have so much power that they can just become a king whenever they want and the players tend to assume that's the end goal anyway, it fits well enough. Sure, nobody today really believes in the concept of a "rightful ruler chosen by heaven" but it's a medieval style setting and the players probably want to be that powerful at some point anyway so why not.

                                                I meant they can't buy into the idea of Solars as returning god-kings because they think it's stupid. A)Who cares? What gives Solars the right to be kings, more than anyone else? It's not like Solar PCs are any wiser than any other PCs (I would say, overall, Lunar PCs are very slightly wiser than Solar PCs). And B)what makes Solars think they should be kings? It's not like someone (generally) comes and tells them all "You were made kings of Creation by the Unconquered Sun!"
                                                (Okay, admittedly the first Solar I played did tell the other PCs that... but in retrospect, there wasn't really any reason he should have known that.)
                                                And C)what about all the Solars who know nothing about ruling? Actually, I play a game where the Night Caste is a governor and prince. He's a master of stealth, wrestling, athletics and medicine... and has no idea how to run a city let alone a province. He mostly leaves it to his bureaucrats, and his underlings try and avoid letting him make any decisions because they know his decisions will be incredibly naive.
                                                Supernal Athletics and a ton of Stealth charms do not make you as good at running a province as a mortal bureaucrat, to be frank.

                                                Actually, the player who most hates Solars (and never plays them) is the one who considers being returning god-kings the most important part of Solars. The players who don't consider being returning god-kings to be an important theme of being a Solar like them much more.

                                                I mean, don't get me wrong, some Solar PCs do want to rule, but they don't consider it their right because they're Solars. For example, in my recent game, two Solar PCs gained political power. Princess (later Queen) Enhenduanna created a magical mountain kingdom and conquered surrounding regions through diplomacy or dragon-based invasion, and her justification was... she could. And also she was a Princess, so raised to believe she should rule. But none of that was because she was a Solar.
                                                Fulgens Cruciatus manouvred to become supreme war-leader of the Djala, and overthrew some local Djala tribal kings and replaced them with puppets, because he'd been appointed by his god to protect the Djala, and considered this to be the best way to protect them... and also, some Djala wouldn't admit his god was god of all Djala, so he had to beat them up until they did.

                                                So, extremely able, powerful people may well rise to power, but I haven't really seen (unironic) characters fight to be ruler and genuinely believe "I'm a Solar so I should be in charge." It's not like becoming a Solar comes with a golden plaque saying "The Unconquered Sun, King of the Gods, says you should be King of Creation."

                                                Now, I've seen plenty of DBs who think they should rule because they're DBs, but then, they've been raised their whole lives in a religion that preaches that.

                                                Although, as I said, I have seen people talk about the Solars' right to rule, but it's not something the player's invested in, it's something they consider... ironic? Like the way we play DBs and say "stupid savages, why are they complaing about being ruled by their moral betters, the Dragonblood? Now where are my hookers and blow." Similarly, Solars saying "everyone should obey us Solars, because we're so morally righteous and sent by the Gods, and we'll kill anyone who disagrees."


                                                But... I'm not really seeing how this applies to Lunars?
                                                I guess the thing about Lunars is that they don't actually (unlike some other splats) have any ideological belief in their own right to rule. I mean, they may take over societies because they need them to fight the Realm, they may take over societies out of greed or a sense of public duty, and they may take over societies so DBs can't, because f*ck those guys. But young Lunars are not told "as a Lunar, it's your right and duty to rule over mortals" the way DBs are (and some people seem to think Solars are).
                                                Which is perhaps why my players who dislike the returning god-king thing generally like Lunars.
