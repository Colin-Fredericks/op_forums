                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                My current model for the Solar Deliberative is something like a combination between the U.N., the European Union, the Roman government, and the Magisterium from His Dark Materials; a collection of colleges, councils, courts and committees, all functioning as international and intergovernmental organisations, under the overall administration of a council of those most politically minded Solars (technically all Solars are considered members of the Deliberative, and it derives the notion of authority over all the Realm from that conceit, but it's unusual for more than half of them to partake in it consistently, and only about a third of them have big agendas with it). Insofar as "the Realm" is essentially the concept of the dominion of humanity over Creation, the Deliberative endeavors to coordinate and centralise all of human civilization (in its various diverse states, nations, methods of government, and organisations) under the ultimate authority of the Solar Exalted as placed atop Meru.
                                I have a useful model that I arrived at after thinking about Exalted and how they would 'feel' for a long time, maybe you will like it.

                                Exaltation cannot be thought of exactly as a national identity or an occupation ; it implies a sort of judgement of character, an innate litmus test that the candidate has endured. Likewise, Exaltation happens, most often, to individuals who can be regarded by society as 'unique'... those who challenge the everyday, who are defined by unusual beliefs and unusual talents that make them stand out from everybody else.

                                So what does this produce? I put forward - These are people who feel very, very entitled. That's not really that difficult to understand, is it? Shit, if I was one of the 300 Solar Exalted, I'd feel pretty entitled too. You were -chosen-. That alone produces a big ego, a big personality that feels they are entitled to weigh in on whatever issue they wish.

                                Now, we get to the arguably even more relevant issue - the Exalted are not career politicians. No, in fact, they are nearly impossible to quantify by any specific means - are they soldiers? are they scientists? are they mystics? They are all of these things and none. Each Exalted has a story. Most of these stories are pretty epic and complicated, involving unique challenges they met and overcame that defined them in fundamental ways. These guys aren't Bob Dole and Bill Clinton. They're Indy, Batman, and Luke. So we're talking /even more/ ego, /even more/ personality.

                                So, how does this alter the character of Deliberative as a government body?

                                I picture Deliberative as being -really- over the top and extreme. Absolute political theater. Madness. Some Deliberative sessions must have been so intense that people literally were like 'wtf is going on' -- when you get these insanely unique people, tell them they are chosen by god and entitled to be the way they are, give them immense power with virtually no checks and balances? You get some blowhards. You get some really, really extreme folks that constantly push reasonable discussion into chaos. You get people that just don't want to play at all, like K'tula over there.

                                On good days, the Deliberative saves the world. On bad days, Creation suffers as the godlings quarrel. Very, very rarely, duels are fought and people die, but mostly the only costs are mortal lives waged on gambits of pride and principle.

                                But on most days? The chamber roars, the endless drama spins on, and the Exalted accomplish far, far less than they should.

                                Who would have thought that these living superweapons, chosen by this specific criteria, might not be particularly adept at cooperative governance?

                                One thing I can say though? Is that with these kinda people at the helm, I doubt it looks very much like any other government chamber. Real politicians would run from that room screaming.

                                In the REAL WORLD, they make sure that Barabbus the Devourer of Slaves is never let into the upper echelons of power. That guy was a crazy ass canibbal warlord thing that was famous for killing a tyrant lizard by biting it to death! He is totally unqualified to vote on taxes! In a million little ways, Barabbus is excluded from the White House, which honestly in the opinion of lotsa folks (perhaps even Barabbus himself) might be better for everybody!

                                ... in Deliberative, Barabbus gets a chance to stand up and bellow out his feelings on any matter he chooses, even those which he is only tangentially informed about. In fact, he might feel he is -obligated- to offer his opinion. After all, he was chosen!

                                    Saur Ops gives some good reasoning for why one shouldn't presume every Caste will have a physical Attribute. It's also worth noting that the thematic archetypes the Lunar Castes are meant to cover are Warrior, Trickster, and Shaman. There's no telling if other splats will work this way (Sidereals and Alchemicals having set Caste Abilities/Attributes could work thematically), but if Lunars do, we may be looking at 4-5 Attributes per Caste, from which a Lunar selects 3 or however many, plus the Favored(s?).

                                        This post is kind of long, rambling, and probably not of much interest to anybody besides myself, so I'm just going to tuck it all in here for anybody who wants to see it, and keep it from troubling anybody else.


                                        Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
                                        In the REAL WORLD, they make sure that Barabbus the Devourer of Slaves is never let into the upper echelons of power. That guy was a crazy ass canibbal warlord thing that was famous for killing a tyrant lizard by biting it to death! He is totally unqualified to vote on taxes! In a million little ways, Barabbus is excluded from the White House, which honestly in the opinion of lotsa folks (perhaps even Barabbus himself) might be better for everybody!

                                        ... in Deliberative, Barabbus gets a chance to stand up and bellow out his feelings on any matter he chooses, even those which he is only tangentially informed about. In fact, he might feel he is -obligated- to offer his opinion. After all, he was chosen!
                                        See, this is the kind of model which is why I think the idea of a Deliberative in which all the Solars take part lacks credibility.

                                        Excepting my idea for what was becoming of it in the final centuries of the First Age, where the centralisation efforts of the Hierophant had largely borne fruit and the Solars were at the height of their madness and excess.

                                        On that note, I do like your imagery as the way that final phase of the Deliberative might work.

                                        But still, with my sense of a lack of credibility, my ideas for earlier (and somewhat more functional) forms of the Deliberative kind of amount to a kind of forum where Exalts of different fields and interests come together, discuss ideas as to how Creation should be with the expertise of each informing the rest, and making decisions about what to come away with in contributions with which to pursue their different fields.

                                        So it's not so much the Engineer Solar taking part in administration and legislation as it is going to talk to Legislator Solar(s) about what kinds of engineering they think should be engaged in and encouraged for the sake of the common good/the agendas of the Solars with the greatest clout.

                                        In my picture, a semi-formal forum was all the Deliberative was for quite a while (and that forum itself arose from how so many Solars were drawn into the sphere of Merela by her own leveraging of a few privileges she'd managed to wrangle out of the extremely vague "Queen of Creation" title she'd been granted, most notably greater requisition authority over the collective Sidereals than anybody else); it only truly became the Deliberative after some crisis (possibly the Niobraran War) brought every single Solar into the system for a while. They didn't all stay part of it for long after the crisis was resolved, but it provided the resources to convert informal coordination of various groups into true international organisations with a direct administrative hierarchy under a collective of Solars.

                                        I'm also basing some of my ideas for how the Realm was organised on the Southeast Asian Mandala model, with Meru as the central point that the various other powers and polities revolve around.



                                        On a slightly related note:
                                        Originally posted by Miss Maddy
                                        Exaltation cannot be thought of exactly as a national identity or an occupation
                                        It's probably not an accurate way to surmise Exaltation (or rather, certain kinds of Exaltation, since the Sidereals are absolutely an occupation and the Dragon Blooded have some pretty firm foundation for something like a national identity), but I can certainly see it as an idea people would form and push for the sake of certain ideologies.

                                        Certainly, in my abridged history of the First Age, something like the ideas that the Solar Exalted should be united by their collective relationship to their patron god, and that this conferred certain responsibilities and obligations, was a part of several of the groups that the Solar who became the Hierophant unified into the Church of the Unconquered Sun, and one that he placed significant emphasis on in its creed (motivated by a desire for Solars to rise above provincial concerns that he saw as a basis for a lot of conflicts in the Realm), and the length of the spiritual hegemony of his Church meant that a lot of Solars Exalted having grown up with such ideas and a lot of others who weren't inclined to personally believe them still played into a bit of the ceremony for the sake of broadening their power.

                                        He intended this as a way to confer the standards usually attached to Solar Exalted vis a vis the rise and fall of ideas and nations and systems to the whole of the Realm, univeralised, broadened to the extent of every Solar's supreme capability, and permanently held up by the Solars collectively so that there would never be a thing that would fail for lack of Solars to champion it. Unfortunately, having brought such an idea to the fore at the time when the Great Curse had become its most extreme, the Solars wound up holding themselves up as a kind of irreproachable reigning caste with the power of each secured by the reinforcement provided by the all; the idea that the Realm would be unquestionably better if the Solars oriented themselves as a collective had become very pervasive in the world's culture, so it became a very powerful rhetorical point by which any given Solar could demand or justify basically anything.

                                        The Hierophant's Curse was in the hubris that thought he could control the Solar Exalted in such a manner, and the depths of his despair when he realised how wrong it had all gone.

                                        This also provided a context for stuff that happened with other Exalted, since Solars expressing these ideas also sought to impose a kind of caste system identity on the rest of the Exalted as well. It created a context in which the Terrestrial dissatisfaction with what the world was turning into was expressed via building up their own interconnected identity over their individual root cultures (not just distinct Gentes, but also diverse cultural groups they had disseminated through by intermarriage, an action widely encouraged by the Deliberative), as well as part of why Lunars were widely viewed as a more homogenous group oriented directly below the Solars (which was itself the idea that the more vengefully minded Lunars sought to organise around when disaster came).

                                        This was the situation the Sidereals were faced with when they had to figure out how to implement their Vision. It was very distressing.

                                        Ironically, the Solars being so collective as they were was exactly why the sudden removal of all of them led to such a catastrophic collapse as it did, greater than anything that could have come before. If the Hierophant could have anticipated that binding the security of everything to the Solars collectively would mean that the loss of that collective would take all security with it, he still would have been untroubled by the idea that it could actually happen.


                                        So... there, that's a whole bunch of ideas I've come up with for what the First Age could have been. Please forgive my extended digression.
