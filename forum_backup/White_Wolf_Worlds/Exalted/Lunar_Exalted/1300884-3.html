        Hmm, that's a good point. In the spirit of interrogating conventional wisdom concerning the book, I'm starting to wonder if that point, to the extent that it's true, has any greater significance than beta components such as the Terrestrials coming first or ideas for all of the types of Exalted to just be different skins.

        It makes me wonder if somebody took an original statement about an early iteration of the Lunars, framed it out of context, mixed it in with an initial hostile reaction to its source book, and then became echoed over following years into a go to narrative for why Lunars "don't have a place in the setting", even though that's not exactly true.

                    EDIT; I'll actually give a less pithy answer to this.

                    Regardless of the canonacity of something within the actual game, Exalted is a game that I'd argue doesn't just support but rather encourages a 'Lost History' narrative. That there are things that didn't get written down or elwere even just lost is a major part of Exalted worldbuildong, from splats to artifacts.

                    These pre 1E outlines offer these same ideas; things lost to history that people may have forgotten about that Storytellers may pull from to introduce shocking twists that reference these 'lost histories'. I think in a game like Exalted, which so heavily presents history written by the winners and isn't afraid of telling you to change what you don't like or surprise your players in the cosmology, these 1E or pre-1E scraps can be more important then they might look.

                    After all, we're talking about a game line where one of the major Antagonist splats for a group of main Exalts are forgotten Exalts left out of History or something like that, and 3 optionally canon Exalts are inspiring a lot of interest from players.

                    More then 1 ST I've played with has used Orange for some purpose for Sidereals. One used it for an unused Caste, another had orange tinted Starmetal as something obtained from the Underworld, making those with it suspected of Deathlord ties.

                    I've also had a pre-Exalt Sidereal who used techniques as a representation of the fact he was a savant who could mimic martial arts just by looking at them

                            Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
                            And, really, if the point of the book is to make the reader want to play a Lunar, having the mouthpiece character be the guy who just pillaged your village, raped your sister, and only saw you as a person because you suddenly Exalted.... that seems like it would leave a bad taste in most reader’s mouths, start the chapter in a deep hole it’s going to need to climb out of, and, even if it does manage to go somewhere nice, the fact that it’s “in character” by the least trustworthy narrator possible, makes it basically useless.

                            Which is an important point to make, I think. The framing of a section can be just as important as the content and, if removing a few paragraphs makes a section inarguably better, that should probably be done.

                            And...I guess part of me gets it? That gives you a “first hand” look at the whole Barbarian mindset and helps cement in your mind what these people are supposed to be like. Except... it feels like it saying “these people are awful and why would you want to play as them” which ...is not helpful.
                            I know I'm simplifying your argument a little here, but in an unalloyed sense, I think it a difficult argument - that Lunar NPCs generally should be admirable and serve as role models for the sort of characters players want to be - in that Uka is far from the worst of the Lunar viewpoint characters you're portraying in the Lunars. (Raksi, etc.).

                            There's certainly an argument that sexual violence should be avoided in this sort of fiction portraying any character who is presenting an argument (because it's just too immediately discrediting and repellent for most of their audience). But I would broadly support the sense of the piece (as I remember it, without rereading) to introduce to players that the Silver Pact has many morally compromised, immoral, conflicted and monstrous figures, up to and including your mentor as a young Lunar, and that to a degree this is fundamentally avoidable, is a good motivation to stand behind the piece.

                            Maybe you balance that out elsewhere with portrayal of Lunars elsewhere in the fiction, and make it clear when described mentors that, in actual play, your mentor doesn't have to be like Uka if you don't want him to, but that the Silver Pact does contain such figures.

                            (If anything I think there's a good argument that Sidereals and Dragonblooded could've stood to emphasis this dynamic more within their fiction. I can't really think of much of a degree to which Sidereal Elders ever cropped up who seemed like non-admirable people, rather than good people driven to morally evil ends by a particular stance about what was necessary.)

                                                    So, couple of things.

                                                    First, the originally-contracted Lunar mechanics writer had to bow out late due to illness, and so the Charms and shapeshifting mechanics and such were written on a very short timeline. Keep in mind this was during a period when Diamond Distribution controlled RPG distro with an iron fist and would retaliate against companies that didn't make their announced release dates, because Diamond also published the big lists retailers used for stocking, and didn't like to deal with blowback from retailers complaining that Diamond's own catalogues were unreliable.

                                                    (This once lead to a hilarious situation where a couple of White Wolf employees used their own cars to barricade a UPS truck in the WW office parking lot, to give them them an extra necessary hour or so to finish packaging stock that had come late from the printers, because otherwise the truck would leave without it and then the book would be a month late and they'd be on Diamond's shit list.)

                                                    Second, the tonal dissonance makes a lot more sense if you assume that GCG wanted to make a book about mighty Conan proud barbarian guys crushing weak city-dwellers beneath their feet and fighting degenerate mutants in the overgrown ruins of a First Age shopping mall, and most of the writers wanted to write anthropology studies, and the difficulty of developing so much mechanical material from a writer who was pulled in late was taking up most of GCG's time and preventing him from reconciling his creative goals with the material his writers were delivering to him.
