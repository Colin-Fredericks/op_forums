    Anyone else started a Lunars game? Or a game with lots of Lunars?

    03-31-2019, 02:07 PM
    Now the book is fully out, I'm just interested.
    What's your story like;
    how have you found it different than running a mainly-Solars game;
    what have you done with Shahan-yas;
    what kind of characters have you got;
    etc?
    What do you think of the Role bonuses for Lunar XP? I'm a little worried about characters who are obviously going to be one Caste, but aren't yet tattooed, getting less Role XP. Not a big worry, but a little one.

    I've run one session so far of my game Heroes of the Frozen North.

    We've got:
    Olaf Keelbreaker, the Steel Iceberg - Full Moon with a Walrus totem and a grimscythe made of his own tusk;
    Amira, a beautiful merchant's daughter who lies at the heart of the social web - Changing Moon spider-totem
    Atal, a shaman from the swamp with Charisma 1 who just wants to make friends - No Moon with a toad-totem
    Mujin, an exotic foreign merchant (apparently) - Casteless with a polecat-totem
    Underfoot, a feral man from the jungle - Casteless with a gorilla-totem
    There's also a red panda who is most definitely not a Changing Moon-caste Lunar, and able to say so with Glance-Oration Technique.

    So, to my surprise, I've ended up with one combat Lunar, one all-rounder, one shaman, and three social characters. How have other players/ST found it? One of the players wondered if people play Lunars as social characters more (in our mixed Celestial games, social Lunars are fairly common), but I think it's more just coincidence.

    I brought them together with a couple of NPCs for a moot, but I didn't really give anyone a Shahan-Ya. I would've if anyone showed interest; but they didn't. No-one took Mentor. Or Ally 5 for a Solar Mate either, actually.

    The moot was a party at the castle of King Arcturus, a Changing Moon who'd united warring clans, which I think worked well with all the social characters, but maybe wouldn't if I'd had a bunch of smelly barbarians.

    I also have to say that the character gen system seemed to work better than for Solars; everyone seems quite balanced, there's no-one with a ton of Abilities at 5 or Willpower 10 or anything. Though that might be the maturity of my players compared to when old games started years ago.

                      Possibly it’s a bit skewed by the chargen rules for Lunars.

                      For Solars, it’s not really feasible to take 15 non-caste/favored charms at chargen, so that you get every Excellency and, even if you did, you probably wouldn’t have particularly good pools everywhere.

                      For Lunars, you have 4 caste/favored abilities where you’re almost certainly getting an Excellency for free already. For the other five Attributes, it’s difficult to not have enough dots in them to pick up two charms each for most of them. And, since you can boost your excellency cap by 5, because you have at least one Attribute at 5, it means that there’s a real incentive to start with, like, six or more unfavored charms.

                      I mean, my Lunar has 7 of 9 Excellencies. But I’m planning to focus mainly on Int and App charms in the future.

                          So I'm currently running a primarily Lunar game for my gaming group as part of our rotation of games. (We have something like 4 or 5 games in rotation at the moment.) I say primarily Lunar because one of our members hates making characters and wasn't especially jazzed for Lunars, but the rest of the group was chomping at the bit for them. So as a compromise we let her bring back a Solar character from a previous campaign. We've only got about 1 session under our belt at the moment.

                          Our cast of characters is:
                          Hardack, the Vegetarian Behemoth. A proud and intimidating Ox-Dragon Totem Full Moon who stands tall even as a mouse and beats enemies into submission with his wide array of animal forms.
                          T'elm, the quote "Monstrous Blue Mage." This Casteless Polar Bear Totem spends most his time as a bear devouring people and monsters and taking their power for himself. He is close friends with Hardack.
                          Oct, an Octopus Folk Ninja and Historian. An Octopus Totem No Moon who collects and spreads knowledge while commanding an army of beastfolk ninja from his homeland the village hidden in the waves.
                          Meurta, the apprentice of the Ghost King. Meurta is a Twilight caste sorcerer* who the rest of the Circle is convinced is an Abyssal due to her obsession with ghosts and apprenticeship to a mysterious ancient ghost that sounds a lot like a Deathlord.
                          And finally Nafulla the beautiful seductress and martial artist. This Spider Totem Changing Moon is a consummate manipulator who has infiltrated the Bureau of Destiny and plays them and the Silver Pact against each other for her own ends. Her only true allegiance being to her Shahan-Ya's ideology.

                          Our game is set in the Southwest, a fair ways north of the Cinder Isles but till south of An-Teng. And our first little story hook has been them dealing with some "advance scouts" from the army of Spear-Empress Bhadri. Which they got involved with initially because these advance scouts were devouring all of the spirits in the areas they passed through, including Ghosts. And this right riled up Meurta, and as Nafulla's Shahan-Ya had requested that the rest of the Circle keep an eye on the "Abyssal" operating in the area they got brought along for the ride. They however became personally invested once they noticed the atrocities the Fae were committing to humans as well. I have plans to work in Solar Mates for a few of the characters who wanted them, but they have not shown up yet and the players established they have not yet met their Solar Mates. Though a couple of them had some very specific notes on what they wanted.

                          As for how I've found this different from a solar game. It's a bit early to say. There was a scene where a couple of the group split of to hunt forms specific to the needs of the situation. (Large forms to carry the children they saved to safety.) Also the party seems just a lot more anti-Realm than our previous Solar games. Where they always found some way of at least trying to work with portions of the Realm in a positive manor. This however hasn't been a huge point yet, as they haven't yet interacted with the Realm in game. But it will come up. I think the biggest thing I've noticed is how all of the Lunar characters really tie back into some aspect of the Lunar themes. T'elm is perhaps the best example, or at least easiest to quantify. T'elm is just straight up a monster, he would tell you so himself. But he's a monster that aids people in need and becomes enraged by sadistic cruelty. He really captures a uniquely Lunar feeling of the Monster red in tooth and claw who some might still view as a protector or savior.


                          *Note Meurta would have been a Necromancer if there were rules for it. But there are not, so instead she just is a sorcerer with a custom initiation and a custom Ghost Summoning spell.
