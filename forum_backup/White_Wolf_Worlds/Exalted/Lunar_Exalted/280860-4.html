    From what I know about Lunars, I would say that it's their endless adaptability and indomitably that appeal to me the most - in theory, I have no idea how it's worked out in practice.                         Well, I posted in the other one on Dragonbloods, so I might as well do a similar list here. Reasons I love Lunars:

                        1. You have a closer relationship to your deity than anybody else. Luna shows up, in person, at your Exaltation. You've MET your patron, which most other Exalts can't actually say, and which really helps give you a sense of the sacred to justify whatever your goals are.

                        2. Oh, man, do I love shapeshifting. Turning into a bird to scout out an enemy encampment from above, turning into a fish to travel downriver, turning into a mouse to sneak into a bank vault or a mammoth to charge an enemy army, it's just really satisfying to me to use forms to accomplish things I can't as a human. I love flying and swimming and burrowing best: new forms of locomotion delight me!

                        3. Hunting new shapes. When the ST describes some terrifying monstrosity, other PCs are like, "crap, how do we kill it." Lunar PCs get to go "I wanna EAT IT and TAKE ITS SKIN!" Perhaps it just appeals to the part of me that loves Pokemon games, but eating a new creature and gaining a new form is WAY more exciting and satisfying to me than uncovering a cool new artifact or learning a new spell. It's so much fun!

                        4. Disguise! I enjoy doing the play-within-a-play thing and pretending to be a character who is herself pretending to be a character. Disguise makes for fun roleplaying, and the majority of Lunars engage in it at one time or another. You're always hiding from the Wyld Hunt by pretending to be a peasant in the Realm or sneaking into fancy parties by looking like the host's daughter or assassinating gods by posing as their virgin sacrifice or seducing Guild Factors in the form of their favorite concubine or pretending to be the family dog to get close the kid you think is getting abused...every Lunar has disguise built right into them, and since I love disguises I love Lunars.

                        5. Talking to animals. No, seriously. Dog-tongue method is like, my favorite Lunar charm. Nothing makes the setting feel as wonderfully mythic as getting advice by talking to animals. Gods are neat and all, but getting relationship advice from a fox or asking a squid if there's any sunken ships in the area is really fun for me! I love it even more when I'm STing, because I love running talking animals. They're not people-smart, and their personalities are just a little alien (or sometimes very alien, for weirder creatures), so I love running them as NPCs for the same reason I love running demons: it's fun to get into an unfamiliar mindset. Animal NPCs are often beloved by PCs and can quickly become Familiars or recurring friendlies, and I enjoy the Lunars-befriending-animals thing that tends to happen in any game where a Lunar talks to critters.

                        6. Ruling in Wyld Places. I love that Lunars are at home in the wild places of Creation, because those places are gorgeous and full of fun adventures! Deep black forests, steaming jungles, towering waterfalls, echoing mountain caverns, the crushing depths of the ocean, the canopy of a mile-high tree, the burning sands of the South, the ice-floes and floating islands of the North...Lunars can easily get to and thrive in these places, and in my experience they tend to get used more often when there's a Lunar in the party. The edges of Creation are where the weird cool magic stuff happens, and I love floating palaces and rivers that run up into the sky and caverns made of tree roots and stuff. I love setting up homes there and building communities that survive in hostile or alien climes. I'm not a huge fan of the Silver Pact, but Lunars are superlatively talented magical humans, and it makes sense for them to desire human companionship and end up ruling groups of humans around them, and I love designing the strange societies Lunars can found and protect on the mad edges of the world.

                        7. The Solar Mate Thing. I'm not a fan of its 2E mechanical implementation, but I love the plotlines enabled by the idea of a Fated Soulmate. Love-that-transcends-lifetimes, eternal brofists, rivalry, undying hatred spanning the centuries, fear of a long-dead abuser, being twins and exalting at the same time literally EVERY time for two thousand years, taking turns killing each other since the First Age...so many fun plots come out of the Fated Soulmate thing. All Celestial Exalts get to play with the Past Lives shenanigans, but it's always been a big part of my Lunar games. The returning Solars are a big deal to everyone else because of their power, but to Lunars they're a big deal less because of their huge historic impact on world power centers and more because, dammit, my old flame is back in town and I'm not sure if we're going to make up or kill each other. The best stories are personal.

                        8. The Wyld Hunt: Solars and Lunars both get to deal with the Hunt...but the Hunt has predominantly been about hunting and killing newly exalted Lunars for centuries, and there's bad blood there. I love Lunar PCs who have recurring nightmares about getting murdered by the Hunt over and over and over in previous lives in different ways. I love Lunar stories where a group of PCs races to get to a newly exalted Lunar before the Hunt finds him. I love Lunar stories where the Wyld Hunt becomes the Hunted, and the Lunars slowly while away a Hunt too strong for them to directly overpower through subterfuge and guile. I love games of cat-and-mouse-and-Tyrant Lizard. I love playing a Lunar PC who pretends to be an Immaculate Monk and joins a Wyld Hunt to get their help finding his Solar mate or a monster they both want to take out. I love ambushing the Hunt with an army of barbarians, or getting ambushed myself. Solars can do all this...but again, the best stories are personal, and Lunars tend to have a more personal relationship with the ones hunting them.

                        9. Threatening to eat people. Seriously, everybody can be threatening, but if you're a Lunar and a local god is giving you guff, you can just politely mention that you love their new hairstyle, they're looking positively delicious today, and everybody involved knows you're pointing out that killing and eating him would be both doable and beneficial for you. It's small and it's silly, but most other Exalt types just don't get to threaten to eat people! Threatening to kill people works just fine, but there's something special about eating somebody, y'know?

                        10. Carrying other party members. My games are usually mixed games, and I love telling people to climb on my shoulders then dashing off as a mammoth or swooping down and picking up another PC as a giant eagle. Carrying other PCs is really satisfying for me!

                        So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                        One of my favorite things about the Lunars is the thematic purpose behind them, as can be seen by the personalities of their Patron...this is slightly inspired by a post I saw on an alternate interpretation of the Solar/Lunar Bond I have read.

                                        Solars: TUS is the absolute master of the direct approach. He doesn't really have to plan ahead, because he is just so freaking good that he curb stomps threats the moment they show up. His Chosen were built along the same philosophy. Masters of the direct approach. They attack, and it is like 'I punch you into the stratosphere.' They defend, and their response is basically 'lolno.' They do social combat, and it's functional mind-control. Heck, even stealth is generally 'I'm not actually hiding, I just edited myself out of your perception.' They solve all of their problems through raw, unbridled power. (The Infernals and Abyssals function in much the same way, as they are built off the same template)

                                        Sidereals: The Maidens cheat. That's the simple truth of dealing with them. The know what you are going to do before you do it, and have planned accordingly. For example, in the aftermath of the Primordial War, when the Underworld cropped up, isn't it so convenient that the Siddies already had charms for dealing with ghosts? Which had never existed before? Again, their Chosen are built along the same design...they cheat. A Siddie will tweak probability and causality to screw you over...and can generally be completely prepared for an opponent when they go up against them, and can always cheat their way out of a fight if they need to.

                                        But Luna...Luna is different. Conky and the Maidens were created to be exactly what they were (though who made the Maidens is still up for debate), Luna got there by fighting and clawing and murdering her way to the top of a pile of dead Othermoons, which he then ate. Discounting the Primordials (due to Geas), there have been extremely few instances that TUS couldn't just steamroll his opponents. Things aren't hard for him...he just does stuff, and it works. The Maidens cheat, they don't play fair. And they don't tend to get involved in fights that require them to play fair. Luna isn't Unconquerable, he doesn't have cheat codes to reality, so she does things the hard way. She can't just curb stomp all comers, she can't see threats before they arrive...so he has to be prepared for anything...has to adapt, has to change. As with the others, this carried over to the Lunars...they are the masters of asymmetrical warfare and adapting on the fly.

                                        That's why I like the Lunars. They, like Luna, do what needs to be done, because no one else can. If there's an enemy that can just be stomped, just call in a Solar, that's their specialty. If a threat can be foreseen, no need to call the Siddies, they already know and are working on it. But for that enemy that the Siddies don't see coming...that can cheap-shot a Solar...The Lunars will rip them to shreds...because Lunars, by their nature, are built to adapt to any threat. A great example of how this can work is in the Ink Monkey's bit on the Fair Folk...specifically "Prince Laashe, the Morning Star." Laashe figures out kryptonite against Conky, and is outside fate so the Maidens don't see his plan coming. Laashe freaking wins. He is just about to take down TUS permanently, and make off with the godspear...when Luna cheap-shots him right out of existence.

                                        (Note: I don't think they were mechanically handled will in 2E, here's hoping that changes to bring them more in line with what I see)

                                        (Also, mixing gender pronouns when writing about Luna is fun!)

                                            I've been thinking about ways that Holden's comment on rough Attribute magic compared to polished Ability magic might have some substance.

                                            On the face of it, I think I can gather something from previewed Solar Charms and Second Edition Alchemical Charms.

                                            The preview Solar Charms do seem to have a certain sense of being complex, or at least comprehensive; when they're not ones that explicitly do multiple things, they look like stuff that kind of gets into a certain process and fleshes out all the little widgets in it. They feel like rather procedural things, which I think lends itself to the characteristics the developers say they want to have Solar Charms to have. I can easily imagine that Terrestrial Charms will be similar, even if what they do is oriented towards a different motif and the actual effects might be generally weaker.

                                            When I think of Alchemical Charms, I get the general sense of them as being not so much things they can do as additions that have been bolted onto them that themselves do things. I think Alchemical Charms might tend to be fairly straightforward in that; they cover singular, straightforward effects (even if these can continually branch out in sub-modules), but this is aided by how they can get large, flexible suites of Charms, and how they can effectively dodge the concept of prerequisites while still referencing it.

                                            I imagine something broadly similar might go for Lunars. They won't get a Charm like Subject-Hailing Ideology, where it's fairly intricate in terms of how and why it affects people, and can potentially cover a wide range of situations, instead maybe something like a pheromone Charm that goes straight into brute-forcing certain kinds of Intimacy. But it could come from an Attribute that can cover a diverse number of Charms, and may itself be part of a cascade that snakes its way through several concepts, in a manner that covers them in a way distinct from the Alchemicals with their large number of Favoured Attributes and their Augmentations to boost stats and their cascades that pass through several different Attributes.

                                            Anyway, stuff that fells like having or using the Charm is a transformation to enable a straightforward effect in a manner similar to modular Alchemical additions.

                                            Also gives me a notion of how the shift in Lunar nature over time might be conveyed; the idea that having different needs means developing different Charms, which itself prompts a shift in one's outlook and even one's form.

                                                I feel that a lot of the things that I love about Lunars has be already said: shapeshifting, animal and moon motifs, the mutative and wyld-manipulation effects, the broad scope of their charms. For me, the biggest reason of them is that they are instigators, agents of change, challengers of the status quo and guardians against stagnancy. Any exalt can cause change, but I feel Lunars have an intimate connection to it; they personify change. Their innate ability to infiltrate, to adapt, to alter what is and what isn't, and to deceive and inspire. The Moon's Chosen tap into chaos and apply it to Creation like a painter to a canvas.

                                                The mechanics haven't always reflected this, but I feel the key tenet of the Lunar is to "be the change". Don't let it be merely a byproduct of your actions, but to grok it. I hope we see more of this in future previews of the Lunars and the upcoming edition.

                                                        I actually liked Lunars years before I ever played my first game of Exalted. My friends described all the types of exalt to me, Solars seemed bland, Sidereals seemed strange and Dragonblooded seemed stuck up and weak.
