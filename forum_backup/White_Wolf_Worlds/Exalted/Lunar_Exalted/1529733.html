    So, is it worth it? I ask because I could've sworn that the pdf for the companion books came with the regular book pdf and deluxe versions. I don't remember going out of my way to select it for Dragon Blooded. I double checked, and it was included with my Sidereal and Abyssal pledges. But somehow, despite getting the Deluxe version, I guess I didn't double check to make sure I got the companion. So now I have to decide if its worth getting, because I've only been using cash and I'd have to beg a friend to get it for me.

    So, what kind of goodies and gems does it have? Are any of the Lunar characters stand-outs? Does the Caul scenario match up well with its Dragon-Blooded counterpart? Anything good, animal wise?

      Dragon-Blooded did require you to pay for Heirs to the Shogunate and/or The Realm actually. Lunars did as well. It appears OPP changed the pricing structure after that. See also how color art and chapter fiction are just kind of assumed now instead of stretch goals.

      So far I'm making my way through, but the stuff on the Shadowfang Vanguard, Mount Namas, and Sublime Danger are nice additions to the shahan-ya and Lunar dominions presented. THey The first two in my view also give great texture to the Northeast and Southeast in context of the recently released Across the Eight Directions. I can see the Shadow Fang Vangaurd especially being on Cherak and Fray's asses, while the conflict in regards to Prasad for the Mount Namas Lunars is a nice add. And Sublime Danger seems like a really good shahan-ya for Lunars who want to do something more anti-Lookshy.

      The Lunar Courts, which I'm in now, also seem to add a great deal. The stuff on the disease gods gives some interesting context to the Second Contagion in At8D that I think is really cool way to show how Heavenly policy impacts how gods act.

      A lot of folks have commented that Ul and Aqadar are especially cool characters. And there's a range of different artifacts, but I'll have to get ot those. Also some really cool stand-out Charms, like one that lets you get Evocations from your Territory Charms.

      And stuff.

                                                      Originally posted by Haerandir View Post

                                                      I should think so. It even specifically calls out that the style has no basis in 'traditional martial techniques', meaning that there's no requirement that you be able to move in a particular manner.
                                                      I think that's a reasonable point that somebody could make in favour of it, but I wouldn't personally go along with it because I feel as though even if you have a Martial Art that isn't rooted in mundane fighting style, the term and the associations carry with them a certain vibe that I think matches best with a humanoid form (for those few martial artists where the distinction matters in the first place).

                                                      ...The very small number of martial arts powers that entail their own brand of shapeshifting notwithstanding.

                                                      Originally posted by Haerandir
                                                      Now that you mention it, I suspect that this is the reason the style was included in the Lunars companion book in the first place.
                                                      See, I think that speaks to one thing where part of the intention with Lunars in this Edition has been to emphasize imagery for them as heroes that is more expansive than the animal forms. Something that came up pretty early in the days when a new Edition was announced was the intention to make a Lunar sword fighter feel like a viable character in a mechanical sense, and I think that sentiment worked its way through to the current signature Full Moon. Tegama Asarkon is modelled as a sword fighter first, to the point that he's said to almost always fight in human form, while he still has legitimacy as a Lunar (particularly exemplified in the chapter fiction of how he lured a Dynast into a trap).

                                                      I would say that was why Sublime Danger was first designed as somebody using a unique new martial art that gave her the iconic feature of wings made of daiklaves, more than an idea that it would be a style well suited to somebody who might turn into an animal. And so it ends up in the book because so did she, and you couldn't really have one without the other.

                                                      On top of which, I think there's something to giving Lunars a few achievements or inventions that aren't a function of their shapeshifting and animalistic side.
