                        "Lunars did not accomplish this kind of thing after the Usurpation (in previous editions), ergo, they don't have the capability to do so meaningfully" can be applied to so many areas of expertise that you could use it to justify the removal of a large chunk of the domain of Exalted mechanics from Lunar capability. It's also a specious argument, as Sidereals have accomplished little of note by making Artifacts while having a decent Crafting suite.

                        I should also point out that the rest of the Exalted have Charms to deal with all Abilities--where they don't (e.g., Infernals and War) it was due to lack of space rather than thematic reasons. All the other Exalted have Crafting Charms, but if Lunars got them it would make everyone else generic?

                        I should also point out that Shapeshifting is, like manipulating fate or making things out of solid light, a means to do things. Saying "Lunars lack the capability to do all these things, but they can shapeshift!" is not a meaningful trade-off. Sure, turning into a cat is neat, but at the end of the day it's either useful or it isn't: it either has a measurable mechanical impact (in stealth, in damage, in flight, etc.) or it doesn't. And I'm sure you'll find that shapeshifting can, one way or another, be used to make things.

                        As an addendum to the above, "utility" is something that everyone gets. Let's look at Sidereals! The ability to bypass combat without having to fight? No one else does it better: not only are they masters of disguise in a way that fools even supernatural senses, they are also the best at saying "no, I don't feel like fighting today" and avoiding encounters altogether. Transportation and disguise powers? They have excellent travelling Charms as well as access to Yu-Shan gates: Sidereals may be the most mobile Exalted of all! And their disguise powers are, as previously mentioned, excellent. If you even want to pretend that they're in the same class under your idea, you'd have to show how they get a similar amount of cool in their budget: assume they're both equally capable at everything, then justify each departure by showing something neat that they gain in exchange.

                        I don't think that Lunars deserve billing as Celestial Exalted under your vision. They haven't done much, they have entire areas of competence that they're not supposed to shine at, and at best they're going to match other Celestials in the things they do excel at. They would work better as a (narratively) minor type of Exalt, like the Liminal. This might even be a better way to deal with the idea of "the Werewolf Exalted", but unfortunately that's not what they're being billed as for Exalted 3. If you avertise them as a Celestial Exalt that is close in power to Solars and about even with Sidereals, people will be justified in expecting them to perform on par with the other two at anything they care to make a character about.

                        Finally, I don't find a blanket "no" to be as interesting as an intelligent "yes, but" or "yes, and". A solution in which Lunars excel at making particular things or at making things with quirks will allow for more character options and be a much stronger statement about Lunars than a simple "they can't do this" (a requirement of years, decades or centuries to make things is equivalent to "can't do this" for players, whom the rules are meant for). Gunnerkrigg Court's Coyote messing up the different tasks he takes out of boredom (e.g., sorting the stars into constellations) or distraction (e.g., sneezing when creating people) and then all of his "gifts" having serious flaws that threaten the user's health or sanity (e.g., the debilitating tree-body for Ysengrin, the host body-killing possession eyes for Renard) are fascinating material--surely this kind of thing is in theme for the tricksters among the Exalted?

                                                    Anyway, what crafting-related Second Edition Lunar Charms do you think will return, or not?


                                                    Flaw-Finding Examination sadly shared a name with a Dragon-Blooded Charm -- and was even so self-aware as to admit it -- but the idea was sound. It's a Perception Charm where the Lunar determines an object's weakest point, and therefore how best to break it. By turning that understanding around, a Lunar can identify how an object is broken, which hastens the task of fixing it.

                                                    There was no Dexterity or Wits Charm to hasten dramatic-time-scale actions so that a Lunar could repair or make things faster (which could be applied to administering medical aid, studying and researching, traveling, or any other form of manual labor), but there could have been. Certainly, Alchemicals got one, and I'm not talking about Creator Fugue Engine. I think it was Inexplicable Efficiency Upgrade.

                                                    Luna's Blessed Hands was rather uninspired and unimpressive.                                                             I think a big problem with this discussion is that we don't really know how Craft is supposed to work in 3E. I mean, in 2E, effective Craft Charms were basically limited to penalty negators and speed enhancers, with cap breakers like Wonder-Forging Genius in the high Essence part of the game. That's... not a lot to work from as a basis for design, no matter how creative you are.

                                                            What it comes down to is where the "hooks" for the Craft system are in 3E. What, exactly, can we do with Craft as a mundane Ability, and how do we do the breakdown between short-term or smaller projects (like cooking or sewing a shirt), longer-term or bigger projects (like building a house or a warstrider) and the really big stuff (artifacts, manses, and stuff on the scale of the Penitent of the Lap)? We need to know the answers to these questions before we try to make any statements about what kind of Charms Lunars should have.

                                                            Based on what the devs have said, it would seem to mean that Lunars will be able to quickly and efficiently set up temporary structures like war camps or impromptu shelters, while having more difficulty with the kinds of projects we normally associate with artifice. On the one hand, they don't rely on massive infrastructure or extended supply lines for their efforts, but on the other hand, projects that absolutely require such things are outside the reach of their magic. Don't know how this will play out in the system itself, but I'm sure it'd be interesting to see.
