            I bypass much of the problem by adopting a traditional-eastern-like take on it. Under such a paradigm, emulating Feng shui, 5-element theory, Yin-Yang theory and so on, there is little need to delve into the specifics of biology or chemistry as understood by modern science.

            Now, if you are unfamiliar with the concepts that surround traditional Chinese medicine (which also pertains to their ancient understanding of the way the universe / nature works) that would be a lot of material to study just to sporadically help maintain verisimilitude in a game. Luckily for me, I already study it as part of my work, so I just have to fudge the details to have it sound right for Exalted.

            If anyone here plays Weapons of the Gods or Legends of the Wulin, those games include enough info about such concepts that if you are good at winging the details and not worrying overmuch about small details and minor inconsistencies you could take a similar approach as mine. Or just read some articles on Wikipedia* XD

                    Originally posted by Omicron View Post
                    Or it could be a fun supernatural setting with interesting in-theme implications to be delved into by the players and fitting its ancient world aesthetics, rather than filing it away as "counter-thematic things never to be explored!"

                    Yeah, either/or.
                    I think it's tricky and perhaps something that should be decided table-to-table rather than established concretely in the manual (the way 2e did.)

                    Part of the premise of any fantasy game is that it's set in a world where at least some of the magical thinking people do or have once applied to the world is true, but I don't think it's possible or valuable to over-analyze the dividing line between reality and fantasy (especially since, by definition, it's not going to make sense, because magical thinking just doesn't when you really look hard at it.)

                    If a player thinks up a cool thing to do using a degree of basic germ-based medical knowledge that their character could plausibly deduce (like having their Twilight who has a lot of recovered First Age knowledge inexplicably insist on boiling all their medical tools even when everyone else thinks it's a bizarre quirk), that's cool and there's no reason to stop them. Conversely, if someone has a cool idea based on some magical interpretation of the world, I think it's fine to say "sure, why not" and go with that. Since those are the only times this is ever likely to come up, there's no point in nailing it down.

                    Virusmen are still goofy, though. Disney Wizard duels not withstanding.

                        Originally posted by Vegetalss4 View Post

                        Personally I would never take a solution which require me to demand that my players don't care about the world of our game.
                        You misunderstand the way in which I mean "Don't care." Go read She Who Lives In Her Name's description in Games of Divinity, and pay attention to the bit where she broke three of her spheres against Creation and diminished it. That was an awesome bit of background fluff up until the very moment people started caring about it and calling it the Three Spheres Cataclysm and extrapolating from it, at which point it started ruining things. Does this mean it never should have been printed? No, because it's great in the context in which it initially appears. It means people shouldn't have started caring about it.

                        Likewise, Creation runs on germ theory, and that's great. But it's not relevant. Making a bit deal out of it is going to cause problems. Swapping it out for something that's safe to make a big deal out of is going to cause even bigger problems, because whatever you replace it with is going to be some stupid-ass shit. And, I'm sorry not sorry to say, if you insist that everything in the setting should be something you can make a big deal out of, you're going to have a dumb cartoon setting in about five seconds.
