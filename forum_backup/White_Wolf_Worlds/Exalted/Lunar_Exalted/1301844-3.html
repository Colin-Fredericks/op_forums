                I was trying to figure out how to respond to this and basically the best way I can say it is this

                You're assuming a direct copy of 2E charms into 3E without actually seeing the charmset being played with, while failing to understand what I'm talking about is the amount of EXP spent on a character is not an infinite resource, and that this means that players will have different investments in abilities. You're just blanketly assuming that every Sidereal homebrew is the same, but that's not even completely relevant to the discussion I'm having so much as a consistent point of confusion in how I read your post. Lioness never really brought up what her charmset she had access to looked like, so you just defaulted to the worst interpretation of it; that it was a mostly copied version of 2E Sidereals rather then an attempt to fit them into 3E. The truth is that what it is doesn't matter, but if you go into the discussion with that as the assumption of what's being argued instead of a discussion on the expenditure of EXP, you're going to end up missing or deflecting the points someone else is trying to make because you're discussing something that isn't directly relevant to the discussion.

                What I'm talking about is the EXP investment in getting new abilities and raising them and buying charms. 9 charms is still 72 EXP, a significant investment. I would argue something like 3-5 Resistance charms is a decent idea of how many charms a combat character who primarily relies on parry or dodge might have around Essence 3, and they might be purchased from Non-Caste/Favored. This would be around 24-40 for Favored, and 30-50 for Non-Favored. I think this may still not really be accurate but it depends on the game; a character's build path never survives interaction with how the ST plays the game unless you're really hardheaded, and your builds will adapt to be more appropriate to how the ST runs the game; in some games, the combat king character may just be the person who has spent 100 EXP, while in others it may be someone whose spent half their EXP on it. This isn't even getting into the fact that, arguably, we aren't really discussing the super heavy combat focus but rather someone who might be on the second level of combat effectiveness; someone who isn't a drag on combat but isn't intending to be the one getting into fistfights with elder lunars and tangling with third circle demons, but rather just someone who has maybe an artifact and some charms, but that's tangential here.

                What does 3-5 Resistance charms get a Solar? Especially, what does it get when the Exalt in question probably doesn't have Resistance 5 or Stamina 5, and is instead maybe sitting around both at 3ish. I'm just gonna use 3 here because honestly it makes my argument better, but thanks to MadLetter's excellent charm cascades you can look at these numbers really easily. Also because, if you look at the charms, once you pass the third charm it's expecting you to have 4s and 5s in your Resistance. In this idea, our hypothetical Solar might not even have Resistance 4.

                A Solar could get to Iron Skin Concentration, giving them maybe 3 extra -0 health levels, the ability to pay 3 motes to increase her soak by 3, and another 3m to increase her soak by 2 . Or they could have purchased maybe 3 Ox-Body techniques, giving them 9 extra Health Levels; 3 -1s and 6 -2s. Those aren't awful. You can do okay and maybe survive a hit from a battle group that rolled poorly when it hit you, maybe. The best hope is that it just doesn't hit you, though, because the inert initiative also means the battlegroup isn't really suffering penalties for 'decisive' attacking you; after all, they just do their damage. The Solar isn't fully immune to battle groups, far from it even, and by that logic, it is unlikely a Sidereal will be immune to it either; they're supposed to be weaker, even if the power tiers have been shortened.

                These aren't going to 100% protect you from the Battle Group, and one thing I've noticed is a cycle effect can sometimes happen; where the Exalt is crashed by the Battle Group, then has to attack the non-battle group opponent to get the Initiative so the Battle Group doesn't rock them.

                This is intended; Battle Groups can be incredibly dangerous if you aren't careful because their AOE attacks and ability to deal damage no matter their Initiative means they can threaten any opponent who happens to be crashed, which can also turn off some of the more powerful defensive charms thanks to the Perilous keyword. They are always going to be more swingy then a normal attacker because they attack more then one person and this innately increases the RNG; no longer is it one person being hit by a fantastic attack roll, but instead an entire group can be hit by one. When this is combined with a massive pool of Automatic successes on Attack rolls and Damage rolls, you remove any of the swingy results that generally result in battlegroups being less effective, but you also turn off a lot of- just in this example- Solar Resistance charms that expect there to be damage dice instead of a bunch of automatic successes. Even a Solar in Aegis of Invincible Might is only protected from 5 levels of the automatic damage, but that's not related to my point.

                My point more is your observation of 'well there's 20 to 40 charms' isn't relevant, and shouldn't be looked at like that. An Exalt isn't going to have all the charms in the Resistance tree, they're going to have some, maybe, if they've gotten to that level of investment and they wanted those charms, so looking at every Resistance charm and assuming any Exalt worth their salt would just have the entire tree is basically going very 2E, where the assumed play pattern was 'I'm going to go into my time manipulating Manse and sit there until I get 500EXP before I start playing.', because the trees were much, much smaller and the game, towards the end of it's life cycle, encouraged that style of play.

                This is important because NPCs can have a pretty large amount of EXP versus a PC, particularly those designed to be bosses for a group of PCs, since they need a lot of attack and defense charms to get over the base effects of being severely outnumbered; though they may have allies, you never want your big boss man to get owned first, after all-, and often times are older; especially for, let's say, a Dragonblooded Wyld Hunt going to attack a Lunar Elder, this charm could turn the already swingy Battlegroups which can sometimes turn a fight into 'oh crud we just all got crashed at once by the battle group' into 'oh shit this battle group is literally killing us by itself'. Stuff like that can really surprise the group as a whole and require some quick runbacks.

                This is a mess of a whole bunch of conflicting and overlapping factors going on here.

                    My first point, which I will say as bluntly as possible. Is that the homebrew for sidereals isn't good enough for the level that it needs to be. Lionness is having this issue because we do not have a charmset that properly compliments the sidereal powerset in 3e. The ones we have have been good tries but not at the level it needs to. Moreover, she was blaming Silken armor for not being good enough when it's not the problem of the armor it's the problem with what we currently have to work with when it comes to sidereals. To be fair, it takes a lot of work to make a good charmset so I don't blame the people who tried.


                    My second point about battle groups was to get back on topic. Converting all your dice to automatic successes is enough to crash anyone who's not supernal melee even they might not be able to stop it because it basically prevents the defenses which work on 1s and 2s to apply. A lot of the defenses are conditional now and Raiton Banquet assault and the commanding buff charm do too much together. Not only that but they stack. IF you use them together and then crash an opponent, Raiton's banquet assault resets and can be used again. Cascading into more damage.
