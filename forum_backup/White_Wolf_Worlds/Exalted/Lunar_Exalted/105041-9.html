        If Lunar shapeshifting is anything like Second Edition, and what I've gathered from First Edition, shapeshifting will hardly be a subsystem at all.

        From what I can gather, it was always a case of "you can hunt an animal and drink its blood and then turn into it for a few motes", with hardly any extra powers or other XP expenditures at all. Everything John has said implies that this will still basically be the case, just with even more utility from what you can turn into; every time you encounter a new animal with some nifty abilities, you can endeavor to be able to turn into it, forever, with no additional cost.

        Maybe it will still have a thing where turning into something huge requires something extra, but tyrant lizards aren't the be all end all of this thing.

        Shapeshifting might be present somewhat in their Charms, but I think for the most part, the Lunar capacity to turn into creatures will just be this thing that they have, an additional extrapolation of their transformative nature that is basically off on its own, and also happens to be incredibly useful and versatile, and helps them play differently to others.

        That last word, that's something so many people have honed in on, to little coherence or destination, whether it be "Lunars are versatile because they've got Charms that can be applied to lots of different things" to "Lunars are versatile, so they can get lots and lots of really different Charms" to "Lunars are versatile, so they should switch Charms around like Alchemicals do". And that last one should be really telling, because Alchemicals are so much more clearly versatile without that seeming to be their thing.

        I feel as though there's been a lot of tripping over in trying to identify Lunars by trying to make Versatility a thing that they are rather than a logical observation about them. Lunars are versatile because, when you approach animals as able to do lots of different useful things, and a person who can really easily acquire an increasing range of things to turn into, well of course they'll be versatile.

        And yes, I'm sure the next question will be "what theme is being expressed by the shapeshifting, and how is it tied into anything else they do".

        To that I say: I honestly think that there's waaaaaay too much fixation for a while on themes as what Exalts are rather than observations about them. It gets really bad when people examine themes of leadership and responsibility of power in Solars so much that they actually start speculating that Solars should be literally powered by how many followers they have.

        From what I've gathered, Liminals emerged primarily out of a desire to make Frankenstein Exalted. I'm sure that they acquire more details as they went through the vetting process, but the lesson that "more mileage comes out of just deciding what you want given Exalts to be" can probably be widely applied.

        So Lunars are shapeshifters because they want folks who can be shapeshifters. Lunars are a bunch of other things, fierce, mad, relentless, beautiful, devilishly clever, because they want folks like that too (focused in a bit more than with Solars). Characters who display such characteristics essentially tend to have a certain streak labelled as "wild", so that's a bit of connecting point, but it's not really so big a deal; the combination of everything Lunars have apart from shapeshifting, and the actual shapeshifting, is probably about as syncretic as Solars being the height of arete and literally shining with the power of the Sun.

        It's just the tropes that Exalted constructs for itself, because it's got a very developed setting, but the setting is more than the places and the normals, and that's why, even if Solars are built in emulation of Batman, they still have the additional details that make it seem more worth playing Exalted than playing some licensed DC game. Same goes for whatever Lunars have that may cause literal transformations in their body that are less comprehensive than total shapeshifting. That kind of syncretism is all throughout Exalted, on every level.

        Somebody said that Lunars are a bit out-of-place for shapeshifters in the source material, but I would say that it's more like taking stuff from the source material like the Nemean Lion, the Lernaean Hydra, or that ape creature from that one Conan story, and reinterpreting them as if they were humans, as well as giving them a measure more sophistication; they might be shapeshifters because all those sources are being blended together, but they're mostly that because why not let the Nemean turn into a literal lion (and a few other things) every once in a while, and why limit the sources you're putting together?

        Lunars aren't anthropomorphic animals in their own setting, but they might be a measure of anthropomorphizing animals (or rather, legendary beasts) from other settings, and then starting them off like that.

        (Whereas Fair Folk are those anthropomorphized Pokemon)

                    Ok, so a theme is descriptive, not prescriptive, like natural laws. Gotcha. But the thing is- it's entirely unhelpful and it shouldn't be the case. Also I don't think it's true.

                    See, we have to be able to come up with our own stuff if we want to keep the Lunars (or any other group) interesting. You have to establish some prescriptive terms to make it doable, even if they are really rudementary. With Solars it's human excelence and that's really enough to get us going from straighforward things like punching hard, to really wierd places like growing extra arms in the image of the Uncoquered Sun.

                    Lunars aren't physics. If they are to be playable, they can't just be the case for study, we need to deconstruct them, so we know what makes them tick. You really, really can't just treat Lunars as a dumping ground for all the cool ideas inspired by specific sources you want to incorporate into Exalted.

                    That is, if they are to be playable. If they are there just to be NPCs, it's fine. But if you want them to be playable and inspiring, they really have to be given some prescriptive qualities, in understandable and objective terms, that inform us exactly what the hell can we do with them.

                    Until we're given that, any and all Lunar discussions will always turn into a convulted dumping ground of weird, incoherent ideas, as this thread has shown. Even if those ideas are incredibly cool and we intsinctively want Lunars to have all these cool things, like nightmare-breating, waeather control, territorial charms, transformation charms, they are so incredibly incosistent.
