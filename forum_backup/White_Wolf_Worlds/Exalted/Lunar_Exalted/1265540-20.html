            On the debate points about "witches"; I would offer that:

            Baba-Yaga to me seems to be mentioned in early Lunar discussions by Grabowski that offered up other figures who were tricksters and monsters (Old Man Spider), not witches specifically. (I find Saur Ops has said this, now that my post is wrote!). So I'm a bit with the folk who see it as not particularly an early thing, though I think that's fine and every edition changes.

            On Terrifying Argent Witches, I will speak a bit more. They may have been inspired by comments from previous devs, but ended up with some shade from former developers because it hewed to a template of being derivative of the Infernal Exalted, which those devs had come to believe was a mistake. Though the developers didn't speak much of them.

            I believe (though there is some degree of interpretation here) the "bad" derivative elements of Infernals: Their strongly purely thematically bound trees (not structured around human heroic actions through abilities and attributes). The malevolence their powers had bundled up into them. Their removal of choice about how to act from and how to characterize their PC from players hands and placing it into the Charm mechanics (buying more Charms sort of a process of becoming more of an inhuman thing). Their often hugely overwritten structure. Their "symbolic mechanics". Most tentatively, perhaps they way they imply that "edgy" heroes who must do evil acts for the good of all are the best that you can get (as opposed to the good people who must do bad things you sometimes find along the moral spectrum in other splats, where those bad things emerge more organically from their social context, circumstances and human limitations, which seems to me more true to life).

            All from Infernals (and anyway I have spoken of this elsewhere enough that it's not useful and probably unwanted to regurgitate it again).

            This is less about whether "witches" is a good theme and more about "Don't do it like TAW" though.

                                I guess that it all clicks for me because, as different as the various concepts are, they’re all united as “the monster in the wild.” The Dragon, Grendle, the Wolfman, Baba Yaga, they’re all outsiders, wild and dangerous.

                                And....well, as others have noted, if Lunars just have Shapeshifting and Animals, it’s hard to come up with thematic angles for the mental charms. You could go with the “Animal Totem” route and define, say, the Owl as Wise and the Cat as Curious, and so on and then build charms that fit those concepts, but then you’re saying “Owls embody wisdom” as a fundamental aspect of the setting’s metaphysics and kind of turning Lunars into a collection of the Fera, instead of shapeshifters who can be anything.

                                On the other hand, without some unifying vision for the Lunars, mental charms become a random grab bag of effects that don’t feel connected to the rest of the charmset (and possibly even each other). So there needs to be some unifying theme.

                                And, yeah, I get that “Dark and Creepy” overlaps a bunch with Abyssals....and potentially Liminals and Infernals and maybe a few others too. I’m not sure how they’re gonna separate Abyssal “Dark and Creepy” from Lunar “Dark and Creepy” but... to be honest, I kinda feel like Abyssal and Lunar charms should overlap to some degree. I mean, the Moon and the Night go together, both splats tend to do the “blood drinking monster” thing, there should be some overlap. That said, there should also be a difference. The Moon is not the Night. A difference in mechanics, in how core things are to the charmset, and a bunch of other factors can serve to make the Lunars feel distinct from anyone else with similar abilities, including Sorcerers.

                                I’m hopeful that they’ll do a good job explaining to us how those distinctions work in the book.

                                        Yeah, I think it's pretty easy to differentiate between the creepy witch of the woods and a Lich.

                                        Originally posted by Sith_Happens
                                        That’s what I figured. Actually I’ve been going further and assuming it took until a few centuries after the Contagion once the Treshold had recovered enough for the Pact to have anything to work with.
                                        Tbh, scattered survivors living in a post-apocalyptic wasteland of marauding hobgoblins and hungry ghosts with basically no government whatsoever... is probably a good time for the Lunars, because that's much more to their advantage than it is to Dragonblood or Sidereals.

                                        Dragonblood take years from Exaltation to being ready to be an adventuring hero, because Exaltation isn't going to be enough to take down a pack of Hobgoblins when you've only just started puberty. So they need a certain amount of institutional support. Not a ton, but some. Also, because they're bred not chosen (except according to some theologians), the more Dragonblood you have, the more you can produce. Therefore the less you have (because 80% of them died in the last few years), the less you can produce.

                                        Also, one key advantage of the Dragonblood is that they can get pretty much as much power out of Jade weapons and armour as a Solar. But making Jade artefacts takes time, mining Jade needs infrastructure, and even scavenging places where Dragonblood may have lost their weapons when they fell to the Crusade requires maps, a search party, exorcists to ward off hungry ghosts, etc.
                                        It's not necessarily extremely hard, but it does require some organisation.

                                        Sidereals also take years to produce. When a Sidereal dies, it will be 15-30 years before another one is ready.
                                        And it's difficult to manipulate society by making sure the right people are in the right place at the right time (or the right people die) when there's basically no society. The easiest way to manipulate a lot of people is to manipulate their government; with no government, you have to manipulate them individually.


                                        Whereas when a young scavenger in the ruins of a Shogunate City is surrounded by a pack of Hungry Ghosts and Exalts, she's ready to turn into a huge magic bear and rip them up right now. A post-apocalyptic wasteland of ruined cities, monsters, bandits, warlords and tribes gone feral is no big deal for a Lunar.


                                        What I imagine is something like this:

                                        The Usurpation happens. Many Lunars die, others flee.
                                        The Lunars fight back in a guerilla insurgency. But they're massively outnumbered by organised legions with powerful artefacts who are already in control of society at multiple levels, and their masters who foresee when and where the Lunars will strike.
                                        The Lunars reshape themselves, abandoning their castes and forming the Silver Pact. They become more successful, exacerbating tensions between Daimyos, stoking the fires of civil war... but they're still living in a world almost entirely controlled by their enemies. While the Dragonblood don't cooperate well, they do unite when they need to, to kill Lunars. Not all Lunars try to burn down the Shogunate, some prefer to rule isolated communities on the very edge of the world, or pursue other aims in secret.
                                        Civil war wracks the Shogunate. The Lunars lurk in the shadows, weakening their foes. They slowly grind down the number of experienced Sidereals, but also lose many of their own leaders.
                                        They're 400 against the world, and they're losing. Silver Pact leaders believe that if they can make civil war so perpetual that the Shogunate's unified anti-Lunar culture breaks down, they might be able to seize territory and have Daimyos ignore it. They do acquire territory in the more isolated parts of the world, but much of humanity is still under the control of Daimyos and their Wyld Hunts. Anti-Lunar propaganda has seeped into the consciousness of humans across the world, even those of whom the Daimyos' control is more de jure than de facto.

                                        The Contagion happens. Most Dragonblood die. So do most Lunars, but they're reborn in battle against the Balorian Crusade. Many die to the Fae Horde, but more rise to take their place. When the Crusade ends, the Dragonblood legions are shattered and broken. But the Lunars, while most are young, still stand at twenty-score veterans of the Crusade.

                                        Across the world, lesser scions of the Daimyos claim their right to rule Provinces... and die at the hands of the Lunars. Only the Blessed Isle, and a few other places like Dehelesen, are too strong for the Lunars to bring down. But in the North, the South, the West and the East, the remnants of the Shogunate are smothered into nothing. Dragonblood wielding their great-aunts' daiklaives and wearing their great-grandmother's armour, and the callow youth of their ancestral guard, die at the hands of the feral children of dead cities, led by beast-gods out of nightmare.

                                        For a hundred years, the remnants of the Shogunate are ground to nothing, until to the grand-daughters of apocalypse, it's ancient dead history. In Coral the Daimyo's palace is a temple of Siakal; in Chiaroscoro its a market; in Great Forks housing for a hundred tribesmen who don't even know what it was; in Thorns it houses a popular brothel. Only a few isolated cities maintain the traditions of hundreds of years of their ancestors: Prasad, Cherak, Lookshy.

                                        But on the Blessed Isle survivors and refugees have united under the Scarlet Empress, who has declared a new Realm. 10,000 Dragons are nothing to the strength of the Shogunate; but they're enough. On great ships the massed legions of Creation's safest continent attack the North, the South, the East. An-Teng, Thorns, Chiaroscoro, the Lap, Cherak, Pneuma, Medo, the Caul... soon they control the coastlines of two continents. The Lunars manage to stop them from making much progress in-land, but there are exceptions, such as Prasad, Linowan and Greyfalls.

                                        In every direction, 400 Lunars battle 10,000 Dragons. The Realm expands and contracts, expands and contracts, allying with other Shogunate remnants like Cherak and Prasad, but the Lunars can't stop its rise to power. But it hits a peak; as the Realm expands, it gets weaker under its own weight, it takes more effort to maintain the ambitions of a rising number of powerful Dragonblood. The Lunars change tactics, get more experienced. After hundreds of years, they become confident; perhaps they're winning? Then, the great victory in the Caul; in a co-ordinated Silver Pact campaign, the Dragonblood are driven out of four sacred cities. Their armies unite in the last city, Faxai. Lunars now control almost all of the Caul.

                                        Now the Lunars are confident. It may take another hundred years, but the momentum is with them. Their tactics, their War Dominions, are working. Lunars are dying to Wyld Hunts less and less, growing more and more experienced. The Realm mostly just controls the coasts. Driving them back to the Blessed Isle is in sight. It was reborn after the Contagion, but after over a thousand years, Dragonblood civilisation totters. And soon Kejak will die. It is foreseen. The leader of the enemy is almost dead, and there's no clear successor.

                                        And then, suddenly, the Solars are back. But it seems a lot of them, somehow tainted, now serve the Deathlords, pushing those ancient ghosts from a minor threat to a major one. And the Empress appears to be... just gone. Will she return? Is she dead? No-one knows, and a Realm civil war is imminent.
                                        Should the Silver Pact maintain its tactics?
                                        Or should they form one giant co-ordinated campaign, and in 4 or 5 years strike? Could they drive the Realm out of the Threshold, not in 100 years, but 10?

                                            To put it a different way:

                                            The Shogunate is the Roman Republic and the Principate. It has civil wars, it has periods of dictatorship and murder. Sulla, Julius Caesar, etc. And the barbarians have their Teutoburg Forest. But Rome is powerful, and massive. There are periods when the whole Empire is split amongst feuding generals, but it still stays strong. The barbarians are no great threat.

                                            The Contagion/Balorian Crusade is the Crisis of the Third Century and the Barbarian invasions of the West, in the 4th and 5th.
                                            Civil war+famine+barbarian invasion almost makes Rome fall... but it doesn't. It survives, but only in the East.

                                            The first period of the Realm is Eastern Rome (aka the Byzantine Empire) circa 300-700 AD.
                                            The Eastern Roman Empire survives. The West falls. Rome survives in a weakened form. It expands in a series of brutal wars; taking parts of Italy, Egypt, eventually taking back the Middle East. But it's inconsistent. The Vandals seize North Africa.

                                            But all that war exhausts it. The barbarians slowly become more powerful, more organised. They rise, in the desert, in the forests, on the steppes. Constantinople slowly loses more and more of Italy to the Lombards. Wars with Persia devastate its easternmost lands. Then comes the desert horde, taking Egypt, Palestine, Algeria, Syria. The horse nomads from the great plains take Anatolia, the men of the north-east forests swarm over Dalmatia.

                                            The Eastern Roman Empire is still rich. Its still powerful. It controls many territories. Its still a major world power with a huge population, but it has lost momentum.

                                            But then comes the Civil War.

                                            And that's when the barbarians* invade and the whole thing falls apart, leaving just a few isolated rump states.

                                            *I'm referring to the French. And a few Italians.

                                                    Very thought provoking analogies. Not surprisingly for me, they do bring out a slightly critical line of thought (might be right, might be wrong, putting it out there as food for thought).

                                                    In the prior editions you had a Realm and the Sidereals as basically defined by internal conflict - factions, Houses. So that's why, even though they're the Powers That Be and masters of the world, they're somewhat ill prepared and flat footed when the Solars return at the same time as they have a leadership crisis. Then this is what sets the scene for the game as it is - defunded Wyld Hunt, Houses withdrawing their legions to the isle, etc.

                                                    They're like that fundamentally because they can afford to be like that - they've faced no real long term external threats that motivate them to unite, and they've only an empire that has waxed and expanded and succeeded and never, ever been on the backfoot. It's "decadence".

                                                    But that kind of infighting is harder for me to think about if it's obviously the case that there's this huge external foe out there, recognizably a coherent entity, that's slowly but palpably gaining ground.

                                                    You, in a House, don't fight the other House, you go out and kill the common enemy that will burn both your possessions to ash. You, as the Empress, don't allow, much less create and support political structures that create infighting in to preserve your own rule, if they weaken you such that an external enemy will wipe you out in a hundred years - you make the necessary reforms and do away with wasteful internal competition, as to do anything else would be to ignore the immediate threat (the external enemy) to avoid the less immediate one (insurrection). Sidereals don't worry so much about whether they support Solars or Dragonblooded, but about an enemy that would destroy the entire ability of the Bureau of Fate to function in world as the preserver of human civilization at all. That's what your politics will *all* be about because it's *the* existential threat. (They especially do this if they're the greatest predictionists in the world who have a tendency to look at the long term).

                                                    This isn't a stance on whether the Lunars should or should not pose the sort of threat and the Realm be in the sort of long term decline that kind of comes through in some of the posts above. I'm just questioning and wondering aloud really on whether it feels a bit like there's a bit of a combination of a Dragonblooded and Sidereal politics predicted on the absence of a significant external threat, and an empire that's on the expanse and knows no challenge (what largely was in 1e and 2e), and a building of heat for Lunars as actually a much more successful challenger making that a declining empire (3e's Lunars), as bits that don't really quite fit perfectly.

                                                        As Eric has kind of alluded to earlier in the thread, smart Lunars avoided doing things that would make their threat feel very immediate and visceral in a manner that would make the Dragon Blooded feel a lot more unified and galvanize them to take action en masse.

                                                        There's also something to be said for the idea that everybody agrees with unity in principle, but are still fighting over who is going to be in charge (and are too heavily conditioned to accept something more parliamentary without a lot more pushing).

                                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead
                                                        You, as the Empress, don't allow, much less create and support political structures that create infighting in to preserve your own rule, if they weaken you such that an external enemy will wipe you out in a hundred years - you make the necessary reforms and do away with wasteful internal competition, as to do anything else would be to ignore the immediate threat (the external enemy) to avoid the less immediate one (insurrection).
                                                        I'm not sure precisely how the Thousand Hazy Paths will be framed this time around, but I think there's room for a system in which the Empress could carefully balance keeping divisions of power and personality within the Dynasty to prevent anybody from becoming big enough to challenge her, and being able to leverage their collective strength against Lunar Exalted.

                                                        Especially if a lot of Lunars were ready to take the long road of bleeding the Realm slowly and letting its own complacency and internal issues help their goals along.

                                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead
                                                        Sidereals don't worry so much about whether they support Solars or Dragonblooded, but about an enemy that would destroy the entire ability of the Bureau of Fate to function in world as the preserver of human civilization at all. That's what your politics will *all* be about because it's *the* existential threat. (They especially do this if they're the greatest predictionists in the world who have a tendency to look at the long term).
                                                        I strongly suspect that the Sidereals will maintain the general byline of having too many things to deal with and not enough resources.

                                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead
                                                        I'm just questioning and wondering aloud really on whether it feels a bit like there's a bit of a combination of a Dragonblooded and Sidereal politics predicted on the absence of a significant external threat, and an empire that's on the expanse and knows no challenge (what largely was in 1e and 2e), and a building of heat for Lunars as actually a much more successful challenger making that a declining empire (3e's Lunars), as bits that don't really quite fit perfectly.
                                                        And finally I'll note that among the ways in which What Fire Has Wrought has shaken up the Scarlet Dynasty a bit is by focusing on the manner in which several Houses have organized around consistent responses to external threats and have profited by it, have disputes based on differing approaches and ideals for it, and in at least one case might have been damned by it.

                                                        Houses Cathak and Peleps are militarily very powerful, in a manner that creates complications around the question of exactly whom that power will serve.

                                                        None of this is particularly unrealistic. It's the kind of counterproductive internal politicking and fighting that has occurred for numerous powers, with or without external enemies, throughout human history, and continuing in certain forms to the present day. One can't even say being in a total war situation is something that will get a society and its leaders to straighten up and fly right, because while that worked a lot more for each of the Allies (and to a lesser extent, between the three big ones, although even that was a tenuous thing that could have easily fallen apart with the right pressures; Churchill freaking hated communism, and could be less than discrete about that in meetings with Stalin, regardless of the benefits of cooperation with the USSR), it was quite a shitshow for the Germans.

                                                        That and it's written that way because people pulling apart when they ought to be drawing together is one of the key conflicts and tragedies of the setting, and has a background of compelling conflict. Like, on the grounds of being thematic and facilitating gameplay alone, it wouldn't particularly need to be realistic.

                                                        It just is, almost distressingly so, as an added benefit.
