                        Legend of Zelda: Both the Link of Majora's Mask and the Link of Twilight Princess could be Lunars, and not for the obvious reasons.

                        Setting aside his previous adventure (and potential Exaltation moments) in Ocarina of Time, MM Link found himself lost and stranded in an unknown land, and then trapped in an alien body when the Skull Kid uses a curse to transform him into a Deku Scrub child. In this hopeless situation, Link finds the Happy Mask Salesman, who offers to remove the curse if Link promises to find the Skull Kid, take back the Majora's Mask he stole, and return it to the Salesman. But Link not only has to trust that the disturbing and mysterious man can be trusted to uphold his end of the bargain or to not abuse the power of such a Mask for himself, Link first has to prove himself by finding the Skull Kid and managing to take back at least his own Ocarina of Time. Which means risking his life against the Skull Kid.

                        But Link decides that the freedom to live in his own body is worth the risk. He hunts down the Skull Kid, reclaims his Ocarina, and then accepts the Happy Mask Salesman's deal to be freed of the curse in return for the promise to steal Majora's Mask and return it to the creepy Salesman.


                        TP Link had a similar start. In chasing after the moblins who kidnapped his childhood friend, Link found himself sucked into a strange world, attacked and overpowered by an alien creature, and then for some reason cursed to transform into the body of a wolf; an agonizing experience which causes him to pass out.

                        When he awakens, he has no idea where he is or how much time has passed, he is still trapped in the body of a wolf, and further trapped by a manacle chained to the floor within a barred and locked dungeon cell. Freedom of the sort he desires is well and truly beyond his grasp.

                        Then an imp appears, and offers him freedom. She breaks his chain, and offers to help him escape if he can figure his own way out of his cell. By exploring his environment and experimenting with his cursed form, he discovers a way to dig his own way out. But then the imp reveals more details about her offer -- she will help Link escape this prison if he agrees to do everything she says.

                        Just like MM Link with the Happy Mask Salesman, TP Link is gambling that whatever situation into which he's getting himself isn't worse than the situation he's leaving behind. Out of the frying pan and into the fire, as it were. At the very least, TP Link is betting that he can win his freedom more easily from the imp than he can from the prison.
