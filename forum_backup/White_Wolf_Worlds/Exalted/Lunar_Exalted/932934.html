    Lunar Castes have always been a bit different

    07-17-2016, 12:26 PM
    Avoiding a potential derail in Irked's Lunar homebrew thread.

    Originally posted by Maudova View Post
    Why are we still stuck with the idea that there are 3 castes? We should have all 5 castes so that Lunars have variability and can focus on areas players want. I think the idea of "breaking" their exaltation a has other more interesting possible avenues.
    What I have to say at the start won't really engage with what you're saying, but here it is:

    "Lunar Castes have never really been about what you are supposed to do, but rather how you go about doing it."

    Dawn Castes tend to be the most physical of the Solar Exalted, but there is still a significant social and mental aspect to their role, whether that be intimidating opponents, inspiring soldiers, implementing training regimens and battlefield strategies, or analyzing and responding to the martial techniques and flaws of others. A Dawn can choose how she goes about being a Dawn in what Caste and out-of-Caste Abilities she favors and invests, but she's still fulfilling a role regardless of her specific methods.

    While the Full Moon Caste finds their physical prowess an easy match to physical violence, the social and mental prowess of the Changing Moon and No Moon Caste have their uses in battle. Like Dawns, Changing Moons intimidate opponents and inspire followers, but they also have a somewhat more natural time employing feints and misdirection, and their ability to train followers more naturally encompasses animals (as Survival is not a Dawn Caste Ability). Like Dawns (for whom Awareness is a Caste Ability), No Moons are capable of responding with great sharpness and speed to the actions of their enemies, analyzing their tactics and flaws, and devising clever strategies to achieve victory.

    Caste-specific Role Bonuses for Lunar players to earn Solar Experience wouldn't be about WHAT they did, but more HOW they did something.

    "Did the Full Moon discover a physical solution to what seemed like a firmly social or mental problem? Did he perform a physical feat that put even his Exalted might to the test?" That sort of thing.


    I'm not convinced that Caste-derived "roles" would enhance or fit the Lunar experience, especially given the Lunar ability to BREAK their Castes.

    The Unconquered Sun chose a Dawn because he felt that the mortal had the right attitude to take and use the Sun's martial power to change the world. Mars chooses heroes she feels will best facilitate her vision of the conflicts that she wants to happen or prevent in Creation. These choices are very firmly tied to roles that these heroes are intended to play, whether alone or with others. These roles are immutable realities of the Exalted Souls granted to them.

    But the Moon Goddess chooses those heroes who valued their freedom more than life itself, to the point where she actually Exalted a woman for an elaborate act of suicide (throwing herself to the sharks). She rewards this thirst for freedom with the power to attain that freedom and survive.

    This seems very purposeless, leaving her Chosen to find their own direction. In the Primordial War, this direction was to cooperate with the other Exalted in earning humanity's freedom to achieve prosperity and dominion over Creation without needing to fear the creators of the world and their favored species. In the Second Age, this direction is to wage war against the Scarlet Dynasty for the freedom to exist.

    Matching this lack of direction, Luna's Exalted Souls are inherently mutable in a way completely unique to the Exalted Host. A Dawn is a Dawn, a Chosen of Journeys is forever an Exalt of Mercury, no Alchemical can change his primary Magical Material any more than a Terrestrial can change his primary Element.

    I don't get the sense that Luna cares WHAT her Chosen do with their freedom. Would she really be giving them freedom if she immediately burdened them with expectations for what they ought to be doing with themselves? Or would she be giving them more freedom by gifting them with Castes that emphasis their personal talents, and thereby offer them more power for attaining their freedom in the ways that matter most to them?

          Only problem I've ever really seen with 3 Castes is that if it's a game for an ideal of 5 players, (as it seems to be for all the other splats?), then you have to decide which Castes to double up on.

          I'm a bit leery about the specific example of only giving a bonus to Lunars for using their capabilities in a counter intuitive way ("Solved a mental problem through a social route" etc.) since I think that's both a bit too much in need of interpretation and I think they should be rewarded for doing specific things than doing counter intuitive things only. Otherwise, I think, yes, and role bonus needs to be comparable to the breadth of the caste and its focus (which could be along the lines physical / social / mental?).

          I'm also not so keen on the idea of Luna as a god who is agnostic of what her chosen do in the world, if we're going with gods who care about that at all. IDK why. Just the idea that Sol is the only god / Incarna who cares about what his Chosen are doing in the sense of giving them a mission or purpose understandable to humanity (Sidereals don't, really, in the sense that Fate is a bit of black box) sort of has a bad taste to me.

                              So StG was quoting my response to another posted stating they wanted to get away from one of the castes being the sorcerers amongst the lunars. I agree with the original poster that tagging any one caste with sorcerers anima powers does inherently limit them. In that sense that if they chose to play that caste for whatever reason and they are not a sorcerer that anima power is a useless ability. It shoe horns that caste into being less functionally efficient for the character played or play someone that is initiated.

                              My suggestion was based on the idea that if you have 3 and the caste you wanted to play isn't a sorcerer then you only have 2 viable options. Right. So essentially the splat is reduced to two castes at that point. If the character you wanted to play was cerebral then you are stuck with a a physical build trying to be smart of a social build trying to outwit people. Which sure they can be played, however they are not exactly comfortable places to play from (people accepting the challenge aside). Forcing one of 3 castes to integrate sorcery as one for their defining characteristics (as lunar caste tattoos do) is not well thought out. Sure you could replace that anima power with something else non-sorcery based as the person I was reply to said but, that leaves lunar sorcerers in the lurch and without comparable anima effects to support it (Lunar sorcerers were supposed to be an important thing).

                              Let compare solars to lunars again. A solar gets 10 abilities. You can make any kind of viable character idea you want with 10 favored/caste abilities. However having all of one stat block plus one doesn't allow you to make a mechanically viable character that comfortably fits within a broad range of roles. Yes sure lunars have a specific set of roles to play as per theme. They don't have to be limited by their core mechanics in a way that limits the characters you can build for them. I've played 2 lunar characters and several solars. It was MUCH easier and more enjoyable to make a character with the solar mechanics than it was to use a lunars.

                              Keeping the limitation of 3 castes is honestly a weak reliance on that mechanics having three attribute blocks to correlate castes too. Dropping in 5 castes with a variety of caste attributes in 3rd edition makes just as much sense as how they opened caste and favored abilities up for solars.
