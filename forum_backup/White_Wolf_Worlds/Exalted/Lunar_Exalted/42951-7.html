                Originally posted by John Mørke View Post
                An Aspect connotes very little beyond an idea of what you will be like. Aspects imply things about demeanor, appearance, and (often latent) skills. They also demonstrate the nature of your power, such that it is essentially different from that of another Aspect. It has almost nothing to do with your expected role. A Fire Aspect is not guaranteed to be a fighter. He might be a socialite. Aspect says so little about the differences in Dragon-Blooded that you can expect similar roles from all of them. They are inherently soldiers, inherently group-arrayed, and inherently familial.

                Castes say a lot more. Castes are fixed. You do not fall out of Castes. It is not expected that a Dawn will be something other than a fighter. Castes align with the person you were before Exaltation. Characters can deviate from Castes, but it is very rare that they simply cease to operate within their definitions. Meanwhile, Dragon-Blooded are not deliberately Chosen for their skills and talents. They are an outgrowth of their parents' (and their parents') Aspects, and so they inherit blood traits, and those are expressed when they come into their own Aspects. Denying an Aspect is not essentially possible because Aspects intermingle in the blood, just as traits of the parents mingle to create children. Dragon-Blooded have flex. Solars do not. They are not inheriting anything so much as they are exemplifying who they always were to begin with. Castes are an extremely strong predictor for roles and social placement as only persons who would fit those roles are Chosen in the first place.
                Based on this... I think Aspects are wholly inappropriate for the Lunar Exalted as I currently understand them. The Lunar Exalted's capability to control their form and the approaches they take towards solving the problems they face present a large problem for a descriptor that is based on things such as 'demeanor' or 'appearance' or even skills that can be latent. For example, no Lunar was born knowing how to fly, but by being able to take the shape of a bird, they overcome this inability. From my understanding, to give the Lunar Exalted Aspects would be to contradict this definition, unless the Lunar Exalted were to be changed so wholly that you've effectively changed the only coherent characteristics they've had over the two previous editions.

                Based on current knowledge of the Lunar Exalted... Castes are much more appropriate. As you define, Castes are fixed. You do not fall out of Castes. This makes the event of the Lunar Exalted losing their Castes as being such a shocking tragedy to the Lunar Exalted. It's very symbolic of the Lunar Exalted being cast out from Creation proper, from losing their previously held stations in the First Age. They've lost their station, they've lost their status, they've lost their purpose! The breaking of the Castes is the twist of the knife in a long string of tragedies they've previously suffered. It is the mystical representation of everything the Lunar Exalted previously knew and stood for, being taken away from them.

                And that's why I think it's important for the Lunar Exalted to keep with having Castes. It emphasizes that even when beaten, when broken, when exiled, when made to be outcasts and monsters, when stripped of their responsibilities and their duties, that they've reforged their own Castes. It is the Lunar Exalted's collective resilience, cunning and genius that allowed them to give themselves a new purpose. In the First Age, they stood with the Exalted Host, but when exiled and reviled in the Age of Sorrows, the Lunar Exalted now stand on their own.

                It's a story of tragedy, suffering, strength and pride. These would be people who bear scars of the blows that should have killed them, but didn't, and now it's their turn to strike.

                This story could be completely removed in 3rd Edition, but that would be a great disappointment to me if it did. It's one of the best, glorious things about them.

                Also, I've always felt that Dragon-Blooded Aspects are much more restrictive than Solar Castes.

                            Originally posted by Black Paper Moon View Post
                            Castes/aspects/etc. serve two main purposes. First and most importantly, they’re there to give structure to an Exalt type to make them more interesting to the player and to facilitate character creation. Second, they’re useful shorthand to describe characters: knowing that you’re up against “a Dawn and a Twilight” is a lot more evocative and informative than “two Solars”. When you’re discussing how to divide Lunars, your priorities should be into making them as interesting and easy to parse as possible.

                            I could write an entire essay on why Lunar Castes are shit, but I’ll be brief and boil it down to three things: they’re overly restrictive, they’re unclear, and there’s too few of them. Let’s hit the points one by one.

                            First off, remember that the main purpose of a Caste/Aspect/Whatever is to provide a structure that’s evocative and makes creating the character you want easier. What the Caste provides is a skeleton that you build around: it’s the marrow, not the meat of what you are. For Solars, a player who is into all of his Caste Stuff and defines himself narrowly according to it is very unusual: the Castes are designed to be used so that you create “a Zenith leader who is also a master swordsman and has an abiding interest in finding and translating old texts” or “a Twilight craftsman who uses clever gadgets to hunt and kill the minions of Malfeas in Chiaroscuro”. The Caste is not who you are or what you do. The Caste is about you having a core aptitude for a certain number of things that you can raise to 5 immediately and learn cool Charms for.
                            Lunar Castes are a straightjacket. They lock you very firmly into a McCombo package of Attributes with redundant competencies that say very firmly “you are physical/mental/social-focused”. The inability to raise Attributes instantly (as Solars do with Abilities) and the high cost of raising them, the higher cost of unfavored Charms (everything outside your narrow Caste McCombo), and the fact that the system encourages specialization means that players are punished very harshly for stepping beyond the archetype—for making your character interesting! The 3 Caste / 1 Favored spread is one of the dumbest things in Lunars.

                            Second, Lunar Castes are simultaneously about Attribute lines (Physical/Social/Mental) and about roles (Warrior/Trickster/Shaman). Physical/Social/Mental as the basis of Castes is crap, because it’s about as evocative as an engineering manual. Other than surface symmetry (which should never be your main design criteria) there are no good reasons whatsoever to associate Lunar archetypes to the Attribute lines. Any reworking of Castes should address this as a priority.
                            Warrior/Trickster/Shaman is better, but still problematic. ‘Warrior’ on its own is not good enough: it’s too generic and stale (fun exercise: picture the Solar Castes being called Warrior/Priest/Wizard/Rogue/Bard). From a systems design standpoint it’s also a problem: for a game that is as heavily combat-centric as Exalted (compare the pagecount devoted to combat v. that for any other system aspect), presenting one of the options as the superior combat option is a mistake. The fact that a Twilight (Scholar) can match the Dawn (General) in combat is an important feature. ‘Shaman’ is also weird—what is it that they do, again? Commune with nature and pray to spirits? Is this kind of thing even in their Charmset? Is there any Lunar that shows what it means to be a Shaman anywhere in the Exalted line?

                            Third, three base archetypes is too little. Other than sticking to Attribute lines for surface symmetry, which I’ve already condemned as stupid, there’s no reason to limit Lunars to three castes. Alchemicals, which are also Attribute-based, get six and are the better for it. Most other Exalted types get five, which affords players a much greater variety in terms of what kind of Caste (skeleton) they want to build their characters around. Whether we go for Castes (e.g., ‘Hunter’, ‘Trickster’, ‘Leader’, ‘Spy’) or Aspects (this would be a hell of a lot easier if Lunars didn’t have a personal animal totem), three archetypes isn’t enough to cover the spectrum of cool characters you can build using the system.
                            Like BPM notes, castes and aspects serve as a starting point for character design. And I agree that the Lunar castes are a bit problematic in that respect. The main reason for that is that their story doesn't line up with their mechanics again. I mean, in 2e being good at physical things meant that you could be a good warrior, but you could also be a good blacksmith, a good burglar, or a good athlete. And it doesn't help you at all with being a good warleader or tactician (not that those fields were well served by 2e's mechanics either...)

                            And Tricksters and Shamen generally both need some attributes from all three. It also doesn't help that, if you define Shaman as someone with arcane knowledge who treats with spirits, the guys who have that baked into their charmset are Sidereals, not Lunars.

                            Thus, to do those three castes, you would need to go back and reallocate the attributes and everything.


                            However, in the spirit of this thread, I'm curious what people would think of Lunars who had the Physical Aspect, Mental Aspect, and Social Aspect? While I grant this is a bit technical, it could be interesting to have a type of character who was all about solving every situation with words and social skills or with their mind or with their body. You would want charms to reinforce this, obviously, by creating charms that allowed the social and mental guys to solve or avoid physical problems with their words and wits and vice versa and everything. They shouldn't be the best charms ever, of course, but just enough so that a Lunar of one caste can legitimately try to achieve most objectives via their chosen paradigm.

                            This would be much like how DBs get charms that broaden the boundaries of their abilities, like how Air Aspects have some athletics charms in Stealth. You would want these charms to reinforce the paradigm too. Like you could give Strength a forceful intimidation charm and Dexterity might get a sexy walk charm, but neither of the two attributes would get broad access to the Charisma charms that let you be forceful, sexy, cute, and demure in turn.

                            I can already hear someone getting ready to tell me that, while that might be interesting, it's not a big enough skeleton to really work from to build a character concept. And, to be fair, they're right. Fortunately, Lunars have a backup source for that, the Totem. I admit it might take some finagling and probably a decent chunk of the book should be devoted to totems in that case, but being able to go "I'm a Social Focused Werefox" seems like it should be enough of a starting position to build a character around.

                            I'll grant too that they should be able to get favored attributes, so they can get outside their paradigm if they need to. I'm not gonna say how many, because that depends on too many other factors in this hypothetical situation that I couldn't even guess.

                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post

It is probably because of this deliberate design that Alchemicals do not have Aspects.                                     Indeed, this is a really good pair of foils.

                                    Alchemicals are made, but it's wrong to think of that making as the origin point. Their bodies are constructed to align with the people they had been in their prior incarnations and will continue to be. There is very much a continuity of who they are before and after, and it is the choices they made and people they grew to be that brought them there. Castes are the best fit.

                                    Liminals are made and that making really is the origin point of the person, if not of the history. There are some things about them that have been defined and shaped by their bodies'/parts' prior owners and their creators, much like how all children have had some things defined and shaped by their parents (hello again, Terrestrial connection!). Aspects are the best fit.

                                    Though the idea of birth/origin is seductive as a determining trait, choosing it as one may be a mistake. Consider Sidereals, who carry their Exaltations from birth. Hypothetical Sidereal Aspects instead of Castes would have each Star-Chosen inherently inclined toward behavior marked by journeys, serenity, war, secrets or endings. This is not so in Sidereals, though it's an easy mistake to make. Those Chosen not yet Exalted are people like any others, but their Exaltations ensure their lives will shape and mold and prepare them to be people appropriate for their respective Castes. Joybringers aren't Exalted for being joyous, they're Exalted for understanding joy, and for knowing (or being equipped to know) how best to create, reduce, use, and otherwise interact with joy.

                                        The moon.

                                        I mean, both Solar and Lunar Castes already use the sun and moon as elaborate metaphors for their caste roles. Turning the Lunar Castes into Aspects just means that the metaphor can be broadened, made more flexible, more inclusive. The symbols and metaphors of each Aspect need not no longer be shoehorned into the Caste roles of Warrior, Trickster, and Loremaster. Rather, any of those three roles, and many others besides, can follow from the symbolism of any Aspect.

                                        For example, the light of the Full Moon has been be associated with the following by various cultures on Earth, in no particular order: revelation, lunacy, madness, berserker rage, lust, longing, passion, beauty, guidance, justice, thieves, magic, love.

                                        The changing of the moon can stand for liminality, in-between states, twilight, mercuriality, deceptiveness, whimsy, transformation, the unexpected, spontaneity, etc.

                                        The new moon can take on connotations of doubt and uncertainty, the unknown and the fear thereof, mysterious shapes lurking just beyond the light, the wonders and horrors to be found off the beaten path, away from the safe and the known and the named.

                                        Note that each of these can easily assume the any of the roles we associate with the current Lunar Castes within its particular thematic niche. The Full Moon has aspects of revelation and mysteries unveiled, as well as madness and violence and powerful emotions (and the manipulation thereof, presumably). The Changing Moon includes aspects of the walker-between-worlds and the Deadly Beastman Transformation as well as the shapeshifting trickster. The No Moon includes aspects of the unnameable horror, the Monster, the blood-soaked Beast, as well as the bogeyman and the mischief-making spirit and the keeper of secret lore. And all that's just the tip of the iceberg.

                                                            I can see potential for Lunars to have either Castes or Aspects. It's mostly a question of which direction the writers choose to take them.

                                                            Personally, I feel like Aspects work a little better than Castes. An Aspect is some core piece of the Exalt that never changes, and that is exactly what a Lunar needs. They might be a mighty warrior one day, and a king the next. Or perhaps the day after that they are a merchant. A Caste doesn't make sense, but an Aspect creates an anchor. Something for the Lunar to fall back upon, something that relates to everything they do, no matter what they do. It also makes the Chimera a little more horrifying, as they lack such an anchor. As their desires and whims pull them in every direction their personality fragments. Of course, I have no idea what those Aspects might actually be, so ... uh ... I'll get back to you on that.

                                                            In addition, a Caste is an extension of what you already are. However, some of the earlier comments on Lunars called them, "monsters with some humanity in them, or humans with a monster inside", which seems to imply (to me anyway) that a character would not necessarily be the same after their Exaltation. You could make Castes fit, but having what is essentially different kinds of roles for different kinds of monsters ... doesn't really feel right. Whereas an Aspect feels like it could describe what kind of monster you are. You aren't compelled to act like a monster, but it's sitting down there, inside you.

                                                            Now I'm just blabbering. Hopefully some of this was useful.
