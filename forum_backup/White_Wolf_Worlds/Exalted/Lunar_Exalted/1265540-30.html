    The discussion really did appear to lean on the idea that a Lunar would be wrecked in moving about these groups. The potential for a PC being limited by such seemed to me that people were discounting what an individual could bring to the table for their own concepts and ideas. That's why I said that. Not really viewing it as a full-on employee arrangement, but playing on allies and backing for your own ends.

    The nitpicky part about dicecaps is because its the most straightforward way to show off your power. Its also the most simplistic, and easy to say in a discussion that cuts to the heart of the matter. Lunars are nearly equal to Solars in power and their charms reflect this. We've seen like 2 charms for interacting with spirits out of dozens. I figure it can get pretty crazy. Just as they have charms for tearing things apart or disrupting a system I figure they also have them for making things work and

    I'm not really speaking to any of the fuller organizational picture. These are groups that want something and a Lunar is capable of working it to their advantage if they're smart enough. This seems more like you took what I said in a direction I didn't intend.

    Yu-Shan is packed with Divine figures that would be valuable allies or terrible foes. Having connections to them would be interesting for a group to explore at their table. Perhaps I'm just not on the bandwagon of being supernegative with splats interacting, but I think it has legs.

        I think it's mostly a matter of how one phrases it. Don't make it a thing that infringes on a Sidereal space. Don't make it something that tries to validate Lunars by giving them a Sidereal thing. Don't make service and action in Heaven a generic thing, tailor it to the circumstances. Certainly don't phrase it in terms of trying to deal with Lunars by getting to new ones first and training them to be servants.

        And given the tone that is being set up for Lunars in things like this preview... Yu-Shan as a whole shouldn't be Sidereal territory, but it's where Sidereal territory is, and the Sidereals are behind the engine that reigns over Creation trying to continuously murder Lunars, so it ought to feel meaningfully dangerous on both sides. Not to an extreme of "you're at risk every second", but enough that they shouldn't treat it casually. There's too much bad blood on both sides.

        So like, even if a Lunar is there as the guest of or on official business with some ranking god, I think there ought to be an underlying tension that some Sidereals are going to plot to take advantage of an opportunity, or somebody is going to be fairly salty and make a move for personal reasons, because it's likely that in the Fivescore Fellowship, you're never more than two steps away from somebody who lost a colleague to a Lunar in a nasty way.

        And if you're there in secret (or move around secretly in between your official business)... it's probably easier for Sidereals to make dealing with you official business, because that's probably not on for people not doing it officially under any circumstances.

        It makes me think about how... I'm not sure when exactly the idea (or mechanics to back it up) of the Jade Pleasure Dome having wards to detect or fight off intruders, but I think that it's a good idea to make the restrictions on that place enforceable in the face of the Exalted, and I think that they establish a precedent for any official institution of Heaven that would have reason to keep people out or from listening in to have a similar system, that a Lunar might be taking a risk with.

                Originally posted by Sith_Happens
                Well it never hurts to start with the one where they’ll have a house and a real job.


                The phrasing probably needs to be adjusted a bit to be something that doesn't sound like it's being pitched to a modern-day university graduate. Like, assuming "a real job" is really a phrase that anybody would use in Creation (with appropriate corrections for language), why does somebody who's become a Lunar care? Is a real job something that they were brought up to value and want?

                You're not talking about a world where people are going into a capitalist job market, people are generally growing up with the expectation that their job will either be the same thing that their parents did, or the thing that the person they were effectively sold to was teaching them, when it's not a case of "good job, you managed to survive into your twenties, head on down to the docks or the fields every now and then and try to outbid everybody else to get some hard labour for the day or the harvest season, and be prepared for a lot of hungry nights and aimless days in the meantime".

                But okay, having established the background of the majority of people in Creation (at least those who live in a stratified society centred around agrarian military, urban, or religious elites), one might go with the idea of telling them "hey, life always seemed like it would suck, but now here's your prospect to get a really prestigious way of exchanging labour for rewards", but... the Lunar instinctively knows that they're a shapeshifter, right? Like, why should they want a job when they probably have the means of acquiring everything they need or want directly through strength or guile, assuming their power doesn't kind of redefine their priorites in what need or want even is? I think there are good odds that somebody who just acquired the ability to turn into a bird will be interested in seeing what it's like to live as a bird (albeit one that is a lot less susceptible to disease or predators).

                Originally posted by Sith_Happens
                If that’s not enough you just give them the same pitch Sidereals get
                That pitch probably works a lot better for Sidereals because they spend their whole lives prior to becoming Exalted being guided and conditioned towards understanding and accepting the role that is going to be ultimately bestowed upon them. Maybe it would be fairly effective on a specific given Lunar who has had a real void of meaning or drive in their life up to that point, leaving them highly susceptible to a charismatic and impressive person coming in and saying "this thing that I'm giving you will be your purpose now", but I kind of feel like the experience of being a Lunar is going to start giving them alternative answers after a relatively short time.

                Originally posted by Sith_Happens
                you get to visit interesting places and do big important things
                I feel as though a lot of Lunars have the means and the latitude to go to more interesting places and do more things they value personally than Sidereals do. Like, even apart from the constraints of their job, Sidereals aren't as natively good at going just about anywhere that Lunars can (so long as they can find an animal that can naturally enter that environment).

                I mean, hey, if you want to fly somewhere, you don't need to be at higher Essence and sacrifice a valued and loyal pet or one of the few friends who can remember you when you're not around to do it. You've got decent travel options.

                And a decent amount of the central premise of the game kind of relies on the Exalted intuitively understanding a bunch of this.

                Originally posted by Sith_Happens

                Originally posted by Sith_Happens

                know anyway they didn’t grow up here like you did.


                Wait, is this a pitch intended specifically for a Lunar who was Chosen from amongst the small number of humans who actually reside in Yu-Shan?

                If the character concept is "you're the one person ever in the Second Age who had actually grown up in Yu-Shan and then became a Lunar", I can see that as something in which some Sidereals might decide there's a reasonable basis for indoctrination, and it can actually work, and their voices prevail over the ones who would say the situation is ideal for killing them outright, but it still feels like it should be a precarious situation.

                The Silver Pact Lunar might have a harder time selling one on the values of taking one's rightful place as a true Prince of the Earth, unless they try to slip in the idea that the Lunar can bring the people they value in under their own protection.

                Still, Sidereals dealing with the Lunar residing in Heaven within their systems might have a difficult time dealing with the viscerally experienced flashbacks to a bunch of themselves cutting down somebody the Lunar feels they really cared about, and it's probably going to be awkward for Chejop Kejak to explain to the young inductee why seeing him so often provokes the weirdest nagging sense that he's a vile traitor who needs to be destroyed.

                                    Talking about generic incentives for generic Lunars to take up a generic "serving Heaven" job runs into all the problems that pretty much have to exist to explain why large numbers of Lunars aren't an arm of the Heavenly bureaucracy.

                                    On the other hand, that a particular Lunar was successfully recruited to fill a particular position for Heaven with particular incentives that would appeal to that particular character is a perfectly good story/background that shouldn't be ruled out.

                                    For example, a Lunar who happens to be a particular lover of secrets and hidden knowledge could plausibly be recruited to serve the Forbidding Manse of Ivy as an infiltration specialist, alternating field work in Creation (surely there are cases where intervention could use the talents of a shape-stealing trickster) with periods of almost-recreational study of secrets from the archives. Or a social predator and sybarite who enjoys the pleasures available in the Cerulean Lute of Harmony between assignments to make sure star-crossed lovers stay star-crossed.

                                    The important thing is that it be rare, which all the generic arguments do.

                                    (On the antagonist-of-Sidereals side, a Lunar who stole the shape of one of the members of a clan of Heaven's Dragons would potentially be in an interesting position to cause chaos in Yu-Shan.)

                                            Originally posted by Verzio View Post

                                            For example, a Lunar who happens to be a particular lover of secrets and hidden knowledge could plausibly be recruited to serve the Forbidding Manse of Ivy as an infiltration specialist, alternating field work in Creation (surely there are cases where intervention could use the talents of a shape-stealing trickster) with periods of almost-recreational study of secrets from the archives. Or a social predator and sybarite who enjoys the pleasures available in the Cerulean Lute of Harmony between assignments to make sure star-crossed lovers stay star-crossed.
                                            I guess that part of my objection to this is the manner in which the overall game sets up each of the groups of Exalted as having a group identity based on their common history and capabilities, that I've fairly bought into, and in which entering edge cases such as "the Department of Secrets will accept a non-Sidereal who has a general inclination towards concealment and infiltration" kind of grate against it. It's like, that the Department of Secrets is by default a Sidereal thing is a foundation on which I'm going to try and build explanations for them having a very particular need for the very particular capabilities and personal inclinations of Sidereals (especially of that Caste), because otherwise, why not just have it be a mixed group of Exalted altogether?

                                            Much like the manner in which the Silver Pact is a particular Lunar thing, rather than an overall alliance of figures arrayed against the Realm and its Sidereal backers.

                                            It might be a bit contrived, but the initial image is something that I found compelling enough to make it work for.

                                            The other approach would be to take whatever elements might make one assume that Lunars aren't generally part of the Bureau of Destiny and make tension arising from the presence or possibility of them an explicit source of drama in the narrative; actively harnessing the distinct rarity of it, and why that might be so, to strengthen the story a bit.

                                            Originally posted by Verzio
                                            (On the antagonist-of-Sidereals side, a Lunar who stole the shape of one of the members of a clan of Heaven's Dragons would potentially be in an interesting position to cause chaos in Yu-Shan.)
                                            I think that a Lunar infiltrating Yu-Shan like that should be a thing that has happened and can happen, but framed as the sort of thing that tends to need to be a short term operation. I think that's necessary for a sense of consistency both with the people who live in Yu-Shan, and the kinds of enemies or infiltrators that they might have; make the presence of powerful gods felt, and make sense of why powerful figures such as other Celestial Exalted can't quite treat the place as their stomping grounds, even when they might reasonably be assumed to want to.

                                            I think it's a lot like why The Realm is apparently going to address ways that the Dragon Blooded deal with being enemies with shapeshifters, because otherwise that seems like an advantage that should be very large.
