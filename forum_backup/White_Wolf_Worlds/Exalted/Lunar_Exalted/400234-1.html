    Originally posted by SachKreiosLucy4
    If our Anima banners are iconic to our "soul", I always found it silly to limit it to our Spirit Shape.

    Mind you, I don't actually mind the animal stuff. Just pointing out a few quibbles, extrapolating by the text forcing us to stick to our furry critter. Sometimes my No Moons envy the Twilights and Midnights and such who can just pick abstract or occult symbols.
    I was happier once I realized that a Lunar's icon only had to reference their spirit shape, not mimic it exactly.

    I could have the image of a giant cat, or a more abstracted symbol in which, upon close inspection, one can see the Cheshire's grin, as with BlazBlue's Taokaka's iconic display.


    More specifically, with many canon Exalts inheriting their symbols from their previous incarnations (ie, this is a hero reborn, and the Exaltation seeks like heros), it makes it kind of weird (because at some point, someone would notice "yeah, we've had the same 3 Octopus totem Lunars, 5 Spiders, 24 Wolves, 32 Cats and (after reaching all 300) oh shit, our Lunars completely ignore 99% of the animal kingdom because we're all replaying the same old tropes over and over.
    I don't see the worth in getting bent out of shape regarding the shapes that people choose, whether players or characters.

    No one ever said the Lunar Exalted Host has to fairly represent the diversity of the animal kingdom.


    Or equally bad - no one recognizes my anima, because unlike the Solars, my legend isn't eternal in any sense. No one will look upon my anima with hope and go "my long dead wife, reborn" or gaze upon it with dread and realize their enemy is reborn.
    Why not? Why wouldn't this be so?

    .
    All of this to say, I liked Chimera as done in 1st Edition because at least I could grow as a character (and change) and have my allegedly "Spirit" shape reflect those changes (I've never had an interest in forming a Garou tribe and serving as a template for some beastmen). Earning the disrespect of other Lunars (while not being auto-killed as a potential monster) was also nice.
    I'm already considering a character who mixed the totems of Tiger and Bull, resulting in the Square Enix studio's interpretation of the Behemoth... but also a bestial representation of the Japanese oni, a demon said to have the claws and fangs of a tiger and the horns of the bull*. The fact that this is very similar to Western visions of devils is a neat bonus of inspiration as well.

    *The entrance to the demon world was believed to be in the North-West, between the directions associated with the astrological signs of Tiger and Ox. So naturally, the Japanese envisioned the quintessential demon as a mix between those two beasts.

                        Originally posted by SachKreiosLucy4
                        If Panther flares up - the 1st Age Heirophant had the same banner. If Swan goes Totemic - Desus also had the same anima banner. Even with Abyssals and Infernals, some seem to acquire "deathly" or "demonic" renditions of their old selves (and I might be misremembering, but I thought one of them explicitly saw images of 1st Age atrocities they committed in their new banner, sustaining the link between identities even as the symbol changed). Etc, etc.
                        Mehhhhhhh. Meh meh meh. This can go as a default. Whoever decided to write this kind of thing as a normative baseline was trying way too hard to impose their view of the game onto all tables of it, and lucky for them they got a platform to do so as a writer, and that's really a problem because of how it stifles choices.

                        If a player says "my PC is named Swan, and here's his anima banner that I came up with, and A Cool Thing is that it's actually the same anima banner as his prior incarnation from the First Age, Desus, whom everyone thought was a cool guy but he was really a master manipulator and sociopath" that's cool, that's a tie that the player has innovated which can have story effects.

                        It's also a deliberate choice. Which is always better than "hey, Ryan, you said you're playing a diplomat/martial artist named Swan, right? And you came up with his prior incarnation, Desus, as a story point that Swan doesn't really know about right? Well you have the same anima banner as this asshole wife-beater named Desus. Hope you don't flare around any god that might remember what a douchebag 'you' were 3000 years ago! Hyuk hyuk!"

                        I feel the same way about the 2e notion of how Celestial Exaltations generally picked the same kind of people consistently, like if Zenith Exaltation #7 really liked to pick young, assertive shrine maidens so most of its incarnations fit this mould, including the current player. It makes the whole thing way too wooden and mechanical for me, and less interesting than the player discovering that their character's prior incarnation was entirely like them--or entirely unlike them--through the natural course of plot.

                        It's quite different if such a thing is spelled out to players right in the books as a thing they have to intentionally subvert or it's assumed they conform to it.

                        "Chicanery-No: If a player uses this Charm in an abusive or exploitative manner, the ST may punch him right in the goddamn face." --TheDementedOne

                            Originally posted by SachKreiosLucy4
                            [/I]I found this happiness as well, although I'm pretty sure I avoid some Spirit Shape options entirely because of their Anima and Tell effects.
                            Probably, the Tell will go back to its First Edition variability, where it didn't have to be a bestial feature. Like Beast Boy's green coloration.

                            I wouldn't let crippling deformities be Tells, though, unless such a thing specifically prevented the Lunar from ever recovering from that deformity. The devs have said they've found a way to introduce Flaws into the game in a way they like, and I could see a Lunar take a Flaw as a Tell as easily as a physical "mutation" Merit.

                            On which note, I want to be able to make a "mutation" into my Tell. I want to be able to choose a bull's horns, and always have the bull's horns in all of my forms (assuming that's what Tells still do), but almost no one can actually notice my horns without a really good Awareness.

                            (EDIT: And by "mutation/Merit", I mean I want my Tell to be functionally weaponized horns that I can use to gore someone, even if they couldn't see my horns until their blood is coating them.)

                            Which also reminds me, I want a sensible explanation as to why my Lunar Essence is trying to reveal my nature through a Tell while simultaneously trying to hide the Tell it is revealing. That's like someone forcing me to wear a shirt that says "Kick Me", but then also forcing me to put on a jacket to hide the shirt.


                            My go-to default has usually been Octopus. Instead of hentacles, I go off the Ink they produce. A creepy Tell - a shifting shadow resembling an Octopus ink blot. A similarly dark and vague Anima banner (and just like an Octopus, I'm deep inside the display of whirls and such). Plenty of people mistook her Tell for an Abyssal with Resonance bleed.
                            That's cool.

                            Incidentally, if you wanted to make her a Chimera, you could cross in snakes -- or electric eels, or moray eels -- so that each of her tentacles headed in an independent snapping head! Sort of like Ranma 1/2's idea of the Orochi, with a single large head (in this case, the octopus) from which all the other necks and smaller heads extended.

                            Going even further with Chimerism might make each tentacle-head different; one with crushing jaws (moray eel), electric jaws (electric eel), venomous jaws, fire breath, keen senses (because the heads improve your environmental awareness as well as attack options)...


                            It was just an idle thought in the sense of "if one of these Lunar moot things really happened, I can't be the only one who notices 1/3 of our entire Host is composed of 10 or so species.
                            Genre-blindness. When superheroes get together, few of them ever mention how many of them are orphans. Or supermodels who choose to wear spandex.

                            Or white, male and heterosexual.


                            The "equally bad" was the implied opposite - instead of "all Lunars throughout history will be the same 300 totems, repeated forever and ever", it was "your Totem has no connection to your previous lives, so no one will associate my 2nd Age Octopus totem with my 1st Age Anglerfish totem".
                            Lunars do change Castes between incarnations more frequently than other Exalted (by virtue of being the ONLY Exalted who can do so, rather than because of how often it does or doesn't actually happen), but Lunars are not supposed to change icons any more frequently than Solars.

                            With Celestial Exalted, whether you have the same iconic banner as your previous incarnation has always been a matter of player choice, just like "how much of his past life does my character even remember?" Or, for that matter, how many other people remember or care about who your predecessor was. (This is true even of Sidereals, aside from the "no iconic banners at all" thing.)

                            The extent to which the character's past matters to his present has always been entirely a matter of what story the player wants to tell.



                            I'm sure there were some 1st Age Swan-totem Lunar ambassadors - who after the Usurpation and bloodshed and suffering - would ditch the Swan and return as a glorious Lion (still a symbol of society and politics and governance - but now the mark of a King, not a Courtier).
                            For such a character, I would make his spirit shape wholly a Lion... but a lion which is the pure white color of the noble swan, with a mane and tail-tuft of downy feathers. (Wings optional and unnecessary.)

                            The function of his new form clearly announces his new ferocity and warlike methods, but it still harkens back to his previous bearing. It shows that he hasn't completely forgotten the manners and ideals that used to be the cornerstone of his identity.


                            Conversely, I'm sure there has to be some 1st Age Tiger-totem Lunar barbarian - who spent centuries shedding blood, acting rashly, eventually forming relationships and watching them die - who in the 2nd Age resurfaces with a patient, peaceful, thoughtful Turtle-totem with a new life started in the far West.
                            Then his spirit shape is a turtle, but with the stripped patterns that helped the tiger blend in with his surroundings so well, and the sharp claws that wait patiently for the right time to be used.


                            I wish I had cool inspirations for Chimera.
                            I didn't think I had any either, but it seems the ideas keep coming. Especially when answering prompts like yours.

                                                    Raksi's Tell (backwards bending fingers) was a nod over to the Raksha of D&D - horrible, beastlike man eating monsters whom (unlike their real versions in Hindu mythology) all possess backwards bending fingers while her totem form was a nod to the Jungle Book (monkey who lives in an ancient ruined city and kidnaps children).

                                                    Eh, at the time, some people thought it was kind of clever (I did), but that was over a decade ago.

                                                    And yeah, in 1st edition sometimes your Tell was just a weird feature, and while it could be a bestial trait related to your Spirit Shape, it could also be something more mundane, such as an unusual streak of hair or a weird colored eye, or it could be something downright inhuman like Raksi's hands, which would tend to bend backwards when she wasn't paying attention.
