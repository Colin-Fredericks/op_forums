    Okay then. It means being generally smarter.

    I'm just going to quote from the 2nd Edition core book, from the chapter on character creation, p.73-74:
    STEP TWO: ATTRIBUTES
    Once you’ve defi ned your concept and selected your caste and Motivation, it’s time to assign numbers to various traits. First, assign scores to each Attribute. The Attributes define your character’s innate capabilities and aptitudes.
    [...]
    Physical Attributes define your character’s physical potential—how fast she runs, how much she can lift and how healthy she is.
    [...]
    Mental Attributes define your character’s intellectual capacity—how quickly she thinks, how keen her senses are and how smart she is.

    P.74:
    STEP THREE: ABILITIES
    Abilities describe what your character knows and can do, what he’s learned rather than his natural potential.

    Mechanically as well as thematically, the main difference between Lunars and Solars is that Lunars’ powers come from innate potential (represented mechanically as attributes), while Solars’ come from learned skill (represented as abilities). It's the source of their beautifully dichotomous yet complementary natures. Like sun and moon.

    When a Solar enhances a roll of (Intelligence + Investigation) using one of his charms (like First Investigation Excellency), what he’s doing is enhancing an ability, investigation. When a Lunar enhances the same roll with First Intelligence Excellency, she is enhancing her intelligence.

    That’s why Solars get more ability dots and can gain them more easily and have ability specialties, whereas Lunars get more attribute dots and can increase them more easily and have attribute specialties. Lunars are generally better than solars in terms of attributes - stronger, more charismatic, more intelligent. Because those are all measurements of innate potential. But Solars are supreme in the area of ability. They are better warriors, better diplomats, better scholars.

    (Attribute + Ability) is the key format for the game, and also the key to Solars’ relationship with Lunars in 2nd edition.

                                                        Originally posted by FromBeyond View Post
                                                        If by being good at philosophy we're assuming being able to come up with ideas then being less intelligent certainly helps. If you don't know a lot, you're free to come up with new things for yourself. You're not distracted by things you know. You're also more concerned with practical matters!
                                                        As mentioned, knowledge and intellect are different things. Frankly, I would say that creativity is different from intelligence, but for our purposes, they're grouped under the same Attribute, so we'll go with that. You could very easily have a character with a great deal of knowledge (high Lore/Occult), but low Int, meaning that he can recite a lot of facts and figures, but can't do much with them, creatively. Contrariwise, you could have another character with low Lore/Occult, and maybe even little Linguistics, to reflect illiteracy, but very high Int, and that would be your unschooled, natural philosopher who can come up with unique, original ideas that stymie the learned folk.
                                                        Someone with low intelligence caught up in practical matters is more likely to just operate by rote, and not THINK about anything as he does so. It's the intelligence trait that lets him extrapolate a greater truth from the mundane world around him (remember the bath tub "eureka!" story)...
                                                        So I don't see low int making one a better philosopher. If the argument is that there are more humans who are philosophers than Exalted simply because there are always more OF them, then yeah, sure. But I'm not sure how that equates to them being *better* at it...whatever that means (being able to come up with ideas, argue them or write papers about them? Exalted have charms and excellencies for all that).
                                                        ​ Then again, what IS philosophy, in the context of Creation, anyway?
                                                        In RL, philosophy is a thing because, so far as we can tell, there is no actual objective truth to life and reality. Or if there is one, then it is hidden (or at least, not obvious) and must be puzzled out. Creation, as the name implies, is an (artifically? supernaturally?) constructed reality, and the nature and purpose of life and reality are quite literally a matter of record, that can be looked up in Heaven, as set by the creators of all. Given that, should "philosophy" as we know it, even be a thing? Or if it does exist, what form is it likely to take in Creation?
