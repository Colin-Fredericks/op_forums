                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                            Eh, it depends. I once had an image in which that Lookshy weapon to turn everything to crystal was made by a Solar who had become obsessed with the idea of fixing everybody into place in highly durable forms, as that was the only way to keep them all safe. Her Limit Trigger was related to people suffering while she wasn't able to keep an eye on them, and she'd had a long battle against trying to keep everything under as strict an observation as possible, struggling with the difficulty and complexity of that when they kept moving.

                                            (So many Limit Triggers have always been about innocents suffering, why do so few people incorporate that into their image of the Great Curse?)
                                            By way of trying to explain this, tentatively I think it may because you have in a sense in Exalted really got two competing versions of the Solar Great Curse going on that led to the Usurpation.

                                            The Curse as operates within characters; where an excess of overflowing virtue bursts out leads to acts of unbalanced extremity that lacks moral wisdom, and are so inevitably destructive to the world around them. (I'll call it A).

                                            This is the version of the Curse that's suited to the heroes the Solars are for PCs; great heroes, who have great potential for epic tragedy where they are undone by the contradictions and unforeseen consequences of their own great virtue.

                                            The Curse as originally described in its first, pithiest and most compelling form of imagery within Exalted; a kind of dark forces that ate at the hearts of the Solar Exalted, turning them to impiety, hubris, decadence, dishonor, selfishness, vanity, tyranny and to singularly fail in their position as leaders of mankind and champions of the Unconquered Sun. (I'll call it B).

                                            This is the Curse of what I would argue here is Exalted's wider narrative about the nature of power and accountability, which stresses that unchecked and unaccountable authority will inevitably turn to moral corruption, abuse of power, and self serving ends, until it is destroyed or reformed by those underneath it by making it accountable and calling it to account. (No matter how those authorities are chosen; for their excellence, from the common people and ranks of the poor, from those who have known adversity, &c.)

                                            Manifestations of the GC, as it caused the downfall of the Solars as depicted in the setting, seem to me to tend to try to square the two streams, and while these grand excesses of compassion are easy to work with Great Curse A, there is something perhaps that is dissonant about them with B...?

                                                11-15-2018, 04:34 PM
                                                Well, in unchecked and unaccountable leaders, I think compassion can lead to devastating consequences. They do things they think are good, but they make mistakes. This happens in even leaders who are accountable, but it's worse when they're not, with no-one to point out their mistakes. This could be a matter of economic or government reform based on ideology or lack of information. It could be liberal interventionism, where desire to stop the suffering in a neighbouring country leads to a war that goes horribly, horribly wrong.
                                                And this isn't even getting into utopian leaders who radically reform society to, say, save the poor or their oppressed ethnic group, or whatever, but in the revolution they unleash a terror.


                                                The thing about Curse B, tbh, is I never really saw the need to make it, well, a Curse. One of my friends told me Grabowski indicated that he essentially put the Great Curse in as an explanation for what he felt was just a realistic depiction of a super-powered monarchy, but he didn't want people arguing that the Solars were too awesome to end up as the bad guys, so he made it a Curse.
                                                (I'm not sure if that's actually true, but that's what I heard.)

                                                And of course, there's no actual rules for it, which then gets into the argument where people go "okay, what is the Great Curse supposed to be like, let's look at Solars in the Core book, oh it's A. So the High First Age was A."

                                                TBH, I think if Grabowski had never written the Great Curse, the Usurpation could still work fine. But it would probably lead to us characterising its lead-up differently.
                                                (And, I suppose, the Great Curse ought to make the Solars more sympathetic to players. I'm not sure it really makes much difference in practice though.)
