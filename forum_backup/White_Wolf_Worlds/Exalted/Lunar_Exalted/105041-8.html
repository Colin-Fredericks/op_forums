                        Originally posted by Jen View Post
                        Soooooo, what do people here think about Lunar's Territory charms, on one hand, their Patron is a crazy homeless hobo in Yu-Shan, on the other hand, many animal does have the habit of pissing on tree to ward off potential rivals ;D
                        I feel as though the broad concept might need to be adjusted a bit to fit the paradigm of many Lunars not wanting to be tied down to particular places or resources.

                        What did they have, a Charm to instantly designate an area as being Owned by them? I feel that one was a bit of an answer to a problem that wasn't already present (I'd assume the regular rules for asserting Ownership should be enough), and a bit of a speed bump for that. I don't think Charms that let you shift your senses around a territory you own are too bad.

                        That Charm Sleeping Dragon Awakens... I'm not sure how it would be adjusted, it felt a bit too intricately embedded in the Overdrive concept... maybe something less tied to defending a territory and more to hunting an enemy, might give it a bit more flexibility, give Lunars a bit more incentive to take the fight to others. It might help create an opening for using other Charms; there's still that idea of Charms that require a setup before they can be fired off, other than their mote costs. I wonder if that might be a way to holistically differentiate Lunars from Solars mechanically; their equivalent to Solars having lots of relatively narrow Charms that synergize well in free combos being having some fairly big, powerful Charms that require a fair amount of Lunar flavored setup to use, but it's a setup aided by their narrow Charms.

                        Does it still feel like Silver Solars if your ultimate attack, even if it takes the form of a super-powerful punch, requires a lot of stalking and hit-and-run strikes, or for you to have waited and taken a bunch of hits in the fight to pump your blood up or the like? Something that, regardless of its actual vector, you can't fire off until either your opponent is really scared (and bloodied up a bit) or you're really angry (and bloodied up a bit)? Might compensate for it a smidge by having more power than a comparable Solar Charm, but that's not strictly necessary.

                        It does put me in mind of that example Holden once came up with while describing a comparison between Essence 5 and 6 Charms.

                        When one considers developer and writer comments about approaching Charmsets holistically, about incentivizing distinct playstyles, and puts that against what we know or can infer from the system as it stands, it seems like an easy way to pull away from Silver Solars, or to otherwise differentiate between Exalts with comparable mechanical effects, is to integrate a combination between Charms that need you to act like the Exalt in order to work, and Charms that help you act like the Exalt (or possibly that boost up your setup while you act in that way).

                        Ultra Deadly Attack Charm that Requires Stalking + Fairly Good Stalking Charm + Charm that boosts your Return Rate for Stalking = Act more like a Lunar than a Solar?

                        Or even, dare I say, Ultra Deadly Attack + Shapeshifting? Where the transformation is not the form of the attack, but part of the vector that lets you build up to it?

                        I'm just thinking of the tyrant lizard statblock we were given, which was pretty minimalist, and yet I feel would still give a pretty strong impression of actually fighting a tyrant lizard, where between the need for specific stunts to recharge certain moves and a statement on the conditions under which it will flee, it manages to dodge the sense of just being generic stats tacked on to something that one might worry will fight optimally. It's not much, but it still feels like it'll make tyrant lizards fight like tyrant lizards. Lunars, and Exalted in general, are liable to have considerably more.

                        Incidentally, another thing Lunars should be able to do; fight using the capabilities of certain animals without certain particular limitations. Imagine how scary a tyrant lizard that attacks like an intelligent human would be? I'm seeing Titans going around with their hands on the back of their necks here...

                        I'm wondering about that being kind of converse as well, now; Intelligence Charms that aid you by reshaping your mind such that you fight more like a beast or devil (represented mechanically by certain modified mental responses, different stunt benefits, what have you).

                                Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                So... Lunars, anyone?
                                Aha! Now you know how it feels! Behold the sorcerous power of Threefold Binding of the Spotlight!

                                I'm gonna be honest, I think Lunars are a bit out of place in Exalted, in ways that go beyond just the usual place-in-the-setting stuff we talk about. The core of Exalted draws on two things: Swords-and-sandals fantasy and wuxia action flicks. Those generally do not have a lot of werewolves; while they do have shapeshifters, it's more often presented as something akin to Sorcery than the "core" of your power the way, say, a martial-arts master or a supremely talented swordsman has their thing as the core of their power. Even the Monkey King (and the other characters in Journey to the West), shapeshifting is treated as a sorcerous-style art they study rather than what they are.

                                Animal-themed tricksters are a thing, but if you want me to be honest, I don't think they fit well into most of the inspirations Exalted draws on. Having Hercules meet an old master or go up against an empire that wronged him feels appropriate to me; somehow, having him (or any of those other factions) face Anansi feels out of place -- the inspirations Lunars draw on are just a bit disconnected, thematically.

                                I think that this is why 1e went for making them "barbarians" and "monsters". Those are things that fit well into the Swords-and-Sandals world of Exalted. I suspect that that's also why the devs are looking in that direction again despite the risks.

                                I think you have to focus on answering that core problem of "how do they fit into the setting?" before you can get the answer to what they can do.

                                                Ok, let's slow down for a bit here. I know that Lunars should be able to jump really high, punch really hard, be very persuasive or smart and so on and so on, but I want to know why are they able to do those things.

                                                Like, with Solars I know that they can punch people relly hard, because they're very good at punching people. I know that a Dragon Blooded can jump 20ft high, because he can move like the air. I know a Sidereal can solve difficult mystery case, because he can gain impossible insight from the stars.

                                                But why is Lunar punching you hard? Why is he able to dodge like the water? Why is he speaking in such a way that you're compelled by his words?

                                                Part of me wants to say: Lunars punch hard, simply because they're really freakin' strong. They can dodge and dance gracefully, because they're simply really agile. At least, that's what I instinctively imagine being atribute-based Exalts means for Lunars.

                                                But how do you begin at "being magically strong/agile/resistant/charismatic/manipulative/attractive/smart/cunning/perspective" and arrive at changing into a flock of ravens.

                                                I just can't draw the connection. I'm left in the dark, nebulous place where I have a good start in the form of baseline atributes and a finish in the form of shapeshifting, but I just can't see the middle. If I can't see the middle, I can hardly come up with examples that are fluid and coherent, not just random ideas out of nowhere.

                                                Honestly, I wonder if it wouldn't make more sense to leave charms as expressions of atributes and change Lunar shapeshifting into astrology-like gimmick only available to Lunars. At least there would be a clear reasoning behind it.
