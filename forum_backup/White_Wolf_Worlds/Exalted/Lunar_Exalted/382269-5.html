People can do tricksters in any splat, but want to find more magic in the splat that's especially useful for someone who's keen on trickster-y stuff. Depending on how seeing through Lunar transformations works out in EX3, those'd already be some fairly potent options in their tricksters' tool kits.

                                    I don't think we disagree on the big picture. If Lunars no longer have any real trickster elements to them, I imagine they won't be billed as tricksters anymore. That'll be the incongruity resolved.
                                    EDIT: I mean, I'll be disappointed if "no real trickster elements" is a thing, as that always seemed like such a rich area, and was one of the big draws to the splat for me. But I at least expect the devs will be forthright about that if it becomes the direction they're going (with the caveat that different people may be thinking of different things when they say and hear, "trickster" ).

                                    I'll note that from what they've said are on the outs, though, "trickster" isn't necessarily one of the things they're ditching.* While it's been a while since we had such a statement, and the context was admittedly not gunning for it in particular, we've had this statement since Holden's giant barbarian post:

                                    Originally posted by Holden
                                    They are, however, intended to partake of stylistic barbarism-- they're designed for it from the ground up and many of their problems in Second Edition came from a deliberate attempt to reject this, I think as a result of conflating it with the other two kinds of barbarism. The fact of the matter is, to the degree that the Exalted splats echo other source material, Lunars are werewolves, tribal legends, and pagan culture-heroes. They're supposed to look rough-edged compared to the more refined Solars; their Attribute magic is supposed to look raw and powerful compared to the deft, polished sheen of Ability magic; their shapeshifting points to a primal font of imagery and aptitude-- their archetypes are "warrior, trickster, shaman."
                                    * Distorting Creation and illusions are indeed straight-up not in their bag of tricks, per John's remarks. As such, Lunar tricksters would be executing the idea in a different fashion than, say, the Fair Folk would.
