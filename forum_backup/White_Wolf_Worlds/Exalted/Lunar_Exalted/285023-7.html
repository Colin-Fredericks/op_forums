        A complex Realm is not one that doesn't need help, it's one that needs help more sophisticated than taking out the few bad apples so the rest of the cart can stay upright.

        Saying "killing this one villain and then striking a Conan pose doesn't make anything substantially better" doesn't mean the villain was good, nor that it wasn't a decent thing to destroy them, it just means that the story doesn't end there.

        A similar thing can go with figures like Raksi; whatever she's like as a person, if killing her means that there's a power vacuum in the locality and the Silver Pact that Realm forces and other Lunar elders and various other figures try to fill, or the Silver Pact has a sudden deficit in power, or some of her own sorcerous insights are lost beyond recollection... well, depending on the type of character you're playing, that might give you pause in killing her, or want to set things up against the future in order to minimise the impact of her death, or even make a moral stand in saying "whatever the other consequences, she's a terrible and dangerous person who needs to go down now" doesn't mean she's actually secretly good, or that killing her is a bad thing. It doesn't even mean you can't go straight to that step, rather than have it come in at the ending. It just means that it won't be a matter of throwing her down the exhaust shaft, and then skip through a montage of scenes of everybody joyfully celebrating how they're instantly free and their lives have improved.

        Although even then, there doesn't necessarily have to be chaos. You can plausibly have it that, between the other things going on in the setting and some of the vagaries of chance, other forces don't get involved and the local situation settles down rather quietly. That's something I'd say happens if the game really doesn't want to stay in this place.

        But even that is at least acknowledging that there are bigger things to the setting, and how characters are thankful at the good fortune of having managed to dodge th worst of it.

        You'll hardly ever get a character who is so terrible that the Circle can smash them down with nary a twinge of conscience and also lacks any connection to anything else at all in the setting that there can't be bigger implications to their presence and absence.

        Even in cases where you do, where you've got somebody like Dio Brando, Akuma from Street Fighter, the Beast from Kung Fu Hustle or the bad guys from The Man With the Iron Fists... they should be there because they're compelling and fearsome, not rendered simplistic so that they can be easy targets.

        I wouldn't even take that tack towards roaming behemoths (or some of the smarter ones, like Arad the Hunter). If it was me, I'd approach them in terms of the Crystalline Entity from Star Trek.

            I think Lunar elders in general...

            I don't think they should necessarily be intrusive presences, or commander figures like Sidereal elders or the Deathlords, but I do think they should have a sense of presence and scope such that other Lunars are aware of at least the outlines of their plans, and it shapes their perception of the world. Like, even if Ma-Ha-Suchi never shows up in a game, or never has any interactions with your character, I'd say a Lunar will often look at the River Province and add a little sign saying "this space reserved for new goatfolk parking lot in the next ten years". That's the impression I get from some of the in and out-of-character discussion of the Black Lion.

            Maybe also defines some of the spaces they know they can move readily in. When you're frequently hunted by the Realm, and you now the Realm can't often approach the sphere of Ma-Ha-Suchi or Raksi or the Caul, those places are going to look very attractive as hubs to issue forth from and fall back to.

            Reminds me of this amusing image I once read of 17th-18th century European armies as being like a parrot shifting their legs along the bars of a cage, where the bars are the innovative cannon-proof fortresses that were the strategic lynchpin of the time, and the cage is traditionally disputed territory.

            That and how they're still a major force and figures in the rhetoric of the Silver Pact. It's a matter of how, when there's a lot of talk of reclaiming their stolen entitlement to the Realm, it helps to have some circulation of the influence of the people who saw it first hand. Multi-generational revolutionary movements often place a lot of stock in the people who were part of the first wave.
