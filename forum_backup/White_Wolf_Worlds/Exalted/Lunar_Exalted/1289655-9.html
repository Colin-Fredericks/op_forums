                        That's fair, it was a ways back, I'll do the wading:

                        Originally posted by Measure of Hope View Post

                        My primary problem with Solar Bond as presented is that for no reason I can discern, the text constrains all Solar-Lunar pairings to having originated with an arranged marriage, which makes me viscerally uncomfortable. That's just not very romantic. Neither is it very platonic. It's like the worst of both worlds. It just makes the story of the bond more political than spiritual. I guess the intent was to make the bond mutually consensual, and give players more agency over whether this story element comes into play for their character, which is definitely good, but the execution just presents different problems, one of which is, ironically, player agency.

                        Maybe a lot of people won't care about the origin of their bond, and it certainly doesn't dictate how the pair relates to each other now, but what if I wanted to specify the origins of my reincarnating power-couple as anything other than this one narrative we've been given? What if I wanted to tell literally any other story about the relationship that led their fates to intertwine? Why are those stories off the cards? If the bond needs to be the result of a singular effort to tie the Solars and Lunars together (and I guess I don't have a problem with a peace deal being the impetus for that), why did they only decide to sanctify this alliance with marriages? I mean, what happened to all the Solars and Lunars at the time who were asexual/aromantic or just, you know, already happily married to someone else, like a mortal? Did they just not get to participate in this whole eternal-soul-union thing, even if they wanted to participate in some other way, by swearing oaths as blood-siblings or something?

                        "Sorry honey, I love you, but we gotta divorce or I'm going to miss out on having a soul-mate for all of time, so I'm ditching you to marry my best friend. No, we can't just swear to be BFFs. Gotta get hitched. It's a whole thing."

                        I know it's easy to golden-rule this, but because it's so easy (ie, nothing seems to break by changing it), I don't see the reason for the restriction in the first place.
                        And further in:

                        Originally posted by Measure of Hope View Post
                        It's just so sad and frustrating that this part of my character's story has been taken out of my hands for no reason. Yes, the current incarnations can relate to each other on whatever terms now, but telling the story of who your character and their mate were to each other when they first bonded so long ago (lovers, comrades, teacher and student, parent and adoptive child, enemies who became best friends) is cool and thematic and mythic and I just ... can't? Unless it's this one thing? If I want to give context to the bitter rivalry between a Lunar and her mate by hearkening back to what brought them together in the first place, I only have this one story to play with, and I don't see why.
                        Basically it's a needless restriction that warps the kind of past-life stories that can be told about the bond and it would be very easy to fix with one line acknowledging other sorts of oaths.

                            I don't think it's hard to look upon the origin story of the Bond as something that intertwined political and spiritual considerations in a manner that I think is very fitting to setting and character (I think those two facets are tied together in the setting and its history more often than they're separate), and I don't see it as mutually exclusive from romantic elements for reasons including the possibility that of the approximately six hundred Exalted who first got involved in the process, at least some of them pushed for it to take the form of a ritual marriage because they had fallen in love with each other, as well as many many cases in which romance came after the marriage.

                            Which has often been the case, historically, with arranged marriages. Which remains the case with a lot of arranged marriage in the societies in which it is practiced. Like, arranged marriage is still heavily practiced in India, as just one example.

                            I think some elements of the setting are stronger when they're given an originating narrative than when they're just left up in the air. Sure, those narratives may end up bugging some people, but then just... change them.
