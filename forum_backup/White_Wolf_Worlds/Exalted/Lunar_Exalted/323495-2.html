            I prefer pushing that further. Not, "this enemy is powerful and deadly and also cunning" but instead "this enemy is so cunning it doesn't matter that we are more powerful and deadly". An enemy that can out think and out plan the players, and I mean legitimately not metagaming information he shouldn't have access to. They know how to get inside your head and manipulate you. The reason I find this so effective is your average player is taken far out of their comfort zone. Unlike the OP idea of a monster thats completely superior there is a certain comfort in confronting your enemy. ultimately that monster intends to fight you. But take that away, an enemy that can't beat you in a fight will keep himself out of your reach. Mess with the psyche of the players, erode their sense of security and make them turn on each other. Best part is this removes that annoying issue of players killing an antagonist before he can get his plans going.

                            If you want a Lunar Intelligence charm set, rather than physically shifting their bodies, have them shift a mentality which in turn gives them a bonus.

                            Consider that Hannibal Lector displays a great deal of traits associated with Lunars, especially elder lunars. He loves the hunt, he considers himself a refined monster, he eats his prey, he camouflages his true nature in public to better stalk his quarry and he tends to defeat his foes by out planning them rather than out fighting them.

                            The mind is the most adaptable part of our bodies, which in turn makes it our most powerful weapon. Yet the Lunars seem stuck in the concept that only the body can change while the mind stays intact with the exception of limit break issues.

                            Though we have to be honest that in the end a charm bonus or ability is still just that. So the fluff and mechanics for it have to be very carefully worded, lest people just start saying the charms are copy & paste of a different splat. Or in the case of 2nd Edition Lunars, just saying "solar core page x" to save on word count.

                            For instance take the Solar charm "Know the Soul's Price", which allows you to understand what a given PC or NPC will break their morals to achieve (being bribed or what they desire most, etc). Well a Lunar charm could do the exact same thing, but the wording would have to reflect the fact that everything breaks down to animalistic/basic terms. So rather than talking about bribery or a desired goal, the charm would let the Lunar know the target wants a mate, desires security or wishes to outdo a rival, etc., etc.

                            This goes back to the idea that to understand a thing, you know what it is capable of. This in turn allows you to hunt your prey successfully.

                            In truth so much of our modern world can be broken down into a half dozen or so basic drives & concepts, the rest is just fluff. In short Lunars and their charm set should be about tossing the fluff and working with basics.

                            It's not that a person really wants gold (inedible, too soft and rare to make a shelter, too heavy for clothes, etc) but what they want is what gold represents, which is a medium to trade for all those things if they don't have them. A Lunar scoffs at the notion because they can just adjust themselves on the fly, but this means they would not value the equipment that gold would buy nor the gold itself.

                            Now just scale that mentality to deal with artifacts, empires or religion and that is a good place to start with Lunars. Admittedly such a play style would mostly likely be a tad bit too philosophical for many people, so it might not work out so well.
