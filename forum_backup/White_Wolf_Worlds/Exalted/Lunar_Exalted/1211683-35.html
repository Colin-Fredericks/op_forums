                    On the subject of what Lunars are for, I was thinking through some of the narrative roles of the mythic trickster, and it brought me back to a key point that might often be forgotten for how fresh it is; that what the Lunars are is explicitly supposed to be something that has changed across the ages.

                    I came upon the idea of Lunars doing the trickster thing in terms of challenging and violating taboos as a way of provoking evaluation and criticism of them, as well as providing a vicarious thrill to those that witness and hear of their exploits. It's something that I think could emerge from Lunar actions in the Time of Tumult while not being an essential function of them, but couldn't shake the sense that it would be strongly fitting to them in the Old Realm; as in, I could see that as being the thesis of one of their old Castes.

                    I often try to think of some things that give real weight to the idea that their old Castes are gone, and that this means there's something very different between the Lunars that a new Solar might meet and those that they'll see in visions of past lives, and putting Castes in the context of their relationship with the Old Realm and the Solars themselves seems like a good way to do it. Sacred guardian beasts, challenging tricksters, seekers of valuable knowledge and lore through predatory guile ala Anansi, things like that.

                    It emphasises that what Lunars are is fluid. They got to be for something in the First Age, because that was a context that afforded that. If one wants to know what they're for in the Age of Sorrows, their Castes are a good place to start, because the thing they have in common are survival and exertion in a world that is hostile to them.

                    Why should the Lunars be about protection against and marshalling of the Fair Folk? The world has given them no time or place for that, and so the world doesn't even deserve that effort. The world drove them out into the cold, and so they dropped having elaborate social functions for the sake of thriving out there and coming back with a vengeance.

                    I think if the book plays its cards right, it can make that premise into one as compelling as any of the others, while still being highly distinct to these Exalted. I think Second Edition was kind of trying to acknowledge that the station of the Lunars had changed with the Thousand Streams River, but I ultimately view that approach as a half-measure; it all revolved around wanting to make it that Lunars had been this particular thing (Stewards), and despite all that they were put through that they were still trying to be the same thing. I think that could only ever end up lacking credibility because it couldn't escape the sense that trying to be Stewards under the shadow of Realm dominance and the Wyld Hunt only ever meant that they weren't doing very well at it anymore.

                    Whereas the First Edition presentation... I'm going to combine things that I know from the books I have with a bit of second-hand hearsay and personal speculation on the Lunar book. Because for all that people rag on their being made playable at just the last minute, I think they also suffer from the fact that the First Edition core's presentation of the Exalted as a whole was still working some of the kinks out, and often portraying a rather simplified model. I'm basing this on the way that the core ascribed Solars and Lunars functions in the First Age based on the literal time of day; that when the sun went down, action and authority went to the Lunars while the Solars would be asleep. It was a very 'fantasy kingdom' take on the First Age, woven into a picture of the general Exalted that was still a bit crude and a little more generic. The gameline got to elaborate on the picture of Solars and First Age later on, making them more well-rounded and sophisticated, but I'd say that the focus on what the Lunars were in the Time of Tumult left little importance for thinking much on what they used to be.

                    So you got something like "the Lunars were kind of sort of seconds-in-command to the Solars, don't think about it too hard it doesn't matter anymore", with all significance of that swept away by their transition into barbaric overlords and ideological primitivists. To my eyes, the bones of the idea of Exalted that have been changed drastically by the turn of ages was still there, and I do think it's a very interesting starting point, but the overlaying details remained very broad and arch. Probably not helped by reports of a divided vision between the writing teams.

                    So my point is, take the premise in the original version, and polish it up a bit. Give a bit more thought and detail into what they were before the Usurpation, without giving any more detail than necessary. Make the focus of their transition be that the loss of purpose and position is the point, rather than being a motive for tearing down everything else as well. But you still get to keep them wanting to tear something down, just a bit more coherently motivated.

I mean by design they were created as spies, assassins and infiltrators, so their abilities include shape-shifting adaptability and toughness. After Primordial War they could use this abilities to become travelers who discover new places and develop new nations and whole new ecosystems.
                            It's a lot like them being dedicated opponents of the Fair Folk; it's definitely a thing that Lunars could and would do, as an application of their capabilities and temperament, but making it the definition of what Lunars are would run into issues of being something not very distinct to them and unmoors them a bit from the heart of the setting.

                            Still, it also sounds a bit to me like one of the things that one of their First Age Castes might have done. Best with other elements that would distinguish them from the Eclipse Caste; that is to say, the things that the Eclipse Caste has in addition to being travellers and explorers is different from what this Lunar Caste might do.

                            I think it's always been the wrong approach to come at Lunars from the perspective of looking for ends that can be given to shapeshifting (often somewhat circuitous ones) and designate that as the Lunar Thing, rather than focusing on the side where being shapeshifters gives them the freedom to pursue lots of different things to be.

                            Hmm, that's giving me a notion of how to picture multiple facets to First Age Castes; a balance between a dedicated function to the Realm (even if it's a somewhat iconoclast or outsider one, like the idea of the challenging trickster) against an application of their own yearnings for freedom.
