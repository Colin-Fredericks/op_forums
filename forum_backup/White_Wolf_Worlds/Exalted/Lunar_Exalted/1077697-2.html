    Moving on...


    Wicked Witch Eyes is a gloriously cheesily named Charm that’s basically a crappy version of All-Encompassing Sorcerer's Sight with a neat (?) little benny tucked on top of it, allowing you to use unrelated skills to explain certain phenomena. The Charm also writes about the setting implications of this, which is a cute little 2Eism.

    Outlander-Detecting Intuition is the kind of multi-purpose Charm I’d like Lunars to have. It lets you identify Outsiders (whatever they are) (truly, my unwillingness to look up what the incredibly dense and technical Charm texts were talking about is probably the number one reason why I could never get into 2E), things touched by Outsiders, the direction of Creation’s borders from Pure Chaos, and fixed entrances and exits from Creation within a generous radius.

    World-Warden Onslaught is an Overdrive Charm and therefore kinda pointless in the 3E paradigm. Also, in general, I’m not much of a fan of Lunars getting Charms dedicated to killing their own version of „sorta-kinda Creatures of Darkness but not really”.

    Cynical Glance Internalisation is an interesting Charm, conceptually. It helps you defend against social influence, because your senses are so sharp, your interlocutor can’t help but seem flawed and imperfect under their scrutiny. I like this one, although under the 3E system, it’d be pretty hard to do any other way than „in response to a social influence attempt, you can reflexively pick up a negative Intimacy related to the character who initiated it”, which is kinda thin and probably should exist as a more generalized effect in another tree.
    …Well actually, it could also allow you to calculate an already-existing negative Intimacy’s Resolve bonus as one level higher than it really is, which is less of a double-edged sword than picking up new Intimacies verbatim, but then it’s pretty much just a very situational excellency. Which is fine, I guess? I mean, Shadow Over Day is basically the same effect except it doesn’t even count as non-Charm bonus, but neither is it subject to the same limitations, and also Lunar Charms should be more robust and less fiddly than Solar ones, which this is definitely not.

    Mad-Moon Feeling Flux is a weird Charm with fluff that is similarly masturbatory to Impossibility’s Arrival’s (it’s ’s’s all the way down). It… enhances what we’d call an Instill action, but then doesn’t really care about the Intimacy it instilled, instead forcing the target to act contrary to their actual Intimacies unless they spend a bunch of WP. It’s… pretty weird. I’m all about a large part of Lunar Perception being incomprehensible revelation stuff, but forcing others to confront your impossible revelations doesn’t flow naturally from Perception’s internal logic (marking it as a high-Essence effect that requires significant investment instead of being available five Charms into the tree), and even for that concept, this is a fairly poor implementation.

    Mythic Outsider Extrusion feels pretty redundant with DBT and Hybrid Body Rearrangement. Also, why is this in Perception? Even in TAW logic, this might as well be in Stamina.

    Walking Argent Impossibility is an interesting Charm. It basically lets you sacrifice a shape for an effect similar to Vanishing from Mind’s Eye Method which also places you outside of fate. Nothing about this Charm fits my vision of Lunars, but it’s a very cool effect that’s really good at communicating the themes and aesthetics its creator wanted to convey.

    Unreal Illumination Escape is a perfect defense branching off of the previous Charm. It’s a cool 2E Charm that probably wouldn’t be very wise to put into 3E, even if it aligned with the themes and aesthetics I’m going for.

    Unwoven Weaver’s Art is another WAI upgrade, effectively making the Charm re-usable and tossing some Training effects on top of it.

    Reality-Flaying Stalker is a spirit-killing Charm with Essence 5 as prerequisite, which is… a bit late for that, isn’t it? It’s also a Charm for re-establishing surprise. I like the paradigm – the multi-functionality and how this wider cluster of Charms upgrades a flashy mid-tier capstone effect in various ways; this mirrors the general design principles I outlined earlier.


    And this is the end of Perception in TAW! Next step: the Irked Charms, which will actually let me talk about and analyze mechanics in a meaningful way! Yay! Also: wow, that was quick. Say whatever you will about pretentiousness and the utter stupidity of the Doomsiren Horse, these Charms could at least be read at a rather breezy pace.

        Time to read Irked’s homebrew!


        Compassionate Mirror Nature is an interesting Charm. I think obviating the need to kill and eat people if you want to steal their shape in a single Essence 1 Charm purchase really undersells how monstrous Lunars should be. On the other hand, not letting people circumvent this limitation supposedly forces the Lunar infiltrator to be a serial killer (an opinion I personally disagree with, but I can see where it’s coming from; also, a hard limitation like „you can NEVER take a human shape by any other way than killing someone” feels incongruent with the fluid and adaptive nature of Lunar Essence).
        You could make the shapeshifting temporary and conditional (say, you knock someone out but can only wear their skin for as long as they’re unconscious and undiscovered), but then it feels more like a disguise with a really weird and inconvenient limitation than actual shapeshifting. Alternatively, I could take a hint from the name, and somehow tie it to a Principle related to compassion or pacifism – eg. by saying you can’t hold more than (relevant Intimacy’s strength) shapes taken via this Charm. In any case, this should be at least Essence 2.
        Fundamentally though, I think my problem is with the weird logic of this Charm. It has nothing to do with Perception whatsoever, and, to an extent, it feels like it’s mocking the idea of the Sacred Hunt. You’re still fundamentally employing the same tool – violence and force – to get your cool mechanical benny, but this Charm feels like it’s saying „oh don’t worry, it’s okay now, since you’re not killing them”. It’s a copout, and it isn’t really rooted in any sort of mythically resonant framework – it’s about as cartoony as vegan vampires. I much prefer MonkofLords’ idea of a Perception-based form-taking trick based on extended observation.

        Interestingly, Irked pegs Honing the Stolen Form as Essence 2. I think this should be available from Essence 1, it’s unlikely to break anything. Or maybe I just haven’t thought hard enough about ways to break things with it!

        Laurels-and-Ivy Technique and Moon-and-Earth Song are just goofy.

        Taste of Luna’s Champions arguably runs into the same problem as Compassionate Mirror Nature: Lunars supposedly master the forms they steal, and time limits run counter to that idea. Yet this one feels cool and thematic to me. Maybe because it’s less about allowing others to master a shape, and more like it’s still your mastery, communicated through the medium of blood, coursing in their veins but still fundamentally belonging to you? Or maybe I’m just post-rationalizing because I really like the primal „blood and guts” aesthetics this Charm is drawing on.

        Flaw-Finding Examination is a nice multi-purpose Charm with some mechanical issues. It’s very likely overpriced, I see no reason why it shouldn’t be Mute, and going by a strict reading of the Charm text, it doesn’t provide any benefit on an extended repair roll unless you do literally nothing else between invoking the Charm and completing the repairs („this instinctive knowledge fades at the end of the scene”).
        Of course, the only thing my latter observation proves is that attempts at clear mechanical language offer no safeguard against deliberately bad-faith readings.

        Monkey Shaping Emulation is a very fun and interesting effect. It basically makes you really good at creating forgeries and cheap knockoff versions of other people’s work, a repurchase even allowing you to endow copied Artifacts with bootleg Evocations. I like how it expresses a theme that is clearly rooted in animal imagery without struggling to make it useful and relevant.

        Eye of the Cat is blindsight and the ability to see dematerialized creatures, rolled into one. This is not how I’d approach multifunctionality in this particular case, but I appreciate the attempt.

        Devil-Restraining Grip makes a triumphant return from 1E. It’s cool and fun! It also has no place in Perception. This should be a Strength Charm.

        God-Cutting Essence is literally the same as Spirit-Cutting Attack. While this is a basic effect arguably every Exalt type should be capable of accessing, I think there are ways to differentiate between them.

        Spirit-Maiming Essence Attack should probably be Supplemental. Otherwise, it’s… surprisingly good at fucking shit up? An unconditional minimum of 3 bonus damage for 3m is a very, very good deal, and so is up to +5 post-soak damage. This is arguably a better-than-Solar effect, considering that Solars don’t get damage enhancers against spirits at all, aside from the pretty crappy Spirit-Draining Stance (which costs an action and takes five turns to pay for itself, assuming mote-initiative value parity). Also, I’d say this belongs under Strength, too.

        God-Body Dissection is nasty. It effectively tears Charms off of spirits. I like it a lot, although if I were to do something similar, I’d make it a Gambit with a difficulty equal to the targeted Charm’s (Essence minimum+2).

        God-Body Consumption lets you eat gods for their delicious, delicious Eclipse-only Charms. I’m a fan. Totally stealing this. Just like a Lunar would. (Except I don’t need to eat Irked to do that, so… I guess not at all like a Lunar would, then?)


        ... And I think this is a good point to leave things at, for now. Next time, we'll continue with Charms branching off of Eyes of the Cat.

                Oh man, you guys are really invested in me doing reading forever instead of starting to work on the actual Charms (Not that I'm complaining; a steady backlog of stuff to read and analyze gives me time to actually put some thought and polish into my own thing, which I'm proud to say I've actually made some progress on already.)


                In any case, let's proceed with the Irked stuff!

                ...But before I do, I'll actually pipe up about one of Tikor's Charms, because it's a good excuse for me to talk about Lunar themes and aesthetics.

                The Charm in question is Sinister Strike Defense, an Essence 4 Charm that makes the Lunar unkillable by ambush attacks - they literally can't fill her Incapacitated Health Level. It additionally prevents her from sleeping, falling unconscious or losing awareness in any way, hereby locking her out of her main source of WP regeneration.
                This is a Silver Infernal effect.
                The way I see it, Infernal Charms are strongly tied up with the aesthetics of becoming. You get a new capability, but you trade away an irrevocable part of yourself in the process. Lunars, by definition, cannot draw on the same theme, because their themes are change and adaptation. The issue is twofold: becoming implies an endpoint, the possibility of completion, which is antithetical to the Lunar ethos of constant change. Infernals change in emulation of a cosmic principle; Lunar change itself is the cosmic principle. A more practical problem is that trading away capabilities like Infernals do is fundamentally at odds with the idea of Lunars as versatile jacks of all trades. Much like Solars themselves, Infernals are all about narrow and specialized power. That's the exact opposite of what Lunars are best at.
                Putting aesthetics aside, the implied reasoning behind the Charm's workings is also... Infernal-adjacent. It's an axiomatic effect: the Lunar is impossible to ambush, therefore she can't be killed by an ambush. "Being impossible to ambush" is a part of the character's legend and its own justification, without much grounding in how they got there. Personally, I see Lunar Charms differently. Lunars draw on primal aesthetics, and going too abstract with them dilutes that aesthetics. So for me, Lunar Charms should either be rooted in the physical, or in a process that directly expresses the Lunar's legend - for example, a perceptive Lunar should have a bunch of Charms that let her do perceptive things. Later on, this can branch into weirder and more esoteric directions, but it has to feel earned - it's not enough to gate a similar effect behind a high Essence requirement and a prerequisite Charm: the more divorced a Charm is from the attribute's basic logic, the more of an investment it should require in Charms that are more concrete and direct in how they express that attribute's themes.


                Wow, did I yammer a lot about that. I'll continue tomorrow with some actual Irked Charms, but for now, sleep calls!

                            What about giving the Lunar Exalted all nine Excellencies from the beginning as suggested by the examples in Exalted 3e?
