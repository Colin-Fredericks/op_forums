                                    Reposted from the "Magic Armor: Problematic?" Thread from a post by Aquillion:


                                    Originally posted by Revan058 View Post

                                    ...I. Wait, what? *scrolls up*

                                    ...I think I blanked that out when I was reading over that post. That. Wj...WHY. Why the fuck would your NATIVE POWERS shut off your equipment, espcally if it's a damn MOONSILVER, SHIFTING WEAPON? You know, the shit that's flowing and shifting and...i have a headach now...



                                    Several reasons.

                                    The main one is that Lunars need a reason to not use DBT. This puts the devs in a bind. One one hand, DBT is supposed to be heinously powerful; that's what it has always been, and it's a bit iconic to how it works. So they can't balance it as "just another Lunar charm" and make it one option among many -- it has to leap off the page. But at the same time, if they do that, any Lunar who doesn't use DBT is going to be weaker. This was a problem in previous editions -- you get Lunars balanced around DBT (so they're weak aside from this one strong option) and you get Lunars feeling they have to use this charm that has a very specific ascetic to it that might not be appropriate for everyone.

                                    The solution is to make DBT require some sort of tradeoff, but to make it a tradeoff in terms of 'options' (so the charm is still powerful as long as you don't intend to rely on those options at this precise moment.) Now, it could trade off with some DBT-incompatible Lunar charms, sure, but that would actually be harder to justify -- you'd have to almost invent un-Lunar Lunar charms in order for it to make sense for them to not work with DBT.

                                    On top of that, remember that DBT probably gives you huge persistent dice pools. In 3e this is going to be a big deal -- most people do not get persistent die-adders anymore; IAM-type charms are no longer a default part of the system. This makes DBT even more powerful than it was before, given how Exalted dice work -- it means that in some ways it can almost be a win button, provided your opponent has no way to shut it down. More specifically, it means that "general" powersets like Evocations are not necessarily designed and balanced with huge persistent dice-pools in mind; so having DBT limit your general powerset access makes for good balance.

                                    I will emphasize this again, because I think you're missing it, although I feel it's been heavily implied by everything spoiled so far: In DBT, a Lunar will probably have a much larger persistent dice pool than a Solar. Or anyone else, for that matter, excluding maybe some situational Infernal shintai stuff. I have no idea exactly how they'll balance this -- absent other tweaks, persistently rolling significantly more dice in the Storyteller system is a massive, game-changing advantage, of the "I win automatically" sort -- but it's a huge deal that will need to be designed around. This is probably part of that.

                                    I mean, that's pure speculation -- maybe Lunars will work entirely differently -- but I think it's reasonable to suspect that "persistently boosted dice pools" will be something Lunars get even if few other people do, since it's fairly inherent in what shapeshifting is. And it's a massively different (and much more defining) power in a setting where nobody else has IAM-equivilants to match those numbers. Turning into a bear now means that most fights against people who can't turn into bears will go down like, well, people trying to fight a bear, as opposed to 1e/2e where everyone had a charm to become as strong as a bear for the scene and shapeshifting was just your version of it.

                                    In terms of themes... this is the part I expect you won't like, but it's there, and I think they've indicated that this is where Lunars are going. It's a beastman transformation. The thematics are what they are; yes, there are a small number of animals that use tools, but none remotely comparable to humanity. It is therefore entirely thematically reasonable that taking on DBT (and temporarily shifting away from humanity into a hybrid state) could limit your ability to use human tools to their full potential. Keep in mind that by making this loss of evocation access a part of DBT, you allow Lunars to be good with tools while in human form -- human-form Lunars are better at evocations than Sidereals, and probably as good or better than anyone but Solars, despite their increased animal associations in 3e. This is possible because the game is focusing on their flexibility and shapeshifting -- a Lunar can choose to focus on their humanity, or they can choose to combine it with an animal form in exchange for physical power; and they can make that decision on the fly and rapidly switch back and forth between the two modes as the situation calls for it.

                                    This is more thematically interesting than a Lunar who just gets all their powers all the time in any form.

                                    (Keep in mind that we're not seeing the full picture here, of course -- DBT will be different; shapeshifting will be different; and we barely even know what Evocations are. But my point is that there are some obvious reasons to make it so Lunars can't use evocation in DBT form.)

                                                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                                            You see, that is the part that doesn't sell for me. To say it emphasizes the character's flexibility to invent a rule saying he can't do X while in Y shape. That doesn't enhance his flexibility... it detracts from it.

                                                            Similarly, I get miffed when they say preventing Lunars from using evocations in DBT form is a way of giving Lunars more options. No, it's not. It demonstrably removes options. It's like creating a rule saying Lunars can't use sorcery in DBT form. What 'option' does that add? Precisely none. Even though you now have two categories of Lunars - sorcery Lunars and DBT Lunars - in truth you've not actually given Lunars any more options, you've just taken options away and forced them down two arbitrary paths because they can no longer go down both at once. That is, maybe, the illusion of having more options. But in truth it's a net reduction. Previously, a Lunar could do anything in DBT form that he could do in human form. Now, he cannot. That doesn't add flexibility, it removes flexibility.

                                                            I understand that games need balancing, and if they say Lunars will not have the ability to use evocations while DBT because of balancing issues - fine. What I don't like is the sell that it give a Lunar character more flexibility, when demonstrably all it does is place limits on what Lunars can do.
                                                            It's not just about raw flexibility, it is about the type of flexibility they have -- about determining which choices are significant.

                                                            Since we don't know Lunar design for 3e, I will use 2e Alchemical design as a reference, because I think it illustrates the point really well. Alchemicals have a major constraint on how many charms they can use at once, primarily due to their commitment costs.

                                                            If you removed commitment costs, Alchemicals would be able to install all their charms at once. That would give them more options, right? Well, sort-of. But it would mean that the choice of what charm to install is no longer a meaningful decision (since obviously you just install everything.) And since modularity is vital to the unique Alchemical theme, that choice ought to be important -- hence, commitment costs. Obviously everyone would like to have every potential option, at all times, at no cost; but characters and powersets are defined by their limitations (and the specific, unique choices those limitations force on you) as much as by their strengths.

                                                            Same with Lunars. Yes, a Lunar in DBT who has access to all their evocations has access to ALL THE OPTIONS; but that means that whether or not you take DBT or whether or not you learn evocations is no longer a meaningful choice. Shapeshifting ceases to be a meaningful choice, because there is now one obvious form to always take for combat which every Lunar will always use. And since Lunars are meant to be a shapeshifting splat, that is an undesirable result.



                                                            Now, I said it would sort-of give Alchemicals more options; but in fact, it wouldn't. Because another key point to get from Alchemicals is how they're balanced and designed around their limitations, and what that means for the choices available to them. The commitment cost is the reason Alchemicals can get such inexpensive, powerful, low-prerequisite charms; and having those charms, of course, increases Alchemical options. (Or, in other words -- the commitment cost, by allowing that design, ultimately increases the number of interesting choices Alchemicals face.) Remove the commitment cost and you have to nerf and reprice their charms to balance, and the next thing you know they're just generic bottom-tier Celestials. They're not going to actually end up with more absolute choices, because... the splat is balanced as a whole.

                                                            Same for Lunars. The limitation on using DBT with Evocations is part of the core design design of both DBT and Lunar Evocation access; one or both of them would have to be substantially weaker without it. In other words -- your evocation-wielding Lunar has the option to drop their evocations for a moment and turn into an overwhelmingly powerful DBT form; your DBT-using Lunar has the option to switch to human form and use powerful evocations. Without the limitation, neither would be so powerful for you, and therefore neither would be as significant of an option -- even though you could have both running at once, the game would have to be balanced so you don't always want to, which would mean DBT would just be one more generic charm in their charmset rather than an option of nearly game-breaking power.

                                                            eg. Solars, for instance, do not have the option to temporarily trade their Evocation access for increased power. That's something they can't do. The fact that Lunars have that choice is, in some cases, a substantial advantage for them.

                                                            (And in this case I'm using Solars as an example very deliberately -- Solar charms rarely have disadvantages or limitations or trade-offs, which looks like an advantage for them. But it does limit Solar options, in terms of what the Solar charmset can do, because disadvantages and trade-offs in one area can be used to buy power elsewhere -- something non-Solar splats tend to explore in a lot more depth. Lunars can trade their hands for wings; Solars can't.)
