    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post

    Literally the one thing they did in 2000 years as of 2e and nobody knows or really cares they did it.

    I'm over the trope of "glorious heroism that is neither remembered nor recognized" being applied to an entire splat.
    I never said it was a good splat, i said the splay had a good idea IN it. And the idea that the Lunars are the first line of defense against the Fae is a GREAT idea - it gives the Lunars more range as characters, both collectively and individually. It gives them a primary threat to face at all times that isn't shared by all the other splats. It was something unique, something to latch onto and say 'This is what Lunars are for' rather than 'this is what Lunars do'.

    Solars are there to be paragons of perfection who decide how Creation will go from now on, they do this through powerful ability charms and high dice pools, and fight against the Wyld Hunt that is out to destroy them. Sidereals are there to manage fate, they do so by toying with the laws of reality, and their main enemies are bureaucracy and the fallen/dead primordials. (AFAIK). Abyssals are there to kill everything, they do so by manipulating death and the dead, and they fight against everything alive. Dragon Blooded are there to drown the enemy in numbers, they do this through teamwork, established infrastructure and the adoration of the public, and their main enemies are Anathema.

    Aside from Shield Wall against chaos, what are Lunars for?... To be the Solar's love interest/rival and the bitch to everyone else's butch. Yeah. How VERY compelling to play second fiddle to literally everyone. *sigh* They shapeshift, sure, but not to any central goal or idea, or to fight any particular enemy. They had the Thousand Streams River plan or whatever it was, but that wasn't compelling in the slightest because it was still about what OTHERS would do, not what the Lunars themselves were for.

    For every other detail of the 2e splat, all I could think about was how much of an inferiority complex this group had.

There was also a First Age Lunar Elder who self-stylized herself as a fairy princess, owning a small army of Fae vassals. She was amusing, being this Disney-esque princess, but treating her servants like dirt because "They're not real people, you guys."

                Ultimately, I think that the Wyld can be a bit daunting to use, considering it's supposed to be the chaotic "Other", but the times we've been there, our ST has used the Fae as a way to have a lot of fun with meta-humor, basically playing the "Fae as Actors" straight for humorous effect. This has given us a bit of normalcy what has been a lot of weirdness. Examples of this that we played through were a forest of pizza pruned by chef-gardeners and a Pokemon Safari Zone-esque Behemoth reserve, if that helps.

                                        Originally posted by HalfTangible View Post
                                        The Lunars had that in the 'shield wall against unreality' bits and I wish they'd done more of it.
                                        Part of the issue is that "shield wall against unreality", that is to say patrolling every border of all of Creation, is a hilariously impractical function even if you had all 300-ish guys working on it. And not in the "well you just have to get good" way either, the way where every Lunar spending all of their time on this one thing would still not be able to do it. Sidereals have a similarly impractical job, of course, but a major tenet of the Sidereal play experience is that they aren't getting everything done that they need to. And that's while being the single most unified and organized group among all Exalts. Lunars have the Silver Pact, but the Silver Pact itself is dubious as a "racial identity" in the first place, much less as something that can coordinate all of the Lunars with the efficiency that the Sidereals have.

                                        In order to give Lunars something that is actually theirs, I think you need to radically redefine at least part of the cosmology. The original setting has them as such an afterthought that trying to say "well, they do this" is trying to cram a square peg into a round hole. And apparently their shapeshifting doesn't cover that.

                                            I think it will help to let go of the idea that they (and the Solars and Dragon Blooded) have a kind of cosmically necessary function in the manner that Sidereals do, rather than a set of qualities that lead them to do things in distinct ways.

                                            Like the idea of what they do being "basically anything, just in a manner you might expect from a shapeshifting monster hero". You have Solar pirates and Lunar pirates, and while the former act by being all exemplary at ship piloting and crew management and swinging between ships and generally swashbuckling, the latter do it by slipping off of the side of their schooners and torpedoing around beneath enemy ships, tearing out their keels, knocking holes in the hull and leaping over like Free Willy taking several sailors over the other side with them (things they may or may not do in an animal form).

                                            I think more milage might come out of that then trying to rope off "special ops" as some particular place for them.

                                            Likewise with the idea that one intrinsically needs Lunars in order to inject chaos into the world or keep Solars spontaneous or anything similar to that... that all still feels like stuff that Sidereals would be responsible for, even if there were occasions in which they'd look on Lunars as particularly useful ways of achieving that.

                                            I don't view the problem with "the setting had no place for them" in terms of whether there's some essential ontological niche they fill so much as the setting not acting as though there were four hundred terrifying, powerful, hunted beast saints from the previous age of the world running around, at least with any more consequence than "occasionally Terrestrials on Wyld Hunts will fight them". Lunars don't need to have something they can do that other Exalts can't (in terms of achievements in the setting rather than expressions of their power), they just need something to give credibility to the idea that whatever others can do that fulfils the same function is irrelevant because it's not as if they're going anywhere.

                                            Which actually feels like an apt way to sum up the whole problem the Sidereals had with them in the Usurpation; they had a Prophecy concerning the Solars and Dragon Blooded and reordering the society of the world, and turned to the Lunars with a "yeah, our vision for the future doesn't really have any place for you in it, so could you make things convenient and just go away forever please", and Lunars responding to that with scorn and laughter and flashing teeth.
