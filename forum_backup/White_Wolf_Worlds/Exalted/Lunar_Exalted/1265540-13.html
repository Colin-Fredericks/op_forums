                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                            The Third Edition Solar Bond isn't a "carried over" Intimacy; it's still the Lunar's own Intimacy, and seems to primarily work from the basis of their initial impression of the actual Solar whom they meet, and thereafter changes depending on the nature of that relationship.
                            Hmmmm...

                            I don't think it's a good idea to mechanically demand that the intimacy be carried over. That's too limiting, definitely.

                            But saying it is just based on their initial impression of them would seem to make it little different from other intimacy. I mean, your first feelings about anyone is based on your initial impression of them. There's nothing magical there.

                            The genre-myth behind it is reincarnated lovers/companions who find each other in each incarnation. I'd say the point of the bond is that this feeling is one that's crossed lifespans.


                            For example, our Lunar, Timid Silk Blossom, was in love with the Dawn, Brasidas. Their previous incarnations had been sworn blood-brothers. But he left the group and later died.

                            If she were to meet his reincarnation, she'd immediately, no matter how boring or forgettable that person was in that circumstance, know they were her Solar Mate, and have some relatively strong feeling towards them.

                            I wouldn't tell the player what Intimacy to pick, because he can decide how to describe it, but the story of the bond is it's a relationship over lifetimes. Story-wise, the Intimacy represents a bond that's lasted through the aeons. So Timid's opinion of her new mate is going to be a continuation of their previous relationship. Perhaps love again; perhaps rage at being rejected. Perhaps fear of falling into that relationship again. Perhaps protectiveness because Brasidas died. It's up to the player, but I wouldn't tell him "your intimacy is about your initial impression of this person you just met." It's about Brasidas and Timid, Righteous Lion and the Dread Wolf, and a hundred other partnerships stretching back to the end of the Solar-Lunar war.*


                            *Obviously one big change with 2nd ed is that the Solars didn't have bonded mates in the Primordial War, or for a while after that. But I imagine that when the Solar-Lunar war was over, and the Solars and Lunars bonded together, many tried to choose partners they already knew and got on with.

                            Similarly, if my Lunar died, and I introduced his reincarnation to the group as my new character, and Prince Meek was rude or arrogant to him on our initial meeting (as Meek often is), I might still pick a positive intimacy because of Prince Meek's generally positive relationship with his Lunar father-in-law, or the strong (if evil) partnership between the identity-stealing First Age Night Caste and his devoted assassin that they once were.

                            Tldr; I agree with Elfive.

                                    Originally posted by Eric Minton View Post
                                    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Pests_Campaign
                                    I thought I'd replied to this, but I guess I didn't.

                                    Yeah, that was one of the examples I was thinking of.

                                    Both Stalin and Mao definitely were not saints, but alongside their more murderous policies, they had a number of policies that, I think, really were born out of a desire to help people, but went horribly wrong. The interesting thing about the Four Pests Campaign and the Great Leap Forward more generally is that the problem wasn't just that it was a bad policy, but also that no-one was willing to tell Mao. And as things got worse, they kept not telling Mao. Cadre leaders lied about its grass-roots effects, their leaders passed bad information up, and up, and up. And if anyone said "wait, this is going horribly wrong" their superiors could point out all the other places where, apparently, everything was fine. So what's wrong with your cadre?

                                    The campaign lasted much longer than it should have, because Mao thought it was actually going pretty well.

                                    The other example I was thinking of was Tony Blair. As far as he could see/was told, he'd heroically saved Sierra Leone with military forces, he'd saved Kosovo with military forces (and been hailed as a hero by Kosovan refugees), and he'd recently liberated Kabul from the Taleban. Iraqi exiles told him how Saddam's forces tortured, raped and oppressed. And so when Bush wanted to invade based on dubious intelligence, Blair was primed to agree to some good old liberal interventionism. And we can all see how well that went.

                                    Anyway, these are all the sorts of things I could imagine a Solar with incredible charisma, the throne of a powerful nation, but not much more wisdom than anyone else, doing even with good intentions. What mortal's going to tell the God-Queen she's wrong?

                                                            It's been a difficult thread to follow (in other words, a Lunar thread) but to me I took the 3e approach as a lot less than an inherited emotion or impression of a person and more of an incessant connection that you can't ignore. You feel for this person you've just met and you can't not feel for them. How you interpret that determines the context of the intimacy, but it could have NOTHING to do with past live memories or emotions. You just know that you can't ignore them if you want to, can't put them out of your head completely, can't pretend they don't exist. Some part of you is always aware.

                                                            Maybe you like that and you get to know them and it becomes a friendship.
                                                            Maybe you love that and it grows into something more.
                                                            Maybe that bugs you and you become rivals and compete in things.
                                                            Maybe you hate that and you just want to be rid of that person and you become enemies.

                                                            My impression is that the context isn't what carries over, it's the root connection. Whatever happens, whatever you think or feel for the person, you're not forced to feel a certain way about them, you're just forced to feel at all. My 2 cents.
