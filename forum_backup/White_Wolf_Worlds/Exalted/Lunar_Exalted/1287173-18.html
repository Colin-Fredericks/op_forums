                    Okay, now for the actual last of the full Dominion write-ups, the much-vaunted Caul.


                    First off we’ve got a bit of expansion on the mythology of it, at least from the Lunar perspective; more than merely being a place where Gaia and Luna walked together, it’s referred to as a place born from the Primordial’s dream of her Incarnae lover, hence some of the oddities inherent to the place. There’s a striking description of what the significance of the place is to the Lunar Exalted, the strange idea that they experience it as a kind of spiritual sibling, as well as an insight into what they feel from the shrine cities that is distinct from the elemental breakdowns significant to the Dragon Blooded. It puts me in mind of prior Editions trying to match Garou elements to Lunars with an idea that they’re guardians of nature on Luna’s behalf for the sake of their lover; it never really fit to their character or the mythology of the setting, but something in the bones of it was compelling to me, and I think that taking it from an attitude applying to the whole of Creation and concentrating it down to a singular place (which thus gets to be really weird) helps to retain the more worthwhile parts of that, give them somewhere that they can really shine, and free up the Lunars elsewhere. Plus, it reflects the attention back on the Lunars themselves, given how the important thing isn’t so much as Gaia as Gaia’s perspective on their patron.

                    Having brought the Dragon Blooded up, there’s also the point about how it is sacred to them as well, having once been a point of common cause between the two; it solidifies a lot of my sense of the place having a significant common history between the two (which I find to compound the tragedy of how they’re currently fighting over it). The idea of them having worked to fix it more to Creation’s reality is more abstractly mythical than my own notions that they needed to defend it from their fellow Exalted (although it doesn’t strictly exclude it), and serving as a basis for its disappearance, we finally get a timeline for the place; so it vanished some time around the middle of the Shogunate, and reappeared some 260 years into the Realm era. Nice and clean.
                    So yeah, I’d say that’s a good starting premise for both sides wanting to hold power over the Caul; that it’s more than just a strategic benefit, more than just resources to exploit, for each side that’s invested in it it’s basically a Crusade. That makes it distinctly compelling, and terrifying.

                    Yes, for all that Sha’a Oka is more of the open war upon the Realm type, it’s still pointed out that his style and his assets still restrict his capacity to fight in direct army clashes. I imagine what sets this apart from the usual, more attrition minded Silver Pact member is the scope; that a population stretched across the entirety of the Caul is persistently harassing the Realm occupation, and that the Lunars themselves won’t fall back from it as they might among the typical Dominions. And there have probably still been times before the current crisis in which the Lunars’ forces seized and held territory, even if just to create a period in which the pilgrimage path would be denied to their enemies. Maybe also an element of how, even when the road to the Last City was under Realm control, it might be persistently surrounded on all sides by hostile forces. So it ends up somewhere between the typically more flexible Dominions and those that are cultivated to engage in active field warfare. At least before the current events in which they got to drive the Realm forces completely back into Faxai, and place each of the other cities under a Lunar general’s control.

                    Huh, wonder what turtlewolves are.

                    Now we’re getting a bit of insight into the mortal inhabitants of the Caul, noted to be descended from a mix of West and Southwestern settlers… ah yes, it took me a moment to recall how Oka was supposed to be the only survivor of the Caul’s disappearance, meaning that the current societies would only be a few centuries old. Given what might be known or guessed about the two Directions, I suppose that the sudden appearance of a huge, verdant landmass would have been extremely attractive to people from all over, before they necessarily knew much about it. Still, it’s interesting to consider the point about them possibly knowing the island better than Oka, for all that they’ve been there no longer.

                    Bit of description of their culture… I wonder if the rites that they conflict over are a more mystically substantial thing or purely religious (with the idea that they dispute something like the order in which they’re told just for that reason still being very believable and somewhat familiar to me). Clans raise their children in common rather than divided into nuclear families… huh, they live in a gender segregated manner that has something to do with their rituals, wonder what that’s about. But it’s something that actually appeals to me, on grounds of being able to invoke some elements of the portrayal of the original Air Nomad culture in Avatar. I also like some of the descriptions of some of the manners in which they live, such as carrying fires on backs, people who swim before they can walk, and using giant apes as war mounts.

                    Yes, there seems to be a recurring theme of how people from the Caul are set apart, with this inclusion of the point about how they’ve got their own separate languages, with a further layer in secret ones employed by each gender (with the amusing note of the masculine one having been lost in trade to a god). And the people haven’t been there very long, so the idea would seem to be that something about the Caul changes people (which wouldn’t be an out of place element for a landmass created in tribute to somebody like Luna). Makes me wonder if that’s a possibility in areas under Realm control, that the authorities have continuously tried to curtail. Makes me wonder if part of how the Lunars see the Realm profaning the island is with their attempts to impose uniformity.

                    The theme is reinforced with the point about them being evasive with non-Caulborn, although something that strikes me here is the notion that people being born from outsiders while living on the Caul is a rare occurrence; I wonder if that means that those brought for Realm settlements have a high turnover rate, or that something in the nature of the place means that people who have not fully embraced it have greatly reduced fecundity, either from mindset or biology. Still, it sounds like one could get some interesting narratives from one making the transition from being treated evasively, to being treated merely aloofly, to departing to fully live among the Caulborn.

                    The more I read about them, the more intriguing I find the idea that the Caulborn have taken up these attitudes and practices in such a relatively short amount of time. Makes me think of the original settlers who came in thinking it was just another land, and wound up getting more than they bargained for, and needed to adapt to the requirements of their new home/god. Each of the described bans gives me an intriguing idea about what might occur for violating them, with the point about hatred of burying the dead in particular making me wonder if the people fear profaning the land, or that it will do something to give their dead back. And the point about needing to be sure that something is a person before you speak to it makes me think that this whole place could really take up a lot of imagery from the Foggy Swamp, with the idea that you might end up communing with some image of your past or future.

                    Aaaand the point about interactions with the architecture take it from intriguing to pretty damn creepy; there’s something really unique and freakish in the idea that marks into stonework won’t stay in the same place. Despite its brevity, I actually find the story of Ragara Dolara being haunted by his name like that to be a very effective horror story, and think that a slightly longer version of it could be something that would really stick with people; I can damn well see the man encountering his doppleganger just standing in the middle of the road with that knife, and it’s incredibly unsettling.

                    It’s interesting how the nature of their relations to the Realm are shaped by a combination of the typical experiences of colonised people and the peculiarities of the Caul, with the mere act of undertaking the pilgrimage being enough to endear Dragon Blooded to the natives. It makes me think that, in the eyes of the Caulborn, whatever one’s motives in undertaking those rituals, to do so upon the island makes one akin to the people who live and breathe the place. It feels like it would make for an interesting growing discourse and cultivated relationship for any Dragon Blooded players who undertake the pilgrimage. From the other end, I think it’s a good nuance to Realm policies that they find the aid of the Caulborn in undertaking the pilgrimage to be important enough to not want to turn them against imperial forces, and try to take a lighter hand with them. Similar is the point of how the Caul, of all places, forms a bit of a break between Immaculate Order practice and official Realm power; that it’s a place where the dogma of the monks can end up overriding servitude to the secular Realm government, and even any reverence that they might have for the Caul itself (it being seen as a place where the Dragon Blooded originated), being unable to tolerate a place where the people adhere to such distinctive rites. I think it really helps convey that there’s something very different about the Caul.

                    So, what about how the people view the Silver Pact? Yyyeesss, it makes sense that they wouldn’t necessarily have a reverence for the Lunars, seeing as it’s likely that most of the Caulborn place no particular stock in Luna themselves, but I’ll admit that I’m surprised that only a minority have any dealings. Mind, looking at more of it, I think it does more good for the portrayal of the Caulborn and the Lunars to present both sides as having diverse relations to one another. That the Black Lion is esteemed by basically all of them makes a lot of sense, not just because their ancestors would have likely seen him first (and perhaps may have been the first new people he saw on his return), and he’s probably the only person in the world who’s as intertwined with the Caul’s nature as themselves. Otherwise, I think it really reinforces the mystery of the place to give several of the Caulborn clans either achievements against the Lunars or assets valuable enough to compel Lunars to treat with them on their terms, even apart from finding allies to fight the Realm. For some reason, I keep needing to remind myself that the portrayal of the Lunars less invested in the people is more callous than hostile (although I have to imagine that some among the Lunars who come there, either for study or to join Oka, find the practices and caginess of the Caulborn to be extremely frustrating).

                    There are said to be a few beastfolk clans, resulting from alliance with the Lunars, of which we get three: The Kongar lionfolk, apparently one of the oldest societies on the Caul from their long association with His Divine Lunar Presence, and with whom they have an interesting sounding relationship; they regard Oka as truly divine, and such an embodiment of the Caul that they make a point of recording omens surrounding him, but don’t actually worship the man. They’re referred to as being in possession of good wood and metal crafts, and build cities in the jungles, which I imagine would be partially a consequence of them being there for so long. The Ninga hawkfolk seem to have a bit more Caulborn weirdness, such as their own unusual language (a little bit ahead, they’re referred to as having the Musical Voice Merit, which conveys the point that they’re non-aquatic beastfolk whose manner of communication and self-expression is modified) and forbid themselves particular words and phrases at times and seasons. They also have an interesting relationship with a Lunar, with Sandswept Garda Empress not being a native member of their people, but being adopted as a clan matriarch. And the Yamalu boarfolk, whose Lunar patron was lost to an Immaculate warrior, but who maintain her martial traditions. I like how their process for choosing leaders doesn’t just emphasize strength, but also courage and cleverness, gives a sense that what they’re looking for is more rounded (although I still feel drawn to these the least). No, the ones that I think more of are the beastfolk that aren’t affiliated with the Pact at all, with the idea that several of them apply their unique advantages to undertaking the Caul’s rites and shape their own taboos; the mothfolk in particular sound fun, both for the image of them and the idea that they don’t believe in the sun. Just the overall sense that the Caul is a place, more than any other, in which transformation into beastfolk could occur spontaneously.

                    Now, onto the Kongar city of Morovath. Nice to see the previous idea about shifting marks on architecture extend to even an inhabited city, with some good lunar cycle imagery to create a city that shifts back and forth between inspiring beauty and forbidding grimness depending on the time of the month. I especially like how the transformations aren’t supposed to be all at once, or even a gradual thing; that they can shift at unpredictable paces, with relocations just from being out of sight for a second. Ah, so the Kongar did not build the city, they merely rediscovered it amidst the jungles and are given peacekeeping authority by Oka over the rest of the inhabitants, the other Caulborn settlers or the children of Lunars come to join the cause; I have to imagine there’s something particularly stressful in being Moon-Touched and not only being dragged along to the Caul, but having to live in a city where most of the rest of the inhabitants will shun you, or at best speak very obscurely to you. The descriptions of how the city’s resources (and surrounding landscape) are dedicated towards war with the Realm is all well and good, but I don’t think anything about the city could top the idea that artistic expression is matched to the phase, especially the idea that the words stay the same, you just sing them differently.

                    Then there’s one of the main events of the island, the shrine cities themselves. Starts off by reiterating the value of the pilgrimage to the Dragon Blooded, while noting that there’s no record or memory of what the Last City of Feng-Yi could provide to Lunars, and they’ve never managed to hold all five since the Usurpation. It gets into a description of each of the cities, and do I care for this to be how I’m introduced to the ones besides Faxai… no. It’s written here as though to serve as somebody’s introduction to the concept, it’s sufficient. It wouldn’t be the first time in Exalted I first heard of something in its more summarised form in a different book to its main description, so in a sense it’s even a bit nostalgic.

                    That out of the way, we’re reintroduced to Faxai and its quirk of the land being so sacred with Earth Essence that no buildings are touched on the surface; it makes sense to point to how its defences remain sufficient to repulse Lunar assault, but it’s curious how it doesn’t describe the starting rite of the pilgrimage, considering the rest.

                    Such as Garianghis of the Wood Shrine, which sounds like it can invoke some nice imagery of Cambodian temple ruins with how it’s so overgrown and overshadowed by an enormous tree. We get a reference to Third Daughter of the Leaves’ turtlewolves, and I’m guessing from the description that they aren’t beastfolk, but rather the offspring of her chimeric spirit shape; I want to picture… wolves with snapping turtle beaks, and spiky armoured shells. I’ve been enamoured of the image of a sacred picture of the world untouched by humanity since I read wiki entries of the Emerald Dream from Warcraft, so I like something reminiscent of that in the Lunar experience of the shrine. That and the idea that they might be drawn to the city in hopes of meeting Luna again, though my favourite detail of that is the idea that it’s so important that the otherwise nasty sounding Third Daughter won’t deny it to her fellows, even if she’s personally more interested in figuring out a way to use its powers to assault the dreams of all Terrestrials on the Caul.

                    Damn, Houshou’s gimmick of burning itself down every night only to reappear in the morning might knock Morovath out of the spot of freakiest point on the place, especially with the idea that the shrine’s guardian monks remain inside the conflagration and, unlike the Lunars, don’t withstand the flame, they just come back as though they’re part of the scenery. Narrating the experience of watching the city burn sounds like it could make for a particularly memorable game scene, even for somebody visiting the Caul for purposes other than pilgrimage; I’m imagining playing somebody there to visit Skathra Venomchild, and as the sun goes down she jauntily gives them fair warning that they might want to depart before everything starts spontaneously combusting, and then transitioning the action out through the streets and watching the finale from afar. Followed up by Skathra and her Lunars going around hunting colonists for sport, because the Silver Pact still needs a few monsters, and some of Raksi’s slot has been freed up.

                    Incidentally, I’m just imagining Jural and Lukha actually managing to subdue Bhadri, and the former invites his boy home to meet the parents and present the captive fae empress in tribute, and Lukha becomes a bit disturbed to see what his lover is dedicated to.

                    Sekima’s experience of entering a ruined city and perceiving the memory of what it was like when alive sounds really cool, as does the idea of Lunars getting a unique capacity to make edits by interaction with the memory forms. I think that Garda-Empress’ plan for the place is the most creative and focused on the long-term; she doesn’t seem to expect to hold the shrine indefinitely, so she’s trying to ensure that in the event that Terrestrials return, she’ll find the memory of the city poisoned against them specifically. This one also strikes me as being where the exclusion of Solars would be most keenly felt; I’m imagining a mixed circle of the three types of Exalted, where the Lunar and Dragon Blood enter and start interacting with things and people, and the Solar is stuck waiting outside awkwardly, watching Circlemates going around touching and talking to nothing.

                    I’ve got to admit, Melilune’s premise of walking into the city’s reflection and talking to the images of other Exalted pilgrims feels very similar to Sekima’s quality. I guess the thing to take from it is that the reflection city is seemingly something that actually remains alive and continues moving forwards, rather than just being a memory. Still, it doesn’t quite leap out to me the way that the others do. It all finishes off with a sidebar of the ways in which some of the Caulborn interact with the cities; I think the most of the idea that Houshou’s ashes are collected for body art and medicinal purposes, while the point about the people using Sekima to visit the memories of their own family members throws off some of my certainty about how the place is supposed to work.
                    We get a short section about the experience of the wilderness, and how the form of lands beyond the shrine cities and pilgrim’s road is inherently malleable and difficult to travel, with geographic features and spatial relationships capable of warping without warning. I enjoy the point about how getting around the Caul requires developing a certain intuition for its moods; even with the line about how native travellers carry seeds to perform impromptu auguries or checks on whether or not to continue on their current path, the main sense it gives me is that for Caulborn and those Lunars who have been around for a while, it’s mostly a matter of not skipping a beat whenever your path does suddenly change, and maybe even being able to navigate those shifts purposefully, rather than never experiencing them.

                    And finally a passage covering the subject of the local spirits and fae, which I’ve been especially looking forward to because I want to see me some of that Pearl Court! Judge Nehemeth was already pretty great, but combine her with this current rendering of the nobility and she’ll be unstoppable!

                    Huh, so the Caul’s depopulation during its disappearance had implications for spirits as well; I hadn’t considered that, although it makes sense. So with a land that needed to start over from scratch, you’ve got a lot of elementals that sprung anew at an unusual rate from its substances, and very little actual activity from gods owing to things like the Lunars being untrustworthy of basically any that might be assigned there by Yu-Shan, not even about fearing them being actual spies, just that there basic reporting might give things away to the enemy. Plus an inability to form coherent divine policy for the place; it’s own inherent fluidity must be bad enough to try and formulate its destiny, let alone being home to such a concentration of hostile Celestial Exalted. The lack of divine supervision probably doesn’t really make a contribution to the nature of the Caul, but I imagine that it makes planning some things around it more difficult; mind, it makes me wonder if a few of the rituals undertaken by the Caulborn might substitute for some cosmological contributions that local gods might have been able to make.

                    And for the fae… they’re referred to in very basic terms as coming out of the odd bordermarch that springs up in the wilderness, there’s no mention of Judge Nehemeth, and the description of how no really powerful raksha are known to operate kind of precludes her presence. Darn. Still, at least we get notes on how the land is unusually resistant to shadowlands, and a favourite for demons to slip into, being free from Hell but getting to enjoy it lacking some of Creation proper’s oppressive presence.

                    Last is a point on how the spirits relate to the local Exalted. The Dragon Blooded are said to have a harder time with them, as their wilder character makes Immaculate intimidation tactics less effective and they’re more likely to get into a fight, something that has to be a big shock to a lot of younger Dynasts who ever journeyed there for pilgrimage or military service. Their characteristics end up favouring the Lunar mythology (having, as it does, so many methods for appealing to a spirit’s idiosyncrasies, as well as a common enemy in the Immaculate Philosophy). There’s a final note on how the spheres of spirits who’ll ally with nobody becomes even more hostile, but I couldn’t say if that implies anything about their function in the call, or merely supplementing its inherent nature with their own snares.



                    And so that’s the long-cultivated hype of the Caul finally given a complete picture. I take away two major things from it: First, that I think it effectively retains a lot of the implications that one might have wanted from the very old “home to a Lunar alternative to the Loom of Fate” thing, while dropping a lot of the baggage that may have held, and giving it a background a bit closer to the Lunars. The other is that I really like the approach to the Caulborn, because it looks at the pulp fiction premise of a strange and isolated land being inhabited by freaky natives, takes the basic essence and aesthetic quality of it, and does its very best job of shaking loose all of the ugly implications from the likes of colonialism and orientalism and the like, and doing a lot to replace it with some basic humanity. And really, they’re an especially good fit for the Caul, as they help prevent its oddities from becoming too ungrounded by giving them a human face with which to interact and process any experiences that might come up. In the end, I think the Caul came off very well, a special little gem in the setting from which one could present a very different kind of story.

                    I had intended to roll the mini Dominion entries up in this post as well, but writing on the Caul took longer than I expected (I’m already in excess of 4000 words), and there are another three pages of them, so I’ll save them for a little bit later. For now, it’s just the next Artifact.



                    Our first level 5 Artifact since Arms of the Chosen sees the return of Death at the Root. This grimcleaver was always a source of frustration to me, something that seemed to have an intriguing backstory and premise, let down by the underwhelming manner in which Artifacts, especially the 5-dot ones, were realised before. Well, Evocations have provided a much more impressive framework for realising the powers of such weapons, so I have high hopes that it will finally be able to live up to its name.

                    Note: I recall finding the backstory to Death at the Root to be something with potential; I cannot recall specifically what the backstory was.

                    Herein, the origin of Death at the Root is given as the Lunar geomancer and architect Shu Ri-Li having been so enraged that the fruits of his life’s labours was being appropriated by the treacherous Dragon Blooded that he destroyed the instrument of his artifice and refashioned it with a huge blade of moonsilver. Hence he was in possession of a weapon capable of denying and sabotaging his new enemies by being able to rend the very dragon lines to power his former works and tear down irreplaceable infrastructure. To fit a story of that scope, this weapon is given a particularly impressive collection of deeds, such as sabotaging the water purification manses of the River of Tears, striking a war manse to temporarily disable the Realm Defence Grid (a level of interaction with it that no prior Edition attributed to anybody), and being used by the Lunar who saved it from the Realm’s grasp to break down the wards of its own storehouse. Now that I’m sufficiently hyped up for it, let’s dive in.

                    From the outset, the grimcleaver gets the significant power to shatter hearthstones without needing the usual skills or examination for weak spots, which sounds useful for just tearing through any stockpiles of particularly precious gems. And then we get its most basic, freely available power, by which its strike is capable of completely shutting down a manse or demesne’s supernatural properties for the length of a scene; even that can probably be very useful for cases in which an enemy assumed they could rely upon the enhancements or safety provided by those powers, and suddenly finds themselves standing alone. The holy site may come back online at the scene’s end, but they might be around anymore to benefit from it.

                    The next Evocation, Welling Sap Strike, gives Death at the Root utility even when not around manses, being able to take in spilt Essence from even decisively attacked enemies to give itself an aura for the scene adding to its raw damage and Overwhelming values for withering attacks, which is super cheap and can be stacked repeatedly. It’s still something that wants to be used in supernatural sites, as severing them also provides some of that bonus, and the Merit rating of them can up the limit of how many times the benefit can be stacked. One more Evocation after that lets it Instantly add that bonus to decisive attacks as well.

                    Dragon’s Egg Cracked at Essence 2 provides a greater benefit to the more basic powers of the axe by letting it basically tie them together; shattering a hearthstone on an enemy’s person with a gambit to instantly sever their manse/demesne attunement, or suppressing the magic site to provide a bonus to the attack on hearthstones that came from it. It also starts becoming capable of attacking sorcery, as attacks on a sorcerer strip away motes they’ve been gathering, or the Lunar Charm Spell-Rending Talon gets to be used for three as the target magic gets chopped up.
                    Cutting the World-Root at Essence 3 enhances the first power to extend its suppression of magical sites to a matter of days, which can cause a kind of shockwave effect for Resonant wielders whose attack rolls high enough, as any of the manse’s linked hearthstones are shattered from afar. The power World-Soul Harvest can be used to absorb some of the power of a place supressed in that fashion, equipping the grimcleaver with a simulacrum of its hearthstone.

                    Essence 4’s Broken Circle Ruin allows for the site-breaking powers to be used on sorcerous workings as well, by default ruining Terrestrial ones permanently while suppressing Celestial and Solar ones as though they were demesnes or manses, unless it was a working generated with a manse as one of the Means, in which case even the Celestial Circle can be undone, and even the Solar is suppressed for at least four months. The difficulty that has to be overcome can be fairly high though; disrupting Solar workings requires a whopping nine successes.

                    And Death at the Root’s ultimate power, Manse-Razing Strike. By means of that Charm, a manse or demesne that has been suppressed (and any defenders probably dealt with by the gathered bled energies) can be taken apart with an extended action over the course of days, as the wielder carves its geomancy to pieces. I’ve not commented much on theme or implications of powers yet because I’ve just been soaking in the intricacies, but this one is comparatively simple, so it leaves a lot of room to imagine. Imagine a Lunar thoughtfully going around the chambers of a manse, every now and then stopping to swing the gigantic axe head into some of its features and structures. Perhaps as the process goes on, it becomes more and more furious, rougher, indelicate, until in the end you’ve got something like a hearthstone room so heavily gouged it was as though a mighty clawed animal dug into it like clay. It was probably a particularly impressive sight whenever Shu Ri-Li deconstructed his own works. On the mechanical side, one thing I’ll note is that failure is given a consequence of immunising the site to the power, but no terminus is listed for the extended action. I’m wondering if that’s an oversight, or the intention is that one can continue rolling towards success indefinitely, but you blow it for good if you ever drop the mote commitment.



                    Yes, I think this rendering of the Artifact does very well. What I like in particular are the qualities to help it function more as a weapon with some flexibility to its application, while still being one that is brought mostly against mystical places and constructions. I also like how, for all its power, it’s ultimately a very understated Artifact; the manse or demesne destruction isn’t in something overt and flashy, it’s more like being a surgeon with a very big scalpel. Death at the Root meets with my approval.

                    I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                        Okay, very end of the Dominions chapter. It’s been a good one, so I don’t need very much from these little ones at the end. Looking at how short they are… would probably only need two or three lines to convey thoughts on each of them, unless any have concepts that really send me on a cascading train of thought.


                                        Okay then, Black Winter Boneyard first; shadowland home of the necromancer Smiling Rat, who maintains security with a private army of zombies, skeletal constructs and phantasmal monsters while he develops his necromancy, which he’s happy to teach to just about anybody. Sounds like a good source for a mentor in that magic for people who wouldn’t want to deal directly with the Underworld. Also good to refer a bit to a Lunar who is slightly on the outs with the Silver Pact.

                                        The Shattersea Bastion, a series of ruined Shogunate forts situated across some rocks of the White Sea’s shore, home to Shadow-Rending Razor and his cult of moon-worshipping assassins. Curious to see a reference to one of those old Luna aspects when the rest of the write-ups have divested them, but at this point it doesn’t really come across as a highly prominent thing, just one of the personas that would appeal to this group in particular. Glad to see another reference to a court of lesser moon-gods and the idea of them providing blessings to enhance the mortals, and the description of the unknowing nearby fishing village gives me a certain gothic feel to the whole thing.

                                        New Dakuo is a city under the control of the Shadow-Fang Vanguard, and their… allies. Well, the idea that they would form a covenant with a Shogunate army for the sake of protecting a mortal populace is sufficient to make me greatly revise my opinions of the Vanguard, and maybe a bit of why they’re shunned by their fellows. I wonder whether it would be better to play the leading Dragon Blood trying to stonewall his Lunar counterpart’s successor as an attempt to consolidate power, or because the new guy needs to earn respect.

                                        The Eye of the Killing Storm is a sacred city of a group of Southeastern steppe nomads who worship a Lunar. Blood Nail, that name sounds familiar from earlier on, but I can’t quite place it… anyway, nice to see Prasad’s expansion causing more people trouble, and that the Pure Way still cares enough to try and deal with Lunars and their worshippers.

                                        Mount Namas, that was definitely mentioned some time before- ah, of course, it’s the Dominion of the Seven Fang Vanguard’s leaders, an ancient mountain that has been a site for war and death spirit meetings since time immemorial. I like the picture of Seven Obsidian Leopard as being so out of place in the modern Age that it’s better to spend all the time among violent gods and ancient ghosts.

                                        Luz Liura is a Wyld zone located in the middle of the Southern desert, creating a private little patch of green for Klesamra Lotus-Seed, her hermit crabfolk followers and the fae she’s made common cause with. I get a tickle from this one playing off of old ideas about how Lunars relate to the Wyld and its inhabitants, even if many in the Silver Pact are supposed to think that it’s crossing a line to toss raksha against the Realm. I’m also all for Fair Folk noble names.

                                        Fortress of the Fulgurite Spire is a more isolated class of Western dominion, which makes it fitting as the home for that weirdo Ul the Burning Eye. I’m not sure whether his human experimentation with diseases on his own population is necessarily all right just because he has a lot of magic in place to ensure no casualties… I suppose it wouldn’t go too much farther to presume that he secured the consent of the people before doing so. I think the description of the fort’s self-removal defences is pretty cool, and I like imagining the Realm trying to launch lightning assaults on it before it can get away.

                                        And finally the Star Jasmine Pavilion, the luxurious estate of the hedonistic Wings of Ivory, who really managed to make the most of a deed name fitting his outlook for his tern spirit shape; it gives me this comical images of some of his ternfolk attendants relaying reports on those sea birds being annoying vermin, and he firmly insists that they’re majestic and beautiful creatures. His manner is a bit similar to Leviathan’s, but I’d say he’s sufficiently different in his motives for keeping to himself, and more likely to be angry at Realm expansion because of how it infringes upon his lifestyle rather than any outrage concerning their history or excesses.



                                        And that’s it, that’s the Dominions chapter. There’s been some really great stuff in here; it recalls to me the best of Masters of Jade, wherein the subject matter (in this case how Lunars relate to and occasionally use mortal populations for conflict with or refuge from the Realm) is used as an opportunity to reveal more of the setting to us, with creative portraits and focused write-ups. But it even surpasses some of that to me, with the depth of human character to the societies, the variety of their formats and how they’re written, and the multi-faceted conflicts that can be found all across Creation. It’s a wonderful chapter, even for those who wouldn’t be interested in the agendas of the Silver Pact itself, and it’s been my pleasure to read it.




                                        Last Artifact now, Weirdflame, and I’m truly intrigued to see what might come in the twin firsts of an Artifact flame piece that is both moonsilver and 5-dots.


                                        Hmm, interesting backstory; the idea of going out to the Wyld to set up a workshop, using the local life forms as raw materials, and when the thing finally falls apart under the strains of chaos converting it into a weapon, it sounds very reminiscent of some old points about Lunars having a preference for manufacturing tools that can be out of reach and deconstructed behind them. The manner in which the weapon is described as having been used sounds like a real horror show, although its physical design sounds quite lovely.

                                        Huh, Spark of Madness sounds like a more direct intrusion into the wielder’s personality than most 5-dot Artifacts, but I guess that’s what you get for harnessing raw Wyld. So yeah, damage your own mental health for a day in order to stoke its internal flames sufficient to shoot continuously and without firedust. Oh, and it already gets into violent warping’s of targets, being able to make a not very hard gambit to do things like twist a person into a terrifying monstrosity, twist their limbs and body parts into non-functional, malignant forms, or burn away all of their distinguishing features, with resonant wielders getting the option to actually burn them as they do it. Some of these sound like they’d actually be a good model for the powers of other beings, such as Erembour’s horn. And Lunars can go and focus their natural transformative powers, good and bad, through the piece getting, to do so at range and without needing to share their blood.

                                        Ah jeez, with a later Evocation, the wielder can get a powerful improvement to attempts to emotionally hurt people that they’ve twisted with the weapon’s flame, to the point of being able to drive them hysterical about the state that they’re in. Huh, I hadn’t actually looked at the duration of the transformation power, but… I’m guessing there’ll be a later Evocation to make them longer than a day, if it’s possible for the inflicted Hysteria to be matched to the alteration to last longer than a few days.

                                        Twisting Ash Brand has a nice description of how it achieves its effect, that idea of spraying a cloud of multi-coloured smoke, it sounds very showy and ominous, looks good as a basis for setting them up to be violently transformed with the reduced gambit difficulty. Ah, and here’s the one for extending the transformations; really love the imagery of a perversion of the phoenix symbolism, and I don’t think the basis for making the afflictions ongoing is all that harsh, not for what you’d be doing to them (and if you ever want the motes back without withdrawing the curse, you can always go put them out of their misery).

                                        Come to think of it… ah yes, here’s where we get the good stuff that can arise from not tying all mutations, good and bad, under the same subsystem, so that even the bad ones feel like they need to do something useful.

                                        And the last of its powers written (although at Essence 4, probably not the furthest it can go), Wyld Nightmare Inferno; create a delightfully large conflagration of mutating flame, that weakens the integrity of people’s bodies against the other transformative attacks, and can even inflict such corruption itself if it damages them enough. I especially like the point about the wielder being immune to it while under the influence of the first Evocation, that image of an individual striding through rainbow streaked flame with a mad glint in their eye.



                                        Weirdflame was a good one to end on, a nice method for Lunar’s to harness power from the Wyld, another example of a rather fearsome and appalling weapon, and a bit of an inspiration as moonsilver devil casters go. I’m imagining a few more of them already; one involves shooting lightless spectral flames from stealth, being able to create zones of darkness by drawing nearby light sources to feed the weapon, and culminating in being able to build up a big charge that gets fired off at an opponent, which in addition to burning them, envelops them in darkness. I’ve got a whole strix stealth hunter theme in mind for it, it would be distinct from soulsteel.


                                        Not much to say about the Artifacts in summary, other than they continue to do a good job of fulfilling the promise of Evocations.

                                                    Looking back, I think that Second Edition Stamina was one of the more solid Attributes, with a decent combination of mechanical rigour and a few unique elements, so I’ll be very interested to see how that’s built upon in this new milieu.



                                                    For its Heart’s Blood, there is Hero’s Heart Challenge, finally giving Lunars a means of acquiring human shapes through contests of physical skill. I find the fact that it requires not just victory (which is probably not too hard in most cases), but accepting a Major Tie of respect to effectively prevent it from being a go-to form for the sacred hunt, even within the range of targets that probably wouldn’t acquiesce to a physical contest in the first place. It means that the shapes you acquire can be something that can format your characterisation, and possibly even create impulses and vulnerabilities that might not be wanted, and thus precludes taking on that particular shape; conversely, it might create a book door to some story hooks, as a player’s character forms a significant investment in some random guard. And for the player who wants to get a bit deeper into the roleplaying side of it, they can always reserve their judgement for whether or not to form the Tie for the other character’s actual performance, trying to genuinely assess in the course of playing through the contest whether their character would find the target respectable.


                                                    That taken care of, we start Stamina proper off with the Defence Charms, beginning with Impenetrable Beast-Armour, which seems rather decent to me as a starter with the capacity to gain at least 3 extra soak or their Stamina in Hardness, even if it is only against a single attack (and even for that, I do enjoy the image of a Lunar’s skin functioning as a kind of reactive armour). Like the Dexterity Charms, I appreciate how it suggests a number of formats for the armouring to take, so that people don’t need to have their cosmetics dictated to them.

                                                    Speaking of Dexterity, Durant Quicksilver Adaptation seems to return some of its spirit of mild cheating, with a permanent upgrade to the Stamina Excellency to allow it to be applied to enhance soak when an attack has already successfully hit, so that they don’t need to commit to knocking the withering damage down a few pegs until they know they’ll need it. And there’s an interchange between the two Attributes as Steel Paw Style permits Stamina to be used for Parry calculation as a permanent upgrade, albeit with the exclusion of light weapons. I can’t imagine that’s a balance issue, so I’ve got to admire their commitment to maintaining the imagery behind the change, as the deflection style of a knife or rapier would be the one out of all of them that wouldn’t fit to the modified form of meeting force with force.

                                                    Den Warden Method provides an unconventional enhancement to defend other, as a Lunar whose Parry is overcome becomes able to provide their own soak and Hardness (including magical bonuses of the time) as a substitute for that of their ward against the attack intended for them. Mind you, it’s still an attack that will roll damage against the protected target, so it probably can’t be the Lunar literally imposing their body before the attack; I’d be inclined to view it as a sturdy Lunar in defence of another being able to extend that hardiness mystically through the principle of their defence (it kind of puts me in mind of some Earthbending stuff, that element of physicality imposed through attitude). It has a Protean benefit for shapes with what I believe is a new Merit, Immense Guardian… no, it’s actually something that the core had particular to the yeddim (as far as I’m willing to check back), which basically allows the animal to provide the effect of this Charm, so that it basically has an alternative effect of penalising the damage roll against the ward (which I’m choosing to see as a matter of an attack that gets past something like a yeddim being forced to be somewhat clumsier).

                                                    Unmoving Aurochs Defense… ah, this one wasn’t previewed officially as such, but I recall playtesters mentioning the principle of it, the idea of a Lunar being able to prevent themselves from being moved by an external force by literally modifying their weight. Given how it can be applied at basically any point where the possibility of being knocked back is reasonably certain, it sounds like a pretty absolute defence against that tactic. I like how it says that the increase is literal enough to actually make the integrity of the Lunar’s footing an issue, which apart from sometimes being a basis for good stunts (I’m picturing a Lunar upon a tightrope who would sooner be heavy enough to make it snap than knocked off altogether, because at least with the former you can hold on and swing down), sounds like it could be leveraged to make the denial of knockback less of a concern, by choosing the right spot to make the defence into a liability.

                                                    Into Essence 2 with Enduring Mammoth Bastion, which seems a rather intricate defence; the Lunar gets to compare only elements of an enemy’s Initiative in their damage roll to Hardness to determine whether it can be attempted, ignoring bonuses to damage from other sources; even if it’s a magical attack made without rolling Initiative, if the attacker’s Initiative is lower, then that will be what is compared to the Hardness. It has an Initiative cost, but only if the Lunar is themselves armoured. I’m not sure how viable it is with the order in which Charms can be activated, but it gives me a picture of a kind of attack arms race; the Lunar raises Hardness after an attack hits, the enemy supplements their damage with magic, and the Lunar gets to say that extra damage doesn’t count and their Hardness wins. It feels like a decent amount of Stamina is also going to be about denial of opposing advantages.

                                                    As an extension of my point about Lunar adaptive armour, Invulnerable Moonsilver Carapace has been one that I always liked, admittedly because I’ve always pictured it as resembling Greed from Fullmetal Alchemist armouring himself in carbon nanotubes or whatever. I actually want to compare it directly to its prior form… right, so it’s a much less immediately extreme Charm now, being lower Essence and not providing a huge soak boost that could stack with armour. But it also has its own benefits that I think bring it out ahead; it’s scene long for a commitment of ten motes rather than costing six motes for each round that you use it, it still provides the benefits of heavy Artifact armour with the added benefit of soak that will hold up to the Lunar’s Stamina in points against effects that would otherwise ignore it, and while it won’t stack with pre-existing armour, the description of using the best of both their traits makes me think it’s possible that when it’s fused with a pre-existing one, it might alleviate the mobility penalty? It definitely lets you keep using the Evocations of your magical armour while possibly improving its traits and releasing the attunement cost so that the Lunar isn’t burdened with committed motes. And to top it all off, the Charm can be repurchased in the form of Evocations for the extruded armour (which can potentially make them cheaper than the powers of true Artifacts), with themes from things like personality, spirit shape and Caste (and I’d say those can be some fairly diverse themes). So… it’s basically a Lunar version of Glorious Solar Plate now, but with a few differences (the limit based on soak ignoring effects being a big one, as well as being available at earlier Essence), and I think that between those and the potential offered for Evocations in spirit shapes, it ends up distinguishing itself admirably, and ultimately being a good realisation of the concept. To top it off, while a Simple Charm, it can be reflexively activated alongside Deadly Beastman Transformation.

                                                    Moonsilver Thew Exertion allows the mass alteration concept to start being applied to grapples, as the Lunar gets to reroll failed dice in either resisting an enemy’s control roll (representing becoming too heavy to shift) or in making their own (representing becoming a difficult press on the opponent), and also allowing resistance to be made at all against the grapple attempts of attackers with Legendary Size, even if it doesn’t provide the power to make such attempts back at them; I’m getting a fun picture of a tyrant lizard that tries to hoist a Lunar aloft, only to be caught off guard by how much heavier they are than they look. And Stone Rhino’s Skin provides some more adaptive defence, as a successful hit or a hazard that is not evaded can be made to subtract 1s on its damage roll from its successes (although the Charm is made to cost more when exerted against decisive attacks). In the spirit of the attacker who is disheartened or unbalanced when they strike somebody that is a stone wall against them, the Charm also robs a bit of Initiative from an attacker if the result deals no damage (which can also be a potential sweetener if the success removal effect didn’t play out).

                                                    Frenzied Bear Fortification gets a start on a bit of that Lunar berserker quality that I’m hoping to see in a big way later on, as they can make it that the value of their wound penalties becomes added to their soak and Hardness, the latter of which won’t stack with armour or animal shapes, but will with other Charms; plus, being animals that are Berserkers themselves allow the wound penalty to be subtracted from the Overwhelming quality of withering attacks. Unyielding Silver Sentinel then goes and makes the protection against knockback even more comprehensive, by being able to pay 2 Initiative and commit 3 motes to allow that effect to persist for the length of a scene, so long as the Lunar is never crashed.

                                                    Hmm, up to Essence 3 already. Quicksilver Aegis Embodiment continues the element of Lunar imperviousness, as invoking the Instant Charm makes soak straight up impossible to reduce, as well as allowing it to apply against normally unsoakable attacks (withering ones, not for how decisive attacks are unsoakable as a rule). Like some of the prior Charms, it has an Initiative cost that is waived if used while unarmoured, which also makes its Perilous keyword non-applicable.

                                                    And we see a return of what I always found to be one of the more flavourful Charms, Weapon-Trapping Body Dominion. It makes use of some of the new mechanics, as it functions as a clash attack made with Stamina and Resistance, with half of Hardness in bonus dice, which can not only ward off the attack, but doubles up by disarming them as the weapon becomes lodged in the Lunar’s body; pulling it out is possible, but takes either a disarm gambit, or a feat requiring Strength 5. Furthermore, the Charm can be effective even against enemies that attacked with their limbs or claws or the like, as the striker becomes caught in the Lunar’s flesh in the form of an automatically successful grapple that rolls Stamina + Brawl, Martial Art or Resistance for its control roll, which also gets to benefit from double 9s. The drawback of all of this is that it entails a certain ready access for the Lunar’s body, i.e. that it can’t be used while wearing any armour that wasn’t made from the Lunar themselves. Still, while I can’t remember the older version of the Charm precisely, I think this one manages to be fairly sophisticated, making good use of new systems to give the process a bit more back and forth, and with a nice, freaky benefit for the event of being attacked by somebody using their fists.

                                                    Let’s see… Steadfast Yeddim Meditation allows the Lunar to add their Hardness to the value of their soak against an attack if it’s made by an enemy with lower Initiative, and can deny them the Initiative they get just for having landed the attack if their damage is reduced to its minimum, which also gets to be a bit cheaper if the Lunar is unarmoured. I want to reserve full judgement for that caveat until I see whether Solars got similar benefits, but I’m really liking how it turns up so far.

                                                    And Wounded But Never Down is looking like some kind of cross between a heightened defence and a healing power… when decisive damage is rolled, Lunar gets to spend motes to negate the damage applied up to a reasonably high limit, setting it aside as a special pool. It’s not an Instant Charm, so the motes for holding off that damage become committed, and the negated damage heals at a not very slow rate of one level every two days, which can still be subjected to magic that accelerates or applies healing. It can only be used once per scene unless reset by incapacitating an enemy with an Initiative resetting attack, and can be stacked up to Essence times to keep dumping damage into the pool, which can otherwise last indefinitely. If the Lunar drops the commitment, damage remaining in the pool returns to their health track. And if they activate it while in a Berserker animal shape, their wound penalty can subtract from the Charm’s hefty 5 Initiative cost. I’ve often wondered at the idea of a power to provide a means of not exactly stopping or healing injury, but setting it aside for a while; I think that this one does an admirable job of it, particularly with the reset condition giving the sense that it’s pushed by somewhere between a steadfast duty and a stubborn anger.

                                                    Unstoppable Juggernaut Incarnation feels like a bit of Lunar emulation of their patron, at least as that deity was presented in Glories of the Most High (or maybe they had a Charm like this before, I don’t know; I’m pretty sure Luna did), as they get to invoke a defence against a decisive attack or environmental hazard that will ensure that it can’t deal any more damage to them than would cross over wound penalty types. It’s costly at ten motes, but against the right kind of attack it can probably be incredibly frustrating. It’s also a bit of a gambit, as it has to be invoked before knowing if the attack would have inflicted that much damage in the first place, but relieves that burden a bit by such a failure not consuming its use limit; otherwise it can only be done once a day.

                                                    Getting into Essence 4 now, with the delightfully names Indestructible God-Monster Incarnation. Invoked for a scene and including three levels of anima in the cost (which is referenced in the flavour text with the striking image of the anima peeling away with skin to present hints of an indestructible body), it provides a scene long enhancement of half of Essence added to soak and Hardness, improving the soak enhancement of the Stamina Excellency by allowing each mote spent to increase it by two (and also breaking the cap a little bit), and discounting a number of preceding Charms for similar effects by one mote. The drawback is that these benefits are reserved only for the unarmoured Lunar.

                                                    Unflagging Predator Vitality lets the Lunar simply halve the damage provided by a withering attack, but the real benefit is in the special protection it provides to the Lunar’s Initiative; under the auspices of the Charm, a withering attack won’t be able to crash the Lunar, so long as the enemy inflicting it doesn’t have more than the Lunar’s current Willpower above them in Initiative, a protection that can be afforded to other means of an enemy stripping Initiative (although it won’t work for the Lunar spending their own Initiative). I think there’s a nice image to this one, of a certain relentlessness to a Lunar unless faced with an enemy of such a greater advantage that something in the Essence that defers to them. It can’t be used with armour, but if in the shape of an animal with Impenetrable Armour, an enemy need to have even more Initiative (generally going to be nine or ten) to successfully crash them.

                                                    Ahh, and now one I’m very excited to see updated, Wound-Mastering Body Evolution. Heh, it’s actually a more straightforward Charm than even several of the preceding ones; the Lunar who suffers a* decisive attack gets to incorporate the insight into the enemy’s killing internet to give themselves twice their Essence in a bonus to their soak, isn’t susceptible to Overwhelming damage, and a whopping twenty Hardness. It costs one Initiative every round that it’s up to maintain for the scene, but so long as you keep the pressure on the enemies whose attacks will basically start bouncing off of you… Anyway, I think that it’s a good choice to really streamline the concept of this Charm into a costly basis for vastly improved defence. The Second Edition concept of being able to immunise yourself a specific weapon or form of attack was… interesting, but so damn weird, and probably way too finickity to keep track of over time. No, better to focus it down to the essentials.


                                                    Okay, Defence Charms look good overall, a nice sense of adaptive defences. Still, a more utilitarian category than a deeply flavourful one, so not much else to say about it.
                                                    Wasn’t too hard to get through that, but… keep breaking them up. I can make good time if I just don’t take three hours before looking at any of the rest. Back in a tick!
