        Originally posted by Eolirin View Post
        That's only a source of tension in a Sidereal game; a Solar circle or a Dragon Blooded or a Lunar is highly unlikely to be party to Bronze/Gold politics. The extent to which those groups are exposed to Sidereal thought on the matter of the Usurpation, it'll be in the context of Bronze Faction party line or Gold Faction guilt. That makes that particular conflict relatively meaningless as a matter of actionable choices on the part of those parties.
        Well, yeah, the Bronze/Gold thing is mostly a concern for Sidereals. However, the impact the Usurpation had on Creation is one of the major reasons the landscape looks like it does, and the Bronze and Gold Factions are now in a position to fight the old battle all over again- only this time, it's not as clear who's going to be on whose side.... nd that's going to shape what the coming conflicts look like in the near future of Creation.

        For example, take the Lunars. Back in the First Age, most of them were the undisputed allies and lieutenants of the Solar Exalted, and they were hunted down by the Bronze Faction all the way to the edge of Creation because of that. In the modern era, however, many of the Lunars have established a pretty cozy arrangement for themselves, and the return of the Solar Exalted (not to mention the Infernal and Abyssal Exalted) is going to mean an end to that lifestyle... unless, of course, they can get some allies in Heaven to help watch their backs. This, ironically, might put a few Lunars on the same side as the guys who once hunted them down, just because it will give them a bulwark against the coming tide of Solar-ness.

        On the other hand, you might also see a few of the Dragon-Blooded Houses in the Realm rethinking this whole "hunt down the Anathema" thing, in spite of what the Bronze Fa- I mean, the Immaculate Order says, just because having a Solar or two on their side could make a big difference against the other Houses in the coming Civil War. Obviously, this is more likely among the more corrupt or desperate Houses- Iselsi, Tepet, Cynis and Nellens all come to mind- but the Time of Tumult is likely to be a time of strange bedfellows.

        At the end of the day, though, one thing is quite clear: the time of the Bronze Faction's dominance is coming to a close. In the face of all this change, it's exceedingly unlikely that the Dragon-Blooded will be able to reassert their dominion, and the Solars (along with their Infernal and Abyssal brethren) will slowly begin their takeover of Creation. At that point, it's just a matter of what the Bronze Faction is willing to do, and what deals it's willing to make, to prevent the ascent of the Solar Exalted.

        Except they weren't decent odds, they were terribly long shot odds, with the whole of Creation at stake. Taking that bet would have been extremely irresponsible; Exalted is several shades darker than an action hero movie.
        Well, the actual odds were never stated, but what we do know was that the Bronze Vision was much better odds than the Gold Vision. Exactly how much better is sort of irrelevant, unless you have a very specific threshold of risk that you're not willing to cross. The point was that the Sidereals had two choices: play it safe with the knowledge that the world would probably continue to subsist, albeit in a reduced state, or risk everything on the possibility that you could reunite the Solar Host and bring them back on the straight and narrow.

        It's a pretty classic dilemma, and I'm not entirely sure how I'd resolve it. I guess it's a matter of what you're willing to pay for a chance at paradise.

                            This faction t-shirt thing is a tad hyperolic.

                            The Fivescore Fellowship had their visions. One of them was unequivocally agreed upon as "Kill the Solars". Not kill most of them. Not kill the bad ones. The Solars, all of them. Not all Sidereals in the room thought that was the plan like Tammiz. They didn't make it out of Heaven to tell anyone else. So it wasn't so much "Sidereals united as team Sidereal." It was "Bronze united as Sidereals who were left." And frankly, with mass homicide, has a bit of "We're all in" to this.

                            As noted also, herding Celestial Exalted via guile and subtlety, while doable, is also like herding cats. Lunar Exalted probably had a range fo positions in relation to Solars. Secondary rulers, Bene Gezerett-style badass righthands, mentors, squires, one man secret police, even the asshole who hates their Solar and spends time on the other side of Creation from his Solar companion (and thus not likely to be contactable in the time scales Siderals might be working with). Regardless of actual job the Lunars are doing for Solars, or lack there of, they are defined as a group as number two. The only way to get higher power is to be the guy in power. This is as a group probably fairly invested in how things are and being human, like being in power and luxury. The guys who don't get along with their Solar probably are still living it fairly large more the sense of reputation, personal power, and maybe a cheque to stay away.

                            This is a group defined historically as individualistic, contrarian and is about as good as you at spy games. That asshole in a forest retret in the East while his Solar companion runs her island in the West and they haven't talked for centuries might be spearheading a spying project for her out there and securing supply networks her way. Out of the three hundred or so Lunars, maybe there's one that could side with you. But he has to have a combination of 1) Available, 2) Actually trustworthy, 3) Useful and 4) Having nothing to lose that may simply not be there. And the more caveats you need in a co-conspirator, out of a group that is bound to have folks trying to fuck your conspiracy if they find out, the less helpful it'll be to your goals. Even the seemingly most sympathetic to your cause Lunar might turn around and sell you out for the bonus. Being the Lunar who single-handedly undid the entire SIdereal conspiracy, with lifespans he has to deal with, means that he'll be an issue if things fall through thosuand years from now. And you'll likely still be dead.

                            It's all down to risk rewards. The Gold part of the risk is already handled, they're dead or in line with threat of death. The Dragon-Blooded are a bit less of a risk since they seem unhappy with things as-is and the prophecy guarantees they'll get some success. The Solar Exalted aren't on the table as the Prophecy said they have to die and we're not told of any caveats to this. Which leaves of the classical four Lunars, the group that is most entrenched in Solar rule, gets the most out of it, loses the msot in its collapse and on top of all that probably complicit if not outright part of the problem of Solar crazy-personhood. Even discounting the abandonment of all things 2e background, stuff like the Black Nadir Conpact and the Five Metal Shrike I think were enough co Solar-Lunar projects that Lunar Exalted were not an option of replacements due to just likely more of the same.

                            To that end, you have a group that has most to lose, least to gain and least to offer in the whole thing. Your conpsiracy needs the least possible issues, as to get rid of the Solars requires you do it right or you start on one of the other paths. It's a bit of Muad'Dib's problem really. The Golden Path was lain with bodies and he wanted nothing to do with it. Too fucking bad, Leto II did what was needed, and it was ugly and threw a lot of folks under the bus. The Sidereals aren't in much better a position in that.
