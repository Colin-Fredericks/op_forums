            As a note, the devs have stated that Liminals have Aspects, not castes. I want to say that they posted a working definition of the difference at some point, but I don't know.
            Ooof. That makes this whole description a bit difficult, because without knowing much about Liminals, we only have one example of aspects, so it's hard for us to say what makes them different.

            That said, the basic idea of defining Lunars differently from most other Exalted seems good, and I think that in particular changing the basic way they're divided up could help with the problem that we have so many exalted divided up into Fighty Class / Talky Class / Casty Class that it often feels like Lunars have nowhere to go. (Most of the other splats' castes are better-defined on those axes than Lunars are and, given that Lunars are Ability-based, they're probably better-defined that way than Lunars are ever going to be -- defining the caste that gets Strength solely as the Fighty Caste is skipping over a bunch of stuff that they should be good at; defining the caste that gets Intelligence solely as the Wizard Caste is, too.)

            I'd say that aspects are more defined by thematic statements about what they are rather than what they do; and that seem to be an interesting idea for Lunars. However, you'd still need interesting definitions, and (given that they only have three aspects, unless that changes, too) they'd have to be fairly broad to meaningfully cover all the attributes.

                        Originally posted by Omicron View Post
                        Castes and Aspects approach the causality of their talents in opposite way.

                        You become a Dawn Caste because you have certain qualities; you have certain qualities because you are a Fire Aspect. Obviously there is nuance to this, but I feel this has a grain of truth. There is that feeling as a Dragon-Blooded that using the powers of a different Aspect is partaking of a completely different nature than your own, both in the departure from your usual aesthetics and elements and from the Essence surcharge you suffer for it.

                        Dividing Lunars into Aspects would suggest this, to my mind - that a Lunar enters an Aspect to become. Rather than having elders scrutinize your life and deeds and deducing which Caste would best fit you, you choose which qualities you desire, which powers you seek. The power of the Lunar Exaltation is raw and protean, and the Aspects of the moon are ways of channeling it towards a more specific purpose, and you make that purpose, instead of it being an extrapolation of who you are.

                        So, a scholar who must find the power to go at the heart of the Realm to rescue his daughter sold in slavery might ask to become a Changing Moon; it is not who he is, but it is who he needs to be.

                        And in turn, once taken, the Aspect informs your nature and shapes you. There is a spark of lunacy in you, and its form is moulded by the Aspect you have taken.
                        I like it. Bearing in mind that I speak from the position of someone with no practical experience of how Lunars worked throughout the history of Exalted... the idea of them choosing their Castes/Aspects based not on who they are, but who they need to be in order to survive, protect and thrive appeals to me.

                                            Castes/aspects/etc. serve two main purposes. First and most importantly, they’re there to give structure to an Exalt type to make them more interesting to the player and to facilitate character creation. Second, they’re useful shorthand to describe characters: knowing that you’re up against “a Dawn and a Twilight” is a lot more evocative and informative than “two Solars”. When you’re discussing how to divide Lunars, your priorities should be into making them as interesting and easy to parse as possible.

                                            I could write an entire essay on why Lunar Castes are shit, but I’ll be brief and boil it down to three things: they’re overly restrictive, they’re unclear, and there’s too few of them. Let’s hit the points one by one.

                                            First off, remember that the main purpose of a Caste/Aspect/Whatever is to provide a structure that’s evocative and makes creating the character you want easier. What the Caste provides is a skeleton that you build around: it’s the marrow, not the meat of what you are. For Solars, a player who is into all of his Caste Stuff and defines himself narrowly according to it is very unusual: the Castes are designed to be used so that you create “a Zenith leader who is also a master swordsman and has an abiding interest in finding and translating old texts” or “a Twilight craftsman who uses clever gadgets to hunt and kill the minions of Malfeas in Chiaroscuro”. The Caste is not who you are or what you do. The Caste is about you having a core aptitude for a certain number of things that you can raise to 5 immediately and learn cool Charms for.
                                            Lunar Castes are a straightjacket. They lock you very firmly into a McCombo package of Attributes with redundant competencies that say very firmly “you are physical/mental/social-focused”. The inability to raise Attributes instantly (as Solars do with Abilities) and the high cost of raising them, the higher cost of unfavored Charms (everything outside your narrow Caste McCombo), and the fact that the system encourages specialization means that players are punished very harshly for stepping beyond the archetype—for making your character interesting! The 3 Caste / 1 Favored spread is one of the dumbest things in Lunars.

                                            Second, Lunar Castes are simultaneously about Attribute lines (Physical/Social/Mental) and about roles (Warrior/Trickster/Shaman). Physical/Social/Mental as the basis of Castes is crap, because it’s about as evocative as an engineering manual. Other than surface symmetry (which should never be your main design criteria) there are no good reasons whatsoever to associate Lunar archetypes to the Attribute lines. Any reworking of Castes should address this as a priority.
                                            Warrior/Trickster/Shaman is better, but still problematic. ‘Warrior’ on its own is not good enough: it’s too generic and stale (fun exercise: picture the Solar Castes being called Warrior/Priest/Wizard/Rogue/Bard). From a systems design standpoint it’s also a problem: for a game that is as heavily combat-centric as Exalted (compare the pagecount devoted to combat v. that for any other system aspect), presenting one of the options as the superior combat option is a mistake. The fact that a Twilight (Scholar) can match the Dawn (General) in combat is an important feature. ‘Shaman’ is also weird—what is it that they do, again? Commune with nature and pray to spirits? Is this kind of thing even in their Charmset? Is there any Lunar that shows what it means to be a Shaman anywhere in the Exalted line?

                                            Third, three base archetypes is too little. Other than sticking to Attribute lines for surface symmetry, which I’ve already condemned as stupid, there’s no reason to limit Lunars to three castes. Alchemicals, which are also Attribute-based, get six and are the better for it. Most other Exalted types get five, which affords players a much greater variety in terms of what kind of Caste (skeleton) they want to build their characters around. Whether we go for Castes (e.g., ‘Hunter’, ‘Trickster’, ‘Leader’, ‘Spy’) or Aspects (this would be a hell of a lot easier if Lunars didn’t have a personal animal totem), three archetypes isn’t enough to cover the spectrum of cool characters you can build using the system.

                                                            All, Just my two cents, and a quickly thought out idea, but I figure that it might be an interesting idea to discuss nonetheless. What effect would modeling the Lunar Exaltation more on the attributes have? Specifically, during character gen. we have to choose an attribute spread that's favors one attribute type (Physical/Social/Mental), is a pretty even spread to the other, and handicaps the unfavored attribute type. Why not model Lunars on this? e.g. - Your Caste is your star attribute type (whether or not the abilities are reworked for the castes is immaterial and irrelevant), you then either pick a second Caste or three different attributes as your so-so attribute type, and then the last three are your unfavored. The crunch effect of this would be (potentially) XP cost and training times. So, a Full Moon (Physical) Lunar that favors the Changing Moon would pay 8 for Physical Attribute based charms, 9 for Social Attribute based charms, and 10 for Mental Attribute charms (Or 8/10/12, 6/10/14 or whatever normalizes the XP curve) and the training time could be Days/Weeks/Months (or some other calculation) to reinforce the attribute based nature of Lunars, while encouraging out-of-caste Charm purchases, but maintaining a focus on the initial attribute focus of the caste. Thoughts?
