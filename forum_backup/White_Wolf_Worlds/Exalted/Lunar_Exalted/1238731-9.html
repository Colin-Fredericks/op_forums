                            I wrote this a few days ago and may as well post it rather than delete it.
                            The false equivalence between potential threshold successes and additional raw damage:
                            On the mortal level each point of dexterity is worth 1 dice or half a success, this means that for every 2 points of dexterity you have over your opponent you can expect to get an additional threshold success. It’s not really comparable to Strength for enhancing attack damage but more importantly it offers no advantage towards an opponent on a comparable skill level.

                            It gets murkier when we come to Exalts; a Solar can overpower a Dragon-Blooded on simple math the ability to add up to 10 dice is better than the ability to add up to 6 dice. There’s scope for the Dragon-Blooded to be the more skilled of the two but still be overwhelmed by raw power.

                            There’s really a trap element to the assertion that Dexterity helps you with damage because threshold successes probably won’t be there when you actually need them.


                            I'm going to use an example with one of my favourite NPCs from the rulebook, the Nephwrack. Their opponent is a Solar with dual light weapons Dexterity/Martial Arts 5, specialty but only strength 2 because who needs it, right?
                            So the Solar rolls their withering attack with a full excellency and a stunt bonus achieving an average of 14 successes, hitting the Nephwrack by 8 for a total of 20 damage, three more than their opponent's soak but still dealing only the same amount of damage as their Onslaught rating meaning all those additional successes were probably pointless.
                            The reason I use the Nephwrack as an example is because it’s not even that tough while the Solar is approaching the pinnacle of their ability for making attacks based on pure skill and shows how much high stamina and good armour can potentially stonewall such a character build.

                            Tangent on the true power of the Nephwrack because I love this evil bastard if he's used properly.
                            If the Solar couldn't reliably resist Dawn of Dead Stars they'd be in a pretty precarious situation, where they're steadily losing 2 points of initiative a round to an opponent they can only reliably do 3 dice of damage to. Which might not sound like a complete disaster because they're probably going to get lucky long before the Nephwrack slowly drains them enough to finish them off with Soul Destruction, except that the Nephwrack also has Mists of Eventide at its disposal that imposes a similar draining effect on the combat and incapacitates people it crashes... that could be a problem.

                            This isn’t intended to be an argument for Strength being equivalent to Dexterity just highlight how finesse isn’t always a substitute for raw power.

                                    I had a big post, but I've been writing it and deleting it a lot.

                                    Anyway, addressing my own suggestion:

                                    I dismissed it myself because it's not that simple. One of the things you have to watch out for (and the OP's suggestion does while mine does not) is not leaving Stamina out in the cold. You don't want to strip tanks of the ability to tank just so brutes can break things better.

                                    While increasing raw damage and overwhelming to be more Strength dependent and less weapon dependent is a good thing (to me at least, to both make Strength more valuable, generate results more like people imagine, and make weapon selection less optimization focused), doing it in a vacuum isn't. It's a band-aid for what needs to be a much more thorough recalibration of the combat system to down play weapons, and increase the function of Strength and Stamina.

                                    One of the things I've grown to appreciate a lot about the Storypath system's evolution of the Tag based equipment, is that there's no base equipment stat blocs. All weapons and armor (at least those on the same Scale and not masterworks/Relics, but those boosts are still straight forward) work on the exact same rules except for what Tags they have. As well the system actually has a straight forward method for designing weapons/armor (Tags have a value, and normal stuff has a cap of 3) to your desires.

                                    You can't lift the Storypath Tag system into Exalted directly (the combat systems are too different), but I think you'd have to do something similar to make weapon/armor selection less important, and thus give more space for Strength and Stamina to feel like they matter in combat. Inflating the numbers alone tends to make things more cumbersome than fun.
