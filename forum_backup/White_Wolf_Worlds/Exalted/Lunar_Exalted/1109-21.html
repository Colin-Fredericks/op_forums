            Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post

            Assumptions of Comptenence/Incompetence.

            I believe the Solar Exalted were not reduced to such a degree by the Great Curse. SOME, perhaps, were... but the vast majority were more-or-less mostly lucid. They behaved in a more or less logical fashion, tainted by extreme virtues.

            None of this implies a gross underestimation of the people that serve them, except in perhaps specific cases.
            Lucid beings do not seriously make plans to dissolve the world into chaos so that they can remake it in an improved version. They do not unleash the Wyld upon vast areas of Creation out of boredom and the desire for military readiness. They do not plan to give every single sentient in the world the power to glass a city with a modicum of preparation and study (and yes, that was Salina's ultimate objective -- to reshape the laws of Creation so that the Circle of Adamant was available to any being who wished to learn it).

            More importantly, the point of the First Age -- or so it seems to me -- is that it had grown corrupt, decadent and mad. If the Solars were "more or less mostly lucid", then why evoke the Great Prophecy at all? Why was the Vision of Gold not the shoe-in answer, if the Solars could, by and large, be reasoned with? This isn't assuming Solar incompetence. This is assuming that they did, in fact, have flaws -- the epic flaws which come with their epic natures in the sword-and-sorcery genre, exaggerated and impelled by the death-curse of the Primordials, which laid on them most heavily of all the Exalted. And the Sidereals -- the prophets, advisors and spymasters among the Exalted Host -- used those flaws to undo them; that's not only in-genre, but also very much in their nature.

            ETA: I am aware that the vast majority of the history of the First Age will be re-mystified in 3E. I do, however, think that it will conform more to the idea of mythic, decadent god-kings, larger than life with flaws to match, than it will to real-world political theory. After all, as far as Creation is concerned, it has long passed into myth, and who but Heaven can truly say what passed in those ancient days?

                It's possible to come up with all sorts of vaguely-feasible-if-you-squint-hard-enough reasons the Usurpation can fly, but why? Let me make an argument that we should instead rethink it.

                We KNOW the real reason the Usurpation feels weird - Sidereals, and fate, were not yet defined when the 1e history was written.

                Players, and writers, had no idea what the limitations of these beings were or what they could do.

                The default archetype of the Sidereals, the 'Mysterious Xanatos Wizard', was used as shorthand.

                The problem is, Mysterious Xanatos Wizard is hyper competent. Gandalf is not really 'balanced' with everybody else. He's there to motivate the story.

                This becomes an issue later on when Sidereals are actually clearly defined, are somewhat balanced in the context of the other Exalted. Further, they do not have the ability to predict the actions of essence using beings to any serious degree (which they... and everybody else!... are keenly aware of!).

                So we have a fiction that is incompatible with the Sidereals as presented.

                This is an issue we should examine.

                It's very possible to have a Usurpation that does not rely upon the assumptions that were made pre1e Sidereals. Back before Sidereals were defined, they always benefited from 'Assumptions of Competence', excepting incidences of tragic hubris. They did what they needed to do to make the story happen, and nobody could really stop them. They are the Deus Ex Illumanati.

                We should rewrite the fiction with the actual Sidereal Exalted, as we came to understand them when they were actually examined and thought through. This would create a better story that could hew very closely to the original structure and intention.

                        I don't believe that the thesis of "Sidereals are hypercompetent Mysterious Xanatos Wizards" is supported by canon as it stands, with the original conception of the Usurpation. The thesis I instead support is "Solars were larger-than-life pulp sorcerer-kings with the flaws of larger-than-life sorcerer-kings, which the Sidereals and Terrestrials used to lay them low in an epic, world-spanning struggle." I also do not know the reason the Usurpation feels weird, because it doesn't feel weird to me; I would appreciate it if, in future, my feelings were left to me to determine.

                        I also find it extremely questionable that the developers of the line -- the original writers who conceived of the Usurpation -- had no idea of Sidereal limitations when they did so. Certainly the execution of certain 1E splats was mangled, but we do know that that was writers acting against the developer's instructions for the most part, and that Grabowski had a clear vision of the gameworld. It seems very reasonable to me that he had a general idea of the capabilities of the Sidereal Exalted when he conceived the Usurpation.

                        (I once saw, but cannot now find, a suggestion of how existing Sidereal Charms could, working in concert, be used to approximate the Great Prophecy. I found it believable, with sufficient stuntworthiness. It implies that it wasn't a single prophecy, but rather a continued aggregation of smaller prophecies which led inevitably to the three choices.)
