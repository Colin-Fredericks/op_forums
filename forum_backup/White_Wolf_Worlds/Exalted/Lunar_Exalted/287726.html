    Ornery 6000 xp Lunar exalt, when isn't it fun?

    10-20-2014, 12:31 PM
    I found my self thinking a few weeks ago that when I'm in the proper mood (i.e. surly, not feeling up to challenges that test me) that a Ornery 6000 xp lunar exalted would be the ideal character to play in an RPG. The surprising part was that it wasn't just Exalted where my grumpy longing to kick ass self wanted to play this character but in fact, as near as I could tell, every RPG game ever.

    Shadowrun,

    D&D,

    all those hundreds of just like D&D but different games

    any World of darkness game,

    legend of the five rings (oh hell yes!)

    any super hero game

    Dogs in the Vineyard

    Pantheon (yes I know that it would be impossible and I'd worse than loose I'd destroy the game- that kind of makes me want to play it more)

    Adventures of Baron von Munchausen (a drinking game of competitive lying dressed up as an rpg)

    Rune

    Paranoia

    Toon

    Star Trek ("Prime directive shrime directive - I'm going to eat his heart")

    Star wars

    Teenagers from outer space

    Call of Cuthullu

    Amber

    Ars Magica

    So if I'm an ill tempered grump can you think of an RPG where it wouldn't be cathartic to play a preposterously powerful Lunar exalted who is an ill tempered grump

    (Noblis?)
    Last edited by Apromor; 10-20-2014, 12:33 PM.

    I want to play this as a starting character, I don't even care what game.

                              Ye tiny gods. Um...huh.

                              A setting that playing a stupidly powerful shapeshifter loaded with 'screw you' abilities wouldn't be cathartic to play in....

                              Even in a setting where combat isn't the focus, it wouldn't matter. You can break out the Pretty Kitty Revolution at fairly low XP levels, and completely upend most settings by co-opting control of all the NPCs. And now that you can control the NPCs, you can make the setting as entertaining as you'd like.

                              A Lunar would straight up rock at Shadowrun, even at lower level. And at 6K XP, you'd probably have Heart's Blood from one of the Great Dragons by the end of the first session, and quietly file it away under 'less powerful forms.'

                              Star Wars, you'd overthrow or rule the Galactic Empire in a weekend.

                              Firefly: pretty much the same story against the Alliance.

                              It is theoretically possible that The Dresden Files RPG might be able to produce some critters in your weight class if your GM is creative enough...but Perfects still unbalance things ridiculously. And the fact that you have enough Essence to spam Perfects every single time something attacks

                              As others have mentioned, C'thulu would probably end up as your spiffy new pet...or you'd eat it.

                              Heck, even the My Little Pony RPG could be immensely cathartic, depending on how dark of a place you wanted to take that story. (New Motivation: Heart's Blood of ALL the Alicorns!)

                              The Game That Must Not Be Named would be...weird to say the least. But you'd win, with ease. (and hopefully you'd bring about the apocalypse)

                              Executive Decision probably wouldn't be quite as cathartic...as it is basically a game about convincing the President of the US to act a certain way during a crisis. Sure, you'd win, with zero trouble...but that still sounds boring as crap.

                                                  A 6k xp character would pretty much always be less fun than a character with far less xp, since that's so much xp that not only would you need a 200-page cheat sheet for their powers, but all their distinctiveness would have been annihilated in the course of finding things to spend XP on. At some point you'd be reduced to just taking ridiculous specialties for the hell of it.

                                                  At this point you would really want to just be playing freeform with no specific set of capabilities for your character, and even then having a character that powerful would severely narrow the pool of stories you might want to tell. In general, becoming more powerful reduces the quality of Exalted games above a certain point, and this is way, way above that point.

                                                  If all you want is to be functionally omnipotent, you would be better off just writing "functionally omnipotent" on your sheet and not pretending to use the system. But I can't imagine anybody else would want to play in the same group as a character whose concept is "functionally omnipotent ill-tempered grump".
