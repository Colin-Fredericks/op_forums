    A concern for Lunars: Alternate dice pools

    07-25-2018, 08:46 PM
    Vance has confirmed that in Lunars 3e we will be seeing new charms that will allow Lunars to attack with Strength, Defend with parry, and likely some other things. Maybe Intelligence for Guile? Who knows. These charms will be likely be endlessly replicated if not in official works then fan homebrew. Attempting to do so is like trying to stop the tides themselves. If we don't see an alternate attack stat for Sidereals, people will then complain that their favorite pet splat is left out in the dust.

    So here's my question: Why are we having what is essentially feat taxes?

    I propose that 3e would only have an improvement if we have alternate abilities for certain actions baked into the base ruleset then things would only improve from there. How you ask?

    1) As mentioned previously, people who want to play as a standard barbarian warlord no longer needs to choose between playing with what is essentially their arm cut off or paying a feat tax for having the 'privledge' of a decent dice pool for his core concept. The same thing goes for using social stats too.

    2) Dexterity is no longer the king of everything ever in combat. Making a quick rule to say that you can use Strength in place of Dexterity for Melee/Brawl attacks? Whoopie, +4 max potential damage difference! Try then fighting against a kiting character, you'd be at a major disadvantage. Dexterity would still be the best, but at least builds that don't want to be gymnasts are viable.

    3) The sooner we nip this in the bud the easier it is to build things down the line. "But Sandact! What about Warstriders!? Increasing Strength Exercise!? Animals with high strength values that Lunars can abuse! Surely this breaks the game!" Want the quick and easy fixes kids? "Only the character's natural strength is used for damage calculation. A Lunar who is in an animal form who uses a lower strength rating uses the higher one. If an animals strength is higher then the accuracy value is treated as +0". Maybe the rest as charm dice but that reeks of dice adders somewhat, but point is we can nip this in the ass now. If we wait until further down the line we'd need fixes for Gentimen's, Sidereals, Exigents, Alchemicals, Infernals, Liminals, and Cosmic Exalts. Plus add a few more in those.

    4) "Dexterity is hand-eye coordination and Strength is raw br-" Oh cut the crap. Multiple games have done this before. Because you want to know why? Balance! It promotes multiple playstyles! With all the freedom in this game it boggle my mind that this still remains! I'm asking for anything insane. I'm not saying that we should make Intelligence also work for melee attack rolls too, that doesn't make sense. Those alternate dice pool charms can be used for more interesting methods. Maybe an Ebon Dragon charm could allow an Infernal to use Manipulation as his attribute for any single roll for some cost? Maybe a Sidereal who perfectly planned out a chosen assault could use Intelligence on some rolls until his plan goes array? Maybe Strength can be used for Performance because your muscles are that good at saying things without words? But at base there should be SOME attributes that can be swapped out for such things! Why is it that multiple other RPG's allow such a thing but Exalted does not?

    [quote=Vance]Errata isn't a thing we get paid for and something we want to avoid.[quote]

    Sweetie I get it. Rolling out errata this early in a line doesn't look good. But this wound is only going to fester and get worse as time goes on. Sure its not the flesh eating disease that was paranoia combat from 2e but if we have a chance to improve 3e for the better why not take that chance? Are you really going to miss the three dollars you probably would've made typing out such stuff? Or put it into a new book as stealth errata, there will be some slight confusion but everyone would at least be happier. Trying to do the same in 2e would be very hard as you have to convert... 30 or so books of material? Right now in 3e you only have 4 (Core, Arms, DB's, Lunars, bits and pieces of Raksha and Exigents). Do you want to pick up a five pound weight or a 50 pound weight?

    Go ahead, state your thoughts below. If you think I'm wrong tell me why I'm taking crazy pills.

      Here are my thoughts:

      1. It fits for Lunars who already have all sorts of ways to bring different attributes to the party, via their stunting and excellencies. They could hit you with how pretty they are if they wanted to.

      Outside of esoteric interpretations of Black Claw style, this isn’t something other Exalts can do.

      2. It is possibly a feature of the Attribute based Charmset, and thus I’d expect Alchemicals (Unsurpassed Mathematical targeting Reticule) and Liminal Exalted (Hand-Eye co-ordination) to have similar charms in their set with different levels of ease of pulling this off.

      3. The Solar charmset (Dragonblooded too?) does similar things for Abilities, see swap Stealth for Awareness when reckoning Join Battle. As far as I know Lunars do not get this trick. So there is some level of mechanical balance there.

      Basically where you saw something that cried out for house rules inclusion (and I admit it’s a cool thing to scream “Screw you Dex, you are nothing to me!”) I see internal system consistency.

                                                          My first thought is that the central premise of your complaint remains unproven and unjustified. "This is a feat tax, which means whatever I want it to mean, so the rule need to change until I decide they aren't a feat tax, because feat taxes are bad," isn't really a compelling argument even if you expand it into a much larger post.

                                                          My second thought is that Exalted would only benefit from loosening Attributes (and Abilities) for a very specific style of play that isn't the primary focus on the game. It works great in the Storypath system, but the Storypath system is a much lighter system that doesn't require nearly as much mechanical consistency in such matters. Exalted is a very complex game, and keeping it running requires a heavier hand from the rules. That's why Exalted relies on Charms for such things (and 3e has so far avoided Merits doing it). Charm design gives the developers the controls necessary to keep everything working smoothly.
