    There's nothing wrong with having more important priorities than the Realm -- but the one thing all Lunars have in common is that the Realm is their enemy.

    You have to be pretty far from the Blessed Isle before you can afford to ignore the Realm, and the bigger your ambitions get, the more certain it is that someone Realm-affiliated will take notice of the filthy Anathema doing [insert ambitious activity here] and move to intercept. And if you start trading favors with other Lunars, you're going to end up acting directly against the Realm sooner or later. Even if you don't give a damn about the Realm, helping others fight them might end up being the fastest way to secure allies for your own interests.

    I'm not sure I entirely like the statement that the Silver Pact is "first and foremost" a weapon against Dragon-Blooded hegemony, but it probably is a natural result of the fact that every Lunar over a certain age almost certainly carries a grudge against the Realm for something.

                    Leaving aside the "If you don't care about the Realm, the Realm cares about you" side of it, and past life grudges, and mortal life, leaving aside all that, if that doesn't touch you at all, the Silver Pact and members thereof being able to effective trade you aid and support for what you do care about in exchange for joining in with their struggle will probably tend to entangle you.

                    I'm sure there are Lunars who stay totally outside those concerns, but I don't think we have to consider that all Lunars who are involved do get involved because of a personal grudge from their mortal or early Exalt lives or to mitigate the threat the Realm is actively presenting to them. Many will get persuaded in, and/or sucked in by trading support to other Lunars that doesn't seem like that big a deal at the time, etc. Social structures built around resistance and opposition can become *really* absorbing to anyone's individual identity and goals, even if you only let them in for reasons that seem totally pragmatic and non-burdensome at the time.

                        Might I suggest a slightly different interpretation?

                        The Silver Pact as a whole clearly has the Realm high on its list of concerns, in fact, the need to deal with them is probably one of the few things that have universal support among its members. However, the Silver Pact is not monolithic either - it has a number of shahan-yas aren't all that concerned with the Realm. The Pact is to the Lunars what the Realm is to the Dragonblooded, a central organization that many (though not all) exist in that has set interests that may or may not comport with any number of its component factions. I will say that I think part this issue is that a lot of the writing tends to be framed around their reaction to the Realm, as in a Shahan-ya will be mentioned as unconcerned with the Realm as opposed to concerned about the Raksha.

                        I would hope that further material will talk more about their concerns with the Raksha especially since we don't know a ton about them in this edition.

                            I'm actually pleased with how the book focuses on politics and motives that have nothing to do with the Realm.

                            Raks, Ma-ha-Suchi and Shadow Fang Vanguard are more focused on their rivalry and their political visions than they are on war with the realm. Rukhsara's is focused on the survival of her people, and the realm is only an incidental concern for her. Leviathan took a thousand-year sabbatical from the war. Sha's Oka is so fascinated/broken by the mystical nature of the Caul that he has trouble communicating to his troops. Feather Drenched in the Blood of the Fallen is more concerned with Sidereals. And Aqadar doesn't even care that he lives in the middle of a realm satrapy.

                            Also, I think it's important that the fatsplat portray a coherent set of themes and a lunar society which newbies can understand quickly and play with. A broader vision should go in other supplements, like how Arms of the Chosen had a lot of fluff on lunar heroes that had nothing to do with the Silver Pact.

What else can be done with the margins besides pointing out they exist, describing a bit of how they relate from a distance to the Pact, and dotting examples of people like that across the book?
                                    I'm hoping we see more as the text continues to be released, but how many concrete examples do we really get? This Chapter is titled "The Silver Pact," I'll grant, but there doesn't seem to be another chapter coming up that gives focus to non-Pact Lunars, unless they're in the Quick Characters. What we have includes a good bit of general talk about how you aren't forced to join the Pact, and indeed how you can end up excluded from the Pact, but most of the Pact-peripheral Lunars mentioned are in fact technically members to some degree or other, and are elders to boot.

                                    Ul the Burning Eye, for instance, is said to tell would-be students that the Realm is "a diversion from the Pact’s true aims," indicating that he considers his estrangement from the Pact a "No True Scotsman" situation and himself an exemplar of the true cause. The Shadow-Fang Vanguard definitely aren't members, but they're still focused on opposition to the Realm and still have social ties to the Pact.

                                    Merchant-prince Hibiscus Cutter is the only named character I can point to in the material we've been shown so far who's both definitely not a member and has goals unrelated to the Realm. Even with Lilith it's said that a number of Pact Lunars still consider her a member even though she's completely cut herself off from human life.

                                    Now, what I will concede is that there are several established Pact Lunars whose top priority is something other than the Realm. Anja Silverclaws' focus on Mask of Winters, Tangaxoan's focus on bringing freedom to Ixcoatli, etcetera. That's a genuinely good answer to the "mono-focused" criticism.

                                    What I'd like to see and I'm not seeing is a Casteless Lunar with a philosophical opposition to the Pact, a la Madame Vert in 2E... though hopefully not pictured as a lizard with boobs. I'm not saying I want that stance to be common, and I know the text already generally acknowledges that you can write a character who fits that role without Creation imploding, I'd just like to see one case study illustrating it.
