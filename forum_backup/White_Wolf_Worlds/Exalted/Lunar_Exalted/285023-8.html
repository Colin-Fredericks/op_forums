            When I imagine figures like Raksi, I tend towards the idea of certain fairy tale monsters and villains, where the idea that they have a tendency to eat children is present as an off-putting and uncanny detail, but may not be the focus of the tale (unless the protagonist happens to be or have a child), nor completely overwhelm the character or any interactions with or responses to them. This includes narratives where your encounter ends with you being relieved to have gotten away intact, while being... not exactly unconcerned, but not really wanting to know how the character will conduct themselves thereafter.

            It's like, I quite like the Baba Yaga from Hellboy, and don't think that everything to do with her has to focus on outrage at the fact that she's eaten children.

            I'd also say that, depending on what culture in Creation you come from, your sense of the sanctity of human life and the preciousness of children, especially the youngest infants, may sometimes lack an especially visceral sense of moral outrage at certain forms of murder, at least if it's something abstract that you're not directly exposed to. Certain prior time periods had people a bit less attached to babies who were very likely to just up and die anyway, not counting occasions where they're deliberately left to exposure because there are too many mouths to feed or have some undesirable disfiguration.

                        I've also got this image that, as a Changing Moon Caste, a figure like Ma-Ha-Suchi should be a lot like Bill Cutting from Gangs of New York in terms of how he looks to somebody who comes wanting to kill him for his crimes, in terms of how despite how he's a dreadful person who has wronged you specifically, he's also extremely charismatic and knows how to win folks over with a combination of his own force of personality and the contacts and rewards he can provide. He might even have a certain tragic, Romantic air to him that elicits sympathy despite one's better judgement (although I can imagine a rather different emotional context to him ruminating on how civilization is crumbling). This is the kind of thing that can make it hard not to be conflicted about following through even if he's still amassing an army trained to do things like use rape as a weapon (in a manner a bit more realistic than the Rutting Pens).

                        Likewise with Raksi, although I imagine her way of tying up and weighing down people who come to her with vengeance and justice in their hearts is a bit more intellectual or instructional. She's got a lot to teach, and might even be a bit forceful in providing it, at least at first so you can get enough of a taste to want to keep going with it. That can lead off in a few directions, including where she always tries to have a bit more to reveal so they never think they can afford to kill her yet, or after so long with her they're enamoured or impressed or even just familiar enough with her that killing her doesn't seem to fit anymore, or even just that in the course of things one has had to become so morally compromised that they no longer really think they're in a position to judge her.

                        Also with the idea that Raksi isn't stupid, and knows that her conduct will bring some people who'll want to strike at her head on, and has preparations to deal with such folks, maybe preferring to pin them in place while she works her literal and proverbial magic on them (which is probably less smart, or being too smart for one's own good).

                        Or it can just be that more righteous characters still have Investigation and Socialize as Abilities, and might analyze situations to try and discern the motivations and characteristics behind certain fearsome acts.

                        And really, none of this actually excludes taking Raksi down in a straightforward hero narrative, just so long as one is willing to acknowledge that such things don't actually require utterly unambiguous and irreddemable villains. I would personally run through such scenarios against people a lot less terrible than Raksi or Ma-Ha-Suchi without any sense of conflict. Somketimes it'll even be with characters who are just bestest buddies with those terrifying ancient Lunars.

                                        Playability doesn't in any sense require that killing Raksi will make the world a better place, or be without calamitous downsides. If those things aren't themselves interesting fodder for a game, then it might, but "everything goes to hell in the Far East" sounds like a pretty great follow-up to "your circle treks to Mahalanka and ganks Raksi".

                                        Is there actually a playability issue here, or is it just that the game isn't specifically patting you on the back for improving society by killing the baby-eating ancient monster? Because I can't see that impacting playability much. Meanwhile, the game does actually need an answer to "why hasn't anyone else done this thing yet, given that there are groups more powerful than you out there", and "because it's pragmatically unwise" seems like a pretty good one.

                                        One experience I keep coming back to in discussions like this, because it exemplifies the matter for me: at one point in a Solar game we had a huge pile of XP, a strong power base, and a lot of experience under our belt, and we had about a year to kill, so my guy was just like "hey, let's go find Bluehaven and kill all the Lintha," and the rest of the party was all "yeah sure". So we roused up an army and sailed it to Bluehaven and beat up Dukantha and had a pitched battle against the Lintha and their demons and then systematically put them all to the sword, except for a handful allowed to leave to spread our legend to their pawns and relatives scattered throughout the world. Was that the right thing to do? Would the destabilization it created cause more damage than the benefits? Was specifically going out of our way to incite the scattered remnants of the Lintha to wage war on us forever a prudent decision? The game ended soon after, so we'll never know, but seriously, who even cares. It made a good story, and the fallout would have also made a good story if it was going to make a story at all.

                                            I think that there's a difference between "interesting stuff happens, whatever you do" (which is how I generally read the way things are set up) and "everything you do results in the world becoming shit, because it's doomed to be shit, and because you, the person playing this game, are shit for thinking otherwise", which is I think what some people are afraid of.

                                            In general I think the game is not designed to have an easy obvious ending, so there's few characters that are just "killing this person is an obvious choice and will solve everything in their region." It's also somewhat designed with the premise that killing is (comparatively) easy but often has drawbacks. None of these things mean that killing Raksi is necessarily going to be clearly the wrong choice (it depends on how she's played in your game, and the situation you find yourself in), but it does mean that the game isn't designed to make it easy to say "all right, we've killed Raksi, now everything is fixed forever here and the story is over."

                                                        Originally posted by powerofvoid View Post
                                                        So, in other words, "you're probably going to want to gank Raksi, because it's probably a necessary but not sufficient condition to resolve the problems in her region"?
                                                        Not necessarily necessary, no. It might be necessary or it might not. Your Solar might wonder beforehand if there is another way. You might wonder afterwards if you made the right choice. The game probably isn't going to hand you an easy answer to that -- it makes for a better story to leave it uncertain.

                                                        Conversely, it's not going to say "killing Raksi is very definitely wrong and will inevitably screw everything up forever", for similar reasons. This also means that the problems with Raksi likely won't come down to one issue of wyld mutations or derangements -- putting aside the problem people mentioned above about how this is a game where playing the Ultimate Doctor or Ultimate Sorcerer who can cure even those things is an intended playstyle, I think that it is somewhat boring to make the problems caused by a problem come from their Evil Gene or Evil Sickness or whatever, which can be punched out of their body or surgically removed and then everything will be all right.

                                                        Raksi doesn't eat people and rule a city of ape-men cannibals because she is twisted by the Wyld or because she's an innocent who was corrupted by some obvious Evil Mutation. She does it because she believes that that is her natural right and because mortal humans are literally no more than animals to her. This is what she actually believes, something expressed in everything she has said and done for the past fifteen hundred years; it's entirely possible that she has never believed otherwise. "Redeeming" her is not a simple matter of giving one speech or hitting her with the magic Cure Evil charm. The cannibal-queen of Spermin is partially a guise she puts on, sure, but the things she does in that role are things she totally feels she's entitled to do as a Lunar Exalt.

                                                        The other alternative (and the much easier one) is to say "well, the number of people she eats is an acceptable cost for the number she saves by acting as one of Creation's defenders." But I don't think that that should be an easy call to make, either. There's a bigger cost to having her around than just the people she eats directly. She's a symbol of what Lunars (and Anathema) are, to most people. If you cut a deal with her, people will see you the same way. If she survives into whatever new world your characters are fighting for, she's going to be a part of shaping that world, which includes teaching a new generation of Exalted that, hey, it's all right to eat a few people now and then, you know?


                                                        Naturally, this is all stuff that will vary from game to game -- I suspect that Raksi's write-up will be designed so using her as a straightforward antagonist is one option but not the only option.
