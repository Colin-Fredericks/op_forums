                Originally posted by TSk View Post
                I've never thought that a surprise-negating charm or a perfect defense would have any effect against the Tell, because the Tell's magical effect isn't merely surprising; it's explicitly unnoticeable.
                That's...even worse in a way. Solars (and indeed, all Celestial splats) are supposed to have Charmsets that contain a Charm-based counter for every possible negative eventuality described by the rules (Crippling, Poison, Compulsion, social attacks, etc). If you can negate surprise, but not this quality of "unnoticeable," then why aren't there offensive one-shotting Charms that exploit this "unnoticeable" paradigm? (Personally I view that "unnoticeable" term as being written by someone who didn't understand Exalted's complex lexicon of terms of art).

                At the same time, if the Tell is so easily exploited by a social surprise negator + social perfect (which can be declared as soon as the social attack is), it really really sucks at its job.

                yeah this kind of meta-thinking is mostly inapplicable when we're talking about a Lunar PC sneaking through the Blessed Isle unless you have Peleps Deled walking around with huge passive Awareness and MDV boosting Charms on looking for hidden Anathema. The real issue it pops up is when you have a non-Lunar PC, and a Lunar PC or NPC who is trying to hide their Lunar status from them, and how trivially easy it should be to see through the Tell using the normal rules for social attacks, but also how bullshit it is for the Tell to circumvent those normal rules in a way that the system wasn't holistically designed to handle.

                Originally posted by TSk View Post
                If I may ask, how do you deal with this? As a player or storyteller, when you encounter a Lunar in-game? What process do you go through, step by step?
                Unfortunately, the only way I've ever been able to deal with it in a good way is with a degree of handwaving. Mortals can't see through the Tell even when the Lunar is in a true form, and it's come up so rarely otherwise that I've basically just had the Tell succeed or fail in a manner that would be most plot-relevant or create the most interesting scene (no, I didn't always fuck over the player, but I did intentionally cause tense situations because those should be fun - guaranteed success stops being fun after a few iterations of it and guaranteed failure is only cool if the consequences of failure are themselves fun, which is not usually the case).

                It's just that the mechanics as presented are such a clusterfuck that making the Tell interact as-intended within the scope of the existing rules is very difficult as it touches on the (broken) foundations of the system.

                As a person who enjoys simulationism (Exalted definition, as well as Forgite), and really enjoys seeing a good ruleset interact with itself (I like to play with moving parts), feeling like handwaving is necessary (versus doing it when I feel like it might add to the game - as a supplement to the RAW rather than as a replacement for them) is extremely unsatisfying to me.

It's an unnoticeable effect unless you have a high enough MDV or some other magic that lets you detect it.

                                        Originally posted by TSk View Post

                                        Yeah, that's been my reading of it. As just another little detail that makes your character feel more personal and unique. The main purpose being to give your Lunar some interesting personal flavor. Not really to fuck with your shapeshifting.
                                        Well... from what I gather, the idea in the original book's outline was that if a Lunar stayed in the same area for a long enough time, people would become familiar with their true forms, and it would inform suspicions about or observations for them in other forms.

                                        That has its place in preventing shapeshifting from being an overpowered disguise power (particularly considering that they can get a diverse range of shapes basically for free), but I think it's mostly just about resonating with shapeshifter and similar folklore and mythology. Like noticing that somebody has a sixth finger or a monobrow and realising that they're a werewolf. Probably some other stuff that I can't recall or was unfamiliar with.

                                        How people respond to that might depend on how the Lunar has been acting, or impressions cultivated by Charms; sometimes to see a beast bearing the sign of a Lunar is a good omen, and sometimes it's a mark of dark times coming. The Haslanti are supposed to be familiar with how to recognise Gerd, and to do so is a comforting thing. It's an additional distinct way for Lunars to relate to people and be perceived; there's something about trying to read a person through the body language of an animal. And I'd be inclined to think that many Lunars assume animal forms just for the sake of it, as an expression of a certain sense of alienation, while still sometimes being close to people.

                                        And sometimes it serves as a logical way to explain why Lunars who venture too far into Realm-controlled lands for too long still need to be wary of the Wyld Hunt, despite how every one of them has notable disguise powers. And even that is dependent on the idea of Lunars who did something that drew enough attention for people to note down your details that they could draw later patterns from.

                                        Which might explain why more Lunars don't rampage upon the Blessed Isle; a Lunar who operates in the heart of the Realm is one whose Tell is not known, and a Lunar whose Tell is not known is one who didn't take the opportunity while in the heart of the Realm to do something that large numbers of the enemy would notice. You can't charge straight into a manse like a bull in a china shop, but you can do more subtle, careful, long term things.

                                        Personally, I don't even really think of it as a balancing trade off for how easily they can shapeshift; it just shapes narratives and play experiences in a manner that fits inspiration sources.
