    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
    If there is ever a detailed explanation for the nature of the spirit shape (and I certainly hope there isn't, because closing down many routes of interpretation for an author to hard-code their own vision is just terrible when you're talking about a game of mythic heroes) I hope this is not it.
    Oh, we're in agreement that I'd rather things not be too detailed. I wouldn't personally mind the explanation, but I'd prefer things be left mostly unspecified. Were it to exist in canon at all, I wouldn't like it as more than just one in a series of possible speculations.

    Which is not to say that I think this is an invalid interpretation, or not worth exploring. It just adds to much of an external element for my liking.

    It's a cool idea, mind, and certainly a Lunar IC might wonder "why is my spirit shape a desert basilisk when I'm from Whitewall? WTF Luna?" but I think the more you start pushing for NPCs (controlled by the ST) to have some degree of narrative control over a player character's self-expression, I start to worry a little. Perhaps for nothing, admittedly.
    Yeah, with all respect, I would see that as worrying for nothing. I mean, preference for external over internal I understand completely, I just don't see it impacting how things would proceed at a table. The spirit shape is such a significant factor in a character that the idea of removing it from the players' hands never once occurred to me, and still doesn't seem plausible. It'd be like the ST deciding to set a character's Caste or Favored Abilities/Attributes or starting Charms when they Exalt. Or deciding, "Yeah, Luna/Sol/Whoever doesn't feel like Choosing you after all, despite that being kind of the premise." Technically possible, but not something I'd expect to be at all plausible. At least if it's not something the group had discussed beforehand, or if the game isn't going to break apart then and there.

    My biggest worry is not that Terezin's player chose this as like...kind of subverting a trope or playing with an idea of character growth, but that they played through an Exaltation scene and the ST was like "OK your Spirit Shape is a donkey lol." I mean no amount of rules will stop a terrible ST, granted.
    Yeah, again, it would be something completely IC. It's too important to put in the ST's hands, outside a game where the group had decided to put it there. Even then, that game that wouldn't last long if the ST had decided to be... well, an ass.

    But even on a narrative level the idea that the spirit shape is being set for you from without, rather than being an expression from within, is problematic to me.
    I can see that. I guess I don't see as much of a concern there, though. Individual Chosen of the Unconquered Sun have the Castes they do because the Sun selected them to be one of his priests, warriors, diplomats, shadowed hands, or intellectual luminaries. Panther, for instance, had life experiences that would have left him quite without the skills of an archetypal priest, but potential was seen there for him to, well, grow into it. I would, in hindsight, probably walk back the "pick something at random" notion a bit; in this scenario, I imagine Luna's very carefully considering the individual in question. She appears to all her Chosen in person, and that clearly suggests she cares a great deal for them. The spirit shape would be a direct a result of who the character is, or who the character could be, as much as another type of character's bestowed Caste.

    It's also an idea that I imagine is particularly likely to be unpalatable if one already has a sour taste in one's mouth from the idea that the gods are directly choosing their Exalted. I get that, too.

                        Hmm... This line of thought pointed me in a direction I hadn't considered before. Wild alternative interpretation/vague speculation, away!

                        With the gods going back to being more important to the choosing of their Chosen, I wonder if it's possible that Luna might have a hand in defining a spirit shape. Luna's rather perceptive, after all, and the gods have some strong understanding of the natures of their Chosen. She could reasonably pick one that strongly reflects the character as he is. She could also pick one that suits what she expects he will become after taking the Second Breath. Or even what he could potentially be and what she hopes to encourage -- said potential may be a big part of what stirred her heart and turned her head in the first place.* Hell, perhaps sometimes she picks something that seems right out of left field because she knows that, if something should be meaningful and feels meaningful to its recipient,** the recipient -- and others -- are going to try to come up with reasons why it is meaningful; whatever reason or reasons they reach will usually be persuasive, and she's very curious to see what they draw from it, how they see it defining themselves, and how they themselves define it. For similar reasons, she may also pick something deliberately against the grain of the sort of thing one would expect a character to receive, or one that otherwise carries with it some interesting lesson, to see how this person who has drawn her interest responds to the apparent incongruity.***

                        Eh, again, mostly musing, here. It wouldn't need to happen for every Lunar Exalt -- I like the idea of not even Luna necessarily knowing what spirit shape one of her Chosen may posses until, well, her Chosen possesses it. Even if entirely untrue, I definitely think it's something that some Lunars might believe or speculate about.

                        * For the purposes of this speculation, I'm not assuming the standard "Lunars Exalt for surviving" is as very definitely true as it was in 2e. Or even if it is, who Luna's likely to pick from among the survivors.

                        ** I get the impression Lunars are as comfortable in their human forms as they are in their spirit shapes, feeling a continuity and significance that they really don't have in other forms (outside DBT). It doesn't have a mechanical impact in their skill with any shape (oh, aside a mote discount for assuming a true form?), but I still like the notion.

                        *** And lo, did Terezin, blonde-maned king of Eresteba (better known as the Lion Province), widely spoken of for his courage and for his great leadership of all men and beasts, drew his Second Breath as a Chosen of Luna. With the spirit shape of a humble donkey. Perhaps he continues with it with as much courage and pride as before, such that even the beast of burden in time becomes symbolic of kingship and nobility. Perhaps it turns his thoughts and efforts more to the laborers and downtrodden of his lands, and becomes beloved by them for his compassion. Perhaps he feels humbled by this form, and becomes a less bold, but a considerably more thoughtful ruler. Perhaps Luna expected one of these, all of these, or none of these.
                        It opens some opportunities for roleplaying, and I can see an ST doing it this way for a player who's interested. In-setting, I think letting the Fickle Lady decide your spirit shape... or anything about you, really... is kind of a terrifying prospect. I mean, she is not exactly the most consistent god/dess.

                                            Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                                            Very few Lunars had established Exaltation scenes prior to second edition, and many of the later ones seemed very similar to Solar Exaltations.
                                            This was always something that annoyed me about Lunars (and unfortunately, I don't think it's fixable without invalidating existing Lunar concepts, so there's nothing to be done about it.) Every type of Exalted has their own unique circumstances and rules for their Exaltation... except Lunars, which just sort of reuse the Solar one with a vague note about 'survivors' tacked on at the end:

                                            DBs inherit their Exaltations.
                                            Alchemicals are built.
                                            Abyssals are chosen at the moment of death and must choose to accept.
                                            Infernals are chosen at their moment of greatest failure, must choose to accept, and spend five days in a cocoon before emerging as their new selves.
                                            Sidereals are fated from the moment of their birth.

                                            Solars are people of excellence uplifted without warning.
                                            Lunars are... people of a very very subtly different kind of excellence, and are uplifted without warning.

                                            Surviving is just a kind of Excellence, isn't really necessary for Lunars, and doesn't actually tie in to their Exaltation narrative anyway, so it comes off as more of a suggestion than something that shapes what they are. You can infer that Solars tend to have been highly skilled while Lunars tend to have been highly... attribute-ed... but that's just crudely trying to retroactively apply their mechanics to their background, and it isn't really reflected in their thematics (see eg. Solar Hercules.)

                                            It would be interesting to try and come up with ideas for a new mechanism or narrative for Lunar Exaltation, even though realistically I recognize that it can't be changed at this point (because there are too many existing Lunar concepts that would be invalidated by changing the narrative under them.)

                                                        Why do Lunars have Spirit Shapes?

                                                        At the advent of the ancient war, the Great Gods wove their weapons around a core of celestial fire, stolen from Heaven by Autochthon, whose body still bears the steel-scarring wounds of its capture. The Dragon was happy to devour its portion of the champion-soul, in the manner of a snake, and so seeded the race of humans with children whose blood ran thick with its power. Alas, its eagerness and greed and lust worked against it, and as the heavenly power rushed out it tore into five lethargic drakes, who fled to shed their skins in the corners of the world forevermore. The other Incarna saw this, and were more cautious - instead of taking the champion-soul onto themselves, they danced with it, working it with their light (this is the nature of souls).

                                                        The Sun's light defines borders, searing night from day and real from unreal and virtue from injustice. He armoured the champion-soul in sunlight, and it shined apart and above from the world. Each was a halo that crowned itself, a token of world-enriching arrogance, all-conquering and too admirable to be tolerated. Soon even Sol was forced to uncup his hands and turn his eyes aside, the light of his children promoting itself to ever-greater brilliance, and the Solars were released into the world.

                                                        The Star's light defines passage, linking moments in space and places in time. She-they strung the champion-soul across countless points of starlight, and it became a thread of as many strands. Each wove itself into the world, seeking to be sought, knowing all by inference and guessing at nothing with uncanny accuracy. In time even the Maidens lost track of their work, the light of their-her children concealing itself behind the shadows it cast, and the Sidereals made their way to the world.

                                                        But the Moon's light defines nothing, dancing happily with impossibilities and nightmares. The champion-soul ignored it, as one brushes off a dream after waking, and there was nothing to be done. Instead, she turned to her lover, Gaia, whose light she shared (this is the nature of love). Understanding her distress, Gaia called on the first of each and every animal she had created, all the great ancestor-spirits of those beasts she bore, and gave them a task - to carry the Moon's light. The stag hung the Moon's light about its antlers like dripping jewelry, and the fish pooled it in its scales, and the tiger bore it in its terrible gaze, and on and on. Together they bore the Moon's light to the champion-soul, which could not ignore them, for they were real. It accepted their gifts, and itself became a thing that was only mostly real - but it remembered the great First Beasts, and ran free and strange as they did, and the Lunars made their way into the world.

                                                        That's why Lunars have Spirit Shapes.

                                                        ---

                                                        Now, see, that took me ~10-15 minutes to type up, most of which was phrasing. It gives people a concrete reason there's spirit shapes, other than "muh apocalypse", which can still be safely ignored as an old myth. It makes the spirit shape more than just the thing you get mutations for in your warform, and excises the "I have earned the gaze of the gods, and so wield the power of... a golden retriever!" aspect - now it's the legacy of an ancient, terrible and beautiful animal-spirit, with all the hooks and potential Charmtech that implies. It even gives Lunars a clear connection to the animal spirits they always seem to be associated with for no apparent reason beyond "hey, I ate one of your dudes and can now wear his skin - we're automatically bros". Hell, it even opens up hooks for surviving Animal Avatars to have links to specific Lunars (i.e. PCs). And if you want, it can even become their unique method of Exaltation - an animal of the appropriate type carries their Exaltation to them, dissolving into moonlight as it does so, taking impossible paths to reach the Chosen. It can even be their spirit adviser.

                                                        That took a quarter of an hour, it does all that, and it's not even particularly good. I don't even like the spirit shape as an idea. Fuck the last two editions for not even managing that.

                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            So you're saying it's possible for characters with no dots in Lore, Resistance and Martial Arts/Melee to have a hidden potential to learn those Abilities?
                                                            Well that wasn't exactly my point. I'm saying they have innate potential to fight, to withstand injury and to master intellectual thought. And that potential is represented as their Attributes. From 2E core:
                                                            STEP TWO: ATTRIBUTES
                                                            The Attributes define your character’s innate capabilities and aptitudes.

                                                            STEP THREE: ABILITIES
                                                            Abilities describe what your character knows and can do, what he’s learned rather than his natural potential.
                                                            See also:
                                                            Physical Attributes define your character’s physical potential—how fast she runs, how much she can lift and how healthy she is.

                                                            Social Attributes define your character’s ability to interact with, relate to and manipulate others. Social Attributes affect first impressions, your character’s ability to inspire or lead people and how attractive she is.

                                                            Mental Attributes define your character’s intellectual capacity—how quickly she thinks, how keen her senses are and how smart she is.

                                                            A character with no dots in an Ability is unskilled, and a character with no dots in an Attribute is crippled and incapable of actions requiring that Attribute.
                                                            Attributes are a character's innate talents, whether known or unknown. Abilities are his learned skills. So if a character has hidden, unknown potential for something, you should see it represented in the relevant Attribute.

                                                            I've always thought that Lunars are probably the best type of Exalted for exploring a character's hidden potential, because other types' abilities are less about potential and more about skill. No one is ever born with Archery 5; it's something you have to train long and hard to achieve. But you might have been born with Dexterity 5 and Perception 5 still have no idea of your potential to be one of the finest duelists in Creation.
