        All good comments in response. This is a very subjective and qualitative thing that probably benefits from a deep reading of all the sources and some creativity in how they're used in the setting as realised in people's games rather than a matter of absolutes. So I'm more just raising this question than looking to answer it.

        I guess I'm saying here that, if it looks like the Lunars are going to win in a relatively short space of time (relative to their Exalted lifespans and the length of their concerns!), then if the Lunars feel certain of it... The Sidereals are gonna be predicting it with high certainty, and they're gonna be orienting and responding and changing their behaviour, and they're gonna make sure this filters to the Dragonblooded leadership and especially the Empress. Or at least they will be trying to. If civilization is losing, all these parties are going to be very conscious and aware of the recession, and they'd be planning in this knowledge. We'd see signs of this happening in the setting.

        The Sidereals defining power, their defining thing, is being able to look at the long term and have a good idea of where things are going, beyond other Exalted, so your strategy against them will always have to be more "You can probably see this, but you can't do anything about it because you're too restricted to move and you've got too much inertia" and not "I can see this, but you can't". Like Wizard says about a system set up by the Empress that is hard to now change. You would still have the tragedy of "people pulling apart when they ought to be drawing together", which is a common thing in Exalted like Isator says, but they should certainly be very aware of it. (The Sidereals can be of course be totally blindsided by some things, but these are definitely things that cut to the heart of their divisions (the Usurpation) and by elements that are vastly powerful and outside of Fate (the Unconquered Sun returns his favour to the Solars, etc.)).

        It's all good if we have confidence that the setting as described in 3e does fulfil all this. I've def. not read as deeply the 3e sourcebooks as most in this thread, so I'm actually relatively ill positioned to say whether that's the case, and I'll take the judgement of the more expert posters.

            Something else to consider is that the Empress was born into the Shogunate, where “legitimacy” in a ruler was a novel concept, and she came to power via the authority inherent in “I have the biggest beatstick in the box,” and a whole bunch of people tried to take it away from her. Later on in her life, her first son decided HE was going to be the heir and had all her other children murdered until she made him stop.

            The Empress is, pretty clearly, a woman who knows how fragile a throne is and has formed her government in response to that. She intentionally set it up with infighting built in and with her as the only referee, so she can absolutely make sure that no-one else can ever be strong enough to take power from her (and, if they ever thought they had a shot, Chejop would warn her about the coup being in the works years earlier).

            Would another governmental model work better? Maybe, but, as far as the Scarlet Empress was concerned, any other model of government would have seen her either dethroned and executed or assassinated long ago, and that’s pretty clearly the main thing she wanted to keep from happening this entire time.

                                                        Someone made a valid point upthread that it's hard to strike a balance between Lunars seeming awesome and scary and effectual, and Dynasts seeming awesome and scary and effectual. This is very true. But for whatever reasons, people seem to be ignoring or sidelining the third party to this conflict -- Sidereals. Who also should seem awesome and scary and effectual, and who thus far have...well, not, really.

                                                        The discussion so far has also focused heavily on Lunars doing damage to the Realm, and ignoring or dismissing the idea of the converse, which doesn't help. And it seems to me like these problems can be synthesized to help solve each other. So here's my vision of the Shogunate, the Contagion and the present day, in an attempt to make everyone look as cool as they should.

                                                        Eric Minton said upthread that the Lunars were effectively losing until the Contagion. The Shogunate was full of warring daimyos and infighting, but by and large, it ran on its own; this means that the Sidereals allied with it had a freer hand to go on the offensive. Meanwhile, angry Lunar Exalted were building up armies at the edge of the world -- but the larger their armies got, the more of their time they had to dedicate to vigilance and paranoia, just as the Realm had to do the same against Lunar infiltrators. Young, ambitious Lunar warlords might come back from a recruiting drive to find their hunters caught in their own snares, their crop yields through the floor, their armies fragmented by a half-dozen new cults who hate each other, half their chain of command killing the other half over grievances that only sound ludicrous after the bodies have cooled, and then when they're distracted by trying to fix all this mess, some innocuous traveler looks up at them with stars in their eyes and pokes a pressure point that makes their soul fall off. Sure, it's not going to work as well on Raksi, but most Lunars aren't Raksi, and even Raksi might not have had the same secure powerbase then as she does now.

                                                        (As a side note, this is the kind of stuff I would love to see Sidereals doing to their enemies. I don't know why it never seems to happen in canon materials.)

                                                        So Lunars learned the hard way not to gather huge masses of troops, to fragment their operations, to always be ready to run, and to bleed their enemies rather than going for knockout blows. The trouble is, the Shogunate legions are well-trained, well-disciplined, well-armed and culturally militant, so bleeding them tends to hurt the Lunars more than it does their enemies. And maybe Sidereals can't disrupt every bigger operation, but they can hit the most likely to succeed (and, just as importantly, know which ones those are) until the threat of their involvement becomes something that helps shape Lunar strategies just as much as Legion doctrine does.

                                                        Then, of course, comes the Contagion, and everything goes to hell.

                                                        Suddenly, the Realm has to rebuild itself from the ashes of the Shogunate, and its Sidereal allies have to develop and spread a new religion to support it, and help guide and advise the Empress so that the new empire takes an agreeable shape. Suddenly the world is full of Wyld zones, remnant raksha, shadowlands, and other threats to Fate, which means that Sidereals have a much harder time finding the space to do, well, anything else. Suddenly, the Dragon-blooded are decimated and have to rebuild their numbers over generations. And meanwhile, the Lunars at the edge of the world are finding that the same tactics they used against the Realm and the Fivescore Fellowship are working even better than before. Moving on to the next after a society they built destroyed itself is what they've always been doing, after all, and while the Contagion is more thorough it's also far more even-handed.

                                                        So now we reach Creation as it stands. Sidereals allied with the Realm now find themselves in the position of having to pick their battles very carefully; they could go mess up that barbarian horde, or they could defuse the tension between Great Houses that would lead to civil war in a decade or so. At least with barbarian hordes, the Legions can deal with them; civil war doesn't have that stopgap. So Lunars now have a much freer hand to build their power bases and bleed the Realm -- a Realm which never recovered to Shogunate levels -- without interference, and are themselves freer to put their enemies on the defensive. The momentum that was against them before the Contagion is now theirs to ride to eventual victory...

                                                        ...and then the Solars appeared, and everything changed again.
