                        I can see Lunars as carrying the uncanny image of the bestial monster that can apply problem solving skills, but that would appear to be emergent from the fact that they're shapeshifters more than Charms.

                        I mean, I suppose one could make shapeshifting something that inhibits a Lunar's rationality for the sake of then having Intelligence Charms to let them use their full mind in other forms, but that strikes me as a bit of an air-breathing mermaid thing.

                        I do have this image now in which a Lunar is incongruously standing as the sole human figure amidst a bunch of beasts, and the presence and direction of the Lunar gives them an out-of-sorts level of coordination and deliberation; it's an image distinct from being a trainer, more like... being Nymeria the dire wolf in terms of her effect on her terrifyingly massive pack. Even some portrayals of Tarzan.

                        I could see Intelligence Charms following the idea that the Lunar is able to warp their own perspective and mental processes so that they can fit their planning and directing activity into a pattern that is significantly different from their usual default; it's like, the Lunar didn't elevate the animals (or people of a certain particularly idiosyncratic culture), they just distilled themselves into a form that could communicate ideas that would prompt responses from which the desired result is an emergent property.

                                        Originally posted by Exthalion View Post

Given that a Solar will always be better at X where X is some task defined by an ability and that all such tasks have an attribute attached to them, what does it mean that Lunars brilliant than Solars (or whatever else) when the Solar will still have the better thought out arguments (Int+Lore) the more acute observations (Per+Invest), etc.?
                                        I think the point is supposed to be that the smart (high-Int) Lunar is going to be able to do things just with Int Charms that the Solar would need Lore, Occult, Medicine, etc. to do. Less need to spread around, because Attributes are so broad already.

                                        In theory this works; in practice, this point is frequently moot. Unless Int Charms are written more broadly such that one Charm can be applicable to thinking about all of Lore, Occult, and Medicine, versus having an Int Charm focused on Lore stuff, another focused on Occult stuff, etc. The latter is more of what previous editions gave us: Ability Charm specificity but just using a different trait as min.

                                        What I'm hoping is that since Attributes are broader than Abilities, Attribute Charms will be more broadly applicable, i.e. a Charisma Charm will be useful with Presence, Performance, Socialize, Investigation, etc. Some Lunar stuff did this (not much) but IIRC Alchemical stuff was on point for broadness.

                                            Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                            I have a real issue with the notion that of all the Exalted, only Lunars might risk losing themselves and basically turning into the Relic as a side-cost of actually using their native powers. This should be a thing that happens to all of them or to none. Losing control of your body and mind for constantly being something you are not (which is one of your bred-and-butter powers on top of it) is just punitive when layered on top of the Great Curse.
                                            Hey, who said only Lunars lose themselves?

                                            Solars lose themselves in their power and become mad god-tyrants, forgetting over time what it's like to be human and justifying ever-grander atrocities and extremes of harmful virtue.
                                            Sidereals lose themselves in "the greater good" and slowly sacrifice the things that they use to define themselves to Fate, and hope they can pull enough strings to make the cost worth it.
                                            Infernals lose themselves in transcendent alien power, becoming less and less human and more and more a cosmic monster until their alien principles matter more than their former lives.
                                            Dragonbloods lose themselves in politics and power, giving up who they are for their families in the hope that someday they'll be the powerful Elder and retain enough of who they are to fix things.
                                            Abyssal loyalists lose themselves in hateful nihilism, and rebellious ones know they've already lost what matters and are trying to regain it.
                                            Alchemicals lose themselves in service to the state and the Maker, at constant risk of letting Clarity and logic override their hopes and dreams.

                                            Lunars losing themselves in foreign shapes, forgetting who they really are underneath all the masks, is just their particular way of doing it.

                                            One of the central themes of Exalted is having tremendous world-shaping power and trying to remain true to who you are and do something that matters to you, hopefully something good, with all that power. Losing yourself amidst all that power is a core Exalted story, like an Aberrant losing themselves amidst celebrity or a Vampire losing herself to the Beast or a Noble losing himself as he merged with his Estate. The Exalted are virtuous humans struggling to remain virtuous given the powers of the gods themselves, and many of them fail and become divine horrors. That's a central theme of the game, and I'd hate to lose it.

                                            Now, I certainly don't want it to be something that's rubbed in your face constantly? Like, your PCs shouldn't worry about chimerism constantly unless that's the sort of game you're all up for playing, it should be something that happens in extreme cases? But I always felt that the Green Lady has always been steppin' on some Lunar themes about identity.

                                            I'm thinking about the Earthsea books, where Ged turns into a hawk and stays in the shape too long and almost forgets he was ever a man. Those are some potent themes! We should be careful with their use, but I'd hate to lose them.

                                            So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                            The absence of a monument can, in its own way, be something of a monument also.                                             -Roger Zelazny
