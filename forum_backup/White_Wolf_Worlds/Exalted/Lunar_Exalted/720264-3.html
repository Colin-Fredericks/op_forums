                                                Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                                Couple things.

                                                1. I don't think that's described as something ALL Lunars do, or that all Lunars go about it in the extreme realpolitik way I described. Certainly some do, but other Lunars probably go about their goals in other ways. The Silver Pact is not the monolith of the Bureau of Destiny, and making it less like the Garou Nation is great for all the reasons that making it the Garou Nation to begin with didn't make sense.
                                                I think you've missed my point in the Sid comparison. I don't give a good goddamn how organized the Lunars are. I like organization, personally, but I don't think it's critical to a play experience.

                                                What is important is accomplishing something. We've had it mentioned that Sidereals will have Things To Do - holes in reality to patch, threats to avert, all manner of work that isn't connected to the Bronze-Gold Divide or other splats in general. We might not have any details, but even that much has me stoked - interesting hooks to take part in as a Sid, yay!

                                                Meanwhile, Lunars at this point have a lot of RIP AND TEAR HUGE GUTS and one paragraph of lip service to 'Oh, yeah, you can totally do your own thing, too.' That... does not stoke me. That the exact opposite of stokes me.

                                                Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                                4. In a way I like the solid greying of morality in the Realm vs Lunar war thing. Lunars are not just some ancient good guys who the current government won't leave alone; they're doing terrible things to support their goals, which is a strategy that human beings employ and always have employed.

                                                Why was their 2e "morality," which to me seemed to be "farcically ignore the actual state of Creation to stick our heads in the sand re mortal societies that will not be able to last without us," a better state?
                                                OK, so, this I can explain.

                                                Step one - forget the actual details of the River. Which societies, the desired conditions, all of that. It wasn't well done, it's not the point.

                                                The point is why the River existed. It was the Lunars and their Elders looking at what had happened in the world, looking at the First Age and the current one and going, "Y'know, none of this has worked properly. We need to find something better."

                                                Nobody else in the setting did that.

                                                It was always, 'Put Myself/Someone In Absolute Charge By Supreme Mandate And Don't Mess It Up This Time'. The New Realm and the Old were organized on the same fundamental principle of autocracy and top-down granting of boons. It's doing the same thing over and over and trying to get it right by sheer repetition without considering that maybe that thing was the problem.

                                                The River was asking, "OK, what can work instead?" and trying it. It's acknowledging that actually fixing the causes of 'the actual state of Creation' is an unavoidable necessity if you want to do more than just treat symptoms.

                                                And that is a hell of a play hook.

                                                Which is gone. And replaced by cynical terrorism, murder, and bloodlust.

                                                Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                                I mean, is this the true ideal version of the Lunar narrative. I dunno. Probably not? I'm really not sure, because we never have had a good alternative to compare it to. We can't look at it like Yozis and say "I prefer the GoD portrayal to the MOEP Infernals and CoCD Malfeas one," because 1e Lunars was an example of total bedshitting and 2e Lunars was narratively incoherent. I'm not sure TAW even answered the narrative question, so much as its goal was to make the mechanics not "Solar Charms, but overcosted and silver-colored."

                                                Are they now filling a niche that was unfilled, and better than the sloppy lack of direction, set on the boring edges of the world, that they had as a splat in the past? I say absolutely.
                                                It's a niche I want no part of, and I'm deeply dismayed to see it happening to a splat I had previously quite liked.

                                                        To build on Valles's point of "It's not encouraging if that's ALL we see of them"... Well, I imagine it's a bit like Archery charms.

                                                        I think we can all agree that a lot of the Archery charms seem to be assuming you'll be using a bow. Like, a lot of them can apply to a crossbow, and many of them to a flame weapon, but it's pretty obvious that they're made with the mind of someone sniping with arrows shot from a bow. Like, how does the "Set your arrow on FIRE!" charm, the "Make an arrow out of Essence" charm, or the "Shoot an arrow that sticks to something and only the Solar can remove it" charm apply to a flame weapon?

                                                        It can be tough to look at other interpretations when they're not presenting options FOR you. The Lunar writeup is presenting them in one major way, and it's not even giving a parallel to Righteous Devil Style, where we can go "But if you want to use this non-standard Archery weapon, you can use this!"

                                                        Basically, it's like if there WERE no Righteous Devil Style, and all we got was a box off to the side saying "Some of these charms don't really mix well with using a Firewand. Well you totally can, just be creative!"

                                                        How something is presented can form a huge impact on how they're perceived, and most of the Lunar writeup paints them as falling into a major group of "Consumed by rage". I fully believe that we'll get a much better writeup through the eyes of the Lunars in their book, but we don't have that book now. And for people unfamiliar with Lunars, what we have seems to put them into one camp.
