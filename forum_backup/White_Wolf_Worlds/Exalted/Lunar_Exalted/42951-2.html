        Originally posted by Arcanus Dicere View Post
        If you wanted to play a warrior, it was sub-optimal to not be a Full Moon.
        Unless you wanted to coordinate attacks or run a military apparatus, in which case Changing Moons and No Moons would be a better choice. Yes, you could conceivably pick another Attribute that could help, but that would only give you a limited in on that arena. Ask yourself, "How much does it help being in charge of a military organization at some level of hierarchy to be able to vastly outpace, overpower and outlast the troops under your command?"

        Full Moons would be dynamite special forces types or spies trusted with autonomy out in the field, but being in any position of authority which relies on the strength of others isn't playing to the Full Moon's being, whereas a Dawn could just learn War and be fine with all of the tasks that involve military organizations. This is why it's a mistake to think of Full Moons as being exactly like Dawns or filling even remotely similar roles. Additionally, those physical properties can have a lot of odd side benefits, such as using claws to climb or carve sculptures (that this option was not given is an issue of EX2's Silver Solar issue; even the poor execution of Lunars in EX1 offered a wider array of possibilities for dangerous body parts in its own fashion).

        If you wanted to player a diplomat or trickster, it was sub-optimal not to play a Changing Moon. If you wanted to play a sorcerer or scholar, it was sub-optimal to not play a No Moon. Sure, the Solars & DB's & Sidereals have their warrior and diplomat and scholar classes delineated, you could still be a diplomatic Dawn or a scholarly Fire or a warrior Serenity. You were never told "this is what you are" because, despite a Caste/Aspect's leanings, you had enough wiggle room to play against type if you wanted to.
        You've full-on fallen into the same trap as the writer of the mortal splat chapter of Scroll of Heroes. If a Lunar Caste has an Attribute that gives you an advantage in an arena, it's a lucky accident. Their strengths aren't really meant to play to clearly defined roles, because while it'd be cool to be able to form plates of armor over your skin, bend light around you like the Predator or see fear, these aren't necessarily things that help all of the time if you're engaged in some form of employment, and relying on them isn't going to necessarily make you good at your job, because, again, with the military membership example, the armed forces don't want someone who can outperform all the other soldiers physically and make ammunition from shed skin. They value someone who can take orders and who won't go berserk if someone they care about more than the organization itself is injured or killed. By amassing many such people, and playing to their strengths, they win battles. If they invite on a dualistic shapeshifter that switches between virtuous and psycho at the drop of a hat... well, you probably get the idea.

        The only redeeming factor with the Lunars in this regard is their shapeshifting. A full-on warrior could still have a mouse in his repertoire, allowing for stealth when needed. A sorcerous No Moon could still turn into a raging bull if it was time to smash or stampede. That's the only area of their capabilities that allowed them to stretch their legs a bit without being told "that's not what you do."

        Whether Lunars get Aspects or Castes, they need better definition and less limitation. Give them five, open the Physical/Social/Mental Castes up a bit to concepts beyond Warrior/Socialite/Sorceror, and then you'll see a lot more characters who are a blend of ideas instead of a singular one pronounced in a different way.
        Don't even consider what you do. With Lunars, concentrate on what a member of each Caste is. The process will be profoundly different from Ability-based Exalted, but that's the point of the exercise. If it's that off-putting, you might not actually like Lunars. There's nothing wrong with that, but the Lunars shouldn't be bent around to be similar to other Exalted for any reason. That was done in EX2, and it didn't work out too well.

                    Okay, I quite like this.

                    So... first of all, I agree that really, Dragonblooded have Castes. If anything, they have Castes more than Solars do - you are born and bred into a specific role

                    .
                    Lunars AREN'T and DON'T. They don't have powers based on "job", they have powers based on "aptitudes". You don't learn to be smarter, or stronger, or even more agile - you *train*, yes, but that's not something you *learn*, it's something you *become*.


                    Arguably Aspects fits even better with the changing Casteless; they shift from having the Aspect of a Full Moon to to having the Aspect of a No Moon, etc?


                    So one idea I like would be making Casteless (needs a new name) more than just a gimped add-on. Give them the same number of Favoured Attributes, and allow them to pick them... but if they ever pick a single Aspect, their Favoured Attributes change permanently (possibly putting them into XP debt?). That, and make the Anima Powers less specialised so a character might want to cycle through them (but otoh, might still well be annoyed that their options are changing by the moon), or alternatively forget "change with the phases of the moon" - have the Casteless be able to choose at will from between the three Aspects of Luna, but only get a lesser version (making them jack-of-all-trades types).

                    Heck, while we're here, make the fixed castes something the Lunars did because they wanted the power and focus they bring. So while in the First Age a Lunar was simply defined by what they chose to work at (a Lunar was simply a Lunar, as changeable as their patron), in the time since they have reforged themselves into human weapons. Whether or not this is a homage, unconscious or not, to the lost Solar's Castes - or even to the Castes of their Dragonblooded enemies - is debatable, though Lunar Elders may well take exception to anyone suggesting this!

As 1E and 2E stand, they're written up - look not just at the Charms, but how they're described in the Traits chapter - as being warriors first, nothing else second.                                             Lunars should definitely have Castes over Aspects. I'm not even sure how this is a debate. The accumulated detritus that comes along with Aspects is certainly not going to work well for Lunars; they're iconoclasts and loners. Trying to stick them in pretty little boxes and tie them up with bows isn't going to go over well.

                                            I doubt Luna's interested in that, either. I think we just need to allow that Lunar chargen really ought to be 2 Attributes from their "go to" problem-solving tools, and 2 more that can go wherever it makes sense for the Lunar. If a Lunar happens to physically subdue or surmount their opponents/obstacles in the trials because it was about to attack something else/someone was going die/etc, that doesn't necessarily mean physicality is their only method. Just one that worked well in that specific instance.

                                            I don't think Luna would look for heroes that one-dimensional. I don't think you'll find many Lunars who, at 200+xp, could be considered that one-dimensional. Why should we start them out trying to fit them into orderly boxes when we know they're going to rip up the gift wrap later?

                                            If I were approaching this particular design conundrum, I'd try to design five interesting anima abilities for Lunars. I'd allow that they can "align" themselves to whichever one makes the most sense for them at chargen. I'd note they don't even necessarily have to keep the same one forever. The only thing they can't do is switch their favored attributes around. Totem animals can be changed when they choose a new anima (however that is accomplished). I'd probably disregard most of the remainder of their baggage; don't bring it up and let Storytellers that want those old mechanics to come back figure out their own implementations.
