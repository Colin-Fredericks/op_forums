            Next up with Endurance Charms, which I warrant will cover a somewhat wider range than dealing with fatigue and injury and the like.



            Of course it starts out with Ox-Body Technique, which I’m going to go ahead and compare directly to the Solar version. The basic principle is the same, a Charm that can be purchased repeatedly to grant extra health levels, with the exact quantity per purchase based on Stamina. The difference is that the levels the Lunar acquires all have deeper wound penalties; for instance, two -2 levels for Lunars compared to a -1/-2 for Solars. The only part where the straight numbers are a bit preferable are where the Lunar is at Stamina 5, getting four health levels to a Solar’s three (although it still has no 0 health levels and includes a -4). Hhhhowever, I think this is offset a bit by the surroundings; a Lunar has more incentive to and benefit from buying up their Stamina in the first place, so it’s a matter of them probably getting the best version in more cases, which will do double duty of allowing them to maximize the purchases (compared to the Solar version, which still has the cap on how many you can get based on Resistance). Furthermore, Lunars are dotted with powers based on turning wound penalties into strengths, so in some respects getting a few more -4 levels is actually a boon, as you’ll get to maximize those benefits without dancing too close to incapacitation. So you’ve got the mechanical underpinnings, from which one can build a certain notion that Lunar Stamina is focused on leaning into the pain.

            Ah good, there’s one of those hibernation Charms, Bear Sleep Technique; put oneself into a mode of stasis, in which rate of healing is increased by Essence + Stamina (so tripled at the very lowest), as well as needing no food and water. It’s an Indefinite Charm, but apparently can’t be ended voluntarily, requiring the Lunar to stick it out either until fully healed or after a pre-determined length of time has passed. Being aware of surrounding phenomena (which can allow one to wake early) requires a roll made at a steep penalty which is said to only be capable of being enhanced by Excellencies, although I can recall that somewhere back up there was at least one Charm that wasn’t restricted in that manner, and it’s not necessary to roll if it’s somebody messing around with the Lunar physically, so it’s always possible to rouse a Lunar from that state with a hard smack across the face. Last of all, it only takes being at Stamina 5, while Essence remains at 1, for that hibernating state to not qualify towards one’s aging. Considering the conditions they operate in, this seems like a good solid Charm for a lot of Lunars to go to ground for a while and come back out, fresh as a daisy.

            Ah, almost forgotten about self-healing with Halting the Scarlet Flow. It’s more of a dedicated combat regeneration now, and is prevented from rendering hibernation redundant, by not being possible to use outside of combat. Still, it might be a bit less finickity than it used to be, with an invocation and flat payment of six motes keeping it going for turns equal to Stamina (so long as they’re not crashed early), during each of which a non-aggravated wound will heal so long. Use has to be chosen carefully though, since it can only be used once per day, and even then won’t reset if the Lunar is still injured at the start of the next day, or didn’t get proper sleep (so that’s me right out). I find that the regeneration might have originally been there primarily as another thing to reference the Gauru, and the combat dynamics of Exalted have always complicated it, so this level of specificity seems fitting to me. On the other hand, it’s still only Essence 1, so I wonder if later Charms might end up expanding upon it.

            On the other hand, they were always supposed to have a special facility for recovering from crippling injuries, and that gets a look with Maimed But Unbroken. The Lunar gets to alleviate the penalties imposed by such injuries, or other crippling effects like the Charm Joint-Wounding Attack, and as time passes (four to two rounds in combat, or per scene out of it) the penalty will be reduced by another point as the injury rapidly heals, being dispelled completely once the penalty is at zero. It is, however, still too early to deal with crippling in the form of limbs or organs being removed or destroyed completely, and is also ineffectual against any crippling effects that don’t entail penalties.

            Oh, the regeneration already gets a boost just for getting to Stamina 4 with Undying Battle Vigor, as they get the Permanent benefit of extending the Charm’s length by one turn each for either dealing at least three points of damage higher than what would be necessary to incapacitate, or by waving the benefit of Initiative Break. It doesn’t strictly say that those conditions need to be achieved while Halting the Scarlet Flow is already been in effect, but I feel as though that’s the spirit of the thing.

            It rolls back to the bottom again with Rugged Beast Endurance, a day-long Charm by which a Lunar is able to adapt to their current environment; while under its effect, they get a temporary specialty in each Survival and Resistance to cope with their current climate, as well as getting to ignore penalties to rolls that the specialties would benefit. It needs to be invoked again if there’s ever a reason to want to change one’s adaptation, but doing so while the first use is active means that it gets to forego its Willpower cost. A repurchase at Essence 3 and Stamina 4 (quite a step up for one initially with 1s in both) allows its protection to also half any damage from an adapted climate’s environmental hazards, which extends to similar effects produced by things like magic. And when in an animal shape native to the environment, it also provides a mote discount to any Charms enhancing the benefited Survival or Resistance rolls. I could see this evolving into the old Directional Mastery Charm, but for the moment I have a preference for this Charm as it is with the more versatile application and less absolute process.

            Hehehe, I was wondering if we’d actually be getting a Charm for effectively disposing of forty cakes, and here it is with All-Consuming Crucible, an all around power for enhancing the act of eating; in addition to having no limit to the amount of food one can put down (as well as being able to do so with unusual rapidity when under duress, using a Stamina + Athletics or Survival roll), it becomes possible to safely live off of any source of mundane organic material, and even adds non-Charm dice to rolls to resist a consumed poison. Once they get to Stamina 5, the Lunar can eat basically anything (even if they sometimes need other means to break it down into bite-sized pieces), and mundane eaten or drank poison becomes worthless. It’s a small thing, but I do love something in the idea of a character who can eat what they please in whatever quantity, more than usual with some of my recent media consumption, and the idea of getting to make a roll to very rapidly dispose of a large mass is just the cherry on top. I’ll tell you, I’ve got a wolverine-totem Lunar with this Charm’s name on her.

            Starts at the bottom again with Disease-Purging Essence, a very simple Charm to allow some failed dice on rolls against virulence or morbidity to be rerolled. Having secured their own disease resistance, the Lunar can then mitigate that of others with Rabid Beast Strike, through which a decisive attack with hand, foot, claw or horn hits an enemy with an enhanced form of wound infection, in which the Lunar’s Stamina gets to serve as its morbidity and virulence values if higher than the default, and it’s also effective against the Exalted, supernatural beings or anybody else who heals in that manner. The resulting disease even gets a chance to start right at Major intensity if the attack is made on an enemy that was crashed, and the Charm affords a means of transmitting any other disease the Lunar might be a carrier of, albeit without the enhanced numbers. I’m wondering if there’s too much chicanery to the idea of turning into some well-known animal reservoir like a reptile, assuming that its carrying salmonella, and using that as a basis to infect people with typhoid…

            False Death Technique is… basically what it sounds like. You get to make a roll using Stamina or Appearance, with double 9s and non-Charm dice equal to wound penalties, to pass off as dead. Magical observation might be capable of seeing through it, but would need to beat the Lunar’s successes on the original roll, and while actually doing anything not dead will typically give the game away, it’s suggested that there are occasions where the Lunar can bluff people into thinking them undead. It even gets to reflexively and mutely invoke Bear Sleep Technique for the Lunar who wants to make use of the immobility, and a spirit shape of an animal to plays possum provides the option of taking it under Awareness.

            Scorpion-and-Toad Absolution is the foundation of one of the old signature tricks, this time rendered as allowing persistent rerolling of 6s on rolls to resist poisons, being able to get the 5s as well if it’s something they’ve survived before, or something derived from an animal that the Lunar can shift into. It’s a Totemic Charm for Wits, which anybody should be able to guess the animal type to allow.

            After imagining the range of Lunars who might congregate in Luthe, I was wondering about the assets that might be afforded to allowing them down there, and it comes in Shifting Breath Inversion, an Indefinite Charm with no cost whatsoever, to allow a Lunar to adapt to be capable of breathing in any environment and be immune to any complications from environmental pressure (or lack thereof). I’d note that, while the focus is on aquatic environments, this would also be really helpful for survival at extremely high elevations. Heh, it took me a moment to understand what the Charm was getting at with all of the talk of aquatic animals needing extra help to function out of water, before I realised that the point was to allow somebody to do something like being a shark that could function on land, which… has implications… a Dynast who things they’re safe from an Armoured Terror off the coast, before it comes crawling up onto shore on creepy mutant boar god limbs from Princess Mononoke. That’s niiiice. To top it off, they can get extra successes to rolls against poisons that could only be inhaled, or against any inhaled poison while in an amphibious shape. Lunars with water breathing spirit shapes can learn it with Wits. Normally I might be a bit against more absolute effects, but when it’s something for the sake of expanding the range of environments they can function in (and bring their animal shapes to), and considering that they’re Lunars… I kind of feel exactly the opposite way about this one.

            And the prior Charm gets a nice little crossover with the eating one in the power of Behemoth’s Inhalation Prana; supernaturally powerful lungs allow a Lunar to breathe in at extreme pressure, drawing in vapours and gases apart from air within medium range, even if they’re magical, to concentrate them in the Lunar’s person (although they still have to deal with any poisons involved). I like some of the described alternative applications of this, such as clearing out an obscuring fog, or clearing a burning building of dangerous smoke. The Lunar at Essence 2 who manages to fully overcome the toxicity or other danger of any such inhalation will get the option to breath it back out as an unblockable decisive attack that uses Stamina at an enemy in medium range, to try and expose them to its danger. And if in the form of an animal of Legendary Size, the increased capacity allows it to be drawn in from out to long range. I think this is Stamina starting to touch off of something more weird, and I am loving it.

            Adder Fang Method was previewed to us in the introduction of Totemic Charms, a power to allow one to generate their own toxins and venoms, either a default one that can only inflict bashing damage when it’s burned through Initiative, or those of any possessed animal shapes, which can even be applied to a weapon or projectile with a good stunt. I’m not quite sure how to read the Protean effect; it says that one in the right animal shape, one can “instead increase a dose’s duration by (Essence)”, and I’m wondering instead of what? Is it saying that the poison’s Stamina duration can be increased by that amount? Substituted for it? I think that language needs to be cleaned up a bit.

            Ah, Blood-and-Tear Elixir Cultivation, this is the one I was missing back in Intelligence, that one to manufacture medicines from one’s own body. A nice bit of oddity in how a Lunar might function as a physician and promulgator of cures, even if they’re at their best when the Lunar of their Solar mate is using them (to reroll 1s on a Medicine rolls) and I particularly like the premise of getting a more effective substance to treat a malady the Lunar has personally suffered, being a kind of reservoir for acquired immunities. Perhaps I expected it in Intelligence partially because that’s what the Totemic effect allows for it.

            Oh my, that was rather a lot of Essence 1 Charms, but it’s finally moved up from that, with Mamba-and-Cobra Mastery, to provide a significant enhancement to the Lunar’s own venom, at the cost of two Initiative, getting a default of making the poison last one extra round and makes it corrosive enough to inflict aggravated damage, and then being able to continue extending the duration for every 10 rolled on the hit and damage rolls. If they repurchase at Essence 3, they have the option for those 10s to instead increase the poison’s damage or the penalty it imposes, or mix and match the three parameters up to a maximum of five bonuses, as well as producing a poison that can never have its duration reduced below one. It would seem to me that Lunars don’t need to wait very long before they can develop the power to make somebody die in a particularly unpleasant manner, just Essence 2 and three Charm purchases.

            Plague Rat Prana follows a similar principle in its enhancement of the power to transmit contagion through attacks, as a Lunar becomes capable of retaining illnesses that they’ve managed to fight off within themselves as an asymptomatic carrier, allowing them to be generally contagious or to transmit them deliberately through attacks. Their control is fine enough to allow them to subtract their own Essence from the virulence of the disease to a minimum of 1, for the sake of not being too much of a danger through exposure to allies or bystanders who are simply standing too close. And then Pestilential Venom Fang allows them to pay a mote to improve the disease within them by adding +2 to morbidity and virulence, also making it capable of subtracting 1s from successes for rolls to resist (it much be making a particularly clever and adaptive pathogen, by exposure to the Lunar’s blood and example). And Friend of the Plague then allows them to round it off by creating other delayed carriers, able to utilise Rabid Beast Bite with just a touch when not in combat, ensuring that the carriers will not have to cope with the disease themselves for days even while they remain infections, and being virtually undetectable as infected characters. It looks like a good suite to assault a locale with illness, and I especially like the implications it has for the Full Moon Caste, how being able to exert themselves physically isn’t all showy kung fu action, it can also be more subtle and widespread.

            Undying God-Beast Perfection is an odd one, having minimums in Stamina and Strength and needing Deadly Beastman Transformation as one of its prerequisites. Still, I imagine it’s the only way to give the war form its persistent regeneration, permanently enhancing that shape so that the Lunar restores a health level every time they reset to base Initiative. It makes me wonder if the more finalised version of that Charm might be a bit different from how it’s shown up in quick character write-ups, possibly something that might particularly line up with this to allow a Lunar in the shape to keep firing off decisive attacks to keep knitting themselves back up.

            Hmm, wonder what a name like Incomparable Bezoar Nature might entail… oh, it’s not about the stomach stone side, just the curative one, as a Lunar gets to be permanently immune to the poison of any animal that they can turn into, as well as letting the Stamina Excellency add two dice per mote for resisting other poisons.
            Poison Blood Prana provides an alternative delivery mechanism for toxins, as close range decisive attacks can be responded to with an unblockable counterattack in the form of a poisonous blood spray, hence adding the wound penalty as non-Charm dice to the Stamina based attack roll to expose an enemy to one of the products of their Charms, with increased duration if they’re in an animal form at the time.

            But they start going for something different and a bit odder with Salamander’s Tail Feint; acceptance of crippling injuries are allowed to negate even more damage, even to the point that it would go below the usual threshold for being allowed to make the substitution (referred in the flavour text as enabled by a process of letting the attack take off extraneous flesh). If the fight is to uphold a Defining Intimacy, it doesn’t even use up their once per story limit on taking crippling injuries; that it can only be used once per scene would definitely put the Lunar ahead of the curve, particularly if they use their other Charm to keep fixing their mangling up. It gets even better with Protean, as being in an animal shape that can naturally shed limbs as a defence doesn’t make it contribute to the per-story limit in any event. Mind, the Defining Intimacy part is the one that really intrigues me; come up with a Lunar that is mad for fighting, and let them throw themselves into the fray with wild abandon, coming away maimed and mangled to take a few scenes to fix it up, and then start all over again.

            Into Essence 3, and something rather Hellsing-esque in Form-Shedding Sacrifice, an Instant Charm with no cost. The Lunar attains a new method of using crippling to negate damage, in the form of sacrificing a shape that they’re in, exchanging their Essence in damage levels for permanently losing the shape. On the face of it, it sounds like an extreme loss, but after a few hours you probably have more faces from random guards and bandits and voles and sparrows than you need, so you might as well shed them to save the real you. I especially love how it can be combined with the Charm to fake death to let a shell of the lost form be left behind, while the Lunar reflexively tries going to ground.

            Acid-Spitting Attack allows them to go a bit more directly bombardier than the Dexterity Charms did, as the Lunar becomes capable of paying motes, Initiative and Willpower to spit acid, an unblockable decisive attack that uses their Essence plus threshold attack successes as the dice to inflict aggravated damage, being able to shoot to medium range and all. Alternatively, they can spew it as a nasty spray hitting anybody in short range and rolling Essence as damage. Both attacks get to ignore Hardness, and the corrosion of the acid causes damaged characters to briefly lose some of their own soak and Hardness. And for one more, instead of an attack, they can go and spray it over an object as a demolition feat with automatic successes, getting to waive the Initiative and Willpower costs, and using their Stamina as the basis for determining what feats are permissible (which can still be elevated with the right Strength Charms). The attack form only gets to be used once per scene, but has the accommodating reset of crashing an enemy (which has to be particularly easy when you’ve just made their armour all mushy).

            Oooh, some more extension to the internal capacity with Storm-Swallowing Technique, as the Lunar gets to suck up mundane and supernatural weather effects, possibly having to deal with some physical afflictions if they’re something like magic acid rain or… electrical storms, probably. It says that it’s only out to medium range (or long when Legendary Size), but I think that probably ought to be read in terms of the radius around the character that gets cleared rather than obligating them to rise miles into the air to get close enough. Ooh, and it’s strong enough to prevent the likes of Stormcaller from being able to reimpose its weather. It can only be used once per day, but it has to be one of the more impressive things the Lunar did that day; just imagine harnessing it to save a town from a tornado or something. And not at a super high Essence, either.

            Ah, finally Flesh-Waxing-Full Regeneration provides that capacity to deal with somebody hacking away limbs or removing eyes. It’s… incredibly cheap, a scene-long Charm for only 1 mote and 1 Willpower; if the Lunar is in a fight, then their crippling penalties will be reduced by one after each passage of the difference between their Essence and 7 rounds, fully restoring themselves once it hits zero. Not being in combat takes longer, as the penalty reduction comes only at the end of a scene and needs to be invoked repeatedly, but again, cheap; four scenes and as many motes later, and you’ll have your arm back good as new.

            Indestructible Recursive Design is a curious approach; the Lunar becomes permanently receptive to healing magic (presumably that of others as well as themselves) even while in perfect health, allowing it to cause them to grow extra organs or tougher tissues, in the form of giving themselves up to their Essence in temporary -0 health levels (which means they eventually get to make up for the shortcomings of Ox-Body Technique, I think).

            After all of those, Perdurant Beast Perfection seems positively mundane, a two mote Charm to subtract their Essence from the dice rolled for an environmental hazard’s damage. It allows resistance to hazards that would inflict uncountable damage with a Willpower point surcharge, but rather than negating completely, it reduces them down a flat 15 damage, which will then be further reduced by this and any other appropriate effect. That is unless they’ve adapted to the environment by Rugged Beast Endurance, in which case they get to reduce those extreme hazards without needing to pay Willpower. The reference to swimming in the Demon Sea is want sells this Charm for me; language like that was used in a Second Edition Charm that just made you immune outright, which felt to me as though it detracted from the image of making an enduring feat against a legendarily deadly hazard. I prefer this more involved format.

            And Endurance comes to an end with a skip to Essence 5 for… Soul Beyond Shape. Huh, what does it- ah, this’ll be your kind of Sha’a Oka trick, saving yourself from death by sacrificing a human or animal shape for it (apparently not one you need to be in at the time). It automatically heals your Incapacitated health level, and automatically activates Charms for faking death and going into healing hibernation for free, and being able to keep themselves safe with Wound-Mastering Body Evolution not needing to have an Initiative cost. Only decisive attacks can hurt them again while in that state, and even if they’re mortally wounded again, they can just keep on sacrificing shapes. It can only last until the Lunar heals a health level, and the process is given a nice touch of visual oddity as the lost shapes are expelled from the Lunar, bearing the prevented mortal wounds. After that, can’t be used again in that story. Isn’t even very expensive, just needs ten motes and three Willpower. Certainly lends a lot to why some of the older Lunars might be hard to pin down.



            Yes, after the more utilitarian Defence, Endurance does a lot to draw in a lot of the more elaborate and mythic, the feats that could really let a Lunar dedicated to Stamina impose themselves upon the world, in addition to not letting the world impose itself on them. As I said before, I think it’s really valuable for the Full Moon Caste for them to be able to be associated with a bunch of feats like this.

            All right, so, going over all of these has… left me completely exhausted. And there are still the actual Berserker Charms to go through. But there’s only four pages of them, so those shouldn’t take too long. I’ll have myself some dinner, and then knock those out.

                Okay, finish off Stamina with the Berserker Charms, which I’m going to guess will have a lot more room to breathe now that they’re not treated as one of the two mainstays of Lunar combat power.



                Starts off again with the iconic Relentless Lunar Fury. First thing I notice is how it can last longer than it could before, being a scene long Charm now. So what does it do… okay, so first it centres itself on the berserker concept by being only possible to activate after having received damage from a decisive attack, rather than just pulling that rage up willy nilly. For their trouble, they get halved wound and crippling penalties, and reap Initiative from any further decisive damage, and if they’re in the form of an animal with the complementary Merit, they also get that old benefit of adding a dice to all physical Attribute rolls. The Lunar doesn’t become mindless, or even necessarily have their judgement clouded, just… hyperfocused, as they’re prevented from making influence actions unrelated to combat, engaging in withdrawal, or even ceasing to fight at all before all enemies are unconscious, dead or running away. It’s possible to end the Charm early, but doing so punishes by instantly crashing the Lunar. I think it’s a good, nuanced take on a berserker rage, and for a starting Charm I’d say that it provides both decent benefits and a good sense of character. Excited to see how the following Charms expand and elaborate.

                First of those is Deathless Fury Unleashed, a Permanent Charm allowing Halting the Scarlet Flow to waive its Willpower cost if activated at the same time as the preceding Charm, with higher Essence allowing it to still be paid to allow the regeneration to continue functioning even if the Lunar is crashed. Then Mother Grizzly Attitude, a more direct Permanent enhancement to the Fury, allowing it to be invoked not when the Lunar is struck, but when they witness such an attack against their mate of somebody for whom they have a positive higher Tie, also allowing the Charm to provide a bonus to Parry used in defending others (not just the person whose injury provoked the rage, it would seem). That idea of expanding the scope a bit, and being able to play out the scenario of being pushed by outrages inflicted upon others is very compelling. I especially like how that lines up with Fury not interfering with their mind very much, that the ward’s Lunar defender doesn’t necessarily have to be terrifying to them.

                Into Essence 2 for Enraged Ratel Persistence, which is not Permanent; instead, its activation allows the Lunar to draw strength from adversity in turning their wound penalty into a bonus for Resolve. That’s something it gets to do all on its own, but in combination with the Fury it ensures that fear or influence that would cause a Lunar to give up on the fight are completely ineffectual. That’s probably pretty powerful as social defences go, but counterbalanced by the specificity of when it can be used, and it helps preserve the integrity of that method of fighting.

                Undaunted Berserker Ire allows a Lunar in combat to substitute Initiative for Willpower for resisting social influence and similar magics, which might be helpful even in the face of the prior Charm if there are troublesome forms of influence that aren’t explicitly trying to end the fight. The exchange rate is rather high, 5 to 1 against the Willpower, but I guess that the higher turnover rate for Initiative can help make it back up easier than spending Willpower would do. When Fury is active, it gets a once-per-scene option to waive one point of the Willpower cost for resisting an influence.

                Another alternative activation condition in Sleeping Tiger Wakes, as the Lunar gets to activate their Fury just in response to somebody threatening or insulting one’s mate or the subject of a higher Intimacy, allowing a Join Battle to be made with half the Essence in bonus successes if this is the way that the fight starts. It’s interesting to me that a Lunar can’t be as angry on behalf of themselves as they can be for somebody else… maybe with the exception of somebody insulting an appropriate Major or Defining Principle.

                Essence 3 now, and Knife-Biting Attitude, which has been mentioned in at least one place before, going to have to look back at what’ll make more sense in context later, but for now… huh, neat, get to activate before decisive damage is rolled, and get Initiative equal to inflicted damage, pushing the Lunar even further as they’re damaged. It can’t normally be used in crash, but can when Fury is active, which seems like it might makes this a rather good standby for pulling oneself out of that state (especially for a Lunar who buys enough Ox-Bodies to get another 12 or 20 health levels, and has the body to spare… hmm, already forgotten if regeneration interacts with all of this).

                Ah, Bloodthirsty Siaka Frenzy is finally giving Lunars some of the power in situations to activate Fury, as well as getting some of the apex predator stuff back in, as they get to use the Charm when they have inflicted damage on the health of somebody else, namely down to at least an opponent’s -2 health levels. Furthermore, they get the option to spend three motes whenever Initiative resets to base in order to add the struck foe’s wound penalty after that latest attack to the value of their base Initiative.

                Wounded Bear Rampage permanently endows a Lunar with a sense of empowerment to the will to survive as their injuries grow, allowing the non-crashed Lunar to get Willpower every time they take damage to their -2 or 4 health levels (further synergizing with Ox-Body), which can rise even beyond their personal limit. Oh, wait, spoke to soon, it doesn’t work every time they take such an injury, as it’s the odd permanent Charm that can only be used once per scene; like one of the earlier Charms, even then it won’t reset if the Lunar still has at least -2 health levels damaged when the next scene rolls around.

                Gone up to Essence 4 now with Frenzied Desperation Strike, a little something for the Lunar that has been crashed, allowing them to launch a decisive attack despite having no Initiative. It took me a couple of reads (and going back to make sure I wasn’t misremembering the crash rules) to figure out what the base damage of that attack would be, but noting how it had a minimum damage of 1 put it together for me; most likely it’s a matter of Lunar’s Essence + 5, then take the negative value of their current Initiative, half it, and subtract it from the other value. Been a while since I did much beyond basic maths, but… yeah, dividing negatives is fairly straightforward, so if you’re Essence 4 and at -6 Initiative, you’ll roll six dice in decisive damage. And hey, if you hit with that, it actually resets you to higher than you were at before, ending up at 0 Initiative. Like, the damage value’s still going to be small, so probably only really ever useful to somebody with no Hardness, but it can probably still be a good turnaround. Only gets to be used once per scene, but if at least 20 Initiative is gathered in a single tick, then it can be reset.

                Terrible Wolverine Onslaught is, I think, a bit unusual as a Simple Charm that can only be activated while under the effects of a prior Charm, being limited to synergy with Fury. If it’s done while in the form of an animal with Strength 5 or more, the control rounds paid to remove the armour still get to apply to the throw or slam’s damage roll.

                                    And it seems like things are getting particularly unpleasant with Spine-Breaking Technique… although the effect is fairly straightforward, simply inflicting a savaging attack on a grappled enemy without needing to reset to base. It’s only once per scene, but has a reset condition of a regular attack resetting to base and then getting Initiative back up to 12 through a withering savaging attack, which should both work for somebody so invested in grappling and again puts my mind back towards the warform’s increased base.

                                    And now it’s into Essence 4, starting with All-Devouring Tyrant Maw, which… allows a Lunar with at least five rounds of control in a grapple to simply swallow up an enemy. It does require a certain logic with concerns to size in doing so, mind; the Lunar isn’t getting some hyperspace gullet, there has to sensibly be room in their current shape for the being that they wish to snatch up. It seems to basically function as allowing the grapple to be maintained while the Lunar is freed up to act otherwise and the caught character can’t attack anybody besides their holder, although the Storyteller is probably free to declare dangers from things like stomach acid or suffocation if they make sense for the specifics. The Lunar doesn’t get to apply the typical grapple actions besides releasing… ah, I misunderstood the earlier point, the Storyteller fiat is just on ways that the grappled character might be further constrained; the danger of digestive fluids is the actual point of this, as the target has to deal with such as an environmental hazard or high difficulty and using the Lunar’s Essence as the damage value, with a comment about how the Lunar has the option to deal with an incapacitated enemy by either spitting them out unconscious, or keeping them into to be fully digested (I’m guessing with a preternatural rapidity) before expelling any indigestible equipment. Rounds of control from the grapple can only be lost from taking damage, and losing control completely that way is represented by getting enough wounds to open a hole that the victim can struggle free from, inflicting another damage level that is unavoidable. Changing shape to something too small to hold the enemy forces the Lunar to spit them free in the process of doing so.

                                    I’m going to assume that a few Legendary Size shapes like the tyrant lizard can still apply their special attacks to try eating people, but for the most part I think that it’s sensible to cover the notion of a Lunar doing so while in a gigantic form (or when they’ve magically converted an enemy into a smaller one) with a Charm. It keeps the thing from having a lot of messy uncertainties, which I think has a higher value than whether or not it strictly makes sense.

                                    Even when at a regular size, a Lunar can invoke the spirit of being a gigantic form mercilessly crushing underfoot with Behemoth’s Footprint Tread, as they pour fifteen motes and three levels of anima into creating a crushing shockwave of force, that rolls a decisive attack on any character in medium range, without discrimination, that applies one third of the Lunar’s Initiative plus threshold successes on the attack roll as a damage pool against each of them, while also knocking any who take damage another range band away and prone. If it’s in an animal shape that can Trample, the damage pool is based on half of the Lunar’s Initiative, which I think might make this qualify as one of the most dangerous attacks to apply against multiple enemies in the game. That’s something like the kind of power a warstrider could put behind hitting multiple people (albeit at a more limited range and with less collateral damage). And for my comments about energy attacks before, I particularly love the idea that a Lunar might manifest this with something like bringing their own foot down corresponding to the image of their anima coming along as a huge spectral foot. It sounds like a good show all round. Also particularly like the idea of doing it while in the shape of a horse or something; rearing up and bringing your forelegs down accompanied by a thunderclap.

                                    Divine Predator Supremacy makes a Lunar particularly stirred by their opponents’ vulnerability, getting to make a withering attack that includes non-Charm dice equal to their wound penalty and doubles dice depending on the depth of that penalty; 9s for -1, 8s for -2, 7s for -4. It’s only once per scene, but incapacitating a crashed enemy (i.e. probably the person struck by this Charm) resets it. Come to think of it, the very existence of this Charm puts a lot of those reset conditions for reaping large amounts of Initiative all at once into perspective; I doubt that they necessarily strictly require this, but the idea that you can ever get a Charm to really elevate the successes added to your raw damage pool (plus the benefits of Deadly Beastman Transformation, plus the Strength Excellency) make it feel a bit less ephemeral.

                                    Rampaging God-Monster Empowerment invigorates the predatory spirit of a Lunar by letting them reroll their Essence in failed decisive damage dice against lower-Initiative enemies and get motes for all of the rolled 10s up to any spent on enhancing the attack (which surely starts with the 8 spent for this Charm), and allows 1s to be rerolled as well if the attack was on a crashed enemy… oh wait, it’s a scene long Charm, the Lunar can reap these benefits persistently across a fight, that’s neat (although it ends with the Lunar’s own crash).

                                    And now there’s (Style) Beastman Avatar, a name with a particularly exciting promise. Ah, it’s an enhancement to Deadly Beastman Transformation by allowing it to be kind of melded with a Martial Art that the Lunar has mastered, letting the warform and Style’s Form Charm by activated simultaneously when assuming the former, with the added cost of a Willpower surcharge.
                                    Across the board reflexive Form Charm activation sounds like a particularly good benefit (even if each additional Style added costs more experience), and I do find something compelling both in the idea that there can be something characteristic to Lunars assuming the warform for the sake of their martial arts, and that the first thing they do upon donning their horrific monster form is settle into a stance or kata.

                                    And to finish off the Offence Charms at Essence 5, there’s Titan-Rending Fury. Simply put, a crashed enemy that attacks the Lunar can have their attack swatted aside with a reflexive clash that doesn’t use up attack for the round, and just wins outright (although I’d warrant that rolling successes is useful for pushing such an enemy deeper into crash). Only gets to be used against the same person once in a scene, unless they recover and are then crashed again. After a lot of more extreme Charms, I find something elegant in the simplicity of saying that a Lunar at the pinnacle of Strength and power can merely brush aside the attacks of people so disadvantaged in a fight that they’re barely worth acknowledging. This is a Charm for demoralising a Solar swordsman who comes to challenge an ancient Lunar in their lair and keeps going even while being severely outclassed. I’ve been hoping that some of Lunars would be casual displays of huge power, so I’m really happy to see some of that going here.


                                    And that’s the Offence Charms. Starts off with a slow burn, but has some very nice realisations of some of the most iconic Lunar powers and then builds up to some wonderfully brutal feeling measures of attack and counterattack.

                                    Took freaking ages to get through, though. Going to have to see if I can double time it for the rest of the Charms after lunch.

It only gets to be used once per story, but can be reset with action to uphold an Intimacy used for the Decision Point when it is Defining (or any level of the Tie to the Solar). That reset condition gives me a sense of characterization whereby a Lunar who manages to see reason against some direction gets really pissed, but then also feels encouraged to immediately go off and do something affirmative for one of their values, helpful to their friends, or harmful to their enemies. The Solar aspect conjures especially amusing ideas when running with the idea that the Tie is negative; I’m picturing a Lunar who has been convinced or mind controlled into doing something, up until the point when it becomes apparent that it’s going to be beneficial for their mate to whom they’ve formed a mild commitment to act as a rival, which compels them to shake it off, before they go off and do some seemingly random act of harm or sabotage to the Solar or their interests.

                                                            Eternal Guardian’s Vow is another permanent enhancement, this one drawing more on the legacy of one’s past incarnations, as the Lunar becomes capable of treating influence that would lead to harming one’s Solar mate or act in some manner contrary to the Tie if it’s positive as unacceptable. Interestingly, the influence of the Solar themselves is not subject to that, although who knows why they might want to tell the Lunar to act in a harmful manner. It does, however, introduce a slight weak point, just to prevent stories concerning a Lunar dedicated to Solar from becoming really one-note, I’m sure; characters can make an instil roll against the Lunar, obliged to roll twice and use the weaker result. But if it works, the Lunar’s protection from Eternal Guardian’s Vow is rescinded against the character who made the influence until such time as the Lunar performs and action that upholds the Tie to the Solar, but only if it’s Defining. I’m glad for giving something in there to allow more engagement with the stories of such dedicated characters, and I think the exchanges that the instil roll will be made to represent could be particularly intriguing play experiences; the introduction of some niggling doubt, say.

                                                            Going in another direction with that is Silver-and-Gold Union; the Lunar is able to activate the Charm to allow a positive Tie to Solar mate that is valid for a Decision Point to function as though it’s Defining (while the actual Defining Tie waives a Willpower cost for resisting influence). It’s only usable once per story, but can be reset if the Solar acts in a manner upholding a positive Defining Tie to the Lunar, that causes them a significant hardship in the performance, as soon as the Lunar becomes aware of that action. This alone would be something to convince me to not give the Solar an immediate Tie; when a Lunar can reap a benefit like that from it, it makes me really feel as though there should be circumstances in which getting the Solar to feel so strongly, starting with getting them to feel anything whatsoever, should be all them.

                                                            Laughing Into the Teeth of Madness, there’s a Charm name that’s speaking to me right now. Hmm, variable mote cost, will be interesting to see what that’s about. It permanently enhances Resisting the Lure of Madness to allow the Resolve bonus to be applied to actions that will help to resist shaping effects to mind, soul, destiny, or body (for those Lunars who haven’t taken their damn tattoos yet). Ah, here’s where the cost is starting to come in; in the event of an effect with no such avenue of resistance, 2 motes can be paid to force one, allowing the Lunar’s Essence + current Willpower to be rolled against the current Willpower of the originator of the effect (or a difficulty set by Storyteller in the event of being from something not a character, such as Wyld exposure), and either character needs to pay Willpower to make up for all of their dice that show failures. The Lunar winning this one (which I’m guessing includes if the other character can’t pay all the Willpower they owe) doesn’t stop the effect altogether, just diminishes the impact, removing any capacity it would have to prevent shapeshifting or the use of Charms, and allowing the introduction of a condition that allows the shaping to be broken if it would be permanent or deadly (although the Lunar still needs to take actions with the right Abilities to figure out what that condition might be). So I’m guessing something like… the Lunar encounters a magic that would turn them to stone, and rolls against it; if they fail, turn to stone outright. If they succeed, the shape they’re in at the time turns to stone, but they can shapeshift into others, and can either hold out until the effect wears off the cursed form, or figures out a means of dispelling it if it would naturally last forever. Gonna have to figure out other stuff for the tattooed Lunar… still, I’m happy to see this premise put in, with slightly altered form (after all, mitigating being turned into a cat by becoming a catperson means something a bit different for folks who could do that already), for Lunars, maintaining the principle of letting the idea of such curses by engaged with in a more interesting way than shutting down altogether.

                                                            It also introduces a workaround for Eyes of the Unconquered Sun, or any other magic that would deny their shapeshifting, to make the Essence + Willpower roll (with double 9s) against the other, to both prevent the effect from taking hold and conferring immunity for a scene. I’m glad for that, and how it’s much lower Essence than the other side of it; I’m not against it being part of that Solar Charm, but on its own something about it didn’t quite sit right with me.

                                                            (Incidentally, I’m having British Bake-Off play in the background at the moment, and it’s helping me feel more confident about the manner in which I’m reviewing everything {blush})

                                                            Chaos-Defying Embrace is a permanent enhancement to the prior Charm, allowing a ten mote surcharge to be paid on the last two Charms in the tree so that their effects can be applied to protect somebody within medium range to whom the Lunar has a positive higher Tie, while we’re given a note that this can’t be applied to an effect retroactively. It can be applied to a Solar mate at any level of Tie (although seemingly still limited to the positive), which also grants Willpower if the protection is successful. I wonder how the mitigated curses might work for somebody who couldn’t shapeshift anyway; I guess the Lunar will need to carry around their statue friend until they can fulfil the conditions to save them.

                                                            Sacred Guardian Renewal is another personal source of affirmation, as the successful affirmation of Resolve against an influence opposing a personal Intimacy reaps that Intimacy’s value back in motes (although again, it requires motes to have been spent on enhancing it). Preventing opposition to a Tie to the Solar treats it as though it’s Defining, or gives one more mote if it actually is. Even with still having the capacity as a mote refunder, I think this one may be a bit more flexible than some of the prior Charms of that kind, and it does do a lot to solidify the sense of a Lunar’s own feeling and values being something very deep and hard within them. I like the sense of contrast in that; the flesh is malleable, but the mind and soul beneath can be adamant, and gives the malleability such purpose.

                                                            Hohoho, the idea in Nightmare Beast Soul that the Lunar’s spirit shape can serve as a kind of internal guardian to ward off interlopers to their dreams is a delightful image. The Lunar is permanently enhanced to have +2 Resolve and Guile as a non-Charm bonus against effects that target while the Lunar is asleep, and if their dreams are entered outright by other forms of magic, a point of Willpower can be spent to manifest their spirit shape as a protective nightmare, whose action is mechanically represented by an opposed Willpower roll in which the Lunar has the advantage from the benefit of double 9s, with success allowing the nightmare to drive the intruder out, the psychic shock of the experience causing Willpower loss equal to 1s rolled on the attempted resistance, and forced to take levels of lethal damage for any Willpower they can’t afford; extending from the image of the protective spirit shape, I love the idea of such an intruder being unexpectedly driven out by a nightmare beast, and being shocked to return to the waking world and realise that they’re developing wounds reminiscent of what the animal would do. Scratches, bites, very very deep hoof shaped gouges…

                                                            Going into Essence 3 with Argent-Etched Taboo, to help draw on the cultures the Lunar values; if forced into a Decision Point from an influence overcoming a Resolve boosted by a positive Tie to a culture, activate the Charm (with a cost of just one mote) to allow one of the culture’s customs that would plausibly oppose the influence to function as an Intimacy of the same level as that towards the culture. Can only be used for any given cultural Tie once per story, but gets reset for them by participation in one of its customs. This one has some really fun sense of personality; that a Lunar can care for a society while not really valuing all of its customs, but if doing so would help the Lunar to stay true to that society, then they can adapt their mindset to do so momentarily. Then feeling a need to take part in some of the culture’s practices, somewhat perfunctorily, but in a manner that at least affirms to the Lunar that it’s a practice that they people they care for love.

                                                            Hmm, Shadow-Mind Meditation. Oh dear, what was I saying about the Lunar’s mind being adamant? The Lunar who learns the Charm can pull together everything negative in their personality to form an underlying shadow-mind to exist independently within them, which will share the Lunar’s Defining Intimacies and whatever the Tie is to a Solar mate, but the Major and Minor ones are its own, needing to either be negatively inclined Ties or Principles of pessimism or scepticism (which must make for a very fractious personality if they’re directly contradicted by any Defining Principles). While the Charm is Instant, its activation requires a process of four hours of meditation to wake up or push down the shadow-mind, and once awake, its Intimacies will function in place of the Lunar’s own... and I suppose from some of the surrounding language that the idea is supposed to be that the shadow-mind (which is still the Lunar, just on their worst day) is in the driver’s seat. So, its Intimacies are in place, not sacrosanct as such, but more resistant to attempts to create or improve positive Ties or make optimistic Principles, and unable to voluntarily form or strengthen such attitudes while in that state.

                                                            I’ve some thoughts, but I’ll reserve them until I think I’ve seen the full cascade.

                                                            Right, Dark-Premise Duality enhances the prior Charm, allowing the shadow-mind to evolve into a true separate personality, a kind of picture of the Lunar on a very different life path. It gets its own set of Mental and Social Attributes, based on slight reallocations of the Lunar’s own, which can change available Charms while the shadow-mind is at the forefront (both to suppress ones that don’t meet the minimum, and buy ones that the true self won’t have the stats to use), including Excellencies. Aaaand that’s it, that’s the extent of this Charm and the shadow-mind in general.

                                                            So, in the end, it did turn out that Lunars got a bit more like the Solar persona Charms than what Manipulation afforded, but it still plays out very distinctly. I don’t actually think it contradicts my point about Lunar self-control; unlike Solars, the altered Intimacies can’t infringe upon the original self, and the shadow-mind can’t be given an entirely separate set of memories or overall personality. It is technically the concept with much narrower scope, but I think that gives it a certain resonating focus; even when a Lunar is mystically altering their own mind, they’re still firmly themselves, just kind of… leaning into some of the more menacing aspects of the Lunar Essence to evoke themselves in a worse light (albeit possibly one that might have greater mental acumen or social grace). I wouldn’t even call it an alternate personality (something I’m kind of glad the existence of this path is curtailing), more of a really dark mood that can be empowering, and switched off on a whim (within reason of the time for meditation). As an alternative distinct to these Exalted, I like it quite a bit.

                                                            Okay, moving on into the last few. After all of that, glad to see something simpler in Intransigent Silver Soul, with which half the Essence can be added as non-Charm bonus to Resolve in opposing influence that would weaken a Defining intimacy or produce a result opposed by Tie to a Solar mate. That level of defence for any given Intimacy can only apply once per story, and has the severe reset condition of upholding the Intimacy with a legendary social goal. Nothing to say about this one in particular, it’s just another solid addition to the ongoing identified thesis of preserving Lunar personality.

                                                            And to finish off the Resolve Charms at Essence 4, there is Beast-Mind Metanoia. And here, at the end, they actually do get a callback to the old notions of social defence through adopting the mind of an animal; used in response to influence that successfully overcomes Resolve, the Lunar can shift themselves into a mindset equivalent to any animal that they could shift into. They’ll still be capable of understanding their known languages, but the range of their own vocabulary and capacity to make sense of more complex statements might be compromised. In cases where an influence would be tripped up by such limitations, it gets to be treated as unacceptable (or occasionally produces an understanding significantly divergent from the speaker’s intent, and the Lunar will pursue that instead; there’s an example given, and I presume it means that the Lunar on this course retains that mindset until the influence is resolved). Being in an animal shape helps the Charm a lot, as it makes it significantly cheaper to assume the mind of the animal that one is in the form of. A defence of this kind can only be used once per story, but can be reset by the ecstatic experience of Limit Break.



                                                            Yes, I think Resolve came off very nicely, giving a good starting place to characterize Wits and encourage keeping them about you. More than that, the way it characterizes Lunar personality and priorities seems like a valuable level of insight across the board. And I like the social influence system enough that ways to interact with it are something I’m more discerning about, and I’m satisfied with the manner in which it was realised here; strong, but with channels to enable interaction, and occasionally provoking interesting responses.


                                                            Yes, I think I’ve made a good start into Wits. If I start early enough tomorrow and maintain my focus, I might just be able to pull this off.
