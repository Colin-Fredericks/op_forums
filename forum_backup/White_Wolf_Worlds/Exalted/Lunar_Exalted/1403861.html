    Repercussions of Lunar actions on The Caul

    07-21-2020, 03:30 PM
    With what we have been told so far about the War on The Caul (in The Realm and Fangs at the Gates) it appears that at least some of the Lunars in command of the Shrine Cities are looking for ways to turn the nature of these places into weapons that can be used against the Dragon-Blooded. While they likely feel that this is both necessary and justified, I can't help but wonder if they have thought through the repercussions of such actions and what it might do to The Caul in the long run.

    Remember, The Caul was a dream made manifest by the love between Luna and Gaia... and the nature of The Caul seems to reflect this. If these Lunars succeed in turning this dream into a nightmare to be used against the Dragon-Blooded... what will become of The Caul? Could such a nightmare really be controlled... or could it turn against the Lunars as well? Would it poison the Caul to the point where where it could not be saved, even if peace between Lunars and Dragon-Blooded were somehow achieved? What could it do to Luna or Gaia... assuming either are still somehow connected to the dream that is the Caul?

    In short... is it possible that in trying to win the War for the Caul at any cost... could they end up causing its ultimate destruction? If so... would that change anything within the Silver Pact in their war against the Dragon-Blooded in the rest of Creation?

      It's an interesting plot hook, although Sha'a Oka presents a complication to it insofar as he is presented as somebody with an extremely deep insight into the Caul, possibly greater than that of any other being. It can be taken as implicit that the policies of his generals have his blessing, particularly when contrasted against the statement that making the shrine cities into Territories would interfere with their geomancy and is thus something he forbids.

      If characters would make an assertion, or have even made a discovery, that current strategies in the war for the Caul would place it at risk, then they would need to undermine the Black Lion's credibility; either successfully argue that he doesn't understand as well as he thinks he does, or that he knows what the repercussions are and is either fanatical enough that he places hurting the Realm above all else or that his motives are compromised to some more alien purpose.

      For my part, I would think it's most interesting if the facts as the players know them are not objective; that there's a possibility that they'd be earnestly making arguments against Oka, even ones that manage to convince, that are in fact totally at fault.

          I see this more of a poisoning of the Dragon Lines than any major change... and like how it doesn't take a lot of chemicals to poison a stream or river enough to be undrinkable, it may not take a radical shift in the Dragon Line geomancy to achieve the effects the Generals desire... and the change could be so subtle that it is barely noticed.

          One doesn't always see the moment a peaceful dream shifts into a terrible nightmare until it is too late. So too could the perversion of the Shrine Cities be a gradual shift that once started is difficult to stop or undo. It could well be the stricture against altering The Caul's geomancy that is forcing the generals to take such careful methods to change the nature of the Shrine Cities and leads to a more subtle poisoning rather than an abrupt change. Just as it can take some time for chemicals to build up in a lake before they become deadly, so to could the shift be a gradual perversion of what was meant to be into what the Lunars desire it to become... only to find out that they are just as vulnerable to the changes as those they sought to punish for ancestral crimes.

          The free reign that the Generals have been given over their territories, despite Sha'a Oka's insights into the Caul may prove to be the the fatal flaw of the Lunar offensive... because he trusts the shahan-ya, he isn't watching them as closely as he probably should... believing that they wouldn't risk the long term goal for a short term victory. It wouldn't be the first time he put his trust in those that may not be as trustworthy as he believes (he did one trust Dragon-Bloods before the Usurpation).

          The simple truth however is that despite any insight into the nature of The Caul Sha'a Oka may have, nothing like what his Generals are trying to do to the Shrine Cities has been attempted before (that we know of), so it is likely that even he doesn't know what the full ramifications of such plans would be... assuming he even knows what they are planning (not saying he doesn't, but it is likely that his Generals aren't telling him everything they intend... at least not until they know how to do it). Even if he did know everything about their plans, he could see it as a necessary step to achieve the larger goal... and deal with the repercussions once The Caul is cleansed of the Dragon-Blooded.

          But lets move past these points... as they are only minor details of how he might let something like this happen. Let us assume that regardless of any concerns he may have to the contrary, he allows the Generals (or their seconds) to proceed and deal with any consequences later. How would one show the likely slow and gradual change in the nature of the Shrine Cities? It is likely that once the changes have been made, it would become necessary for the Lunars to allow the captured cities to be "liberated" by the Dragon-Blooded so that their pilgrimages could expose them to the alterations the Lunars have made. It would likely take some time for the effects to be noticed... too quickly and the Dragon-Blooded would learn something was off before they were properly weakened. It would likely require them to get all the way to the Final City to learn the full effect of what had been done... or at least one of the side effects... that entering the Final City not only no longer guarantees the next child born will receive the Second Breath... but no child born from those who make the pilgrimage will ever become Dragon-Blooded. Of course by then, many will have been exposed to the alterations of the Shrine Cities and the more subtle poison will have begun to spread... twisting their experiences towards the aims the Generals intended.

          But where would this end... and how could the alterations begin to infect the Lunars... how long before they noticed the changes are affecting them as well (likely not physically... at least not for Lunars with Moonsilver Tattoos)? Could it be reflected in their acting more bestial... their rage getting the better of them more often as they slowly build up Limit faster (possibly even to the point of acquiring permanent Limit)? Once the do know the effects of what they have done, undoing it may prove to be a challenge... as they would have to retake each Shrine City to have a chance of undoing the changes that had been made... assuming such changed could be undone. With the rage (Limit) building faster with each passing month, week or day... how long could they remain united and coordinated enough to do so? Would the changes need to be done at the Shrines Cities... or would the Lunars need to reach the Final City to undo the problem there... requiring them to control all five Shrine Cities to make such a pilgrimage through the poisoned Shrines? Might saving the Caul even require the unthinkable... both a Lunar and Dragon-Blood to put aside their hatred of each other to make the journey to the Final City together... united as Luna and Gaia were when they first made the dream that is The Caul?

          Just some thoughts as to how this could unfold... as both a Lunar or a Dragon-Blooded game (or as a combined game). Any further ideas on where to take this seed?
