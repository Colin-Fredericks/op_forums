    Thinking 3E: Lunar Logistics and the Adventures in feeding a nation.

    10-20-2014, 09:59 PM
    So I thought that now that 3E is close enough that I can taste it it's time to get the old noodle thinking Exalted again. Consider this a new series of posts if/when I can called "Thinking 3E".

    Today's little thought exercise is about Lunar Domains, war effort, and their impact on the Realm, Threshold, and the game in general. Later I'll talk more about the Lunar/Realm war and fun things you can do with it: Espionage! But for now let's talk kingdom building.

    Any Solar player can tell you building up an Empire to take on the Realm in previous editions was a real nightmare at times. You had to build followers, resources, and then take land as you grew. All the while fending off Realm Legion assaults, Wyld Hunt assassination attempts, and Sidereal activity. We'll put Deathlords and Fae off the table for now. Let's focus on just dealing with the influence of the Realm. Lunars have it hard if 1E and 2E are anything to go by. From what we know 1st Age Lunars were infiltration specialists, show and awe combat types, and really good bodyguard special forces. That doesn't really lead you to be Kingmakers. During the Usurpation the Lunars had a LOT of their Solar built infrastructure pulled out from beneath them. They had to from scratch rebuild their resources, followers, and then deal with combined Sidereal/Dragon-Blood attacks. Oh ya, and that's even BEFORE the Great Contagion and the Wyld invasion. Let's list some of the possible drawbacks being a Lunar ruler:

    * Lack of easy access to Heaven and it's gods. This hurts more than you think. I'm sure Lunars have friends among the various godly forces but the majority of the big players have to contend with the Sidereal sway in Yu-Shan. This means Lunars are forced to deal with local Elemental Courts, rogue gods, and various "under the table" support they can find.
    * The Immaculate Faith. It's going to be a pain in the arse to buy lots of goods from a merchant, nation, farmer if they think you are an ages old abomination out to eat their babies.
    * The Threshold may or may not have the resources you need. Being too far in any one direction has it's issues. The extreme west you run into having to deal with ships (His Lunar Excellency must have a "deal" with the Lintha to get ships past the Realm patrols. I swear.) Extreme South is a lack of water, North can kill your followers pretty fast, and the East while friendly in some ways presents poisonous/crazy endless uber trees. You have to pick a middle ground between utility and isolation.
    * Always on the move. The Sidereals have a Realm wide dragnet, and I'll bet they cast their eyes to the Threshold to ferret out Lunar supply lines and allies. Given time expect Realm/Wyld Hunt/mysterious things taking out your resources. You'd have to constantly keep moving your supply lines around or they WILL be taken out.
    * Charms that may not aid in nation building. Lunars in the 1st Age were build more as combat engines and spies. Unless 2nd Age Lunars are out there developing new charms tied to maintaining and growling civilizations, and I hope they ARE! I'd love to see more Lunar Social charms, then they have issues.

    But being a Lunar has other benefits through, things they have that anyone going against them has to take into consideration:

    * Shapeshifting allows for crazy tricks like replacing or subverting entire organizations and the wonder of it is no one has to be wiser at first. You can redirect entire shipments of Guild supplies to your ally nations, or a 3rd party to 'pass it along' to your people. If your infiltration is found out, just move on to another group and start subverting there.
    * Sorcery/Necromancy. Lunars were pioneers at the art with the Solars. The very stories of how the 2 arts were discovered had Lunars there DAY ONE. This is big. A lot of Lunar magic would still be in play. Sure the Usurpation would hurt some of the knowledge as Lunar elders were killed off back then, but there are teachers out there. Sure they may be crazy and demand tribute and other unfriendly things, but the Silver Pact offers training for a reason. Sorcery and Necromancy allow for all sorts of neat ways to solve logistics problems.
    * Beastmen and Barbarian tribes. Ya I know it's a old trope but having loyal family, or close knit groups that semi worship you helps. The trick is training them to be covert or tactical enough to help prop up your kingdoms. Barbarian doesn't mean dumb, but unless you train/support them it is wasting man power.
    * Raw physical might. What? No really, being able to fight against a full DB battle group also means you can do crazy impossible tasks. Imagine pulling a full barge as a over powered Killer Whale, or moving with the speed of the wind to run deliveries of critical supplies, etc. Lunar physical might can be used to enhance your kingdom, but it takes a creative mind and a more personal touch.

    So those things listed how does this impact my say Solar game? Well the Adventure in feeding a nation is where the STs and Players have options Let's focus on Solar characters for now since they'll be the first to get a taste of this. Let me showcase a few ways it may come up, or can be brought to come up:

    * Solars may run afoul of Lunar's infiltrating a local nation trying to get supplies and recruits for their cause. It may cause the Lunars to combat, try to subvert, or recruit the Solars. Especially if they undermine years of careful infiltration and cover stories due to Solar charms/magic.
    * Solars may find their organizations and operations directly addressed by the Lunars. Because Solars need a lot of the resources that Lunars do when dealing with the Realm. Either covertly or not Lunars can approach Solars to act as inbetweens on furnishing supplies.
    * Solars might find themselves running into a Realm or Sidereal attempt to uncover Lunar activity in the area.

    And all of this isn't even dealing with the Lunar Domains themselves.

    What is your take on the subject?

          On the subject of gods, remember that while Lunars may not have much in the way of allies in Yu-Shan, it's not very difficult for them to make allies with the local Terrestrial Court or Elemental Court or other gods who actually live there - or, even bring in a new spirit to the area.

          If you're setting up a kingdom in the south, find some local water spirit of Oasis or such. Offer to give the spirit a ton of worship in exchange for the spirit providing drinkable water to your people.

          Under normal circumstances, this sort of thing would be illegal for a spirit to do, and they would have to worry about being brought up on charges by the local Celestial Censor. But, thanks to the Mandate of Heaven, spirits are fully allowed to work for Celestial Exalted like this. So this is an easy win-win situation for everyone involved - the Lunar gets a spirit to give his people water (or fire or whatever), the spirit gets a ton of prayer that it normally wouldn't, the people of the kingdom get access to assistance from the spirit, and the best part is, this all totally legitimate.

          With spirit and elemental allies (not bound servants, but actual allies whom you are paying with prayers) it becomes much easier for a Lunar to set up a kingdom in a place where it might not normally be possible, or to grant his followers access to materials that might not naturally occur in a certain area.

                          Another ability of the Lunars that shouldn't be underestimated is their ability to manipulate without revealing themselves. Probably 99.99% of people cannot identify a Lunar that is shapeshifted, and 2E has the following charms: Glance Oration Technique and Falling Leaf Distraction. Those two together allow you to perform Social Attacks without speaking, and making the target think that the idea was their own. As long as you use Essence slowly enough to draw from your personal pool, and not spin up your Anima banner (which would break your shapeshift), you could stroll into a caravan as a cat, and when you walk out the other end, the entire caravan has decided to redirect and head towards your town. Or, heck, you could replace one of their horses, and convince everyone to go where you wanted them to...while still being a horse.

                          As for being in The South, and having to deal with no water....well, the Terrestrial Circle Spell 'Water From Stone' solves that problem.

                                                  What is your take on the subject?
                                                  I think most of these problems aren't too bad actually. You don't have the level of access that Sidereals do to gods, but actually the Silver Pact has had spirit allies for ages, especially in the Bureau of Nature, and in Terrestrial Courts. After all, for like 1500 years any god who didn't like the Bronze Factions' hegemony couldn't turn to the Solars, it was the Lunars who were the enemies of the Bronze Faction. So it's the Lunars who have long-standing alliances. This is probably more with older Lunars than younger ones though.
                                                  Secondly, as was said later, it is incredibly easy for a Lunar to not look like a Lunar. Not all, but a lot of Lunars can just look like someone else. So if they are identified by the Wyld Hunt, well, they can just use a new body.
                                                  In terms of resources, and being on the move... well, that's the same with Solars. Except Lunars tend to be much more mobile, as most of them can fly. Also, they themselves are quite good at coping with a lack of resources, although that's not something they can really pass on to their human civilisation.
                                                  The only big weakness is their charms aren't great for it. But they're not terrible either. The charm for giving people intimacies to their a social group they're exposed to is very useful for making people loyal to the new culture, for example.
