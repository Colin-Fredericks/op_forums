                    The issue is that as stated already, this is of taste. Great Prophecies that due to responses of to them cause great upheavel are huge genre institutes that Exalted draws on. They show up in classical stuff like the Bible and Greek Tragedy, they show up in later stuff liek Romance of the Three Kingdoms and Shakespeare's Julius caesar. They still show up in modern fantasy like in Tanith Lee's Flat Earth and even Dune with Muad'Dib and Leto II. Giant prophecies which define policy and impact history in a huge curse is pretty big. Making it a footnote deflates its point of being in the legend, derails the impetus ofr the Usurpation, and also deflates the reasoning for the current setup beyond petty squabble.

                    It might fit more real politk tries to describe Creation, but Creation is also a genre piece. And genre pieces include Great Prophecies, Usurpations and Calibration Feast Betrayals in teh same way it has stuff like the evil empire in the center of the world or armies of the dead.

Which I have yet to see an argument why that's the case, simply dismissive statements that it is the case. Exalt sort isn't Clan from Vampire, it can matter more than just what your power set is. And in the case of Sidereal Exalted especially, comes with a job descritpion.

                    The second is a simple want of making vague the Usurpation. But to be hoenst, it already is vague. We know who played in it, we know why it happened and we know its effects. We know why the Calibration Feast happened. The mechancis of it all? Who cares. WHatever resistance lost. Potential dissentors? They lost or died. Or are unique enough to be left-open for STs to use or not. The Usurpation can be as detailed or thought-out as it needs to be. It seems from my understanding is you thik it's too detailed. But in a weird since, I think it comes from you over-thinking what's going on. The result you want is a watered down Usurpation with little narrative momentum to it. When from the First Principles of the game, it's the watershed moment of the entire setting. It's Shin Huang Di unites China. It's Caesar crossing the Rhine. It's the Duke Ferdenand (sp?) getting shot moment that defiens everything from then out.

                    Finally, you seem to think you know better than everyone else on this matter. Yet I have yet to see a substantive description from you on what such a world would be like besides vague "It'd be more legendary." If the geopolitical landscape looks like neither previosu edition that's a bit odd there. And it seems that there is some revision of First Principles about. Mainly in making it so that Lunars were reasonably impacted by the Usurpation, rather than toss the oe of setting's three major historical events and deleting it.

                        My issue isn't, again, so much with the Great Prophesy... despite the fact that multiple posters attempt to straw-man that... and more that DURING the Great Prophesy, everything that has been written or stated about the High First Age and the various Exalted types / characters that populate it is thrown own in favor of a old school splat-based cWoD showdown.

                        The Sidereal / Dragonblooded alliance itself is awful ; in specific, it's terrible because it suggests the Dragonblooded rebel 'as a race' because they have just 'had enough'. In pure cWoD fashion, Clan : Dragonblooded has decided that they can no longer tolerate the world as it stands. As a gestalt hive-mind, they flip a switch and suddenly throw in with the rebels.

                        Not only is that massively simplistic, but let's talk about this honor guard thing a second. The 10,000 honor guard Dragonblooded at the Red Wedding Usurpation were -all- traitors?

                        ... in order to buy this, we need to rely upon assumptions of competence / incompetence : every single Sidereal who tried to get a Dragonblooded agent near the throne succeeded. Every single Night Caste / Lunar who had a chance to detect or stop this failed or did not try. No safeguards were in place. None of the spymaster caste were spying. Despite the fact that these were the 'honor guard'... hand-selected personal bodyguards of the Solar Exalted.

                        Beyond that, we have the reaction of the Lunar Exalted. They stand aside and retreat to the Wyld. Ever since 1e this has made no sense ; it was justified in the original edition by literally suggesting the Sidereals had a mass astrology effect that just made them do that.

                        Think about this for a second.

                        The Mystic Xanatos Wizards pull off the Red Wedding Usurpation, but that's not all. They literally have a magical spell in the first edition concept which is so powerful it FORCES all the Lunar Exalted into the Wyld, having them abandon lifelong mates and holdings.

                        Cue the entire history of Lunar Exalted as cWoD Werewolf analogue, as hunted vagabond raiders who never have a home and are always outsider fuzzy angst BLUH I hate it.

                        Sorry, but anybody who can't see how the 1e Usurpation is a huge cWoD-style story that relies heavily upon absurd assumptions of competence on behalf of the 'Gandalf' analogues isn't looking closely enough.

                                            They're humans, they have human brains, brains that reassure them that everything is fine, you can rest and close your eyes, even for a moment. That you are sure there aren't any enemies of the state left, because you did a good job. That reassure you that your people adore you and that you are doing a good job keeping the peace.                                                 Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
                                                The Sidereal / Dragonblooded alliance itself is awful ; in specific, it's terrible because it suggests the Dragonblooded rebel 'as a race' because they have just 'had enough'. In pure cWoD fashion, Clan : Dragonblooded has decided that they can no longer tolerate the world as it stands. As a gestalt hive-mind, they flip a switch and suddenly throw in with the rebels.
                                                Of course that's ridiculous. That's why what you're saying didn't happen. All that's needed is that a sufficient number of DBs went along with it or did nothing for long enough that it was too late by then. There totally would have been DBs loyal to the Solars who fought other DBs. We hardly need a detailed breakdown of how many of them at the Feast were loyal and protected Solars, how many were traitors, how many just followed their leader, how many had been manipulated somehow by Sidereals, etc.

                                                I just don't think the splat conflicts are as you say it is. There were Dragon-Blooded who would have preferred to keep the Solars. There were Sidereals who were against the plan. To return to the original subject, it's entirely possible that there were some Lunars who were actually fed up enough with their Golden Overlords that "psst, hey, we're going to slaughter all the Solars at the Calibration Feast, you interested?" sounded good to them. For some reasons already mentioned, i.e. Lunars were closer to Solar rule (that one actually surprised me a bit, but I'm curious to see what will be written about the First Age in Ex3), it's much riskier to try and suborn them compared to the Dragon-Blooded (if only because they'd be harder to assassinate if the discussion doesn't go as you wanted it to go :P), etc, trying to turn Lunars traitor against the Solars wouldn't have been a wide-spread tactic in the days leading to the Usurpation.

                                                And hey, if a handful of them DID help at the Feast, they probably got betrayed by the Sidereals at some point so the Vision of Bronze could be realized. Or maybe they left by themselves when they realized they'd been manipulated into helping something worse than they'd thought. Or maybe there's a couple of Lunars who worked with the Sidereals even into the Shogunate and beyond! Maybe they're the past life of a PC or NPC. Maybe they're still alive right now! That's kind of a cool story idea. Good thing there won't be a sidebar in the Ex3 core that says "you cannot do this thing ever, all Lunars reacted to the Usurpation in exactly the same fashion".

                                                So, nope, I don't see a problem with the splat-based conflict. I will grant that the Usurpation does need both some incompetence and some competence from certain parties, but between some mitigating factors (Solars were going mad, they were mostly watching for betrayal from their fellow Solars instead of the ever-loyal Sids, etc) and the whole "it's a cool, epic betrayal that happened far enough into the past that the details don't matter all that much unless you decide to base your game around some of it" thing, I'm pretty much okay with it.

                                                I will also note that I have zero knowledge of what they wrote about it in 1e. And dang, that astrology thing is pretty dumb.
