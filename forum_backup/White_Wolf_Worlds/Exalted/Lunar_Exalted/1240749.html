    Demographics of Lunar Survivors in Creation (Math Request)

    07-31-2018, 03:52 AM
    Hi,
    I was having a debate with my girlfriend, and neither of us were exactly up on calculating the numbers. We were wondering how old the various 300 Lunars in Creation are, based on different possible percentages of effective Wyld Hunt kills-per-year. I realize some parameters are required to frame such a question, so I went with these. Not what I think Canon is, or trying to say it is. Just curious.

    1) Assume the Wyld Hunt kills three out of four Lunars in their first year Exalted.
    2) Assume any Lunar not killed by the Wyld Hunt by the time they've been Exalted five years, never will be.
    3) Assume 33% of Lunars will die before reaching the end of a mortal lifetime (100 years)
    Question: Beginning this casualty-cycle of assumptions with the Calibration Massacre, and continuing it to RY 768, can it be determined what the various ages of the Lunars in Creation might be?

    Edit: I realize some sort of % is required for likelihood of Lunars dying from Year 2 until Year 5, but not sure what the value should be. Pick something you consider reasonable, you're the one doing the math.

    Thanks in advance

      We are missing some information to solve this, but that's fine.

      I've read somewhere that the lunar host is now 400, but let's use the classic 300.

      Let's pull out 50 of the 300 right off the bat as 1st age survivors. Leviathan, Lilith, Ma-Ha-Suchi, Rakshi, and many more! Maybe many more, whatever. These 50 lunars will not be looked at all.

      Now, we have 250 lunars, that we need to run against a very nasty piece wise summation function, with 768 steps. Or, OR, we write a very fast C# program to knock that out for us.

      I'm pretty sure the time between the Calibration Massacre and the realm forming is pretty big. I found a number on a wiki that 750 years, so I did 750 + 768 for the total number of years this ran for.

      I assumed for for ages 2-6,a lunar had a 30% chance of dying, but after that, only a .3% yearly chance.




      Code:
       int firstAgeSurvivors = 50; int lunarHostMaxSize = 300; int startingLunarPopulation = lunarHostMaxSize - firstAgeSurvivors; int numberOfYearsToRun = 768 + 750; List<Lunar> lunars = new List<Lunar>(); for (int i = 0; i < startingLunarPopulation; ++i) { lunars.Add(new Lunar()); } for (int i = 0; i < numberOfYearsToRun; ++i) { for (int j = 0; j < lunars.Count; ++j) { if (lunars[j].age == 0) { //3/4 chance he never sees his first birthday if (Random.Range(0,4) == 0) { //HAPPY 1ST BIRTHDAY! ++lunars[j].age; } } else if (lunars[j].age < 6) { //30% chance a year for a lunar less than 6 years old to die to the hunt if (Random.Range(0,10) > 3) { //HAPPY BIRTHDAY, YOU SILVER ANATHEMA! ++lunars[j].age; } else { //May Lethe take your soul into it's loving embrace lunars[j].age = 0; } } else { //A lunar older than 6 years has .3% chance of dying a year. float v = Random.value; if (v < .003f) { //The bards will sing of your fall lunars[j].age = 0; } else { //HAPPY BIRTHDAY, YOU MOON BEAST ++lunars[j].age ; } } } }


      The program generates this data set: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets...it?usp=sharing

      Take away: A chunk of lunars are stuck at the bottom, about 60 of them. The rest of the host is a bit older. The average age is 291.384 and the median age is 176.5. This is all based on the assumptions you gave, canon or not.

          I think that for a subject such as this, it's better to try and develop an approach based around what image and circumstances you want to convey, rather than try to express vaguely defined setting elements in hard numbers and devise a calculation for it.

          For instance, I think that there are maybe only about a dozen Lunars that survive from the First Age, and that they're often isolated and weird and most of their fellows avoid them if possible. There's a hard core of something between one and two hundred Lunars that have been around since the Shogunate, and provide the main thrust of the Silver Pact. Of the ones that have only ever been around for the Realm Era, there are varying ages, but a slight trend towards ones that have been around for at least a couple of centuries, because to me that would emphasize both that it's kind of hard to kill a Lunar (if the Wyld Hunt is killing with some frequency, it's liable to be incarnations of the same Exaltations over and over again, occasionally managing to actually bring down an older one rather than merely destroy everything that they've built and forcing them to flee), and to not have the Silver Pact come across as a small number of older ones with a grudge surrounded by a large community of young dupes that are just ripe for being convinced to defy the system.

                  So I kind of agree with Isator here, but I want to add a more, in regards to versimillitude.


                  Now, while Exalted (and any RPG) exists to tell a cool story, and let you tell cool stories, we reject stories that seem ridiculous. So it is understandable to want to come up with something reasonable.

                  If your answer was "95% of Lunars get killed every year, so of 400 Lunars only 5 of them are over 1000, and basically all the rest have been exalted for 5 minutes" that would seem ridiculous. Similarly, if you said that 80% of Lunars are 1000 years old, that doesn't make sense because a)the Wyld Hunt would keep dying repeatedly and the Realm would have given up on it, b)shouldn't most of them have died in the Great Contagion or Balorian Crusade, which was nearly 800 years ago?

                  But between these two extremes... there's an absolute ton of variables.

                  Personally I'd probably say something like:
                  5-10 Lunars who are over 1500 (Essence 5-6); Raksi, Tammuz, Ma-Ha-Suchi, Lilith, Leviathan, and a handful of other First Age survivors if you want to put them in.
                  Another 20-ish between 768 and 1500 (Essence 4-5)
                  Another 50-ish who are 500-768 (Essence 3-5)
                  Another 100-ish who are 200-500 (Essence 3-4)
                  Another 100-ish who are 100-200 (Essence 3-4)
                  About 100 who are 15-100 years old (Essence 1-3)

                  But you could change those names massively, and it'd still make sense.
                  How many Lunars does the Wyld Hunt kill a year? 1%? 5%? Both are reasonable, but that's a massive difference with a huge knock-on effect on Lunar demographics. How many Lunars died in the Great Contagion+Balorian Crusade? 90%? 30%? Again, both are reasonable possibilities, but it's a colossal difference.

                  The issue with statistics and demographics, is that the smaller number of people you're talking about, the more swing you can get in statistics. The Realm could kill 10 Lunars one year, and 1 the next year. With only 400 people, the amount who die to random accidents, monster attacks, fighting other Exalts, etc, could vary a lot.

                  So, essentially those above numbers are numbers that make sense to me, but are also to my personal taste. I want the majority of Lunars to be significant and powerful in order to make it clear that Lunars have been, for the last 1500 years, the main opposition to the Realm, and that Anathema are terrifying and dangerous threats to 20,000 highly trained Exalted aristocrats armed with magical artefacts, despite their only being 400 of them (some of whom don't even bother fighting the Realm because they're doing other things).

                          Now, if we do take your assumptions, we can try to work out some numbers, but my point is you could change these numbers massively to fit your taste, and it'd still be fine for versimillitude.
                          (Also, I wouldn't assume that if they're not killed within 5 years, they never will be. Or that the Wyld Hunt's kill count within one year is that high. But these are the numbers you asked about, so we'll give it a try)
                          1) Assume the Wyld Hunt kills three out of four Lunars in their first year Exalted.
                          2) Assume any Lunar not killed by the Wyld Hunt by the time they've been Exalted five years, never will be.
                          3) Assume 33% of Lunars will die before reaching the end of a mortal lifetime (100 years)

                          I'm going to assume that there's 400 Lunars, and that the time lag between one Lunar dying and another Exalting isn't significant. A few weeks at most.

                          You just gave a death rate of 75% for the first year, and 0% for the 6th year (well, 33% between 6-100 years).
                          So, I'm going to go with falling death rates rather than 75% a year, since a Lunar who's been exalted 4 years is harder to kill than the first year.
                          So we'll use:
                          75% for the first year
                          60% for the second year
                          45% for the third year
                          30% for the fourth year
                          15% for the fifth year.

                          Overall, a Lunar therefore has a 0.032725% chance of surviving 5 years.
                          Thus, for 400 Lunars, 13 will survive 5 years.
                          But then of course, before 100, about 3 of those will die, leaving 10. 10 of 400 is 2.5%.
                          So, a Lunar has a 2.5% chance of surviving over 100 years.

                          You didn't give a death rate after 100 years, so I'll take it that your assumption is that the chance of dying after that is statistically irrelevant.

                          But we can't start with 400, because the Wyld Hunt didn't start with 400 brand new Lunars.

                          So, we need to work out when to start from: we can't start with 400 new Lunars because there was already plenty of older Lunars. Obviously a lot of Lunars died in the Usurpation, but plenty survived. A better starting point is the Great Contagion+Balorian Crusade. Now, 90% of humans died in the Great Contagion, but in the only rules we've had for the Great (not Great-ish Contagion), it killed less Exalts.
                          Specifically:
                          90% of Essence 1 Lunars
                          80% of Essence 2 Lunars
                          70% of Essence 3 Lunars
                          60% of Essence 4 Lunars
                          50% of Essence 5 Lunars
                          40% of Essence 6 Lunars.
                          Etc.

                          Since of course, the Wyld Hunt had been hunting Lunars throughout the Shogunate (and was more effective then than under the Realm), we'll just have to pick a number, and I'll say the mean Essence for Lunars was 3, so 70% of Lunars died from the Contagion.
                          Of the 30% remaining (who, according to the previous fluff, all rushed up to fight Raksha and tons of them died, but I'll be nice and reduce the number a lot), we'll say a third died (and also a ton of young Lunars who exalted a few months ago after tons of Lunars died in the Contagion, but they're under a year so not important).

                          So that gives you 80 older Lunars.
                          And 320 new Lunars.
                          (All the numbers below are rounded to the nearest integer)
                          Of those, 8 will survive over 100 years. We're assuming they're not going to die after that.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 2 RY, 8 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 3 RY, 8 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 4 RY, 8 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 5 RY, 7 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 6 RY, 7 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 7 RY, 7 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 8 RY, 7 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 9 RY, 6 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 10 RY, 6 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 11 RY, 6 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 12 RY, 6 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 13 RY, 6 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 14 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 15 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 16 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 17 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 18 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 19 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.
                          Of new Lunars exalted in 20 RY, 5 will survive for over 100 years.

                          And so on. Basically, the number of survivors slowly reduces (because they're coming from a smaller pool), but at a slower and slower rate.
                          But there's also not that many young Lunars left, because there's so many Elders.

                          As it's a 2.5% chance a year to survive, and the other 97.5% try again, and there's more and more Lunars getting into lower death likelihoods, by 20 RY, you've got over 200 Lunars who've survived more than 5 years and will survive a century.

                          So, by the time you get to 768RY, about 300 Lunars are 500+ years old, with ever-decreasing age brackets.

                          I'm not completely averse to that, but I suspect most people would prefer most Lunars to have been exalted for 50-150 years, not 500+.

                          I think probably you need to have a higher death rate for the first 100 years (say, 50%), and a substantial death rate after that (33% per 100 years maybe).

                              I want to thank Remulader , and The Wizard of Oz for the math. You two are great, the kind of people who are the argument for having forum discussions to begin with! Thank you, again
                              There seems to be some disagreement in the conclusions the two of you arrived at, but that makes the topic more interesting.

                              See, the underlying debate was: I took the position that, while the Wyld Hunt (during the Shogunate, and then later, once everything got back on track with the Empress/Realm) was brutally effective at hunting down and killing both the newly-Exalted Lunars, and likely many of the very young Lunars, any kill-rate that leaves survivors at ALL, when you're talking about beings who both a) Get much, MUCH harder to kill as they come into their power, and grow accustomed to being perma-fugitives with death warrants on their heads, and b) Have ridiculous natural life spans, the overall trend (over centuries) would be that the number of Lunars making it into the multi-centennial age-bracket is slowly, yet steadily, increasing. Paired with this was a supposition that many Wyld Hunt kills are actually cases of a small handful of Exaltations, each being rapidly recycled.

                              We actually got started on this during a discussion as to whether or not Bronze Sidereals would try to track the individual Lunar Exaltations, and maintain bookkeeping on the current fate of those Exaltations. From there, we got into debating whether or not the Sidereals would ever have made such bookkeeping available to the shikari. It's always been a bit of headcanon of mine that the Bronze Sidereals tasked with overseeing the Wyld Hunt (described as a punishment-detail in the Cult of the Illuminated book) might have made such bookkeeping available to the Shogunate-era Dragon Blooded, in the hopes that the shikari's true comprehension of the danger even one or two Anathema being missed would eventually pose. That they'd wrapped their bookkeeping of individual Lunar Exaltations up in specific demonic naming-conventions, rather than calling them Exaltations. Their hope had been to increase the zeal and relentlessness of the Wyld Hunt, but access to the data revealing the number of living Anathema was slowly increasing had the opposite effect, and the post-GC/Crusade reformation of the Wyld Hunt afforded the Bronze Sidereals the opportunity to remove such bookkeeping from Dragon Blooded memory. The few shikari who survived the GC/Crusade to join the reformed Wyld Hunt of the new Realm, retained a vague awareness of such bookkeeping, and this is where the notion of Anathema being specific possessing demons came from.

                              OTOH, my girlfriend took the position that, while it's true the Wyld Hunt is less and less effective against Lunars as they age, that (prior to the Empress's disappearance) this was where Chosen of Battles and Chosen of Endings came in, focusing on killing the young 6-50-ish Lunars unwise enough to leave the most distant edges of Creation, and that the large majority of Lunars were being kept in a sub-50years Exalted state by the 1-2 punch of the Wyld Hunt & Sidereal assassins.

                              I recognize the truth lies somewhere in the middle, but the debate grew lively, and well...

                              It stoked my curiosity however. Remulader 's data seems to suggest that, of the two of us, that my supposition the Wyld Hunt is rapidly recycling a smaller percentage of total Exaltations, and that this fact likely isn't readily apparent to the Wyld Hunt, could have some truth.

                              Modeling the pre-Empress-disappearance impact of Sidereal hunters on Lunars 6-100 would probably help.

                              The Wizard of Oz 's numbers are a possible explanation for where the Gerd Marrow-eater generation of 350-ish Lunars are coming from.

                              Isator Levi 's points are well-taken (and a good summation of where the age-groups are coming from). Drawing the balance as a campaign setting-point is of course for the individual Storyteller to decide, based on the needs of their game, but I see no harm in trying to figure out what the Silver Pact would look like without the artificial conceits required for this to be an RPG (Lots of char-gen power-level Exalts representative of PCs). The Silver Pact has always been pretty nebulous, once it gets away from talking about what the ancients are doing, and I think EX3's inclusion of Lunar Dominions/Caul, and a lot of other Lunar-related topics are going to make a clearer vision of the body of Lunars as a whole more important to the setting than they've thus far been in a big-picture-understanding kind of way.
