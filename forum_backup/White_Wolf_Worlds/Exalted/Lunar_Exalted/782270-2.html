        Sadly that is what they tried to do with the Thousand Rivers society building fluff, but botched the landing.

        The biggest issue with Lunars is that they are Celestial level exalts that can't be allowed to be productive. Consider that if they can change the course of history like any Celestial can, then Creation would not be needing the Solars to return to "fix things". If the Lunars can't make setting changes, then why are they Celestial grade exalts.

        I have found how badly this concept has hampered Lunars when I ran a game for some new players. They were new to RPGs and they were cutting their teeth on Exalted rather than something easier on a mechanics level. So I decided to simplify things by setting the game a few centuries prior to the Jade prison break. That way all they had to deal with was setting information, dragon-blooded and the occasional Wyld related issue. For the new players this format worked pretty well.

        The setting how ever started to unravel within 3 games due to smart players. The only thing that could keep the Lunars in check were sidereals and you can't roll those out every time. By the time the Jade prison broke, the Empire was in a shambles due to Lunar Predation. Things were almost to the level of cWoD where you have Vampires afraid to leave the city because the Werewolves rule the country side, except in this case the Terrestrials were afraid to leave major cities due to the Lunars and their allies. Interestingly enough this fit with the Lunar mentality of making humanity tougher through harsher treatment, but it still put the setting through a shredding machine.

        Fun times over all in my opinion, but in general too many STs are uncomfortable with PCs actually making changes to the setting that would alter things too much.

                                    I've been thinking about the nature of the Exalted in general, and it's got me considering some new things about Lunars.

                                    For instance... not all Exalted are function oriented the way Sidereals are, they're not brought into an actually institution and given a codified position with a mandate and a set of duties and a standard operating procedure, and powers that are tools to engage with those functions, but I would say that pretty much any Exalted are Chosen to fulfill something. For the Solars, it's not simply a matter of being powerful people who can use that power for whatever; it's about being a passionate, motivated person, the kind of people who are already inclined to pursue things that are grand, that bring changes, and being appointed to the role of a culture hero. The people who are Chosen as Solars are the people who, by their character or their ambitions, will protect and guide the world. They're not infallible in how they do this so they can make mistakes or have regrets or be criticized, and it's not necessarily something they'll do at all times, and their methods may not always be palatable to us (so you can have a Nobunaga figure who is motivated by things according to ending an extended period of warring states, which still entails burning down temples filled with civilians), but it's there in the DNA of what Solars do.

                                    That's the impression I get from looking back at an excerpt from a Grabowski outline, and I think it may be something they're subtly emphasizing throughout this Edition. Certainly, this corebook contains fairly overt reference to the idea of Solars as heroes.

                                    The way I've been thinking about it is that Solars are a lot like the Avatar from the show (particularly in how nobody seems to ever assume that being the most powerful person in the world might lead any given Avatar to try taking over everything).

                                    Anyway, the point is that even with Solars, you've got people who will be doing particular kinds of things, it's not just "you're powerful now, do as you please with it".

                                    So, on to Lunars.

                                    I already brought up the idea that Lunars are Chosen for character first and then develop their own functions later. The idea of Luna rewarding or resonating with a certain kind of person, a person who has broken past some kind of constraint or confinement*. It's a power that reflects the character and circumstance in which it was granted, but it's also wild and furious, and has needed to be bound to some purpose.

                                    In the First Age, this was based off of cleaving to the Solar Exalted, with Castes formed to the purpose of assisting them. We'll have to wait to see how this goes, but my assumption is that this would take a form of "Caste that complements Solar or covers their limitations or weaknesses" rather than "lesser version of Solar's Caste that is fit to be a sidekick". I think if one views Solars in terms of that culture hero role (which is something greater than the characteristics of their Charms, so you can have Melee Charms that are solely about being more powerful in fighting and still assume that this is always in the hands of somebody in the culture hero role), then I think that distinction alone can create a different picture of what Lunars are and were before Charms even enter into it (and so can more comfortably settle in to some Charms that are kind of... straightforward as being powerful at actions).

                                    Then you've got the Second Age, in which the Solars and the Realm that Lunars were bound to is gone, and the ruling power hates them. The role afforded to that wild, furious power is how to survive and fight back against the forces arrayed against it. The Lunar is more than simply a rebel**, although rebellion might be part of the role they've assumed in this Age.

                                    But then I've got to wonder what's on the cards for Lunars if they should destroy their enemies. It can't be to take up that guiding culture hero role; apart from Holden once clearly pointing out reasons for it not fitting their character, it just goes back in the direction of them coming across as doing Solar things in a paradigm where they can't really compete with Solars.

                                    Then I think about some statements in the core, some comments from the developer (mostly about Lunar pursuit of freedom and that idea of changing themselves rather than the world) and some of my own changing ideas about Exalted, aaannnddd...

                                    I have this idea where the thing Lunars are fighting for, and may even continue fighting the Solars for, is a world in which it's possible to be comfortably Casteless. To be rewarded by Luna for that act and chaacter of escape or defiance or non-conformity or survival and then just stop with that; to not need to have a role to hold it together. To be given power, and be able to use it for basically whatever they want (with an understanding that this will entail living lives of adventure and intrigue, using their rad powers to slice and dice their way to fame and fortune, more than arbitrarily setting people on fire for fun or something). I say "Casteless", but I mean that somewhat figuratively, since the description of the Castes in the core already entails a bit of the idea of them using their power for their own benefit; it's like, being a No Moon Caste Lunar doesn't necessarily mean solving mysteries as some indication of some greater cosmic function or fulfilling some role, or require them to do it in some particularly distinctive manner, it's just... the thing that you want, or the way of getting things that you want. On that note, it seems as though the vestiges of that idea of being powerful in a way that you are ultimately free is also something partially expressed by how the core presents the Lunars, in things such as references to ones outside the Silver Pact and one of the selling points given for them.

                                    So I think that's part of what a Lunar is; something on the way to being really the only Exalted who are for nothing but themselves (and that as a thing without judgement).

                                    * Incidentally, if I characterise Solars as people who can be turned into culture heroes, that alone can provide a sound, if subtle, difference between them and Lunars.
                                    ** Similar to the Solars, but being another point made in that Grabowski outline.

                                            Originally posted by CapitanTypo View Post
                                            I forget which edition it was said that Lunars were created to be the consorts of the Solars, So what exactly was the purpose of the shapeshifting, you might ask?

                                            I always found this explanation a bit sad because it means that in a solar/lunar marriage there IS a hierarchy, and it's by design.
                                            First Edition Exalted was supposed to be the secret prehistory of the cWoD line back when Exalted was first being launched. Thus each exalt type reflected one of the Main cWoD lines at the time. The Lunars were the analogs to the Werewolf: The Apocalypse werewolves and other shifter breeds. Strangely enough the Solars were the Hunter analog and the Dragon-Blooded were connected to the Kuei-Jin, while the Sidereals were the mages and the abyssal were the cainite vampires.

                                            The sun and moon consort concept was a staple the myths and legends that Exalted was drew upon for that old school legendary feel. Thus the source materials they drew upon had such hierarchy in place to begin with before the game designers ever got their hands on it.

                                            So many of the original design concepts make sense when viewed from the perspective of being connected to old world of darkness lines or draw direct inspiration from things like the labors of Hercules or the adventures of Enkidu & Gilgamesh. But once they started moving away from those sources and delving more into distancing Exalted as a stand alone line and gaining more and more inspiration from things like anime, the original designs made less and less sense, but you had to keep them to maintain a degree of consistency between editions.

                                                    Lemme preface this by saying that I'm grateful to be a part of such an invested community; this thread has given me a lot of ideas for my lunar games, and along with Irked's conversion, has helped me ensure that Lunars will get to play a neat role in my current game, which has otherwise focused exclusively on Solars and the Dragonblooded (with some Abyssals thrown in, but the Solars don't know that yet).

                                                    Lunars.

                                                    I only started getting really into Exalted around about a year or so ago, well into the timeframe within which Morke and Holden were writing long posts about the issues they've had in past editions, and a little bit about their vision for their place in the setting moving forward. So, I'm approaching this subject with only a faint idea, for example, of how Ma-Ha-Suchi was depicted in 2E, which I take to be bad somehow.

                                                    To me, Lunars in and of themselves conjure an awesome collection of images. The strength of an enormous man-beast hybrid, rippling and surging with muscle. Razor-keen senses and instincts. A thousand faces in a thousand places. The wealth of imagery and themes of the animal world tied to ancient cunning and centuries-long foresight. I find animals pretty mind-boggling as it is (blame my biology training), and the thought of adding Essence to the already insane capabilities incipient in the natural world is exciting. Wise shamans, peerless hunters, slippery tricksters. Physicality.

                                                    Then there's how they fit into Creation as a whole. I've personally always enjoyed the idea of them being the largest, most effective spy and guerilla force in the history of Creation. Powerful individually; in some cases, such as Raksi, Ma-Ha-Suchi, and Sha'a Oka even powerful enough to wrest and keep territory, but generally outmatched by both numbers and inherited technological capability. Living out the the dynamic tension between surviving the Realm and defeating the Realm while wrestling with an ancient, terrible bond. I can understand why some would hate to play second fiddle to Solars/Abyssals/Infernals, but I've never really been bugged by this because I think it opens up a lot of neat stories involving re-brokering these power dynamics in the forging of a new age. Or hell, even going with it, and playing a loyal, die-hard partner to an unstoppable, occasionally power-mad Solar. Or redeeming a nihilistic Abyssal (or joining them, as a Lunar of Death!). And if you want to hold onto having a loyal Lunar mate as a Solar (something which I think people generally agree is awesome), that dynamic is going to have to stay a part of the setting in Lunar games.

                                                    If you want to play an unstoppable general or an irresistible orator, Solars already provide that role, and I think it's unfair to critique Lunars for not being that (which is a subtext that I've seen come up a number of times when people complain about them). In terms of raw power, while they may be behind Solars and associated brands on average, any military commander or historian will tell you that adaptability and flexibility, the hallmarks of Lunars, outweigh raw strength as often as not. Hell, it takes the combined strength of the Dragonblooded empire -and- the Sidereals to maintain a delicate stalemate, and even that with several large Lunar dominions still in place. I don't get the impression from their write-ups in this edition at least that they've been sitting on their hands/tentacles/claws, waiting for the Solars to show up. I get the impression that they've been the single biggest problem for the most powerful people in the world for centuries.
