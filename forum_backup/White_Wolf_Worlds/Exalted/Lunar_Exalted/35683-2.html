    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    Perhaps that's the relevance of the "Lunars' association with animals is Gaia" line; that, through some mythic qualities deriving from their god, there's a certain connection to Gaia there, but that's about all it is, so it manifests in assuming an animal form, and not much else, so they can otherwise just get on with being Lunars.
    It may be that I'm misreading your intent. We actually do have indications that the EX3 spirit shape is going to be more than just an animal form that a Lunar can become. It may even have a big impact on how easily a particular Lunar can access appropriate parts of the Charmset, the last word we had (spoilered for space) :
    Originally posted by John Mørke
    It is still the animal you choose to be the basis for your DBT. How much I force it into your character's appearance remains to be seen. If you don't want to be a DBT Lunar, I'd rather you think of DBT / spirit shape as the tool you relied on when your Lunar was still young, inexperienced, and less powerful, rather than ignoring it entirely. I want to help people build away from a DBT-based character through powers that work when you are in your human form and don't work when you are in DBT.

    Once you reach that point, it is my intention that the spirit shape, through a clever use of keywords and associations, provide you a favored discount on Charms whose Attributes you don't favor, going so far as to change the Charm's prerequisites and shift it to a logical place in your current Charm trees (if one exists). So even if you ignore your spirit shape in roleplay, its themes are there helping you by providing a framework to power up your character.

    I also realize that one of the reasons people search so furiously for meaning beyond DBT is because a single animal is so constraining, so I am looking at completely redoing Chimeraism so that it is an accepted state, so that players can make cool-looking hybrid monsters with lots of different traits.

    Note that everything I've said here is "Maybe" not "I promise" and is subject to change. This is about as far as I am willing to reach into my giant vault of future noise to show people, but I feel like the general tone of acceptance and readiness for big new ideas has reached a level where such a classified view won't confuse and upset anyone.

    My hope is for mythic associations and symbolism that are actively helpful, as more of an option for Lunars than they've had. That's about all I've got. I certainly don't want it crowding out other approaches to spirit shapes that other people enjoy more.

    Honestly, in the talk of "clever use of keywords and associations," my mind conjured up something like, "Got an animal you like for your Lunar? Cool. Now, pick X of these keywords that suit your spirit shape. Go with whatever ways feel right to you: symbolism, mythic associations, scientific properties, whatever. These keywords yadda yadda yadda." In a setup vaguely like that, mythic/symbolic influences from the spirit shape could get just as much mechanical reinforcement as the player wished, just like any other considered factors.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            You know, that other thread, the Lunar Picture one, that actually started me thinking "is it possible that getting all caught up on the symbolic or mythic associations of the native animal form of every Lunar is just a big distraction and frustration, and that they really don't need to go much beyond a fairly neat animal".
            ... [cut for focus] ...
            It feels so much more straightforward to just pick an animal and go with it.
            ... [cut for focus] ...
            Perhaps that's the relevance of the "Lunars' association with animals is Gaia" line; that, through some mythic qualities deriving from their god, there's a certain connection to Gaia there, but that's about all it is, so it manifests in assuming an animal form, and not much else, so they can otherwise just get on with being Lunars.
            The problem here, though, is that the Spirit Shape / Totem is repeatedly called out as being important to the character. Just as I've known people who will obsess over the tiniest details of their character's appearance, I myself would go insane trying to come up with a suitable animal for any Lunar I create. When you present a player with a permanent decision, and tell them it will serve as a defining point for their character, it is only natural that some players will want something that they feel they can grow into or connect with in a way that defies "pick an animal and go with it."

            And that is before you get into the mechanical and story effects of the Spirit Shape. It defines your Tell, it initially defines your Warform and starting mutation set, and (perhaps most importantly for some of my players), it is the ONLY form you can permanently acquire without killing or boning.

            Now, if you were suggesting that 3e Lunars might benefit from a decreased emphasis on Spirit Shape, I concede that could resolve some concerns, but I feel it would also take away something that has been a major defining feature of the Lunar Exalted.

            Personally, after playing Alchemicals of late, I've come to appreciate their "State-Designated Legend" as a touchstone for guiding the character and their growth, without being locked into anything.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            That was what I was thinking before I looked back in on this thread, which reiterated to me the idea of "welp, some people still want Lunars to be Silver Infernals. Specifically the Second Edition type, specifically in terms of 'let's figure out all kinds of details that make Lunars entirely incompatible with one another in a manner that would surely make a book a nightmare to write'".
            I can certainly empathize with this sentiment, but I don't think this concept of highly-symbolic Spirit Shapes MUST go the route of Infernals, Shintai, and Devil Tigers. The walrus example, for instance, could just be represented by a basic set of mutations (3e will have them as Merits, I believe), Stamina/Wits Charms, decent Willpower, and good roleplaying to bring out the personality the Spirit Shape suggests. Maybe, farther along the way, once there are no more suitable Lunar core Charms, THEN the player will work with their ST to make custom Charms, but by that point I'd imagine any splat other than Sidereals will be looking to do the same.

            BUT (and I stress this because I suspect it is the root cause of the Silver-Solar / Silver-Sidereal / Silver-Infernal labeling), we do not currently have a cohesive set of core Lunar Charms, and so Lunar players who desire to do anything besides combat will often turn to custom material, which will naturally borrow from other splats' mechanical styles. I expect (and hope!) 3rd Edition will alleviate this need.

                        Its silly to talk about she-hyena penises when we are having a serious talk about Lunars. If you want to be taken seriously as a splat, to distance oneself from the feral' wipe with pine cones' view, this is exactly the thing that makes people believe that's what Lunars are. That Lunar players are contented with those themes.

                        The Spirit Shape, from rumors of 3E, is a font of your identity. Not only informing upon parts of your behavior and mores but also establishing your paths to power. For the Lunar it is more clear and important than the 2E concept of an epic motivation, it is what you are, aspects of you soul and being given form and substance. It's deep stuff, but I understand its not a train everyone's gonna want to ride.

                        Unlike an epic motivation to restructure the Realm or create a Northern Peace Summit as a checks and balances to the rebirth of god-kings (Solar and Lunar//Yurgan and Gerd Marrow-Eater) - someone like 'Himanada, He that Grinds the World' has a guiding principle that takes him far beyond the establishment of such a peace and even works on the micro as well as macro scale. Head into a farm village and see them hauling water up from a river, boom, your into fixing that with a well or centralized cistern. Perhaps you rail against the Silver Pact and their hide-bound rules to well...bind your hide. It provides endless stories and is a guiding light. That is something we all need in the early game, when we are not familiar with our character or their beliefs/behaviors, as well as late game when the scope of Creation, and the awesome power we wield, threatens to blur our focus and scatter our concepts.

                                Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                Personally, I favor symbolism and mythic resonance of an animal before anything else.* The physical traits are cool, too, but the associations have always been my favorite part, whether playing them straight or playing against type. I'd suggest looking up animal symbolism or reading a lot of animal fables/stories. Human cultures have created no shortage of both. Whether starting with a human character and then finding an appropriate animal, or starting with an animal and then working back to the sort of character to receive such a shape, I think it works quite well.

                                * Tangent: I'm really hopeful that this winds up playing more of a role in EX3's execution; in 2e spirit shapes, physical traits were the only ones backed up mechanically. I'd be thrilled if animals even without much direct utility and/or with outclassed stats, but flush with symbolic meaning, still felt mechanically useful as a spirit shape to a Lunar. The idea hatewheel contemplated about choice of spirit shape letting one get easier access to and/or shift around Charm trees sounded like it could really work with that. I'd find it very cool if you could let mechanics back you up when selecting between, say, a raven, a dove, a mockingbird, and a sparrow.
                                There are already some mechanics out there that can support these concepts to a degree, granted with limited knowledge of the new mechanics and time, these are just ideas thrown at a wall.

                                In Changling you have effectively a Fey spirit shape, the Ice Queen and so on, which only becomes more complex as you adopt a court and noble order. These are meant to be marks upon your spirit that change you and inform upon your perspective in the world. They are all great sign posts that could be adopted for our own spirit shapes - granted using different methods that resonate better with perhaps the Werewolf line or Book of Spirits.

                                Only because I recently checked it out and Numenera is on my mind as well - they have multi-threaded descriptors
                                "I am an adjective noun who verbs."
                                Which is simple and helps to inform what kind of No Moon or Elk Totem Lunar that you are. Granted, I feel its a bit simplistic the way they carry things - running it like multi-classing. But, the base concept, still useful

                                When one looks at legends of the Goldfish:

                                It came from the crucian carp - selectively breed by the ruling dynasty to bring about the golden hue into the population when it was simply a mutation. Gold or Yellow being the same in Chinese culture as the royal purple in the West. The fish took on symbology of wealth, bounty and surplus and the gift of one well wishes for those same things. If China had founded the United States it is quite possible that the gold fish would be our emblem rather than the bald eagle - as the goldfish is the American Dream:
                                goldfish represent the notion that it is possible for anybody to achieve whatever they want
                                That tells so many stories - hopes and dreams for the downtrodden of Creation.

                                At the same time we have Jōzu, the Whopper, who is said to have eaten the moon and captured the goddess the moon represents (Yumigami). This Spirit represents hunger in any or all of its forms. A strive to consume or become more or to devour foes or to absorb nations or peoples under its cult.

                                With the countless myths built upon animals it is really up to the player to connect to, or even build, a legend for their spirit shape and give it meaning. That there is, proposedly, a pool of mechanical benefits to connect to totems, in a free form manner, that would be a wonderful solution. Between whats been said about the use of keywords applying to Totems and the freeform nature of Exigents, I think the future is bright.

                                The biggest issue is the investment of time and energy involved - how much does a player want to devote to establishing their totem. Currently many Lunar players wait till they grow into the character to establish a totem. Others have issues settling and may even change their totem over time. When you're going to spend hundreds, if not thousands, of hours with his character - giving your totem some though/love is well worth it. If mechanical scaffolding is in place to guide - most excellent.

                                    I suppose it all comes down to player preference for flavor details, then. In a related example, I've heard many complain about how "All Daiklaives are the same," making the incredibly rare, indestructible swords rather trite or rote when they do appear. The answer? Give them names and unique histories, and then, if this allows, expand on this with unique abilities. The same goes for Lunar spirit shapes. True, if you "just" have a fox or frog or whatever totem, then you'll not be penalized in any way, but it makes your character all the richer for having that extra description, of not only what that Lunar can do, but also themselves as a person.

                                    This may be my fondness for GSPs showing, though, as the idea of having a history for one of the component aspects of what makes your character, a bestial, mysterious shapeshifter godling, just seems to click, even if it never occurred for me prior to this conversation. In a way, its this narrative freedom that Exalted allows, that attracted me to the game in the first place; where a starting PC can radically alter the setting with some effort, as opposed to the movers and shakers all being crazy old man cosmic wizard lords who are also teenage girls (TM).

                                                    2e was a disaster for spirit shapes, because linking them to mutations -- while clever and well-intentioned -- meant that the way to get the most out of your shape was to reduce all potential shapes into "what mutations can I get from this"? Aside from leading to a disproportionate number of wacky Lunars, this took them in the opposite direction from the monsters they were intended to emulate. "Monster people" and "beast people" lean heavily on the animals with the most cultural purchase, which is to say that they skew heavily towards animals that resemble humans -- overwhelmingly they're fair-sized vertebrates who have a major role in human life, and the bulk are mammalian and cat-sized or larger. The problem with these animals is that they're the ones most likely to have "superpowers" that humans get for free, like being warm-blooded and intelligent, so they're poor mutation packages. I liked the way 2e Lunars handled spirit forms at first, but in retrospect, a system that discourages Lunar players from taking a wolf as their spirit shape is kind of fucked up.

                                                    Ignoring all that, though, what animals are the most thematic and (to use the devs' term) mythically resonant? You can obviously look at actual mythology and popular metaphors here -- animals have always been a safe outlet for racial archetypes, e.g. the deceitful snake, the ferocious wolf, the stubborn goat. The academic term for this is "zoosemy" and if you search on that you can find more detailed studies of the practice as it relates to specific places, time periods, and animals. If you just want to build a "top ten" list without digging through world myths, look at popular culture, especially furries and animal otherkin, because the prevalence of animals in those areas reflects their dominance in the human psyche. Because there's such a strong and unbroken line of animal metaphor throughout history, I don't think our modern conceptions have changed all that much.
