            I like lists. Therefore, some of my favorite totems:

            1. Tigers. Feline base-forms are pretty standard...but so COOL when you add stuff on. Winged tiger? Fire-breathing tiger with golden scales? Super-long serpentine tiger? Tiger with paralyzing venom? Chitinous Geiger-style Alien tiger? Giant city-stomping tiger? Tiger with poisoned quills it can fire at range? Ghost-tiger that walks through walls? Tiger is a great template to add neat stuff onto, and it always looks cool because tigers are really pretty. This is true of many of the generic spirits shapes. You can make 'em really awesome by building your own legend. A phantom tiger who walks silently through your walls and stands over your bed and drinks your breath as she kills you with nightmares about being hunted through the jungle is a completely different character than a tiger literally burning bright who drips molten metal and tears into Raksha armies with glowing iron claws...yeah. Tigers, awesome. About covered that, I guess. Lions are neat too, I guess?

            2. Insects/Bugs/Small Arthropods/Invertebrates. Bombardier beetle Lunar saboteurs, Stag Beetle Full Moon seducers/seductresses with Twin-Faced Hero, Wasp-totem lunars with orderly armies of insect-children, cunning spider-totem planners, badass socialites with parasite totems, mantises who know Mantis, armor-plated poison-weilding scorpion warriors...whatever weird and freaky concept you wanna go with, there's a bug who is ALL ABOUT that one thing. Popular bugs often have mythic associations (biblical locusts, Anansi stories, Egyptian dung beetle myths, etc), but feel free to make up your own mythic stories and associations for your bug o' choice based on its real-world attributes. One of my recent favorite characters (a Nobilis character known as the Mosquito Queen, based on that legend about how all the animals wanted to team up and wipe humanity out but the mosquito talked them out of it) would make a great Lunar.

            3. Frogs! Poisoned skin, super-jumps, sticky tongue-whip, sweet tree-climbin' abilities, can breathe underwater, known for singing songs of seduction and working at night, occasionally for eating things that look like they should be way too big for it to eat. Lots of mythic/magical associations, lots of neat powers, and different frogs offer a dazzling array of personality types: a Lunar based around a Golden Dart Frog totem might be a venom-packing socialite who struts his stuff like a peacock and just DARES his enemies to go after him, while a less flamboyant tree frog would make a great second-story man or stealthy thief/ninja type. Frog totem full moons might be aquatic ambush warriors or overly muscled terrors who leap hundreds of feet over their enemies and snatch them up into the air with a ridiculously long tongue. Frogs associate well with wisdom and shamanic imagery (they eat their own skin every day!), and you can probably stretch the "I'm an amphibian" far enough to justify supernatural regenerative powers to your ST even if frogs don't usually have those. They're a surprisingly good broad and adaptable sorta totem.

            4. Giant Lumbering Sea-Beats: maybe it's just me, but I love whale and walrus and seal and shark-totem lunars. Their totem form is only really useful at sea, and I really like that limitation. It adds a sort of "my place of power" feel which I like. I like when Great White Shark Lunar is injured and on the run from the Wyld Hunt and can lure them out onto a ship to chase him and then just smile a super-toothy smile and say "now it's my turn" before taking advantage of his terrible ship-smashing super-mobile aquatic spirit shape. Sometimes it's really fun to try and lure your enemies into your arena, and to work with limits outside that arena. Sea-beasts are great for this because they're generally large and possessed of a great deal of stamina and symbolic staying power. Cultures all over the world have been making legends and myth out of sea-beasts forever, so themes are easy. Essentially, you get all the fun of being a Tyrant Lizard Godzilla, but it doesn't get boring because you don't turn into Tyrant Lizard Godzilla every session: you're only going into your terrifying Kaiju-form when the game takes the battle to the water. Also, at some point you're gonna get dropped out of an airship and turn into a whale to land on somebody and their army, because trust me, the rest of your Circle is trying to think of ways to abuse you for fun and profit.

            5. Deep Sea Life: These are best for creepy characters, and I love me some creepy characters. Angler fish make great face-stealing social serial killers, giant squid and deep-water sharks make for interestingly flavored warriors or scholars, the Giant Marine Isopod is the most terrifying Deadly Beastman on the planet, and there's no character who isn't cooler if he can glow pretty colors to accentuate his coolness. I like Lunars as monsters and nightmares, and deep sea fish have huge eyes and giant teeth and alien forms. I want my lunar to distend his jaws and stomach to suddenly snap up and swallow a struggling enemy whole, or to use the themes of a deep-sea jellyfish dangling poisoned strands invisibly over large areas to ensnare anything that drifts into it (like spider-totem lunars only weirder). I love these especially for No Moons, because deep-sea shamans and sea-witches are some of my favorite ancient and dark magic images. You can go Lovecraftian here in a cool fey black-water-under-the-moon sorta way.

            So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                    Honestly, I find there are so many options for spirit shapes that my challenge is narrowing it down. Of course, I make a living studying animals, so I probably have a bit of a bias. Sources:
                    • Walker's Mammals of the World (2 Volumes) - usually you can find it in your local library
                    • Handbook of the Birds of the World (16 Volumes) - usually available in a good library...may need to hit your university.
                    • The Reptile Database (http://www.reptile-database.org/) - for some reason, the reptile guys actually moved online early
                    • Amphibian Database (http://www.amnh.org/our-research/ver...world-database) - few pictures, but cool details
                    • Fishes of the World - often in a good library...also in likely to need a university or big science section
                    I assume there are similar references for inverts, but I don't really do much with them. So that is the taxonomic side. The other way is to mine folktales, not just myth - there are some odd little references tucked away in folktales, including in some kids books.
                    Anyway, hope those help!

                                                        That's something I've considered for some time, especially with the Lunar I'm employing in Noodle's PbP game here; how to properly convey the idea of a "Perfectly melding between human and totem forms" of the warform without devolving to " Upright animal with scary face" or " scary person with animal bits." The official art seems torn on this, too, with many going the former (Strength of Many, Tammuz, and of course, Ma-Ha-Suchi), while only a few go the other way (Anja Silverclaws, and to a lesser extent, Seven Devils Clever). While the former approach seems fine for those who wish to express their raw physical might, it doesn't gel well, at least to me, to those Lunars who get by on their looks, suavity, or wits. What amount of "Animal" traits is enough to convey the use of DBT, without making it just an excuse for catgirls?

                                                        Actually, that's something of a roadblock I've had when I've tried to explain how cool Lunars are to newcomers to the game. They either see them as catgirl planeteers or furry vikings. The worst bit is I try to refute their claims, and then the Wardens of Gaia rear their heads, and I have to concede their points. Is it really that bad to play an urbane Lunar without being ostracized by many of your peers for not being "HURR, CIVILIZATION WEAK, BARBARIAN STRONK?"

                                                        Finally, I've noticed a lot of love for insect totems here. If you're not familiar with it, Terra Formars may be something you'd be interested in looking into; a manga about astronaut soldiers spliced with bug genes fighting cockroach cavemen on Mars. Schlocky? Totally. Amusing as a battle comic? Incredibly.
