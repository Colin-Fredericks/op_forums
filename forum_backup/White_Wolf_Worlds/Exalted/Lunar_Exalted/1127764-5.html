                        So, extremely able, powerful people may well rise to power, but I haven't really seen (unironic) characters fight to be ruler and genuinely believe "I'm a Solar so I should be in charge." It's not like becoming a Solar comes with a golden plaque saying "The Unconquered Sun, King of the Gods, says you should be King of Creation."

                        Now, I've seen plenty of DBs who think they should rule because they're DBs, but then, they've been raised their whole lives in a religion that preaches that.

                        Although, as I said, I have seen people talk about the Solars' right to rule, but it's not something the player's invested in, it's something they consider... ironic? Like the way we play DBs and say "stupid savages, why are they complaing about being ruled by their moral betters, the Dragonblood? Now where are my hookers and blow." Similarly, Solars saying "everyone should obey us Solars, because we're so morally righteous and sent by the Gods, and we'll kill anyone who disagrees."


                        But... I'm not really seeing how this applies to Lunars?
                        I guess the thing about Lunars is that they don't actually (unlike some other splats) have any ideological belief in their own right to rule. I mean, they may take over societies because they need them to fight the Realm, they may take over societies out of greed or a sense of public duty, and they may take over societies so DBs can't, because f*ck those guys. But young Lunars are not told "as a Lunar, it's your right and duty to rule over mortals" the way DBs are (and some people seem to think Solars are).
                        Which is perhaps why my players who dislike the returning god-king thing generally like Lunars.
                        The Solars get to be god-kings because they're going to take leadership roles one way or another. That's basically it, and it's not a very good justification. Heck, my current dawn is actually high priestess to Zelos, god of strength. She's subordinate to him (kinda - she's not really very good at her job)

                        I'd argue that the Solars lost their "right to rule by divine mandate" when Sol turned his face away from them, if anything.
