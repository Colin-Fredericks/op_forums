    On Lunar Factions in 3rd ed

    02-09-2015, 12:38 AM
    In 2nd ed, most splats had different factions. The Realm had their houses, their internicine intrigue and byzantine backstabbing. The Sidereals had their Gold and Bronze factions, and the independents. The Abyssals had the Deathlords, who might fight each other.
    This allowed you to do both your splat vs the outside, or your splat vs your splat, or your splat vs the world while infighting at the same time. It also gave you a bit of inspiration, especially the Dragonblooded Houses (in my experience, most dynast concepts can be 90% summed-up with "I'm playing a House X, Y-aspected guy/girl who does job Z" which makes it easy for people to think of characters).

    The Lunar factions did not do this. Frankly, I don't even know what the point of the Lunar factions. I suspect just because everyone else had them? They can't really come into conflict, because they just don't really have any significant differences.

    They had:
    The Wardens of Gaia: all Lunars want to safeguard Gaia, but we care about it slightly more than other Lunars!
    The Swords of Luna: all Lunars believe in defending Creation from the Wyld, but we care about it slightly more than other Lunars!
    The Winding Path: all Lunars, as per 2nd ed, are committed to the Thousand Streams River, but we care about it slightly more than other Lunars!
    The Crossroads Society: We are sorcerers. We do sorcery. What's our political goal? Uh, more sorcery for us?

    Then there's the Sun King Seneschals. Now, partly, they are the "all Lunars want to defeat the Dragonblooded, but we care about it slightly more than other Lunars!" faction. But they are also the "we've spent a thousand years training all young Lunars to be perfect mates to people who are all dead and are never, ever coming back". It seemed a lot like the writers forgot that most Lunars had never seen a Solar or thought they ever would.
    Saying this, as badly written as they are, they're still the best faction because they might actually disagree with other factions, and there's a few different characters you can get out of them (guy who wants to put the Solars in charge, girl who wants to control the Solars gold-faction style, dude who found the Solars useful as a rallying cry and ideal but finds them really existing extremely annoying, girl who joined to enforce the Creation-Ruling Mandate and protect humanity from rogue Gods and gets really annoyed how suddenly the pro-Solar politics that was irrelevant before is a big thing, guy who joined to fight the Realm and thought Solars were awesome but finds them really disappointing in practice). Whereas being a Sword of Luna is no different to saying "my guy doesn't like Raksha" so it's not really a political faction at all, just a point of view.

    I don't know about other people, but I've never really seen any of the factions come up in games at all. The only time I've seen them ever come up was when I had the Sun King Seneschals offer to "train" (ie brainwash) the Solar PCs in my game (who turned them down).
    When I played a pure Lunar game, I don't remember any of the factions ever being mentioned at all.

    So, what do you think Lunar factions should be like in 3rd ed?
    Do you think I'm wrong and the factions are fine?
    Do you think the basic ideas are fine and they just need to be written better?
    Do you want to see new factions, and what ideas do you have for them?
    Do you think Lunars should just have regional power-blocs (ie factions like this pack of Lunars with this plan for the south-east and if you don't get on side they'll push you out of the region)?
    Do you think Lunars should have absolutely no politics whatsoever?

                          The Crossroads Society didn't really need a political goal. They were a sorcerous society. To the extent that they were political, it was along the lines of "sorcery is an esoteric, specialized art that requires a lot more training and instruction than learning our native Charmset on your own does. As it is in the best interests of Lunar's everywhere to continue to have access to this powerful tool, we're going to club together and share knowledge, notes, and take responsibility for training up the next generation of Lunar sorcerers."

                          And you know what, that makes sense as a faction. It works. It doesn't really have a grand goal, but not every faction needs that.

                          The other 2e factions are definitely shit, though. I mean... I kind of, sort of see what they were going for with Swords of Luna and Sun-King Seneschals; the first was "we want an organization for Lunars who are going to completely dedicate themselves to fighting the Wyld" and the other was "we want an organization that's going to be available for Solars to seek out or for Lunars who want to seek out Solars and work with them." Neither really... quite... worked. And the Wardens of Gaia and especially the Winding Path are just dumb.

                          I do take issue with your characterization of the Sun-King Seneschals, tho. Their agenda wasn't "training all young Lunars to be perfect mates to people who are all dead and are never, ever coming back." First of all, the Solars did come back. Second of all, hitching your wagon to the star of the Solars isn't exactly a dumb move. There was nothing subservient about it, tho. I mean, these are people who were looking to Lilith for leadership, and Lilith still hasn't made up her mind if she's going to slowly torture Swan to death or not.

                          No, the dumb part of the Sun-King Seneschals was that it was a faction larger than a few Elder Lunars and some of the people they've personally trained. After a millennium, you kinda gotta accept that the Solars probably aren't returning.

                          If I were going to re-do them, because "faction of the Lunars that wants to ally with Solars" makes sense, they would be a young, hungry faction that only just emerged; there should be very few SKS's who have been members of the faction for longer than three, four years, because it only became a politically viable stance once the Solars got back. This should be causing fault lines in Lunar Society, because some of the Elders who have managed to co-exist for years peacefully are suddenly now split between "Yay, they're back! Time to team up and kick ass" and "Do you not remember the sickening monsters these people were?" and points in-between, with the younger Lunar's not really getting the weird millennia-old grudges and damage at work.

                          The more I think about it, the more it seems to me this should definitely be a political faction of the Lunars with real numbers and clout. Just... done better the SKS's were. Because those guys were kinda lame, but the idea underlying them? Sound.

                                          I imagine in 3rd edition, Lunar Exalted will generally be divided into one of two groups - those who are actively engaged in war with the Realm and those who are not. You could probably consider the first to be the "Anti-Realm" faction, which would likely be the only thing you could consider a true "Lunar faction." From there, you'll have smaller subgroups devoted to different things.

                                          These subgroups will probably revolve around a powerful/influential Lunar - Raksi may have a group of Lunars who help her out, or assist her, in exchange for magical training.

                                          That's not to say that Lunar society will revolve around ancient, First Age Lunars. Rather, any Lunar that gets famous or well respected could end up with a group of other Lunars tagging along and assisting or following him, and you could easily end up with a situation where a group of older Lunars end up following a young Lunar who just Exalted a few years ago, if that young Lunar has good plans, ideas, gets lucky, has useful contacts or whatever.

                                          I imagine that Lunar society would tend to praise success and action than simple age - so someone like Lilith or Leviathan may be given a modicum of respect for their ancient prowess, but realistically none of the Lunars are going to give a fuck about them because they're not out there doing anything. Meanwhile some young Lunar who Exalted 5 years ago and successfully drove the Realm out of a tributary would probably have a lot of respect and possibly other Lunars flocking to his banner.

                                          I would point towards something like Robin Hood as an example of how I imagine most Lunar "societies" might function - Robin Hood started acting as a bandit/fighting the king, for his own personal reasons, got pretty famous, and ended up attracting other people to his cause because they liked what he was doing. Ultimately, this is how I tend to imagine Lunar factions/society will be like - largely informal gatherings of people who follow a charismatic figure or ideal and work together, at least for a time.

                                          If one Lunar is successful at doing something, then other Lunars who share similar interests will probably take an interest in what he's doing.

                                          As for actual "factions" you probably won't see those, but there may be some things that resemble them...

                                          For example, you may have Lunars who spend their time traveling through the Wyld, the Underworld and Malfeas, exploring and understanding those realms. And other Lunars call these people, "Nomads" though these Lunars don't consider themselves any sort of united group (they're just individuals who like to travel to other worlds) and sometimes they get together and swap stories and tell each other what they've seen in their travels, and once in awhile some of them get together and travel around because they want company. But not really a "faction," so much as a shorthand label for Lunars who heavily engage in one type of activity.

                                          Another one might be "Infiltrators" which would be a label for those crazy Lunars who sneak into Realm bastions, disguise themselves as Realm citizens and spend months or years "behind enemy lines" acquiring information or sabotage things. Sometimes they might work together, but most times they would just be individuals who happen to share a similar MO. It doesn't hurt for them to talk about things, but this group would be more of a fraternity than an actual organization or faction.

                                          And of course your "Crossroads Society" which is based around Lunars interested in magic, who sometimes swap information and facts.

                                          At the end the term "faction" implies a certain amount of organization, and more importantly, the idea of a shared, collective goal. I don't think any of these groups would have that sort of thing - it would just be a label for individuals who happen to share interests. When Lunars share a goal with another Lunar, they would probably end up choosing to follow or work with that particular Lunar (like the Robin Hood example). So again, the only real "faction" you would probably see is the one composed of the Lunars who are working towards the destruction of the Realm.

                                                      It's difficult to try to reorganize the Lunars into factions when we know their splat is going to be seriously reworked and reorganized and probably provided with 1-3 overarching goals in a way they didn't quite have before. Most likely 'fight the Realm!' and 'fight the Wyld!' are going to be among those goals, but it's not clear precisely why those will be the goals (other than pure vengeance of course).

                                                      Factionalism will be,as others have noted, inherently limited by the high level of dispersion the Lunars seem to be stuck with. Sidereals are concentrated in Yu Shan and have always had superior travel tricks, something they will presumably retain, making it easier for them to function as a cohesive group. Dragon-blooded are concentrated in the Realm, Lookshy, and any place where they happen to be able to build up a lineage (something Lunars can't really do, beastmen are not in the same league as more exalts).

                                                      Regionalized factions makes sense to me. The Abyssals and the Lunars share a common 'exalt suddenly, make crazy run to safe space' origin scenario, and the abyssal developed a regionalized structure based on the various deathlords (their safe space) they ended up working for. Lunars may end up in a similar clumped fashion, with every major Lunar clump having a 'support the mighty elder who keeps the Wyld Hunt at bay' and an opposing 'our mighty elder is bat****-crazy' faction, among others. it's just that Lunar factionalism may play out through regionalized subgroups of say, 20-30 Lunars loosely connected across a fairly broad geographical area, rather than across the whole of Creation and the entirety of the splat.

                                                          From the archives of Chancel Aleph, for your reading pleasure. (I suggest hitting up that site's writer quotes section, it's full of gems like this.)

                                                          Originally posted by Holden
                                                          They don't make sense. Let's run them down, keeping in mind the whole time that Lunars are separated by vast distances and that the point of having a faction is to provide mutual aid, assistance, and protection to that faction's members.

                                                          So we have the Crossroads Society, who are basically a bunch of silver Twilights. Okay, this is the one faction that gets a pass from me- they're basically a large, extended study circle, and they make sense in that context. They swap research and lore and sometimes get together to help educate freshly Exalted No Moons. They're like the sorcerers' clubs that have existed among all civilized Exalted factions through all of history.

                                                          That's fine, and that makes sense. That's how sorcery and advanced mystical engineering work in Exalted.

                                                          They don't really work in the sense of "there are five big factions vying with one another" or anything- but it makes sense that Lunars with that interest would talk to one another. So, okay.

                                                          Then you have the Swords of Luna, who are uh, the guys what kill faeries. Okay, faeries are assholes, and they live in the same place the raksha do, so it makes sense that a lot of Lunars would spend a fair bit of time tussling with the raksha.

                                                          But I mean, is that it? Do they get together to swap tips on killing raksha or…? I mean, it's not really that their agenda doesn't make sense so much as that it's not enough to be a main agenda, certainly not set up against the other four. It just seems like a thing Lunars who live in the Wyld do, some of them more than others. This one isn't really nonsensical so much as unnecessary. It doesn't need to exist as a faction. They don't exactly have to roam far and wide and employ specialized methods to kill raksha, after all. They live right next door.

                                                          Also, until very recently, you couldn't run raksha as series antagonists because they melted on contact with any kind of hostile Exalt. So you can't really build a game around the Swords of Luna- no recurring NPCs, just an endless mook-killing exercise. Hell with that.

                                                          These days, the raksha milieu is complex enough to make the Swords of Luna seem dull and thuggish.

                                                          Okay, moving on, the Wardens of Gaia make no fucking sense. Is this just a Werewolf throwback? Why do Lunars care about preserving the natural world? Luna loves Gaia, sure, but you can count on one hand the number of living Lunars that have ever met Gaia. So she's just a peripheral mythological figure for them- that's a hell of a lot to ask to form a faction around.

                                                          In their modern incarnation they're a kind of senseless mishmash of ecoterrorism and Wyld-mutant-centric Thousand Streams River… eh? What? What are they even trying to do? Why do they associate?

                                                          The Sun King Seneschals likewise have all kinds of problems. One of their biggest issues is that they're consistently written to be idiots— either they're naive morons, or they're schemers in the vein of the Gold Faction only vastly less sophisticated. This feels like a clumsy sop to "some people want to tell Solar-Lunar stories."

                                                          Granted, they're the only faction that really makes sense AS A FACTION, but that still feels wrong— the bond is a very intensely personal thing for every Lunar, and it's been out of commission for 1500 years. It should have a much larger place in Lunar history than just "the stupid faction."

                                                          Finally, the Winding Path makes no sense. I mean, it's very simple— this is the Thousand Streams River faction— but why the hell is that a faction? Nobody's agenda gets in the way of theirs except I guess the Seneschals. But most of all, they don't need to work together. The last thing you want is another Lunar coming in and messing with your project. In fact, you want them to stay the hell away. The most these guys would do with one another is engage in a little quid-pro-quo trading muscle or expertise. That's simple one-to-one dickering, that doesn't need to be a faction.

                                                          Basically all of the factions are focused on things that are either irrelevant or too small to be defining to Lunars or they just don't make any sense.
