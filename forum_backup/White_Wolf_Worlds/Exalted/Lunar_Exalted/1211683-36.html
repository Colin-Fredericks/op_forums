    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    I have mixed feelings about the premise of Lunar Dominions coming across in a manner that would feel as though they're equivalent to the many different groups of Dragon Blooded.

    Like, the Terrestrials being so numerous and diverse that you can divide them up in their own book along the same lines that the core divides the different Exalted. I can't quite imagine an equivalent for Lunars.

    But i mean mixed feelings in a genuine way; I can see merits. I want to get my thoughts in order before I go into that.
    Dragon-Blooded don't constitute societies all on their own, because even in the Realm there aren't enough of them for that. There's also their mortal family members, and the countless thousands of mortals over whom they rule.

    Even if the only Wanasan that outsiders see are the Exalted ones, those exorcists should be the tip of the family iceberg, with mortals constituting everything below the water's surface.

    Lunar dominions are rather short on Exalted to make their societies magical, but we know there are kingdoms out there without any particular Exalted presence who still have magical prodigies that capture the imagination. That culture of people who have a method for ten men to sire one child, and the place where only sorcerers may rule, for examples.

    It would be much easier for Lunar dominions to have such defining magical novelties because of the manses, artifacts, and workings that Lunars could use to empower their kingdoms and peoples. And of course, there's the Lunar ability to turn people into beastfolk.

    I'm interested in that kingdom of allied snake people and bird people, and they don't even have a Lunar founder or ruler! Imagine if they did!

        One thing I like about Ex3, and this isn't aimed at anyone in particular, is that the Exalted don't have any explicit "purpose" other than being their patron's contribution to the War of the Gods. It isn't hammered home what they were for, like the Incarnae got together and engineered exactly how they'd go together. They were born of their power in a miraculous fashion, not programmed. We even have some Dragon Bloods having to be won over to the side of the other Exalted instead of being portrayed as being some obligatory army. It was also recently spoiled that the Solar/Lunar Bond was born out of a great conflict between the Solars and the Lunars instead of some sort of petty demand by the Unconquered Sun.

        Instead of being some pat little system specified to function just so, we instead have an organic culture that grew out of these people and the power invested in them by their patron's. So you would still see many Sidereal's coming into the Vizier type role, and Dragon Bloods filling out the ranks etc, but it isn't so forced and twee.

        I'm excited to see what all the Chosen of Luna can be, and not worry about them trying to fill some role other than that.

                            I'm... not really in an effective mindset for responding to a point that I made four months ago.

                            ….

                            Oh wait... actually, this isn't so hard.

                            I think if Lunars were approached in the manner of such divisions, it would warrant a chapter for sure, but I don't think that the Lunars should be approached in the manner. It kind of plays on a thing that people have been complaining about with them for a while; a sense of being too monolithic, too clumped together, despite their numbers and how logically far apart they should be. That and my personal preferences as regards some elders, in which they're rather isolated figures; deeply respected, but often from a safe distance.

                            I think Sha'a Oka should be a special case, and one that would be rolled into the book's chapter on the Caul in any case.

                            Even if one was to say that such groupings only represent an overall minority of the Silver Pact in total, there would be a problem in the level of emphasis given in an entire chapter to the subject; the level of emphasis can override impressions compared to the substance of the text.

                            So, Dragon Blooded. The only Exalted so numerous and diverse that they can form entirely self-contained, and often tight knit communities within their overall numbers. You can conceivably have Great Houses alone that have more Exalted than all non-Terrestrials combined, and they'll actually live close by to one another and have grown up together and have strong interpersonal ties. Many of the Dragon Blooded are unlike the others because being Exalted suffuses the whole of your life; you can be born with the expectation of it, it can shape formative years (especially if you Exalt as an actual child, the norm for even the outcastes and unlikely among other Exalted), and it can inform perspectives on children. There's a cultural idea to that which can form the manner of outcaste groups even when they're not related by blood. The likelihood that they can get together in groups larger than five also creates stronger grounds to form a distinct group identity and culture; I warrant that the Forest Witches are more like a society than basically any Celestial circle.

                            Because of how communities intersect, that also means that in places where groups of Dragon Blooded are embedded, they can feel more interwoven with the fabric of a society. The ten thousand Exalted of the Scarlet Dynasty, the five thousand Exalted of the Seventh Legion, the two thousand of the Dragon Clans, these things are not apart from the Realm, Lookshy and Prasad. And there are a lot of touching off points, of numerous individuals and nebulous group identity, that can leave a deeper impact than the domineering and legendary individual.

                            And there are so many Dragon Blooded, scattered all over, able to take innumerable forms and those forms often consisting of many individuals.

                            So they start off with a strong advantage in being something divided up into groups that are worth elaborating on to the point that a book filled with the whole Charm set doesn't have space for all of them. The Lunars are playing catch up there.

                            Now, I do think that it's possible that Lunar Dominions could be elaborate enough to be somewhat equivalent to a bloodline or community of Terrestrial Exalted. That they could be written as so inundated with Lunar magic and a distinct community identity that they're practically characters in their own right, and interesting enough to expand beyond their home book. That is a way to write them.

                            But I... don't think that they should be? My reasons for that are twofold.

                            One is that I think it gets into the old problem of positing the Lunars as having their own Realms in miniature, and the threat of crowding out the rest of the setting. I feel as though, even by the standards of Third Edition, the idea of many or most Lunars having elaborately magical domains is just something that overwhelms the setting. Particularly when it's phrased in terms of them taking a small tribal community and loading it with manses and Artifacts and blessings... for one thing, there's the question of what the Lunar is motivated by, and for another it seems to grate strongly against the thesis that the Lunar Exalted are lacking in resources and not best suited for playing the infrastructure game, because that's one in which the Dragon Blooded generally have the advantage.

                            That blends into my other reason, which is the manner in which the core book presented Dominions as transitory and disposable. How the general model of them is to build them up and unleash them on the Realm, over and over, in order to sap the resources of the enemy. It's a process that I think makes a lot of sense with the premise of the setting, and it's one that I find compelling enough to want to stick around. I find it incompatible with an approach to them that would warrant expansion into a supplement.

                            And thinking about the core again, I also find a new objection. I like the descriptions in the core largely being a matter of process and strategy, that the thing which generally defines a Lunar Dominion being the goals and operations in pursuit of them, rather than having a lot of flash. I find it interesting and challenging and quite appropriate to Lunars (especially considering the asymmetrical fighting approach).

                            Lunars will have powers to imbue places with challenges that reward successful petitioners with power, sure. I feel as though the advantage to that of many Lunars will typically be setting something that can sustain itself in a suitable spot (one that is or may yet become hostile to the Realm), and then leave, rather than as part of a general network of power that one will probably need to sit on and maintain (even if it lacks upkeep, if it's too dense and accessible, it kind of becomes a target for the Realm). Communities adjacent to something that a Lunar laid down a few centuries ago do seem liable to be shaped by them, but I feel as though that generally shouldn't be much more elaborate than the kind of description that the core book gave to a lot of places, or What Fire Has Wrought gave to a number of less important satrapies.

                            Overall, I think that Lunars will not need more than what their book will provide them, and that too much extra would be reaching a bit. Backer rewards of magic and quick characters for sure, but nothing really equivalent to the true meat of Heirs of the Shogunate.

                            I have approximate knowledge of many things.
