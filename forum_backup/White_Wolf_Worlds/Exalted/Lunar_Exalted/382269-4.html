        I'm a bit late to this subject, but I personally am among those who really don't like the moonsilver tattoos. Why? Because I'm not a tattoo person. I would find it totally awesome if my Lunars didn't have to have their skin marred by tattoos in order to function as Lunars. Barring that, it'd be nice to be able to obfuscate the tattoos with minimal effort.

        As for Ma-Ha-Suchi, I can easily see him as believing himself to be a righteous rebel set out to crush a usurping corrupt dictatorship. No mental illness required. Unless you consider being an Exalt to be an illness in that one would rather make history than have history... uh... made onto... him? I dunno. I kind of lost the train of thought there, but my point is that he's a demigod who hates the Empire, so building up an army built around its destruction seems pretty much normal. Maybe he hates too much... probably he does. However, one has to realize that not all aberrant behavior should be grounds for classification of illness.

        And finally! On the subject of Chimeras... is it true that most people play Chimeras as mindless murder-machines? That seems rather strange to me, even as one who learned Exalted from Second Edition. Every Chimera is a hero who has essentially been lost to their most defining urges as well as lost their form, but that hardly means that all they do is murder everything all the time.

            Originally posted by turkeygiant View Post
            What would his motivation for making this change be other than good old despotic power? He seemed like he was pretty content with the pleasure of the first age, so what is stopping him from seeking those same pleasures in the age of sorrows? It's a interesting thought!
            I imagine in part because the Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded expelled him and all other Lunars (where they didn't manage to kill said Lunars) to the outskirts of Creation in a violent coup and then set up a widespread religion and world-straddling colossus of an empire with killing or otherwise keeping them relegated to the sidelines very high on the list of priorities. Also, the First Age is not the Second: the full heights of pleasures he may have enjoyed in the former are no longer nearly so easily obtained in the latter, if available at all. Someone who'd grown intimately accustomed to the lap of First Age luxury could regard the what's possible with Second Age luxury as a pale, unsatisfying shadow.

            Also also -- and putting entirely aside that someone who's been violently driven from his comfortable home context and known the death or suffering of huge swaths of people he cared about is prrrrrrobably gonna hold a teensy bit of resentment, never mind some concern about it happening again if he doesn't take corrective action -- the previously mentioned world-straddling colossus of an empire (still very happy to kill him) is seizing all of the desirable resources it can to prop itself up and provide the highest pleasures of the Second Age to its scions. So, if asked why he is waging war against the Realm instead of just "enjoying the pleasures of the Second Age," should he answer at all, he may angrily snarl, "What do you think I'm trying to do!?"

                        You still have a lot of 2e lore holding you back. Just as surely as the Ebon Dragon isn't going to be a creature of anti-Virtue in EX3, it's highly unlikely that they're going to keep MHS's background from DotFA and Lunars 2e, especially since everything from DotFA is going to be scrapped and Lunars 2e made Holden swear a blue streak on the forums some four years ago or so with its characterization problems.

                        What MHS thinks is probably going to be a slightly modified form of what you got in his E:tL description. Rather than hating on all civilization, he specifically wants to water the fields of the Blessed Isle with the blood of the Terrestrials and all of their supporters, right down to the last frightful peasant, because they're all complicit in the evil that is the so-called Realm of the Scarlet Dynasty. Unfortunately, he doesn't really trust the Threshold states neighboring his Nameless Lair to resist the plague of the Dynasty on their own, and so is looking to set up shop first in the River Province and then overflow into other regions. While this could be beneficial in some respects - he's not about to let the Mask of Winters stay in the lands of the living - it's more likely to involve the entirety of the Scavenger Lands that remains alive being pressed into service in a military training regimen that makes Lookshy seem small and subdued, with the veneration of Luna set up at the head of everyone's temple and all other deities put behind her. Everyone is going to learn how to hunt, and they'll sacrifice any dissenters to the Bloody Huntress at the behest of Her intermediary, the Prince of the Northern Jungle.

                        If you have access to a copy of Exalted: the Lunars, just read "the 'Realm' of the Scarlet Dynasty, if you can even call it that" whenever E:tL uses the word "civilization", "weak settled folk", and the like.

                                Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                I'm away from my books, but I believe things are more severe than you to recall. Every Chimera ultimately gets its Motivation replaced with "Survive," for one thing, and I'm pretty sure that's not the last of the worrisome changes. It's not just a matter of being "no true form, constant/super-easy Limit Break."
                                I, likewise, am not near my book, but I believe that those are really the only three things that chimeras are saddled with. Now, being in constant limit break forever means that they're certainly not going to be terribly rational, but unless every chimera has the "murder everyone that they see" limit, it doesn't preclude them from still being characters.

                                Granted, the only thing that chimeras were ever shown as being were mindless monsters in second edition, so that certainly helps color people's opinions of them. Still, if a chimera had the Curse of the Mother Hen limit break, she'd be a ridiculously protective being of constant mutations bent on her own survival. Certainly something different than the beast that just eats everything.

                                I once ran a story that the players particularly enjoyed, about a chimera that had been haunting a shadowland for centuries. Originally the shadowland was something like a mental institution that had been appropriated by a scholar who tortured inmates in order to purposefully shadowland the place, then he studied the effects of the underworld on the minds of the insane. The Chimera in question was once an inmate there who Exalted and escaped, then returned years later already twisted. Using chimera charms, she killed everyone there, then spend the next two centuries endlessly rebirthing and hunting down the administrators who tortured her and the other inmates. Her limit break was the Curse of the Drunken Monkey, and endlessly murdering the people who she hated so much was one of the most enjoyable things she could think of. There was more of course, involving her chimeric beastman children, a nearby town that the chimera had terrorized into a stockholm syndrome type scenario, and a hekatonkhire that sang a song of cannibalism, but the important thing was that the chimera was an actual character instead of a mindless monster.

                                That's not to imply that she wasn't an antagonist of course, she totally was. So... my point is that chimeras can be fun! Even the second edition types!

                                                A few Hekatonkhires are statted up, but there's no base Hekatonkhire frame to build off of or anything. The one I made was initially inspired by Soul Reaver's Zephon and a bit the Red Queen from American Macgee's Alice. It ended up not being too similar to either of them other than some aesthetics. The Hekatonkhire, Voiceless Singer of Desperate Hunger, was a subterranean being that sang an inaudible song that inspired cannibalism. People would start seeing other people as suitable food choices, but after over a century even the trees were devouring one-another (very slowly) and the buildings in the area as well. When a cannibal or someone who was cannibalized died, the Hekatonkhire would snare their soul and spin it into soulsteel wire with which it would create items of beauty. It was composed of stone tentacles as large as trees that permeated the shadowland centered on the House of Rightful Thought (the sanitarium), and when it wished to address others the tentacles would bleed Old Realm script (it didn't talk, as it had no voice). For an ancient death behemoth it was actually quite reasonable and had no interest in fighting. It only wished to sing and was unhappy when the player characters killed its muse (the chimera).
