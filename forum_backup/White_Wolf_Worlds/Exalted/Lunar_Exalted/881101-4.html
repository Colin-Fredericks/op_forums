        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
        ​
        I was addressing your point at the same time as snarking about the possibility that it's a digression from the subject under discussion.

        Still, since the subject has been digressed to:

        What would a theoretical society of tiny people bring to the setting, and to any given game? What can they offer to, and how do their issues concern, player characters? How can they relate to any society around them? What compels one to go down to their level?

        What do they add to things?


        Well, you end up with a race that make useful allies as spies, assassins, scouts (they can train and ride ordinary birds) and potentially craftspeople (incredibly intricate clockwork, f'rex). You get to use faerie tales - literally, the ones about the tiny faerie folk - in your story, perhaps having the PCs have to figure out what is behind the "curse" laid upon a particular person/place (it's these tiny people bedevilling them). Just one of them as an orphan foundling can make for a compelling character.

        Their relative lack of physical power makes them vulnerable to exploitation by larger neighbours, so a society of them could well be militarized, oppressed, or otherwise in an interesting position relative to the rest of Creation.

        Their food needs are, if we want to get realistic, likely to be quite high; they'd need constant sugary food access to maintain their metabolisms - and such foods are delicacies to bigger folk, and thus again, a potential point of conflict.

        A travelling show of little people (with a bigger human running the show) can make for an interesting bit of local colour, and also make you wonder about exploitation.

        And, finally: they'd make an interesting PC, now wouldn't they?

                                The interesting thing about this thread is to see that a lot of people conceptualize shapeshifting really differently from me. Much of the reason I say "sure why not" toward it is that changing into animals and changing your own size are filed right next to one another in my mind in terms of "types of shapeshifting" -- I have them both indexed as core motifs of ancient mythological shapeshifters, based in what seemed to me to be a more intuitive, physical notion of shapeshifting uninformed by modern tropes on the subject. You do still have superheroes who do both, but it's very much "classic old-school" shapeshifting, which I felt was closer to the root of Lunar themes than direct animalism. I can't really defend that reading, Lunar themes being what they are, so that might also explain some of the difference of opinion.

                                When I think of Lunars -- just personally -- I think of them as being thematically associated with ancient Greek monsters, recast through the lens of a person and possibly a protagonist. The animalism is there, but I see it more as a metaphor for frightening external forces rather than a core theme. Of course, Lunars have been all over the place and we all come to terms with them in our own way.

                                (The reason I still come out leaning against Ant-Man scale is because I don't like Lunar shifting to be surreal, since they have a very worldly, hyperreal feel going on. At some point radical alterations in physical scale start to come unmoored from people's actual experience of the world in a way that I think is better suited to the Wyld, which is overtly surreal.)

So it just seems like "...what the christ are you talking about?" when people start crying that growing and shrinking powers is "diluting their concept as therianthropes" or whatever. Like three quarters of all halfway known therianthrope myths live in an extremely, extremely limited narrative slot. I've never felt that therianthropy alone is anywhere near enough to sustain a full player splat that actually justifies its own wordcount. I've always wanted my Lunars to have more mystery to them, since the Moon has always been a symbol for the mystical, the changing, the knowably mysterious. A Lunar performer in my mind should be entirely capable of sitting down in a clearing of the forest under the full moon and playing Moonlight Sonata by tugging the actual moonlight rays like a harp. But I realize THAT kind of ethereality would be a dilution of the primal and physical therianthropy focus the devs want to go for, the same way or even more than all the face stealing is).

                                        But growing and shrinking? That's just another facet of the same thing.
