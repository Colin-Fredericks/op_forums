    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
    Personally I love oWoD.

    Mating in human form with an animal, or in animal form with a human? YUCK. That's bestiality.

    But I think Lunars absolutely should be able to make yeddimfolk by shapeshifting into a yeddim and mating with a yeddim. I really don't understand the objection to this existing as an option. When you shapeshift, you become that animal. It's not an illusion, it's not a mind-game. You literally became a yeddim. You're a sapient yeddim with super magic powers and a human soul, but still a yeddim. Even moreso if the yeddim is a true form. That is not bestiality, it's shapeshifting. And for players who find any sex uncomfortable there can be other options too. (see below)
    I'd just stick to sorcery for everyone. It seems like pretty much anyone could pull a Dr. Moreau, anyway, and, again, the minotaur didn't have any Lunar figures around to beget him. Just a woman, a bull, and the vengeful spite of the gods.

    I really have a big problem with beastfolk always or mostly being Wyld mutants. The Wyld is fundamentally inimical to Creation. Anything mutated by the Wyld is probably helping to destroy Creation or at least human civilization. So having the Lunars allies with soul-sucking Wyld mutants who want to destroy civilization or taint the land because they can't even survive in Creation, fundamentally makes them villains equal to the loyalist Abyssals in depravity. That's going back to the 1E "Lunars want to destroy cities and aqueducts and farming, Hulk smash." I really am not interested in Lunars as allies of the Fair Folk or out to destroy civilization, but that's what you have to do when your allies and armies have to be Wyld mutants.
    The Wyld isn't necessarily super-antagonistic, and the people that get altered aren't, either. Both are simply just strange.

    It also means that if you want to raise an army, you have to be out on the edge of the map. Lunar games should not be limited to those places. Lunars should be capable of functioning in cities, in the Scavenger Lands, in the Deshan, wherever Solars can go. Part of the appeal to shapeshifting and being so adaptable is being able to adapt to anywhere. So why tell Lunar players that if they go 500 miles from the edge of reality, suddenly all their resources are gone? That's crappy.
    Not necessarily. There are probably a lot of pockets of beastfolk left when the Wyld overflowed the last time. There are also isolated pockets of chaos around Creation that have left legacies; one even exists in Nexus, of all places. The origin of the Lookshy rebel also indicates that some bits of Wyld might have been sealed into containers in the First Age. But then, that is only the most common way, not the only one.

    Other ways to get beastfolk faster: Option #1 in my mind is the round up an army of beastfolk from the beastfolk who are already around. The 3E corebook has Prince Diamond helping some random wolffolk tribe and there's no mention of a Lunar anywhere in town (otherwise, the local Lunar probably would have explained to that river god what happens to gods who interfere with a Celestial's worshippers).
    Likely left behind from what I just mentioned.

    Also, beastfolk shouldn't only be anthropomorphic animals. I like those, but what about hawkfolk who look like baselines but with the eyesight of hawks? Why not camelfolk who look human but need as little water as camels, without the hump? If you can use some Lunar equivalent of genesis crafting/Verdant Emptiness Endowment to infuse animal blood into adults, why not flyfolk who look human but have the unbelievably fast reflexes of flies? Holy crap, flyfolk would be a terror in battle!
    Beastfolk get their capabilities from those changes. Without the changes, they don't have them. Some might have more human faces, like one of Yena's Daughters who doesn't have a muzzle, but in that case they don't get some other benefits, such as, say, improved olfaction.

                                This has most likely already been addressed, but the thread was already tl;dr by the time I got here.

                                I liked the role that 2E gave us; The Stewards. They protect Creation from the Fair Folk and other unsavory otherworldly creatures. The Lunars with their ability to learn FF charms and the anima abilities of the original five castes suited them to combat the Fair Folk.

                                I think this next bit is my headcanon, so just a head's up. When the gods were planning the War, the gods reasoned the Primordials may become so desperate that they might invite the Fair Folk into Creation. The Primordials could defeat the Fair Folk, they've done so many times before, and undo the damage to Creation wrought by the fey. So the Lunars were designed to be able to become whatever they needed to be in order to defeat what ever the Wyld dreamt up.

                                The Lunar design plan paid off. The Primordials realized that they had underestimated the Exalted and invited the Fair Folk into Creation. However, the gods, the Lunars and the Exalted under their command were able to defend Creation's borders. This allowed the Solars and their armies to stay focused on laying low the Primordials.

                                In the Second Age, the role of the Lunars hasn't really changed much. They consider themselves the defenders of Creation's borders although of course they lack the First Age and even Time of Glory infrastructure and teamwork that allowed them to do so nearly flawlessly. Additionally the Underworld is making moves in diverse places in Creation. The appearance of these "Deathlords" with their Abyssal Exalted and the spreading of shadowlands is cause for concern for the already stretched too thin Stewards.

                                                I think it's apparent from some developer comments that the role of the Lunar and the nature of the Lunar are distinct, but somewhat related.

                                                ​That is, the nature of the Lunar is mutable, so the role of the Lunar has been different things at different times.

                                                ​In the Primordial War, let's say the role of the Lunar was to be the fierce seconds of the Solars, either alongside them or apart from them being powerful companions and delegates.

                                                ​In the Realm, the role of the Lunars was to be its guardians from a variety of threats, within and without. Not as a thing that Lunars are exclusively qualified for, it's just what they dedicated their power to in a different Age of the world, one less defined by a grandiose war.

                                                ​In the Second Age, the role of the Lunar is to be perennial survivors in the face of extreme opposition, and saboteurs of the architects of that opposition.

                                                ​There's an idea of this mutability expressed in a change in their Castes. It may also be in a change in their powers, but I'd say we'd have to wait and see on that; I would think such a thing would be more sensible as an impression of how their powers develop and interact, rather than outright different Charms, but who knows.

                                                ​Under the circumstances, I would say that it's foolish to try and start with what the Lunars are like in the game and extrapolate them all the way back to their origins (and that trying to find them a place in the Primordial War in terms of a specific strategic or logistic niche to be given, rather than "Luna was in on the whole thing as well, so throw her champions into the mix" is the wrong way to approach that bit of backstory in the first place). At the same time, I think it might help to break away from the idea that what Lunars do in the setting and play needs to be something highly distinct, considering the possibility for unique ways of playing them.

                                                So, disregarding the Silver Pact (which the corebook is remarkably explicit on many Lunars doing, for all that people overlook it entirely), nothing about Lunars especially impedes or informs them doing things like living lives of adventure and intrigue.

                                                ​As for the idea of a "Night Realm"... you know, that's the kind of thing that I would have been down on in the past, but in the spirit of my current ethos of "approach 'canon' as something flexible and imperfect even in diegesis", I could accommodate the idea that the whole idea of a distinction in government based on what time of the day it is emerges from the First Age being recalled imperfectly and with an emphasis on mythology in the timespan that the game takes place in, which would attribute the half-remembered actions of the Lunars to some kind of mythic Realm of the night, rather than being a literal statement on the idea that every Solar tucked into bed at sundown and society fundamentally transformed.
