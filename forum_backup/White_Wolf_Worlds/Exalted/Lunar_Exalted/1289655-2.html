                        Alright, I promised on the Discord to write something out when I was home and could do a long, coherent argument. So I will! As preamble, note I don't think it's actually a big deal if you *do* go for a reciprocated bond. But much like I'll argue that BP isn't actually a design mistake necessarily, I can argue for the 3e version of the Bond quite well.

                        The Bond, as presented, has absolutely nothing to do with Solars except insofar as they (and Abyssals, and Infernals) are the objects of it. But the Bond itself is not a Solar thing.

                        It is the double-edged sword the Lunars installed in themselves to keep the Solars in check. It is, in implementation and in setting, the precise opposite of the hidden glowing weak spot a Solar could hit for massive damage. The Bond does not demand the Lunar love the Solar, it demands only that they feel. As the Dionysian counterpart to the Apollonian Solars, that's entirely appropriate.

                        The Lunar charms we've seen make the Bond both a valuable help when assisting a Solar the Lunar approves of, and a deadly weapon when harming a Solar they do not. What it isn't is the 2nd edition weakness. As such, I believe it should be evaluated as presented currently, and not as you might think of it. (That said, I would be highly surprised if one of the Lunar Manipulation charms do not allow them to instill an intimacy in their bonded Solar.)

                        This is, of course, besides the practical aspects of having Solar-pertaining rules retrofitted into the core via a supplement, or the setting detail that not ever Lunar is bonded, and not every bonded Lunar is liable to ever meet their bonded Solar. My point is in 3e, demanding that Solars be affected by the bond in more than a few weakpoints in charms strikes me like demanding that Solars ought to be able to shapeshift a little.

                                                            In the absence of specific mechanics, past-life flash-backs are an excellent canon-supported way to have that mutual moment of recognition and connection when a Solar and Lunar bond-pair meet, if that's something the player or ST wants to do.

                                                            My primary problem with Solar Bond as presented is that for no reason I can discern, the text constrains all Solar-Lunar pairings to having originated with an arranged marriage, which makes me viscerally uncomfortable. That's just not very romantic. Neither is it very platonic. It's like the worst of both worlds. It just makes the story of the bond more political than spiritual. I guess the intent was to make the bond mutually consensual, and give players more agency over whether this story element comes into play for their character, which is definitely good, but the execution just presents different problems, one of which is, ironically, player agency.

                                                            Maybe a lot of people won't care about the origin of their bond, and it certainly doesn't dictate how the pair relates to each other now, but what if I wanted to specify the origins of my reincarnating power-couple as anything other than this one narrative we've been given? What if I wanted to tell literally any other story about the relationship that led their fates to intertwine? Why are those stories off the cards? If the bond needs to be the result of a singular effort to tie the Solars and Lunars together (and I guess I don't have a problem with a peace deal being the impetus for that), why did they only decide to sanctify this alliance with marriages? I mean, what happened to all the Solars and Lunars at the time who were asexual/aromantic or just, you know, already happily married to someone else, like a mortal? Did they just not get to participate in this whole eternal-soul-union thing, even if they wanted to participate in some other way, by swearing oaths as blood-siblings or something?

                                                            "Sorry honey, I love you, but we gotta divorce or I'm going to miss out on having a soul-mate for all of time, so I'm ditching you to marry my best friend. No, we can't just swear to be BFFs. Gotta get hitched. It's a whole thing."

                                                            I know it's easy to golden-rule this, but because it's so easy (ie, nothing seems to break by changing it), I don't see the reason for the restriction in the first place.

                                                            Edited to say: I'm otherwise loving the Lunar previews. I don't want the fact that the Solar bond is all I've talked about in relation to Lunars so far to give the impression that I'm not hyped for this book. It's just one thing that's bringing me down in a sea of very awesome things that are giving me joy.
