                                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                            Just because not everything they do is described, that doesn't we just assume they haven't done anything and have been sitting on their hands for the past 800 years. o_O Are you crazy? They're Celestial Exalts! Of course they've been doing things!!
                                            Here's the thing. Sure, we can assume the Lunars have done some epic ambiguous things things. But we have no examples of what. We have nothing to point to and say: "that's what a Lunar can do after 700 years, or after 2000 years." We have no author who cared enough to lobby something interesting into publication. Lunars as a splat are ridiculously focused on the distant past rather than the present that is the default setting of the game. But despite their obsessive focus on the past, they have nothing to show for it.

                                            What we have is mostly a bunch of people who alternatively mope or play out revenge porn for an event (granted, a terrible, world-changing event) that happened 2000 years ago, and then we have a ton of them hanging out in the Wyld like losers (fearing probably-described-as-too-competent Sidereals) who eventually just die forgotten and fighting the vanguard of the Balorian Crusade. Sure, a given ST can fill in gaps with whatever he wants to fit his story, and to hell with the rest of the setting. That's an okay tack to take, for one table. But when authors write books and create splat histories, they have to do it with their eye in multiple places: (1) they have to do it with an eye to "this is a thing that someone in this splat did, as an example for the scope of awesome things you can do given enough time and effort"; (2) they have to create big (but coherent and reasonable) plot and story hooks that might influence characters from that splatbook or other splatbooks to hook their backstories on those hooks if they want, or provide hooks for STs to base in-game stories off of; (3) they have to do it in a way where it fits into and does not undermine consistent setting history (basically, the history has to make logical sense).

                                            In the case of Lunars, authors have have not bothered doing (1), and have put relatively little effort into (2) by making most of the canon Lunars elders and removing them from any contact with the world beyond their fiefdom. They have other splats doing those big things and occupying so much of that narrative space that to simply add some "big things Lunars did" out of categories (1) and (2) to the setting, they might unbalance (3) in ways that make the setting not make sense when viewed from the outside. Instead authors have chosen to keep (3) largely as it was for 2 editions (not radically revising parts of history) and instead spelled out nothing great that Lunars have done, giving prospective players and STs very little to reference about what their Lunar can do, when 300 people like that Lunar have done nothing noteworthy of recording in a book for inspiration.

                                            Saying "the ST can fix it" is a Rule Zero Fallacy event. Of course the ST can fix it, the question is, can he fix it well while the rest of the setting looks somewhat the same? And the answer is, simply, no. Or at least, not easily, and most people won't want to put in that level of effort. The ST can certainly throw in a gigantic overt Lunar kingdom into, say, the Hundred Kingdoms, where 50 Lunars rule, like, states of a confederation evoking the Holy Roman Empire. The question then becomes "why hasn't the Realm attacked it, because this place is openly run by Anathema" and "given 50 Lunars working together, what changes to the political landscape of the world that differentiate from the setting baseline have been made." If the answer to the second question is "none," then the Lunars are worthless paper tigers; if the answer is "a lot!" then some of those events have to be detailed, whether it's Lunars basically taking over Nexus and in a cold war with Lookshy and have taken Thorns as a protectorate after 30 out of the 50 Lunars stopped the Mask of Winters in his tracks, at the cost of 20 of them which has caused some successorship issues in the Holy Lunar Empire's component states. But then the question still remains what the other 250 of them are doing and/or have done...

                                            We know what the DBs did. We know what Alchemicals did in Autochthonia. We know what the Sidereals did (and there are only 100 of them). We know what the Solars did a long time ago, kind of, but they've been out of the picture for so long that it doesn't really matter what the 10 or whatever of them that weren't in the Jade Prison did: mostly get killed, hunted, and killed again. Perhaps we know or can guess what they did because their themes are much more sturdy and established frameworks. With Lunars we have a group of 300 myth-heroes, some of whom are over 2000 years old, and really nothing of note published to show for their lives, and themes that were an antisocial mess in 1e and mayo-on-white-bread in 2e. When the authors can't decide where a splat should be in the world, potential players don't know what to expect from playing that splat, what a default story might be like, what to expect and what is reasonable to reach for. What does that tell a person who is initially interested in the splat?

                                            Basically you can add a big thing in a vacuum without thinking of larger setting consequences, but if you don't, it looks very tacked on and sloppy. If you go through a ton of setting consequences you may be making more woe for yourself than you intended starting out, and the players may have more trouble recognizing the setting and will have to be brought up to speed or whatever.

                                            Also it's a problem when so much attention (and word count) is paid to the elder Lunars in the setting (who are about as far away from the PC spectrum as possible) rather than the Lunars who actually might be playable as PCs like Strength-of-Many and Heysha Black-Asp. The other "established" splats (by that I mean "splats with living elders") don't have this problem: despite Chejop, Sidereals somehow don't have this problem with their NPCs since the young ones get some attention and are interesting (Black Ice Shadow in particular maybe); ditto with DBs. But for some reason everybody just wants to write about fucking Lilith being sulky as an owl for 2000 years as if that's interesting, christ.
