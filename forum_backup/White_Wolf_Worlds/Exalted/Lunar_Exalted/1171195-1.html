    Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
    It's something the devs explained thoroughly; even Dragon-Blooded can't just crank out hundreds of Dragon-Blooded babies. The best chances they have at their kids Exalting is when there's years and years between conceiving said kids.
    This is true. Of course, just to point out it's also something that while the readers know about, might not be something that the people of Creation are entirely familiar with. I suspect that there are probably a few Dragon Blooded rulers out there in the setting who have a massive harem comparable to that of a Khan or an Egyptian Pharaoh and who are trying to have as many children as possible with their mortal spouses in the hopes that some of their children will Exalt, because they don't know the ins and outs of Terrestrial Exaltation and doesn't realize that they need a set period of time in between having children.

    That's presumably not going to happen in the Realm or Lookshy or Prasad, because those kingdoms are probably more aware of some of the aspects of Exaltation and over the centuries (or through looking at Shogunate era material) realize that, to a degree, the longer one waits in between having children the more likely the child is to Exalt. But many of the Lost Eggs and Outcastes in Creation probably don't have that sort of "meta" knowledge about their Exaltation.

    In the end what's important, of course, is that while a Dragon Blooded might try to sire or give birth to a legion of Dragon Blooded, that simply isn't something he or she can actually accomplish.

                                    Even setting aside questions about breeding farms, a lot about the subject of Dragon Blooded reproduction has always been vague in a way that was not helpful when it's an important subject about their lifestyle and politics. For instance, there would be a few places saying that, on average, a couple might have one child every 12 years and maybe have something like ten children ever, but there was never really anything that laid down how many of these children would actually Exalt.

                                    ​So the concept of progenitive Essence can help clarify that if, as I suspect it will, the answer will be "for two well-bred Exalted, basically all of their children can be expected to Exalt, but only if they wait a decent amount of time between children". It makes a significant aspect of their society a lot less muddy, which will probably help with playing in that setting and devising character backgrounds.

                                    ​It also plays into some of their adjustments to the gender dynamics of Dynast society, such as the attitude toward extramarital children.

                                    ​As for the First Age, I have a suspicion that the current writers aren't quite as big on the idea of there being so many of them as there were in Second Edition, but assuming at least a notion that there were more than ten thousand, I would say that can be chalked up to living longer with the aid of anagathics, more of them being able to have children as a result of a somewhat safer world, and maybe some additional mystical resources to help reduce the downtime.

                                    ​Because the idea of progenitive Essence is not the only thing they're using to add a bit more consistency to Dragon Blooded numbers; Eric has consistently made comments suggesting to me that there's going to be a bit more emphasis on the mortality rate that prevents many from having children at all, as well as frequently cleaning out the prior generations.

                                    That's another side to it; you don't need to treat people like brood mares in order for a population of people with significantly higher lifespans to explode over time, because population is a matter of both birth and death rate. Even if we assumed that Dragon Blooded had no more children than the average modern Western person, and did so between about the same ages, you're still dealing with the fact that if they keep living for centuries afterwards, then the population is going to keep growing and growing. That is, after all, a very significant part of the population explosion from the 19th century onwards; improved medicine, nutrition and living conditions allowing more people to live longer. Just look at a picture of a family tree going back over a couple of centuries, and imagine if all of those people were still alive, and multiply it by several dozen families.

                                    ​The Age of Sorrows becomes a bit unwieldy if there are too many Dragon Blooded around, so adding new elements and emphasizing old ones behind keeping it relatively compact has its uses.

                                    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
                                    Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.
