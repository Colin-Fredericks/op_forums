    I guess I'm just a bit a cynical of there being a type of Exalted who's patron has strong links to the Wyld, who have a history of both battling the Wyld and using it as a refuge, who represent themes of animalism, shape changing and "barbarian" culture (where Wyld Barbarian, Wyld beasts and Wyld mutations are all strongly linked to these themes), who have moonsilver tattoos specifically to help them both mutate in creation and hold form in the wyld, now have nothing whatsoever to do with the Wyld. It seems to take lots away without adding anything to me. While it is true Raksha are creatures of the Wyld I dont see them treading on each others conceptual space. Liminals don't tread on Abyssals space yet interacting at the edges of the Underworld as Lunars do with the Wyld works. The Lunar's have close links to the Wyld than any other Exalt type (raskha are not exalts) I fail to see what is gained by rewarding this. I get that the benefits of being able to participate in Shaping combat was pretty limited and difficult to use. So re-imagining how the Wyld informs their aesthetic and mechanics is key but I think severing that tie is out of context for them and takes away an interesting arena for story and development.

        As a big fan of Lunars, here's my take on it. Grain of salt. Rule Zero. My own interpretation, etc etc etc.

        Having Lunars (or any other Celestial level Exalted) as second-fiddle Ed McMahons to the Solar level Exalted is terrible writing. It limits our options players and storytellers can engage with, unless we do a lot of finagling to work around the limitations. Some parts of earlier editions really seemed like Lunars and Sidereals (and probably Alchemicals - I never quite caught up with them, nor with Raksha) should just be grateful to sit at the kid's table while the grownups were talking.

        I would like to see a well-played, in-theme Lunar be right on par with an equally well-played, in-theme Solar. I mean that in a storytelling sense more than in a dice crunch sense, because that's how I emphasize my games. A Lunar who's using the right Shape, Transformation, Fury, etc for the situation should be an iconic terror. That, of course, applies for Solars using their perfected skills well, for Sidereals twisting Destiny their way, and so on across the board. Emphasizing theme should be rewarded with crunch.

        The theme that I'd like to see for Lunars is that they change themselves in order to change the world.

        As a short aside here, and so you know where I'm coming from, I'd also like to see Terrestrials brought closer to level in power and coolness. Their specific theme being amazing teamwork, a pair of them should be about on par with a young Celestial, a Circle of them about on par with a pre-centennial Celestial, and beyond that notions of parity get strained anyway.

        I prefer the idea that each of the Celestines & Primordials brought their own nature to their Exalted, but not roles or responsibilities per se. It seems very out of character to me that those sorts of beings would be at all concerned with the details of their creations' tactics and decisions. Rather: "You exist as I have made you. That is enough."

                While it'll never come to be, I've also always dreamed of Lunars being more tied to the Wyld, rather than less tied. But I also think being 'more tied' doesn't mean they should only be useful in the Wyld.

                If I was to design Lunars, I'd shift away from animals and the like, and focus on the chaos - they change shape because they have no permanency, because they embrace that they can have many forms, not because of a sacred connection to animals. Lunars in my design would be terrifying and potentially destabilizing entities, who do impossible things by turning off the logic and laws of the world. Think the 2e SMA Quicksilver Hands of Dreams (though mostly at smaller levels, except at high essence I guess.). They would turn parts of Creation into Wyld storms, just for a little bit, and remove Fate from their tiny army so that they can win ten to one against the Realm's army they were 'fated' to lose to. Their armies would be able to fly not because they created a hand crafted army with ships and etc like a solar, but because they removed the idea of 'humans can't fly' from existence for awhile.

                They would be utterly and completely terrifying to Sidereals, who rely on Creation and stability. They would be weapons created to attack the world bodies of the Primordials, and the ultimate rejection of the world they made. During the first age the whole Lunar/Solar bond thing would have been a willing thing, Solars helping to 'ground' their Lunar partner, keep their Chaos in check via their Order. Now without it, you get what are basically freeholds in Creation - Ma-Ha-Suchi isn't raising an army by fucking goat people/fucking people as a goat, he just has turned his entire home into this massively mutating Wyld Zone, and everyone who ventures into it becomes a terrifying monster man.

                A Lunar crafter would turn soup into a sword in an instant, would be capable of causing a forest to grow into a manse all on its own, would turn silk cloth into something as hard as steel. A Lunar warrior would bypass someone's guard by altering which direction their arm was moving instantly, or fire a dozen arrows at once that the wind all happened to carry to their target. A Lunar socialite would be a haunting enchanting beauty or terror, along the lines of a Fae, able to cloud the mind in a haze with words.


                But unfortunately, the Devs and many players prefer the 'sacred animal' approach to Lunars, and want to keep them tied to Werewolf stylings.

                    It strikes me that Lunars and Dragon-Blooded don't necessarily need to be as strong as Solars to be interesting. They just need to not suck.

                    Like, if you think about 1e trying to establish itself and give a vision of the world via mechanics, you can see how Dragon-Blooded, almost accidentally gigs hatred a bit in the sense that they felt the need to specifically make their powers worse than Solars.

                    The sense I've gotten from reading discussions and listening to interviews, the benefit of another new edition is to look at that retrospectively and try to more fully realize the Dragon-Blooded as mechanically interesting without an explicit design goal of demonstrating them as second-fiddle to Celestials.

                    I mean, we still know that a Dragon-Blooded can't reach the heights of power a Solar can, but that doesn't mean that the Dragon-Blooded are just left in the mud, you know?

                    Lunars have the more complicated issue of how their first book came out in a messy way, but it doesn't mean they can't enact a similar notion. Read anything that Morke or Holden have said about Lunars and it's clear as day that they're fans of Lunars, so I certainly expect that, free of things like the Knack problem, they'll be able to explore mechanics of Lunars without just making them Silver Solars or Not As Good. If anything, I get the sense one should focus on method rather than totally fixating on power when looking at the difference in how a Lunar handles a problem versus a Solar.

                            I think I agree with you quite a lot, here, but let me expand on it. Purely whiteboarding follows.

                            Lunars adapt themselves.
                            Lunars in the mundane wilderness adapt to that wilderness. Animals, plants, barbarians. Rain, fog, snow, oceans, grasslands, forest.
                            Lunars in the Wyld adapt to the Wyld. Stories, waypoints, hobgoblins, unshaped things. Places that don't make sense. Places that invade your nightmares.
                            Lunars in the city adapt to the city. Buildings, gangs, commerce, bureaucracies. Politics, intrigue, skullduggery.
                            Lunars on the battlefield adapt to the battlefield. Armies, mud, blood, gore, and War.
                            Lunars in Yu-Shan adapt to Yu-Shan. (The Usurpation Sids were strangely lucky to be able to ferret them out)
                            Lunars working alongside with Solars adapt to the Solars. The same (to the shock of everybody in the age of sorrows) goes for the Solaroids. It might also go for Sids, Dragon-blooded, or others.

                            [[ At low levels of Essence, a young Lunar may gain Shapes, Survival, Bureaucracy, War, etc. related Charms or Knacks that let them adapt to where they are or what's going on. It's long lasting (scene or so) and comes at the expense of being adapted to other areas at the same time. In the case of working with the Wyld, other Exalted, etc, it functions by enabling pack-tactics coordination, or by borrowing Charms or magic, or taking them by hunt or challenge. It might replace the concept of Lunar Fury, in that being in a battle is pretty much a Fury styled adaptation. It might also replace the Solar-Lunar bond, in that adapting to other Exalted is similar. ]]

                            Lunars adapt their surroundings.
                            At middle levels of Essence, a Lunar may share the benefits of their adaptations with their allies:
                            Friends instinctively sidestep hazards in the wilderness, and resist (or shape!) the mutating influence of the Wyld.
                            Allied battle groups hide in the forest or the snow as if they've evaporated into nothing.
                            Allies seem to bolster from the Lunar's amazing stamina and speed as they march, and never seem to be harmed by the weather.

                            Alternatively, a Lunar at this level may choose two, eventually three or more adaptations at the same time:
                            A Lunar battling in the Wyld matches their own martial puissance with instinctive use of the strange terrain.
                            Investigating an overgrown city, the Lunar is a shadow lurking equally well among the buildings and the jungle.
                            Lunars adapt the world.
                            At the highest levels of (playable) Essence, a Lunar may change their present environment in ways that match another.
                            Ma-Ha-Suchi has his Wyld zone, exactly as you mentioned, but he might create one in a city or on a battlefield.
                            Friendly battle groups rally out of the population of a city at the Lunar's call.
                            A vast wilderness may hide a shortcut through a Waypoint to cross the whole distance.
                            The tight grasp of an Unshaped may be forced into the purview of Destiny.



                            It's easy to poke holes in this, but I think with some evolution it would be pretty fun and iconic.
