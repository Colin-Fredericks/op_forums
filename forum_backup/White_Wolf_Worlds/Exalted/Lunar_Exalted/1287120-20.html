                        on a different track, I think there's only two charms in the new set I think are a bit weird.

                        I think Chain-Breaking Fury is a bit iffy towards the idea that if someone has socially influenced you, you've been hypnotized and brainwashed. I don't think the actual power is that powerful or broken; the idea that when you've been persuaded to do something you reconsider when you see it's against something else you care about isn't too bad, but the idea that the fluff describes it as becoming furious at the idea that you were 'used' or 'deceived' and that you immediately get a Major intimacy of Rage feels, to me, like it's pushing the envelope on what the social system means, which feels like a step back from the Psyche keyword, which is clearly used to differentiate between simply being good at persuading someone and magically debilitating their ability to resist.

                        The second charm is Wound-Mastering Body Evolution. For a charm that makes you immune to Withering Damage, it feels like the cost of the continuing effect is pretty meaningless. It's meant to be a reflection of Aegis of Invincible Might, but it's probably way better. Both essentially make you immune to Withering Damage (the effects are slightly different, but both roughly give you 10 soak and then a way to ignore Overwhelming. The major benefit of Aegis here is that it also helps you against Post-Soak Damage, but Lunars have other charms to help prevent that), and 20 Hardness.

                        Their activation requirements are somewhat similar, enough I don't think there's one that's easier then the other. It activates when the Exalt takes a Decisive Attack; for Lunars, they take the damage, for a Solar, they activate Adamant Skin Technique. Neither character can be crashed (as both are Perilous), so they both, realistically, can be activated multiple times per combat without too much issue, since Adamant Skin Technique doesn't actually have a reset condition, while Lunars are excellent at taking some Decisive Damage but making sure it's only a small amount. The only major thing to look at, to me, is the cost; Since Aegis is Permanent, it doesn't have a cost, but Adamant Skin has a cost of 8m, meaning you need to pay 8m to activate Aegis. Wound Mastering Body Evolution is only 6m, 1wp. However, since Adamant Skin is it's own charm, it's not really that different.

                        However, Aegis of Invincible Might has far more requirements; you have to Decisively attack every turn, or pay 8 motes on combat charms, and if you stop making Combat Actions at all, the charm immediately ends. This means the Exalt is either spending a massive amount of Motes per round- surpassing normal mote regen-, or having to make a bunch of Decisive attacks at 3 Initiative. This means they may not be able to penetrate Hardness, or the damage they're doing is very minuscule (we're ignoring the powerful combos with Brawl here, because then we would need to get into how Wound Mastering combines with other Stamina and Strength Charms and the like, and those combos are, I would argue, pretty potent themselves), and that they're at risk of Crash themselves if someone has post-soak damage adders or similar. Meanwhile, the requirement for Wound-Mastering Body Evolution is...you pay 1 Initiative per turn. It just doesn't feel like a fair payment for a charm clearly supposed to be the same style of effect; there's far fewer requirements on the Exalt's actions, and the drip is minuscule, not even costing motes at all. So long as a Lunar can effectively hit a foe, or gain Initiative through dodging or repelling their attacks through their Stamina or Dexterity trees, Wound Mastering Body Evolution puts no requirements on the Lunar to play any differently then they did before, a decision I feel is questionable for an incredibly powerful charm, even if the Solar version is more broad in applying to every attack, while the Lunars only have this effect against a single person.

                        However, I may not be looking at this in the correct way, and I'm curious what others thing about these two charms.

                                                Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                                                And here I was thinking of Xuthal of the Dusk, where the vile amorphous god of titular Xuthal slithered about its city, devouring people when the mood struck it… so the people there turned to the black lotus for escapism, instead of just fleeing the city.

                                                People are weird animals, and even knowing that something could arbitrarily kill someone isn't always enough to get them to leave. My grandpa lives pretty much directly where the wildfires are blazing through California, and no one in my family's been able to persuade him to leave his home.
                                                This is gonna be my last post on this because at this point this thread has been talking about this for the better part of a week, two new previews have come out, and we're still arguing about the Wood Shrine. There's people who think it's dumb and there's people who think it's not, and no one is gonna budge an inch. So I'm just gonna highlight why I think it's dumb one last time and then not talk about it again.

                                                1: One person dying to a person in a dream isn't beyond the pale. The Lottery is a good example of this; it's a singular act for an instant that informs a community. It becomes dumb when it's not 1 person once, but rather 2 times for one person; once 1 person, then again with 5 more. At that point it just comes off as silly and too grim; it's not someone refuisng to move because 'hey, everyone dies in car crashes all the time, whatcha gonna do?', it's 'if we took our city and moved it 5 feet to the left, no one would ever die to a lightning strike ever again. But that's too much effort.'

                                                2: It's easy to ignore or rationalize one person dying to a person hunting them down and ripping their throat out. It's less so when everyone falls into a dream, dreams of being hunted down, then 5 people die. in your fire analogies or whatever else, it's 'well, it sucks for them but at least it didn't happen to me'. in this sequence, it's more like everyone sees their home burn down and you have to hope you're one of the lucky ones whose Insurance policy kicks in and pays out for it.

                                                3: It's boring. All the other shrines have two distinct effects; the challenge and what occurs during it, and the after effect when the pilgrim completes their journey. These are, for every other shrine, completely distinct effects that are totally different and have entirely different reactions. The Wood Shrine does not have this. Instead, the same thing happens again, but it kills more people. This isn't very interesting, and seems to speak of an idea that for a Wood based action, there was difficulty thinking of something interesting happening, and instead they just went 'Blood and Claw!' then paused and went 'BLOOD AND CLAW!', but louder. Why not, instead, when the pilgrim finishes their journey, millions of fantastical flowers bloom across the city, filling it with beautiful sights and smells, before they wither away at the day's end. Or perhaps the nearby animals enter the city without fear, and rather then hunting down humans, celebrate festivities with them, with wolves letting children ride on their backs or boars dancing in the street, and then at the day's end, they return to the wilds and the 'natural' order of the world resumes? These both highlight Wood ideas; the cycle of life, the interaction between Man and Nature, but do so in ways that isn't just 'predator and prey' and then 'predator and prey, but more so'.
