    Originally posted by TSk View Post

    Just like how the Past Life Background is only available to Infernals.
    No, not just like it. Breeding is a rather intrinsic trait of the Terrestrial Exaltation. Past Lives explicitly is the result of a Celestial Exaltation not being scrubbed between time empowering a legacy of heroes. Breeding requires a Terrestrial Exaltation. Please note that its mechanical effects include boosts to Essence pools, which is almost certainly something that you don't want to deal with piling onto other Exalted.

    But I don't know a single ST who doesn't rule otherwise. I wasn't talking about 'raising breeding', anyway. As I said, his breeding should be the same as it would have been before Lunar Exaltation, which is excellent. The idea that Celestial Exaltation wipes away Breeding is, to me, kind of silly. I can understand why it might be put into the books, as a barrier to twinks, but it's the kind of thing responsible players and storytellers are better off just ignoring.
    Mortals don't get Breeding as a Background, so it technically doesn't wipe it away. You can get Terrestrial Heritage, and count as a well-bred patrician, or have no significant measure at all. That's the extent to which those who do not carry enough blood of the dragons to be Exalted carry the blood of the dragons. The Breeding Background denotes that you've taken after a powerful lineage of Terrestrial Exalted, by being, first and foremost, Terrestrial Exalted.

                                Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                And if someone in your game plays an ex-Dynastic Lunar, and he has a child, you do not allow that child any more chance of Terrestrial Exaltation even though it's a member of the Scarlet Dynasty?
                                There are two schools of thought here which kind of mirror the biggest schools of thought for Exalted playstyle. Neither is right or wrong; they're merely different vectors for storytelling and approaches to the game.

                                Simulationist: apply the rules, which in 2e are "roll a die." Lunar might be counted as a patrician with good heritage for purposes of the roll, meaning the child will Exalt on a roll of 1, or 1-2 if the other parent is also a patrician of good heritage. In 1e, there was a table, I think in the Player's Guide, that spelled out the Breeding rolls for DBs, different results in Exalt-Exalt pairings based on the parents' Essence and the primacy of "genetics" if the parents were different kinds of Exalts but equal Essence (Solaroid > Lunar = Sidereal > DB). This is the rule, and it's based on random chance, but that's kind of realistic in the world? A well-bred patrician is still not a Dragon-Blood and even the most well-bred patrician doesn't have as much inherent "genetic" power within them as a "mudblood" Outcaste does, because the fact of the matter is the Outcaste Exalted DB, and the patrician didn't. Even coming from a long line of Dragon-Blooded, a patrician is still not a DB, meaning the Blood of the Dragons didn't catalyze within them to Exalt them Terrestrial, for whatever reason (being superseded by a Celestial Exaltation would do that). Honestly under the rules if you have a former-Realm patrician Lunar and a mortal having a child, that child is much more likely going to be a Lunar Half-Caste, especially if the Lunar is higher Essence.

                                Narrativist: here the main question is "how does the child's ultimately Exalting (or not) serve the story?" Keeping in mind that this question won't be answered for over a decade in-game (I've rarely seen games go that long IC, though timeskips might happen I've only heard of it happening a handful of times; usually a game either successfully ends and this is played out inane epilogue, or dies and the question is never answered). Relevant concerns include: is it more compelling that the Lunar's child Exalts as a DB, or shows himself to be a Half-Caste? What's the player's preference? Even with the player's preference, what is the story the ST wants to explore? That would probably be a thing best discussed early in the game or at chargen, for the ST to have the idea in their head and be able to weave it into their overarching story. By this I mean that a player is free to tell the ST "I would like my kid to ultimately be a DB" but the player is not entitled to that outcome (no more than they are entitled to any plot-based outcome), and the outcome might narratively depend on other factors like the nature of the other parent even if there is not a roll.

                                            Except that's exactly what a patrician is. "Patrician" is shorthand for "Dynastic Great House member who is not Exalted." It's cool if you want to ignore the rules, but when you're talking "why has no Lunar in Creation done this," you're talking about the game, which in part necessitates talking about the rules. Also just because you ignore a rule doesn't mean that everyone or even most people will...

                                            If you want to talk about a narrative perspective, I typed this yesterday, didn't post it, but maybe it's relevant now:

                                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                            I don't think you've ever played a game of Exalted, have you? It's not just the corners. We're told in the 2e core book that everything detailed in all the books is only a small fraction of the entirety of Creation. A small fraction means the vast majority is undescribed. And yeah, that's room for a lot of nations, cultures and states, many of them unique, interesting and powerful. A few non-Realm, non-Lookshy Dragon-Blooded states doesn't necessarily change the shape of Creation anyway.
                                            Such a place as you describe would have to be the corners of the Threshold far from Realm and/or Bronze Faction Sidereal scrutiny (just as a note; the All-Seeing Eye is presumed to be competent, and the Bronze Faction is presumed to have enough interest in the Realm to clandestinely tip them off to something of this nature if they discover it), or it would have to be supernaturally well-hidden (say, like the Caul in 3e). If the Realm were to learn about some new region that had a concentrated number of DBs in it, they would certainly move to try and either form an alliance or outright bring those DBs into their fold. I'm not necessarily talking war (though they might threaten if negotiations didn't go anywhere and they felt threatened somehow) but they'd certainly send spies, envoys, and lavish things to help convince the Lost Egg State that it's in their great interest to be supported by the largest DB nation in Creation.

They could certainly go further if they tried int he Scavenger Lands, because Lookshy would probably prefer alliance over bringing them into the Legion structure, but Lookshy still doesn't like Anathema.
