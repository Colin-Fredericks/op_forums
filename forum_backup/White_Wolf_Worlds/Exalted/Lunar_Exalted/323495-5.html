    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    There were people as educated as Einstein who never could have figured out Relativity.
    I think someone else with his "Physics" Ability could've though (to the extent that Attributes and Abilities as realistic things which operate similarly to Exalted's even exist, which is not much).

    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    There are educated people who lack a capacity for innovation and problem solving that less educated people are applying towards factors in their own context that may be less technically intricate but that are still improved upon by them. A fairly typical person can be taught how to assemble and service a spacecraft, while somewhere in the world there's a super genius of a subsistence farmer figuring out how to turn their limited resources towards notably improving crop yields or saving on labour. It's the difference between building a modern skyscraper and building Newgrange. It doesn't really have any pertinance to Exalted, but it's a good thing to keep in mind. for life in general.
    Kind of agree. I don't disagree with the general thrust. Although with the specific example of the subsistence farmer I think it is hard for me to imagine any sort of supergenius who wants to literally apply his genius in full, to perfecting the art of subsistence farming. Even if he's being compelled to do it, there's a certain point at which even a genius (of any sort of personality I find easy to imagine, which isn't ridiculously psychologically limited) goes "It's just subsistence farming ffs".

    And if they're not being compelled* (by circumstances economic or direct pressure), and have any wherewithal to use their intellect, I expect they tend to be pretty soon not be doing subsistence farming.

    Either the mismatch of talent to task resolves itself, or it (sadly) doesn't but either way I feel like no one is investing exorbitant amounts of intellect in obsessively perfect versions of commonplace tasks.

    *on being compelled I think even very hierarchical societies of limited resources tend to go "Obvious supergenius? We'll have some of that where we can best use it". *If* they're able to recognize it at all, which is probably much harder when lots of people's ability is only being tested against problems with a relatively low ceiling or a low power to be able to distinguish the quality of solutions (e.g. because there are too many uncontrolled factors).

                    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                    Hey I'm not asking why doesn't the Lunar have Ability Charms, but whether using an Attribute Charm to substitute for not knowing an Ability will appeal to many character concepts or is a good logic overall.
                    A Dexterity attack Charm does not substitute for Melee or any other attack Ability. But it needs to be able to function even when you have absolutely no training whatsoever, or else it's just an Ability Charm posing as an Attribute Charm.

                    An Attribute Charm should never NEED an Ability score, though the best should still benefit from an Ability score.

                    Avoiding Silver Solar Intelligence Charms is really hard unless you design against the possibility that the Lunar has absolutely no dots in Lore, Investigation or whatever Ability is relevant to the Charm's purpose. In fact, designing with that goal in mind should definitely produce sufficiently inhuman results that lend towards a uniquely "Lunar" experience.


                    By contrast, Ability Charms may always assume that the Exalt has at least one dot in the relevant Attribute, because there are generally never any rolls which do not combine (Attribute + Ability) for a dice-pool.

                                        the problem of Lunar Charms, that you seem to be talking about Sunder, is that its kind of hard in general to figure out what Intelligence is absent of education. we-as a civilization- don't have many examples, because we associate education and intelligence together pretty strongly. mostly because the most intelligent characters and heroes we know are probably well-educated to show how intelligent they are, because the things you get education on, are probably pretty complex.

                                        while the inverse, a Lore 5, Intelligence 1 character would be someone who is very slow to figure things out, but has trained himself to memorize so much information and such that he seems smart because he worked really hard to be able to memorize and retain all of it, and thus would be really dependent upon what he already knows to fix the problem.

                                        so I guess a lot of Lunar Intelligence charms by such a definition would be rapidly figuring things out in their head from what information they already know. knowing more stuff would be a problem for Perception charms.
