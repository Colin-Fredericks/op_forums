    [EX3] Lunar Physicality - Tricksters & Shamans

    11-09-2013, 08:10 PM
    Among other biggies, we have two big facts from the devs about EX3 Lunars:
    1) They're going to be very big on physicality, and
    2) The archetypes embodied by their Castes are Warrior, Trickster, and Shaman (Castes presumably map to Full, Changing, and No Moons; no statement on the Attribute layouts).

    The former is important to highlight. This holds firmly enough that illusions are explicitly something Lunars don't do.* Given the second point, this came as a shock: illusions are a big tool in the toolbox for Tricksters and Shamans I tend to imagine. What Warriors will be up to doesn't puzzle me; physicality fits them like a glove, so they're not the focus of this thread.

    * I presume there may be effects that skirt this: I quite like the idea of a Lunar so persuasive his marks will actually see whatever he claims is there to see. It could be debateably more amusing if everyone so persuaded still sees what is actually there, but believes everyone else sees something enough to doubt their own senses, playing along and agreeing the emperor does indeed have clothes -- whether or not an emperor is even present.

    It also raises other questions. Is physicality meant to describe all Lunar Charms or is it just a prominent, prevailing trait? e.g. Can a Lunar just be that amazingly strong/smart/charming/whatever for an effect, or must there be some change worked for it? And how far can physicality be stretched, here: is it only about changing the Lunar herself, or can the Lunar work physical changes on the world and living things around her, such as with Insidious Lunar Transformation? Does stuff like Wasp of the Labyrinth Trick still have a home?

    The non-combat examples we've gotten have so far included fun with pheremones, glorious god-bodies, and brain modification. There's no telling yet if this means their Charms are all and only about changes, and then only to the Lunar. Though it does suggest such things are at least quite/most common in the set.

    I trust the devs know what they're doing and have no shortage of cool stuff for Tricksters and Shamans to get up to. I don't think they'd pick a paradigm that hangs those Castes out to dry for the third time in a row (barring broken mechanics). I just have a lot of blanks at the moment. Changing Moons and No Moons were kind of wanting for cool tricks before, and many of what few they had don't seem to fit anymore; I can't right now think of other cool stuff that might be taking its place.

    So, how about it, everybody? What do you think or want the bounds of Lunar physicality to be? And within that paradigm, what sort of awesome ideas do you have for Tricksters and Shamans to break out? Inquiring minds want to know!

    Inquiring minds will also not turn away further clarifications/examples from the team. >_> ... Please?

                      Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                      You're thinking backward. Define a physical change, and then think of the benefits that it would produce. Don't think of the benefits first.
                      Growing additional brains is a particular one I dislike, just because it seems far too Tyranid.

                      When I think about it, I realise I have two basic standards of inspiration for Lunars; shapeshifting and totem/anthropomorphism mythology. When it comes to implementation in the context of Exalted, for the former, that generally means changing from one thing into another, either in whole or in part, or kind of stretching out or rearranging what you already have. For the latter, sometimes, occasionally, it means adopting some resonant animal traits or mechanisms, but mostly it means adopting the logic of why totem mythology is used, and reapplying it to people. Like, Renard is a fox not because he does anything that necessitates the biology of a fox, but because the idea of fox conveys his persona. So just think of what signifiers in human forms convey personas, at least in our collective narratives, and imagine the same basic appearance shifting through those signifiers. Like the difference between Superman and Clark Kent.
