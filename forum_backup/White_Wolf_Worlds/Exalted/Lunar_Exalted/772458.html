I'm hoping that this will be roughly as well-known as terrifying argent witches was, considering its fame. But at the end of the day, here's what I have. Feel free to check up on it whenever you like, and I'll make an additional post here when it's well and fully done! Also feel free to ask questions.

                              I'm trying to focus on the practicalities of the Lunar Exalted, providing a framework for people to play them as usable characters. Giving setting information, as well as mechanical information. It's mostly a supplement to what's in core, and a few hooks here and there. Thematically, I want them to exist as iconoclasts. The solars are kinda mainstream in some ways, they're the big fellas who are popular and shiny. Not so with the Lunars, while they do have places where they're popular they don't naturally exist within the mainstream of civilization and society. If there's a place where they fit like a glove, it's likely a war dominion that they've engineered themselves. As said in the writeup, I want Lunars to exist in duality to the themes of the solar exalted. Embodying the positive aspects that they are not. Where the solars are strong consistency, they are adaptability that can fill the gaps. Where solars are refined skill, they are raw power. They're the other side of the solar coin, but the solars are the other side of the lunar coin equally.

                                      Yep. Adaptability charms (One of three charm sub-categories) focus on boosting general capability, and filling holes in where the lunar's shapeshifting might falter. Protean charms focus on externalized shapeshifting, and Primal charms focus on playing up the connections to life and nature. What used to be knacks are mostly relegated to Protean and Adaptability charms, as the latter are generally more passive bonuses and the former are generally more active uses.

                                      Playing a lunar is supposed to feel weird. Unusual. It's supposed to feel like you're the one always outside of society, but always intermingling with it. These aren't the individuals who attempt to mold the world into their image as an end, when they do something resembling this in the form of war dominions it's very much a means. This is where they ultimately differ from the solar exalted: The solar exalted and particularly the Infernal Exalted (If I had my druthers, anyway) are world-changers. The lunars on the other hand, are more subtle and more effective. Yes, a lunar can try to be a conquering warlord and bring hell to the Realm and its satrapies. But really, a lunar who embraces their lunar-ness will know that they do that not for personal goals and wants, but as an agent of revolution pursuing an ideal. They're all about chasing that ideal, that intangible principle whereas solars are about chasing that concrete goal and powerful vision for the world.

                                              I'm not super-keen on the chasing-an-ideal angle, but the rest sounds decent. And Lunars are incoherent enough that no two visions are ever going to match.

                                              Anyway, I looked over the writeup. Skimmed the fluff, read the crunch a bit more carefully. My comments:

                                              -I like the "Chimera are vulnerable to moonsilver" bit.
                                              -Linking Abilities to faction seems a bit pointless.
                                              -Given Ex3's more-flexible approach to favouring so far...maybe characters should favour 2 Caste Attributes and 2 Attributes of their choice.
                                              -I think you should be allowed to be a Silver Pact member with little real hate for their enemies. They're not gonna turn away Lunars for being insufficiently hateful, are they?
                                              -Dunno about the Full and Changing Moon animas. They appear weak and strong respectively, and they seem Charm-ish to me. The No Moon anima's cool though.
                                              -Favors is an interesting background, but I really don't think you should limit people to canon NPCs.
                                              -Heart's Blood seems weak. A Merit dot is worth 3xp; while killing a tiger is an accomplishment, I wouldn't spend 9xp to avoid bothering.
                                              -I like Mask.
                                              -Blazing Pace seems too strong to me.
                                              -Entanglement is pretty cool, but can't you already grapple with your bare hands?
                                              -I like the weaker versions of Inhuman Palate, but the 5-dot version feels weak.
                                              -The last sentence before the Charms section doesn't make much sense.
                                              -I don't think Spine-Breaking Technique makes a good root for a Charm tree.
                                              -Burrowing Devil Strike looks insanely powerful. Heart-Seeking Fang might also be a bit much, I dunno.
                                              -I don't think Shattered Carapace Triumph should use Resources rating as a difficulty. Expensive =/= durable.
                                              -In general, this tree is too linear. Which naturally pushes the Strength prereqs up to an unfortunate level, cuts down on choices, and makes people buy Charms they don't want.

They're too brute force and/or raw power for finesse and social niceties.
