    Originally posted by FromBeyond View Post
    Ok, let's slow down for a bit here. I know that Lunars should be able to jump really high, punch really hard, be very persuasive or smart and so on and so on, but I want to know why are they able to do those things.

    Like, with Solars I know that they can punch people relly hard, because they're very good at punching people. I know that a Dragon Blooded can jump 20ft high, because he can move like the air. I know a Sidereal can solve difficult mystery case, because he can gain impossible insight from the stars.

    But why is Lunar punching you hard? Why is he able to dodge like the water? Why is he speaking in such a way that you're compelled by his words?

    Part of me wants to say: Lunars punch hard, simply because they're really freakin' strong. They can dodge and dance gracefully, because they're simply really agile. At least, that's what I instinctively imagine being atribute-based Exalts means for Lunars.

    But how do you begin at "being magically strong/agile/resistant/charismatic/manipulative/attractive/smart/cunning/perspective" and arrive at changing into a flock of ravens.

    I just can't draw the connection. I'm left in the dark, nebulous place where I have a good start in the form of baseline atributes and a finish in the form of shapeshifting, but I just can't see the middle. If I can't see the middle, I can hardly come up with examples that are fluid and coherent, not just random ideas out of nowhere.

    Honestly, I wonder if it wouldn't make more sense to leave charms as expressions of atributes and change Lunar shapeshifting into astrology-like gimmick only available to Lunars. At least there would be a clear reasoning behind it.
    I don't think it needs to be seen as so incoherent. A lot of (maybe most?) Lunar Attribute charms are actually shapeshifting effects, including most of the combat charms. Hide-Toughening Essence, Armor-Forming Technique, Invulnerable Moonsilver Carapace are soak charms, and all involve shapeshifting. The healing charms seem either explicitly shapeshifting or could be explained as shapeshifting. Claws of the Silver Moon is definitely shapeshifting. Perception charms like Eyes of the Cat can also be explained as shapeshifting if you want to.

    If a Lunar punches really hard or jumps really high, why can't you just assume it's because he's shaped her muscles into steel? That's always been my assumption. The general theme is that Lunars adapt themselves; whatever they do, they do through change and adaptation.

    Of course, being Exalted by the power of a Celestial Incarna does give you certain divine power all by itself, regardless of whatever shapeshifting powers you might also acquire.

    Originally posted by Jen View Post
    I'm more concern about how the Obvious keyword manifest in Lunar charms, Infernal/DB/Sidereal have a bunch of different kind of "sparkles" to choose from (red wind, green fire, stone, flower, christmas tree,...) while Lunar can only glow silver, even Abyssal and Solar have more special effect than Lunar, and Solar is the King of Blandness !
    Dunno what you mean exactly, but there's room for a lot of personalization in terms of special effects. Demonstrative bragging technique: the only character I'm currently playing has an anima banner materializing as white petals surrounding a stylized coyote amid shifting patterns of light and darkness. The petals are of night-blossoming rain lily (Cooperia pedunculata), and materialize whenever his anima is at the 8+ level or he uses any Charm with the Obvious keyword. His Tell is their lingering scent, always present but rarely noticed.

    The latter fluff is because my ST has permitted me to use the Past Life Background normally limited to Infernals, representing the character's inheritance of the soul and the exaltation of a powerful elder. When the Background effect kicks in, the coyote disappears entirely and is replaced by a burning white rain lily in the air, the penumbra of light and shadow becomes a steady canescent blaze, he changes from male to female and his caste goes from No Moon to Full Moon.

    Originally posted by Jen View Post
    Oh, I was talking more about Charms than Anima's color though. Claw of the SILVER Moon, Purging the Tarnished SILVER, Invulnerable MoonSILVER Caparace,....etc.
    Well, silver is a nice color. It's well suited for stuff happening at night - which is another theme of Lunars that we don't hear much about. And it looks nice if the game is happening in your head as a black and white comic.

                                    Originally posted by Ars Fan View Post
                                    That's not a retcon.

                                    The legendary account about the origins of sorcery way back in in the Book of Three Circles (the first edition magic supplement published in September 2001) has Brigid approached by a "forest-spirit" whose description matches the second-circle demon Mara (from the first edition Storyteller's Companion), and the whole situation pretty much matches the summoning parameters for Mara (someone crying over their lost/separated love, shedding tears into a spring, in the forest; a shooting star appears, which is also a sign that Mara is coming). The whole thing fits together all too well to be accidental.
                                    Sure, it implies that Mara is one of the many diverse spirits involved in Brigid's journey to enlightenment. Demons are part of the rich tapestry of Creation's magic.

                                    But she isn't the entire story, and nothing in there implies that she's any more meaningful to the overarching story of Sorcery than anyone else Brigid met in her journey. Neph fixated on her appearance in the story and ignored the fact that Brigid had to make contact with every other aspect of Creation's magic as well. (Again, part of the story involves an extended stay with a lord of the Fair Folk, which makes no sense if you go by 2e's version.) The way I read it is always "Brigid's story had to touch on all of the aspects of creation's magic, which includes demons; but we will be subtle about demons because they have heavier implications than the others and not everyone will want to recognize that they have an important role in Creation's supernatural landscape."

                                    2e retconned that to "the story of Brigid is about how she was handed the secrets of Sorcery by Mara and the rest of her journey was not important", which is not at all what was present in the original. Mara's (implied) visit with Brigid is one chapter in a larger narrative of how she attained enlightenment; but the 1e story is not at all focused on Mara or the Primordials the way 2e retconned it.

                                    1e's version is a subtle (allegorical) story about a journey to enlightenment which makes the statement that Sorcery is broad and mysterious and transcends any one aspect of the universe to encompass the underlying truth of the whole; 2e's version is, basically, that the Yozis are broad and mysterious and transcendent and therefore gave this Sorcery thing to the Exalted for their own reasons. One is a story about how awesome sorcery is, and the other is a story about how awesome the Yozis are.

                                        The story of the Loathly Lady seems like an excellent vehicle for communicating the "Beauty and Ugliness" that I see as a characteristic of Lunar Exalted.

                                        Solar Exalted are the purest beauty, always beautiful. Abyssals are hideous; they can play at beauty, but it's a disturbing beauty that doesn't truly hide what's wrong with them. Infernals are terrible in their beauty.

                                        I think Lunars are the ones capable of being tremendously ugly yet also wholesomely beautiful. Not the tainted beauty of the Infernals, the fake beauty of the Abyssals, or the constant beauty of the Solar Exalted. When the Lunars are beautiful, they are as lovely as the Solars, but when they get ugly, they get as ugly as the worst.

                                        The lady and the monster. The ugly witch, the beautiful beast. The light and dark sides of the moon.

                                        The story of the Loathly Lady also provides a plot through which to tie that duality with the concept of the Solar Mate, or the more general idea of the one person or people around whom a Lunar centers her identity.

It's a very different one if such fear and attraction are respectively inspired by a li'l baby bunny and a nonagenarian covered with filth.
