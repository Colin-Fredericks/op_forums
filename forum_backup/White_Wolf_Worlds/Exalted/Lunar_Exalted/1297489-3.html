                    Okay, sorry it's been a while I had to re-read basically all of the Great Houses and the new Dominions to do the comparison, and since it's less math it's less obvious. To me anyway. Also I didn't really get to talk about the fluff aspects of WFHW because there weren't any to compare to, so this is my chance there.

                    Isolation vs Integreation

                    The first thing that stuck me as I re-read these chapters was that every great house chapter has a section on their enemies and alliances, entwining every great house in a complex web of relationships. Dominions, on the other hand, don't mention eachother at all.

                    I think that this can offer a really interesting play experience, as well as comment on how the different Exalted work. For one, most dominions are ruled over by a single Lunar, or sometimes a Shahan-ya and their small school. I think this can lead Lunar characters into a really cool and more personal and close experience in their games. Like in a Dynastic game, you're a small fish, there's this enormous power above and beside you in your House, and all that is attached to the great Superpower that is the Scarlet Empire. Without just going off on a Ledaal Shadow Crusade or some other kind of safari, it's nearly inescapable.

                    For Dynasts you have elders, your elders have elders, you have superior military officers, and monks who are further along the path of enlightenment. This is both a blessing and a curse. It makes for a position where a sister or a cousin is rarely far away from your help, and vice versa. You have a relatively easy back channel to Creation spanning networks of trade, and wealth and spies and soldiers. Assuming you can navigate the complex bureaucracy and ties of loyalty of course.

                    On the other hand some of your greatest enemies come from within, with that same access except centuries more experience and time to cultivate it. The immense size also makes it difficult for a small group of Dynasts to affect any major change on their own. If you're looking to abolish slavery in the Realm that's a kind of insurmountable task that most won't even attempt.


                    If you're a Lunar in a dominion though, that's a very different story. For one, you're the big fish. Apart from your Shahan-ya, if you even have one, there is nobody above you. Even if you DO have a Shahan-ya, that's where it stops. Outside of informal respect for elders or great heroes of renown there is nobody above them. Depending on your particular dominion you very likely tread the line between prince and god. Part of this comes from the singular power of the Lunar Exalted. Like Solars a Lunar is powerful enough to prop up an entire nation on her own, or at least be a defining person within it.

                    There's also a freedom in that isolation, which Lunars are adept at creating. Some are so far away from the Realm, or simply hidden, as to not deal with it at all. Relatively safe from the threats that living in an imperial satrapy might force upon you, these dominions take off basically the only shackles on Lunar freedom. They're your own little world to play in and refine, or strike out from at your leisure. The closest thing we get to the Alliances and Enemies section is Neighbors, most of whom are not remotely as powerful as the forces of the dominion that you live in.


                    Resistance Cells, Families and More

                    What that above kind of leads into is that Lunar dominions, at least the Silver Pact ones, are resistance cells on a giant scale. They share no central leadership, often even within the dominion, and so can't have their hierarchy disrupted. They also each act in their own, unique way, which means you can't codify any strategy for fighting them. Unlike most resistance cells though, that can live entirely within the place they're fighting, many Lunar dominions are so large as to become empires themselves.

                    The Empire of the Bear, for example, is so large and powerful it basically shucks off the feeling of being a resistance cell. Unlike other places, like the Mountain of the Spider King, which feels like a refuge and rebel base to fight the Realm, the Empire of the Bear feels like a grand nation who's main concerns are internal stability, expansion and diplomacy. That kind of lets a Lunar player do a game a lot closer to what we would expect a Realm game to look like.

                    Others are places like Skandhar-Bhal, which I really liked. It's like a Lunar Shangri-la or Shambala. I really like the refreshing feeling that it's not all about a war with the Realm, and is just a place that anyone can go to see enlightenment and peace. This is the kind of place that's either perfect to become beset by an invasion, threatening their gentle home with violence of the outside world, or one where you can run a game completely removed from the very same.



                    I think that's all I have to say about the Dominions chapter and how it relates to the different ways Lunars play and feel in their areas, as opposed to Dragonblooded and their institutions. I think since there seems to be more of an interest in it, my next post will be about Lunar combat power and abilities. At first glace though they pretty well roll over Dragonblooded, and with shapeshifting seem to be more than a match for any non-Dawn Solar.

                                                        Okay, sorry for the late update, let me explain.

                                                        Comparing DBs to Solars was really easy, I read the archery tree for each and compare the two, or I get an overall feel for their abilities and contrast where their strengths are. For Lunars, obviously, they don't have an archery tree. So then I thought "Okay, I'll do it the Lunar way. I'll read all the Lunar combat stuff and compare to the DB and Solar combat trees, that'll work. Except no, not really, because remember before when I said that having to have only a few dice adders and such meant the developers were free to go crazy on cool other unique abilities that didn't just add damage? Well they did, so Lunar combat trees are full of distract gambits and poison and all sorts of stuff that changes the game, so I'm actually going to have to divide this down even further. So with that in mind...


                                                        Offense

                                                        Accuracy/Defense

                                                        So, something that I mentioned in my DB review, is that DBs are missing a lot of the non-charm accuracy boosters that Solars have, keeping their defense and to-hit relatively bounded by their dicecaps. Lunars do not suffer this problem, at all. A lot of them are more stealthy options though. For example there's 2 charms in essence 1 alone that allow rerolling of failed dice, up to Essence or higher of 3/Essence. Through the charmset though they tend to be a bit more conditional than the Solar versions. So where a Solar will just have excellent strike, and reroll 1s until they no longer appear, a Lunar will reroll (essence or 3) 1s and ignore a point of defense granted by cover, weapons, or full defense.

                                                        That's actually a theme in the Lunar charmset, the tools that you have made won't help you. Not walls, nor weapons, nor forged steel plates.

                                                        The same kinds of things happen for Lunar defensive options. Actually, they have a lot of charms here for punishing people for missing them. Each time you attack a Lunar and don't hit, she gets stronger and you get weaker, sapping your initiative, and boosting her defense. It makes them feel really monsterous, but not in like, an evil way necessarily. It's just that once somebody strikes at you, and fails, terror takes hold because they know they'll never get as good as shot as that one again, and they couldn't even make that one.

                                                        There's also some sick interplay that only Lunars could ever do, like adding Parry to Evasion, or visa versa, for a charm. Because when you're a Lunar the motto is "You don't have to choose, pick both." Another good charm that exemplifies this is Steel Paw Style, which lets you calculate your parry with your Stamina instead of your Dex, so that's awesome for big bruiser types who might not want to invest in the best stat in the game. There's also some capbursters here, like sapping successes from your opponent's attack roll.


                                                        I feel like I actually need to stop and talk about how important this accuracy thing is, mechanically. To make myself a little more sure about this I ran a few test combats with Lunars and Solars/DBs. Because there's so much Lunar stuff and I'm running these on my own, I used Essence 1 Lunars, Essence 1 Solars, and Essence 5 DBs. Also I assumed everyone had max combat stats and a mix of charms available to them at their essence. I also gave everyone ox-bodies, usually in the range of 3. The combats went like so

                                                        Lunar Brawler vs Solar Brawler
                                                        Lunar Swordsman vs Solar Swordsman
                                                        Lunar Swordsman vs DB Swordsman
                                                        Lunar Brawler vs DB Brawler
                                                        Lunar Bear Grappler vs Water Dragon Master

                                                        Bolded above are the victors. I was pleasantly surprised with the Lunars and the Solars actually, they seem really well matched. Both times the Solar won initiative, but that was pure luck. Also the basically free stuff like Rising Sun Slash put them just barely ahead in the swordsman matchup, and they just didn't get as lucky as a brawler, he missed his Heaven Thunder Hammer that was supposed to end the fight, and...well then the fight got ended anyway. In retrospect I maybe should have tried Hammer on Iron instead, but sometimes that's just how it goes.

                                                        With the DBs, I am proud to say, it is just as I suspected. Since you have to declare and spend all your charms before you attack/defend, you stand a decent chance of wasting a lot of motes on an attack that won't hit, where the Lunar didn't have that problem, she can boost the crap out of her damage and be fairly confidant that it will help. So not only are you hitting reliably and not wasting motes on your damage boosters, but you're also not getting hit reliably, and forcing your opponent to waste motes on charms that never activate. Against the Water Dragon master she never even got hit once. He just wiffed two attacks, and she decisive savaged him turn 2, and ended it with a throw turn 3. Shrugging Water-Dragon Escape was woefully inadequate next to the Crushing Bear Hug from the bear, and Grizzly Bear Embrace charm.


                                                        Unfortunately I'm out of time to go over damage and soak, but after that it should be done with the least interesting parts of base Lunar combat. After that I'm going to look at their super cool tricks, a lot that have to do with disarming, distracting and defend other-ing. I'm sure many other things that start with D as well.

                                                            For me the Lunars charms seem to be slightly less powerful than their counterparts Solars (when there is a charm with which they can be compared) but more flexible.
                                                            A Solar specialized in melee is excellent in that field but a Lunar specialized in that field can easily become an Archer or fight unarmed with the same charms or quzias acquiring only a couple more. While the Solar can not do anything similar.
                                                            In combat I think that is where Solars and Lunars are more even, with a slight advantage towards the Solars, but it is really very small, a bad roll and it is over for the Solar, although that is equally true for the Lunar.
                                                            Although I personally believe that the Solars are superior in Archery and Thrown, having trees of charms dedicated to that gives them a versatility in ranged combat that the Lunars do not have, although that is no problem for a Lunar that can leave aside the ranged combat and look for body to body where you will have an advantage, given the versatility of your charms, on the Solar specialist.

                                                            That the combats were 50% was to be expected and demonstrates the good work that has been done in calculating balance the powers of Solars and Lunars. The Dragon-Blooded as always must fight in a group.
                                                            Although supernatural is very powerful by itself, but seeing the examples maybe less than I thought, although I guess its effects are more noticeable in more experienced characters when a Solar in Essence 1 or 2 already accesses Charms Essence 4 or much sooner than other types of exalted.
                                                            Also influences that in Essence 1 and 2 the Lunars have more nicknames than a Solar, more or less the same in Essence 3 and less in Essence 4 or more.

                                                            Some build Lunars or some of the Lunar charms that seemed to me more powerful also had certain vulnerabilities to certain Solar charms that allowed to deny them or to reduce considerably their advantage. good for them because they need every advantage they can get in a fight against a Lunar, as it should be.

                                                            Create Lunars with the right level of power to almost equal the Solars, but being a centimeter behind, which is compensated with its greater versatility, was something that seemed really difficult to achieve but I would say that it has been achieved while at the same time Lunars feel very different from the Solars. When you compare the charms of both you have a very positive feeling.
