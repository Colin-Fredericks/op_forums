    I love Raiton's Dark Auspice.

    It's just so... straightforward. No motes or willpower required. Just a small bonus that doesn't require activation.
    I assume there are equivalents for other kinds of spirits, I hope they're also permanent.

    I also like to see that, unlike previous editions, No Moons as Shamans is properly supported. In 2nd ed, while you got some stuff about the Wyld, there was no social stuff. Social stuff was for Changing Moons. But this edition happily hands you Social benefits against spirits despite being the Mental Primary Caste, because the point is not that you're the clever-clogs Caste, it's that you're the Nightwitches, the shamans who deal with gods, ghosts and demons. (Not to mention charms that indirectly help you socially against spirits, by telling you stuff about them that will help you know what they want.)
    I noticed there were several Lunar XP conditions for No Moons that were actually social-based (with a small "s"), but specifically about spirits. Making peace between spirits and human communities, that kind of thing.

    I like God-Skinning Hunter Art as an expression of how Lunar craft is, well, Lunar, as opposed to just giving bonuses to crafting artefacts of Moonsilver like a Solar would.
    I hope there are other charms that entail incorporating furs, hides, fangs, etc, though I guess there's a limit to that as charms need to not be too similar.

    As both my current and retired Lunar PCs favour Intelligence, I'm intrigued to see the Intelligence charms. They were very limited in 2nd ed (you mostly took it for Sorcery), and now they presumably have mostly lost mental defence to Wits (which is what Resolve is based on). So I'm expecting to see plenty of cool, new stuff.

                        Okay, looking forward to seeing the Dexterity Charms on a few fronts; getting a better sense of the Full Moon, some more direct Lunar combat power, and any more twisty weird stuff.

                        Skipping straight over the already seen Heart’s Blood Charm and… oh right, Emerald Grasshopped Form also qualifies under that header, that makes sense. I actually think the idea of needing really high Dexterity to do that lends itself to a fun image of needing to be really flexible to compress your flesh and Essence into something Miniscule. I think the high cost both lends characterisation and a bit of balance, while effectively offset by waiving it for going between forms directly. And yeah, makes sense to make it Totemic for characters with a Miniscule spirit shape.

                        There’s a follow-up with Tyrant Mouse Dominion, allowing the reduction of any other shape (explicitly including human ones) to Miniscule size, being able to take moonsilver Artifacts down with you. Becoming a shrunk down human isn’t really for me, but I know there are other people that value it and can’t begrudge it to them. Mind, some of the descriptions of the limitations (I’m presuming this is generally how the Merit works) alleviate a couple of concerns, particularly with the limits of dmage.

                        Okay, Offensive Charms. Start with Finding the Needle’s Eye, a somewhat basic enhancement attack that still lends some flavour with the combination of rerolling dice and ignoring defensive bonuses. This one is actually of interest to me particularly, because it might provide a model for how a moonsilver powerbow based on flexibility in its limbs works, a thing I’ve tried to figure out in the past. Anyway, being in the form of an appropriately flexible animal provides a bit of extra Defence mitigation.

                        Coyote-and-Badger Tactic feels to me like another thing effectively melding animal imagery, Lunar deftness and denial of enemy advantage in its use of distract gambits to remove enemy Initiative, and I get a particularly strong sense of the Protean benefit coming through in assuming a shape that is more inherently suited to the Charm’s function, the idea of making yourself something that can act that way better.

                        I like how Weapon-Snatching Coils describes its effect (for three guesses what) in terms of either being uncannily twisty or actually springing tendrils, just to encourage either approach to a player’s tastes. I particularly like the Protean image of being something able to grasp and fling away a weapon with a tail, although for some reason my mind keeps fixating on an iguana. Striking Mospid Method has less to show in the basic description of rerolling dice for an attack roll, but that’s all right, and even a bit desirable; some stuff just needs to be good Dexterity.

                        Foe-Driving Attack is similar, although it has a bit of personality in the idea of pressing on an onslaught, a bit of characteristic fury. And then starting up again with Many-Armed Warrior Panoply… hmm, they finally did come back with something to let attunement for multiple Artifact weapons be reduced. I wonder if this is something that might fit for Solars, or if it gets its leeway from the whole cross-weapons thing of an Attribute Charm. I’ll want to think more about this one, although I’ve no problem with it for itself.

                        Needle Quill Technique conveys an image I’ve always liked to associate with Lunars, converting a body part into effective ammunition. If there was going to ever be a realisation of bombardier beetle imagery in a Lunar Charm, I think that being able to use caustic secretions as Flame weapon ammunition from Bombardier Spittle Alchemy is fitting and reasonable.

                        Hmm, what kind of nasty thing might Stinging Ichneumon Scourge do… huh, not as bad as I was thinking, just letting the self-made projectiles to burrow or chase an enemy to keep the onslaught penalty on them for a few rounds… ahh, a decisive attack is the one most distinctly conveying the idea of it embedding, thus allowing it to last longer. Makes sense.

                        Right, there’s Sinuous Striking Grace to model heightened instinct and awareness in being able to add Dexterity or Wits to determine Initiative order, and get a little attack bonus if you’re going for somebody who hasn’t acted yet, a nice predatory aspect to it.

                        Hmm, probably going to be a few Dexterity Charms that provide basic necessary functions… what am I saying, it’s still only Essence 1, plenty of room to grow.

                        Okay, bomb through them a bit… Cunning Beast-Warrior Reflexes provides improvements to flurrying a physical action with something, Wasp Sting Blur… shuts me right up with an attack that gets bonus dice and a reasonable possibility of making a follow up decisive attack that calculates damage by the target’s onslaught and ignores Hardness, that sounds pretty nifty.

                        Aaaand into Essence 2 already. Maybe I’ll come out with more than I planned.

                        Consumptive Worm Hungers is a fulfilling my expectations of an upgrade to the Ichneumon Charm, modelling an even more aggressive persistent attack by other repeatedly draining Initiative based on the Lunar’s original damage roll, or repeatedly rolling a Hardness-ignoring damage die. I like the extended hunter element of this, even if the gambit to remove an embedded quill isn’t very difficult and so any unmolested person or animal could probably manage it. Still, keep the pressure on, and it might wear down an enemy or victim something nasty. If nothing else, it might increase the perceived necessity to direct attention and Initiative at the quill.

                        Twin Fangs Strike… I recognise the name, so I’m really compelled to compare effects… hmm, closest thing before seems to have been Twin-Fang Technique, in which you got to attack with two weapons in a row. I think this pack hunter thing in which the beneficiary of a distract gambit gets to make their attack immediately. It synergizes with Deadly Wolf-Pack Onslaught, which really builds on the image with being able to make one distraction that can benefit multiple allies, as well as its per-scene reset of making a decisive attack on somebody under heavy onslaught, and probably something particularly strong in the Protean effect to provide Initiative to allies who are crashed.

                        Go back to something more straightforward with Hunter’s Eye Precision to let a ranged attack be aimed reflexively (which is obviously Totemic with Perception, for when your spirit shape is known for keen eyesight). Birds-Fall-From-Flock Targeting does something a bit more interesting in permanently endowing a Lunar with the capacity to aim at multiple nearby people at once, allowing flexibility in who they opt to shoot, while I find the surcharge it allows to attacks in which you roll first and then choose who you’re actually going for, with the particularly nice idea of forcing a bunch of potential targets to defend themselves before knowing whether the blow will land on them or not. I like that element of unpredictability and trickery there.

                        All right, full version of Octopus-and-Spider Barrage. I think it’s ultimately stronger than the QC one, an across the board multi-attack, can be done with any Ability per attack, reflexively change weapons, and with up to a number of attacks one higher than the character’s Essence, to a total of five. Initiative is still divided, but unlike a lot of similar attacks it doesn’t ignore Hardness; considering the potential size and the flexibility, I’d say that’s a fair cop. Oh, actually, it also lets a chosen number of those attacks by levelled on the one opponent, and the Protean effect lets successful attacks add dice to the following ones… yeah, I’d say this one can be pretty strong if used under the right circumstances. I like how this one gets the root concept of the original idea, while also not tying it directly to using each limb (either obligating a human form to use their feet or making an actual octopus or spider overpowered).

                        Aaaand last Charm for the time-being, Unerring Fang Technique. Full Excellencies on withering attacks get to add a non-Charm success, and extra dice for every ten they roll. That’s just gravy.

                        Yes, I think these are off to a good start.

                            Ehhh, screw it. I can finish off the Offence Dexterity Charms.

                            Okay, Essence 3 starting with Agitation-of-the-Swarm Technique.

                            I remember, ages ago, at one of the earliest times of postulating on Lunar Third Edition Charms once a baseline was established with Solars, I speculated that in comparison to Solar Charms that entailed more complex processes, Lunar Charms might be uniformly more direct and singular. It’s been interesting to see ways in which that was ultimately off the mark, with how many have gone for effects layered on top of each other. In this case, a sufficiently effective decisive attack can forgo a bit of damage (a limitation that can eventually be overcome) in exchange for instantly disarming or unhorsing the target. When I look at this one, even though it’s not in the description, I get the impression of Lunars at least looking like they move in ways that are really weird, if it’s not outright stunted to include some manifestation of a bodily transformation to push the additional effect along.

                            Toothless Pride Tactic… I actually feel as though Lunars are consistently engaging an element of having advantages to mitigate being outnumbered, if not actually being better there, so I’ll also eat crow on the at least one occasion where Third Edition White Reaper Style had no direct resonance with them. The image here, of a disarm gambit that can be applied against multiple people, including at range and against whole battle groups… quite apart from the tactical value, just picturing scenarios in which it would be used… a Lunar surrounded by a gang of toughs trying to intimidate her, only for her to blur briefly and they all find themselves disarmed. Or an army that is trying to cross a bridge that an obstinate cloaked figure is sitting in the middle of, and when the general makes a threat a silver blur seems to shoot through the middle of the nearest formation and it takes troops a few seconds to register that their spears are missing, and the general decides maybe it would be better to seek a different bridge. It’s delightful.

                            There’s Deadly Raptor’s Flight for the Lunar that wants to be moving at full pelt and have their aim remain fixated on a chosen target. And then Thousand Claw Affliction, providing the uncommon multi-withering attack; it may limit how much Initiative can be gained from any one foe (although with enough of them that might end up still getting more than would be common), and considering that this also allows different Abilities and weapons, seems well suited to fighting in concert with good allies. The Protean effect to up the limit of Initiative that can be gained per attack really awakens the cheesemonger within me, and he wants to find a colony of mutated six-armed apes so that those attacks can still be made with bow and sword.

                            Running Through the Herd allows the multi-attack Charms to shift the Lunar by a range band after every successful attack, which doesn’t need to count as their movement action if in the appropriate shape. Supreme Predator Alacrity is interesting as a scene-long Charm without a cost, just requiring the Lunar to win Join Battle, and thus providing a mote discount to a variety of lock-on and improved accuracy attack Charms against everybody lower in the Initiative order. I like this one for conveying a sense of a rising fighting spirit that has a predatory edge; a sense that a Lunar is a bit more tensed up at detecting weakness, sluggishness or hesitation in foes.

                            And then Lightning Stroke Attack, for the Lunar who wants to be sure that they’re getting the best result from a hit, allowing them to make a decisive attack roll twice and get the better offer, and only pay for Charms enhancing both of the attacks once. I’d almost wonder if this should have a limit per scene, but I suppose that requiring high Initiative gives opponents latitude to prevent it at all.

                            Now into the Essence 4 Charms. Heart-Piercing Instinct continues that theme of the Lunar invigorated by sensing weakness, giving them a scene-long capacity to add dice for aiming (or convert them directly into non-Charm successes) when attacking an enemy within reasonable range that is crashed or starting to get badly wounded. Twisting Moonsilver Stroke reverses the order of and broadens its prerequisite, allowing a successful gambit of any type besides grappling to be followed immediately by a normal attack on the victim. For this I just like the image of snatching away a person’s weapon just to cut them down with it, although it can also pair nicely with some custom gambits to mess with people in other ways.

                            Finally for those there’s Unhesitant Scorpion Lash, in which a Lunar using the multi-withering attack can either reflexively press a decisive attack on somebody that has been crashed, or use Octopus-and-Spider Barrage to get at as many crashed enemies as the normal limits will allow, with a Lunar in the correct form getting back some Willpower if one of the targets is taken out. I’m really liking how the new combat system is really lending itself to a tree growing out of Octopus-and-Spider Barrage; sure, it was always an iconic Lunar Charm, but I feel that all of this is giving it a deeper characterisation than just “extra limbs is the Lunar answer to flurrying attacks”. Especially fun since the Charm often had the unfortunate distinction of being highly valued due to poor wording encouraging a desire to pack on as many limbs as possible to make some really ridiculous flurries.

                            Okay, what terrors await at Essence 5?

                            Greatest Huntress Mastery; in the highest expression of going for the jugular of a downed foe, the Lunar can spend motes and anima levels to target a crashed enemy over whom the Lunar has more than twenty Initiative with an unblockable, undodgeable decisive attack. Can only be used once per scene, and the reset condition is a very hefty requirement to gain at least 20 Initiative from one attack, that crashes an enemy, who was also above the Lunar in the Initiative order. I’m a little bit uncertain about this one; I’d want to compare it to any Solar Charms to make an attack nigh-unavoidable, and wonder if that reset condition is even possible. That said, I do like the imagery, of the anima trailing from the attack to form after-images that converge on the victim; it’s got elements of a cool ki attack, evoking a predatory spirit, as well as having plasticity from several methods that a Lunar can attack through.

                            My final assessment of the Lunar Dexterity Offence Charms is that it’s really enjoyable how a number of Lunar qualities, predatory, flexible, even playful and tricky, effectively come through in a lot of powers, without needing to incorporate any more body transformation than desirable. I find them to have effectively fulfilled the promise of making a Lunar swordswoman feel appropriately powerful even if of the type to not want to heavily transform their body in battle.

                            Come to think of it… actually combining these with the likes of White Reaper and Single Point Shining into the Void…

                                                I suspect that the Defence Charms will be about as long as the Offence ones, so I’ll handle them at the same pace, and then see about going through what’s left to the end.


                                                Okay, start with Agile Beast Defense, something that supplements the basic element of giving Initiative for successfully defending against an attack with some more of the predatory imagery in getting extra Defence against somebody at lower Initiative or who rolled 1s. The Protean benefit of being a small creature allowing the Charm to be declared after you already know how well the attack will go seems particularly good, if effectively offset by how being so small means that you’re primarily oriented at defence. Bending Before the Storm is similar, being able to alternately subtract half of the penalties to Defence, or all of them against those lower-Initiative attackers. And then Ferocious Guardian Beast Stance incorporates a bit more of their imagery to create a scene-long effect in which they can reflexively make defend other actions for allies in close range, and treat anybody attacking a ward as having lower Initiative for the purpose of preceding Charms. Ah, the Charm only works for defending a designated person, and ends if somebody else is defended, that makes sense, keeps a whole circle from clustering around the Lunar.

                                                Golden Tiger Stance brings in a picture of Lunars mirroring the movements of an attacker, modelled with them getting +1 to Defence and no onslaught penalties to failed attacks for a turn, and lets Bending Before the Storm cause successful defences to also drain some Initiative from the attacker. Coiled Serpent Strike is fairly basic as an introductory counterattack Charm, although it has an Essence 2 repurchase to let it counter multiple attacks on the same tick and putting off resetting Initiative until the end, to get in that advantage when outnumbered.

                                                Into Essence 2 with Ever-Evolving Defense, which emerges from the universal Charm Quicksilver Second Face, enhancing that rapid shapeshifting as a defence by letting its successful use acquire extra Initiative, and a repurchase at higher Dexterity allows a surcharge to be paid to let the Charm be used against an already rolled attack, on the condition that the Lunar assume a shape that has not been used in the scene (with another Charm letting that surcharge be waived). I like using some of these Dexterity Charms to enhance that defensive application of rapid shapeshifting, to help that image retain a significant place in their defensive suite.

                                                Fleet Gazelle Exercise rests on an image of Lunars being supremely elusive prey (and maybe a bit of revelry in outpacing pursuers) with a scene-long bonus to Evasion and getting Initiative after every round in which attacks failed to hit them. Followed by the image of evasion to twist an attacker into overextending themselves, being used after dodging a decisive attack to make an attacker lose Initiative for every 1 on their roll (also 2s, if in a Tiny or smaller shape), up to the Lunar’s Essence. I might think it’s not fully in the spirit of Lunar Charms to specify a defence, but between the usefulness of the effects and the image that they’re drawing upon, tying it to Evasion is appropriate.

                                                Foe-Baiting Sidestep keeps going, letting the Lunar who has successfully dodged a close-range both move away by a range band, and drag their attacker along with them. I can’t fully recall, but I think this is fairly distinct from a lot of Dodge Charms to move after evading, and I imagine it’s intended for things like pulling an attacker away from the benefit of their allies, or into better range of the Lunar’s own. That and the suggested application of pulling them directly into harm’s way, even if the person gets the opportunity to recover. Or heck, since it doesn’t use up the enemy’s move action, it might just force them to waste it to get back into a previously advantageous position, rather than advancing or retreating as they might have intended.

                                                Incidentally, having already seen Flowing Body Evasion, it’s interesting to actually see all of these Charms that a Lunar might use on somebody even when their attack was actually successful.
                                                Ah, but Parry gets a bit of love as well, starting with Gleaming Crescent Block, by which such a guard can cause 1s to be subtracted from attack successes, and which gets to combine with Coiled Serpent Strike by letting that loss be added as dice for the counterattack’s damage roll. To just skip a little for the sake of keeping that theme going, I’ll observe Vigilant Mastiff Technique, which grants +1 to a Parry for protecting somebody with defend other, and forces anybody who wins to direct the attack at the Lunar, which can function against even unblockable attacks if the Lunar knows the Charm Heron Sheds Rain (coming up a bit later).

                                                Finally, there’s Sensing the Deadly Flow, by which heightened battle instincts are represented in suffering no defensive penalties from wounds, onslaught or grapples, as well as waiving the Initiative cost and Perilous keyword of Golden Tiger Stance, and discounting the cost of Bending Before the Storm.


                                                I think the defence might lend itself a bit less to vivid imagery than proactive attack does, but that what is managed here so far makes a good show of it, particularly anything that lets Lunars cheat a little with being selective for defence against attacks already known to work, and the number of things that can be done to an attacker who failed.

                                                    Okay, upper ends of Dexterity defensive power.


                                                    Cunning Prey Reversal has fun imagery to the name, of building on its prior to show that prey and predator are not a dichotomy in Lunars, by way of giving them bonus Evasion against a decisive attack, and lets them get the lost Initiative from when the attacker misses, with Protean to go in for the “only use it when you know it’ll work” thing. Snarling Watchdog Retribution, that was in Glories I think, furthering the protectiveness element by letting a Lunar with ten or more Initiative make a decisive counterattack against somebody going for their ward, which is made especially potent by being able to add the ward’s Initiative to the roll to determine damage, without the ward even needing to reset Initiative.

                                                    I see that Flowing Body Evasion has been slightly modified from the preview, where adding it to a base defence had the potential to be a costly waste if the attack actually failed, so I think it’s beneficial to have the basic form give you something in added Evasion, before it gets into the Willpower surcharge to subtract Evasion from the damage of successful attacks.

                                                    Okay, Heron Sheds Rain was mentioned before, what do… ohh, they’re getting their high power parry up front as well. So on the base of it, Parry can be applied against unblockable attacks, and a surcharge lets the character roll their Initiative and add successes to Parry as a non-Charm bonus, which sounds like it has the potential to make some especially strong blocks, necessarily offset by the reset of Initiative (which is held off until after some necessary counterattacks are made). I feel as though, on the face of it, the actual defensive power behind this parry might be stronger than Heavenly Guardian Defence, although that Charm has it beat in being less overtly costly (although it might hurt for stopping a bigger attack), and only being available once per scene barring the rather heavy reset condition of getting at least 20 Initiative in one go.

                                                    Heh, and speaking of being predator and prey simultaneously, Shadow-Chased Silver Defense represents the image of a Lunar somehow moving to escape and intercept simultaneously, allowing them to Parry and Evade at the same time, not only adding the values together, but letting Charms specific to either form of Defence co-exis… hahaha, oh that’s delightful, to have said “it’s slightly contrary but necessary to specify Charms to defence type” and then ultimately have them reconnect at a point of “Lunars can do both at once”. Wonderful. Sure it makes sense that it doesn’t fully work if faced with an attack that one of the defences can’t be applied against, and it can only be used once per scene, but it’s fairly cheap and the reset condition of defending from three decisive attacks is fairly accommodating. This is a good one.

                                                    Ah, and this line stops with an Essence 4 Charm, Becoming Water’s Envy, in which the Lunar gets a particularly weird and mystical form of defence by way of responding to an attack that successfully beat Evasion by rolling current Willpower + Evasion, to establish a threshold that the damage roll needs to beat to be able to apply itself at all (with failure qualifying as a dodge). I think the wording on the last line needs to be a little bit clearer, but I take it to mean that an attack dealing damage lower than the established threshold but enough to fill the Lunar’s health track will not be stopped by the Charm; I can see that both as a necessary balancing concern, and something to go with the imagery of the Charm as constituting a battle of wills. Either way, it can only be used once per day, barring the reset condition of effectively dodging every attack in a fight, which I’d say is challenging, but conceivable.


                                                    So yeah, Defence ultimately came across fairly well, being all sinewy and tricky and powerful. Dexterity has made a good case for Lunars in combat, once again doing the good job of letting one Attribute effectively carry a lot of the pressure.

                                                        See if I can’t finish off the Dexterity Charms.

                                                        Looking back on the Second Edition write-up, I recall how the ones for moving around were the Dexterity Charms that I found… interesting at all (I’d kind of forgotten how weakly written Dexterity overall felt to me). So I’m looking forward to seeing what might be done with that angle in this set up.


                                                        But, got to start small, with Graceful Crane Stance, which functions identically to the Solar Charm of the same name as usual. It then gets to immediately leap into something weird, with Cat-Footed Grace described as having the image of a Lunar regaining footing by shifting one’s form directly upright, mechanically represented with a reflexive rise from prone action that can be made off turn, doesn’t count as movement, and can reroll 1s. And then Wind-Dancing Method, allowing for controlling one’s descent to be able to do things like slow down falling (increasing the time and reducing the damage from a fall), and being able to horizontally cross a range band without it counting as a movement action; this one is described as alternately making one’s body lighter or growing the kinds of membranes that flying squirrels and the like have. It makes me wonder if a few of the mobility Charms might entail some more overt transformation.

                                                        Instinct-Driven Beast Movement allows brief bursts of high speed as an automatic success to a movement action or appropriate extended action, and getting some extra Initiative if it supplements a successful opposed action in combat, with a Protean effect of a speedy animal getting to take that Initiative reward away from an opposing character. And Spider-Climbing Attitude simply allows one to cling to and move around on walls and ceilings and the like (with Lunars that have spirit shapes that can do likewise being able to learn it with Wits), although it requires continuously putting in motes across turns. I’m wondering if a later Charm might relieve some of that burden.

                                                        Heh, Wounded Beast Flight: “I’m not running away Finn, this is all part of my plan!” For Lunars to harness the motive of their injuries to make it easier to move out of a fight, ignoring wound penalties for disengage and withdraw rolls, and not needing to lose Initiative when disengaging under sufficiently bad wounds. In all seriousness, I like the way the first line characterizes this all in terms of rage, the idea that it can motivate in directions besides furiously fixating on an offender.

                                                        I’ve been waiting on a reminder of the previewed Swift Killer Style, by which a Lunar can adopt the image of a speedster fighter or hit and run attacker, getting to reflexively move a range band away in whatever direction they want (allowing flexibility in follow up tactics) when they close in on and hit an opponent, with flying or aquatic forms being able to rise fairly or descend fairly long distances, in return for paying a bit of extra Willpower. I was already interested in seeing this the first time, a more direct combative Charm in the midst of a lot of previews focused on the personal, transformative or mystical elements, and think it pairs especially well with a number of the actual combat Charms (those being why I wanted to see it again, now that there’s context).

                                                        Shifting Octopus Trick is a root Charm to let a Lunar’s body become flexible or gelatinous to make keeping a hold on a Lunar more difficult, enhancing actions to escape bonds, with the benefit of doubling 9s and starting with their Essence in successes against ropes, chains or manacles that are magical, making escape from the mundane a guarantee regardless of rolls result, with failure just making it awkward enough to entail some dramatic complication (an element that I’d honestly like to have seen in some Solar Charms), and being able to double 9s to escape a grapple, with animal shapes that can contort being able to double 8s instead. The dramatically interesting failure thing is a nice way to handle a power like this, and I feel that being so low down might create an interesting basic assumption about how Lunars need to be handled.

                                                        Quicksilver Armor Approach describes itself as involving a Lunar’s flesh flowing into or out of armour, and while that might often be pictured as entailing actual fluidification, the image it most recalls to me is an event from Fullmetal Alchemist; namely, when Alphonse Elric is accosted by a bunch of human chimerae (in that setting, hybrid beings created through alchemy), they effectively take him captive by one of them with snake features sliding head first into his neck hole, adjusting herself within through contortion, and getting her limbs inside of his and bracing them up. I don’t know, it always stuck with me because of how the artwork effectively made it look very sinuous, and kind of weird and violating for Al to experience, so it’s where my mind is going with this; I think it would still constitute a sufficiently weird and unique way for a Lunar to get into a suit of armour. Anyway, in addition to letting one don armour with time reduced from minutes to turns, they can briefly remove the mobility penalties of armour (which puts me in mind of the old moonsilver attunement bonus, which I was always fond of), and at Essence 2 can purchase it again to be able to provide that assistance to putting armour on another person or animal (that being something where I would see more fluidity), and able to relieve a mount’s strain under armour as well.

                                                        Essence 2 starts with Predator Grace Technique, a short but sweet Charm in which a Lunar benefits from rushing a wounded enemy by adding their penalty as non-Charm dice to the action, which in addition to the described image of going for the weak first, I take as Lunars being able to excite themselves with the sight or scent of a weakened enemy. Ferocious Hound Pursuit is taking the same cue as a bunch of previous Charms directed at lower-Initiative enemies, this one letting them replace the usual effect of a successful rush with instantly advancing a range band towards the target, which doesn’t count as a move action. And it all comes to Silver Jaguar Pounce, in which a Lunar can immediately advance a range band closer to an enemy that was just crashed or had wound penalties increase, and getting their Essence as bonus damage dice if a decisive attack is made no later than the Lunar’s next turn.

                                                        Yeah, a lot of this is the kind of stuff I like in the Second Edition Charms, the image of honing in on prey or a designated rival. A lot of this is probably ultimately more balanced than it was before, if only because some of the powers get to be spread out across a larger number of Charms.

                                                        Shifting Many-Legged Stride gets back into some of the weird with the description of a Lunar’s legs continuously changing to adapt to the landscape, although it’s fairly low-key and unintrusive (by which I mean it can be adjusted to taste); anyway, a movement action gets to ignore penalties from terrain, and the concept of adapting to impeded movement gets extended to gambits such as grapples of unhorsing (I’m getting the picture of a Lunar’s feet becoming hooked and flattened to grip to a horse’s underbelly, which must take the animal some getting used to), adding the Lunar’s Essence to the difficulties. In a fast animal form, crossing such terrain also lets one get some extra dice for the move action, and a little more to the difficulty of the gambits.

                                                        For even more definitely not running away, there’s Bounding Hare Evasion, for a disengaged Lunar to get an extra range band away from an enemy (in lieu of being allowed to move again if pursued), although the spirit of it prevents its use on the same turn that the Lunar attacked somebody. And for the occasions where a Lunar can’t not run away far or fast enough, there is the Cornered Rat Frenzy; usable when a character has Initiative 10+, they start by adding Essence in non-Charm dice to disengage rolls, and if it still fails then a reflexive decisive attack can be levelled upon the opponent who beat the roll (although they lose the Initiative from the failure before getting to make the attack), with 10s on the disengage roll added to the attack as non-Charm dice, and incapacitating the confining enemy then allows the disengage action to have succeeded. The Charm doesn’t count as an attack for the prior one’s purposes, and a Lunar who knows one of the multi-attack Charms from before can pay Willpower to be allowed to make an attack against any enemies that beat the disengage roll.

                                                        Quicksilver Guardian Defense folds back to one of the Defensive Charms, acting as an enhancement to Ferocious Guardian Beast Stance to allow defend other actions to extend to short range, as well as having the option to spend Initiative to instantly cross a range band when a ward is attacked, if doing so would bring them in range to defend other, which doesn’t use up the move action. It also finally inclines one of those Charms to the Solar Mate, allowing spent Initiative to be recouped if the Lunar’s defence succeeds, or they take the hit for the Lunar. There’s a Protean effect to let flying forms ascend after moving to protect an ally, but I feel a little bit shaky on the application of that. That might just be because it’s getting late, and my attention is starting waver a little.

                                                        And Mobility Charms come to an end at Essence 3 with Relentless Cheetah Pace, with which predatory instinct allow a rush action to be made against an enemy at medium range, spending the next two turns automatically crossing the intervening range bands in addition to any normal movement, with the bonus of combining with Ferocious Hound Pursuit to cross the distance instantaneously, which has to be a rather terrifying final sight for many people to have experienced. Probably a lot of people being told they’re going to be broken like a Kit-Kat bar.


                                                        So I think the Movement Charms effectively capture a lot of the imagery that I found compelling in the past, and then expand upon it. They also synergize very nicely with some of the prior Offence and Defence Charms, helping to both characterize Dexterity and extend its capability. My initial impulse was to be a bit off about it being largely combat oriented, and having less for general improvements to raw speed output, but… my first following thought was to not really care, since it didn’t feel strictly necessary for that to be an area of expertise, that was immediately followed by the idea that a lot of Charms like that may end up with Strength and/or Stamina instead. Which I would actually be all for, both on grounds of those feeling appropriate to outputs of raw power or endurance compared to Dexterity having a focus on more precise movements, and to continue that element of certain competencies being distributed across Attributes.


                                                        It's getting late and there’s some more of Dexterity than I was expecting, so I’m going to leave those until tomorrow, and then get a start on Intelligence.

                                                            Ah jeez, I have not managed my time today effectively at all. Woke up earlier than I have in days, and then putz around taking ages to write one of the shortest sections of commentary I have yet. I'm gonna be paying for it tomorrow when I'm trying to power through Intelligence.

                                                            Heh, I wonder if Dexterity getting more subcategories than the prior Attributes might contribute to its unfortunate godstat reputation. Anyway, I’d like to see how its flexibility provides an alternative approach to Subterfuge.

                                                            Starts with Night Stalker Approach, which being an Instant Charm, entails adding some motes to the cost of Charms that enhance things like Larceny, Stealth, and anything done while in concealment to let them be mute, with small spirit shapes getting to take it as Manipulation. Seems fair, both as an Essence 1 Charm, and to generally not let Lunars readily keep hidden while employing their powers. Skulking Rat Spirit lets a Lunar reroll 1s (and 2s when in a small animal shape) on rolls for Stealth, concealing evidence and covering tracks, to represent an image of being able to depart without leaving a trace. And Unseen Predator Menace starts bringing in the scary stuff, with Stealth rolls being unpenalized by concealment in combat and sapping Initiative from enemies that fail to beat the rolls, which the Lunar can get a chance to steal if they’re in the shape of an ambush predator.

                                                            Snake-Finger Style (another great name) is used to double 9s on a variety of larcenous actions dependent on nimble hands, and prevents any drawbacks from lacking proper equipment. And we’re already in Essence 2 with Midnight Phantom Movement, a name that puts me in particular in mind of sign-offs that would be attached to threatening messages in the Irish Land Wars; the actual effects enhances Lunar Stealth rolls to maintain concealment, and prevents them from being penalised for crossing terrain in which such concealment would be especially tricky, with ambush hunter animals shapes also getting an enhancement to when they rush an enemy from out of that concealment. I’ve got to say, it’s nice to be seeing these Charms after, shortly before doing so, having been thinking about the tiger specifically in terms of different values it might have as a shape.

                                                            But, like the other Dexterity Charms, there’s another facet to the Lunar ethos, found in Elusive Prey Approach, allowing a Lunar to follow a successful dodge by reflexively rolling to enter concealment, albeit without removing the associated penalties on its own. And it returns to the larcenous character with Thieving Magpie Prana, conferring a potentially heavy bonus of non-Charm dice to rolls for pickpocketing or general theft for when the Lunar is currently unobserved, with the added benefit of putting a timer on how long defeated potential observers have before they’re able to notice the object missing.

                                                            Flashing Steel Reversal is one of these growing out of some of those combat Charms, to improve what Lunars can do with disarming; when attempted on lower-Initiative enemies, they get the unusual ability to add an Ability (Larceny of course) to the attack and Initiative rolls, and with a free hand and close range get the option of reflexively readying the removed weapon for themselves, although it takes Essence 3 to be able to overwrite the commitment for Artifact weapons, and the Lunar is otherwise stuck wielding it with its awkward weight. Rather than itself, I’m suddenly thinking something about the old Charm clouds, which I might elaborate on later.

                                                            Night’s Swift Fang is a scene-long enhancement that lets a Lunar who is hidden from all enemies at the start of a round to get a bit of extra Initiative, and take their turn first, being able to make an unexpected attack with a free Excellency if their Initiative was already high enough to go first. It can only be used once per scene, with no reset condition, but an appropriate animal shape improves the extra Essence acquired.

                                                            For Essence 3, they get Argent Whisper Cunning to flurry Stealth actions with anything besides attacks or other things that break concealment, unless they make a repurchase allowing a Willpower surcharge to let the Stealth flurry with a decisive attack, with a successful attack obligating them to use up their movement action to move into a new concealment area and attempt Stealth. If in an ambush predator shape, the flurries don’t even need to take penalties.

                                                            These stealthy attack Charms are fine, just not much more to say on them.

                                                            Let’s see… Shadow Talon Execution, can make a regular decisive ambush attack but get to add extra successes to the attack roll (animal shape gets double 9s on the attack roll, potentially maximizing the damage) that sounds decent. Silent Swooping Owl, makes the Lunar and their use of tools completely silent for a round. Twisting Penumbra Veil… ah yes, this was previewed. It’s a shame, it’s the more interesting of these Charms with its clashing an attack with Stealth, but I’ve already talked about that. Although I will say that, in context, that kind of power to return to concealment feels a lot more important.

                                                            And then it gets to end with an Essence 4 Charm, Cryptic Unknown Beast. Ahaha, that idea of forcing characters to reroll their successes on Awareness rolls against Stealth in order to convey the idea of being momentarily visible before vanishing to mess with people is great. It’s once per scene, but is reset by the process of the whole preceding style by incapacitating an enemy with an unexpected attack and then effectively concealing oneself from enemies, even being able to roll Join Battle again if the saved Stealth roll conceals from everybody, with the right shape.


                                                            Subterfuge does a few nice things. It rounds out Dexterity’s characteristic theme, feels like it encompasses a comprehensive stealthy fighting style in its own right, and contrasts effectively with the kind of subterfuge that Appearance can provide.

                                                            But Dexterity is not done yet. I’ve got some other business to attend to, but when I’m ready I’ll try and zoom through the Swarm Charms to finish it off.
