    Speculation on the Lunar Curse?

    02-08-2015, 12:24 AM
    Originally posted by John Mørke
    Lunar thematics will be more tightly focused on the dualism of a "good" person who has a monster inside, or a monster who has a good person inside. Rage, transformation, and shapeshifting will be much more strongly evinced in their narrative and their mechanics.
    Originally posted by Cromage
    I like the idea of "dualism" but I'm not so keen on the "human vs monster" and have actually seen that more in the province of (gasp!) other splats (don't kill me). I like the idea of Lunars being champions of two worlds--exiles, outsiders, emigrants, and wanderers--and having to either choose one world or another or somehow trying to reconcile them.

    ETA: More importantly I always hated chimerism and its tendency to drag Lunars into really dumb places. Maybe 3e will make it not suck.
    Originally posted by John Mørke
    Human vs. monster isn't on the schedule. I'm not turning Lunars into a werewolf story.

    Chimeraism is going to be way different in EX3.
    Originally posted by Miss Maddy
    To what extent is the 'frenzy' aspect of the Lunar Exalted going to tied to all of this? Will Lunars be more emotional or moody? Will the Full Moon give limit? Will Lunar Limit breaks involve largely animal-themed expressions of primal emotion?
    Originally posted by John Mørke
    Frenzy aspects are choice driven, not the default state of all Lunars. Full Moon insanity is getting tossed entirely out. Lunar Limit Breaks are undetermined, but are likely to change from what they were.
    Based on the above exchanges, I'm assuming that the Great Curse of the Lunar Exalted is not going to grant them a "Man versus the Animal Within / Inner Monster" narrative that reflects the Alchemical Host's "man versus machine" narrative (Clarity).

    Not having a Great Curse that drives the Lunars to fits of animal behavior also helps distinguish them from the Solar Exalted, who do have an "inner monster" struggle because their Great Curse drives them to fits of great passion (or fits of terrible dispassion).

    Still, what does that leave? Identity disassociation? Ie: Multiple personality disorder?


    Note that the inner struggle highlighted by their Great Curse may very well be completely distinct from their other inner struggle, which seems to center around their old but strong association with the Solar Exalted and how the disappearance of the Solars left the Lunar Exalted without a place in the world. The Lunar struggle to find a new purpose may be their reflection of Sidereal Arcane Fate.



    Also, to clarify the very first quotation:

    Originally posted by limaxophobiac
    Not big on the concept of dualism like this [between human and monster] in general, it seems to imply that there is some sort of necessary conflict between being a "monster" and a "good person" that require the two to be somehow separate.
    Originally posted by Holden
    Entirely true, and largely phrased that way because if we had simply said "Luna Exalts people who are monsters on the inside or the outside" a lot of people would assume that we meant Lunars are Dexter, which, no.

      Originally posted by Gayo View Post
      This is one of those topics where I feel like it will be vastly easier to speculate once the corebook is out. There are two big lacunae in the way of guesses: we don't know what Lunars look like in Third Edition, and we don't know what the Solar Curse looks like in Third Edition. The dev comments on these two subjects have been interesting, but they're pretty thin grounds for extrapolation.
      Yes, discussion will certainly be made easier after the corebook is out. But it's not impossible, either.

      We know that Solars are meant to evoke the greatest of Greek heroes, including their fits of excessive passion and other overreactions. We don't know the precise mechanical form this will take, but we know the character behind the mechanics and the inspiration behind that character.

      I just want to see if we can guess at the character and inspiration behind the Lunar Curse.

      Solars have to struggle against going overboard in anything they do, especially when their power is more than enough to take them exactly that far. Abyssals have to struggle against their fate to kill the world by inches (and miles). Alchemicals have to struggle against losing touch with humanity; Liminals might need to struggle in order to get in touch with humanity in the first place. Sidereals struggle to remain open-minded despite being in a position to "know it all, and know it better than you".

      But against what does a shapeshifter tend to struggle, when you eliminate "his own shapeshifting" and "the light of the full moon" and "losing his humanity in the face of the beast within"?

      And make no mistake, the Lunar Curse isn't going to do something as stupid as punish Lunar players for turning into animals, because turning into animals is the point of playing the Lunars in the first place. If anything, it would be more appropriate for the Lunar Curse to act up when they don't shapeshift.

      The Lunar Curse will also not involve becoming more like the animals they turn into, because I quoted John Morke in the very first post, and he said he's not turning the Lunars into a werewolf "man against inner monster" story.



      Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
      If Jekyll and Hyde isn't on the table, perhaps Ratkin Rapture is. They were "Gnosis frenzies" that imparted distorted visions and premonitions when the rat was too much in tune with the spirit world. It included a number of effects that would be similar to being delusional, paranoid, etc., in addition to more mystical consequences like tuning out the world of matter and seeing only the spiritual landscape of the world.

      Going off of that last point, it might end up changing perceptions in a matter similar to the protagonist of Saya no Uta, who sees (and hears, and smells and otherwise perceives) humans as horrible, monstrous creatures and vice versa. The trailer for Oculus also comes to mind, but it's painful like that episode of Clone High where Abe's mouth keeps getting cut up, so beware.
      Tying Lunars more closely to the immaterial side of Creation does make sense, since the developers are drawing more inspiration from the Garou and the sources that inspired the Garou.

      Hmm. You've seen the Karas movie, right Saur Ops? Perhaps the Lunar Exalted have a similarly special relationship with the immaterial side of Creation, and all of the spirits that live there.


      Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
      I feel like the inner struggle of the Lunar might not be a conflict, but rather a side of them that leaves them with a fairly powerful passion for the taboo or the extreme, something that can carry their reason along with it, and that many will allow it to*.
      Getting carried away by passion is what the Lunar Limit Break already did, and that didn't work because it was just copying the Solar Exalted.

      I'm not sure that struggling to avoid wild flights of abandon and excess makes sense for ANY non-Solar Exalted except for the Terrestrial Exalted, who in some ways are "the Solar Exalted, writ smaller".


      their manifestation of the Curse is something else... Maybe at one time it was being too heavily devoted to and dependent on the Solars, and then informed their evolution through the Second Age along lines of "let's become the monsters everybody is saying we are"...

      Hmm, the natural state of Lunars being highly individual, to the point of needing certain restraints based on their context within relationships to others, with their Great Curse as a force to make them too much what other people might observe them as being, rather than being themselves...
      A curse of meeting and exceeding people's expectations of them might be very interesting, indeed. Certainly, it would introduce a powerful element to their other struggle, which is to find a new place and purpose in the world.

      It's hard to find yourself if you're constantly struggling to avoid becoming what other people think you should be.

      (It would also be another enormous, ironic allusion to how the player base has been treating the Lunar Exalted since their first splatbook was released.)


      Originally posted by FromBeyond View Post
      Change of identity isn't really something you can enforce and model mechanically though.
      Sure it is. For a given value of "identity". Or maybe, "some parts of your identity".

      Intimacies (Ties and Principles) are important parts of a character's identity with mechanial representations.

      A Curse that targets the Lunar's identity might erode a particular Intimacy each time he acts against it, or the Curse might building a new Intimacy every time he acts in favor of it, or perhaps the Curse starts to change the emotion context of his lesser Intimacies every time he acts against (or in favor of) his stronger Intimacies.

                                      Whatever it is. I hope it's sweeping and tragic.

                                      The idea of the Great Curse as laid out in the original, first form of the Exalted Core is solid IMO (please let's not start that again). It is intended as a spur to emulating epic tragedy, or as human passion and flaws to struggle against (with triumph possible).

                                      When it becomes an idea that each form of Exalt has a tendency to its own particular and random form of idiocy or mental illness or source of inconvenience, no matter whether those are anything to do with actual epic tragedy, when they just become Sanity level 0 stuff, then it becomes a farce.

                                      So I would only say for Lunars, whatever the Great Curse is for them, please, please, please put the aim of genre before finding some unique curse that it is reasoned that they could suffer from their inner nature, but which fails to generate interesting, emotionally touching and grand stories and explore human character. Start from what stories and character we want, then work backwards to try and fit it into what the Lunars are.
