                        Originally posted by Hark View Post
                        There is literally an entire splat that tells players in every version of the Lore that they are objectively the bad guys and should feel bad for playing them. It can get frustrating to DB fans that they are always portrayed as bad guys. Yeah, this is Lunars and the wrong place for this discussion because the Lunars book absolutely should be portraying the Realm as bad guys, but the DB book made them bad guys too, so there is some understandable frustration going around.

                        I put a lot of effort into planning out a rather epic Realm Civil War campaign and it fell apart before it began because I couldn't get the players to buy into the Realm culture even a little bit.
                        So, before typing this response I took the day and re-read the entire section on Great Houses, and the new Lunar setting pieces that we got to compare. I don't think that the DB book necessarily always treats the Dynasts as villains, but it almost never treats them as heroes either, which is something the Lunar book does really well.

                        In really oversimplified short, the good parts of the Realm are that they unify areas under their control, build infrastructure, promote trade, defend the people from outside forces and apply a kind of rule of law to protect people from inside forces. The villainous things they do are extract wealth, destroy culture, restrict freedom, and enforce it all with violence and fear. Also they acquire new lands through either invasion or exploitation.

                        The good parts of the Silver Pact are that they fight the tyranny of the Realm, promote freedom for the cultures they target, and the societies under their direct rule flourish with their guidance. The villainous things they do are act like violent insurgents or criminals who leverage the suffering of innocent people, and have a callous lack of concern for collateral damage in their war.



                        Both of those offer a really good setting to be heroes or villains in. As a Lunar hero, the path is obvious, your enemies in the Realm are the soldiers who are lining up frightened civilians for mass execution, or the invading army of the Realm who's decided that an independent border kingdom has been a thorn in their side for too long. You're infiltrating the cruel satrap's palace, assassinating him, and destroying precious Thousand Scale records, or maybe even raising an army yourself and kicking them out once and for all. All of this culminates in a great battle sparked off at a would-be execution, where the tables are turned and it's the executioners blood on the sand. The great liberators, exalted heroes who cast of the chains of slavery, be they literal chains or metaphorical ones. Heroes who might even start on overthrowing the Empire in its entirety.

                        As a Dynast hero, you're battling the inhuman march of undead that a Lunar necromancer has unleashed upon your satrapy. Their cold hands grasping for the throats of farmers and children, but your hearth, and your brave soldiers who have sailed here from 1,000 miles away to defend them, won't let that happen. Then once the people can sleep soundly in their beds again, you must decide whether to hunt down that Lunar anathema, or start work on investigating a possible guild ploy to get the entire town addicted to opioid drugs as a means of controlling them. You could even look to the people that your house's harsh taxes are draining dry and think to yourself "There's got to be a better way than this." and start on actually fixing the Realm.


                        The Lunar book does a super solid job of showing both sides of those stories, the DB book, not so much. There a lot of Lunar heroes in the Lunar book who fit the description of the above kind of PC hero, but very few Dynasts in the DB book who fit the Dynastic one.


                        On the other hand, fuck yeah Lunars: Fangs at the Gate. I fucking like the DB book and this one is shaping up to be even better. All of the characters are really interesting, they're not flawless, and they're not exactly taking up a ton of space that PCs might want to be filling, but at the same time they're acting as great inspiration, and awesome NPC characters to interact with.

Think about ancient Rome and Han China there. The roads weren't for the folks in Judea or the former state of Qi. They were for the empire to better extract that wealth.

                                    To me that's the big thing though that gets lost in tehs ediscussions. Empires are institutions, which people are part of. Individual people are still able to be judged as good or not. Empires by their nature are about controlling other nations for extration of wealth or exertion of power. Folks can be good or bad in that context.

                                    And I think in this regard, the Realm is actually not an evil empire. Evil empires in fantasy often are evil because it goes out of its way to be sadistic/destructive/etc. Your cliche fantasy empire is either some asshole state ran with assholes, or it is this thing where apparenlty you have super powerful aboslute monarchs who decide its moral character (basically buying into the idea that a state is just its sovereign or something).

                                    The Realm's issues, though, are not becuase it's full of evil people, but because it's built on exploitive institutions. It doesn't just murder villagers becuase some nobleperson has cart blanch to that. It does it because, much of the time, there's a reason to it. That reason is mind, grand scale theft. But we seem to be able to ahve Rome be something folks find fascinating and a cultural touchstone even though it crucified a lot of people and did outright ethnic cleansing when push came to shove, and often for shit that's actually pretty petty for the Realm (glory and such, like conquering Britainia). The Realm's wrongs are built on real world wrongs, not cartoonish ones. It's what makes it both more interesitng an empire, and more compelling to play in.

                                    Mind, that I think I live in the Empire right now kind of makes me less willing ot be so judgemental of the people living in that system. Because a lot of the "But folks who live on teh Blessed Isle have it good" kind of can be turned ot the US and arguments I've seen of "Even the poorest in the US is better than in less devleoped palces". WHich, even if true (it's debatalbe) misses a point that some places are bad because of the US. "Peasants in the Reealm are relatively safe and secure" kind of hits a simlar tune to me.

                                                            Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                                                            I was hoping to see a moratorium on this kind of talk. -_-
                                                            Consider it the death throes of the subject.


                                                            Originally posted by Blaque View Post
                                                            I guess a thing to me to remember is that while the Realm does those good things, it should be noted it's not altruistically a lot of the time. Think about ancient Rome and Han China there. The roads weren't for the folks in Judea or the former state of Qi. They were for the empire to better extract that wealth.
                                                            And it's not even the most efficient use of those resources. It's all short-term and sinking sand.

                                                            Imperialism, among other things, is the desire to take the resources that others have already produced, without needing to go to the effort of producing those resources themselves, or offering as much in free exchange as the second party would demand.

                                                            "Barbarian raiders" who would rather raid agricultural societies for food and metal tools rather than growing their own crops or mining their own steel are a form of imperialism, especially if they formalize a demand of tribute in exchange for NOT raiding people. For example, the Danes and their practice of "Danegeld" (literally "Dane gold") appeasement against victim nations.

                                                            This is just one step away from imperialism, as the Danes didn't attempt to occupy or govern the people they stole from, but you can't have full imperialism without this basic level of vampirism.

                                                            Regardless, it's a fool's game.

                                                            The Realm of the Scarlet Dynasty could instead focus on building things for itself. Instead of building roads in foreign countries, they could build more roads in their own country. Instead of training more soldiers than they need for self-defense, they could train only as many soldiers as they need to defend their homeland, and so tax their farmers less to maintain national defense. Or they could hire out the legions to foreign nations as mercenary defenders, which would be an additional source of national revenue without the same costs associated with military occupation, which would still lead to taxing the farmers less.

                                                            If the farmers are taxed less, they seldom suffer from famine or malnutrition. They can produce enough to feed themselves and still have surplus goods to trade with each other and other peasants. The commoner-level economy starts to grow and thrive, creating a middle class, which creates a demand for education. With a more educated commoner class, they discover new techniques and tools for improving agricultural production further, as well as discovering new products to create and exchange with each other. More wealth and assets are generated in an iterative process that produces more wealth and more assets.

                                                            This same process would be repeated in the Threshold if the Scarlet Empire wasn't forcing those countries to tax their farmers to death to pay the Realm's tribute (Danegeld), and forcing its citizens to house the Realm's occupying soldiers.

                                                            This is a long game, but not a fool's game. There are no rebellions, there are fewer wars (since foreign trade is far more profitable for both sides), and no one nation becomes dependent on another nation for its quality of life.

                                                            If the Blessed Isle were, tomorrow, somehow be cut off from the rest of Creation, its economy would collapse in short order because it doesn't produce nearly enough wealth at home to sustain itself. They have the raw resources, but not the infrastructure to process them.

                                                            Meanwhile, the Threshold would shortly experience a renaissance as Satrapies stop taxing their farmers and let them get back to the business of surviving and making the world iteratively better for themselves and their children. No longer forced to pay Realm tributes, countries would stop raiding their neighbors, so those neighbors could also stop devoting so many resources to defense and return to the business of living and making the world iteratively better for themselves and their children.

                                                            Imperialism creates a blackhole of resources and human energy, which ultimately degrades the entire world.

                                                            formerly Tornado Wolf, formerly Inugami
