            Responding directly to the question posed by the thread: I suppose it depends on what you think Lunars are about. I mean, obviously, one could say they're about a lot of things, but if we look roughly at what they represent and advance within the setting, compared to other released Exalt-types...

            To me, Solars are mostly about improving things. Given that improvement is extremely subjective, Solar games focus heavily on what can be fixed, what should be encouraged, and what should be destroyed. The Realm is clearly broken, but Solar opinions may differ on whether it's worth salvaging. Deconstruction requires games to challenge what it means for things to be "better."

            To me, Dragon-Blooded are mostly about legacy. It's natural for short-lived mortals to want to leave a better world for their offspring than they had, and it's natural for this to lead to conflict with other clashing legacies, including changes wrought by younger generations, who see things very differently. Dragon-Blooded expand this conflict, and explore the fact that power hoarded across generations becomes a tyrannical force, which requires more and more work to maintain. Deconstruction requires games to challenge whether securing a legacy is worth the price paid.

            To me, Lunars are mostly about freedom, to be who and what they want, and to relate to the world in a way that suits them. But freedom is personalized, and sometimes groups of people acting in good faith have ways of life that are mutually exclusive. The privileged enjoy rare freedoms, whether earned by oppressing others under the guise of fair competition or divine right. Lunars have incredible freedoms, enough to allow them to see life from a variety of vantage points, and determine whether it's worth risking their own freedom to secure freedom for others. Deconstruction requires games to challenge who deserves freedom, given that so many cultures' definitions of freedom are incompatible.

            Given all of this, that the Realm (a tight-knit, oppressive, collectivist culture) is fundamentally inimical to what Lunars (a loosely-organized, laissez-faire, individualist culture) are about, and that it takes up half the known world, it would be strange if the Lunars focused less on the Realm than they do. They exist in the game (not necessarily in the setting) to challenge what the Realm stands for, and if we shied away from that, we'd end up with, well, the classic "but what do Lunars actually do?" question. A game about freedom doesn't really mean all that much without it being challenged, and without being encouraged to advance it in-setting.

            If this feels like a monofocus (particularly since attention has been specifically paid to talking about the Lunars who don't fit into this paradigm), then it would seem likely that the OP has a different understanding of what Dragon-Blooded and/or Lunars are about than I do.
