            Okay, finish it off with the iconic power of our Lunar Exalted.



            A part of fluff establishing the principle and why the foundation is in the sacred hunt, and the describing the basic mechanical premise; a miscellaneous action costing a commitment of four motes. I’ve gone a bit back and forth on whether or not I like shapeshifting requiring a commitment, but in the end I have to suppose that it comes down on the side of a mechanical and balance necessity, over image matters of a Lunar being able to maintain an alternative shape indefinitely without effort. Then there’s a sidebar both establishing the limits of that shapeshifting, and clarifying to the best extent possible on what does not qualify as a mundane animal for the purpose of it (particularly in light of the larger range of Creation’s fauna). This one I’m all for, from the sense in the past about the original shapeshifting becoming diffuse and obscure under increasingly elaborate forms.

            So, what are the specifics of an animal shape… Essence, Willpower, Initiative, health, Attributes, Abilities, Resolve and Guile stay the same (the As in there surprise me a bit, but let’s see what else there is). Can’t use mutation Merits that aren’t coming from Charms and the Tell (so I guess that resolves my question of the upper limits of its appearance). Right, so the Lunar’s Attribute and Ability stays the same, but they can use actions the animal could do with its pool if its better, which counts towards the dice cap (so you’re not getting the full scope out of that tyrant lizard by default). The Lunar still gets to use their own stats for other actions, possibly penalized if they end up unsuited to the shape in question. When it comes to attacks in particular, the Lunar can get much more benefit out of the increased damage rolls, including being able to put their full Strength Excellency on it, and even for a form where the Lunar’s natural attack pool is better, the animal’s weapon provides a slight accuracy bonus. They’re also not limited in performing feats of strength, but that comes with a cost of being limited to their form’s strength (presumably barring appropriate Charms).

            What else… using the animal’s Evasion and Parry counts as Charm dice, but their soak and Hardness don’t, and they can also get the use of special abilities and Merits (which also count towards the cap). In anticipation of this one, I went back over some of the dinosaurs, and some of those have some really good armour. A Lunar in the form of, say, an ankylosaur is somebody that a less powerful opponent such as a Terrestrial will be hard pressed to inflict damage on at all.

            The Lunars can train themselves to use the mundane latent abilities of their animal forms, with the particularly good benefit of letting such a development apply to all forms capable of it; teach your hawk shape to peck out the eyes, and you won’t need additional training to do it while you’re a pigeon. And last of all their language skills become sharply limited in an obvious manner.

            All of that under the caveat that becoming capable of Legendary or Miniscule Size requires the appropriate Charms. If I’m reading correctly, a Lunar who performs the sacred hunt on animals with those sizes without the appropriate Charms still acquires the forms, just can’t transform into them yet. I’m unsure how to read the point about having them as spirit shapes; I guess it’s something like that shape still informing your hybrid body and Totemic powers, but you may not be able to become them. I already know for a fact that it’s not a matter of turning into a miniature tyrant lizard.

            Ah, I had wondered if mutated animals would count as magical for shape acquisition purposes. So the answer is no, but to prevent them from becoming an easy source of Supernatural Merits, the value of those Merits is added to the cost of turning into them. Makes sense, and possibly resolves some questions about beings created by sorcery.

            Hmm, here at the end of the animal stuff, I wonder about the spirit shape… no exception is made for it, and this would be the place, so I guess it’s like any other animal shape. That might be necessary under these circumstances, although I must admit to missing the concept of “true forms”.

            Now, for human shapes, even if you don’t get their knowledge and memories, I’m inclined to view the matter of copying their “semblance” as meaning that you not only get their appearance, but a few nuances such as their gait, how they carry themselves and the way that they talk; enough to keep the form from having severe intrinsic shortcomings as a disguise. I’m thinking things like having to go out of your way to not have a limp, even if you don’t feel any actual limited mobility or discomfort. I think this doesn’t contradict the sidebar about Guile; if actions like that are needed to recognise differences, they can hardly be obvious. Other than that, yeah, not getting the shape’s stats seems sensible all around.

            And how about how all of this is acquired, in the sacred hunt? I’ll admit, with the descriptions of some of the alternative hunt Charms, I was expecting something a bit more intricate, but under the circumstances I guess that a “sacred hunt” acquired through physical intimacy just amounts to declaring your intention to take the person’s shape before you do your thing with them. I have mixed feelings; I did like some of the connotations of something with a bit of a deeper intricacy and ritual to it, but must admit that acquiring new forms is such a big deal for playing Lunars that such might create a sense of tedium, and it’s not strictly out of character for Lunars. Still, that it allows for indirect killing resolves a question I had in response to a point from somebody else: they said that having an animal’s shape connotes already being more powerful than it, but here I can see just shooting a tyrant lizard with an arrow tipped in nasty poison and following it until it dies, if you want more combat prowess than you’re natively capable of.

            I do like the idea that hunting people for their shapes is intense enough to cause a Tie to form.

            Bit of Storyteller advice to skip hunts that aren’t really necessary on stage, and a neat point that Lunars don’t need fancy powers to acquire the entirety of their libraries if they’re subjected to a deadly sacred hunt (with the same rule applying for any other transforming human targeted).

            I think the rules cover all of the bases, but one thing I miss a little bit is any description of the appearance and experience of the changing form. Sure, even with such a thing people are likely to picture it however they want, and there’s probably never going to be a description that isn’t unsatisfying (if not silly) to someone, but I do think it could be a decent place to add a bit more characterisation to Lunar magic.

            Next is the Tell, and after the amount of build up I’ve been getting from other references I’m particularly interested to see how this functions.

            Okay, basic idea is still the same, a feature that is characteristic to a Lunar and remains consistent across shapes; unlike the prior Edition, it doesn’t need to be specifically based on the spirit shape, or even overtly supernatural. I’ve always been a bit unclear on if that applies to other human forms, but when the capacity to gain such is now innate and it straight up says that the Tell appears in any form, I guess there’s no question. I’ll admit that, personally, something about that has never fully sat right with me; it’s not an objection to the Tell in principle, just the sense that the stolen human form feels indelibly marred if it’s obligated to carry something like your iconic face scar. Maybe I’d be better off just looking at the concealment rules more in terms of the Tell actually being obscured upon an insufficient observer, rather than present but unnoticed.

            Okay, rules about seeing through it, something that the last Edition at least made very convoluted. It appears that the innate concealment of the Tell applies even in the original human form, so it’s just a flat difficulty of 7 for a character to roll Perception + Awareness to perceive it; I’m guessing that in the case of a Lunar’s core identity, there are features in which it’s less about literally seeing the feature, and more about realizing that it’s the one marked across their forms. For a person who’s detected the Tell before, they get three bonus successes to start, making the roll a lot more manageable (I’d say in a lot of cases that’s for people who have seen the Lunar when their anima was glowing), and it counts as a disguise for magic to help see through such. A person who succeeds can see the Tell, and make the connection to another form that they’ve seen the Tell in; if they fail, they don’t get to try again until the Lunar changes shape. An observer can gain some bonus dice if the Lunar is acting distinctly out of character, and even more if a read intentions or profile action has discerned more subtle discrepancies. The observation roll might be penalised, but the Tell can only be truly undetectable with the Subtle Silver Declaration Charm, and Tells as physical animal features can be modelled with the right Merits, in which case they become more apparent (presumably just in the true form), but still require this rigamarole to perceive and connect them in other forms; I presume you’re not obligated to take a mutation if you want your Tell to be claws or horns or something, it just leaves such a feature purely cosmetic, with no mechanical benefit.

            It’s a bit wordy, but I think that the basic concept is straightforward enough, and it covers all of the bases.

            Okay, a few points to round out shapeshifting… almost all equipment vanishes to Elsewhere when in an animal form, but moonsilver armour (and some weapons) can innately reshape itself appropriately, Martial Arts are completely incompatible with any animal shapes, good good, it doesn’t provide any healing and crippling injuries carry across all forms… ahh, concerns with size, this is of interest to me. The basic rule is that, if within a space that wouldn’t accommodate the shape turned into, the shapeshifting action needs to be flurried with an appropriate demolition feat to break those confines; if it fails or can’t be done, then the Lunar will simply not change. Changing shape while inside another character into something that would tear them apart from within is stated to simply not function, not any other kind of shapeshifting that would innately cause damage. That’s never been a premise I especially liked, so I’m ready to take it at face value. Honestly, I’m kind of good to not even bother contriving an explanation for it; suspension of disbelief will serve for me. There’s a final sidebar to basically remind that the rules can’t cover every eventuality, and not all combinations may work for all people, so adjust for taste.

            The rules for the Solar Bond are next, but I saw them ages ago. I don’t have much else to say on it, beyond reiterating that I like the manner in which it is mutable.

            Now, the Moonsilver Tattoos, something else I’ve been interested in the specifics of. We get a basic description of their value in fixing the Castes, as well as a clear description of the experience and implications of that for a Casteless character. There’s an explanation of how diverse the tattoos are capable of being, fulfilling basically any aesthetic preferences, and how in addition to fixing Caste they provide complete immunity to the Wyld, and magic that would alter their shape without their consent. A one mote commitment allows the tattoos to be concealed indefinitely so long as the Lunar’s anima isn’t glowing.

            But the most interesting departure from prior Editions is how the tattoos explicitly aren’t an Artifact. On the one hand, that means they can possess no other powers as they could in the past, and would have particularly big implications with Evocations. On the other, the whole attitude to applying them has been overhauled; where before, it was somewhat proprietary (including the use of a Charm), here it’s rendered as virtually trivial, with the idea being that so long as a Lunar has the materials and knowledge (with a note on how most Silver Pact members teach the process freely), it almost takes care of itself. Even making a dice roll for it is recommended to be exclusive to high-pressure situations.

            I actually think this is really cool for a number of reasons. The basic one is that it frees players and characters to treat the matter with as much or as little ceremony as desired; it can be a big deal where a carefully considered initiate goes before a council and makes a big thing of declaring their intended Caste, and then a respected shahan-ya steps forward to reverently apply the tattoos while reciting some narrative about Luna… or can be done in the field by a young Lunar who learned it a week ago to help get the fellow they’ve discovered right into the thick of things (the latter is a scenario I find especially compelling). Not to mention the freedom to not be reliant on the Pact for tattooing. The wording does at least limit it to requiring another to perform it for you, but I prefer that as well, given the intimacy I ascribe to fixing a Caste. Overall, it’s very interesting to me.

            (Incidentally, I never got around to saying it in the Silver Pact chapter, but I also love how the Pact as a whole isn’t described as having standardised ceremony or procedure, meaning that anything from interactions between individual Lunars to the format of councils can be just played by ear. I like the notion that approaching one shahan-ya might be something that requires very intricate and precise displays of etiquette and ceremony, and approaching another might be as simple as walking up to their door and shouting for their attention, and that the standards of the Pact as a whole mean that the one who prefers the former can’t be too pissy if approached by somebody in the manner of the latter.)

            Okay, what can Lunar XP tell me about character and Caste? …Ehh, for the most part, for the three Castes, not much that wouldn’t be apparent just from reading their descriptions; the advantage of them being so broad and down-to-earth, I suppose. Though I do like the trickster element for a Changing Moon role bonus of solving a problem that they created for themselves, that’s very Loki, and I’m liking the premise that the No Moons are the ones who actually foster communication between people. The Casteless one actually takes the prize here: the idea of role bonus for an achievement that lets them recognise something about themselves is good enough, but what I find especially interesting is the idea of XP for such accomplishments in basically… being a Lunar. The most basic essence found in being a face stealing shapeshifter. It’s actually a rather compelling bit of additional characterisation to being Casteless. And yeah, also getting such from effectively protecting some system they’ve invested themselves in is also neat.

            Training times and raising Essence fairly standard… all right, bring it home with the shiny new Lunar Great Curse!

            Errrbpaprrr… riiigght, so where for a Solar it’s a case of things conflicting within them or contrary to their most dearly held values or illustrations of the limits of their power break into them and compel them to extreme and dramatic displays of action, either to express their frustrations or try and redress their grievances, for Lunars it’s stress and strain causing the Curse to appeal to their innate monstrosity, and trying to tip their natural balance in favour of that. A lot of the basic functions of how that reaps Limit are the same as for Solars, with action contrary to Major and Defining Intimacies calling for rolls and a Limit Trigger, and the samples and standards of the Trigger are very similar to Solar ones, although I have a bias to impress upon them a more emotional context (especially provocation of their inner rage)… actually, scratch that, I do see something innate to the text in which the subject of the difficulties is always focused on the Lunar (and occasionally their Circle).. yes, a lot of Solar triggers are about their response to things happening around them, things happening to others or by others that the Solar can’t stop, but for the Lunar it’s all about what has happened to them, what is hurting or hindering or constraining them. In essence, I could collectively characterise it as a chain that a Lunar is still struggling to be free from.

            The result of it reaching a breaking point is not a Virtue Flaw, a perversion of heroic character, but a Monstrous Urge, a compulsion to act in a manner forsaking human reason or sensibility. The basic mechanics function the same, but the resulting activity… let’s see, the examples are furiously acquiring and hoarding something of material value, going about obnoxiously proclaiming and proving their own perceived superiority, having an extreme violent response to anything posing the slightest impediment or inconvenience, sowing chaos and disrupting stability for its own sake, becoming an uncompromising avenging beast, fixating on a specific target to stalk and harass from a distance, conducting all of their business in a manner intended to maximize terror for everybody involved and witnessing… aw jeez, designating a category of living being to obsessively hunt, slay and devour (which can include humans), and obsessing over being worshipped and beloved and engaging in grandiose displays to draw that attention.

            So yeah, I find this to be sufficiently distinct from the Solar Curse as to be interesting, and really round out the characterization of Lunars and how they’re perceived by others.

            What’s interesting to me is that, in my eyes, a Virtue Flaw is something that could be perceived by the right person as the Solar still conducting themselves heroically, even if the manner becomes extreme. I feel that it can often be harder to separate from the Solar’s general behaviours and ambitions, and even if it gets to the point where an observer (like a Sidereal) is recognising that something has gone terribly wrong, it might conceal itself in the image of a Solar that has become too unreserved and unrestrained in the exercise of their power.

            For me, Monstrous Urge looks like it would generally be less covert. That it can often seem a lot more like a very extreme shift in behaviour, into an activity that is monstrous in its priorities, something that many could view and see the Lunar not acting normally. Where it most closely aligns to how a more lucid Lunar might act, it unfortunately aligns with some of the worst things that their powers can achieve, or activities it might guide them towards, meaning it’s also a Curse that illustrates and emphasizes the most disturbing things about the Lunar ethos. We’re told that in the functional Lunar, humanity and monstrosity are in balance and can be freely shifted, but a person who has seen what they become when their wrath or frustration boils over, it probably seems more like the monstrosity is something lurking beneath the Lunar’s surface, and enough provocation can let it loose. In my view, Monstrous Urges might be consistently scarier than Virtue Flaws.

            It could be seen as going some ways towards why the Sidereals didn’t trust the Lunars with the Usurpation, even if they couldn’t be the highest priority in sealing away. That in the eyes of Sidereals, Solars can eventually be corrupted by the extent of their power, and the things they can do with that corruption are strong enough to be intolerable, they would see the innate mercurial nature of Lunar power as something that ultimately can’t be predicted or controlled, even in a more isolated circumstance.

            The thing I’d find most unnerving in that scenario is the possibility that some Lunars are also under that delusion. Something about what Lunar Exaltation represents, the innately liberating quality of it, the degree to which it is concerned with matters of identity, makes the Great Curse tainting that with an idea that they’re innately unbalanced, that their liberation exposes a horror, feel like a greater crime by the Primordials than the other Curses.

            I mean jeez, that power gave Leviathan a body that properly expresses his identity. It’s got to kind of suck if he’s also under the impression that it’s a power that means getting too frustrated can cause him to fly off the handle and go around obsessively eating people.


            So yeah, Traits chapter ended on a high (if somewhat sombre) note. Hopefully I’ll get a decent break before more Charms.

                                        My image of the Primordial War has increasingly shifted away from the conception that was more explicit in Second (and kind of implicit in First) as a widespread, dedicated, organised system managed by the gods, than... kind of like the parts of the Silmarillion after the Battle of Sudden Flame. So you've got somebody like Solar Tuor being prompted by Sidereal Ulmo to journey to the hidden Dragon Blooded refuge of Gondolin and rise to great renown among their ranks, except maybe this time you convince them to go off and make direct war against something significant to the Primordials, and are at the front of their efforts.

                                        Like, I already formed a conception a few years ago of the different Exalted types emerging independently (if around the same time), and for the most part getting together and organising to do battle against the Primordials was something that they had to manage on their own. The Sidereals were the main connective tissue; the ones more consistently in communion with the gods, and dedicated to pulling a lot of strings to bring lots of Exalted together and pointing them at things that could strategically or literally hurt Primordials. And you'd get stuff like Solar Samson being enslaved for years by a group of Lintha, before finally getting the right opportunity to bring the house down upon them, except maybe this time Samson lives.

                                        The point is, in that model, the Lunars now have a place in which, for the most part, they're off doing their own thing, and the unions of Solars and Dragon Blooded are often content to give them a bit of a wide berth. In a lot of cases, it results in Lunars who functionally serve as a vanguard of Exalted assaults, often throwing themselves with gleeful wild abandon against Primordial forces or insinuating themselves into their secrets and societies in order to sew discord. Within that picture, no Castes felt like a good fit; that Luna's gift was a powerful liberation, to set themselves against the fetters of the enemies of the gods, and joyously tearing them apart.

                                        It also helps put the origin of the Silver Pact into context for me; that their initial conception of the world governed by the Exalted was utter freedom, against which a Solar proclaiming authority provoked extreme rage and drew so many of them magnetically just on principle. And in that concentration, they got to experience two things; a much much wider experience of one another (not to say they never associated in the war, just not on that scale), and a stronger firsthand look at how impressive the Solars were, experiencing a kind of opposition that the demons and monsters had never offered them.

                                        It cooled down their moods, and both sides realised that there was an opportunity to make concord. So they drew on the examples of their respective patrons, and engaged in a union of Chosen of Sun and Moon; three hundred Lawgivers to three hundred shapeshifters, with the remaining one hundred standing in witness. The Lunars vowed to serve, protect, teach and test the world that the Solars would create, the Solars vowed honour and fidelity to the Lunars, who would ever be the first in their counsels. And in the consecration of that union, the Lunars gained the capacity to form Castes, to engage in the roles they pledged to the Realm; guardian, courtier, priest, trickster and loremaster. It constituted giving up some freedoms*; their forms became a little bit more immutable, the powers of their animas would not shift and transition, the affirmation of self-discovery replaced with that of fulfilling their chosen duties. But it also gave them stability, a place in the world, and considerable power.

                                        Then the Usurpation happened, and a lot of the foundation of that stability cracked. Fortunately, the lessons of the First Age prevented the Lunars from merely returning to the anarchy of their progenitors; they forged new, simpler, more raw Castes to protect themselves and what they valued in a world of new enemies, and make unrelenting war upon them.

                                        * I'm picturing First Age Castes as a bit more rigid than Second Age ones, with something like lesser choice in key Attributes.

                                                            Now, for my own read on the first snippet of Charm preview:

                                                            Nest-Raiding Slyness: Dexterity’s Heart’s Blood Charm, through which a shape of person (or animal, for once) can be acquired be getting into a target’s home while present, not getting caught, but also doing something that makes it clear they were there in a way hurtful or outrageous to the prey. I like this in basic principle for giving Lunars an element of transgression, for it being another representation of a trickster archetype, and for what it implies about Dexterity Charms in general, the likelihood that they will be more than just the physical.

                                                            Twisting Penumbra Veil: But for the benefit of the physical, there’s this power to perform the unique action of clashing an attack with a Stealth roll, to not only stop it but immediately move into concealment. I like a few things for this one; taking advantage of the Attribute quality for pulling application of an Ability into an unusual circumstance, and being another instance where it feels like an animalistic and mystical quality are being represented at the same time, neither more prominent than the other. That and feeling as though this could be the crux of a fighting style, pointing Full Moons in general towards something more diverse than the stereotypical direct rampager.

                                                            Living Hive Transformation: Still, for something more directly animalistic (even if remaining rather witchy and creepy), I don’t think you can beat having the Lunar’s body writhe with miniature bestial transformations. I feel as though this one might satisfy people who want something more protean in Lunars, without diffusing their whole characteristics. And mechanically… hmm, commit 3 motes for a scene to make small, but penetrating, decisive counterattacks any time somebody at close range hits you, especially when they’re trying to grapple. What I like in this is a sense of Lunar fighting including a persistent denial of options to an enemy, or at least punishing them for trying to power through anyway. Plus girding Deadly Beastman Transformation a bit with the idea that it can pull some other Simple Charms along with it.

                                                            Raiton’s Dark Auspice: Intelligence Charm to give a bit of that world walker attitude, which I know people often felt was lacking in the direction of the Underworld. I think it’s another good instance of a multi-faceted Lunar Charm, and I absolutely love the complete weirdness to a ghost of somebody whose shape the Lunar has feeling a (virtual) Major Tie of friendship for them; you don’t get a Protean benefit for it, but imagine using a Charm on a ghost while actually being in their form?

                                                            Insidious Lunar Transformation: Do a bit of that Circe thing, feeding a person some blood to forcefully transform people into animals. Or animals into other animals, technically. Perhaps permanently for a strong Lunar against a weak-willed target. I wonder what the shelf life is on the blood after it’s been shed (considering the idea of tricking consumption), and implications that might have for setting up several people at once. Still, even for one, I’m imagining a Dynast being summoned to their mother’s home, and being greeted by her seated in her stately chair in the shape of an oclot or something, one of the attendants explaining that she seems to have been cursed by a Lunar. Still, it also has a number of viable applications besides impeding or weakening a person; helping allies or charges out of tight spots, for instance. Overall, a nice Charm.

                                                            And last of all,

                                                            God-Skinning Hunter Art: Something to incorporate a bit of rough Lunar imagery into the act of crafting Artifacts, reaping gold points from the Essence of a supernatural being towards the process of crafting something including their remains, as well as getting the opportunity to immediately awaken some Evocations, either for oneself, somebody who helped in the killing, or one’s Mate. I like this for the hunter and skinner imagery, and adding a distinct and powerful Lunar take on the crafting system.
