                    Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                    Canon Lunars are silver Solars. TAW Lunars are silver Infernals.
                    so damned sick of that comparison.

                    No, TAW's are not 'Silver Infernals', especially not the revised version. Having Charms that follow thematic trees rather than strict, straightforward mechanical progression is not unusual in Exalted; if anything, it's Solars who are unusual in building so much of their charmset around the latter. Yes, the original TAW's had some effects copied from Infernals. Infernals were the best-built rules in town at the time, and TAW had 400 Charms back then, mostly written by two people. Of course some of them were lazier jobs than others.

                    - - -

                    That out of the way, yes, TAW's focus more on the weirder aspects of Luna. For my part, I think that's a good thing. I think pigeonholing Lunars as barbarian werewolves is shallow and limiting, because it reflects only a tiny part of lunar (small l) mythology.

                    Which is not to say that barbarian werewolves isn't a thing Lunars should be able to do, and TAW's can do that, but if you look into the mythology of the moon and figures associated with it (example; Tezcatlipoca, the Smoking Mirror, Enemy of Both Sides, Lord of the Near and Far), they usually go to a lot of weird and wonderful places. Focusing on that also lets you build a more effective contrast with Solars, because once you're delving into the weirder myths it's a small step to things like Dionysus, and then you've got the Apollonian/Dionysian contrast. Mythology is full of this stuff that's absolutely ripe for Lunar inspiration. Seriously, I have nine pages of reference material for TAW inspiration as part of a supplement I'm working on, and I didn't even look that hard. Passing all that over to focus on barbarian werewolves and only barbarian werewolves seems almost criminally small-minded to me.

                                                        Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                                                        Lunars are not Mystique for a reason, though - by broadening the definition of "shapeshifter" to that broad a degree, you get an incoherent mess - which is what we don't want for a third time in a row.

                                                        Imagine if Sidereals drew on every myth related to the concept of Heaven - the angelic Judeo-Christian stuff would clash pretty damned hard with the Chinese bureaucracy heaven, and readers wouldn't know what to make of it.
                                                        Okay, first off, the stuff Mystique does is the sort of thing you can easily replicate with Solar Larceny. I seriously disagree with the idea that having Lunar shapeshifting being able to represent that is at all onerous.

                                                        Second, Sidereals don't draw on myths related to the concept of heaven. Or at least, their magic doesn't. Sidereals are fatespinners, prophets and puppetmasters, not the Heaven Exalted. Heaven is where they live, not who they are.

                                                        But even if Sidereals were the Heaven Exalted, I disagree that drawing on every myth related to the idea of Heaven would necessarily make an incoherent mess. That kind of incoherence doesn't happen just by drawing on different myths - drawing on different sources is a good thing, when it's done intelligently. A wide array of sources is a large part, for example, of why Tolkien's work has endured for so long; he had a deep knowledge of North European myth, epic, and folklore, which he filtered through Roman Catholic theology, which he also knew a fair bit about, and his own literary skill and imagination.

                                                        Incoherence, at least to me, is a product of trying to maintain clashing elements of disparate sources without resolving them, not simply drawing on a wide array of sources to begin with. It's a large part of why Exalted itself is such a compelling setting for me, because it draws on so many different areas of history and mythology and, for the most, harmonizes them. Witness SLS talking about the four levels that Exalted operates on in his thinking.
