                                                            08-15-2014, 07:55 PM
                                                            Part of the core rules include the notion that if you're being affected by some power, then OOC you are aware of it so that you can choose to spend resources to resist it. You are aware when a surprise attack launches, so that your character can reflexively (and instinctively) activate a surprise-negator to protect himself. The notion is the same with social attacks - you are aware of it OOC when brainwashing magic is used, even before you have your character spend Willpower or use a perfect, because the effect can affect your character and by the RAW you have a right to counter it (though framing social interaction in the attack/defense parlance of combat - that should have been the first sign that the whole notion was fucked, when you approach making friends or persuading people in some similar manner as to sticking a knife in them. Digression).

                                                            So in the game you have "secret" effects which can be used on your character (surprise attacks, traps, written social attacks delivered through Letter-Within-a-Letter Technique or through Charms that make the social attack a surprise), which the player is allowed if not required to be aware of, in order to be able to respond if they choose to do so.

                                                            Unless you're not allowed to be aware of them in certain fringe cases that seem quite arbitrarily chosen. It's these cases that make statements against the core rules, which themselves are either good or ill, but value judgments aside they're still the core rules and for consistency's sake you should either say "unless it's Obvious you have no way of knowing there is magic in that request or attack and you have to respond as you choose - usually with maximum paranoia" or "you know OOC everything about the attack, and if you're not worried about it IC then of course you can ignore it but the player is explicitly required to be allowed to make that decision for the character, not have a result imposed on him." The latter is the baseline rule. Except when it isn't, for arbitrary reasons.

                                                            The Tell therefore wants to use the existing social combat mechanics, except it also wants to negate the portions of them that require the defender to be informed of the effects bearing down on him. Which is really fucked up:
                                                            Originally posted by Exalted, p. 173
                                                            In general, social attacks follow the same 10 steps as normal attacks, with slight changes appropriate to the nature of such encounters. These differences are explained here, with steps not listed following the exact rules of physical combat.
                                                            1) Declare Attack: The attacker must decide the traits used for the attack. . . The declaration should also indicate the desired effect of the attack so the defender knows how to respond.
                                                            Originally posted by Exalted, p. 145
                                                            1) Declaration of Attack: The attacker’s player states that the character is attacking and what Charms (if any) he will activate to enhance the attack, excepting reroll effects. If the attack cannot be stopped by a particular mode of defense (dodge or parry), the attacker’s player must include this information in the declaration.
                                                            Look, I don't particularly care for the extent of these rules, but they exist. I would prefer that the OOC rules tend towards more "wow, I have no idea if there's a Charm in that attack or not, so I'm gonna have to risk not deploying my own Charms to counter it" (this is assuming a 3e where perfects are not spammable and are either once-a-scene type things or have a cool down) over "I know everything about this attack so meh, my (M)DV can handle it." But we're talking 2e mechanics, which do require that social attacks follow the same rules. So, ok, an DMDV of <12 always misses the tell...oh wait, if you're in human shape, that drops, if it's the Wyld Hunt or people alerted by the Wyld Hunt and/or who have previously seen the Lunar, it drops more...the Tell starts out by violating core transparency rules, and then it's completely shit against the people you actually might want to hide from, so it's terrible on both ends of the spectrum.

                                                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                                            Excellencies represent deliberate charm use. My understanding is you can't use them in a passive sense, to defend against attacks of which you're not aware. Against an unnoticeable/unexpected attack (such as the Tell), you're forced to rely solely on static defenses and charms that explicitly allow you to use them in that situation.
                                                            This requires the ST to declare "something unknown is affecting you, do you want to try and counter it? It's considered surprise" to which most players will say "what do I need to do? I have surprise negators for physical and social combat and also perfects for physical and social effects." So you pop a Charm that negates social surprise, and then either activate a social perfect or use an Excellency, and that is that. That's the draw of transparency, and I hate it, but swinging on the pendulum too far the other way means your characters are killed from surprise or secretly convinced to give up their goals and live in huts, and that's bad too.

                                                            Sure, you as an ST can just have notes from your players' sheets and know their capabilities, and maybe only tell them if you know they have a chance of beating it. That's not a bad idea though I think some players would really resent the rules being used that way.

                                                            And deliberate use IC is completely governed by OOC decisions, which must be allowed to be informed. Characters are explicitly unaware of unexpected attacks unless their player negates surprise; however the player has a right to know they're being attacked from surprise, even if they can't or don't counter the surprise. This is a great example of the problem with couching a "this thing about me resists being seen" in social combat terms, because Exalted explicitly have Charms to get around that and players will use them given the chance, and that just kills everything about Lunars being able to blend in.
