                Originally posted by Tiresias View Post

                No, it doesn't, because if the Vision of Bronze is just Sidereal propaganda, the moral argument surrounding the Great Prophecy is invalidated and the Bronze Faction is automatically evil. The entire point of the Usurpation is that it was morally ambiguous and both sides had perfectly valid points.
                First: It's important to look at the perspective of how the Sidereal are being used in game; discussing this out of that context is a waste of time. Exalted's setting is useful only insofar as it informs actual play. If the Vision of Bronze is propaganda, the Sidereals are set up as a major antagonist in a Solar game, or extremely manipulative bastards in a Dragon Blooded game. That is an extremely functional role for them to take; they're already kinda set up that way anyway, but this provides a few layers to peel through when examining their motives. It doesn't assume the entire bronze faction was in on it, opening the doors to a complete re-examining of the Sidereal's position as an act of a Solar Circle; revealing the treachery could be used as a wedge to bring the Sidereals in general back behind the Solars, something that could be a good stepping stone in a campaign centered around unifying groups against massive threats to Creation, or around restoration of first age wonder.

                If you're playing Sidereals, the faction determines where you go: You would almost certainly not have it be propaganda in a Bronze Faction Sidereal game. It'd be a powerful revelation in a Gold Faction game though; that's the sort of secret that'd completely shake up the existing order. There are a lot of stories to be told around that.

                Second: The Great Prophecy taken at face value is overwhelmingly in favor of Bronze. There's barely any moral ambiguity there at all. Gold faction doesn't really gain any strength until after Usurpation for a reason, certainly there is zero dissent at the time Usurpation is decided upon, because it'd have taken one Sidereal breaking ranks to stop the whole thing. In fact most of the ambiguity about the Great Prophecy stems from the Sidereal's version of the great curse, which casts doubt on the whole thing anyway.

                                        I don't think anyone's ever said that the Vision of Bronze was propaganda, although that might be an interesting idea to pursue in your own game. Rather, the source of tension in the game is a matter of what could have been, had they attempted to bring the Vision of Gold into being, rather than pursuing the Vision of Bronze. A Bronze loyalist would say that the Usurpation was justified and necessary, considering the potential for disaster from pursuing the Vision of Gold; a Gold loyalist, on the other hand, would point out that things aren't exactly going swimmingly right now, and a couple hundred shining heroes might have been able to avert all this disaster.

                                        The point is not to prove one right or wrong (unless you want there to be a clear answer in your game), but rather to show that there is no way to prove the Gold Faction would've done any better. It is an eternal "what if?", prone to endless speculation and regret.

                                        Second: The Great Prophecy taken at face value is overwhelmingly in favor of Bronze. There's barely any moral ambiguity there at all. Gold faction doesn't really gain any strength until after Usurpation for a reason, certainly there is zero dissent at the time Usurpation is decided upon, because it'd have taken one Sidereal breaking ranks to stop the whole thing. In fact most of the ambiguity about the Great Prophecy stems from the Sidereal's version of the great curse, which casts doubt on the whole thing anyway.
                                        ...what? No, the Great Prophecy is not "overwhelmingly" in favor of Bronze. There's a reason that there was a Gold Faction prior to the Usurpation, and why the Bronze Faction had to straight up murder all of the Golds that got in their way- the Gold Faction were the Sidereals who were willing to take a huge risk for a brighter future, while the Bronze Faction were those Sidereals who thought the potential cost of the Gold Vision failing far outweighed the potential benefits. You see this sort of struggle in governments and businesses all the time, where someone proposes a new, risky idea but gets shot down because everyone else would prefer to do things like they've always done, rather than take a chance on something new (and potentially much better).

                                        Of course, there's a reason people hate risks: if you fail, you're going to lose something, even if it's "only" sunk costs in terms of time, money or other resources. Sometimes, you can lose a lot more than that, depending on what exactly you're betting on. The Gold Vision was the ultimate gamble, with the ultimate jackpot- and with the whole world as the wager. On the one hand, you can see why a lot of people would turn down that wager, even if it had decent odds... but on the other hand, you can also see why someone might take the wager as well. Which side of the line you fell on likely was a factor of how risk-averse you were... and it doesn't take a lot of risk-aversion to not want to gamble one the fate of the world.
