    If I want to make a pro-social character, something of a leader, let's say, than I don't care one way or another for "trickster" or a "shaman" or a "warrior". If I want to make a quirky anti-social genius character, than I don't care one way or another for "trickster" or a "shaman" or a "warrior". If I want to make a mercenary goon with only motvation being gold, I don't care one way or another for "trickster" or a "shaman" or a "warrior"

    I don't think it's necessary to think in those categories. It's like saying "we're gonna give players the ability to pick powers and abilities whcih fit with their characters being a trickster or a shaman or a warrior and nothing more". And I don't see a point in doing that.

    Just pick a theme and make mechanics that will compliment the theme. If it's physical prowess, thank great, that's ok. I'm sure people can come up with archetypes that will work best with this theme on their own, the devs shouldn't limit themselves to creating mechanics that will describe only these three archetypes.

You could still make a Lunar leader but the powers would lend themselves slightly better to outsider cunning than straightforward leading.

                            When I think about it, I realise I have two basic standards of inspiration for Lunars; shapeshifting and totem/anthropomorphism mythology. When it comes to implementation in the context of Exalted, for the former, that generally means changing from one thing into another, either in whole or in part, or kind of stretching out or rearranging what you already have. For the latter, sometimes, occasionally, it means adopting some resonant animal traits or mechanisms, but mostly it means adopting the logic of why totem mythology is used, and reapplying it to people. Like, Renard is a fox not because he does anything that necessitates the biology of a fox, but because the idea of fox conveys his persona.
