                            0. What is the purpose of this charm? Is it "I want to look like not me", "I want to look like I live in this area / like I am a foreigner", "I want to be able to use shapeshifting to get part of the way to disguising myself as someone", "I want to change my level of beauty", "I want to look like a general type of person"? Depending on your design goal, "don't look like a specific person" might or might not matter. Appearance might not matter, or it might be the only reason to use this charm.


                            1. I want to mostly look like me, but different enough that people won't recognize me. Or close enough that I will look like a sibling, or a twin, or an older/younger version of myself. How do you mechanically handle "I want to look this percentage like someone"
                            2. As written, you should be able to impersonate someone's features, but it should take time and depend on your references. If you work from a photo of Hatewheel, it should be harder than if you follow Hatewheel around for a week and update your features as you watch him.
                            3. Is mass conserved?
                            4. Is there a difference between "the way a person moves" and "physical features that influence movement"? If I try to make myself as fat as Negazer, will I have trouble walking? Does this let me imitate a supermodel's strut? What about imitating the movement of King Edward (super-extreme scoliosis, it was basically physically impossible for him to stand up straight). Can I imitate the way a martial artist walks if I'm not a martial artist. If you can't imitate how someone moves, how does that affect disguise difficulty?
                            5. Is this a "puff of smoke and you look different", or a "push and pinch your face like molding clay" transformation? The puff of smoke is probably easier to rein in since you can't go back and 'improve' your transformation.

                            A roundabout way of "no copying someone" might be, say, you can use this to look like a generic person of country, or some region that has a single god. But then would all Lunars look the same if they shift into "form of Great Forks"?
                            What if there is a specific, limited palette of changes (a la MMORPG character creation)? Say you can change hair length, hair color can ONLY be blonde, red, or brunette, skin can only be one of 4 skin tones?

                            Actually, Luna has what 5 primary forms? Would limiting this charm to "5 forms that are alternate yous" work? Maybe also letting you remove one alternate you and replace it with another alternate you every so often? It gives a good reason for not imitating specific people, gives you enough forms that you can survive someone revealing secret identity #2 by switching to id #3, and a swap-out rule would let you move from far South to far North, and get rid of forms that would stick out like a sore thumb. I'm reminded of Moorcock's Eternal Champion. Also gives a reason for Appearance not changing.

                            I would also want to know if this is meant to be a quick, temporary change (Sherlock in the 1st movie disguising as a beggar), longterm sleeper agent, or something in between (Mission Impossible disguise would last for a scene or two).

                                            Revisiting this question in light of the corebook's preliminary rules on The Tell.

                                            For the record, the rule is that the Lunar's Tell is plainly obvious in his human form, and only someone who has seen the Tell has the opportunity to spot the Tell in ANY other form, which requires being suspicious enough to make a "Detect/Pierce Disguise" action (page 224) with a dice roll of Perception + Awareness at a flat difficulty of 7.


                                            Using a Charm to copy someone (or something) else's appearance without Heart's Blood can be balanced by making the Lunar's Tell more obvious in that guise; anywhere from "just as plainly obvious as in his human form" to as something as simple as "you still have to make the same check, but you don't need to have seen the Tell before", with various gradations in between.

                                            It's also balanced by the fact that the Lunar is only gaining the target's appearance, and none of their dice-pools or any of Charm-derived advantages that trigger off of Heart's Blood, such as potentially recalling memories, stealing magical attunements to distant manses, or assuming the thread of their Fate.
