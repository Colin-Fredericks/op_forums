            I'm going to expand real quick here.

            RPGs are cool because they're fusion of roleplaying storytelling and a game. We're not just sitting around telling cool stories where we can do whatever we want, we can attempt whatever we want, but the rules are there to tell us when it is appropriate for something to succeed. We have a difficulty 3 lock to pick, the legendary super thief picks that almost every time, with his dicepool that he's modeled his character around, it is appropriate that he only fails that task like 5% of the time.


            Combat and initiative is done the same way, it's just a bit weird because unlike a lot of other games where it just says "How often is it appropriate for a character who's this physically strong to strike somebody that skilled in combat, and how much damage is it appropriate to deal?" Exalted says "When is it narratively appropriate for this attack to deal enough damage to slay a wounded opponent."

            I can roll Join Battle with my little dinosaurs, and decide that I want one of them to deal a real physical wound, but their initiative says that it is not, at this moment, narratively appropriate for them to be able to straight up kill an unwounded important character. It is only narratively appropriate if they deal a non-mortal wound or two in the initial ambush. Whereas Octavian who's spent the last two rounds beating the crap out of my exhausted DBs, and now has 30i, after such epic clashing and the characters just barely managing to keep his staff from crushing their skulls in, it is now narratively appropriate for Octavians hit to turn one of them into paste.

            The Hardness value that you get simply means that as long as you yourself are not crashed, it is never narratively appropriate to deal such a small wound. Any hit that actually drives metal deep into your body is going to be one that somebody had to work for at least a little.
