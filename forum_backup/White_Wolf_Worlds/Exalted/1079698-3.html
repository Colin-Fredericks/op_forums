                        Basically I feel like, more than anyone else, Sidereals tend to go where the job takes them.

                        And the job usually takes them out of Yu-Shan but not always. Yu-Shan is also their home base, their HQ, but they don't do their jobs in the HQ mostly, they are in fact the external agents of said HQ. The Bureau of Destiny has it's esoteric role but it also has its practical role and in that role the Sidereals carry out various different missions to fulfill the objectives of the Bureau in service of Heaven.

                        I like that being a Sidereal is about doing a job. But when I look at all of the various material which I take inspiration from for Sidereals, it's generally not something where I picture them sitting in an office working. They often have organisations backing them up, but superspies, journalists, assassins, monster hunters, mysterious advisors, occult masters and so on tend to be portrayed as out there getting involved with other people in some way, not doing paperwork. Sometimes, they have to go back to base to do paperwork and deal with an editor/superior/etc but not always and not that often.

                        Like a Sidereal might have their station in, say, Prasad. And in that station they do various jobs. On occasion they get called back to Heaven for whatever reason, which is often a bit inconvenient and annoying and they might find themselves out of their element, but at the same time, they do belong and they do have a right to be there, in the Bureau of Destiny, for a meeting with whichever superior wants to see them personally for whatever reason. It's a bit unusual not to send a memo, though, wonder why they're personally pulling me back in for a few days? That sort of thing.

First and Second edition gave quite a few people that idea, that the primary focus of Sidereal characters is Yu-Shan, not Creation. I hope 3rd edition spends a bit more time making it clear that Yu-Shan is a cool extra in a Sidereal's game, but the vast bulk of Sidereal's time is spent in Creation. For example, the Conventions should generally be more important to most Sidereal player characters (who are low Essence) than the Bureau's, because those player characters spend most of their time in Creation, dealing with the Conventions and only rarely ever actually go to Yu-Shan and deal with the Bureau as a whole or as a location directly.

                            When they do deal with the Bureau, it will be by written prayer messages or dreams, not really in person. Spending a too much time describing the inner works of the Bureau of Destiny tends to make it seem like Sidereal spend a heck of a lot of time in Yu-Shan rather than Creation. And I think that perception is ultimately somewhat bad for the Sidereal Exalted as a whole.

                            I think, in the end, the goal should be to make it clear to would-be Sidereal players (who are playing low Essence, young Sidereal) that going to Yu-Shan will probably be a really awesome highlight of the Sidereal experience, rather than something that their characters do every couple of weeks.

                                                            04-18-2017, 05:51 PM
                                                            Originally posted by Paradim View Post

                                                            I'm disagreeing with the assumption that it has to be either Creation or Yu-Shan.

                                                            Why not both?

                                                            For example, the Conventions are functions of the Bureau of Destiny. Describe the place they have in that bureaucracy and how the Conventions impact Creation with their involvement!
                                                            A lot of it comes down to perception. The more word count you devote to something, the more important that subject feels to the splat. When the Sidereals book spends the bulk of its setting-focused word count describing Yu-Shan, and Sidereal's interactions with Yu-Shan, and a fairly small amount of word count describing Sidereal's releationships with Creation proper, then it makes Yu-Shan feel central to the Sidereal game experience, and what they actually do in Creation a bit of an afterthought.

                                                            So I'm not saying get rid of Yu-Shan from the Sidereal book, but unlike 1st and 2nd edition, I hope 3rd edition spends more word count in putting Creation front and center in the Sidereal story line. Because in the earlier editions, Creation tended to come off as just a place you go to do missions before you come back to your "real" home, Yu-Shan, where all of the important Sidereal stuff happens. And I don't think that creating the perception of Creation as secondary and of less importance was good for the Sidereal Exalted or the rest of the setting.

                                                            Spending a lot of time talking about Yu-Shan, and making it feel like that's the central core for Sidereals is, much like the Loom of Fate, or the philosophy behind Sidereal Charms, or how Sidereal are "The Super Exalted" who do everything is awesome in a Sidereal focused game. But ultimately most games take place in Creation - it's supposed to be the center of Exalted after all, and this means that the Sidereal book (like every other book with the exception of Alchemicals) needs to make the primary goal, "Make sure this group fits into Creation." How they fit into other worlds might be interesting, if there's enough room to discuss it, but how a given group fits into Creation is the most important factor.

                                                            Ultimately the Sidereal book needs to be approached with the idea that the Sidereal are simply another type of Exalted. The authors need to focusing on ensuring that the setting fluff and mechanics smoothly integrate into the rest of the setting, and remember that Creation is the most important primary setting piece in Exalted. Making Creation feel secondary or of less importance should be something that should absolutely be avoided.
