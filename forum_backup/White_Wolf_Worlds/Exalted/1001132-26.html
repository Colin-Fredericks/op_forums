            1) Fire arrows are certainly possible, but there are several...... well, not drawbacks, more caveats, with using them.

            In the Hundred Kingdoms, Men-at-Arms are responsible for owning, maintaining and supplying the majority of their own equipment. This includes ammunition. It isn't an official law (although Kasai could certainly make it so, especially since he has mandated ownership of spears and bows for every household), but generally each Man-at-Arms is going to show up to a muster with at least 24 arrows. The militia are going to have less, especially since 1) arrows are expensive, 2) you don't need that many to hunt animals, which is what the men drawn for militia-duty were using their bows for, but even still, chances are they are going to have at least 10.

            The more forward-thinking Longbowmen are going to bring more, probably at least 60, and the more forward-thinking commanders are going to bring arrows along with the food for their army.

            Please note that those numbers might sound like criminally-few, but they really aren't. Yes, well-trained longbowmen can loose arrows at about 12/minute, but that is an 'OH SHIT' rate of fire, really only used in a pitched battle when the enemy is advancing. More 'usual' rates of fire are more along the lines of 6/minute or even 4/minute, which is still pretty goddamn fast, especially when there are 60 arrows aimed in your general direction, and when you consider that the average longbow is 5+ feet long and has a draw-weight approaching 100lbs. Using a longbow is exhausting, and the officers of longbow regiments will usually institute 'rotating fire' drills, where different sections of the regiment alternate fire, so as to give the archers a break.

            What this has to do with fire-arrows, I have no idea. But, still.....

            Fire-arrows are probably the most common anti-fortification weapon in the Hundred Kingdoms, as most of the buildings, castles included, are built of wood and wattle-and-daub. However, they are pretty uncommon, especially in field-battles, as they require a high degree of preparation beforehand to be used.

            "fire arrows" are longer, thicker (and heavier) than regular 'war arrows', and almost always have a cage-like head as opposed to a stabbing point. This means they 1) take longer to load, 2) have less killing potential, and 3) weigh more than regular arrows, so the soldiers can carry less. There aren't that many in the supply depot of Kasai's army, for example.

            However, arrows-that-are-on-fire can be improvised pretty simply, although they suffer the same drawbacks. if not more, as 'real' fire arrows. Take small shafts of bamboo, tie on some feathers or even leaves to one end, and jam some pine-pitch or other flammable substance into the open front. They don't have to be good or even decent as arrows, just 'enough'.

                        Camp was much like losing your virginity: hot, sweaty, at times uncomfortable …… and over and done with far too quickly. I had a good time, thanks for the well-wishes.

                        “Tell me what you all think. <That includes you, as you will be part of this. Any recommendations, based on what you know of your own forces?>”

                        The war-leader from the Paskamansett pipes up, “< Well, the youngbloods would serve as excellent flanking attackers. Of all the forces here, they would be the most capable of climbing the sides of the hillfort, and the best suited to sneaking around through the forests and over the hills to the sides of the fort. The only thing is, they don’t have very much ‘staying power’>”

The youth-bands are the very definition of ‘impetuous’, and will be truly savage in the attack, eager for glory and reputation as they are….. but chances are they will rout if faced with any serious resistance. They don’t have armor, and don’t fight in formation, so their ability to defend themselves is hampered. Generally, we use the youth-bands as skirmishers and ambushers, screens along the line of battle.>”

                        The other chiefs laugh, “< Chief, if you gave them enough booze, they would charge into the very gates of hell. They are more than willing to do whatever we tell them to do, especially if there is blood and glory involved. Getting them to stay there and to stop is the hard part.>”

                        Masanori rouses himself, “Along that section of hillside, which, again, is the flattest and gentlest, there is a series of earthen embankments, ditches and the like. Most of them were here when I got here, and I didn’t focus too much effort into building them up, instead focusing effort on repairing what I could of the wall.”

                        Another officer asks, “And those were…..?”

                        “Not very much, to my embarrassment. After 5 years, I had built a palisade around the western perimeter and about the gatehouse, which is on the southern side of the fortress. Pine logs, set on end into earthen berms, supported by drystone and thornbushes. The rest of the wall circumference was, and still is, little more than piles of rubble and earth. I reinforced that with piles of brush and more thornbushes, but that was mainly it.”

                        “…..why so little reinforcement?”

                        Masanori sighs, “Because it takes a great deal of efforts to make bandits do anything, much less work together. A great portion of those 5 years involved me stomping on other bandit-groups across the Akibara, and their attention-spans for anything other than wine, women or song was …lacking. Even with the crucifixions.”

                        You interrupt Masanori’s ruminations, “So, what you are telling me is that the eastern and northern sides of the fortress are basically unfortified?”

                        He shakes his head, “No, my Lord. While all those sides of the fortress have are berms and bushes, they will still take some effort to overcome. The thorns around here grow an inch long and more, and I piled them deep on the embankments.”

                        “Will they burn?”

                        He blinks, “…..Probably. I planted most of the bushes, but whatever didn’t fit I just piled on top. They should be good and dried by now….. do you have any mangonels, to launch fire?”

                        “…..Do I have any what?”

                        “…. I see.” He rubs his chin in thought, “ I suppose if you use enough fire-arrows, you could set the bushes alight. It hasn’t rained for a few days, and what water there is should be well-drained by now….”

                        Masanori continues, “ Anyways, the only ‘real’ way into the fortress is the main gate, and is where I concentrated most of my rebuilding efforts. The palisade is strongest there, and I ordered a watchtower built. The gate is made of slabs of pine, reinforced with oak beams. A good ram should be able to take it down, with a decent amount of effort…. The problem is getting to the gate in the first place.”

                        He points again to the map, “ there is a road, of sorts, leading to the main gate. About 30 feet wide, it was even paved at one point, and any number of troops could travel along it without getting hung up. The main issue is that the entirety of the road, from start to finish, is within sight and bowshot of the fortress. The berms block in both sides of the road, looping across the face of the hill, but they don’t provide cover from the fortress. So, while your Lordships troops push a ram up the hill, they will be under fire from archers the entire way. Whomever built this fortress knew what they were doing….”

Trust me, it was hard enough organizing the troops I brought over to you, much less other bandit-groups under my banner. On top of that, most bandits don’t have any form of standardization, especially when it comes to weaponry. Everyone fights as an individual. There isn’t going to be any massed-volleys of arrows coming at you, just a whole bunch of individual ones.” He stares at the fortress for a moment, “ Ahhhh, I estimate there is maybe …… 400 fighters with ranged weapons in the fortress?”

Other than that….. most of the youth-bands will have some sort of long-range missile weapon, usually a sling or a bow. Others carry javelins.>”

We sit here for 4 days, feasting and enjoying ourselves, while they start eating roots and rats. They will see it, and that is going to start hurting real bad, if you get my drift. They will go to their bosses and demand more food, and the bosses will start arguing and fighting with each other. They certainly won’t be working together.”

They will be demoralized, hungry, divided, while we will be fresh, reinforced, and eager. We will need the time to build a ram and mantlets, anyways, so why not make the most use of that time?”

                        You turn to the rest of your officers, “ The rest of you…. settle in. Let us fortify the camp a bit more, put in some proper facilities; a field kitchen, some latrines, that sort of thing. If the kitchen could be placed upwind of Dun Nashuon, all the better. Set the troops on full-rations; give the bandits something to salivate over, and Sir Shin Takeshi will be along in a few days with more food anyways. Other than that, supervise the construction of assault and siege-equipment; a ram, mantlets, ladders, the works.”

                        Turning to the Tribal warleaders, “< As for you….. please have your youth-bands hunt and forage around the fortress. The more food we have, the better, and it will give them a chance to scout out where they would prefer to attack.>”

                        And so begins the Battle of Dun Nashuon, the next morning at least.

                        To your surprise, you find that there isn’t all that much for you to do, not personally at least. The expanding of camp and the building of siege-equipment is by-and-large handled by your retainers. The scouting and hunting of game is done almost automatically, and brace after brace of deer, mountain goat and rabbit are sent to the camp kitchen like clockwork. Trees are cleared from the paths of approach, setting up a staging area for the coming assault.

                        All the while, you watch the fortress, and those in the fortress watch you. Mostly, you are just ….. bored? Bored. Granted, you have the battle-plan to write, but that is mostly copying down the suggestions of Sgt. Oxblood, writing and revising and rewriting, until your hands ache. The only bit of excitement you get for the first two days is the arrival of Shin Takeshi with his milita-units and the food-reserves, and you knew about that for two days beforehand, forewarned by your scouts.

Having a God/Goddess as the commissioner has its benefits, apparently, and you can’t wait to actually try the thing in battle, moratorium on fighting or no), you finally settle in to get some sleep, only to be awoken by the clarion call of a bugle sounding the picket alarm: enemies on the perimeter!

You don’t have time to pull on your full panoply, and instead grab your dagger, thrusting it through the cord holding your undershirt shut, and pull Moonlight Over Water from it’s sheath. The white-oak shaft is surprisingly cold, as the night is warm, and your fingers numb at the touch. Puzzled, you go to examine the weapon in the dim light of your desk-lamp, but get startled from your reverie by the repeated bugle-blast and the throwing-back of the door-flap of your pavilion. Standing on the other side of the opening is one of the soldiers assigned to your personal-guard, halberd held at the ready.

                        “Forgive me, Lord Jianghu, but there reports of enemies attacking!”

                        “I am aware, thank you…. How many and where?”

                        “ Northern side of the palisade, by the main gate, it sounds like. No idea on the number.”

                        “ Understood. Send my retainer, Masanori, in to assist me in arming myself.”

                        “My Lord, sir Masanori isn’t present. I don’t know where he would be found, either.”

                        The camp is a frenzy of movement, as soldiers are woken up by the sound of the bugle, stumbling from their bedrolls and struggling to equip themselves by the light of banked fires. Most of the Men-at-Arms are relatively calm, having seen this before, but the militiamen are all-but panicking. Generally, an attack at night means a burned farmstead and dead family members, and the several of the militiamen you pass are in the throes of terror at the prospect of combat. You do your best to settle them with calm words and a steady hand, but you find yourself almost as unsettled as they.

The mist swallows everything a few scant feet away from the palisade, and as you peer into it, you feel your heart start to pound.

Walking usually helps, and I was walking the ramparts when I heard the alarm sound. I was one of the first ones here. I will be more responsible in the future.”

He is greedy for status, yet a coward at heart, and will suck your dick only so long as it takes him to stab you in the back. The other bandit-leaders likely didn’t take any of his shit; he wasn’t very popular, and he has apparently run here with his tail between his legs.”

You….. well, you just don’t want to die, simple as that. I joined him to protect people from men like you, who would slit their own mother’s throat if you thought it would get you some respect.”

                        “….Bullshit. Not a week ago, you were talking a big game about how you were going to kick ‘the kids’ ass! Now you drink his piss and call it wine?”

                        Masanori glances at you apologetically, “…..Sorry, I had to make my defection a surprise. That meant I had to make you seem lesser than you are.” Turning again to the field, “ Regardless, what does that have to do with your defection? What, pray tell, do you bring to the bargaining table?”

                        “I bring my followers, the same as you!”

                        Masanori says nothing, then turns to you and speaks quietly, “Well, that changes things, my Lord.”

                        You frown, “Does he have a lot of troops?”

                        Masanori shakes his head, “No. He was a mid-level bandit-leader, little more. He was a glorified toady, attaching himself to larger, more skillful bandit-captains. They didn’t like him very much, especially since he betrayed them as soon as a better position came along, but they couldn’t turn down the bodies he brought with him. As for troops…… eh. He had a lot of ‘fresh-meat’ under him. Good for shaking down peasants, not so good in a fight. But….. that can be amended with proper training, and they won’t have that many bad habits to break…”

                        “So, can I join your side yet? We are hungry, and the night is cold….”

                        You step forward, calling out, “Come forwards, into the light, let me get a look at you!”, while giving Sergeant Oxblood a significant glance. He nods, and gestures to the archers in the parade-ground. They step up onto the wall-walk, and at the command “NOCK”, draw arrows and set them to bowstrings.

                        As they do so, the bandits slowly, as if filled with trepidation, file forwards out of the fog. When they enter the range of the lantern’s light, they kneel and lay their weapons at their feet.

                        You pan your gaze across the assembled ruffians, several dozen all-told, noting that they wear a varied ensemble of armor and gear, with most of the fighters wearing little-to-no armor. Weapons are mostly spears, in various degrees of poor quality, with a few bows and only one or two sidearms among the throng. One of the bandits, the one in the heaviest armor (although that doesn’t amount to much!), remains standing, and when he catches a glimpse of you, leaning heavily on your spear, he laughs out loud.

He drops to a knee, yet keeps looking you full in the face, and under his half-helm you see an ugly leer spread across his face. As your anger rises, you note in sudden shock that the shaft of Moonlight Over Water is frigid, making your left arm go numb to the elbow, and the exposed skin of your face is pale and prickly with the cold.                                                             You fine folks chose "army service reward" as part of your recruitment efforts, meaning after 5 years of military service, the mustered-out soldiers will be granted a 40-acre plot of land and an ox.

                                                            By that, I basically sought to emulate the Roman Empire after the Marian Reforms: serve in the army, and receive lands after mustering out, usually in regions they conquer, along with citizenship. This served many purposes: 1) decrease the amount of urban poor that had no lands of their own, 2) increase the population in frontier regions, especially with people that can defend themselves, so on and so forth. Citizenship on top that was just gravy.

                                                            In Jianghu, about 1/3 of the land isn't even touched; fields lay fallow, orchards choked with overgrowth,pastures lay barren. And that isn't even touching the lands that will become available when the Akibara gets cut back, or the hills around Jianghu get settled.

                                                            After 5 years of service, Kasai will cede these lands to the veterans of his armies, who will then do what anyone with a new lease on life would do: start a family, pick up a trade, grow crops. Remember: 40 acres is a pretty nice plot of land in the Hundred Kingdoms, and an ox is a pretty expensive draft animal, useful for pulling a plow or carrying goods to market.

                                                            Once the lands are actually put to good use, the economy of Jianghu will expand: food will be shipped out, trade-goods will be produced, and perhaps most importantly, taxes will be paid. Part of the deal is that the mustered-out soldiers will be granted membership in the Peasant class. Peasants pay taxes on the land they own, and also serve as soldiers in time of war.

                                                            However, before the 5 years of service is up, Kasai functionally has an army that he can do whatever he wants with, whenever he likes. Remember: Peasant Men-at-Arms are farmers first, and fighters second, and the 'campaign season' in the Hundred Kingdoms is only about 60 days long. Since these new soldiers are not yet farmers, instead being 'professionals" who soldier full-time..... they don't have to go home at the end of the summer to prevent crop failures. All this nonsense about Kasai worrying about the time left before he has to send the army home won't be an issue at all when the new soldiers start arriving.

                                                            Want to surprise-attack the forces of Yue in the beginning of spring? Sure, go ahead! ROFLStomp your way through Sanada Kotaro's domain, while the peasants are working at the autumn harvest? Have fun! Be an utterly-crazy bastard, and order a march in the dead of winter? Entirely possible!

                                                            That sort of behavior will make the other Lords in the Hundred Kingdoms piss their fine silk kimono's, as that screws with the traditional military balance of "campaign during summer ONLY". Almost all aspects of military methodology in the Hundred Kingdoms is oriented around that little fact of reality ..... and Kasai just broke it over his knee.

                                                            When they aren't being used, Kasai could station the army at points across Jianghu, allowing him to be able to intercept threats before they become major, or work on road-building, land-clearing, bandit-killing, aka stuff that needs to get done.
