        It is commonly believed amongst the various peoples of Creation that when they die, the Wheel of Reincarnation sends their spirits first through Lethe, to wash away all memory of their previous lives, then deposits them in the Well of Souls to await their Rebirth into the world of Mortals. This is because this story is both true and verifiable by those who interact with gods and spirits who are being honest.

        However, this explanation by itself doesn't appear to have any moral structure; those who were "good" in life seem to be treated the same way as the "bad." Creation's inhabitants thus tend to add some "insights" to this process, in order to support their preferred moral beliefs.

        Thanks to the Immaculate's efforts, the belief in Enlightenment and reincarnation "tiers" is very common, but perhaps just as common is this view:

        How Lethe removes the memories of a person's life depends on the person in question:

        1. If the person was "good," they go to a Paradise, such as Elysium, or Valhalla, or (confusingly enough) Heaven. There, the worthy souls are plied with their culture's prescribed rewards, giving them such constant delight and joy that all recollection of their pale, troubled lives is washed away, and they can move on to their next (hopefully better) life without a hint of regret.

        2. If the person was "bad," a dark fate awaits them: plunged into realms of torment like Tartarus, the Abyss, or Hel, each and every sin is tallied and accounted for with suitable (often ironic) punishments. These tortures scourge the soul of the sinner, cruelly stripping away everything that made them who they were until nothing remains, before roughly hurling them into the Well to await their lesser incarnation and possible atonement.

        3. A third possiblity is often added for those who were neither exceptional nor heinous: a grey, featureless Purgatory, Limbo, or Plain of Asphodel, in which those who were merely "average" wander aimlessly through the mists for untold eons, neither tormented nor rewarded. The unending journey and lack of stimulation ultimately causes the shades to slowly forget everything, and they eventually meander into the Well for another chance at doing better (or worse).

        Whether or not any of this is true, who can say? But it is certainly an appealing story for many, and it has lodged itself into the myths and teachings of countless cultures across the world.

        *Yes, this is a very elaborate justification for characters saying "go to Hel" to strangers and having the desired effect.*

            In an effort to justify an Exalted / World of Darkness crossover while still keeping the two universes wholly separate, I have come up with the following Headcanon:

            Zen Mu was a prototype Creation created by the Primordials, along with the prototypes for what would eventually become humans. Eventually they abandoned Zen Mu because they couldn't get some unknown quantity about Zen Mu to work properly without scrapping the entire project, so they did, leaving it to slowly erode in the Wyld.

            The Raksha decided it was malleable enough to their chaotic whims that it wasn't completely offensive to their sensibilities, and would amuse themselves by toying around with the proto-humans at their leisure, eventually becoming akin to gods in the porto-human legends.

            After the Primordial War, Zen Mu drifted on the ocean of chaos until it met a cosmic Lagrangian Point centered between the universe bodies of Malfease, the Corpse universe of the Underworld and the World-Body of Autochthon in Elsewhere exile. There it remained, slowly absorbing the Essence radiating off of those three universes while still remaining (mostly) isolated from them.

            This lead to magic as we know it slowly dying off, replaced by the technological miracles of Autochthon that we know today. Likewise, night terrors of the Lunar dreamt by the Neverborn eventually gave rise to the Werewolves as a physical manifestation of their nightmares. Mages and Vampires rose similarly, each a warped and distorted reflection of the Exalted from nightmares of the Neverborn, seeped in the hatred of Malfeas, and coaxed by the Essence of Autochthon in to a coherent form.

            (this probably doesn't hold up when looked at closely and compared to Exalted's and the World of Darkness' cosmology, but hey, headcanon)

                    I have many but here are a few off of the top of my head.

                    1) The Elemental Poles & Pole Lands
                    - The Elemental Poles reside within Creation and are easily visible from within their own direction, and partially visible from a neighboring direction (Thus the Pole of Earth is visible from all directions on clear days). The only exception is the elemental pole of water which outwardly manifests as a maelstrom, and therefore not visible above the sea.
                    - The Elemental Poles are extra dimensional in that there is more space around them as u get closer and you can only reach them via very specific paths or powers. It also means that he only accurate maps of creation will have a 3D set of cones set onto the elemental poles. It also means that it is faster to circle around at a specific diameter rather than cross near the poles.
                    - As one gets "further in" to the elemental pole's perimeter the element dominates more and more. This truly impacts the Creation the most at the elemental poles of Water, Earth and Wood, as the Air and Fire "pole lands" are far too inhospitable. The Elemental Pole of wood is actually inhabited with kingdoms of Elementals, Wood-folk, Gaian/Sextes Jylis Devas, and Gods.
                    - The Elemental Pole of Wood is called Yggdrasil by easterners, and from different directions appears as different kinds of trees. From the North it is a pine. From the West it is a fruit tree, from the South a Banyan tree, from the East is an Oak. There are legends of special approaches to the Yggdrasil that reveal yet more facets of the tree. Each version of the Tree is entirely separate with different settlements, gods, elementals, etc... and you may move between the facets of the tree via special portals and paths.

                    2) Calibration
                    - Calibration Timing: Calibration can happen at different times of the year, but the Celestial Bureaucracy of Fate always informs the gods as it approaches. During the High First Age Calibration only happened once a century or so. During the Low First Age it was once a decade. It has been steadily decreasing the average time between each cycle so that it is now, on average once per year.
                    - During Calibration the Sun and Moon do not rise. Before a Calibration the world stocks up on candles, lantern oil, fire wood and similar items.
                    - The World is in various levels of darkness, with the Elemental Pole of Fire being the greatest light source (It lights the South in a "bright twilight" and giving a soft glow to southern sections of neighboring directions.
                    - The Sky is Not Empty: but allows naked eye viewing of certain celestial objects not normally visible (especially in the north), including: The Brown Red Star (Malfeas - which rises the first day of Calibration and sets on the final day), The Gray Star (Autochthon), the Comets, Celestial Archipelagos, the largest Wyld Vorticies, etc...
                    - Creatures of Darkness: Demons, Ghosts, and Horrors can be accidentally summoned through human actions, and some locations allow direct access for these beings to enter Creation. This means the Wyld Hunt and other Holy Orders regularly go on purges to cleanse the land of CoD.

                    3) Dragon Blooded Houses
                    - Lesser Houses: In my Creation there are perhaps 3 dozen lesser Houses of the Dynasty that rule territories in the threshold in the name of the Great Houses with some degree of autonomy. This was done by the Scarlet Empress to keep the Great Houses engaged in the Threshold but also to limit/clutter their command structure as well.
                    - Non-Dynastic Houses: Lookshy as the only meaningful nation of DB outside the realm always bothered me, so I added more. This also meant that DB champions were far more common than in the standard setting.

                    4) The Lost Empire of the North - The Eye of Autochthon was used to create a vast and powerful Empire that rivaled the Realm, but they vanished in an apocolypse when the Eye was misused.
                    - The Eye is an actual piece of Autochthon, part of his mind/a-3rd-circle-soul, and partly why the Autobot can't wake up. It was stolen from him by a team of Solars during the First Age which then sent him into Elsewhere on Autopilot.
                    - The Eye is 10% sentient. 90% Reality Altering/Generating Computer.
                    - The Eye Altered the fabric of Creation to make the Empire's Tech work beyond the normal limits of Thaumaturgy - Called Sciences.
                    - The Departure of the Eye removed this alteration,the Empire ground to an immediate halt as 90% of its Science failed.
                    - North is filled with the ruins of the Empire and its greatest legacy are large numbers of Automatons that still function and protect the ruins.
                    - A few ruins had Reality Engines that still power the Alteration for a radius around the ruin, allowing the Science to work. These settlements were crushed by the Realm when obvious so the only remaining ones are hidden in some way.

                                        I don't see the Ebon Dragon as objective evil. My headcanon: As the principle of deceit, his nature is to be dishonest. He really can't help it. But here is the thing: Dishonest about what? Well, about everything. Including who and what he is. But if he is dishonest about what he is, then what is he, really?

                                        Nothing. The Ebon Dragon is a hole in the logic of reality. He doesn't actually exist, except that he has tricked reality into thinking he does. Heck, he has tricked himself into thinking he exists. The Shadow of All Things is no more a real, discreet thing than a shadow is - after all, a shadow isn't really a thing, it's just a trick of your mind interpreting occluded light as having meaning.

                                        Sure, he can influence the world and he has this crazy plan to get out of Malfeas and that would probably suck. But ultimately the Principle of Deceit only has power through deceit; if you don't succumb to his lies he is, in the end, powerless. In fact, he has a rather nasty weakness, that he is aware of in kind of a peripheral way but not directly for obvious reasons: If a sufficiently powerful individual were to find out the truth of his nature and punch him in the face with an effect like Thunder Breaks the Clouds, he would realize that he doesn't actually exist and *poof*. Not even a Neverborn would be left. He has to lie to himself constantly, just to sustain his very existence.

                                        As far as why he isn't objective evil: Well, he's pretty subjectively evil, honestly. Mostly the Yozi all are. But the fact that he is lies personified doesn't make him any more evil than Malfeas is objectively evil for being rulership personified or SWLIHN is evil for being hierarchy personified. While it is an ugly truth, the truth is that the world would not work very well if deceit did not exist.

                                                        Oh, we're doing Ebon Dragon headcanon? Sweet, here's mine:

                                                        In the Time Before and the Age of Glory, the Dragon's Shadow was The Adversary. That is, it was his nature and task to test the works of his fellows, even if that meant challenging them to destruction. As the Primordials created and refined the concepts that went into their Creation, they brought each one to him, who stress-tested it to see if it would serve their needs, and consistently exposed their weaknesses and failings. Some appreciated this, many were frustrated by it, but all had to admit the usefulness of his function and how it improved their work.

                                                        And the Ultimate Darkness was pleased to serve in this fashion...save for once. He prided himself on being able to find *some* flaw, however small, in everything placed before him. And so, he tasted frustration himself when testing the nascent Unconquered Sun, as, for the first time, something was wrought that he could find no flaw in. Eventually, he resigned himself to admit the mastery of his Kings' work, but counted it as a slight that he would never forget....

                                                        Nor did he give up his testing ways, although the poor, benighted creatures the Titans populated their world with never seemed to realize how his interference with their lives and nations was all for their betterment. When they rose up, empowered by the traitor gods, he was the first to realize that the greatest among them partook of the Perfection that had best him once, and that they would resist his challenges in like manner, and so, he acknowledged their right to exist and win by surrendering.

                                                        That was the last choice the Dragon's Shadow ever made. The soul alterations inflicted upon him transformed him into the Ebon Dragon, who prides himself on retaining the most nature of his former self; this may be, but he has still changed, perhaps more than he knows.

                                                        The Yozis, remembering his talent at finding the weak points of anything, have given him the task of challenging the God's handiwork. But the Shadow of All Things no longer challenges to improve, so much as to corrupt and destroy. And even he may no not realize that his own self and actions are not exempt to his obsession...
