        I feel like we're going off-piest now.

        If I make a character designed to be the best combatant in the world, it's because I want to tell a story about the best combatant in the world.

        Exalted supports this.

        Exalted, further, explicitly tells you that 3 dots is the level of a skilled professional -- a real stand-out of exceptional talent.

        And the static difficulties support this.

        Your Parry 7 mortal absolutely cannot beat Octavian. They can beat a Blood Ape, maybe. They can't best two Blood Apes at once: and as First Circle Demons, Blood Apes are literally the weakest demons in the game.

        You can play an optimized Scholar who also just happens to be the greatest swordsman in the world. That's Raptors in an F-16 awesome. That's Exalted.

        But you are still playing literally the greatest swordsman in the world.

        If you feel that lacks narrative weight then that's not a system problem -- that's on you and your ST.

        Mortal NPCs aren't running round with Parry 7. Most Exalted NPCs aren't built that way. The average encounter isn't with foes who are the best in the world at what they do, so if you make a character who IS the best in the world then you will (usually) win. That's system working as intended. Oblivion style "bandits in Daedric armor" would be deeply unsatisfying for me. In Exalted, if you optimize yourself for combat, you should be an unstoppable terror in combat.

        And if you don't want to be an optimized, unstoppable terror in combat, then don't play literally the greatest combatant in the world.

        It's like playing an Epic Level D&D game and complaining that the only monsters in the MM that can challenge you are CR20+ which means you'll never get to fight a harpy or a wyvnn. The CR1-19 monsters are for level 1-19 characters: they're not for you. If you want to turn up and one-shot them with your awesomeness then so much the better. You are awesome, lean into it. Describe the fantastic abilities which make these beasts unstoppable to mortals then roll right over them. But recognise that most people are not the greatest swordsman in the world.

        If the ask is "more behemoths, more elemental dragons, more 2/3 Circle Demons, more elders", then I'm down with that.

        But if I'm in the top 1%, I don't want to play DBZ -- I want to play One Punch Man

                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                But if I'm in the top 1%, I don't want to play DBZ -- I want to play One Punch Man
                I see. Do you want to replicate Saitama's experience of becoming totally bored and disillusioned in fighting since he effortlessly overcomes every foe without fail? Because playing the One Punch Man in a system like Exalted 3e would be so horrendously boring. I know this because I've done it.

                I played a Brawl Supernal Dawn Caste named Simurgh, with the intention of making him the strongest warrior in the Empire of Prasad. I figured 'hell yeah, I'm gonna be a big badass dude who has bombastic awesome fights with powerful Dragonblooded and behemoths and giant godlings and takes on armies with nothing but his bare hands.'

                The reality was a series of ultimately identical battles that each ended in 2 rounds. Round 1: Simurgh wins Initiative with Awakening Eye and Crashes his opponent. In return, his opponent swipes back for paltry Withering Damage. Round 2: Simurgh makes a decisive attack and defeats his enemy. Rinse and repeat for 11 sessions.

                I found no enjoyment in this. Simurgh's obscene strength had no meaning. He could punch a dude through the air over 10 city blocks, but he's no better at say, running, than a normal person. He's a kaiju of dice tricks and combat math that nothing in the world can stand up to, and that got boring, fast. He's not even optimized particularly well: I chose Incarnated Fury Attack in character generation and forgot to take Falling Hammer Strike until session 10.

                I figured that, by investing so heavily into combat charms, I'd be signing up for big cool anime fights with fun back and forth and shifting advantage, but that couldn't be further from the truth. In my experience combat with Simurgh became less and less interesting the more I invested into it. This seems like a fault with the system to me.

                Though, this is all anecdotal. It could be that I wasn't seeking out the right enemies or something like that, but I'm not sure.
