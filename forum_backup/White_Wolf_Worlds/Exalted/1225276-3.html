                                            Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
                                            Which also makes no thematic sense because wrought iron was literally just a shittier version of steel! Pretty much every use of wrought iron was replaced with steel because it was the same application portfolio but superior!
                                            We are talking about myths told before even alchemy was much of a science. I don't think chemical composition really factors into matters all that much.

                                            During the dark ages in Europe, iron could be forged by most smiths in a coal fired forge (that alone would make almost any forged iron have a carbon content that would chemically speaking make it steel). But that wasn't enough to make what was called 'steel' at the time. Steel required higher than usual grade ore and crucible forging, as a mere coal fire wasn't hot enough to purify that ore sufficiently. I believe that's also where the term 'cold iron' comes form: it was smelted at a lower temperature than the superior steels.

                                            Chemically speaking, most types of iron were actually low grade steel, sure. But there is a different definition based on the practicalities involved.

                                            But if that's not enough: Iron literally is the difference between wild and tame animals.Wild horses didn't have horseshoes. Iron shoes mark a horse as domesticated and a tool or pet of civilisation.

                                                Look...

                                                Iron is metaphysically associated with Earth essence, harmful to the Raksha. The more you work it/alloy it/whatever, the less useful it will be against the Raksha.

                                                Also? Iron weapons are SUPPOSED to be shitty! Lots of old Raksha charms have things like "mortals get a bonus to resisting this if in contact with cold iron."

                                                It's not just about stabby times, it's about enabling Creation to feel more real by giving it superstitions. This guy who lives south of Winter Folk territory wears a horseshoe around his neck. This lady who lives near where the Lion Folk live wears an iron necklace when she goes out at night. This Guildsman who trades with the Raksha wears an iron coin in his shoe at all times to protect him from illusions and glamors.

                                                Weapons made from iron are probably ceremonial as often as not, and while they're technically usable against the Raksha, let's be real-a mortal's not gonna win that fight. It's mostly just there so the Raksha think twice about fucking with you, because if you get a hit in it's going that wound is going to have to become a long-term part of their story. Iron is there to remind the Fair Folk that you're real and can impose reality upon them.

                                                Steel just feels more symbolically mundane-it's less raw, less earthy, more ubiquitous, more useful, less esoteric. A magic weakness-metal should be something inconvenient to use and carry around, otherwise it'd be ubiquitous and feel less magic and interesting!
