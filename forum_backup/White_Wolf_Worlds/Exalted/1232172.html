    Discussing issues with the Solar Craft charm set

    07-03-2018, 01:46 PM
    I'm involved in a project to create charms for a different set of Exalted, and since that will involve charms that interact with Craft, I wanted to discuss the currently-largest Craft charm set, Solars, and determine what the issues are with it, so as to avoid such pitfalls in our own work.

    Note, this thread is not about discussing the basic Craft system, of project slots, craft XP, and so on. It may not be perfect, but it's what we've got, and I'm actually pretty satisfied with how it works on its own, before charms are applied. So, please, if you want to discuss the pros and cons of the basic Craft system, do it somewhere else. There's plenty of threads.

    With that out of the way, I personally think there's two main issues with the Solar Craft charms. First, there's just too flippin' many of them. It's the largest single charm tree currently, weighing in at 56 charms. I like Craft being a hefty charm set, with a lot of things to do, since "peerless craftsmen" is one of the major Solar archetypes. But the charmset currently uses a lot of those charms for what, in my opinion, are rather boring effects - just providing extra slots or craft XP. This also ties into my second big objection to the Craft charms as they stand, that they actually mostly subvert the whole point of the Craft system in general, that it provides a big incentive for a crafting-focused character to use their craft in play, looking for reasons to do crafting to earn craft XP. A whole bunch of Solar Craft charms simply provide a bunch of free craft XP, removing this incentive, and returning the Solar craftsman to their "downtime specialist" focus that they occupied in previous editions.

    So, here's how I'd address both of these issues: cut out a bunch of charms that just provide craft XP, or that make it more efficient. I'd probably cut or heavily modify Ages Echoing Wisdom, Peerless Paragon of Craft, Supreme Perfection of Craft, and Divine Transcendence of Craft - basically, all the charms that just provide free craft XP. I'd merge Tireless Workhorse Method, Dragon Soul Emergence, and God-Forge Within (the slot-granting charms) into a single charm that improves with Essence and repurchases. I'd similarly merge Efficient Craftsman Technique, Copper Spider Conception, and Spirit-Gathering Industry (the charms that deal with making various projects cost less craft XP). I might merge Brass Scales Falling, Red Anvils Ringing, and Chains Fall Away, but I do like how they encourage the actual craft objectives, so maybe not.

    I think these changes would at least condense the charm tree a fair bit, and remove some of the places where it avoids the craft system, rather than building on it. What do people think? Does anyone have any other suggestions about the Solar Craft charms? I'd welcome discussion of other homebrew charms, just as long as, as I said above, we don't get into discussions of the basic crafting system in general, and confine it to Solar or other Exalted charms that take the basic crafting system as their foundation.

      Then he got Sublime Transference and Supreme Perfection of Craft and within a story he had so much Craft XP that I stopped making him count it (he had something like 300 gold xp).

On the other hand, it can be quite important at helping players not get caught out by the system when, say, they've used all their silver xp and everyone's asking them to do a major project, and they have to somehow explain in-character that they physically can't do the major project they've done 20 times before today for some reason.)


      So, my overall point is... do have a few Craft XP charms, because not all games give crafters the opportunity/motive to do an absolute ton of basic projects (except if the player is jammy and gets sxp from 100 basic projects in a day, which I've seen... but that's probably not how it's supposed to work, and generally something the ST allowed because he was fed up of how the Crafters didn't seem to be able to do something). Just don't make them too good or too bad.

So you've got to think carefully about what other objectives will mean it's for stuff that actually matters. Saying, for example, Lunars get craft xp for making stuff out of creatures they've killed sounds obvious, but would mean they'd just make stuff for no purpose beyond silver xp, which isn't really the point.

      I think my main suggestion would be to have plenty of charms that let you do particular things with craft, rather than just more dice/craft xp/slots.

      For example, the DB craft set includes:
      Use Craft for a feat of demolition
      Craft an object which does an inspire roll on all who see it
      Do a major project as a combat action, for example make a weapon in the middle of a fight
      And the normal craft faster and ignore tools, like the Solar set.

      That's not actually that much, but then the DB craft set is pretty small (17 charms including the excellency). I think a little over half the charms are not about dice/slots/terminus/craft XP, whereas for Solars it's about a fifth I think.

          I'm gonna point out a particular problem I've noticed in the overall solar charmset but please correct me if I'm wrong. Almost all of the charms that grant free (non-craft) XP are in the craft tree.

          Exegesis of Distilled Form allows a conversion of White XP to regular XP
          Spirit-Stoking Elevation allows you to buy several charms directly by using White XP
          Supreme Celestial Focus lets you buy dots of craft with gold XP
          Design Beyond limit allows you to get an evocation for free.

          The only other charm I know of to gain xp via charm use is in lore. Tireless Learner Method allows you to gain back more xp than you lent out using Flowing Mind Prana. However, this is limited by the fact that it costs xp to use and on average you won't even gain back as much as you spend on it. If you spent 10xp on FMP then on average your first roll would get you 5 xp back. Second roll would only get you 3xp on average. You'd get 8xp back and spend 1 xp that can't be recovered. It's seriously diminishing returns after that.

          Considering that it's possible to get so much craft xp that you eventually just stop keeping track of it (according to some player experience) this would indicate that a craft heavy player could potentially start massively outspending their peers in regular xp. Even if this doesn't count towards essence this raises an eyebrow for me at least. I should note here that given the way the craft ability is structured, I don't think it's a problem to have a charm such as Supreme Celestial Focus to buy up lots of craft. There's a lot of diminishing returns with picking up extra types of craft after all. Not so with general conversion of wxp into xp.

          There are two explanations as to why these charms are in craft: First, there are so many craft charms and abilities that it was ruled unfair to a craft focused players vs other concepts and as such steps were taken to increase a crafter's ability to purchase all the craft charms. Second I think that somewhere along the way the writers stumbled over the terminology of craft xp and linked it too strongly to actual XP and thus wrote in charms to make them exchangeable.

          I feel that any new craft charm set should do away with any ability to buy XP with craft XP. And also significantly drop the number of craft charms as the OP has suggested.

                  As a general thought on the Solar Charmset:

                  You can do a huge amount of reduction (part of the problem really) by condensing some of the Charms into a single Charm that automatically upgrades as Craft X/Essence X. We don't need three Charms that add slots, they're not that potent, as already mentioned. So a Charm that gives you more Major at C1/E1, more Superior at C3/E3, and more Legendary at C5/E5. Two less Charms (including one that requires repurchases per slot), no function lost, less clutter of this sort of Charm in the trees.

                  The Craft Charms seem really obsessed with forcing repeat Charms for Basic/Major projects, then Superior/Legendary ones (if not split those two up to), which adds to the bloat with little positive impact. If gating off bonuses to higher ranked projects is important, you can do automatic upgrade Charms. Buying new Charms should really do something new, instead of, "remove the restriction of using the Charm on this thing," in something as big as the Craft Charms.

                  On my whole weird Charm Slots as Charm currency thing? I actually initially envisioned it for Solars. I just though that it could work better for Alchemicals when someone brought up Alchemicals in that thread, because it has a neat feel towards their Charm style (assuming it doesn't change in 3e).

                  When I was first thinking about it, I was still sticking to the figurative idea of it. A Solar isn't literally a walking workshop, but their excellence lets them make things with little to nothing like even the best mortal craftspeople need workshops for. It just seemed that the Charm aesthetic for the Alchemicals let them be actual workshops if you slot in the right Craft Charms and all.

                      armyofwhispers

                      You are super overestimating Exegesis of the Distilled Form, have Spirit-Stoking Elevation completely wrong, and are slightly underestimating Tireless Learner Method.

                      -Exegesis of the Distilled Form:

                      This is a very hard Charm to actually get to use. You need to complete 5 Legendary Projects before you get to use it. That is the creation of 5 N/A Level Artifacts/Manses, wonders of Creation shaking power.

                      You have to amass empire ruining amounts of resources, extremely rare components, and have reached the height of Solar crafting power to be able to complete the works.

                      I`d say the Charm Suite that will let you amass enough Craft XP, Craft Power, and speed to do this is around 25 Charms. That is 200 Experience. The limit EotDF gives you per activation is 20 Exp. So to even break even on your Craft investment will take you making 50 equivalents of Eyes of Autotochton, Mantles of Brigid, Emerald Thuribles, Ascendant Nova Phoenixes or Karvaras. And at the very minimum 10 stories, which could range from anywhere from 20 to 80 sessions, which with weekly play would take from 5 months to an year and a half.

                      That is if you manage to make 5 of the most powerful objects the world has ever seen every godamn story.

                      So no, EotDF is not a concern out of crazy white rooming. Consider yourself lucky if you get to use it a few times in the whole campaign, and in a long running campaign at that. That is if you're a Craft Supernal that invest super heavily in it. Most people will never get to use it.

                      -Spirit-Stoking Elevation:

                      This will let you fuel Lore, Occult and Medicine Charms that cost Experience with White Craft Experience by paying 5 WExp for every Experience. It does not let you buy Charms.

                      -Tireless Learner Method:

                      You actually roll all your Experience until you recover it. This was clarified by Vance a while ago.

                      So lets say in Story 1 you invest 10 Experience in Flowing Mind Prana. At the end of Story 1 you roll (and re-roll non successes once) and you get 5 sucesses, one of which is a 10, So you recover 6 Experience and keep 5 Experience to reroll at the end of the nest Story.

                      So you basically will never come up short, and every 10 you manage to roll is a bonus Experience you get.

                      -Wrap up:

                      So, there are some Experience shenenigans, but they involve Spirit-Stoking Elevation and Lore Teaching Charms, but this will let you give insane amount of Experience to other people, and even then at mid to high Essence levels, unless your Circle builds around and for it (and rotates who is teaching), and even then it will be limited by story constraints such as training times, and allowing the teacher to basically brainwash your character.

                      It is not a great problem, it is one very specific, mid-high Essence build, that very few people will stumble upon.

                          Solar Craft's main sins, to me, are a fewfold.

                          1) It over-values some resources. As noted already, it kind of puts too much work into slots. I think that in the end, there needed to be like...a slots Charm, tops. Something I did with my own houserules is at least have it so that if you're swimming in slots you could just do more with them. But in the base system, there's no need for a Charm to discount them, one to give you permaentns and then a later one to give you permanents fo antoher kind. Slots aren't that expensive, and that the Charms themselves can be overly complciated (Copper Spider Concentration is a mess) don't help.

                          2) It undervalues other things too. The extra craft xp Charms are not bad until you get to Peerless Paragon of Craft, which then more or less explodes the entire damned craft xp economy, invalidates every previous Charm, and to boot, kind of helps you circumvent ever doing things with rolls really to get craft xp again. This is before that again, they're spread out probably over too many Charms and often have systems that are more complext han they need to be, or in the case of I think the first couple, give so little real xp that they tend to rot on your character sheet after a bit.

                          3) A few of the Repair Charms have special highlight for being just really bad. This is in part because it seems like Morke might not have been clear that repairing is prettye asy, and anything that buffs normal craft will buff repairing things. As such, the Charms are pretty niche since they basically are like the already over-common broader Charms but to a specific task, and often are flat-out worse than the more general Charm.

                          4) The Power tree is actually not bad like, in powerwise. It lets Solars achieve pretty impressive feats of artifice. The issue is that I think while I like dice tricks, they kind of are insane about them. Things like triple-10s, buncha weird rerolls, the different double-Ns upgrade Charm. And even a Charm that lets you get more Charms, which I gather is just asking for player decision paralysis. The houserule set I use is by someone who did what they could to get the same general results, but with like...half the Charms and complexity. I think that it's not a question these Charms are bad, it's a question on whether these are Charms people would actually find enjoyable and I think they are where the threshold of fidgety dice tricks gets overkill for many.

                          5) There's a general trend I think with above of over-complication in general actually. Like, Focus-Shifitng Arete isn't a terrible Charm, but in the end, it's a lot of dice rolling and such to get over a +1 difficulty. Like....it should just be spend points, get effect. I think there are a few Charms in the Solar Charmset like this in general, mind. But this one always stands out to me as it's so much work for the effect it gives.

                          6) There's a notable lack I think of cool "wiz-bang" effects. Craftsman Needs No Tools, Vice-Miracle Technique, Dual-Magnus Prana, and the such are cool powers. But there's not really so much there like those. The houserules I use currently I like most in part because it has a few like that, like one that makes your workshop yours in a style similar to Ship-Claiming Stance for instance, or one that has this effect like a Dr. Who Sonic Screwdirver Artifact. I think a bit of the interest in making clever system tricks rather than Charms that made Craft characterful was a bit of a shame. More stuff to play on Crafting with Intimacies, Crafting towards specific kinds of things like food or art, branding, workshops, all that. I think the Solar Craft Charms focus oddly too much on Craft the system more than it needed to and didn't put as much into Crafting characters as it should.

                          It's kind of similar to some other trees that I have felt a bit sour towards in Solar Craft, like how Solar Survival focuses way more on being a beast master than a survivalist than I'd of liked.

                          Anyhow, those are to me the big ones. This is again, someone who actaully likes the base Craft system overall. But I do think that if there's a of the Exalted system I'm often let down on in further look, it's Craft Charms and I think they highlight in kind of an extreme way many things folks have issues with in small parts around the rest of the system in a big way.

                                                  I mean maybe this is from the fact that training times are often stupidly long and are also part of the problem with scaling XP that is my great bugbear, but I think that the Solar training charms are in fact intended as written to 1) allow the beneficiary to *permanently* advance beyond their own EXP totals [rather than just bypass training times], and 2) with TLM, potentially allow the teacher to derive an XP benefit as well, rather than what would happen with XP debt, which would eliminate one of the two; either the beneficiary just gets their XP "prespent, early", or the teacher doesn't ever recover anything beyond what they invested.
