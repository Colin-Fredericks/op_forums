        3e Core, p. 28 and The Realm p. 97 both state that all Immaculate monks (not just Exalted ones) study martial arts. The Realm is even more explicit in stating that all monks learn one of the five Dragon Styles to the point of mastering both the unarmed and armed basics of that style, just to reach the First Coil and actually be assigned to any duties within the Order. Every Immaculate monk across creation receives training in something that requires the Martial Arts Merit to achieve mechanically.

        3e Core, p. 163 specifies that the MA Merit represents "systemic training" from somewhere like the Immaculate Order or dojos.

        3e Core, p. 230 says martial arts tournaments are a thing in Creation.

        3e Core, p. 426 this is probably the big one, so I'll quote: "Creation’s martial arts are the stuff of legend, powerful fighting styles that emulate the ferocity of mighty beasts or follow paths of esoteric wisdom. Martial artists come in many forms throughout the world. The vast majority are mortal men and women who’ve attained extraordinary martial prowess through intense study, discipline, and training." I'll also just note once here that throughout the MA section of Chapter 7, there is a stress on there being numerous students and sifus of most styles, leading to the strong impression that it's not just the Exalted doing all this.

        3e Core, p. 427 says that Snake style is flourishing in the dojos of the South and the East and is seen across Creation.

        3e Core, p. 447 notes that most dojos across Creation only know Silver-Voiced Nightingale Style as Nightingale Style.

        The Realm, p. 137 notes that Silent Thunder Prefecture has a history of martial arts from when an Immaculate taught the peasants to aid them against the Empress.

        The Realm, p. 172 talks about the Thirteen Schools of Wu-Jian, which are underground martial arts societies.

        DB 3e, p. 65 says that House Sesus has a secret location in the Skyhewn Mountains for teaching "devious schools" of martial arts.

        DB 3e, p. 301 mentions the multitude of groups that practice Golden Janissary Style.

        DB 3e, p. 303 Mantis Style is also very popular in the East and South apparently (makes you wonder about the West and North getting some love with popular MA styles in those areas), as well as commonly studied by the Immaculate monks.

        DB 3e, p. 305 There is nothing here.

        Lunars 3e, p. 67 Skandhar-Bhal is a whole settlement to support the Order of the Moon-That-Fell with it's own warrior monks.

        Lunars 3e. p. 298 Centipede Style is... popular in the East and South (see?) but also the West! Such much so that there are Snake vs. Centipede rivalries in some places, and dojos that mix the two in others.

        Lunars 3e, p. 300 Falcon style is common in the South and had multiple masters spread it to all the other directions.

        Lunars 3e, p. 303 Laughing Monster Style has, "spread to numerous dojos, secret societies, and criminal syndicates."

        Lunars 3e. p. 306 Swaying Grass Dance Style is, in honor of its origins, secretly taught to slaves and rebels to increase their chances against their oppressors.

        Hopefully that's sufficient to make my point about how the setting is presented.

                                                            It lacks elegance it the transition from mortal to Exalt; even just as a matter of background.

                                                            Regardless of whether you're playing a pre-Exaltation game, or this is backstory that you feel is important enough to put dots down:

                                                            You're playing a character that was an IO monk and as since left the Order while still mortal. You have, [Element] Dragon Style 1 because that's a requirement for monks to know, but haven't invested in MA beyond that. As a mortal, it's just one 1 Ability dot for some background flavor. OK.

                                                            Then you Exalt as a Solar. What happens? Do you have to buy the Merit because you're an Exalt now? Even if you have no intention of increasing your MA let alone trying to find a sifu for one of the Dragon Styles? Do you have to raise your MA rating (none of the Dragon Styles have a Charm with a prerequisite below MA 2) so you can qualify for a free Charm in a Style you're not going to use?

                                                            While the free Charm wasn't part of it, this is precisely where my variable cost MA Merit option came from: whether a minimal cost for mortals, or a minimal cost for some flavor to an Exalt, it's a smooth transition for more organically built characters.

                                                            I don't see armies of Martial Artists sweeping the land, and so using a 4 dot Merit for rarity is probably fine.
                                                            As cited above, mortals training in Martial Arts Abilities is not rare according to the setting. Having the Merit for the sake of rarity makes no sense.
