                    Stealth

                    Number of Charms: 23
                    By Ability requirement: 0/1/4/5/13
                    By Essence requirement: 6/7/7/3
                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Reroll all dice other than 10s on a Stealth action. Enemies take -1 Awareness success for each 1 or 2 rolled. Enemies take -2 successes for each 1 rolled. Stuff happens if attacker rolls six 1s and 2s and the Solar rolls at least one 10.

                    Themes: Go unnoticed, unseen, and unsensed. Appear from the shadows, murder someone, and disappear, reappearing only to kill again. Vanish even from people’s memories.

                    A much smaller tree this time – less than half the Charms in Socialize! - though many of the individual Charms are quite large. Stealth is fourth from the end – let’s get to it!

                    Charms:
                    1. Reroll all dice other than 10s on a Stealth action.
                    2. Perfect immunity to sight-based detection as long as you don’t move.
                    3. Blend perfectly in the crowd. Checkpoints or magic actively opposing your cause may still detect you.
                    4. Join Battle using Stealth; if you act first, you’re also hidden.
                    5. Ignore the -3 penalty to moving in stealth. Gain Initiative while you remain hidden.
                    6. Hide someone else.
                    7. Infinite Stealth Mastery. You can hide in plain sight, but moving range bands automatically reveals you.
                    8. Roll Stealth against target’s Resolve; if you succeed, you’re imperceptible to him until you Join Battle or try to steal something the target cares about.
                    9. Reroll Join Battle mid-fight and add the Initiative to your pool.
                    10. Reset #9 after incapacitating an enemy.
                    11. Enter stealth reflexively after incapacitating an enemy.
                    12. Defend Other from concealment.
                    13. Concealment counts as cover.
                    14. Commit motes to temporarily hide anima levels.
                    15. Hide so well that everyone forgets about you.
                    16. Render yourself proof against detection from one sense (except sight).
                    17. Attempts to detect you by hearing alone take -1 success for each 1 or 2 rolled.
                    18. On using #9, everyone forgets you were there for (Essence) rounds.
                    19. Hide in a target’s shadow, controlling his body.
                    20. Move one range band in stealth without penalty; incompatible with #7.
                    21. Enemies permanently take -2 successes for each 1 rolled on Awareness rolls.
                    22. Still got that pencil? So the attacker rolls and hits you Decisively, and then he rolls damage. Between the two, he rolls six 1s and 2s. You roll Stealth against his Awareness; if you succeed, he misses, and if you roll a 10, you enter stealth.
                    23. Teleport back to a chosen hiding place.
                    Essence 1 frontloads us with a lot of power here – Easily-Overlooked Presence Method (#3) is Assassin’s Creed in one easy package, and Invisible Statue Spirit (#2) is one of the few really uncontestable effects in the game.

                    Guardian Fog Approach (#6) helps with a basic problem specialists often have – the rest of the party can’t keep up with them. Nice!

                    Blurred Form Style (#7) is confusingly worded. The fluff claims the Solar blends “perfectly” – that could be rules text in E-OPM, but here it’s immediately supplanted by “(Essence) auto-successes,” so it can’t be the actual effect. If you plan to switch range bands, though, this Charm seems to automatically reveal your location – so if you plan on moving around, this is more liability than help.

                    Mental Invisibility Technique (#8) is just kind of hilarious – you can be dancing around making faces at the guy, and you’re still invisible until you kick his cat. The caveats on this Charm are a really good idea, and I’m glad they’re included. So far, Stealth seems to be something of a Swiss army knife power – roll Stealth for Join Battle! Roll it for mental influence! ALWAYS BE STEALTHING.

                    Shew, Shadow Victor’s Repose and Flash-Eyed Killer’s Insight (#9 and 10) are just mean. That’s a big pile of Initiative every time you drop a guy (rolled with Stealth, if you like!). Picture your favorite “Wait, weren’t there four of you a minute ago?” scene – this is that. If there’s no sufficiently Awareness-heavy ally, you’re in for a world of hurt vs. a Solar assassin. … And then Hidden Snake Recoil (#11) just makes it worse, letting you vanish reflexively. Not sure this is as great if you’re part of a party – you basically leave your allies to take the hits while you play Predator - but for a solo combatant, this is vicious.

                    Can you use Sun-Swallowing Practice (#14) to walk around with three levels of anima ready to go at all times?

                    Okay, I was onboard with the Murderghost paradigm, but Mind Shroud Meditation (#18) is too far for me. If you incapacitate someone, everyone else forgets about you, with no opportunity for resistance – that moves from being Batman to being a Benny Hill sketch.

                    Fivefold Shadow Burial (#21) is nuts – that’s a permanent change in opposing Awareness rolls to something like .3 sux/die average. I’m a little bit uncomfortable with that – this pushes us out of “really scary” and into “can’t realistically be opposed without a dedicated Awareness build.” (Sure, recurring 1s shuts it down, but how many characters are going to have that?)

                    On balance, I really like Stealth – the raw power here in a relative handful of Charms is huge. If you aren’t thinking about Stealth in combat, specifically – if you just want to sneak around and Do Crimes – even the E1 stuff is probably sufficient for you. That’s a little disappointing, honestly – it seems like all the high-level stuff is for being a Deathninja specifically. Still… you can definitely play Arkham City with this, and what more could one really ask? My biggest concern right now is that it’s all too good – that Fivefold Shadow Burial especially is strong enough to shut out any opposition that isn’t built specifically to counter stealth.

                    Yes: Easily Overlooked Presence Method; Shadow Victor's Repose.
                    No: False Image Feint (#22). You track how many 1s and 2s someone has gotten on every attack roll, right? And you keep track of that information once they’ve gone on to damage, just in case this is the time that comes up six? (At least six? Exactly six? How does this Charm interact with effects that say you don’t return to base Initiative until such-and-such time, like a magical flurry?)
                    Just Plain Weird: Shadow Replacement Technique (#19) feels bizarrely out of step with Solar power themes. I can see an Infernal pulling “I mind control you from within your shadow,” but that’s a pretty far cry from excellence and angelic miracles.

                                    Survival

                                    Number of Charms: 24
                                    By Ability requirement: 1/1/4/4/14
                                    By Essence requirement: 3/11/10/0/0
                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Recurring 5s and 6s; enemy 1s count as your 10s. Recurring successes. Recurring 1s.

                                    Themes: Resist the elements. Raise a cute, cuddly puppy and FEED HIM ON THE PAIN OF YOUR ENEMIES.

                                    Huh. This is one of the very few trees with almost nothing in the E1 range. It also has nothing over E3, though – all the Charms are crammed into that awkward E3 range. So that’s a little tough for a starting character, but any Supernal benefit dissipates a relatively short distance in.

                                    Charms:
                                    1. Gather enough food for one or more people in any environment.
                                    2. Remove penalties and minor troubles for rough environment. Does not resist environmental damage.
                                    3. Wild animals are friendly, or at least won’t attack.
                                    4. Lead a small group safely through the wilderness at 20 miles/day. (How fast is normal?)
                                    5. When tracking another, you have recurring 5s and 6s and his 1s count as 10s for you.
                                    6. Your familiar is unshakably loyal and produces motes and willpower. You can see through its senses.
                                    7. Train a new familiar, or train an existing familiar to use its latent, magical, or blatantly supernatural powers.
                                    8. Call your familiar to attack alongside you.
                                    9. Recurring 1s on a training roll. Grant a familiar bonus stats with an XP investment.
                                    10. Permanent soak/hardness boost for your familiar.
                                    11. Extra health levels for your familiar.
                                    12. Command your familiar, granting it bonus dice on its attacks.
                                    13. Your familiar can see dematerialized spirits.
                                    14. Your familiar gets to use your Join Battle roll, with bonus successes. Bonus dice granted to this action by #12 gain recurring successes.
                                    15. Ignore drowning and toxic gas; reduce all environmental damage by your Resistance.
                                    16. Bonuses to your attempts to leave no tracks with a small group. Mundane tracking automatically fails.
                                    17. Perfectly conceal yourself or your stash against mundane senses.
                                    18. 1/scene, your Familiar’s Decisive attacks roll over extra successes into damage.
                                    19. Use your Stealth Charms while possessing your familiar.
                                    20. Your familiar grows, gains stat boosts, and develops beneficial mutations. (Wings!)
                                    21. Your familiar grapples spirits, making them vulnerable to you. Cheaper in conjunction with #12.
                                    22. Your familiar permanently generates 1i/turn, except when #24 is active.
                                    23. When you land a Withering attack, your familiar gains half as much Initiative as you do.
                                    24. Your familiar becomes huge and intimidating. It can attempt huge feats of strength and gains major bonuses to movement actions. Its attacks will hit. Its Initiative does not reset. It CANNOT DIE, or even be wounded by Decisive attacks. If it Crashes, the Charm ends.

                                    Okay, I have no idea what Food-Gathering Exercise (#1) is saying. “In addition, for every hour the Solar continues to use this Charm, the Solar works toward a climactic encounter with the rawest expression of survivalist skill possible… This encounter will happen regardless of Storyteller decree, but is still contingent on a difficulty 6 roll in the worst case scenario.” I’m having trouble parsing that – is it just saying, “If you use this Charm for a day, you’re guaranteed to also find a larger food source at some point, unless there’s literally nothing?”

                                    Those first three up there – that’s what you can get out of Survival at E1. It’s not nothing, but it is a little disappointing; at E1, Stealth lets me be Altair!

                                    Beast-Mastering Behavior (#7) includes rules for training a familiar under normal circumstances, followed by how the Charm modifies those. Wouldn’t these rules be better included… well, anywhere other than tucked into one Charm? On the plus side, this Charm will absolutely let you turn a mundane squirrel into Pikachu.

                                    Why do several of these Charms have dedicated sidebars with one sentence of information? Shouldn’t this information just go in the Charm? That seems both more compact and more useful.

                                    Sidebar notes that, should your familiar die, you get all the XP invested in it back. That’s a nice gesture, and it makes a lot of these Charms feel like less of a gamble.

                                    Ambush Predator Style (#14) is going to grant some big Join Battle rolls – at E5, with a maxed-out pool and support from Familiar-Honing Instruction (#12), you’re looking at something like 14.5 successes for 7m. That’s not bad – and it’s even better if you use those Ride Charms to share Initiative. (Ride + Survival in general seems like it should be a pretty nice combination – although, how does that work? Ride assumes your mount has no Initiative or independent actions without a Charm, but this seems to assume your familiar does have Initiative and its own actions. So what happens when you ride your familiar?)

                                    Element-Resisting Prana (#15) is the first Charm in about the last eleven to not be about enhancing your familiar. Which, the familiar tree is pretty cool so far, but it’s nice to see some more breadth – though we have to get to E3 to see it! Note that, at Resistance 5, you can ignore any damage below the level of lava – but a volcano will still kill you over time.

                                    I really want to see some guy’s dog grapple a dematerialized Octavian to the ground with Phantom-Rending Fangs (#21). Like… that’s just a fabulous mental image. “Sic ‘em, Rex.”

                                    The thing I’m curious on, with all this familiar-enhancement, is how well your familiar can actually hit things – in an Excellency-heavy world, it’s awfully easy to throw down a few motes and ignore that critter you just spent ten Charms on. Familiar-Honing Instruction does a lot to help with this once it’s Reflexive, I think, but prior to that, it looks like rough going.

                                    …And Deadly Predator Method (#24) resolves that concern. That’s a hefty mote commitment to get things going, but while they go… good heavens. A Solar’s pet will end you.

                                    … so what happens when you run DPM, and your familiar is Defending Other on you? Are you both just functionally immune to Decisives until it Crashes?

                                    I love the familiar tree, even if it doesn’t quite cash in until the very end. Survival as a whole feels like it could use some more toys – everything not familiar-buffing feels almost like an afterthought – but those Charms it does have tend to be pretty potent and useful. This is a solid and extremely powerful tree.

                                    Yes: Deadly Predator Method. Good sweet heavens.
                                    No: Probably Hardship-Surviving Mendicant Spirit (#2). “You are immune to bugbites and nettles” just doesn’t stack up to some of the other things in this list.
                                    Just Plain Weird: The undefined interactions of riding your familiar. This is, like, the first thing people are going to want to do, right? So how does that work? Does the familiar render a bunch of Ride Charms superfluous, or does it become drastically less competent when you jump on its back?

                                                The way I've read riding your familiar: normally, your mount does not roll initiative, even if it is a familiar.                                                         Thrown

                                                        Number of Charms: 24
                                                        By Ability requirement: 1/1/3/5/15
                                                        By Essence requirement: 7/9/8/1/0
                                                        Nonstandard dice tricks: Reroll one nonsuccess for each 10. Reroll 6s equal to the 7s you rolled. Gain Initiative equal to the 9s and 10s on your last roll.

                                                        Themes: Strike a large number of targets. Attack without breaking stealth. Render your target unable to defend against you. Make a lightning-fast alpha strike.

                                                        Charms:
                                                        1. Calculate Accuracy on a Withering attack as if you were at Close range. At Close range, subtract 1 from target’s Defense.
                                                        2. Cheap Excellency on your first attack if you roll the highest Join Battle. At E3, doesn’t reset your Initiative.
                                                        3. Take your first turn earlier, even though you don’t gain extra Initiative. Works cross-Ability.
                                                        4. If your Decisive deals 3HL, the target takes -3 to all die pools for the scene.
                                                        5. Negate the target’s cover.
                                                        6. Make Thrown attacks from Long range.
                                                        7. 1/scene, make an undodgeable attack with a full Excellency that also strikes all targets nearby.
                                                        8. Reroll one nonsuccess for each 10 on a Join Battle. If you roll highest, attack (Dexterity) opponents.
                                                        9. Target struck by a Decisive attack can make no sound for several rounds.
                                                        10. A thrown attack appears to come from a different direction. At E3, make thrown attacks from stealth without revealing yourself.
                                                        11. “Reroll 6s equal to your 7s” on a Distract or Disarm gambit, + bonus successes.
                                                        12. 1/scene, keep your Initiative after disarming the target.
                                                        13. Knock a dropped weapon one range band.
                                                        14. Bonus Initiative when you Aim to make a Decisive from cover or stealth.
                                                        15. Your Withering attack strikes all enemies within short range of the target.
                                                        16. When you use #8 to strike a target Decisively, you may retain extra Initiative equal to the 9s and 10s rolled.
                                                        17. When you fool an opponent with #10, you can make a Decisive ambush attack
                                                        18. Automatically hit with a Distract attack.
                                                        19. Roll a Decisive attack or Disarm gambit twice, taking the better result.
                                                        20. When you hit with #18, you also Disarm the target.
                                                        21. When someone tries to recover a weapon you’ve Disarmed from him, you get a free unblockable, undodgeable Withering attack on him.
                                                        22. Double damage on an ambush attack.
                                                        23. Throw an accurate, extra-damaging Kamehameha.
                                                        24. Throw an attack that strikes multiple enemies with Withering blows before reaching a final enemy with a Decisive.

                                                        I’m not hugely impressed by Steel Storm Descending (#2), but once it auto-upgrades, a free Decisive is pretty nice. Given that you won Join Battle, you’re probably throwing quite a bit of damage when you fire that Decisive off.

                                                        Joint-Wounding Attack (#4) doesn’t seem that great at low levels, when 3HL is a big chunk of your totals – but as the health levels for both you and your enemies rise, this is a way to put an opponent in a bad way while doing a pittance of damage.

                                                        We get a sidebar on “elected targets” in battle groups – which, again, feels like it would go better back in the core rules for battle groups.

                                                        Cascade of Cutting Terror (#7) is kind of a big deal – just being a reflexive attack would be big, but the free Excellency and the undodgeability make it a pretty great once-per-fight pop. Against, say, an enemy archer, this is going to be devastating.

                                                        Why is Swarm-Culling Instinct’s (#8) “reroll one nonsuccess for each 10” not just recurring 10s? In almost all rolls, there’s no statistical difference. The power itself is probably the cheapest multiattack Charm in the set – if it lets you roll high enough. Thrown in general looks like it has a great opening game, and a much weaker followup.

                                                        Shower of Deadly Blades (#15) is again a very strong one-use move. Oddly, it seems to assume you’re using the “ammo save” rules – but even those rules presented “track ammo” as an alternate solution. Why assume that this one is in use? More generally, Thrown is the only place in the rules where ammo consumption seems to be a concern. Thrown is also odd in that it seems to have no option to make weapons – 2e had “Spirit Weapons,” but that’s conspicuously absent here.

                                                        Crimson Razor Wind (#17) drops a bombshell with almost no fanfare: one distracting roll, and you get a free ambush attack – that is, a strike at Defense 0. That’s a big deal for a reusable Decisive attack. Edit: Falling Icicle Strike (#22) makes it an even bigger deal – your basically-guaranteed-to-hit attack now doubles its damage as well. That’s a pretty solid one-hit-kill combo; very little short of a perfect defense is going to get you out of it.

                                                        Shadow Wind Slash (#19) is similarly a big deal – 2m for a Decisive attack with a reroll. That’s effectively a big boost to average accuracy. Why is the Disarming version of this effect so expensive? “Reroll your Disarm” is more expensive than making both a normal attack and a Disarm. (Also, this Charm seems to assume that gambits are not Decisives, which, again, is not unambiguous.)

                                                        Fiery Solar Chakram (#23) describes doing extra damage for creatures of darkness, but oddly avoids the actual term.

                                                        In general, Thrown looks like lots of big single-use attacks, as noted above. There’s not a lot to add to your accuracy, but if you want to attack from stealth or win the fight before anyone else even knows it’s started, this is your go-to. The potential for big – and lethal – ambushes just makes it nastier. If you can live through that opening volley, though, there’s not a lot of meat-and-potatoes here; a Thrown combatant might want to either have a backup attack or a plan to run away quick.

                                                        Overall, strong tree with no obvious dead Charms. Curious to see how the balance does in play.

                                                        Yes: Lots of good choices, but probably Cascade of Cutting Terror.
