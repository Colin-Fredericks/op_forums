        I'm incredibly excited for Essence (and these more-detailed looks at Getimians and new!Infernals do not disappoint!), but I do have a few concerns at this (admittedly early) date, which aren't objective enough for for errata, but really don't seem right to me.
        Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
        The Lunar passive powers are clever because it makes them narratively powerfull but doesn’t give them an edge with other named characters. Like a rampaging Full Moon npc is unscathed by the the entire city state militia, but that invulnerability doesn’t equate to an advantage against your named PC swordsman facing them (obviously their Charms still can). Very nice genre emulation.
        I'm a little less thrilled about this.Full Moons only get a milestone for defeating a significant foe in single combat, then their anima is focused on tearing through weak trash. I'm not even sure whether it's good at that job, since the Solar/Azimuth passive implies that Battle Groups are not "Extras," so a Full Moon might still take damage from that militia. But even if the ability is good for the one thing it does, it looks like Essence is following 3E's example of making Full Moons better at styling on trivial threats (which almost everyone is good at, by definition), then giving a different Lunar caste the defense buff which is far more valuable in serious fights - amusingly, it switched from No Moon to Changing, meaning that anyone has a better shot at optimal combat powers than the combat Lunars. That's silly.

        Speaking of the contradiction in Full Moon milestones, a lot of these milestone conditions seem like they'd be wildly different difficulties to get in play, which I really hope is not the actual goal. A Full Moon Lunar needs to win in a specific type of fight, but a Dawn only needs to fight at all? I've hardly ever had an RPG session where you couldn't argue that the PCs "took an action toward
        furthering a long-term goal," (Air Aspect), but you need a much more specific setup to learn "story-important" secrets for your Marrow Aspect, and Breath/Soulsteel Milestones are almost literally "you get one for picking an argument with the GM after the session."

        Lawyering rather than roleplaying is also my biggest worry with the Alchemicals' Community Spirit. Design any ability, then pick a "group" as broadly as possible, in the hopes that you always have it. It seem unnecessary to invite all of that trouble, when Living Artifact already does a great job of underscoring the Alchemical themes of community through logistics while still respecting the practicalities of many PC groups. A second trait aimed at representing their unusual bodies and souls could avoid the whole thing without hurting their core identity.

        It's Exalted, so we obviously can't get through a round of mechanics without super seduction powers. Since it's inevitable, I do appreciate that they gave them to horrifying Frankenstein Japanese Ghosts, to at least keep things surreal.

        Sidereal Anima powers buffing your allies feels like an even stronger statement of purpose here than usual, given the multi-splat focus, and I love it. However, I do wish they all felt like buffs which multiple party members would actually want. Journeys (movement), Secrets (stealth, though only for scouting/spying), and Endings (noticing danger and trying to talk people down) are all good examples of this, but Battles and Serenity need help. War and Craft are some of the canonical examples of Abilities that one person is using while everyone else sits on their hands, and I'd love to see those Chosen get to provide the same kind of support to their whole playgroup that the other three enjoy.

        Also, I really hope that when we see the system mechanics, I can piece together some kind of theory for why 4/5 Sidereals "transform her Essence in dice to automatic successes" on relevant rolls, but Journeys lets people "add the Sidereal’s Essence in dice any time they would need to roll". I know just enough from teasers to see a few slight differences, but the effects are so similar that I'm mystified by the choice to not simply choose a template and stick to it. Though that's obviously nothing new to Exalted.

        There are also a few abilities where I am genuinely not sure what they do. Can Laws Of A New Creation remove people's belief in object permanence? Can an Untouchable Changing Moon do things like tear people in half or say "I am the Devil Empress" in perfect Seatongue, and still have significant characters be incapable or responding to them as anything save a normal squirrel? Does the Sidereal choose the location Greater Sign of Mercury teleports people to, and the "safe location of the Storyteller's choice" just keeps people from telefragging, or does the Sidereal just decide "teleport these people somewhere," and the destination is up to the ST? Inevitably, those are some of my favorite abilities in the entire preview, but past experience tells me that a lot of people will have their own intuition on those questions without even questioning it, and if those people reach different answers and then play together, it could get awkward.

        "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                                I'm a pretty cynical person, so in the short term, I don't think any one's belief has a lot of impact on what people actually do - once a lynch mob is formed, no longer believing in the arguments for its creation doesn't matter, because people also don't want to look bad, or to have others turn on them. (Or, for a more relatable example, nobody has to honestly believe that "the customer is always right" to keep acting like it's true due to social pressure.) So in my mind, eliminating a belief in a small area for a single scene does roughly nothing, because beliefs only matter over the long term of shaping behaviors. But this is an Iconic ability, and I assume the writers have different intuition than my own, if they would only share it with the class.

                                Conversely, you can also make a behavior taboo, but I don't even know what qualifies. "Hitting people" is definitely a behavior, as is "talking," but this ability "cannot force characters to stop making attacks or social influence actions," so those don't count. What about holding weapons? Breathing? Probably not.

                                When I look at this ability and consider using it against, say, "a soldier's duty is to obey the chain of command," that might:
                                1. Automatically disperse any organized army which can see the Winter.
                                2. Grant a minor bonus on social influence actions to change the views of soldiers within fifty feet or so.
                                3. Do anything in between.

                                I know people will think I'm being willfully obtuse, but I am not. I do not know the authorial intent. I do not have a strong guess. I do not think that all of the people who think the meaning is "obvious" actually agree with each other. I do not think it's unreasonable to expect rules, which exist to give us some shared expectations in a game of make believe, to provide some context for these questions.

                                    On a more positive note, I was highly skeptical of pre-defined Virtues returning, but today's preview looks really smart. The virtues are broad enough that very few gameable characters won't be able to find a pair that fit them, they encourage players to find larger drives for their PCs than selfishness or apathy (a trap I've seen a lot of really good roleplayers fall into accidentally), and the need to link Intimacies to virtues looks like a deliberate attempt to fix one of the biggest critiques of 3E social influence.

                                    Building up a suite of shallow Intimacies to justify them as supporting each other ("you like oranges, and my pet monkey can bring you oranges") doesn't make a lot of sense when the only Intimacies the game tracks are ones tied to a character's primary drives. We'll need to see the social system to know how that all shakes out, but I like what I see.
