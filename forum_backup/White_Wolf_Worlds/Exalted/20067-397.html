    Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
    So how do you effect someones resource rating if they have 0-2 Resources but steal someone diamond encrusted hat or some such, something that could buy yacht or villa?
    Are you sure that it could?

    ​For one thing, much of Creation isn't really going to be somewhere that you can buy a villa; there isn't a housing market, there aren't realtors who facilitate the transition of constructed houses in the countryside from one owner to another. Most of the time, people build their villas from scratch, or inherit them from family, and when somebody can't make use of them anymore they're frequently going to be abandoned and left to rot. Not to mention the fact that the villa tends to come with the large estate. Strapped for cash rich people might occasionally sell their lands and homes amongst themselves, but it's not really going to be a case in which some random person with a jewel-encrusted hat comes up to them and offers it in exchange.

    ​People who construct yachts on commission (the world won't have enough wealth to create a market for people creating yachts and then keeping them around waiting to be sold) don't seem likely to accept a fancy hat as payment.

    ​So the question becomes, how do you fence it? It's going to be difficult to find somebody of that level of repute who has the cash available who wouldn't just keep the money, which can be spent, rather than give it to somebody for the hat, which cannot.

    ​Passing yourself off as a high class haberdasher and selling it to an already rich person might work, but it requires a lot of setup.

    ​A lot of these things are valuable because a rich person paid a lot of money for them and the ownership of such is a visual indicator of being rich, but making that value transferable is a bit difficult.

    ​As for the question of what it does to a person with low Resources who suddenly acquires a large amount of transferable wealth, well...

    ​First of all, remember that Third Edition slightly modified what each dot value represents. Resources 1 is no longer somebody being slightly more than dirt poor, it now refers to the minimum for a person of means such as an agrarian landlord or small-scale merchant.

    ​The other side is that it depends upon how you, the player, opt to use the money. If you decide to reinvest it in some of whatever the basis of your characters Resources are, such as purchasing more land or expanding your business, then it can serve as the in-character means of increasing your rating, and can be something that serves as an effective shortcut to such since you didn't have to dip into your savings or go into debt over an extended period of time to cultivate that increase.

    ​If you just want to blow it on extra luxuries, then at best you're giving yourself a little vacation which will probably turn out to be a lot shorter than you were expecting, and at worst you're setting yourself on the course for bankruptcy.

    ​There's also the matter of how in many societies, buying expensive things is going to be complicated by formal or informal reservations on who can buy what based on class. Not to mention interests that the authorities will take; people suddenly displaying the capacity to spend beyond their documented means is not something that only started being really suspicious in the 20th century.

    ​Besides, your specific examples have to be some of the worst things to buy period, because they keep costing things even after the initial construction costs. What are you going to do with a villa when you can't afford the full time staff it requires for service and maintenance? How are you going to furnish it?

            It's like I say at the end; you either have a slightly easier way to increase your Resources rating naturally through investments, or you blow through it quickly on frivolities. Maybe you can buy something nice where the benefit is not needing to go into debt for it, but simply having additional cash does not automatically increase your status.

            ​Mind, those are just considerations for somebody who already has Resources dots (which, reminder again, always denotes fairly high property ownership now). If you're a person with Resources 0 who suddenly finds yourself in possession of thousands of silver pieces? Blowing it all on expensive indulgences is basically the only thing you can do with it. You can't even use it to just ensure that you can live without concern at the social status you've always been at, because where are you going to keep all of those coins that they can be safe? Assuming that personal banking even exists, it's probably a lot to hope that you exist in a system that is so free that you can turn up with several kegs of silver and nobody is going to ask where you got them.

            ​The long and short of it is that simply handing a pile of currency over to somebody is not sufficient to raise their Resources, except in very short term ways.

                        It really depends on who you are selling it to, I guess.

                        If you try to sell the jewels all at once ..... almost nobody is going to be able to afford them. Plus, it paints a big ol' target right on your back. Even if the original owner is out of the picture, you still are the person trying to sell fist-sized jewels. Enjoy your mugging.

                        In order to effectively get any money from the jewels, you are probably gonna have to recut them. Cut the larger ones into smaller and smaller pieces, and hawk them off as traders can afford them. That, of course, will take value (probably pretty drastically) away from the initial gems, and via the gemcutters fee, but you might be able to actually make a semi-quasi-regular profit off them.

                        That is, of course, until the local gem-merchant/jewelers guild sends legbreakers to your house for working around them.

                        But, before then, I would say that recutting the gems and selling them at a steady rate would arguably give you Resources 3, for as long as you can get away with it.

                        Of course, you would have to find a gemcutter who would want to work with such obviously stolen goods, and probably travel to a place where there was a large enough market, and compete with the already-established jewelery-sellers, who aren't going to take your intrusion into their market sitting down. If you don't want your legs broken and your belongings set on fire, you are probably going to have to join their guild or guild/equivalent, which costs money.

                        I dunno, to me selling a large quantity of jewels seems better for an actual "adventure" plot than flat "moneymoneymoney"
