                                                    I'm my current game I'm running with a slightly more philosophical rendition of the Jade prison.

                                                    Essentially, after the usurpation, the devastation wrought left the world's populace largely in despair - the new world order imposed by the Sidereals is the calculated balance of *just* enough turmoil to satisfy the power players and *just* enough insurmountable social structure to keep the lower classes in line. Balancing the world on this fulcrum of despair and hopelessness while rewarding the more avid and aggressive citizens meant that no one was worthy of Sol's love, adoration and - by extension - Exaltation.

                                                    There were always the fringe cases of someone hoping against hope but they were so few and far between in this new world order that a marginally organized Wyld hunt could track them down easily enough and dispatch them in so much pomp and rituals that it would further dissuade the populace from dreaming the impossible dream.

                                                    The rise of the Scarlet Empire was exactly what the Sidereals needed after the devastation wrought by the crusade and contagion; a new state ordered religion that actually prohibited greatness on a spiritual level, the final nail in the coffin to any potential Solar Exalted. All they had to do was let the empires influence expand, and clean up the last remnants of those few dreamers that had slipped through the web of mundanity. Everything was going great for almost eight centuries and then... the unthinkable happened.

                                                    Suddenly the Realm's influence turns inwards, those Sidereals tasked with maintaining the balance in the threshold areas loose the looming threat of a world-spanning empire and the people start to think that maybe things could be better, and the light of hope begins to return to the world.

                                                            IMO 3e is probably not going address it directly.

                                                            Because having a solid answer doesn't add anything to the game, and leaving it undefined grants ST the ability to make up whatever best suits their own story.

                                                            "Oh but the deathlords would have to know and they'd share that information with the Abyssals!" Why? Unless the deathlords are cartoon villians revealing plots to captured heroes or deliberately telling the potentially vastly more powerful Abyssals things they don't need to know, there's nothing to be gained from it.

                                                            "How did you get my exaltation then?!? Oh Eye and Seven Black Rabbits That Peer Into The Abyss to Forsake The Chains that Bind.... *deep breath* to the Eternal Stone That Bears No Death?" Again, simply honest answers work well. "I traded a bunch of land to Bob for it. Canny ghost that Bob. No idea how he got it." or "As part of a convoluted arrangement with forces so dark the living can not bear to hear even the names of the forces uttered!"

                                                            (IMO again) having it defined just gives players(and the STs) a reason to go "Ah-Ha! my super-clever Abyssal clearly KNOWS they were a Solar before, and is therefore doing X!" or "Well, everyone knows Abyssals are inverted Solars, of course X, Y, Z are all true."
