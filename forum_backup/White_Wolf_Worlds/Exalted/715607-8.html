        Originally posted by Eric Minton View Post
        "Her player may then make a pronouncement against an ongoing project based on knowledge her studies reveal, creating bonuses for those who act in accordance with her accurate predictions, and penalties for those who go against them." In the latter part of the example, the rebels are going against the prediction by depriving Mnemon of the breathing room to consolidate her forces while gathering resources until winter. Therefore, the rebels suffer the penalty.
        But the actually-provided prophecy doesn't say anything about Mnemon having "breathing room." It says:
        -Mnemon won't attack until fall at the earliest.
        -Over the summer, she'll consolidate her forces.
        -Over the summer, she'll conserve water and gather resources.

        That's it! There's no rider constraining the rebel activity at all - indeed, Mnemon spending the summer consolidating would seem to make summer an ideal time for them to attack.

        Edit: Rephrasing a little, I understand the prophecy to describe Mnemon's plans. If Mnemon's actions are in accord with her predicted plans, then sure, she gets a bonus. But "Hey, we're going to come kill you now, Mnemon," has nothing to do with Mnemon's plans - she's doing a great job of consolidating forces, right up until she gets murdalized.

                                                            The example reads fine to me. It predicts two things will happen:

                                                            1) Mnemon will not attack the rebels before fall.
                                                            2) She will spend the summer months consolidating forces, gathering resources and conserving water.

                                                            It then specifies that if Mnemon herself takes action to violate one of those (in this example, #1, by attacking early), she will take a penalty. If the rebels take action to violate one of those (in this example, #2, by interrupting the prediction that she would be consolidating forces etc. during the summer rather than fighting rebels), then they will get a penalty.

                                                            I'll admit that I would have prefered a somewhat clearer example, as all of these lore prediction actions and charms are opening up questions of the in-game mechanism by which these things work. A prediction involving the choices of one actor ascribing penalties to another actor based on their own choice took a few readings to grok, but I think Minton's details clear up any further issues I had.
