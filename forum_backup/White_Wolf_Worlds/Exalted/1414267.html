    How do you like your Specialties?

    09-17-2020, 05:34 PM
    So I'm toying around with building several characters, and re-reading some older (1E) stuff for inspiration and flavor, since 3E has really inspired me to get back on a serious Exalted kick. And I came across a section in Ruins of Rathess that talks about how foraging in the jungles is difficulty X, but that most people aren't going to be ok with eating thumb sized grubs, so large game is difficulty X+2. And it caused me to consider changing Survival - Urban on my former street rat to Survival - Gross But Effective. Because, to be honest, while Urban gives some flavor, the odds of it ever actually coming up once you have a circle is about nil. Someone in the circle will have resources, or the ability to talk the party into a place to stay. But the ability to crawl into a dead Taun-Taun or eat giant cockroaches, or smear myself with yeddim dung might at least be useful. But (while obviously a question for my particular storyteller), I'm curious as to people's preference for Specialties.

    TL;DR - Do you prefer clear mechanical Specialties, like Awareness - Join Battle, Resistance- Environmental Hazards, and Socialize-Guile, or flavorful ones like Melee - Perfectly Balanced Weapons, Socialize - Making it Up as I Go Along, and Brawl - When Really Angry?

          The only character I've made for 3E (for myself to use in an actual game, as opposed to characters made for other people or as thought-exercises) had/has... Awareness 5 (Join Battle), Craft: Architecture 4 (Masonry), Athletics 5 (Rushes), Earth Dragon Style 5 (Tetsubo), Lore: Heptagram education 5 (The Shogunate).

          Probably not what I'd go for now. I really like the "Survival - Gross But Effective" one, and I'd probably build towards something like that in the future.

          The "problem" is that Dragon-Blooded really need that Specialty. You need to be absolutely sure you're going to get it on the rolls you need it on. Awareness (Join Battle) is probably the single least... aesthetic thing I've ever done in Exalted... but it's also pretty essential to my character. I mean, it wouldn't be if I hadn't made a character who revolved around calling Celestials and House Matriarchs punk-a**-b****es and having the conjones to back it up. The average Terrestrial PC won't need that specific Specialty... but they will need specific Specialties in the Abilities which are core to their concept. Solars and Lunars have a lot more flex for taking things that just seem cool.

Like Melee (High Flying Acrobatics) so when the random thug decides he wants a piece of you, you just punch him with no specialty, but when you really need it you vault up 12 feet into the air, and backflip into an attack.
                      I call those sorts of Specialties 'signature' specialties, or WSAV specialities. (After the Sidereal World Shaping Artistic Vision charm that often used behavioral or stylistic triggers to get the most use out of). Things like Brawl - Dirty Fighting or -While Really Angry. On the one hand, they'll apply almost all the time. They really might as well be 'while stunting' a good 90% of the time. On the other hand, most veteran players pick specialties that apply most of the time anyway. You don't see a whole lot of mounted dawns with dodge: Unarmored. Pretty much just martial artist types. I go back and forth on how I feel about them. Generally I play with an experienced group of continuous players I've known for years, and we're pretty good about not invoking stuff like that every time. (And as a ST, sometimes you fight in a Nexus basement and your spec doesn't apply). I don't think it's any worse than Melee - Swords, at any rate.

                                              Originally posted by Scrollreader View Post

                                              Eh.

                                              Look at the difficulty chart on Page 185. Look at the NPC dice pools starting Page 495.

                                              Most of the time, you REALLY don't need that extra die. Defence 4 is enough (so long as you're not running with something silly like 2 Soak, and don't mind a bit of dramatic back-and-forth with Initative Shifts and other excitement in your combats).

                                              And for Stealth... outside of fellow Exalted, a Fair Folk Lorelei has the most dice to oppose Stealth with "Senses 12 dice" (also, I contend Stealth should usually be a flat difficulty and not an opposed roll, unless the opposition is dramatically appropriate). Dex 3 Stealth 3 Solar Excellency and a basic Stunt gives you a significant edge here.

                                              Ahlat only gets 11 dice. Most things have 5-8 dice. You don't need Dex 5, Stealth 5, Specialty in "When wearing my Sneakers", Exceptional Dark Cloak, Belt of Shadow Walking, etc..

                                              But a Dex 3/Stealth 3 Dragon-Blooded doesn't even break-even with the Lorelei with a Full Excellency + Stunt. They need that Specailty because it adds an extra die and a whole success.

                                              (A Tyrant Lizard gets an average of something like 9 successes to oppose Stealth, even though they only get five dice, which is better than most Exalted -- certainly better than the Deathknight's 7, but if you rule the Bronze Faction Assassin can full Excellency, she is hitting 10. So... you need a Specialty for T-Rex guard dogs and the deadliest super-spies in Heaven.

                                              But for conventional guard dogs, sabre-tooth tigers, Elite Guards, mystic Creatures from beyond the world's edge, two-handed sentries, Directional War Gods, Celestial Censors, Immaculate Martial Artists or Experienced Dragon-Blooded, and Guild Princes... 3+3 is enough, 5/5/1 is ridiculous overkill for a Solar, unless Stealth is like, their whole Supernal "thing" and they're playing, not the Mission Impossible character who can break into anywhere, but the Macavity character who literally cannot be caught.)

                                                  I guess I'm somewhere in the middle here. I don't like purely mechanical Specialties. I feel that picks should be something a character would actually be able to express in character, or have expressed about them. "I have trained in many weapons, but the spear is my favorite," or, "Don't even try it, out pal here can spot a lie from a mile away," and that sort of thing. At the same time, I want ones that are practical given the mechanics over dropping character resources on overly specific or hard to adjudicate. ..

                                                  To the value of them in general... I've grown to view them as one of those relics that probably doesn't need to be there. I'd way rather see DB dice-caps get something close to the Lunar stunting method than continue to rely on Specialties.

                                                      Sure. And for a Supernal solar it's more a question of theme, than the one extra dice on top of your 10/20 dice roll with a bunch of charms. My Str 5, Ath 5 Ath Supernal doesn't have Athletics - Feats of Strength because I think I'll need it to tip over a wagon. He has it because he is the strongest one there is. And so he can lift one more plate than the other strongest guys. Or throw a Yeddim at someone. A stealth 5 dex 5 character doesn't want to sneak up on Ahlat, he wants to sneak up on Ahlat over a nightingale floor (-2) while injured (-1) when Ahlat knows he's coming (-2). Or hide behind Ahlat in Ahlat's shadow in his house in Yu-Shan for a day. Or do so on the move in combat without much cover (-5). You know, epic stuff for the epic stealth dude.

                                                      My survival 3 - Gross but Effective dude is fine if his spec doesn't apply. He's not hanging his hat on surviving in hell, or turning a tyrant lizard into a pet. He just wants to be more or less able to make his way wherever, and survival 3 does that. The other thing is mostly flavor, but encourages him to act and stunt a certain way, but could provide bonuses.

                                                      If I'm a dex 3 dodge* 3 Eclipse the difference between evasion 3 and 4 seems far different. And like you said, even for a maximum skill DB, a success or static adder is 50% more effective with a specialty.

                                                      Let's take a not-terribly combative dynast. Maybe he went to the Thousand Scales because men don't have enough self control to make good warriors. He's a fantastic bureaucrat, and getting better at politics. He's got dex 3, ma 3 and an Excellency (at least). That's pretty good (I mean, he's still a DB) If he has a spec, his parry is 4/6. If he doesn't, his parry is 3/4. One of these characters has a whole lot more to worry about from two or three Conscripts (literal peasants dragged from their fields) than the other does. That makes a specialty a big deal for him. (He's obviously going to win, in either case). But he's an elite martial artist (by the book's or a mortal's definitions, if not so among the Chosen), and the applicability of his specialty is likely to matter an awful lot more than either of the Solars above.

                                                      * Sidenote: It really kind of bothers me that investing the same amount of skill at specifically not getting stabbed or shot or whatever is no better than the side effect of getting good at punching people or stabbing them (and since there is no medium weapon equivalent for evasion, worse in many cases). But that's an unrelated issue.
