                                                        Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                                                        What do Terrestrial Gods get from prayer?
                                                        When rules for constructing spirit characters were given in Second Edition (and possibly in First, if it's in Games of Divinity I've not familiarised myself with it), a character's Cult rating was included in the equation given for how many Charms they got.

                                                        On top of the benefits of Cult itself, whatever they happen to be in a given Edition, gods were supposed to get a somewhat vague general increase in power from being widely worshipped, whether that be more Charms and a slightly higher Essence rating, or being able to reincarnate more quickly.

                                                        I doubt we'll get quite the same calculation for how many Charms spirits have in this Edition, but the same principles might apply. Indeed, with the example of Ahlat's Charm powered by his hecatomb, it can go a step further with the idea of some gods having specific powers that are boosted or restored by their characteristic worship.

                                                        ​EDIT: In Second Edition, they also had the idea of living sacrifices restoring Willpower equal to the Essence rating of the being sacrificed, so killing a mortal in the name of a god doesn't actually benefit them more than killing a chicken.

                                                        In this Edition, I'd say it can be the kind of thing that forms the conditions for the Charms of certain gods with associated natures instead.

                                                        As with Cult in general, though, I think the political reality behind some worship is a significant thing to hash out, and doesn't strictly need to exist in the mechanics, so I think often a Terrestrial god who is able to get people to kill each other over it mostly just benefits from the level of dominance implied by such an action; not just as something they get off on, but as an act of ritualized subservience that has a very powerful effect on people intended to make it harder to break away from the cult.

                                                        That and how, even if offerings might form an aspect of what make's a character's Cult rating, the nature of the offering can be gratifying in and of itself; I'm sure that dogs of the unbroken earth like to actually consume milk and meet from domesticated animals, for reasons slightly distinct from it being an aspect of their worship. So, depending on the nature of a sacrifice, a god might get a similar form of gratification from having a living thing, including a human, sacrificed directly to them, even if it doesn't take the form of literally eating them.

                                                        Incidentally, it occurs to me that the current Edition doesn't state that only gods or Exalted can benefit from Cult, and even outright implies that normal humans can, combined with how it's focused entirely on Willpower now.

                                                        That gives me the idea of a society that makes an organised tradition of placating a behemoth living up on the hill by feeding it somebody every week will also be providing it with a basis for a Cult rating. Hell, you could do the same with a mundane, but particularly storied and possibly fierce, animal.
