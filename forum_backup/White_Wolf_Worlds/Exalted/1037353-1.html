                This work has been a godsend for the campaign I'm in. Especially the introduction of the martial arts. I love the flavour of Hungry Ghost but it feels a little lackluster in use. Blood-Scenting Hunger doesn't really do much of anything that's useful and has too many charms working off of it with no real incentive to have it running. The form charm itself is also a little awkwardly worded. I feel the intent was to trap the user in the form for its duration, as a sort of blood-fueled frenzy, but it makes no mention of that being the case, even if it is implied (apologies if I am completely off on this). It also highlights one of my major concerns for the style, in that it doesn't really feel like it has an identity of its own. All it really does is provide a mote-generating engine for its charms or spells, but there is no real reason to actually take it. The charms aren't exactly consistent in themeing and there are precious few spells I'd consider using in combat so the only use is to funnel all that necrosis back into the charms.

                I'm not exactly the best when it comes to balance or mechanics though, but my ST is so I'll paste in a couple of her thoughts on it:

                Thoughts from a game design perspective:
                - I'm not super thrilled with the structure, little things like having two defence-penalising charms in a row are inelegant and make it feel less cohesive as a martial art.
                - The form's too bland for my taste in terms of mechanical identity; the flavour's cool but in practice its big defining strength, rather than something like "I get two turns every round" or "I can counter every attack against me" is "I get lots of motes", which is blehhh. I'd have reframed it as maybe getting some pool of bloodmurder points that can be spent to enhance the effectiveness of later Hungry Ghost charms or converted into necrosis, though that's a totally off the cuff idea that I haven't thought through at all.
                - There's not enough Terrestrial in the style; Unnatural Shambling Deftness especially is something that needs to have a Terrestrial limitation because that's specifically something DBs are meant to be bad at.
                - There's a bit too much hanging off Blood-Scenting Prana and not enough that Blood-Scenting Prana does by itself, which is especially painful considering it's a simple activation.
                - The non-Terrestrial versions of Leaping Horror Approach are like... yeesh. Outrageously large leaps for really painful costs, it's overpowered and underpowered at the same time in ways that make me all sorts of uncomfortable. Fine for DBs though.
                - Spell-Smothering Technique is, uhh, what. Why is a martial art suddenly casting Counterspell. That kind of disconnect is fine for SMAs, but it's going a bit far for a regular MA.
                - Soul-Flaying Strike is not a 3e charm. Aggravated damage was all the rage in the past, but it doesn't mean as much these days, so it's a very narrow effect.
                Some other thoughts of mine, that are just to point out some possible inconsistencies: Hungry Ghost mentions stunting unarmed to lethal, but doesn't actually have unarmed as a form weapon. And likewise Dark Messiah feels like it should have unarmed as a weapon? It's very fitting but that might be intentional. I also feel as though you could do a lot more with blood magic as an idea, right now there is just blood lash and a couple of spells with the tag but it's just full of potential to do more.

                I know you haven't been getting much critique on this, so I hope this helps!

                        I don't have anything as of yet, like I said I'm really not the best at mechanics or balancing. Right now I've just been focusing on figuring out evocations to use for my Blood Lash user. Some miscellaneous ideas I can throw out without any care for possible system balance or overlap though in terms of spells could be stuff like using blood to heal (whether it's your own or others, the latter seems more fitting though), some more advanced manipulation outside the lash, maybe even controlling other people's blood? Just some thoughts that came to me as I was typing this.

                        As for the keyword itself, I'm not sure what you could do with it aside from what you've already done. I like the idea of being able to use sorcerous motes for those spells, but the character I play won't have any use for that. Being able to substitute lethal damage instead of willpower is very nice and flavourful as well. I think it's in a pretty good spot, all things considered. The real thing is just spells themselves. Moving away from blood stuff even, a lot of it is super niche and only has real use when interacting directly with ghosts. I'm not sure what exactly could be done about that, because it's Necromancy and that's obviously its thing and you don't want to step on Sorcery's toes too much, but unless your game focuses a lot on ghosts and the underworld then most of the spells are kind of pointless. Like I said though, I don't know how much could be done about that that doesn't conflict with other things or goes against lore (I will admit, I'm not the greatest lore buff either, having only gotten into the system within the last year).

                            You should avoid using blood evocations for healing. The main theme of being a blood lash user is that you pay a price for your power. Letting that same power heal your wound negates the price you paid for it.

                            As for the spells, the majority of the spells written so far are conversions of spells from previous editions. There are many spells that rely on ghosts. But if you think of the Underworld as an entire world and the ghosts as its inhabitants, then those spells become real scary. Swap the term ghost for human and imagine what the spells would do to the people of Creation. The spells may be restrictive for stories set in Creation, but they are the reason why necromancers are extremely feared in the Underworld.

                                You must have misunderstood what I meant. Those were ideas for spells, not evocations I was writing for the blood lash. That said, with it as my control spell I almost never need to use my own blood to form it. An example of the kinds of things I'm messing around with for evocations would be having the lash adapt properties inherent in the blood used to form it (for example, a fire-aspect DB would create a lash of flaming blood, a wood-aspect would adapt the poisonous properties of their blood, etc).

                                As for the spells, yes, and that's an important thing to have, but at the same time it shouldn't be the extent of what necromancy can do. Being able to do to the living what you can do to the dead is absolutely terrifying and would be an excellent reason for it to be feared. Since necromancy incorporates the manipulation of flesh and blood as well as ghosts, it's a natural extension of what it can do.
