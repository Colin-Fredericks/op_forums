                            And finally, I have finished integrating the Primordial Exalts to the History section (not their full writeups for the project), as well as a few Eras from the different artifacts sections (and some other modifications for other sections as well).

                            I must say that those Exalts somewhat gave more more headache than the Celestial Exalts. I mean, previously I could just made up a whole new Incarna, killing them off and then work on some fictional history. However, the Primordial Exalts were more complicated because, well, the primordials generally didn't made Chosen. Sure, there are a few canon outliners (Autochthon, Sacheverell and Oramus), but the vast majority, as it seems, either didn't knew how to do so or didn't want to due to the circumstances. While I have added some "original cast", there is a limit to how many primordials I can make up before it feels weird, and there are themes which I want to explore for existing ones, hence me going through an headache that every time I decided on a new patron, I had to think about both how did they learn the way to create Exalts, why they did so, and how they did it when they are either dead, gone or bound by the surrender oaths. Still, I think I did do my best.

                            Among the homebrew primordials, we had 2 which were pretty original and one which was adapted from the Memorial Exalted project. Sedna was a very easy choice for me- the idea of the Glacials as the Chosen of a primordial related to cold who have escaped Creation during the Primordial War was one of the first ideas that came to my mind long, long, long time ago (basically when I became aware of Exalted for the first time). The reason is that ever since I was a child, I was obsessed with "Winter-based" fantasy forces, probably because of how the Snow Queen was my favorite fairytale. As such, I found it very easy to adapt Sedna, a goddess related to the arctic irl and the name of a dwarf planet found deep in the Kuiper belt (hence the name) to the setting as one of the primordials who have lurked beyond Creation only to invade it later. Things like the Ten Year Winter or the Frozen Star Intrusion from the artifact eras have allowed an easy adaption. You might ask yourself why did I used Sedna and not Hunanura, which was implied to be a primordial related to cold in 2e, but I wanted to use Sedna for the astronomical reference and, well, Hunanura was dead in 2e, so you may consider it as a behemoth created by Sedna or something in this setting. Relating her to the dying thing which sleeps in the Northwest was just a fun little easter egg. I still need to think about what did the Glacials actually did in Creation, but I guess I will develop it later.

                            As for the other two, Thalassa was pretty much made as a neutral primordial as reference for how in greek mythology the sea took a neutral stance in the Titanomachy, while also serving as the patron of the Chosen of the Depths- the ide for a new Exalt splat which was discarded early on. Still, I like the idea, so it is going to show up here- I originally considered them to be present across the time of Creation, but while thinking about possible history for them I eventually decided that with Primordial Exaltation taking the form of a "process" rather than an "object", it would be interesting for the process to be forgotten after the Niobraran War due to all Exalts being killed, only for it to resurface a millennia later. Mnemosyne, on the other hand, have been mostly the same as in her presentation in the Memorials project- she is the primordial who invented the idea of death and drew the Underworld for Creation, and which sided with the gods in the War, creating her own Chosen. I have changed the circumstances behind her death, however, with her getting killed by the Neverborn while trying to create a process for their reincarnation. Still probably need to expand on the actual history of the Chroniclers, as the Memorials are called in this setting.

                            This gave me (when Autochthon is included) 4 types of Exalts, which meant that I needed at least 5 more to complete the "set". For this, I have turned to canon primordials. Gaia was a very intuitive choice, as people have talked a lot about the possibility for her having Chosen of her own. While I considered to do something else, in the end I decided to stop beting around the bush, as the Animal Avatars were implied a lot to be "Gaia's answer for the Exalts only in animal form", so I decided to just role with it. I am yet to decide whether or not those animals with a human soul would have a human or animal body (I am wary of using shapeshifting due to Lunars being a thing), but regardless, I think it is a good way to integrate those guys. The more "druidic" aesthetic was instead taken by the Arcadians, the Chosen of Cytherea- which is something I like because I really wanted to do something with her, and her Chosen masquerading as the Gaian Exalts feels appropriate (and it was implied in 2e that she has some connection to dragon lines, so we are going to play around with that part). Isidoros was also a easy choice- after all, originally he was supposed to have Chosen of his own, like Oramus and Sacheverell. The Unfettered, however, are going to be somewhat different from the Revelers, but I may try to draw inspiration from them to a degree. I have also considered Ramethus (hence why you would see references for him in the writeup), but eventually I decided to give him a different role.

                            Now we are going to the more difficult choices- SWLiHN was chosen simply because I read somewhere that someone had an ide for her having Chosen with insect like aesthetic, and because the Three Spheres Cataclysm felt like a naturally way to introduce an instant for Exaltation. Surprisingly, while I started thinking about her Exalts, the more I started to "get" their fictional history, creating the Burning Pyre Empire in order to integrate them (something I pretty much like). Szorny was not the first Choice for me as a candidate for Exaltation, but I do like this Yozi and I did originally used him as a catalyst for the corrupted version of the Lunar Exalts, so while it took me a while to think how did he found out the secret of Exaltation and why it would use it, eventually I managed to piece it out together (not to mention that having the Silver Forest teaching Isidoros how to make Chosen and Isidoros just rolling with it while Szorny taking his time just felt appropriate). Using Ta'akozoka for the Yearned, however, was something that I had in mind from the beginning of this project- in the original draft, his Chosen were some kind of corrupted Lunars, yet in this iteration he made ones by himself. Him doing so as a Neverborn was not a easy choice to make, but considered that according to many fan writeups Ta'akozoka is one of the primordials to favor Necromancy it made sense that he would keep a sense of lucidity compared to the other Neverborn, something which would allow him to play around in Creation and eventually make Exalts of his own- sacrificing that lucidity as a toll for the act.

                            Now, this should have been enough Exalts for the Primordials, but the thing was that there were some candidates that I wanted to integrate and simply didn't had a room for them. While I considered a state similar to the one I used for the Celestials (one set which resonates with the primary MM from the family and one with the secondary), I didn't had enough ideas for a whole new set, and the project is bloated as is so I need to trim where possible. This is where I decided to introduce five more patrons which represent the primary colours of jade, as none of the Exalts actually resonates with it (sure, the Gnostics resonate with yilistr, but it is not the same thing), and it would help to reinforce each of the colours as its own MM. The main figure which I wanted to adapt was, actually, Jheel Khan- I was always interested in this primordial, and with the description of Vainglory it felt appropriate to use her as the power behind it. There is a note that unlike Ta'akozoka, the Accursed are not truly the Chosen of the Neverborn which Jheel Khan became, but were created from the last remnant of her living Essence through her jeweled blood, which was later cursed by her own ghost. The Sophists were another idea which I made up for the original project and which I really liked, so integrating them was a fun experience. Kimbery having her own Chosen felt almost natural, even though that the whole Lintha spin around their Exalts put some challenged in order to adapt how her Chosen came into being. The Withered were actually a mishmash of different concepts that I had, with Metagaos being "draconic" felt like a good enough reason to use him as a patron for a Jade related Exalts. Finally, the Ebon Dragon was the most difficult choice I made for a patron, one of which I am yet to feel completely comfortable with. However, using his Exalts in order to integrate lore from 2e Infernals (in terms of them being a "secret spy org" and some pieces from the RotSE) felt like a fun experience. Oh, and if you ask yourself "how did Jheel Khan and Ta'akozoka got Exalts while Mardukth didn't when he is the most well known Neverborn in 2e", don't worry, he is around- just in a different section of the project.

                            So yeah, this is a comprehensive reasoning for why each of the primordials was chosen as patrons for this project. Hope you find it of interest while I start working on the writeup for the Exalts themselves.

                                            Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                            Now, I feel like I need to detail a bit what exactly I have been doing with the Lesser Incrana- originally, the idea of them was using it as a blanket term for "less powerful Incrana which are also less known", a way to integrate a few new Exalts with minimal disruption to the setting. Basically, all of the Incrana were to have the same esoteric origin, with the Lesser ones simply being less powerful gods, somewhat on par with the patrons of Terrestrial Exaltations such as the Dark Mother, the Elemental Dragons or Ketu. However, as I started on working on them, something felt a bit "off" about the attitude- sure, it could have worked, but the whole metaphysical shenanigans which are going in the setting have started to feel stretched- if all Incrana are just the same type of being, why the magical materials which the Lesser Incrana are tied to are less important? Also, why to have seven of them? What makes Hemera, the Goddess of Day's Sky, less important than, let's say, Nox or the Maidens? Why Iris, the Goddess of the Rainbows, have avoided making Exalts during the Primordial War? Why Neith and Ouranos were significantly weaker compared to the true Incrana? Basically- if not all Incrana were made equal, why were they made at all?
                                            Sorry if i come off too bluntly, but to me those sound very much chicken & egg dilemmas. Would anyone even think of the Magical Materials as such, instead of forgotten among countless esoteric components/reagents, if there were no Chosen of the Incarnae or Dragons to resonate or disonate with some over others in attempts to draw out evocations out of artifacts made with them? (Not even going into the matter that sometimes those things can go a little pear-shaped, such as Gorgon in Arms of Chosen being made of white jade but also resonating like Soulsteel for those chosen with an affinity for that material - or the matter of white & black jade in general sometimes just going well with things touched by the essence of the Underworld)

                                            The Incarnae while mysterious and sometimes distant are undeniably still puisant, present and active in Creation's affairs - but how loud is the fall of a tree where nothing exists to hear it? Are the Dark Mother, the Elemental Dragons or Ketu less truly puisant than the UCS, Luna or the Maidens or just more elusive & idiosyncratic on the peculiar priorities in the making of their Chosen? The same principle might be said to apply with any "lesser" Incarna patrons. More obscure/secretive does not exactly have to mean weaker.

                                            As an aside, i've toyed with the idea of Uranus, Neptune & Pluto as either fallen Maidens - or copycats/impostors entities claiming to be so - in a few past games of my own, among a few other things relating to [alleged] dead Incarnae.​​
