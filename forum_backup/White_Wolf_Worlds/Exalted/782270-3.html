                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                "Jeez, what was the big deal of Heracles to the Greeks and Romans, all he did was fight a lion, some birds and a swamp lizard. Any liquered up hillbilly could do that with a shotgun at the zoo."
                Uh ... yes ... and and the lion and the birds and the lizard were in the story and got killed as the antagonists they were - they had no agenda. It's extremely rare to have animals in myths which are protagonists because of their animalness. Anansi and Mantis and Inchneumon might be animals, but in their myths they act just like humans. Simba might be a king of the lions, but the story is not "that is how lions behave in Africa", the story is "Hamlet as told with animals". So I don't really understand why Lunars seem to be limited so strongly to "like animals in nature" instead of being "like animals in fables".

                Another problem I see with Lunars is the definition of "best survivors" mostly as "can adjust to any circumstance". I would argue that - at least in our world - the best survivors are humans simpley because we don't adjust to the circumstances but adjust the circumstances to us. This is partly related to the strong predator/barbarian theme of the Lunars and the inherent limitation of this: predators are often the weakest part of an ecosystem and rely on prey (or metaphorically: civilization to plunder) and die out without them, while the "weak prey" can survive on its own.
                Of course, changing Lunars from ... let's call it "reactive survivors" to "active survivors" and supporting this with charms would turn them from "inwards" Exalted to "outwards" Exalted and apparently the designers don't want to do that. But in my opinion this means that Lunars are inherently limited.
                Well, yea, the Spider King is apparently such an "outwards Lunar", but he seems so ... off ... and different to other Lunars in the corebook.


                Anyway, my group has decided on "Lunars can be anything they want". Similar like Luna has the othermoons with their own themes, the Lunar in our group can chose any theme he wants and incorporate it in his charmset. The number of themes he can have (and in more crunchy terms: the roles he can fill/support in the party) is limited by Essence and his commitment - he is not able to dabble into anything a little, but instead choses a handful of focusses which he expresses through as many attributes as possible.
                Of course, this does not fit in any way with the official stance on Lunars and how their splatbook will look. :P

                            Originally posted by Ghosthead
                            In theory, I'd also like to say that, as a notion, which may or may not hold up under scrutiny, I like the fact that on some level all the Exalted splats really should be are designed with the kind of depth that they can think deeply about changing and shaping human societies for what they believe to be the better, and can act as guides in how to achieve that, in real terms, replete with all moral hazard.
                            I feel as though the problem with this is that it's the kind of thing where you ultimately get each type of Exalted as being an advocate for a kind of Hat for a society to wear (so you get things like people talking about Solars as advocates for a particular kind of civilization, say, urbanized and with complex stratification, compared to the Lunars as a kind of tribal one and... I'm not even sure for the rest), which would seem to have a few issues to me. The most obvious is that it would grate against the rest of the game's structure in which the Exalted are supposed to be notably different from one another, as well as their hierarchy (which would also mean you kind of bias in favour of what the Solars advocate, given their advantage), which was done to avoid the pitfalls of them being reskins of the same thing. It also looks to me like something fairly shallow, because depth wouldn't support its necessary rigidity. There's also the matter of how, while part of the attitude of the game is remaking the world, this is more a matter of stories resulting from the application of mechanics rather than mechanics themselves, because it's not a management sim, and it would otherwise be something that kind of leaves a character focusing on something like Melee out. That leads into the final point; that the Solar Exalted are at the core of the game, meaning they're at the forefront of fulfilling that central narrative premise of "passionate, motivated people have the capacity to change the world"; I think it would represent a failing of that premise being used by the core of the game to say "actually, you can only do it in a particular way". At this point, I don't see a compelling reason to make the story in which primitivist advocate Solar and urban Solar (and possibly their dispute) are not equally valid. A central tension of the game is the world holding its breath to see what kind of world the returned Lawgivers will make, and I think that's diminished if they're only given a limited selection; I also don't think it helps the credibility of the other ones to say that they have their own subsets of world changing that they're naturals to, but have either failed to implement, or the implementation.*

                            I talked about a difficulty in grasping the Thousand Streams River from the surface level, but with this perspective I have a new sense of what is wrong with it. Namely, that in trying to figure out something for the Lunars to have been doing, it took a premise covertly at the heart of the central narrative of Exalted ("returning heroes to renew hope and justice in the world"), made it overt, overly technical, and in the hands of characters to whom it was not suited and, worst of all, couched it in terms of the cynicism that the premise was supposed to be against.

                            The Thousand Streams River said "you have to remake the world into something in which the Exalted have no place, because their reign is illegitimate and unjust", and the game just doesn't really work if it assumes that of the Exalted. The game is supposed to grab people's attention and give them direction for play with the idea that they're playing characters whose return to power can be truly hopeful and righteous if they succeed. If it creates a strong advocacy for the idea that the characters you're given to play are not legitimate, then what are you supposed to do with them?

                            That, I think, may be the biggest issue with the Thousand Streams River; its core premises are directly contradictory.

                            * Yes, I know there are people who will look at the idea that the other Exalted are not naturals to the task of guiding and renewing the world as a suggestion that they are worthless and incompetent compared to the Solars, to whom they are obligated. To that I say: when the premise of the game is that you're coming into a world at the end of history, without hope or fresh impetus, then I feel that to say that the other Exalted should have equal facility with that Solar narrative is to render them failures and incompetent, and that to say that this isn't part of what they do frees them of some of the potential strain to their credibility. I mean, that the world has fallen so far in the Second Age still reflects badly on the other Exalted, but in terms of their interactions and certain key decisions and even just their luck, not on things pertinent to the means by which you play them. To play a Lunar as focused on something other than the task of renewing the world is to be able to play a Lunar who can do those things well. Not everybody needs to be a culture hero to guide the world, and under other circumstances the world could have done without culture heroes. Even placing that responsibility on the returning Solars need not make others subservient to them; not if the Solars can be guided or constrained by the advice or opposition of others.

                                                    Personally I think Lunars would be best positioned as those who bridge the wyld amd creation. The scouts, emissaries, explorers, who go before the Solars. They are fringe dwellers and hunters by purpose, not by default, and their greatest strength is their immunity to the wyld.

                                                    As for them being the least powerful, i think that's an unfortunate effect of meta gaming in design. The original writers wanted each exalt type to function mechanically different from each other and lunars, whose charms are attached to attributes, got the un-playtested short end of the stick.
