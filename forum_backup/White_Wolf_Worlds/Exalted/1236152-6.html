    Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post
    I generally abide by a rule of "only one stat at 5, no ability above 3 unless it's a large part of my character's concept". Solars are actually amazing for this - you can throw your Charms around to the point of not having more than 3 in any single ability (including your Supernal), and just by virtue of the free excellencies and the strength of starting Charms, you'll still have a viable and fun to play character at the end.

    Dragon-Blooded require a bit more finagling, but after playing them a bit (we've had like four sessions), I find that the combination of having to purchase excellencies and kinda crappy low-level Charms is weirdly helpful - you can use a good 40% of your starting Charms to start with a reliable broad expertise, and you'll still be able to meaningfully contribute in your secondary areas of focus while ceding the spotlight when it comes to challenges you're completely unsuited to. (Also, the Ability 1 prerequisite on excellencies means that you can get away with dropping a few in favor of cooler stuff in your primary area of expertise, then picking them up one by one in play on a "whenever needed" basis, because the training time is literally just "spend half an afternoon on this".)
    I actually have a very similar chargen rule when I'm the storyteller. Only one attribute or ability (players choice) can start at 5. No starting abilities above 3 dots without significant backstory explaining higher proficiency, and even in those cases, no more then three abilities above 3. This rule is specifically for Solars. I haven't yet run a Dragonblood game, but I would probably relax the limitations a bit due to the unique education Dragonbloods receive.

        Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
        It's honestly pretty disingenuous to the writers to make these kind of comparisons. A better way of asking these questions would be to say: What level of mastery does it make sense for a Dragon Blood to pull a weapon out of thin air? Or: What level of mastery does it make sense for a Solar to pull a weapon out of thin air? Obviously the writers felt that a Dragon Blood had to master swordsmanship to a greater extent because that fits with the overarching themes of DB and their power structure as a whole. Whereas a solar swordsman of 'expert' level should be able to do so as well.

        I really don't want this to come across as overly harsh, but, uh, neither of those provides an explanation? "It is so because the devs decided that this is appropriate" is a tautology - everything in the book is there in its current form because the devs decided that's the way it is appropriate for them to be there. "It is so because themes" is meaningless unless you actually explain how those themes manifest.

        Allow me to provide an example:

        Solars embody the themes of the Unconquered Sun in their Charmset. As they grow in Essence, they emulate the perfection of the Unconquered Sun in all of their endeavors.Therefore it makes sense for their Charms to come with high ability prerequisites - if you fail to constantly strive to perfect yourself in the most basic foundations of your abilities, how could you possibly grasp the deeper mysteries of the divine essence that courses through your veins?

        By contrast, the Dragon-Blooded Charmset expresses the Exalt's growing command over the elements. They start out with fairly straightforward expressions of pure skill tinged with elemental symbolism, but as they progress in Essence, their ability to influence natural phenomena grows more overt, until it blossoms into literal command over the elements themselves.

        One of these themes lends itself well to the ability to create an item out of nothing. The other one, not so much.

        Yet apparently it is harder for the "as I grow in power, I gain control over the elements" splat to create a sword out of natural elements than it is for the "I emulate the perfection of the Unconquered Sun through my skills" splat to sword so hard that their ability to do so literally becomes a sword.

        Thus Spake Zaramørke:

        I don't see Glorious Solar Saber as summoning a magic blade, but rather the blade as a fundamental outgrowth of your skill, your anima bursting outward and hardening into the shape of that skill to form a weapon. It is your soul made physical.
        (...)
        [Glorious Solar Saber] comes out of the sword as an outgrowth, an extension of his hand, his spirit. (...) He wouldn't ever feel like he has the power to form objects of light. Only the power to manifest pieces of his soul that he has spent a long time forging through skill and practice.
        Glorious Solar Saber is a transcendent miracle made possible only by such a dedication to wielding weapons that doing so became a fundamental part of who you are. The other Charm is a fancy way to control the elements, an extension of the most basic capability any Dragon-Blooded can just do by default.

        Yet the latter requires a deeper dedication to swordsmanship than the former.

        The entire point of my complaint is that there is a discrepancy between the themes expressed by the placement of these Charms in the context of their respective charmsets and the mechanical requirements of obtaining them.

            But what if we look at it like this: remember that the books are always saying that the Charms are not actually named magic powers that the characters have, but are just written that way for us - the readers - so that we can remember them. In all actuality, what is being represented by these Charms are the inner strengths and wonders that the characters are able to achieve.

            So, if that's the case, then how could someone who is only the best swordsman in town (Melee 3) do some of the things that are covered by these Charms that currently have Melee minimums of 4 or 5?

            Think of it in Superman terms. Let's say you want to create Superman as an Exalt type that focuses on Attributes instead of Abilities. And you want him to have a Charm that is called "Stronger than a Locomotive" that will allow him to stop a train running at full speed. Well, how can the minimum Strength for that Charm be anything less than 5? We think of the power coming from the Charm, but it's actually coming from the character. The Charm is just a way for us players to keep track of what's going on.

                    I think the GSS vs DGW thing is kind of a moot point because they're pretty much incomparable. Solars get to summon an artifact weapon at melee 3 because Solars are extremely powerful. DBs on the other hand get a charm that they can use to almost always buff their already artifact weapon, which makes it more powerful than GSS because it enhances an already artifact weapon. Also any DB can get this from chargen whereas almost every Solar needs to wait 12 sessions before they get to start using theirs.


                    Other than that I'm seeing a few problems here that I might be able to clear up with the limited wordcount I have.

                    DB Charms Suck in Power Level

                    Yeah, compared to Solars they definitely do, they're closer to mortal power levels than any other splat. The thing is, you need to start thinking more like a mortal to be effective. Things like the weapons with the Smashing or Chopping tag are actually important, you need to rely on your artifact weapon to give you more of your edge, as well as teamwork. I've played several games with DBs using the new rules and my players have torn through packs of claw striders and Dogs of the Unbroken Earth.

                    Admittedly there was a little more back and forth there than I'd expect from a Solar group, but I liked that. Rather than just have the Dawn one-two AoE combo the group to death by herself the party had to work together. They also had to rely more on things like the small army they brought with them, and making clever deals and compromises rather than just ploughing through everything with their power.

                    The one thing I will say is that it sucks to be so far from the super cool Essence 3 signature charms, since most games I know barely run long enough to get more than a session of play out of them. DBs are maybe supposed to be a slower burn game.

                    DB Charms are too heavily skewed to high ability dots

                    Yeah they are. But lets not pretend like Solar trees are immune to this either. Sail doesn't even have one charm that's prerequisite is Sail 1, or Sail 2 for that matter.

                    Purchasing DB Excellencies is Restrictive

                    Yes, but only at chargen, and only if you spread yourself thin. Which I encourage you to do. As a DB you're going to pick up 19 charms, not counting sorcery, MA or evocations, before you hit Essence 3. So you'll have a total of 34 charms by then. That's 2 charms away from buying every single Lore, Dodge, Archery, and Bureaucracy charm, to pick trees at random. So unless you're planning on being a complete master of 4 different trees, you can definitely afford to spread yourself out around 6-7, with some dips into a few more. It's that slow burn again.

                    Aura Sucks

                    http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...68#post1217268

                    It may not be intuitive, which I understand might be a problem, but in the second half of that post "Aspect" I go into how to use aura effectively. Even being a fire aspect, who's specialty is using earth aspect charms inside the water aspected brawl tree. Just ignore the part where I talk about entering aura pre-combat, it's harder to do that I thought.

                    EDIT: Yeah also I forgot in the FIRST half of that post I talk about why being a DB martial artist is way cooler than being a Solar one, as long as the Solar isn't MA supernal, since you've got like, an 18 session lead on them. Or 4.5 months for a weekly game.


                    It isn't for everyone, but DBs are a LOT less defined by their charms, and a LOT more defined by their merits and actual character. A Solar might be an amazing fighter who trained hard and can beat back an army with his bare hands, play a tear jerking flue tune, hurl an elephant through a stone wall, and leap over a mountain. He also heads a mercenary company, and has some contact in the guild.

                    In contrast a DB is the Dragon Commander of the Fifth Eastern Fleet, granddaughter of The Ghost of the Sapphire isles, and keeper of Bal-Shara, the Jade Thunder direlance, who will stop at nothing to avenge her sister. Also she can lift a cart over her head, can hear through the earth, and can slay any mortal in single combat. The feats are less impressive and character defining, but that just makes the other flavours come out more.

                    Also if you like being able to excellency almost anything you care to do, or hate how aura can change your combat style, or have difficulty finding fun in a game where you need to take things like essence 2 earth elementals seriously, then DBs aren't really for you.
