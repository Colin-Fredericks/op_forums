                    Originally posted by Rater202
                    I might be drastically misinterpreting this, but I've heard that in 1E, the fist step towards Chimerism was Sprit Shape changing from one animal to another while keeping a few traits from the original animal.

                    Goat to wolf with a goat's horns and hooves, for example.

                    If MahawahI'msougly is a Chimera by 3E's deifintion, he probably did his "Wolf with goat horns" thing to himself.

                    Of Course, if he screwed up his Chimerism and gave himself the "horns and hooves in all forms thing" and he became a Chimera around the time of the usurpation, he could well pull a Doctor Doom and blame it on the Sids/Solars/Dragon Blooded/X when it's really his fault in the first place-especially if his Human Form isn't actually all that Ugly beyond the horns and he's just so vain that he can't see beyond his one flaw.
                    Chimerism in 1e was actually like chimerism in 2e, only with fewer rules about how it happened. Switching your totem was seen as courting chimerahood, but obviously if you had tattoos, nothing was going to come of it. 2e just made the state of affairs worse by making it a mechanically-preferred state to be in, which could technically be had by cutting off only half of your Limit bar, collecting your cool new powers, and going on your merry way.The taboo against totem mixing was ported over to 2e as well, kind of, in the form of Perfected Hybrid Interaction, with the same nonsensical line about other Lunars looking down on it as "courting chimerism". EX3 is a change in that it's reassigning the term "Chimera" to mean the formerly somewhat taboo practice of mixing totems from 1e, and making it acceptable, with no Wyld-tainted knockoffs of The Thing to be seen, at least not among the Lunars.

                    As for Ma-Ha-Suchi, his 1e description, upon re-reading, does in fact seem to make him like the Lunar Deled. He requires zealous hatred of the Realm from his beastmen brood, and while a courteous host to all who offer him proper respect, will sacrifice even other Lunars to Luna at one of the many celebrations of the Bloody Huntress that he regularly and frequently holds. His goat-wolf-man-ness is never mentioned as being important or an underlying cause of any of his personal "quirks" regarding the hatred of the Realm. He's simply a god-chief plotting war, and he has designs on using the Scavenger Lands as the head of his spear against the Realm.

                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                        There really isn't anything about Ma-Ha-Suchi that depends on Lunars having some devolving into horrible monstrosities mechanic.

                        He definitely won't suddenly be portrayed as mentally ill. Equating mental illness with evil is a hella prominent thing that is hella gross, and not something I'd really want to contribute to.
                        Saying a characters delusions could be fuel for their evil acts is not the same as saying all mental illness = evil. If Ma-Ha-Suchi has delusions of persecution which drive him on a vindictive campaign against all those who he imagines to have "wronged" him, I would say he is mentally ill. Mental illness whether it be psychopathy or paranoid delusions can cause people to commit evil acts, and honestly I think it should be part of the narrative conversation with most villains. Do they have a legitimate motivation for their acts, or are they operating on a general disregard for the well being of others, and a delusional view of the world?

                        Somebody who starts a bar-room brawl may just have some simple psychological anger management issues, somebody who leads an army of goat-men against the world over imaginary sleights from a millennium ago, while completely ignoring their own despicable actions...that is a mental illness!

                                            The biggest problem with Second Edition Lunars is that they kidnapped and force-fed you into drinking the kool-aid.

                                            In First Edition, Chimera were simply socially frowned upon --- but viable for those who grow (for example, someone who looks like Ma-Ha-Suchi might have started with goat (calm yet head-strong?) or wolf (pack loyalty, feral) -- and after a century or four (of either meditation or warfare) -- transitions to the other beast (goat - the serenity of age? a stronger will? --- wolf - bravery forged in battle? loyalty from new friends?). Chimerism could serve as a symbol of character growth.

                                            In Second Edition, it was illness and madness and often a death sentence by your peers -- a luxury only First Age elders could enjoy (and even then, taken as a sign of their corruption).

                                            In First Edition, they played up the Barbarian savage part a lot --- but akin to Solars, Terrestrial Lost Eggs, etc, it wasn't absurd to imagine a Lunar who shunned his peers, stuck to the cities, did their own thing.

                                            In Second Edition, the Wyld Hunt excels at hunting Lunars -- and if they don't scare you off, you have Lunar Elders who will borderline kidnap you to "save" you. The young Lunar can expect -- after Exalting in a moment of epicness -- to soon after be chased or dragged away from home and family (and maybe by the time you return, if you can find the way, they'll all be victims of whatever you were fighting).

                                            Don't want tattoos? Tough -- either get branded like cattle OR turn into a monster (and the book even mentions that some Lunars won't even risk you turning into Chimera, forceable tattooing or simply killing the young Lunar and hoping a barbarian gets Exalted in your place).

                                            Grudgingly accept the tattoos? Fine -- but first you need to go through the Wyld, doing some arcane tests for some artifact-disfigurement we don't want, by people we can't stand. This adds further problems if you want to play characters with religious or ethical quandaries or who simply do better in urban environments. This isn't merely Abyssal-style "you can join a Deathcult" - no, you have to deal with these neo-shamanistic psychotic-Elders.

                                            .....who then consider that you owe them a debt.

                                            It's 100% feasible and canon-friendly for you to be kidnapped (and thus unable to defend your people), tossed into the Wyld, indoctrinated by ancient god-monsters who will murder or cripple you if you refuse their gifts (or forceably tattoo you) and THEN be held in DEBT to them.

                                            .

                                            And before anyone claims this a criticism ala "its bad a splat has a culture" or "its bad a splat has a distinct look" --- um false equivalencies.

                                            Terrestrials have one massive culture -- with 13 or so sub-variants -- and Outcastes all throughout other regions. They can look completely human to elemental-in-the-flesh.

                                            Abyssals have multiple Deathlords -- each with distinct regions, social norms, goals -- and ronin are options. They can look completely human, immaculate beauty or decaying undead.

                                            Sidereals have two major factions -- a few minor ones -- and multiple different Bureaus and specialties to define themselves. They can look completely human and have heaven's artifacts at their disposal to look more unique,

                                            Lunars get "here's the Silver Pact -- leave your home, adopt our ink, acquire a debt, agree to our mores, become a Wyld shaman and on rare occasions, build a society maybe, if we don't find it too offensive". Want to hide your tattoos? You're penalized a charm slot. The whole Silver Pact write-up is probably the most extensive antagonist chapter the splat has (even though many seem to drink the kool-aid).

                                            .

                                            (of course, this is all ranting about 2E and I've love to see what happens in 3E, for better or worse)
