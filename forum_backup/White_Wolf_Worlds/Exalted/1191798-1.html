                        (Following may be tl:dr, but I'm trying to keep it fairly short and sweet)

                        I might explain it like.... roughly, in 1e, of the original Exalted you had:

                        a) Solars, who were the most powerful and got a lot of written adventures, hooks, fluff (some people would argue the setting and game was almost *purely* about playing Solars)
                        b) Dragonblooded who were weak, but got a lot of written adventures, hooks, fluff (they got their own Aspect Books, Exalted: The Outcastes, and so on)
                        c) Sidereals, who were in between Solars and Dragonblooded in power and had little setting material, but because they had a high concept, an imaginative sub setting and imaginative, tricksy Charms, were very popular online and had writers write memorable, cool things about them, and lots of cool fans saying awesome about them
                        d) Abyssals, who were Solar tier, powerful and basically death-y Solars,
                        e) Lunars.... who pretty much had none of these things, and wonky mechanics (and some political propositions about civilization and barbarism that some folks *really* hated).

                        So, clamor for Lunars to be better mechanically, and have a high concept, and to be more written into the setting.

                        This got stronger in 2e when the Infernals came out, and everyone got obsessed with both Infernals and pushing the envelope with complex Charm mechanics that were supposed to do weird yet high concept things that Said Things About Things in a grand way (whether or not Infernals actually did so wholly successfully being debatable). Charms that did classic, genre defining things were somewhat frowned upon, and 2e Lunar Charms were very much *not* high concept at all (many were scarcely even memorable)! And hence various homebrew reimaginings of the Lunars that sought to make them very high concept (and basically sort of like Infernals - Terrifying Argent Witches is one example of such homebrew).

                        Things are probably more chilled out today, and it feels like people just want a Lunars that is generally true to their earliest inspirations, have a solid place in the setting and are well executed; rather than trying to make them a high concept thing that's away from their first origins in the setting and such.

Infernals let me play a shapeshifting demigod without the threat of being kidnapped and converted.

The present edition, though, doesn't mess around with presenting Dragon-Blooded organized together as serious threats, and there's no individual bond to anyone like in 2e (it's strictly opt-in like in 1e), so the dimensions of play might shape up a bit differently.
