                        As much as I love both Sids and MAs...I'd really rather they not be "the MA exalted".

                        Personally, I'd rather see Astrology punched up into shape so that it is their first, best response to most situations, and is what they are most known (and feared/respected) for!

                        As it is, when 1st and 2nd Editions established Viziers as "peerless martial artists", what most people *heard* was "the best fighters"...which clearly isn't the case, so it makes people confused. What was meant was that only Sids could *make, practise and teach* the most powerful MA styles, and only Solaroids can learn them otherwise (and even they can't practise them as efficiently, since they can't use discount sutras). This plays into their roles as teachers. Granted, previous editions could have done a better job with mechanically representing the teacher role; we didn't see anything of the sort until late 2E gave us Crane Style, followed up by Throne Shadow.

                        I'm confident 3E will rectify this from the start.

                        But, yes, Siddies are meant to be great teachers, not strong fighters, hence the "glass jaw" Ox-Body...

                            04-04-2014, 03:12 PM
                            Originally posted by Morangias View Post
                            I have to tell you, having ran a few Sidereals' campaigns, for once I'd like to see a non-MA Sid. It's dull as fuck when the main thing differentiating the characters mechanically is what second Style they used (second, because the first was invariably VBoS for Secrets of Future Strife and Blade - this was pre-2.5).

                            As such, I'd rather not bring Sids' mastery of MA even more to the foreground than it already is. And it is - they learn CMA as good as Solars and better than everyone else, they learn SMA better than Solars both in terms of costs and effectiveness once they hit the Form, their "hero style" is actually a meta-style focused on teaching MA to others, they invented the damn thing in the first place!
                            While I quite understand where the complaints in this thread come from, I'm with Morangias here.

                            Let me address a few points:

                            Sidereals aren't really better at MA than other Exalted

                            True, as a mechanical matter, and a good thing too. This isn't really much of a 2e-ism, either: even in 1e, Martial Arts were something that Sidereals emphasized and they were just about as good at them as Solars. Which is to say, better than pretty much everyone else. You cannot reasonably make a splat mechanically superior in combat using MA than Solars, or else you've consigned yourself to everyone else having MA as a distinctly second-fiddle option. It already is in some cases!

                            Sidereals need something to make them stick out!

                            That's SMA.

                            I know this isn't a good answer in 2e, where there's about a style and a half worth of unborked SMA in the game, but this is where the thematic comes in. It's where you start seeing the answer to 'what makes Sidereals martial arts masters?'

                            Y'know that 'simple comparison' section in Scroll of the Monk? Here:

                            A mundane martial arts master can split a brick
                            and jump over a hedge.
                            A Terrestrial martial arts master can split a boulder
                            and jump over a house.
                            A Celestial martial arts master can split a city
                            wall and jump over a mountain.
                            A Sidereal martial arts master can split a soul
                            and jump to Yu-Shan
                            This is both a technically valid comparison (sort of- I don't think CMA actually allows city-splitting unless it is a very small city) and practically quite misleading, because it seems to indicate a ramping-up of power. SMA isn't about ramping up power, it's showing off the martial artist's absurd mastery. It's that turtle in Kung-Fu Panda who could disable the big bad villain by lightly tapping him a few times. It's about feeling a light brush on your stomach and looking down to see your body unraveling into nothingness.

                            I've seen some accusations that this is essentially sideways progression- just using Martial Arts for things Martial Arts really has nothing to do with. Well... yes. Look, even unbroken SMA styles are powerful as hell, but you can't represent supernal enlightenment simply by doubling the numbers on CMA charms and calling it a day. There's really nothing for SMA to do other than stuff like that.

                            So yes. SMA are fairly potent combat charms which also serve a utility role, and are flashy. They are also the advantage that Sidereals get over everyone else, because the very most you can reasonably do for actual combat is make a Sidereal Martial Artist as good at MA as a Solar- and even then, the Solar MAist has a better mote pool.

                            Sidereals should be better at teaching martial arts

                            I'm divided on this issue.

                            In general, I think people who want Sidereals to be better 'teachers' have the wrong idea in mind. A Sidereal is a premiere advisor, font of all arcane knowledge and- just by default- knower of a thousand dark secrets of Creation. The archetypes they draw from, though, are the great sages who sit on mountains or secretly make the young Emperor wise, not Ms. Frizzle.* They're the masters which appear when the student is ready, not the masters which ready the student. The fact that you don't learn their many powerful secrets easily is a feature, not a bug.

                            But I'm not sure that Martial Arts are exactly the same. On the one hand, you've got the above theme covered with SMA, the hard-to-learn yet legendary-secret-styles which only a mighty hero who is already a master could possibly comprehend.** On the other hand, actual Sidereals are mostly going to want to train Terrestrials or promising mortals, or even Exigents. Throne Shadow provided both a means and an incentive to do this, and I can't say that it ruined anything. I wouldn't be unhappy to see Shadow Fingers and related sifu charms return in 3e.

                            Martial Arts kinda suck as a combat option

                            Yeah ):

                            Well, we'll see how 3e addresses this, I suppose!

                            Sifu

                            ...is a bad background and should feel bad. Why on earth couldn't this effect be baked into Allies, or maybe some sort of other Mentor thing? If I have the trust of another Sidereal such that they are prepared to teach me their most powerful secret techniques, why wouldn't they be willing to lend me some cash or help guard my house or whatever?

                            The answer, obviously, is that Sifu is supposed to invoke the aforesaid 'wise sages who don't leave their monasteries', but Sidereals are also political and practical creatures, with much more complex alliances than that. The background should never have existed.

                            Maybe Sidereals are TOO MA-centric?

                            Morangias brings up an excellent point; every Sidereal character on earth must focus on the progression of their martial arts styles before all else. This is the greatest tragedy of the entire Sidereals book, because it contains the following trees:

                            -Sidereal Thrown, AKA 'Unstoppable storm of deadly shadow-blades'
                            -Sidereal Melee, AKA 'Blind swordsman'
                            -Sidereal Archery, AKA 'Yes, I am out of arrows. But I am not out of lingerie.'

                            And Exalted Modern, of course, has-

                            -Sidereal Firearms, AKA 'He's firing his finger-gun at random into the air and it is killing everyone.'

                            And aside from Melee, those might as well just not exist because most of your combat charms have to be Martial Arts.

                            I have pretty high hopes that 3e will address this problem, hopefully either by creating synergy between native Sidereal trees and martial arts or by incentivizing the purchase of those trees in some other fashion. If that were to happen, I think it's entirely possible that the Sidereals 3e book would actually bring eternal ultimate joy to the world.






                            *NOTE: You can totally play Sidereal Ms. Frizzle and you goddamn should.
                            **Not that he would want to under the current paradigm. 'Oh look I spent double exp to learn a charm which costs me 20m 2wp! Sure am glad I didn't waste that on two Solar Melee charms or something!' I think this is a separate issue which doesn't need to be addressed here, though.

                                I have actually played both of my long-term Sidereals without emphasis on their martial arts, because I love their native Charms so much. (Also, I kind of hate the way Martial Arts became das überability back in 1E sometime and usually refuse to build characters around it on principle -- Throne Shadow was such a perfect character fit that I actually did take it for my greensid, though.) I completely agree with Mercury and Morangias: I do think Sidereals are too MA-centric, and could do with a step back towards "esoteric wisdom in general."

                                The problem, in addition to those already stated, is progression in longer-term games (which just so happen to be the games I tend to play, so I have a bias.) Over time, Sidereals have two choices -- either take Martial Arts, or grow sideways to master all the different competencies available to them (and then take Martial Arts when they run out). The former deemphasizes the wonderful Sidereal Charmset and thus is suboptimal. The latter, though, becomes the widely-reviled 1E Lunar Problem, particularly in a Sidereal-centric game: the only thing differentiating two Sidereals in battle is their weapon martial art choice. Also suboptimal.

                                The suggestion of greater synergy is a good one, but implemented alone it runs straight into choice two, as above. That problem can be delayed but not solved entirely by writing many more Sidereal Charms, as I've more or less come to expect given the size of the other new Charmsets we know about. For me, it comes down to this: Sidereals need a way to grow vertically, in and out of combat, without being locked to MA or Occult as the only options. Personally, I'm hoping Astrology can bear that weight in 3E, or that John and Holden will wow us with something we've never seen and couldn't have expected. Given 3E as advertised, I'm cautiously hopeful for either option.
