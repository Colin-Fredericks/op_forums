            Thinking about some descriptions of the powers of the Sword Empress, I think that the capabilities for Raksha nobles can end up being fairly diverse, but whatever they do, there's a shaping element to it. They don't merely attack, they throw strikes that blight and wither (or they make themselves superior dancing warriors through mystical self-examination). They don't train armies, they conjure them up. They don't build natural things, they craft fantastical materials via poetry and ritual. It's even in the manner in which they take influences from others, and probably how they influence in turn.

            I propose that tends to meld qualities together, often overlaying the direct with the abstract and artistic.

                            I had a sequence of occurrences that led me to imagining Raksi descending from her towers to personally examine and deal with a phenomenon of fruit contaminated by the Wyld in a manner meaning that, when you open it up, it had something weird and inedible inside of it. Maybe the discovery of that area that mutates things to have qualities of yellow stone, I don't know.

                            Apart from reminding me of how I need to actually sit down to watch Annihilation, it put me in mind of the depicted Raksha nobles as conveying a kind of personification of this state of the Wyld; that for them, being that kind of weird amalgamation is their natural state, and something that they thrive in.

                            Well, it might be said of a lot of things native to the Wyld, so the nobility might be distinguished by ones who particularly represent it with something regal and edifying, an air of sophistication.

                            And now that I'm writing all of this, I'm thinking of some other things to draw from it; that where nobles have powers to mutate others, rather than being free form, it has the form of shaping in a manner to share their aesthetics (even when those are in a more spiritual or psychological manner); really, being like living, walking Wyld zones with intent (which might end up being an accurate description of them). Or the idea that the process for Raksha growth (not just within nobility, but possibly the development from commoner to noble if that's still a thing) consists of a process of increasingly elaborating on those amalgamations, internalizing things that they encounter and react to (especially when they resonate with and can connect to already existing traits) to create ever more range and complexity in power, and more sophisticated purpose.

                            The power to extract oaths might be seen in terms of Raksha interactions consisting of a dedicated process of directing those amalgamations, directly internalising and personifying roles of liege and vassal.

                            A lot of that does end up being in line with prior depictions of the Raksha, but personally I find conceptualising it in terms of an ongoing process of characteristically Wyld mutation to make it a bit more visceral and compelling, and to help make more sense of what their powers might be and how they develop.

                            But I'm also not seeing it as wholly divorced from narrative conceits, rather... externalising that from themselves a bit. I'm being driven by things I read when I took a look back at Masters of Jade recently; the idea of Guild members spreading narratives intended to alter local fae into more easily dealt with forms. In that approach, it makes narrative less of a thing inherent to the Raksha themselves, and more into something of Creation that especially beguiles and can be made to serve them. Narrative both in the sense of storytelling, of devices to convey theme, imagery and an ongoing structure, and in the sense of conveying information in ways that shape perceptions towards particular agendas.

                            In that sense, the reputation of the Fair Folk as expert liars might not be in the sense of effective understanding of what lies are believable to what people, but that their shaping magic can spread into their very words, and their intent to influence; they can effectively relate ideas to their native personal theme without it needing to properly make sense, and at stronger levels their voice ends up effectively shaping your mind towards whatever belief system is in line with their nature. It never quite gets into outright mind control because that's too direct; it at least has to be in the Raksha saying something, even if the thing doesn't particularly make sense.

                            Hmm, now I'm recalling the experience of being in a dream, where you can feel as though you're very conscious of everything that is happening, but take it all at face value; I've had times of feeling very rational inside of the dream about things that were somewhere between quite unlikely to utterly ludicrous, without any sense of contradiction or recognition. I'm now finding that would be a compelling basis for depicting the experience of being deceived and manipulated by a more powerful Fair Folk noble.

                            Heh, this is one of those posts that started off shorter, and kept growing as putting the words down got me thinking of more and more implications.

                                I always liked the idea that Raksha are kind of fascinated and horrified by how doggedly humans pursue safety and comfort.

                                Like Raksha can approximate being desirous of something. It's a solid motivation,but it's one of many. You COULD be a boundless hedonist, but you could just as easily be a grim ascetic or vengeance-driven determinator.

                                So when they look into Creation and see that most people pursue and would prefer to be in stories of luxury and power? It's weird. Like why would you retire to be with your family when you could be a scorned beggar on the street?

                                I think some Raksha would interpret this tendency from humans to be a form of gluttony. 'They don't want to see the full gamut of experience! All they want is to eat and live and be... happy?'. With that in mind, I interpret the flashier, more luxurious assumptions Raksha take on to be their best impression of human nature.

                                Like "This is what you want, right? All the money you could ever need and everyone loves you.".

                                This also goes some way to explaining why Raksha further into creation tend towards this kind of Bon Vivant expression while the ones further out are often more alien and esoteric.

                                TL: DRIthink Raksha that go among humans take on celebratory and luxurious forms because they don't understand what makes humans seek out security and comfort.

                                    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                                    Clothing yourself in the Five Elements is pretty iconic to earliest and rawest iteration of the Fair Folk in Exalted.
                                    Clothing yourself in the primary element of your environment, but not just any element.

                                    Raksha aren't going to wear the earth unless they're based somewhere underground playing at being dwarves and cave trolls. It would definitely be interesting if a raksha has to establish a fairy castle underground, which acts sort of like a manse from which he can draw Earth Essence that allows him to use Earth elemental powers. Or maybe they do, in fact, need a manse or demense.

                                    Requiring this sort of connection to an environment lends itself to forest-dwelling fairies becoming Forest Elves, and desert-dwelling fairies becoming Fire Elves. It ties them to a location, and from that restriction, they gain power.

                                    So when you're dealing with an elemental raksha, you take caution. But you also know they have a vulnerability you can attack to deprive them of that power. You know they have a treasure you can steal from them for your own power - a demense or manse.

                                    Previous Editions already made raksha keenly interested in taking demesnes or manses to build their homes, so why not expand on that?


                                    It also makes it meaningful to a raksha's story when they exchange a body of fire for a body of water. Because perhaps they were driven away from their fire-aspected demense and they had to flee to a water-aspected demesne before they returned for revenge?
