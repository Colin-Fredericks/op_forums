        The Realm uses a numbering system akin to the Ptolemaic dynasty, denoting their decent to their house founder. Mnemon, therefore, would be Mnemon I, Ragara Bhagwei would be Ragara II Bhagwei, and so on.

        This gets more complicated when you add in households, which do the same thing! Say one of Cynis' great-granddaughters named Haru formed a household. She would be Cynis IV Haru I. And her poor grandson Usuke ends up as Cynis VI Haru III Usuke.

                                We have tropes like Action Girl, Femme Fatalle and the Brains because we like seeing those characters again and again.

                                The Lover is a bit of an odd one out in that regard, since her position in the setting necessitates she be more interesting than 'another Femme Fatalle like any other'....

                                ... Lemme take a stab at it.

                                ---

                                The Lover is most famous for her own greatest indulgence (Lust) but is a purveyor of all forms of sin. She has proud men showered with praise that never satisfies, drives the gluttonous into eternal starvation, keeps the envious from ever stepping out of the shadow of whoever they envy, etc etc. She's also a well-versed diplomat, and gets what she wants through trade and talk for the most part. She manages to keep her influence hidden and secretive, which is one reason why she seems to have accomplished so little compared to the other Deathlords - she HAS accomplished much, it's just that very little of it has her fingerprints on it. Trade a vase here, and the warlord who it was stolen from burns the village down within a month. And other such things.

                                The insidious thing about her is that she only needs to convince you to indulge yourself once, and you'll never be able to stop. It's the major reason why the Silver Prince is said to fear her, aside from the fact that he knows how far her influence spreads - against anyone else, he has a way out. One slip-up with the Lover, though, and he's a slave forever.

                                The Lover is the slippery slope given form. Even start on the dark path, and it will consume you as surely as if you threw yourself in head first.

                                                            The Celestial Incarnae are quite literally the sun, moon and stars...and nothing else. They can, with effort, create "avatar" bodies to interact with gods, mortals and exalts with, but it's clear that they are not really *there* as their thoughts are not easily translated. If one travels to their celestial bodies and has permission (or powerful magic), then one can "go inside" them, for they are also essentially gigantic, sentient Sanctums. Here, they can speak directly to their visitors, either through avatars or manifesting voices, but they prefer to use Hosts, beings that they have brought within themselves for just this purpose (as well as to maintain and lead the communities that exist in some of them).

                                                            The Games of Divinity exist, but are only played by the Incarnae, and that term is merely a lesser mind's attempt to encapsulate the experience. In reality, the "Games" are an unimaginably immense and complex interaction of extended Essence entanglement, which is part game, part orgy, part debate, as well as other experiences most beings have no context for. Gods and mortals can "tune into" the Games with the help of the Incarnae, but gods find the experience addictive and mortals find it instantly fatal. No exalt has yet done so, but would probably take it as gods do.

                                                            During those times when the moon fades away, she literally leaves Creation, slowly phasing out into the Wyld, where she entertains herself by hunting the Raksha. The stars are constant, but no one knows where they go during Calibration, and the Maidens have never told. When the sun sinks below the horizon, he rises over the western edge of Creation's bottom, and during the course of Creation's night, his shining face melts the ocean that is frozen there every night. In the center of this ocean sits a massive island, which is actually the base of the Imperial Mountain extending from the bottom of the world...
