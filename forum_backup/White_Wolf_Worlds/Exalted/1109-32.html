                    I really, really hope that Lunars have other options that are just as good as DBT. I tried playing Lunars a few different ways, and they were always... kinda lacking. I remember getting knocked around quite a bit by enemies that the rest of the Circle took out relatively easily. The day I took DBT, that switched, and I was able to take out enemies the team was struggling with. DBT just upped my abilities so much that the rest of my combat strategy went from underpowered to overpowered. I don't want a whole bunch of Lunar strategies that are basically good strategies, but when you add DBT, its better. Forcing Lunars to not use Evocations while in DBT seems weak, but I would prefer it to a situation where having an Evocation based combat strategy is simply made better by having DBT.

                    I do not want a situation where everything a Lunar does is made better by DBT. If that happens, everything Lunars do will have to be balanced around the fact that the Lunar will be in DBT whenever there is no overriding need to NOT be in DBT.

                    I am totally ok with DBT. I love DBT. I just don't want every Lunar ever to be DBT all the time.

                                                I'm really interested in seeing how the No Moons are handled.

                                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                        If you ask them to leave should their answer be unsatisfying to you, possibly.
                                                        I've never yet asked a player to leave a game, for that or any reason.

                                                        Generally speaking, when you ask a player something during chargen, you're not saying, "Answer the way I like or you're out of the game." The worst that would happen in the case of mammoth Lunar player, if he had no explanation of why his character had Dexterity 5 and Strength 3 other than "god stat", is that I might ask him to choose a different totem animal, one that actually had a high Dexterity score and would therefore seem more appropriate for him.

                                                        The reason is because the whole point of the spirit shape is that it's the animal that best reflects your nature. It's the animal you. It's not some other thing you change into. It's your true form. Is nature is your nature, its instincts are your instincts. You can't separate them. And just as your spirit shape is an animal repreesentation of you, so you, in human form, are a human representation of your spirit shape, because the nature of both is the same.

                                                        It seems the consensus on this thread is that your totem animal only reflects your nature as far as your personality is concerned - but I've never read it that way, because you can't really separate, say, the instincts of a fox, with the Dexterity and Perception a fox relies on to hunt. Physical and social attributes are a large part of an animal's nature, and if your spirit shape reflects your nature then it should reflect your attributes too.

                                                        And in my experience, it's always more enjoyable in a Lunar game the more in common you have with your spirit shape, because you get to play on that in a variety of ways. If the fox Lunar is the one who, even in human form, has the best hearing, the one who always notices a trap, the one with the quickest reflexes, well, it makes you feel like you're actually playing a fox Lunar, someone whose spirit shape actually fits. And it makes the character feel more like it actually works, and the game experience is more enjoyable. Or at least that's how it's always been for me.

                                                            I realize this was a few pages back, but eh, I'll chime in anyway:

                                                            Originally posted by TSk View Post
                                                            No, because by definition, creating a rule saying X cannot be done in Y form removes as many options as it adds. It gives the option of using X without Y; but it removes the option of using X with Y. There is no net gain in options. And from the individual character's perspective, his functionality has decreased. Previously he could do two things at once; now he can only do one at a time. That's nothing but a decrease to his flexibility.
                                                            There's a different way to look at it than that. I mean, yes, entering form Y can prevent the use of X, but it's also kinda objectively true of just about any kind of shapeshifting into distinct forms. It's also an incomplete statement. One also generally chooses to enter form Y in order to improve (or quite often allow) the use of Z.

                                                            Like, say a Lunar turns into his spirit shape, a snake. There are some clear disadvantages, there, the lack of limbs topping the list. But that's also not quite the point. If the Lunar's changing into it, he likely has a purpose in mind for which a snake would be well-suited, such as employing its sense of smell, slithering through smaller spaces than his human form could manage, employing a venomous bite, and possibly keeping someone constricted. The form has its pluses and its minuses, so it's the Lunar's job to apply it when its pluses are of greatest use and he's decided its minuses are outweighed by the benefits or he'll be changing to a different shape such that they're non-issues. To one degree or another, this is true of all forms. Picking the right form for the right job is honestly what's struck me as a lot of the fun of such a shapeshifter. If that playstyle is part of the design goal, I fully approve of decisions that better enable the fun in it.

                                                            So, looking at DBT as another form, it stands to reason it, too, would have some minuses to go with its pluses. In EX3, one of those minuses is going to be a lack of Evocation access. It might also help to consider that it may be getting more than just a new minus. Personally, I have a hunch DBT is going to be even more powerful than it's been before. Aquillion had a very good point about one way that might be: in a land without common persistent dice-adders, the physical* Attribute-enhanced DBT-user is all the mightier. But it might extend beyond that. If Evocations are the big deal that they're meant to be and DBT is meant to be balanced against the lack of them, perhaps it could go even further than buffing up Attributes? I could see Charm trees building off of it,** developing powerful effects usable only while in DBT based off the unique synergy of human and beast it allows, much as Evocation arrays draw on the relationship of a wielder and her Artifact.

                                                            From that perspective, a Lunar wouldn't be able to use Evocations while shifted into DBT, but would have access to something that might be seen as analogous, while still being able to shift back to human form if circumstances change and an Artifact's Evocations are needed. In this way, a Lunar swordsmaster with Evocations unlocked in a daiklave and great proficiency in DBT would be much like a Solar swordsmaster with Evocations unlocked in two daiklaves: neither can use both at once, but can gain clear benefits from being able to switch from one to the other as circumstances demand. It'd also be reasonable that such a hypothetical DBT-rooted Charm tree might be able to go places that Evocations wouldn't.

                                                            * Sidebar: I wonder if it will still be restricted to Strength, Dexterity and Stamina next time. Perhaps it might be "pick whichever 3 (or whatever) to boost and justify how;" e.g. reaction time may quicken (Wits), as senses heighten (Perception) and the Lunar becomes all the more visually striking (Appearance). Even if the form is generally meant for combat, if there are numerous capabilities across the Lunar Charmset that would be great for battle and would benefit from heightened Attributes, I could see something like this working out. Eh, just musing.

                                                            ** Fair warning: anyone who says something like, "It's a Knack" will make me laugh at them. We don't know if Knacks will even be a thing in EX3, much less if DBT will be among their number.
