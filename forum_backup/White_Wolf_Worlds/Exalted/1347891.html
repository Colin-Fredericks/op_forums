    When Should NPCs Launch Decisives?

    11-12-2019, 10:38 AM
    Basically I was trying to think at what initiative should the antagonists be trying to fire off decisive attacks? If they’re just trying to deal lasting damage than almost any time they’re over the enemy hardness is good. They might die faster but they’ll take a chunk with them. Should they wait until there’s a t least a reasonable chance that they’ll knock out a player, even if that chance is never likely to happen? Do animals that don’t realize Exalted may be more hardy fight as though 7 health levels will be a killshot?

    When I’ve ST’d I’ve almost exclusively used the strategy that enemies don’t launch decisive attacks unless they have a pretty good chance of taking a foe out of the fight, but maybe that’s not the best way. There are even a few enemies that seem like they might really want to open up the start of the fight with a decisive attack. Is that unfair to the players because I have an endless supply of enemies and they don’t have an endless supply of health levels, or is it intentional because it’s ultimately not that deadly but does add to the drama as intense and dangerous places build up some temporary battle damage?

          I think NPCs using Decisives on turn 1 is part of the game and comes with ups alongside the downs. In some cases it will hurt, but in other cases a pouncing tiger might waste their whole ini pool against a PC with hardness before they realize their mistake.

              I don't think there's a clear answer to this other than "what feels right for you." I've had several combat situations in recent story arcs where the PCs have been in very good positions and often kept the bad guys at low initiative enough for the bad guys to only rarely get a chance to do a decisive strike before they'll be locked down at low initiative or in crash. When I know that a fight is looking like it'll turn into that, I may want to do a decisive strike at lower initiative to put some pressure on the players. In a different situation when the fight looks worse for the PCs and they're the ones running low, I may want to crash one of them and have that big potential one shot kill instead of grazing blows mainly for the dramatic impact - to make the big bad look bigger and badder. I rarely put my players in situations that look like it'd TPK them, though, never actually having perma-killed a PC in a fight in 3E yet (though I've perma-crippled some of them).

                  I think you are taking the the problem "upside down" (I don't know if that makes sense in this sentence I just watched Stranger Things and it seems to make sense).

                  To me, Initiative is a strategical game for the player, and a representation of what's happening in the fight for the characters.

                  When a character really damages another, it means the player launched a decisive attack. When he almost really damages another, it means it was a withering. For the characters, in both cases he was trying to damage his opponent. This is what's strictly written in the books.

                  Now, my personal interpretation of this system is the following : For the players, Initiative is here to provide an actual "mini-game" to interact with, with strategical decisions and stuff, in order to have fun and abstract the fight. So if you consider your NPC doing a decisive attack is a good strategical play from the player or ST perspective, you should do it. The character doesn't realize he will expose himself after a decisive attack because he will only have 3 initiative left, neither does he realize than when he throws a 5 initiative decisive attack on someone with 10 hardness he will not damage him, it's the player who knows this information. From the perspective of the character, it's just the heat and surprise of the fight.

                  It doesn't mean the character must do stupid actions because he has no meta-knowledge. For example, a character doesn't have to necessarily use a decisive attack when he has enough initiative if it should put him in a suicidal situation. It's the player behind the character that chooses the strategical option he decides to apply, and the initiative system then translates into the game world by incarnating luck, stupidity, wit, courage, passion, or anything else accordingly to the mood of the fight.

                  My conclusion to your exact question is thus : If the narrative objective of the ST in a fight scene is to have his NPCs weaken the players and then die, he should follow this strategy for his NPC. It doesn't mean his NPC are suicidal, because from their perspective they are just fighting an enemy and putting all they have on the line. Only if in the context of the fight they are given a good reason to stop or change their plans (for example, they are terribly hurt, or they realize they become overwhelmed with malus because they are blinded and mauled, or the players do a well argumented social roll that uses the Social System effectively), they should maybe question changing plans, in accordance of the psychology the ST has given them. But for as long as the ST can use his optimal strategy, his NPCs should be the vector to apply it.

                  Edit : Of course, the strategic choices of the Storyteller should be informed by what the characters have a chance to know. If her NPC never met the characters, he shouldn't know one of them has 10 hidden hardness thanks to a strange power. But if she sees a guy shining in full Orichalcum armor, then the ST can consider he possesses the strategical information that Big Shining Guy has hardness, and thus not use his character to attack him without enough initiative.

                      I'm almost the exact opposite.
                      My NPCs frequently employ decisive attacks against my PCs when they could be building initiative so that I minimise the risk of potential killshots and create windows for another PC to intervine with a Defend Other action if one of them is clearly struggling.

                      On the other side of things, I only expect most NPCs to get lucky once if at all. I don't want to waste that on accumulating initiative that they won't live long enough to use.

                              I think 15i (less for obviously weaker or injured PCs), 10i when the NPC has any kind of Alpha Strike abilities, or when Crashing a PC is a good benchmark.

                              It's not a guarenteed kill against a normal mortal, but it's a decisive advantage, especially pack hunters. It keeps the combat dynamic, passes on concequences. If you roll above average you're still unlikely to kill a PC.

                              The combat system is mechanically transparent. I don't think it's particularly meta to use the salient knowledge the rules specifically impart you with. The meta system knowledge is the mechanical representation of how the characters perceive the flow of combat. You know if a character is Crashed, because in-character they seem on the ropes. You choose whether to launch a Decisive, not because you don't intend on killing your foe when you Wither, but because you choose not to overextend yourself to try to land that killing blow.

                                      I tend to fire off decisive attacks if the NPC has the initiative to do it.
