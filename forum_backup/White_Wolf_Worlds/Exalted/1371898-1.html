            Originally posted by Zale View Post
            Also, suggesting people simply not use charm combos that are intended to be really good just outlines the fact that the combat rules are failing to work properly, not that the players are at fault.
            TAP is about rushing forward in a blur and turning your momentum into a powerful killing blow. It works exactly as intended and is really good.

            That's not a combat rules problem.

            The problem is that some people don't want to awesomely win fights in one move -- they want a dramatic back and forth struggle.

            Exalted lets you have a dramatic back and forth struggle: look at all the Brawl Charms I listed earlier, look at the ENTIRE Dodge and Resistance tree.

            Exalted does not let you have a dramatic back and forth struggle if you build a character with super-speed designed to kill their enemies in one blow. (Unless, you know, you fight something level appropriate like Octavian.)

            It's like taking Dexterity 5 and complaining your character isn't a bumbling oaf: you can make your character a bumbling oaf if you want, that's not a system problem. You so it by not taking Dexterity 5 -- or even by taking it, but not using it.

            TAP costs Willpower. It's three Charms into the Athletics tree. You have to go out of your way to pick it up, and you're perfectly good at combat without it. It's balanced and thematic, and if you don't like using it then don't use it.

            If you want to house-rule it off your table, then have fun with that. But every play-style that you personally don't like is not a problem with the rules set.

            I like the preternatural swordfighter who leaps forward, slashes once and sheaves her sword as her opponent falls.

            But then I also like the rising drama of back-to-back challenges, the thrill of escaping the Wyld Hunt and Guild assassins, I like fighting groups of powerful enemies whilst outnumbered, I like epic battles against mile high super-enemies. And I like the dramatic fallout of what happens after the battle: the social and emotional concequences.

            If you're spending two full Excellencies and TAP each combat to kill someone in one blow... what's happening next? Because in my games, you are now sitting at 3i with two opponents left to kill, the world now knows you are Anathema, and once you've killed those opponents you've still got a full day of physical, social and mental challenges ahead. Not to mention what happens tomorrow as various characters swear revenge from the shadows.
