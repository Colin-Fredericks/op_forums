                        Originally posted by Rater202
                        You want a Solar PC or NPC that's depressed, okay, but pick something that isn't as idiotic as "I believe myself to be a monster because I glow gold and I'm really good at stuff."                         First off the Immaculate Order doesn't teach that Anathema immediately become monsters. Again, it teaches that they inevitably become monsters over time. It also doesn't teach that they are immediately mind controlled or anything, only that the soul of the Anathema will slowly replace their own over time. So the fact that a Solar doesn't turn into a monster right off the bat in no way contradicts what the Immaculate Order teaches. In fact, it's possible that the first time a Solar enters Limit Break he views that as confirmation of what the Order has taught.

                        Second, it's entirely possible a Solar has memories of committing all sorts of horrific atrocities during the First Age (you know, the sort of things many Solars did?)

                        So if you are told that the Anathema were terrible monsters, and you have memories of yourself performing terrible, monstrous deeds, and you're told that the soul of the dead Anathema tries to usurp control of your body, an sometimes you lose control over your actions and do stuff you normally wouldn't do, why wouldn't you believe what the Immaculate Order taught you? At the worst it's plausible that the Order is right.

                        Again, Exaltation is doesn't immediately give you Out-Of-Character knowledge about the nature of the world. It gives you some hazy flashes, some rough memories and maybe some voices or visions. In no way does it immediately give you insight into how Creation works, or what is a lie or isn't a lie. That's up to each person to choose what do with those visions, and the idea that everyone comes to the exact same decision and nobody is influenced by their beliefs prior to Exaltation just seems kind of wonky of to me.

                                        Originally posted by Rater202
                                        Suicidally depressed Solars are a thing that shouldn't happen at all though, at least not because they were Immaculate-If a solat thinks "better kill myself because I'm an anathema, even though an anathema certainly wouldn't think thoughts like that and I don't feel any compulsion to act the way the immaculate order says I should" then he's either to thoroughly brainwashed by immaculate doctrine to do the sort of thing that would earn him a Solar Exaltation, was already suicidal when they Exalted(not touching it), or they're the kind of idiot incapable of thinking for themselves in which case they probably lack the drive to go out and earn a Solar Exaltation.
                                        Nitpick: you don't "earn" a Celestial Exaltation. It's not something you get by virtue of putting in time and effort. Many thousands of "worthy" people exist for every Celestial Exaltation. Instead, you're given the greatest gift that can ever be given, with no strings attached, because one of the greatest gods in the world felt a connection to you, felt something within you resonate with its infinite glory. And in that moment of connection, it deigned to pass a reflection of its power to you.

                                        You can be a devout Immaculate and have a moment that fundamentally connects with the nature of the Unconquered Sun. I see that one mortal monk who stands up to Deled being in a particularly good moment to receive some kind of blessing for standing up for the weak when it would be easier to keep his mouth shut.

                                        Let's not talk about it as if someone can work real hard at Exalting Celestial and make it happen due to that hard work.

                                        Let's also remember that Intelligence 1 characters are perfectly supported by the rules. And you're hyperbolizing Immaculate doctrine - as has been said, the Immaculates don't tell mortals not to go forth and be awesome, or be ambitious, or work hard, or corner a niche in a field they excel in. They mainly reference a spiritual hierarchy with the DBs on top, and not trying to reach beyond your station by dealing with gods or wielding magical items or thinking that you'd do a better job running the world than the demigods do.

                                        Originally posted by Rater202
                                        You want a Solar PC or NPC that's depressed, okay, but pick something that isn't as idiotic as "I believe myself to be a monster because I glow gold and I'm really good at stuff." Because a newly Exalted Solar isn't compelled to perform monstrous actions, or evil deeds, or anything. If you were body jacked by a demon, you wouldn't think you were still you, you'd be either dead or forced to watch as something else controlled your body and spoke with your mouth. An Anathema, if the Immaculates were right about them, would probably remember being an anathema.
                                        Here's the thing. If you were kind of a middling thief who picked pockets in a marketplace and lives hand-to-mouth and suddenly have the power to sneak in and out of the imperial mint and steal money beyond your wildest dreams, wouldn't you?

                                        If you were a gladiator who did pretty well and now can drop people in 1 punch instead of 10, thus making it less likely that you'd get injured, wouldn't you?

                                        If you're a local trader getting pushed out by the Guild and you can now wheel and deal with the bureaucrats on a level the Guild can't even imagine to give yourself favorable state contracts, wouldn't you?

                                        If you were a lawyer who did ok but not great and suddenly had the power to be more convincing than your greatest rivals and win every case you tried and pick up high-rolling clients in the process, wouldn't you?

                                        If you and your family were constantly under threat of dangerous force from some group, and now had the power to permanently end that threat without the possibility of getting caught, wouldn't you?

                                        These are things that the Immaculates might call "alien desires imposed on you by a demon." They are in fact your own very human desires, your subconscious recognizing what is now possible through the grace bestowed on you and pushing you to take what has been denied you, to punish those who have transgressed you, to sit at the table instead of begging in the street. It's very easy for the Immaculates to cast these as the outside influence of a demon, and it's easy for other mortals to cast judgment on you, because people certainly would view achieving these desires as selfish and unfair and morally reprehensible (even if they would certainly do these things themselves given the power to do so).

                                        Evil is relative. You smash a baker's shop window at night to steal bread to feed your starving family. You may not think that's an evil deed but the baker surely does.

                                        The Idea that someone could still believe the anathema BS after Exalting themselves and realizing it requires far to many leaps of logic for one individual to make for me to take it seriously in anything but a parody situation.
                                        So your neighborhood in a Realm satrapy is being terrorized/shaken down by branch of the local guard that has a Realm patron - these guardsmen ride with him on his hunts, or whatever, and his patronage gives them general impunity to local law. The Realm doesn't care that this is happening at all, because it's not affecting the tax flow. This has happened for years and you have made the payments from your meager income, and still your family and friends get hassled for more. You now have the power to kill all of those gang members at night, without any real chance of being identified or caught. You do it; your neighborhood is a safer place, but you've committed multiple murders to do so.

                                        Can you not see where a person might believe this power has changed them? That they're no longer the person they thought they were? That their family is safer, but at what cost to their soul? Or their perceived morality?

                                        These are tough questions that deserve to be explored in the context of gaining superhuman levels of skillful mastery. There's nothing gained by sweeping them under the rug.

                                                            As for 'not knowing you are possessed'...

                                                            There is a folk story about meeting a demon in the crossroads. A version I heard was, there was a criminal who was so strong and confident, that he bragged he was not afraid of anything. And so, when he heard that you meet the devil in a certain crossroad at midnight, he told everyone he would wait there, for the devil to show up. Which he did, and the devil never did show up. So, he came out, bragging everyone else is a coward, and only he had the guts to do it... and, since the devil is a lie, there's nothing to be afraid of, no punishment waiting for you. He became more and more cruel, utterly vile... showing that he did meet the devil in the crossroads. And the way of the devil was to not appear.

                                                            Same thing here? To know all the tenets of religion are a lie, to be given carte blanche to act in the world... that can be seen as in and of itself damning.

                                                            I imagine many an Immaculate-Born Solar rode the rush of power, defeating the local Satrap or Dragon-Blooded, and only sunk in to them they might truly be anathema when they parade the corpse to people, expecting adoration, and see only fear in their eyes instead. Or the twilight that gets told to look around to see that he is anathema, when he is surrounded by demons and sat on a throne of singing flesh.

                                                            Such moments are far too resonant to want to remove them from the game entirely.

                                                            Hell, it is against the very nature of the game; it doesn't tell the player what is right and what is wrong, who are the good guys and the bad guys, why would the character have more certainty?
