                                        1. Lets you move out to another range band.
                                        2. Gives you a reflexive move if pursued by someone who failed to contest your roll.

                                        As long as you're at close range to an enemy, they have to disengage to move out to short range from you, and you get a roll to contest them. How exactly you do that can vary - most foot soldiers and bandits aren't the Flash.

                                                            Originally posted by TGUEIROS View Post

                                                            I'm saying in relation to other markers on the battle. You are in Close Range to your opponent and in long range in relation to a bridge. Can you drift towards that bridge and eventually reach it if your opponent that is contesting and winning the disengage doesn't want to?
                                                            If you're at close range to a guy and long range to a bridge, there is no way for you to advance towards medium range with the bridge without moving out of close range with the guy. You gotta disengage.

                                                            Can you actually reach the bridge without disengaging, if you don't care that your opponent is on you?
                                                            Generally, no.

                                                            If the entire context of a fight is you and another guy duking it out as both of you run towards the bridge, the Storyteller might ad hoc it otherwise, declaring that, in effect, the bridge moves a range band closer towards each of you on each round without requiring any additional movement action on your part. This is a case-by-case assessment based on what works best for a specific scene, not something that you can extrapolate a universal rule from.
