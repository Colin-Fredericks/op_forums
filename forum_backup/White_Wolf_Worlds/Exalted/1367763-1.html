            I'm in the 'fails on modern ethics grounds' side of it. Demon summoning where you magically bind them to loyalty, or force them to loyalty through a contest of wills, breaks any non-absurd post-abolitionist worldview of ethical behavior.

            Demon *thaumaturgy* where you just yank them across Cecelyne and then ask them nicely to do things, which they may refuse, no mind-bending involved... is still kidnapping, but is less-bad.

            However, I like to abandon modern ethics at my table, and look at things from the in-universe angle. It helps me storytell the world.

            Exalted is permeated with ethical codes where slavery is often defended and practiced, where all men are not created equal, where the divine right of kings still reigns, where there is a hierarchy of life - even within species, etc. In this time of great (as in terrible) heroes, the slavery of demons is the price of their war reparations. The powers that won the world, and thus those who set the rules, have deemed it just that they may be so ensorcelled, and so it is seen as just. It is a predicate set to bring out the character of the heroes that the players embody - to find the bottom of their character, we give them power (to paraphrase Lincoln).

            Will they choose a civil liberties worldview, that even my worst enemies deserve freedom? Will they literally 'demonize' demons, strip them of humanity, call them the other, and justify their slavery? It is for the player to decide, and for the table to judge them for it.

                    That begs the question - if you are the demon's jailor, are you responsible for its actions if it goes free?

                    I think the core of the question is who is responsible for demon behavior. Does the demon have agency, and thus can take responsibility for its own actions? Then it's the demon's fault. Does the demon have a nature that will override its agency? Then the demon is faultless, it is the demon's master or jailor or bondage-breaker who is to blame.

                    Which brings me back to either sharing the definition of humanity with demons (in which case they deserve freedom) or denying them human rights because they are not human (in which case it depends on their nature what it is ethically permissible to allow them, which might justify their indefinite imprisonment.)
