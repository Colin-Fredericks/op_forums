    Just a couple of quick notes:

    1) Racism

    I am attempting to quasi-accurately portray at least some of the real-life racism and discrimination that most real-world groups felt for each other. Like, picture the English and the Welsh/Irish/Scottish. They hated each other, considered each other downright barbaric and less-than-human at times ...... and, really often, intermarried and interbred with each other more often than not. Such as it is/was in my Hundred Kingdoms campaign. On a macro level, the Lowlanders and the Hill Tribes didn't really like each other, but on a micro level they mostly got on alright.

    Coincidentally, I am basing the Hill Tribes off of not only various Asian tribal groups (mainly the Montagnards/Degar and the Ainu) but also various Celtic groups, mostly the Welsh.

    Also, it goes without mention that many of the Lowlander clans and villages detest each other as well. "Don't trust those shifty bastards from that village over there, they are all thieves, the lot of them!"

    However, if you guys feel like I am stepping on the racism-pedal a wee bit too hard, then I could certainly tone it down. I thought of two main methods through which Kasai could interact with the general population; how he interacts due to racism, and how he interacts due to class differences. I, personally, thought "knight-expie vs peasants" was a little bland.

    2) Kasai and gettin' some lovin'.

    Kasai is interested in women, but it is ..... a wee bit complicated. On one hand, he is Oathsworn, a member of the nobility, and any woman of common birth he is with will be treated little different than a concubine. If he weds a commoner, he should expect to hear a lot of tittering from various nobles, disparaging of her virtues, and if he were to feel enough for the woman to wed her, he wouldn't want to put her through that. Not to mention a complete ass- ton of duels to defend her honor. Of course, if he felt strongly enough about the issue, he could also not really give a shit about gossip, much like he already does.

    On the other hand, as a Hill Tribesman-descendant, he is unlikely to actually receive any marriage-offers from Lowlander nobility. Ancestry nonwithstanding, his family is poor and not well known, both strikes against him. It would take a lot (say: becoming the retainer of a powerful Lord, or becoming known as a hero of various sorts, etc) for him to receive some offers.

    His best chances of being offered an actual "noble" marriage would be from one of the Hill Tribes, which wouldn't do much for his social standing among the Lowlanders. They have their own "nobility" (think ulchelwyr http://homepages.rootsweb.ancestry.c...es/tribes.html) that Kasai could marry into if he catches someone's eye.

            Bring her aboard it is, with forewarning about lack of pay and equipment.

            I would, however, like to mention that Kasai isn't poor, or, really, dirt poor. He has some money, although nothing worth noting using the Resources system, and nothing approaching the levels needed to support a number of followers, nor equip them with shiny new arms and armor. For example: Saiko got her glaive from the dead Oathsworn bandit, not via Kasai buying it for her.

            In my Hundred Kingdoms campaign, I ruled there was a monetary system "underneath" the Guild-backed silver system, using copper and bronze coinage. 1 silver Dinar can be broken into 8 bits, and each bit was worth something like 4 or so bronze coins, and each bronze coin was worth around 10 or so copper coins. Nobility would use silver, and townsfolk would use bronze/copper. Peasants would use the turnip : P. One Dinar was worth the approximate agricultural/industrial output of a single household for a month (although many peasant familes have holdings worth far less), for reference. Not that the peasants would see the actual money, of course. Instead, they would pay their taxes to the Lord in food or goods (cloth, pottery, raw materials, etc), who would then take (or have some lackey move, more likely), the produce to towns where it could be exchanged for actual money.

            So, right now Kasai has a relative bunch of copper and bronze coins a-jangling in his purse, which is enough to support him and Xuansu under moderate means, so long as they stay to the cheap side of town and eat the Creation-equivalent of "street-meat" or fast food. Adding Saiko to the mix strained his ability to stretch funds.

            Arms and armor are expensive. Kasai's armor and weaponry are worth more than Saiko's entire village several times over, and while his father held rather poor lands, noble .families bankrupting themselves to equip warrior children (or provide dowries) isn't uncommon. Which is the major reason said children (and Kasai) leave to find their own fortunes, and try to become retainers to Lords.

            It is also expensive for said Lords to keep said retainers. They laze about holdings all day, eating your food and drinking your good wine, and they need equipment and suchlike on top of that....... which is why most Lords will give most retainers the effective "boot" and subinfeudate their own lands. If a retainer owns land, you don't have to pay for their upkeep.

            Hence, feudalism. The need for more and more land is what initially caused the Lowland peoples to drive the Hill-Tribes from the valleys, and is what causes the constant back-and-forth wars between the domains of the Hundred Kingdoms.

            On Money:

            I don’t particularly like how the EX3 handbook describes money and Resources, and so I basically just use the D&D system in a fashion for actual prices, modified according to real-world examples, with the Silver Dinar replacing the gp.

            So, a dagger (not a utility knife, but a two-edged fighting knife made of differentially hardened and tempered steel. There is a significant difference between the two, in cost and manufacture at least) would cost around 2 Dinars. Same with a Spear. A straight sword would be between 75 dinars (for a shitty sword) and 150 dinars (for a nice one). A chopping backsword, like a dao or falchion, would be about 50 dinars. Kasai’s mail cost about 150 dinars, and his bow cost 100 dinars. Xuansu cost 250 dinars.

            Now, for some comparisons: you could trade (at wholesale) a bushel (56 lbs) of dried corn for about ¼ of a dinar, or a bushel (60 lbs) of wheat for about ¾ of a dinar. A sheep or goat would go for a single dinar, while a pig and a cow would go for about 3 dinars and 10 dinars, respectively. A pound of workable iron ingots would go for about ½ a dinar, with most ingots being 5 lbs. A bolt of wool cloth measuring 4 square yards would go for 2 dinars a bolt, linen for about 4 dinars, hemp about 1, and silk and velvet between 20 and 100.

            Most peasant farmers and unskilled urban laborers will make, on average, about 1 dinar a month on their labor and products. Most of that, in turn, will go towards rent, food costs, taxes, and other necessities, with the peasant/laborer making only around a dinar a year after that. Skilled urban laborers (like the journeymen seen in the forge), as well as more well-off farmers, the class that Men-At-Arms are usually drawn from, make on average around 2 Dinars a year, after expenses. Landed Oathsworn usually make about 5 dinar a year, off their peasants labor and production..                                     Now, in order to differentiate between the two, one must first understand the difference between a “peasant” and a “serf”. The major difference between them is land ownership. Peasants own their own land, usually given to them by a Lord, while Serfs rent land from their overlord. While both are “commoners”, Peasants tend to be wealthier (due to generally owning more and better farmland), better fed (so, bigger and stronger), more respected (peasants usually are offered positions of management and responsibility by Lords), and sometimes have more free time (since they are wealthier and own more land, they can generally afford a farmhand/servant or two, leading to them needing to do less work personally), leading to more time available for practicing at arms. And, since they tend to have more disposable income, they can actually afford arms and armor.

                                    Most Men-at-Arms will be equipped with a spear and shield, a halberd, or a longbow, plus a backup weapon and a quilted jacket and helmet for protection. The spear/shield and halberd is self-explanatory, but the longbow, itself a holdover from the Hill-Tribes (for whom it was, and still is, a favorite weapon), has long been a favorite of both Men-at-Arms and their Lords, to the point where the longbow is seen as a symbol for the Peasantry, much like the sword is the symbol of the Oathsworn.

                                    Much of the land in the Hundred Kingdoms is forested, hills, or forested hills. That means hunting is a common pastime, both for nobility and the peasants that own land. This leads to skill with the bow, but some Lords have taken it even farther, mandating that farmers that own specific values of land have to be proficient with certain weapons. Below a certain amount; Spear and shield. Above a certain amount; Spear, shield and longbow. Even the serfs are not exempt from this, and they have to be proficient with spear and crossbow, in case of invasion.

                                    Serfs, on the other hand, usually don’t have the money to buy weapons, and their Lords usually wouldn’t want them owning arms anyways. But, in order to comply with the letter of the law, they have to make sure their serfs are at least familiar with arms, in order to defend themselves and their Lords lands. Hence, they usually stockpile spears and, whenever economically possible, crossbows, in their manors, to rapidly equip serfs in case of invasion.

                                    So, to summarize the three “classes” of warrior in the Hundred Kingdoms.

                                    -Oathsworn; Heavy Infantry or Cavalry (The “hammer”), archers focusing on accuracy and speed.

                                    -Men-at-Arms: Medium Infantry (The “anvil”) or light cavalry (think Hobilars), archers focusing on massed fire and range.

                                    - Serf Levies: Light Infantry (screening, etc), massed direct fire

                                    We haven’t seen any peasants yet, for reference. Both Saiko and Blue-Pines were serfs.
