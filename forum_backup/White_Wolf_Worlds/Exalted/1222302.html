    How to create good antagonists?

    05-26-2018, 05:23 PM
    As a new Storyteller for Exalted, one of the most difficult things is creating antagonists to engage my players. The core book comes with some examples, and I guess there's the monthly examples, but I find it very difficult to gauge what will provide a suitable challenge for my players, without destroying them utterly. Also, I'm very bad at writing charms, and I'd wish I had some guidelines for doing it.

    At the moment, I am i the process of creating some demons to fight my players. That provides an extra challenge, because they also need a strong idea. If it had been a mortal, or maybe a Dragon Blooded or an Abyssal, the book provides me with some guidelines (I would make an Abyssal pretty much like a Solar). But demons need a concept, and they have to be built, pretty much from the ground up. I guess the same goes for spirits and elementals.

    Any tips, tricks or guidelines? What is a good challenge, what are some things you've done to make for interesting fights, and how do you design charms for your antagonists?

      I'll start with stuff on a more universal use. It comes up in D&D type games more, but action economy helps you focus on what you want fights to be like. Each PC fighting a couple of guys can be dangerous unless their offense can't touch the defense of the PCs. And vice versa, a big villain does actions that may hit everyone and might take actions to fix, or the villain just takes 4 turns a round. I'd say figure out a quality or quantity of foes to use, then match up the dice pools to roughly hit half the time for credible threats, lower for mooks, higher for bosses. Take Excellencies as assumed used for those calculations.

      For demons, only do a couple of stock lackies. Even a Blood Ape can be rough, so don't shun the classics, or just steal a good power or two from them or the gods/elementals elsewhere in the book and change the description. Think up anything 2nd Circle or an Advanced first circle as a proper match or boss type.

      Part of the beauty of the Exalted combat is the Initiative system representing so much. If the whole party is Crashing, help remind them before they take the decisive hit, consider how much the baddies really want to give chase, or how well the PCs may escape. You as the ST don't have to make decisive attacks until you want to. Make one too soon at a decent initiative PC so the demon misses, then let the PC respond and take initiative. Or it hits but doesn't boost the damage, so only a little damage is made, and it resets to a vulnerable 3 base for a riposte.

          Specifically with 2nd Circle Demons, the easiest spin I found was to give them a thematically-appropriate Martial Art, fill the whole tree, and either let them have Mastery despite not being Solar, or give them unique, boss-fight-esque Charms that complement the rest of it, like AoE attacks, Defense manipulation, etc. For the more sorcerously-inclined, the Nephwrack in the Corebook does a great job exemplifying the type of thing a "boss fight" caster needs, namely crowd control and ways to generate sorcerous motes for dramatic effect as the fight goes on. Same goes for Ahlat for the more melee-inclined. If you have the spare time, run a simulated fight against your party's character pages so you can assess how appropriate the power level is. Believe me, it helps!

          From a game design's perspective, what you can do is look up a random built-in boss fight - say, Ahlat - and make sure you understand why every combat-relevant Charm is there. What purpose does it serve? Why did whoever write this think it's going to come up enough to be worth spending 90 precious, precious words on. When you write your own, ask yourself if that sort of mechanic you'd want your players to experience. Knowing what kind of Charm you need, "filling holes" you know for a fact are there, tends to be a more grounded task than staring at a blank page until an idea pops out. Using the Ahlat example above, Auroch King’s Wrath is very clearly a Turns Red mechanic; the turning point in the fight when he realizes these damn kids're tougher than he thought, and he might actually lose this one. This is also known as "Phase 2", or the part where he is no longer messin' around.

          For writing and character design: No one is the villain of their own story. Even the Deathlords and Neverborn can be sympathetic to some extent. A cool but flat boss fight is perfectly fine at times, but if your antagonist is integral to the plot, they ought to have a clear-cut motivation for what they do. The players don't have to agree with their ideals - and certainly not their methods - but just the fact that they're more than Generic Doomsday Villains makes them memorable, and, heck, could even prompt your players to try something other than charging blindly into a fight. This is easier with human villains, obviously, but Gods and Demons too have loved ones, have suffered loss, may desire revenge for past slights, or aspire to be more than they are.

          Edit: Much like other forms of writing, examining other people's work can only help. If you get a single idea off an entire blurb, it was already worth the read. I also recommend reading Tvtropes' more mechanical and thematic Boss Battle tropes, because even something like making the fight take place while falling from a Skyship might be enough to push it from "neat" to "awesome".

                  Originally posted by EliasHelfer View Post
                  Any tips, tricks or guidelines? What is a good challenge, what are some things you've done to make for interesting fights, and how do you design charms for your antagonists?
                  I'll echo the other statements in this thread, and continue onto something else that I think needs to be stated.

                  Interesting fights.

                  If anyone straight up fights an entire group of Solars, they deserve all the pain coming to them.
                  But, with that out of the way.

                  If you have an antagonist working against your PCs, first thing to consider is the point of the action scene. Make it about more than just fighting, multiple moving objectives and reasons to engage the entire team in multiple ways.
                  Make them take hard choices, smash them in the Intimacies and always consider if the point of the scene is something else than a straight up slugfest.

                  Throw battle groups at them. Not to kill them, but to threaten what they hold dear.
                  If you want to put them at a disadvantage, have them fight in a Demesne, allowing your Villain to regain 10m per round, and have the Demesne explode when you are done with the confrontation.

                  An Exalt can do so much, just by being around. Let them find their own way out of trouble.

                  -----
                  Crafting Opponents

                  There's something to be said about Exalted as a game. There's so many weird exceptions out there, that we might not have to justify odd abilities and such strangeness. It's a big Creation, so you can just pull in all manners of creatures.
                  Want an elemental? Pick your favorite God from mythology, cross it with a Pokemon and voila!

                  When it comes to Demons?
                  Two points.
                  One, they have a purpose. A 1st Circle has been made by a demonic entity for a specific purpose. It might be an alien or incomprehensible purpose, but it is there.
                  Second and Third Circles are fragments of Souls, and can derive from their respective progenitors, having their own purpose for existing.

                  Two.
                  Demons are alien and weird. They are not humans in mind and thought, and dwell in a place that can only be described as nightmarish, where time has little meaning and the entire city is a living, imprisoned titan.

                  But, keeping that in mind, my previous statement still stands.

                      Thank you all for your replies.

                      @Cryo-Seraph: The advice about managing initiative is great - thank you, I'll try to keep that in mind! Also action economy. So far, I've tried to have about one foe per PC, unless they're fighting someone much weaker.

                      Regarding Demons, though, I want some on a very particular theme, so I want to make some on a similar power level to the 1st Circle demons in the book, but some that work differently. That's the difficulty, cause I am not always sure what the balance of the demons is.

                      @Artex: Running simulated battles are a great idea, but also very time consuming. I don't always have time for that. Or energy, for that matter, though I guess doing it a couple of times would help understand the balance a lot better.

                      @Growls: Making the fight about something other than just fighting is good advice. Thanks!


                      If you will indulge me, I'm going to post my next big fight here. I've been messing with this for a while, and I think I've got a good setup, but there might be something I've missed.

                      The setup is this: They've gone off to a faraway place to look for information about this robber baron who lived 500 years in the past. He had several demons serving him, and some of those demons are cropping up again, so they're trying to figure up what's up with him. But he's still there. When an army came to get rid of him 500 years ago, a Sidereal had joined them in order to make sure his spirit did not escape and cause further havoc - so they bound his spirit in a cave, hidden inside the mountain. But that meant that he stayed there all these years, getting more and more angry, and also somewhat unhinged. His spirit causes the spirits of the slain soldiers to linger, reenacting the battle every night. He also has one of his demons still under his control: his building demon, who built his castle overnight. The items for summoning the others were taken as loot.

                      When the PC's appear, he thinks they're in cahoots with the Sidereal, and gets furious. Also, he sees an opportunity to get out of his imprisonment. So he calls upon his demon, sounds his horn, and rushes to attack.

                      There are three PC's: A Dawn fighter, a Night stealth assassin and an Eclipse diplomat/sorceror.

                          The Batragar seems like lots of fun, especially the instantly built prison move to trap a person. I feel weird that he has no hardness (maybe some until damaged and then it breaks off?) but his counterdamage power is a nasty trick to balance that out some.

                          The dead horde, are they zombie or ghostly in form? Im assuming ghostly since the horn brings more in magically. Keep the Robber baron in the horde so he can benefit from their danger, (avoids asassin moves better) and have the Batragar separate the party as best it can with its powers to grapple & imprison.

                          The PCs should quickly identify the boss and will aim for him. Since he is the buffer, keep him safe via his allies. The Dawn will likely do best chewing up the dead horde, while the Eclipse sorceror is likely the one to identify the demon and avoid any tricks it has (like making sure any decisive damage is not done in melee if they know about the debris counter power.) This should mean the Batragar should be sent after the Dawn or Night if they appear as their roles. However, the Eclipse may talk it out if he is the closest to the boss. Be okay with that too.
                          Hope the fight goes well and is fun.
