    [Ex3] Sidereals, Charms and Character Concepts

    08-10-2015, 05:38 PM
    So... I'm kind of drunk and procrastinating right now, so I thought I'd write about some Sidereal charms I don't like, why I don't like them and what I'd like to see in Third Edition. Nothing specific, more like some trends that seem problematic and what approach could make things easier for me as a player.

    Like the fact that Sidereals have about a dozen charms to summon spirits. Now, I've got a problem with Spirit-Binding Battle Pattern because it's fueled by human sacrifice and any spirit you summon using it will suck unless you have four other Sidereals working with you, but the bigger problem there is that spirits are complete shit, which going by Ahlat's charms in Third Edition, is no longer a problem. That still leaves a couple of Charms like Water and Fire Legion, Sky Spirit Demand and one of the effects of Terminal Sanction, not to mention sorcery and thaumaturgy. Plus, you know, I'm a goddamn Sidereal. I don't need charms or magic to bind spirits to my service when I can just walk up to them and politely ask for a favour.

    Which brings me to my next point. I'm going to assume that Sidereals had all these charms in second edition because social combat as a system was completely fucked, so binding spirits was the only real option for Sidereal social charms. I would absolutely love it if Sidereals had social influence charms like Mark of Exaltation that made it easier for them to interact with spirits through social influence. If I'm going to play a respected member of the Celestial Bureaucracy, I expect to have powers that allow me to act like a respected member of the Celestial Bureaucracy. I don't want to have to magically enslave the people I'm supposed to work with on a daily basis.

    And another thing. I'm iffy on the fact that Sidereals get their familiar charms in Ride instead of Survival like Solars do, but I'm mostly okay with it. If I'm going to get a divine familiar, I might as well ride it anyway. I really wish Sidereals charms were more applicable to the ability they were slotted under so Sidereals aren't complete shit at riding animals, but that's another problem. No, my problem with Sidereal Ride is Riding the Dragon.

    Let's say I've got this crane familiar. I raised it as my familiar. I used Spirit-Shape Companion and Godly Companion to turn that crane into my most loyal companion. We traveled all over Creation and kicked ass together. Maybe I even thought it some of my kung fu just because I could. I have invested a hell of a lot of time and experience into that crane. And then I use Riding the Dragon to turn that crane into a fucking dragon so we can kill Octavian together or something and once the charm ends, that crane is gone forever. That's bullshit. Don't get me wrong, I would love to play a Sidereal who goes around finding animals with auspicious destinies, claiming them as familiars, uplifting them into divinity and then ultimately releasing them as elemental dragons, but it's not something I would ever do with a familiar I actually want to keep around. The whole thing just seems like a complete waste, like the charm tree doesn't want me to play a Sidereal with a familiar.

      Fuck Riding the Dragon so hard.

      The problem with Riding the Dragon is that it's not a fuck-up. The way it works fully makes sense within the overall design of Sidereals. It does exactly what it's supposed to do.

      And that... that reveals intrinsic, underlying motifs to Sidereals that are bothersome. Because the original design behind them is excellent, most of this is obfuscated, or sold to the reader much more skillfully in such a fashion that people don't realize the darkness.

      But Riding the Dragon is an in your face reminder. Riding the Dragon is that Charm which is there as a statement that the underlying motif of being a Sidereal is of being the kind of person who nurtures relationships with mortals, or animal familiars, over years or decades, and then when it's convenient for you you just coldly lobotomize them for a temporary boost in power.

      Riding the Dragon flatly rubs your face in the fact that Sidereals are assholes working for an asshole bureaucracy of assholes whose job involves being an asshole for the "greater good" except the greater good is coordinated by corrupt assholes who want bribes. And this is intentional! This is the original perfect Moran design, not a screw-up! Exalted: the Sidereals is that book where a young Seer grabs a mortal she's known for years and lobotomizes him because she needs to arrive somewhere faster than her Gold Faction rivals.

      If you look closely, the entire design of Sidereals is underlain with such dark, cynical motifs which grin wickedly at you from between the lines. And that's... that's often ignored because ignoring it is easier. And honestly? I'm fine with ignoring it. I'm not sure I want to play the kind of person who uses Riding the Dragon or Shun the Smiling Lady or any battle-pattern which work better if you consciously attempt to maximize casualties for your own side to fuel it.

      Bits and pieces of Exalted setting.

                      In theory I like that the Sidereals have different Charms for dealing with different sorts of spirits that grow from different places, although I would prefer that they were not "forceful" by default.

                      I do have to agree that, even if I do think it's overstating to say like Omicron does that Sidereal Ride makes that statement, I really am probably not so keen on reflection that selecting Favoured and Caste Abilities locks characters in to cascades which have a set degree of coercion and exploitation (Ride is coercive and exploitative, to a lesser extent so are Socialise and Presence, Performance and Linguistics aren't). It's very attractive that each tree has a story and theme expressed in a sutra, but...

                      I hope they would find a different strategy that changes the feel of these, so that each tree is clearly distinct in theme without locking a character into using exploitative magic just because a particular Ability is his style.

                                                  I feel that in 2e, especially, there was this feeling that sorcery is so useful that when you got to a certain level of essence, there was this assumption that a celestial would invest in it. And it was more a case of background justifying why not, rather than why. Sorcerery became a bit of a norm.

                                                  I'd prefer it if Sorcery was once more treated as weird and esotoric, difficult to attain and mysterious in nature. I'd want most Celestials to not be sorcerers, I'd want the ones that are to be seen as somewhat apart, beholden to strange forces and hidden occult laws.

                                                  That way, nobody would say "who needs Riding the Dragon when you can summon a Demon Wasp?" as the assumption would be that the majority of Sidereals can't summon a Demon Wasp, and aren't going to learn to either. Remember, Sorcery is harder to attain, in setting, than the mechanics allow.
