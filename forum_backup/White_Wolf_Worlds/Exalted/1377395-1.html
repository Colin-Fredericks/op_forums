        Is that a fair assessment, because I have an argument to address that but if I've misunderstood I don't want to be accused of a strawman.
        So a few points of difference, before getting into this: If a fight would be resolved without rolling dice I don't usually have it in the docket. I just...don't run things like "10 wolves jump 5 solars and get slaughtered." There's no clear narrative point to it. The hungry ghosts are closer, but that fight taking 2 turns seems...fine for it's narrative purpose.

        That first set of questions is very improtant however. I expect that if you, the ST, know I have 30 demons at my command, you would account for that either mechancially or narratively. Sometimes that means fights that were a bit difficult would be trivial. Sometimes it means swapping a Mortwright for another Abyssal, or the Abyssal spends a turn or two focusing on the BG, letting the Dawn get hits in better. Sometimes it means "you parading 30 demons on the battlefield to end the fight quick got the ire of the local populace". It's very doable to make a BG feel impactful.

        So your assement is mostly correct, although I object to "artificially harder". If summoning 30 blood apes makes the fight a simple win...let them have the simple win. Let them roll the simple win. Winning and showboating over weaker opponents can be fun! Especially if the combat is narratively important! To crib from my own game, there was a fight where 5 blood apes took on two solars, a giant lion, a dragonblooded, and a fae-touched human. The blood Apes got slaughtered in 2 turns, and only barely landed one punch--but it was fun! Because the players got to be awesome! Let the Twlight's battlegroup oblierate the enemies--the Abyssals gonna go through them like mincemeat anyway, and the Dawn's almost certaintly still going to be the show off in a fight!

        I had another combat where the party lead 2 BGs (different cities millitias with diferent training) to ambush an enemy army. It was a two turn route, and they massacred the miniboss--but it was rewarding to see the dice play out the plan they made. The math rocks make it feel real!

            Originally posted by Epee102 View Post
            This is not RAW, by the by. It depends on the size of the BG, so maybe the horde of zombies would soak it up, but its is far from universal.
            Page 208:

            "Area Attacks: Like other characters, battle groups can only make a single attack on their turn, but these are often large attacks, consisting of hundreds of flashing blades or sheets of arrows darkening the sky. As a result, when a battle group makes a close-ranged attack, it makes a single attack roll and applies that roll’s results to all enemies in direct contact with the group (depending on the battle group’s Size, this could potentially cover a considerable area). When a battle group makes a ranged attack, it must select a single primary target for the attack. If this target is another battle group, the attack resolves normally; if it’s an individual, then the attack is also applied to all other individual characters (friendly or hostile) within close range of that target."

            Your battlegroup can only target a single battlegroup with ranged attacks, and only characters embattled with it in melee. (If you embattle another battlegroup upto a size larger than you, they can't move.)

            It's not universal but it's pretty close.

                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                Summoned demons, mortal armies, retainers/allies/familiars.

                I'm not saying "never bust out the dice". But yeah, I'm certainly saying that not busting out the dice for your party NPCs is usually more fun.
                I'm with John on this one. As a player I can take a retainer captain-general of my army, who uses his own merit dots to get the actual army and the resources to support it. I can also grab a Tyrant Lizard familiar, and a few more young dragonblooded bodyguards. So then when I'm rolling into battle you've got me, my captain-general, my two bodyguards, my T-rex, my battlegroup of soldiers and my captain's bodyguards as well. Then the Twilight asks to roll in himself, his battlegroup of bloodapes, his Tomescue bodyguard, his special assassin made by the Ephemeral Induction Technique, and Octavian, who's he's just learned to summon.

                At some point you have to go "Okay, guys, I know you bought all those merits, and you should be able to use them, but this is making combat a nightmare, and it's making the whole campaign terribly unwieldy."


                What I try to do now is actually take a leaf out of the RPG Star Trek: Adventues. In that you have main characters, who are basically the PCs, but also a host of supporting characters. Who are nebulously NPCs, except in cases where one PC is off without the other PCs somewhere. So for example if your first officer is planetside with two supporting characters, then the players take control of them. It can even be the case where only supporting cast members go and do something, and the players just transition there.

                So if I've got a circle of players with like 10-15 various powerful familiars, battlegroups, retainers, etc. We have a bit of an agreement that they're not just going to bring them everywhere. For one it's almost totally pointless if they're powerful Solars, since adding those factors is just going to make them win harder, in a lot of cases anyway. What it's better to do with them instead is to make them go places so the PCs can have influence in more than one place, which can either be onscreen with the players taking command of the NPCs or just offscreen success/failure.



                That way players still change the game with their merits, just like how they can change the game with their spells or their charms or their actions. You get to really feel your character as a military commander sending forces off to do one thing or another, while maybe keeping one battlegroup with yourself at all times, but it doesn't become awful for me to run as an ST.
