    Varos, the City-Crawlers
    Progeny of the Keeper of the Forge of Night

    A Varos resembles something between a crustacean and an arachnid the size of a dog, with a chitinous body, a dozen long limbs, legs and claws and pedipalps alike bristling with setae, with long whiplike antennae and eyestalks that give it an uncanny awareness of its surroundings, able to sense through walls and structures as though they were clearest air. Only when curled up within their basalt shells for protection or slumber can other demons hope to catch them by surprise.

    While other demons may inhabit the infinite brass city of Malfeas as their prison, the Varos find it a nearly perfect home. Shaped as ideal urban hunters, they may slip and swim and scuttle through the structures made by men and by demons for their business and for their dwelling, treating the sturdiest walls like smoke and silk curtains. With a small effort they may extend this blessing to those objects and beings they have taken in their claws; for most, the only time they will know the same oneness of being with the city is when they are the Varos’ prey.

    And make no mistake, the Varos are predators to the core. Hunting in packs, a dozen or more may rise from the streets of the demon city with all the warning of a descending owl, tackling other demons much larger than themselves into the ground and burying them without a trace in seconds. Surrounding demons who can do so often take to the brass-bound skies rather than see if the Varos are sated with their current prey. Those who cannot fly will usually find out shortly one way or the other. Regardless, to be swarmed, dragged underground, and eaten alive is often a kinder fate than to be stalked by one alone: such prey are added to the larder of the Varos who took them, left half-fused within the walls and ceiling. This serves to sate the demon's hunger when necessary, of course, but Varos often consider their victims as living trophies. The city-crawlers may even treat these souls with something like care and unwanted affection, sharing lesser meals to prolong their life, lest they expire, spoil, and be no good for display anymore. After all, if one has a Citizen bound in their keeping, surviving only at their mercy, how impressive must they be?

    The Varos detest the world in its natural state, unshaped by its inhabitants. Left to their own devices, a Varos constructs a hollow senseless simulacrum of a city out of whatever bric-a-brac they can bind together, resulting in something structurally stable, but functionally questionable: streets dead-end unexpectedly, while great towers made of wood, stone, soil and bone cut off or angle unexpectedly in random, sometimes nonsensical directions. Chambers and floors within may be shut off without windows or doors, and rooms too-large abut to others too-small.

    Sorcerers may summon the Varos as abductors or assassins—or protection against the same—or may enlist their aid to construct buildings or battle fortifications.

                                    HALASZ, THE FISHERS OF MEN
                                    Demons of the 1st Circle

                                    "... And thus, I shall make you fishers of men..."

                                    ~Unknown

                                    What's that, up in the air? It's a bird! It's a plane! Ohgodhelpmehelpmehelpme~!

                                    The Fishers of Men are a strange breed, their limbs ending in specialised spinnerets. The silks are secreted from the ends of their arms, which are covered in fur, a cross between a man's hairy arms and the bristles on a spider. The web has a variety of properties; sticky, light, strong, sharp, etc, which are all useful, desired greatly by sorcerers, and are used by the Fishers of Men for their traps and strategems. For the Fishers of Men consume the flesh of other beings, both sentient and non-sentient. Deep within the City of Brass, they are always looked at with a wary eye. Some use their silks to cover up holes and pits, letting unfortunates fall through and impale themselves on great solid spears of silk. Others span silken threads across slopes, and those unlucky enough to move through find themselves cut in half. But it is their final usage which is the most infamous and gives them their names.

                                    The sticky threads of the Halasz can be used to attach to any object with a strength bordering on the supernatural. And using their native magic, the Fishers can yank on this thread, moving their target in seeming violation of the laws of mass and movement. Younger Halasz simply let their threads dangle from high up arches or balconies, grabbing those passing below and reeling them up. More intelligent ones set up entire networks of webs up in the sky, attaching between building tops or even clouds, whilst using individual silk strands to hang bait in front of more impulsive or unintelligent demons. And the oldest of them all, ride on clouds of silk that catch the wind, and reverses the pull of gravity to hold up loads. Such errant clouds can be steered against the wind, giving observers warning that something is deeply wrong before they get hoisted up, never to be seen again.

                                    Halasz are used for various purposes. Their silk can be used in construction, either hauling massive weight loads, scaffolding, or integrated directly into the masonry itself. The silk can be rendered down into alchemical mixtures or used as body armour, and last of all, the incredibly powerful silk can be used to tear down boulders, walls, entire fortresses, if enough Halasz are thrown at the problem (but then again, that counts for almost all demons). More discreet sorcerers use them for kidnappings; one moment the target is there, and the next moment he's a hundred meters up in the sky, and swiftly moving out of screaming range.

                                            Lyuk, the Hunting Burrowers

                                            Demons of the 1st circle

                                            "Ok, everything looks safe here."

                                            *fives minutes later*

                                            "Oh god!"

                                            Some demons are long runners, predators who hunt via wearing their prey to exhaustion. Others are front facing fighters, those who wander onto the battlefield and devour whomever they defeat. And others simply lie in wait, and have prey walk right past them.

                                            The Lyuk are a strange breed, with a specialized ability for warping space. Not dissimilar to the sanctum creating powers of gods, they finds a surface, and 'burrow' down. Not into the surface's solid material, but into the space between spaces. There, they hide iin their pocket dimensions, lying in wait and sensing the world outside through their charms, casting out lures and tirelessly observing the world through their 5 segmented eyes, jaws waiting.

                                            In the pocket dimensions of the Lyuk, time moves like molasses. Food does not spoil, and flesh does not rot, and people do not starve. Some enterprising sorcerers have used the Lyuk as storage areas or guardians for this purpose, for as ambush predators they only need to eat once in a great while, and they can be trusted with inanimate objects or unstable alchemical reagants. Others use them as hiding places that cannot be detected by mortal means. Other join them in their ambush, adding their own soldiers in a deadly first strike on an enemy.

                                            Charms:
                                            Casting call - The Lyuk creates illusions to have people get within range. The older they are, the better. Younger ones create illusions of gold, screams for help, or the appearance of a pretty young maiden in need of rescuing. Older ones can recreate the voice of your father, or the screams of your mother

                                            Strike from the shadows - Anyone striking from the Lyuk's pocket dimension gains a bonus when they strike from surprise. This bonus is increased if they either match, or outnumber the target.

                                                Cercopis, the Six-Fingered Monkey Thieves

                                                Among the Second Circle, there is no certainty who was responsible for creating these demonic larcenists and letting them loose throughout Malfeas' infernal sprawl. While there is speculation that their creator has since disappeared or been forgotten, it is more likely that admitting responsibility for the Cercopis' genesis would invite a great and terrible ire from their peers, none of whom are spared from the chaos wrought by these First Circle demons' simple instinct to cheat, lie, and steal. While demons of the Second Circle cannot deny the Cercopis' usefulness and make extensive use of them in their schemes, feral colonies of these six-fingered monkeys plague Hell's shifting streets and alleyways, serving as nuisances to their contemporaries of the First Circle in their kleptomaniac games between individuals and rival families. Many only hear their cacophonous whoops and cheering in celebration of a successful score when it is far too late.

                                                Cercopis bear a great resemblance to Creation's macaques, although some key differences betray their hellish origins. They cannot hide their green fur, thus they hide in the shadows, nooks, and crannies of urban landscapes and demonic estates, and their eyes glisten with a subtle light of burning jade and emerald. The most distinctive feature of their silhouette are their six-fingered hands, each with a symmetrical pair of thumbs, index, and middle fingers. They are the rogues of the First Circle demons, operating in packs to distract, trick, and beguile their marks before making a grand and frenzied getaway with all manner of personal possessions - a common sentiment among Hell's denizens is that any Cercopis you can see are not the ones you should look for. Cercopis are deceptively cunning and cruel, valuing their prizes on how much their loss would inconvenience and distress their owners rather than adhering to ideas of inherent material value.

                                                While the instinct to steal material goods beats strong in their demonic hearts, their size and natural talents makes Cercopis into decent spies and scouts for Second Circle demons and sorcerers who dare to handle them. Their knack for occasionally replacing goods on a mark's person with items of similar mass, weight, and shape allows them to be used for nefarious means, with more than one sorcerer using these six-fingered pickpockets to plant damning evidence on their rivals, smuggle goods across borders via unsuspecting travellers, and slip combustive materials or deathly toxins into the vicinity of their enemies. If caught, Cercopis will fight vicious and dirty to outnumber on their assailants and furiously scratch and maul at soft flesh, eyes, and hair.

                                                Cercopi colonies amass exquisite hoards of trinkets and valuables, a communal collection of wealthy prizes from their schemes and competitions to outdo each other. Occasionally, these colonies are willing to exchange their prizes with outsiders and rival Cercopi families, although never giving anything away for free: currency and material value alone does not sway the Cercopis, instead looking for opportunities to deprive the other party of things they deeply care about, such as family heirlooms, memoirs of deceased friends and family, or Artifacts uncovered by Exalted adventurers. To successfully swindle and steal from a colony's hoard begrudgingly impresses the Cercopis while woe befalls any thief who is caught in the attempt or warrior who tries to take their prize by force alone as they suffer a brutal death at countless six-fingered hands, accompanied by a choir of hellish screams and wicked howls.

                                                    Concept: A riff on certain varieties of ants

                                                    In front: The demon's head and face is near invincible. Its skin and carapace is harder than castle walls. Its eyes are xeno-crystal, and fire and acid spits from pores on its skin. No-one can hit it without getting struck by a counter-attack, and it can survive nearly any hit (relatively), and it can deal almost as much damage in return. Near impregnable. Swords shatter upon it. Sorcery merely scars the surface. Arrows veer away, as if sensing its impregnability. And it can 'spread' its invincibility to nearby objects, surfaces, and beings.

                                                    At the back: Soft flesh, weak armour, capable of being struck down by nearly anything. Fast-moving legs that can be crippled. A soft, fleshy belly.

                                                    Tool-concept: This demon was created to be the equivalent of doors. All demons have a limited amount of power, and this one was created to have all of its power focused on the front. Its gigantic head serves as battering ram, gate, movable wall, and barricade all at once, shielding an infantry battalion behind it or blocking an entrance to a castle. A powerful but limited tool that does well in well-planned, static battles or situations but can be easily side-stepped by a more agile enemy.

                                                        Uveg, the air-spinners

                                                        There, a shimmer in the air. There, a whisper in the wind. Look again. That's the nest of the Uveg, the airspinners. Strange men who look like ordinary mortals, except for the utricating, glassy hairs upon their backs, their 6 limbs, and their gaunt faces. The Uveg look upon the world in terms of shapes and essence, not in colours. In fact, they consider colours and light to be similar to fire and electricity in consideration to mortals: dangerous tools that can be reshaped into deadly weaponry.

                                                        They build vast cities amongst the demon city, often using their 6 limbs to transform the nearby essence into the famous glassy silk they are so famous for. Their cities are vast, with an incredible amount of detail and amenities, from walkways to bridges, to fountains to gardens, to boulveyards to vast halls. All of them completely invisible. Visitors have been driven mad, dying of hunger and thirst or by their own hand while trying to transverse these cities while the entrance lies less than a hundred meters from them. These foolish explorers attempt to explore these cities in search of things like gold or artifacts of jade, not knowing that the Uveg despise these things for their propensity for bright colours.

                                                        Brighter men instead, bring specialised sweetmeats that the Uveg are partial to, and gain in response excellent and deadly artifacts of all kinds. Gauzes which hide anything from sight. Near-invisible tools for secrecy and wetwork. Glass windows that show daylight even in the darkest of nights. Glass brushes that could absorb the hues from objects and transfer them to another, creating hues which were impossibly bright, or even indescribable. Dresses which glowed in the dark and lit up in choreography to dance moves. And most prized of all were the the Coloured Bolt Throwers, weapons which fire prismatic hues of deadly energy, at the cost of the surrounding colours when the weapon is fired (this causes disfigurements in the skin, but the Uveg do not seem to mind).
