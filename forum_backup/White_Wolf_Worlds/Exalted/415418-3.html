                                                    That's not how it works. At all.

                                                    So there's this thing called the division of labour. The basic idea is that more advanced societies develop more specialized knowledge, so a greater number of people work in a narrow field. In less advanced societies, there's less specialized knowledge, so people can perform many different jobs. It's easier to learn all the things you need to do in order to run a farm than it is to learn how to perform brain surgery, basically. At the same time, division of labour allows people to develop more unique identities.

                                                    Not only does that apply to tabletop RPGs, it's also one of the fundamental building blocks of good design.

                                                    In a game with few (splats/classes/archetypes/whatever you want to call them), the designer has to fit a large number of narrative options - all of which have to be mechanically represented - in a small number of boxes. The splats need to be designed to include narrative options that are semi-unrelated, which makes it more difficult for the splats to develop unique identities and undermine's the game's coherence. However, if a game has many different splats, the narrative options can be grouped together in specialties. It's easier to represent these specialties mechanically and it's easier to create unique splats that maintain their identity and that support the game's general coherence. Having fewer Exalted is what makes the Exalted less special; distinction is what creates uniqueness.

                                                    The same thing applies to charms. Having charm branches with different specializations makes it easier to create characters who possess their own unique identity and who are distinct from other characters even when their general concept overlaps. It's also extremely useful in regulating characters' degree of proficiency. What John said way back when about how Essence 1 charms have a lot of utility to people get more bang for their buck if they want to spread out a little, while Essence 2 charms are weaker on their own, but get really powerful when they synergize? That is a stroke of fucking genius. Dabblers get a decent reward that makes their small investment worth it, while specialists get that, plus smaller rewards that add up to a huge benefit once they put the required effort in. This also insures that dabblers can't overcome specialists, since they won't be able to invest as heavily in Essence 2 charms.

                                                    Adding more Exalted also empowers players by giving them more options. The number of Exalted - no matter how many there are - is fundamentally a mechanical constraint placed on the players to limit their options. Giving players more Exalted also gives them more choices about which characters to play and how to play them, which gives them more power over the game as a social experience. Exigents in particular invite players to take an active part in game development instead of telling players what to do. That is absolutely huge. Tabletop RPGs are one of the few entertainment media that are able to give players that kind of power. Video games can only ever manage the illusion of choice.

                                                    Adding more Exalted is also useful because it extends the game's lifespan. It creates more opportunities to sell books, more opportunities for homebrew material, more opportunities for storytellers and players to expand their games and more opportunities for the fans to discuss the setting. It creates more opportunities for people to take an interest in the game and give it a try. That's really important if you want to attract new players.

                                                    Now, I'm going to quote Pierre Bourdieu's The Practice of Reflexive Sociology for this next part, because it is really, really fucking important:

                                                    In the social sciences, as we well know, epistemological breaks are often social breaks, breaks with the fundamental beliefs of a group and, sometimes, with the core beliefs of the body of professionals, with the body of shared certainties that found the communis doctorum opinio.
                                                    Most people don't know how to think. Most people can only reproduce what they've been taught. Most people don't think about how things work. Most people just assume that the world should cater to their preconceptions.

                                                    Most people wouldn't be able to fix Exalted Second Edition, simply because they're incapable of reconsidering the fundamental design assumptions that broke Exalted Second Edition in the first place. At best, they realize that certain outcomes aren't conducive to gameplay, but they have absolutely no idea what those outcomes are contingent upon. The best they can ever hope to achieve is to work around the problem... which only breaks the game even more because the "solution" depends entirely on the existence and continuation of the problem.

                                                    Now, learning how to think is hard. I know it's hard because I spent three years trying to figure it out until things finally clicked. In those three years, I've had several theoretical breakthroughs in my thesis and my comprehensive exam because John or Holden happened to make a comment on system designed that was also applicable for my work.

                                                    The reason why I am unconditionally supportive of Exalted Third Edition is because John and Holden have repeatedly demonstrated that they are able to break with the flawed assumptions of Exalted Second Edition and fix the problems. How do you fix animals? Make them unique and challenging! How do you fix the West? Give it rare resources and make it necessary to the rest of Creation despite how fucking far it is in order to emphasize the historical themes of the West of the Age of Exploration! How do you fix Artifacts? Create Evocations! Expand the map! Add more Exalted! Create new content instead of just regurgitating what's already been done!

                                                    What John and Holden did requires a very rare talent for reflexivity, and it is worthy of immense respect.
