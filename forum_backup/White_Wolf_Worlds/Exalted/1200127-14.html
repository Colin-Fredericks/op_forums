        Okay, hopefully Presence will be less dense than Performance was.

        Excellency Glowing Coal Radiance lets one buy automatic successes and roll extra dice for every 10 on their roll. Then there’s Eternally Argumentative Flame to reduce or eliminate penalties for flurrying Presence or Socialize influence rolls. And Unbearable Taunt Technique is another power to effectively provoke somebody to hostility, which can also be deployed in combat to make somebody focus on attacking the user specifically, and penalizes their Initiative if they resist.

        Burning Dragon Mien provides a threaten roll with double 9s, and in combat this causes the target to lose a bit of Initiative. Warm-Faced Seduction Style lets one make a persuade roll to seduce that also instils a Minor Tie of lust or desire, and with the benefit of Fire Aura, allows one to convert bonus dice added by Appearance into non-Charm successes. I wonder if they might get any more Charms to seduce, but for the moment, this is one that has a marked contrast with Solar power of the same type, in terms of the mechanical path it takes and how the result is thus less extreme.

        Debate-Sparking Bonfire is another Charm to provoke, but works a bit differently than the prior; it can target more than one person at once, and the hostility it creates needs to have a social component, even if it’s physical violence. I’m imagining the scenarios in which this might occur; the social situation that one might wish to sabotage by prompting a wave of acrimony, or trying to rib a rival into undermining their own image by rising to bait or acting uncouthly.

        Hot-Blooded Ardor allows one to briefly treat their Appearance as one dot higher, or even greater by expending their Fire Aura. Then there’s a Water Charm, Passion-Transmuting Nuance, and I dread to imagine what that underhanded element could do in Presence. Oh, it’s actually relatively benign, allowing the user to change one inspired or otherwise significant emotion to another, and give it the force of a Major Intimacy. Hell, that can even lend itself to some beneficial applications. Fair enough there.

        Fearsome Dragon Presence allows a Terrestrial to briefly impose a penalty on people who would oppose them, as a result of their intimidating aura. Moth to the Candle is a somewhat more powerful form of Charm to draw enemies to focus on attacking them specifically. The Earth Charm Grinding Millstone Argument lets the Dragon Blood immediately reset an attempt to persuade somebody after failing a roll, and lowers the benefit of an Intimacy already used to bolster Resolve.

        Haunting Words Infliction is the Signature Air Charm. With it, one can make a boosted Instil roll to give another an Intimacy that the Exalt possesses, and keeps the argument echoing with them even if they don’t get the Intimacy, meaning that any time within the next few weeks they encounter evidence that could be used to try the Instil roll again, the affected character makes it against themselves, with the Exalt’s Essence in non-Charm dice added. It’s a nice thing to bring out some of the Air Aspected qualities; passionate belief in one’s ideals, and a concise and logical tone that can be hard to ignore.

        Virtuous Mountain’s Shadow is used in response to a witnessed social influence that one wishes to disrupt, allowing one to make a counterargument from one of their Major or higher Intimacies that allows witnesses to treat the Intimacy as their own for the purposes of resolving the social influence, with those who do so gaining it for themselves as a Minor Intimacy. It’s another interesting one to represent the Earth place in society.

        Now, to see if Fire can do better for its next Signature in an Aspect Ability… Terrifying Fire-Dragon Roar… ah, I think I remember this one: Presence as a means of actually breathing fire. That’ll do the trick. It’s got a rather decent range, adds Essence to Initiative to determine the damage that is divided between targets, and also functions as a threatening action to make people try and flee, even if the flame didn’t hit them. It works nicely.

        Fluid Recollection Insinuation is back to that good evil Water stuff, with a Charm to convince somebody that a false prior encounter occurred, applying a Psyche effect to create a false memory reinforcing the claim. Implausible claims can be made, removing successes rather than inflicting a penalty. It can create a Minor Tie to the user if the target never actually met them at all, which can only be eroded by the influence of others, at which point Willpower can be spent to realise that the memory was fake. People who already knew the Exalt also form a Minor Tie, but don’t need to remove it before they have the option to realise that the memory is false.

        There’s then Spirit-Cultivating Leadership, a Charm that can only be used on people with a Defining Tie of loyalty to the Dragon Blood. It’s extremely cheap and persists over days to give the person so empowered improved Resolve to protect their loyalty, three specialties in a variety of Abilities, and extra Willpower each day to resist social influence. Not a bad mix of effects there.
        Back to Fire with Blazing Heart Ascendency, allowing one to limit the Intimacies that a person can use in a Decision Point against influence to persuade or threaten made with Presence or Socialize. The character so affected can only use Intimacies based on passion to resist, rather than things such as abstract Principles. However, it only works on people that have such passions in the first place; somebody who doesn’t has nothing to inflame, and is thus immune.

        Heartstring-Pulling Approach is another more straightforward Charm; it adds a single non-Charm success to a Presence or Socialize influence roll, and if the target’s Resolve is lowered by a Major or Defining Intimacy based on a passion, then it increases the Willpower they need to spend to resist. Nice and simple.

        Back to combative effects with Aura of Invulnerability, which makes a kind of transition from the emotional to the physical; the user can roll their Willpower to gain extra health levels, and adds their Charisma to their soak. This is offset by how the damage inflicted on temporary health levels transitions to their actual body when the Charm ends (which can happen prematurely, since it has the Aura keyword), although they’re specifically protected against being killed by this.

        Vivacious Dragon Beauty is another seduction Charm, this time with Wood, and grants a bonus dot of Appearance. I… will need to look back at the other Charm that granted Appearance to see how they square up, because on the face of it, that seems a little bit minor. Unless… the significance is that somebody using a lot of Performance will have an option to raise Appearance as well? Need to look back over the others later…

        There’s an Earth Psyche Charm now in the form of Mind Like Fertile Fields. With that one, an Exalt can use a common aphorism as a Presence roll to place somebody briefly into a kind of placid, hypnotic state, during which Influence rolls can be made against them that their Intimacies don’t get any relevance to… at least for Resolve. It doesn’t say so, so I presume that Decision Points still function. It’s another one that compares directly to a Solar Charm, definitely less powerful, but good in the added capability it can give for the Dragon Blooded. Although, I would like if it was written a bit more; not because it requires any clarity, it’s obvious enough, I’d just like to know a bit more about the perspective of the recipient. Are they aware in their passive state, for instance?

        Following is yet another Earth and Psyche power (as well as needing the Aura), Entombed Mind Technique. This is used for a more extreme kind of hypnotism; it drives a person targeted with the prior Charm into an oblivious sleep that they can’t be roused from without magic for several hours, and won’t waken from on their own for at least a day. In that time, the same kind of influence roll as the prior Charm can be made against them once. But it can also be repurchased at Essence 4 to be able to place somebody with a Willpower lower than the user’s Essence into an indefinite sleep, with a kind of stasis effect to keep them alive and preserved, and can only be broken with magic (and to which only the Exalted are immune). Repurchasing again at Essence 5 extends this to outright petrifying a person, an effect that the user can revoke with physical contact and spending motes, and otherwise requires magic such as Order-Affirming Blow. Sooo, yeah, that’s definitely taking the concept of suggestive hypnotism and the Exalt making it their own.

        The cascade stops at Essence 4, back with a Fire Charm: Dragon Warlord’s Convocation. Targeted at a single character, it lets a Dragon Blood make a Presence roll with double 9s to create a Tie of loyalty that starts at Major, and can strengthen and existing one directly to Defining. Unlike other instil actions, it requires a Decision Point, as well as spending two Willpower. Furthermore, for the rest of the story in which it was used, it can’t be voluntarily eroded, unless the Dragon Blood harms them personally or something else to which they have a strong Intimacy. Its power is offset by only being possible to use once per story, unless reset with achieving a legendary social goal.

        ​Presence is good there, nice mix of social and combat effects, and the former are vivid in a manner that gives an interesting picture of what Dragon Blooded societies might be like.

        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

            I’ve actually been a bit interested to see what Resistance might get, since it’s an Ability where one might expected to find forms of bodily transformation, or at least wreathing oneself in elemental substance.

            Of course to start is Ox-Body Technique, which doesn’t actually have an element, since it’s never even really supposed to be a Charm even by the standards of “Charms aren’t things”; it’s a representation of the hardiness of the bodies of the Exalted. It functions on the same principle as the Solar one, where the exact benefit depends on Stamina, improving as the Attribute goes up, and able to be purchased repeatedly, the main difference being that it can only provide two health levels, and the best it can do is give a -1 level. The actual excellency is Purifying Blood Ascendancy, adding successes and letting one reroll 6s.

            Following that is Body-Cleansing Prana, a Charm that can be Air, Fire or Water, enabling one to resist poison or disease; Air for inhaled toxins, Water for ingested or blood-transmitted ones, and Fire for disease, doubling 9s on the resistance roll. A repurchase at Essence 3 allows one to dramatically expel poisons specifically in an unblockable, short ranged attack, so long as the resistance roll is high enough. Already the Ability is going for some neat, and even a bit nasty, application.

            For a proper Earth Charm there’s Uneating Earth Meditation, assisting the Exalt in being hardened against deprivation of food and drink by eliminating the penalties for them, and increasing the time that can be gone without consumption, followed by Untiring Earth Meditation, to alleviate penalties for like of sleep while in contact with ground or stone.

            Then there’s Impervious Skin of Stone, letting one double their Stamina for soak calculations against a single withering attack, with Earth Aura allowing one to reserve use of the Charm until an attack actually hits them (but before rolling damage).

            There’s a Water Charm called Eternal Tide Endurance, allowing the Dragon Blood to convert 1s on an incoming withering attack roll into additional soak to apply against it, with the Aura letting one apply this to decisive attacks to create Hardness. That’s followed by a Wood Charm, Supple Viridian Scales, allowing one to momentarily reduce their armour’s mobility penalty by a little bit, or else grant a non-Charm die to movement and disengagement in light armour, with Aura being able to extend the Charm so long as the Aura is maintained.

            Ah, here’s the good stuff, (Element) Protection Technique… unless this is about being protected from the element. Let me see… yeah, the latter, gain improved soak and a bit of natural Hardness against attacks in the form of the element, and subtract Stamina or Resistance from the damage of an environmental hazard. But hey, at least it’s not something dependent on repurchases for the element that you want anymore. It also notes that it won’t work on more indirect forms of the element, such as Earth against metal or Fire against magically generated pseudo fire such as a Solar Bolt.

            Elemental Aegis functions in the same manner as Melee’s Elemental Sheath, although one thing it has going for it is a slightly more interesting visual element, with the suggestions for how the element might remove the armour directly off of the Exalt’s body.

            Continuing with the theme of meditating into being like the earth’s lack of need for things is Unsleeping Earth Meditation, to acquire the effects of a full night’s rest with just an hour of meditation (although after a number of days of continuous use, it can’t keep substituting for actual sleep), Unbreathing Earth Meditation to greatly extend the time that one can hold their breath, and Unfeeling Earth Meditation to let one briefly ignore wound penalties (with the extension from Aura requiring a repurchase at Essence 4).

            The Signature Air Charm provides us with Body-Like-Clouds Meditation, which allows one to take on the quality of air such that withering damage is lowered by the character’s Essence (which can even take it below its minimum damage), and steals Initiative from an attacker who hits but fails to damage.

            Perfected Scales of the Dragon lets the Terrestrial harden into an impervious crystal form, granting a rather significant Hardness for a round, which can stack with Hardness provided by other Resistance Charms. Decisive attacks that are thwarted by the improved Hardness don’t reset the attacker to base Initiative, but cause them to lose it as though they’d missed, with it granted to the Dragon Blood. It also comes with a cost of preventing the user from attacking or moving on the turn following its use.

            Raging Fire-Dragon Spirit allows one to take on a quality of fire, becoming faster and stronger at risk of burning out; they get a bonus Strength dot, can ignore a bit of wound penalty, and add some bonus dice to rolls for attacking, rushing or feats of strength. Apart from costs such as an aggravated health level and having a Duration of Aura, using it means that Stamina stops being added to soak, and a point of Initiative is lost at the end of each round. This one I like for being an instance of that transformation thing I was talking about at the start.

            With Fathomless Depths Replenishment, one can meditate for an hour in order to make a roll of Essence + Stamina, with successes providing temporary -1 health levels. This one has a strange and interesting cost in terms of how those health levels stick around; when the Charm’s Duration elapses, undamaged ones fade, but damaged ones stick around, counting against successes on future uses of the Charm; that is to say, it can’t be repeatedly stacked on top of itself, so if, say, you have three damaged temporary levels and roll four successes, it will count the three you still have against the total allotted. The temporary health levels need to be healed first by any recuperation or magic, and the longer they stick around, the more painful they become i.e. the higher their wound penalties; when they do heal, they vanish. It’s funny that the flavour text for this Charm describes it as contemplation revealing oneself as eternal, when the result becomes something that is capable of lingering and festering; eternity has its disadvantages. I suppose that Dragon Blooded Water is capable of even screwing up itself; truly the spirit of the Second Edition Ebon Dragon lives on in its inky depths.

            Well-Tended Garden of the Soul instead lets one create a reinforcing loop of life energy; Wood Essence is focused out through physical contact into surrounding plant life (rejuvenating any that is sickly or dead), and then, should it not be interrupted by the Exalt crashing, taking decisive damage or being forced out of range of any plants, it flows back into them, allowing for Essence + Stamina to be rolled with successes healing non-aggravated health levels. It’s an especially pretty and flavourful use of Resistance as a means of healing damage.

            Moving on, there’s Earth Bears Witness, by means of which one can shunt decisive damage to themselves into surrounding earth, or structures made from it (which can create minor dramatic effects such as difficult terrain, as appropriate). Particularly large and impressive forms of stone or earth even allow one to increase how much damage can be negated. With higher Resistance, one can purchase variations of the Charm in Water and Wood, functioning the same but with appropriate variations (and now I wonder, does subtracting the highest possible damage by sending it into something like a lake cause it to churn up in a manner that might cast waves over the battle?).

            Immovable Mountain Technique lives up to its name, rooting one to their spot and making mundane attempts to shift them, such as being knocked back or thrown from a grapple, fail outright, and even magic that would do so is mitigated, although the precise manner of how is necessarily a Storyteller call (a suggestion is given for Heaven Thunder Hammer). It has the Perilous keyword, so it would fail in crash anyway, but it also ends prematurely if the Dragon Blood should actually move or be moved.

            Ripples-on-Water Defense is obviously another Water Charm, used to either make a withering attack subtract successes for 1s that appear on the dice roll, while decisive attacks apply that number to rerolling successes, starting with any that show 7.

            For those who max out their purchases of Ox-Body Technique, Dragon’s Unfailing Vigor provides one more benefit to the Terrestrial Exalted constitution, granting a single -0 and -4 health level each.

            Essence 4 lets one use the Water Charm Flowing Dragon-Body Endurance, which, for the duration of their Aura, allows an Exalt to apply the value of their Initiative as Hardness against decisive attacks. The Charm can’t be used in armour, but it means that while it’s active, being hit by somebody with a lower Initiative is basically impossible (barring appropriate counter magic), while it’s also something that is variable in a manner nicely evocative of water.

            And up at Essence 5 is an Air Charm, of all things: Revolving-Hurricane-Force Defense. The character is able to roll Stamina + Resistance, with non-Charm bonus dice equal to their Essence (so, basically, five), and gains a bonus to soak equal to the successes rolled against withering attacks by enemies with lower Initiative. Its duration is tied to Aura, and the bonus drops by one every time an attack actually manages to hit the Exalt.

            So in the end, Resistance didn’t have much in the way of personal transformation after all, nor very much interacting with armour, come to think of it. Still, what it does have looks robust and functional, and there are a few rather pretty effects dotted around in there.

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                            The last few cascades have been rather dense, owing to being related to combat or social influence, so I’m hoping that Ride will be comparatively relaxed.

                            The Excellency lets one add successes and lets the user’s mount ignore a point of mobility penalty from their barding. Then there’s immediately an Air Charm in Heaven-Racing Leap, allowing one’s mount to make a gravity-defying bound to automatically jump one range band forward, or use 10s to reroll failures on a movement roll including jumps, with Air Aura allowing one to leap vertically so long as they have something to land on.

                            Tread Rooted in Life allows the Exalt’s mount to ignore penalties to movement actions resulting from plants, probably including things such as roots and heavy vines and the like. Or… I’m actually a little bit unclear on if it ignores penalties altogether, or just removes two points, or is absolute for one action and just two for any other ones. I need to look back at the Ride rules. In any case, one can pay experience points to get variations for Air, Earth and Water (the last applying to partial immersion or wet and slippery surfaces).

                            Great Heart Companion lets one either give their mount improved Resolve against threaten rolls or increases their own Defence against unhorsing gambits, with Wood Aura extending the duration from Instant to Aura and providing both benefits. Cloud Harnessing Technique is then an Air one to let a mount spend the turn walking or standing on surfaces that would otherwise be unable to bear their weight, as well as ignoring difficult terrain.

                            Dance of the Jade Bridle allows an Exalt to very rapidly tame a wild animal for a mount with a stunt appropriate to the animal in question, with a variety of possible Attributes. The animal that is beaten will submit to being saddled and mounted and forms a Minor Tie of loyalty. It can’t fully tame a wild animal on its own, but it serves as an effective starting point, with guidelines provided for how to continue the process.

                            Reins-Like-Roots Unity is another Charm to assist against unhorsing gambits, making 1s detract from successes and increasing the difficulty of the gambit, with Wood Aura able to maintain the duration.

                            Earth gets a Charm with Creation-Turning Hoof, which is used to impose a penalty equal to the Speed bonus of one’s mount onto an enemy’s roll to oppose their rush.
                            (Hmm, when did I get to describing every individual Charm? Eh, I guess at a certain point their more basic function Charm became a bit more interesting)

                            Anyway, Ebony Spur Technique, that’s a fun name. If an ally that is also mounted lands an attack of their own within short range, the Dragon Blood can reflexively ride up to the enemy and make a decisive attack that gets the mount’s Speed bonus added on. That counts as one’s combat action, but not their movement, and it can be reset within a scene by crashing an enemy while mounted.

                            Hmm, a Charm for coordinated cavalry attacks, not a Charm to protect one’s mount from Anima flux in sight, a prior reference to the House of Bells teaching mounted warfare… I think I can definitively conclude that the idea of Dragon Blooded having a distinct aversion to riding animals has been consigned to the ash heap.

                            Speaking of, there’s Ass-to-Elephant Method to let one use the power of Earth to let a mount add their Speed bonus to their Strength for purposes of feats of strength, both in terms of rolling dice and the minimum feats they can try. That’s got to make for some very effective draught animals for the purpose of things like dragging large treasures to be shipped back to the Blessed Isle.

                            Now, Signature Air, Untethered Pegasus Spirit. Hmmm, what could this do? But yeah, the Terrestrial is able to make their mount… not fly as such, rather not fall if they should gallop out onto an empty space, like over a cliff or pit. The closest to true flight it gets is being able to spend Willpower when using Heaven-Gracing Leap to rush enemies in the air to do… something. I’m finding it a little bit difficult to figure it out exactly through all of the range band terminology, but that’s just me. I get the basic gist that it helps one ride their mount up to an enemy that is flying. Finally, it’s another one that insures one against falling to death or severe injury if any conditions that would disrupt the Charm are fulfilled.

                            Mountain-Trampling Hoof lets an Exalt strengthen their mount to serve as a damn battering ram, adding non-Charm successes to a feat of demolition against large structures. The Strength requirement to attempt the feat is lowered by building up momentum across multiple range bands, and the resulting feat can be instantaneous even if it would normally take time. The mount is also not fully protected against the force of the impact, with any 1s on the feat’s roll being used to roll bashing damage against the animal. The Charm can’t be used for an animal that is on the verge of unconsciousness or death.

                            Fire gives Charge of One Hundred Generals to make a rush attack against a battle group or a noteworthy enemy that needs to be further than medium range, but no farther than four bands away, immediately rolling to Join Battle if it hasn’t already been started. If the rush is successful, then both the Dragon Blood and mounted allies and battle groups will become locked on to the target, allowing for continuous movement towards that target without using up the movement action for the next four turns, until within close range. Each range band crossed in the charge provides one extra Initiative point. It seems like a bit of a circumstantial Charm, but seems pretty powerful if it can be used. Plus, it has a rather striking image to it; whether it’s the Scots Greys at Waterloo or the Rohirrim at the Pelennor Fields, nothing beats a good cavalry charge.

                            Ride Beneath the Waves is a more basic enhancement for a mount to go through water; they become amphibious, is capable of swimming effectively, and can run at the bottom of bodies of water without penalty. Plus the rolls to control the mount underwater will get double 9s.The only complication is that the benefits don’t actually extend to the rider; if they want to go underwater as well (which I’ll note that they don’t necessarily need to), they’ll need to use other powers to breath.

                            Heh, I’ve sometimes wondered if there would ever be a Charm that would use the term “exaltation” more colloquially. Anyway, Dragon-Among-Horses Exaltation is used to anoint a steed with one’s own blood, imbuing it with some of the Exalt’s own power (maybe not so colloquial); the anima banner builds up out of them, and it gets the benefits of either improving Strength rolls and withering damage, Dexterity rolls, or Stamina ones with soak, as well as getting increased Hardness (which does immunise it to anima flux, so… I guess it’s a matter of how flux takes a higher anima to even start inflicting damage) and some extra -4 health levels. Ah, I missed how the Charm’s effect is permanent and costs experience points, but there’s a very neat note on how the Scarlet Dynasty has access to rare, highly bred horses descended from stock crossed with elementals that waive the experience cost of the Charm being used on them. Even if it’s not the most powerful Signature Charm of all, it’s among the more impressive alignments of element and Aspect Ability, if only for how it has a cultural expression.

                            Now, Indomitable Warhorse Endurance functions as a special defence for one’s mount, being able to spend Initiative to reduce a decisive damage pool against the mount. It’s not as good as the kinds of similar powers that the Exalted can apply to themselves, only reducing the damage by up to Essence. It can also be used outside of combat against an environmental hazard, for a flat reduction of (Essence) dice to the damage roll.

                            Oh jeez, I spoke too soon about being less dense, there are so many more of these. Damn necessary expansion to neglected Abilities! I’m gonna speed through most of them.

                            Seizing-the-Reins Approach. Fire Charm. Lets you take control of the mount of somebody that you’ve unhorsed as a custom gambit, have it reflexively make a withering attack. Fire Aura lets it function reflexively. Next!

                            Unbreakable Stallion Spirit. Provides a few general bonuses to riding an animal and keeping one’s seat on it. Next!

                            Seven-League Gallop. Charm to greatly increase a mount’s speed, as well as an improvement to movement actions in combat timing.

                            Trail-Blazing Dragon Steed. Lets one leave a trail of fire in the wake of a steed charging at full tilt. Creates a brief environmental hazard, that might be longer in a sufficiently dry environment.

                            Vanishing Cloud-Rider Ways enhances disengage rolls, and with Air Aura also refunding the Initiative cost of success.

                            Essence 4 now. Blazing Charger Attack is a Charm to create a rush with double 9s, being able to reflexively move across more range bands at the end, and reflexively making a decisive close-quarters attack at the end. It also lets one use a lance to make an impaling attack without needing the usual requirements that I cannot remember. While Elusive Skirmisher Tactic empowers attacks made in the opposite direction, being able to reflexively make the classic Parthian shot, or get a bonus to the raw damage if they do aim.

                            Last is the Essence 5 Charm, Horses-Like-Dragons Stampede. The Charm allows one to make a dramatic action to round up a herd of wild, ridable animals (using the framework of a group of Ride-based rally for numbers actions) to build up the size of a battle group up to a maximum of 3, making a herd whose approach cannot be concealed without appropriate magic. But its function in combat is not as a battle group, but as a disruption while stampeding across the battlefield, imposing the Size as a penalty to Join Battle rolls, with anybody whose roll is beaten by the Dragon Blood taking damage from being trampled, with damage based on dividing a combination of Initiative and herd’s Size among targets, and not resetting Initiative to base if it succeeds. Finally, it can be learned in different elemental variations to marshall things other than wild animals, in the appropriate terrain: Air gets avalanches (that can knock damaged people back and down and hits them with extreme cold to penalise movement until they receive medical treatment), Earth has rockslides to also knock people around and then bury them (requiring a feat of strength to pull loose and stand back up), Fire ignites a blaze in dry grass to create a bonfire to continue damaging anybody who was hit in the initial attack (as well as possibly creating a sustained burn on its own), and Water lets one drag a river or similar body behind oneself into battle, which then becomes directed to create a beneficial environmental barrier.

                            I’d say that Ride has done well in filling out the tactical dimension of the Ability here, making the choice to use a steed more significant for the Terrestrial Exalted, and the last one has what might be some of the most dramatic and impressive elemental effects that they’ve had thus far.

                            Okay, next is Sail. Sail is going to be simper, right? Yeah, nothing but drowing people and brainwashing sailors, and maybe the occasional thing to pilot a boat. Yeah, it should take, like, ten minutes.

                                                            Okay, Dragon Blooded Sail. Gonna try and go back to that attitude of skipping over many that provide basic enhancements, and focus on the ones with distinct effects and implications. That being said, just noting how the Excellency, Fine Passage-Negotiating Style, adds successes and rerolls 6s.

                                                            The first couple, Storm-Outrunning Technique and Ocean-Darting Manuever, are in that mould of providing some enhancements to certain kinds of Sail roll. Where it starts to get interesting is with Dragon Mariner Attitude, their own version of a power to represent general experience and style as a sailor. In particular, with some of the qualities provided by Water, they can exert a kind of control over their own reputation, alternately acting as a magnet for people who would provide services to a sailor on shore leave, those who would be looking for a seafaring hero to solve their problems, draws the respect of sailors under their command (and sailors in general if they’re trivial characters), or else able to exert a general aura of being menacing and disreputable (including getting the Hideous Merit). Come to think of it, this one doesn’t precisely represent being a sailor, so much as being able to control the kind of sailor that people perceive you as.

                                                            Skipping over one to the Air Charm Hurricane-Predicting Glance, which allows the user to make a Sail roll against a difficulty determined by how familiar they are with the local waters, which if successful allows for flawless prediction of the weather long their charted course for the next day, including the highly unlikely and unexpected, but not things produced by magic. It also bestows a specialty in dealing with that weather phenomenon, until they’ve gotten through it.

                                                            With Old Salt Spirit, I get a sense that capabilities that Solars get all at once with Salty Dog Attitude are distributed among a few Dragon Blooded Charms. Before, there was Deck-Striding Prana to improve rolls to move around or stay balanced on a ship, and now this one to boost Resolve both against general influence for fear, as well as things that would draw them to bring their ship into danger and prevent them from going out to sea. I find it interesting that their minds can conflate these things. As another special benefit, the bonus to Resolve can increase to half their Sail (rounded up) if they should use a stunt to reference memories of their past adventures at sea. I particularly like that as an idea for resisting fear in general; some giant rock monster tries to threaten them, and they bolster themselves with memories of the time that they escaped the grasp of the dreaded spindly killer fish.

                                                            Hmm, Seven Seas Wind-Luring Chanty seems familiar, but I wonder… did it used to be an Occult Charm? I have a distinct recollection that there was an impulse towards keeping Abilities elementally pure for things that wouldn’t partake of all the elements, so where a need was seen for something to call upon wind, it was placed in Air’s Occult rather than Water’s Sail. Well, whether it was anything and whatever it was before, now it’s a Charm to make a Sail roll (using Charisma, since it’s supposed to be about calming the ring with a rousing song), which lets them use successes to lower penalties from ill-favoured wind, or else boost their ship’s speed a bit in fair weather.

                                                            Given the material of most ships, it makes sense that there would be a Wood Charm, Sturdy Bulkhead Concentration. An Exalt can spend a level of their own anima to spread it out over the ship, reinforcing its structure against perils and enemy weapons, allowing the player to roll some extra non-Charm dice on an already failed roll to get through a hazard or deal with an enemy’s stratagem. Raising success enough to beat the initial difficulty reduces the damage suffered, without necessarily eliminating it completely or changing how the result of the roll registers as failure.

                                                            The Signature Air Charm builds upon Seven Seas Wind-Luring Chanty. When faced with threats from the air such as hurricanes or electrical storms, the Charisma + Sail roll can be made with double 9s against the hazard’s difficulty, with success dispersing it entirely, and failure still affording the option of dealing with it mundanely. It’s limited to once per story, but is reset by either dispersing or navigating through a hazard for the purposes of achieving significant goals.

                                                            Water’s counterpart in Earth gets Hull-Shattering Avalanche Impact, another to let one reinforce the ship with a level of anima, for the purpose of giving the ram stratagem double 8s, and lowering the number of threshold successes needed to increase the resulting damage. It can also be used outside of combat to wield one’s ship for feats of demolition, rolling Wits + Sail with the ship’s Speed in non-Charm dice, and being able to engage in Strength 10 feats. It’s a curious image, of dealing with something like a sea wall or fortress by ramming a trireme into it, and being able to expect to succeed.

                                                            Dragon Sets the Seas Ablaze has the rather dramatic effect of letting one discharge their anima through their ship’s ranged weapons, letting one make a broadside stratagem with double 9s, and a capacity to set the targeted ship on fire. The fire will continuously roll a little bit to damage the ship’s Hull, and the burning ship can make a special stratagem to put themselves out, although even failure keeps them from rolling for damage in that round. Mostly, it’s an interesting image, of an Exalt pouring their Essence into a ship’s weapons to add some energy to the attack, although it’s counterbalanced by being only once per scene with no reset condition; it has to wait for the opportune moment, rather than laying a barrage.

                                                            Shipwreck-Strewn Tempest Wake is the first of these to depend on Aura. With it, a ship that has succeeded in getting ahead of a pursuer or breaking away from naval combat can stir up a naval hazard in their wake to create an obstacle for those pursuers if they want to keep up the chase. The hazard has its own rules, with failure to navigate it not only inflicting damage, but throwing them off course in a manner that prevents effective pursuit for days. It’s a power that can apply to multiple ships, and its once per story use can be reset by achieving a major goal through seafaring prowess or managing to reach a key place through sailing.

                                                            And Benediction of the Living Ship allows the Dragon Blooded sailor to put a bit of vitality back in the wood of the ship, giving it a capacity to pull itself back together, as the Exalt rolls their Essence plus an applicable specialty to repair Hull damage. The scented aura or sprouting fruit of the ship in that time also heals the user and allies aboard of one non-aggravated damage level, which can also be used to harvest enough food to keep the crew going for the rest of the story, if it’s needed. It finally provides an extra success to resist poison or disease while aboard. I like the way that this one synthesizes exerting a kind of manipulation of the ship’s wooden structure to deal with specifically naval issues; turn your shell of dead wood into a little floating garden where you won’t starve or get scurvy.

                                                            Now, with Vanishing Fog-Bank Escape, you can expend a level of anima (a lot of these Charms are functioning on the need to put your anima into your ship or its weapons or the sea), to create a cloud of mist to aid one’s escape. An escape stratagem is made with a reduced Momentum cost, and the number of Momentum the user has over a pursuing ship adds bonus dice on the roll to escape. For rolls to pursue at sea, it can also be used to impose a penalty on the roll to keep following. And outside of actual combat, it can be used to create a fogbank that will generally obscure the ship, and persists for a scene unless something strong manages to disperse it. This is a Charm that does not have a limited use, so one can repeatedly build up their anima and pour it into creating fog, if it’s needed.

                                                            Pirate-Masquerading Method builds on the mist generation to create an illusion over the Exalt’s ship, to cause others who see it to regard it as one flying their own colours, proverbially speaking. A roll is made to determine how hard it will be for other people to see through the illusion, which can be attempted by any nontrivial character that is close enough, although doing so with their mundane senses suffers a penalty. It can also be seen through automatically by somebody who actually makes it on board the ship. It can instead be used to obscure the Dragon Blood themselves, adding Sail to dice rolls to disguise oneself as a member of a noteworthy society of sailors, such as a Tya or Lintha, and giving a bonus to Guile against actions that would expose them as an outsider. I’m just imagining some of the uses that House Peleps could put this Charm to; making raids on neutral nations or possibly even the shipping and ports of other satrapies, and leaving behind witnesses who can only say that the attacker was disguised as one of their own.

                                                            Bellowing Thunder Admiral is another Air Charm, this time to shout one’s orders over any obscuring din and fill the hearts of one’s shipmates to courage and valour, adding a success bonus equal to half of one’s Essence to command actions for the benefit of battle groups of sailors. They also get that same bonus to their own rolls against rout, and get the same bonus to Resolve as the Exalt gets from Old Salt Spirit, so long as they remain within medium range (and honestly why should they ever not be they’re on a ship where the fuck else are they going t-). I feel as though there have been other Charms that could modify their type under the right circumstances, but this one calls attention in the style of Martial Arts to special activation rules in the form of being able to use the otherwise Simple Charm reflexively after winning Join Battle for a boarding action.

                                                            Coming up to the end is the Essence 4 Charm Ship-Seizing Dragon Talon, through which a Terrestrial can provide their boarding actions with double 9s, for a rather reasonable price of 1 mote and 1 willpower point. Success allows them (and any allies) to then roll Join Battle with bonus dice equal to threshold successes on the stratagem roll, and waives the Willpower cost in Bellowing Thunder Admiral.

                                                            Finally there’s the Essence 5 Mute Charm, Fog Shroud Ambush. The Dragon Blooded captain is able to shroud their ship in mist again (without needing to spend their anima), this time to waive the Momentum cost for concealment in naval combat altogether, and allowing the next round’s attack or escape stratagems to succeed automatically, without regards for an opposing roll. Even if the opponent has supernatural senses that could see through the fog, the Dragon Blood is able to impose their Manipulation as a penalty to the opposing naval roll. It can only be used once per scene, but is reset by incapacitating an enemy ship with a naval stratagem.

                                                            And that’s it for Sail, comparatively short and sweet. For all of my snarking before, I am actually a little bit surprised that it didn’t have things for recruitment or sabotage in the end, but otherwise think it’s very effectively focused on providing benefits and added capabilities to actual sailing, and effectively conveys the formidable challenge of dealing with sailors that have some actual power over the water.

                                                            Speaking of water, that’s it for the Water Aspect then. My final assessment is that their element crossed with their Abilities does manage to make them very distinctly evocative.
