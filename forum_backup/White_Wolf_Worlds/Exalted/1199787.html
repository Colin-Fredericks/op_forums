    The Time of Salt and Iron [Quest]

    03-26-2018, 03:10 PM
    The world is dying, the air filled with smoke and blood.

    The Shogunate is gone, the Legions withdrawn, the Dragons caught up in their ruins of empire. And, with the Dragon gone, the lesser beasts of the world have emerged to worry the edges of society.

    The beginning of the end was first noted in RY -393, when the barbarian peoples that lived on the eastern banks of the River of Tears started migrating across, into the Provinces of the East and North. Initially, this wasn’t met with too much interest, in fact, such migration was allowed and even encouraged, with some Daimyo granting the barbarians land in return for service as foederati mercenaries. In but a few decades time, however, the trickle became a stream, then a flood. No longer were bands and clans crossing the limites, but entire tribes, confederations and nations. These newcomers were not content to settle on the borders, nor to peacefully integrate with the Shogunate citizen population. With the Interregnum in the Threshold coming to a boil, the Legions were by-and-large unable to offer assistance, and the local limitanei forces could not stem the tide. As province after province fell, and more and more barbarians migrated, waves of refugees started moving into the Threshold. Those officials and commanders stationed on the borders of the Threshold provinces often noted that the barbarians that harried at the steps of the fleeing refugees seemed….. frantic. Desperate, and such desperation often manifested in brutal, sometimes-suicidal attacks in effort to win land away from the River of Tears. Contrary to ‘popular opinion’ about barbarian war tactics, this was highly unusual.

    As waves of migrants and war-refugees moved into the backdrop of the Interregnum, said officials soon learned why the barbarians were so desperate to move.

    The Great Contagion.

    The first outbreak was recorded as happening in RY-249, in the city of Seleucia, a subsidiary suburb of Deleheshen. Within 20 years, the pandemic had spread Creation-wide, transported by the wide Shogunate trade network and aided by the ceaseless civil war and breakdown of infrastructure of the Interregnum.

    The horrors of the Great Contagion resonate in the souls of society even today (namely, in the public sanitation and health programs of Lookshy and the Realm), and the effects of the Contagion on society, coupled with the devastation of the Interregnum, ended in an almost-total collapse of society Creation-wide. There are areas of Creation where the effects of the Contagion can be felt to this day, and the catastrophe must have seemed like the end of days to those who managed to survive it. Areas with high population densities, such as urban areas and much of the East, suffered an estimated population loss of around 90%, and other Directions suffered proportional dieoffs. Cities were, almost as a whole, abandoned, and these refugees from the urban centers fled to the countryside, stripping it bare and passing along not only the Great Contagion to those still unaffected, but other communicable diseases as well. Trade and accompanying infrastructure broke down, and as many people died from lack of food and clean water as they did the pandemics. Manufacturing ground to a halt as well, with goods only being produced on a local level, and themselves being cruder, a callback to an earlier age. Settlements in isolated areas tended to fare ‘better’ than those on main thoroughfares and trade routes, but this, in turn, led to outbreaks of xenophobia against refugees.

    The Great Contagion ravaged Creation for upwards of two centuries in some regions, spreading then dying back like some living thing. Even the Exalted Dragon-Blooded, the Princes of the Earth, were affected by the disease. It is unknown if they were able to die from the disease (it is recorded that some did, in fact, become infected), but even still, the population of the Exalted host was cut in half, at the least, from the ceaseless civil wars and societal upheaval. This, in turn, left the survivors largely at the mercy of the restless dead and the Fair Folk, both of which experienced a resurgence of activity not seen again until the Fall of Thorns. Entire regions were converted to shadowlands, and entire villages were dreameaten by the rapacious Raksha, feasting off the despair of the survivors.

    Yet still, the survivors not only had to suffer the effects of society collapsing, coupled with the restless dead and the Fair Folk, but even the very environment seemed against them.The weather got colder and wetter, and a creeping cold swept down from the north, dropping snow on regions during late spring and early autumn, even down to the shores of the Inland Sea. The growing season was diminished, both in length and effectiveness and area, leading to regular famines and disease outbreaks resulting from malnutrition even 150 years after the first collapse. Survivors went to war, not only to defend themselves from the Dead and the Raksha, but also to take more fertile lands. The face of the Sun was hidden for weeks at a time, not only by the stormclouds, but also plumes of smoke from burning cities, and when the wind blew from the East….. it was heavy with the sickly-sweet scent of rot. It must have seemed like the very light of Creation was guttering out, and everywhere you looked was affected by Famine, Pestilence, War.

    Death.

    It is now, in this end of the world, that your story begins.

    Your name is Rechila, son of Hermeneric, and you are a prince of the Swadian Confederation, a group of 12 tribes sharing the same language, culture, government and gods. Twenty years ago, in RY-118, your father led the Confederation out of their near-barren homelands of the North, seeking to escape the death-throes of the inner Threshold. Instead of heading South and West, like so many other refugees, he led your people East, to the margins of the River of Tears. Still fertile, the valley of the River of Tears is all-but-empty, and could be the salvation of the 15,000 survivors you lead….. but it isn’t completely empty. Xenophobic survivors, desperate to protect both themselves and their holdings. The Restless Dead, of the Contagion and of its aftermath, who seek to drink the blood of the living. The Fair Folk, the Cold Ones, eager to feast on the despair of your people. Cruel gods, driven to misanthropy by the death of their worshippers and a lack of a response from Heaven, will seek to prey on you in their own ways.

    Your father, Hermeneric, is the well-respected King of your people, having first led them from their homelands 20 years ago, but he is old, and the strain of this migration has taken its toll. He will rely on you more than ever in these coming times, and your ascension as King at the hands of the Althing is all-but guaranteed.

    In this time, this end of days, what sort of King will you be?

    If you are so interested:
    1. Temperment: How do you present yourself to others?
      1. Sanguine: Associated with blood, the element of air, the season of spring and the dawn, you are extroverted, emotional and people-oriented. You tend to be charming, cheerful, passionate and expressive, but also undisciplined, disorganized, frivolous and impulsive.
      2. Choleric: Associated with yellow bile, the element of fire, the season of summer and the afternoon, you are extroverted, unemotional and task-oriented. You tend to be determined, confident, honest and practical, but also hot-tempered, insensitive, vindictive, and arrogant.
      3. Melancholic: Associated with black bile, the element of earth, the season of autumn and the evening, you are introverted, emotional and task-oriented. You tend to be dignified, detailed, thoughtful and organized, yet also critical, dogmatic, pessimistic and untrusting
      4. Phlegmatic: Associated with phlegm, the element of water, the season of winter and the deep night, you are introverted, unemotional and people-oriented. You tend to be polite, dependable, pleasant and perceptive, yet also indecisive, passive, stubborn and lazy.
      5. Eclectic: Associated with the element of wood, you are all of the above and none, changing as the mood takes you.
    2. Values: What virtue do you hold dear?
      1. Valor: Without bravery, what is the use?
      2. Compassion: All we are is how we treat others, nothing more
      3. Conviction: You have to see things through. Always
      4. Temperance: The only real luxury is the luxury of self-restraint
      5. Willpower: Strength, true strength, comes from within.
    3. Mother: While your father was the King of the Swadians, from a long line of nobles, your mother was:
      1. Also a Swadian noble. Coming from a noble lineage on both sides of the family, your word carries much weight (Influence 3)
      2. The princess of a Shogunate successor-state. Given in treaty to ensure the then-foederati remained loyal, your mother’s name may still carry weight in her homeland (Contact 1: dux Gallia, speak Riverspeak)
      3. The daughter of a chieftain of the Eridanian river-folk. The Eridanians range up and down the River of Tears, trading both materials and rumors. (Contact 3: River traders)
    4. Weapon of choice: As a Swadian warrior-noble, you are expected to be skilled at the arts of war. However, there is one weapon you are renowned for your skill with.
      1. Sword: the egcheru, the Northern derivative of the Shogunate spatha. Broad-bladed and heavy, the egcheru is the traditional signifier of Northern nobility. (Melee specialty: individual combat/duels)
      2. Spear: the ger, a strong-shafted spear usually about 7 feet in length, usable both on foot and on horseback, in either one or two hands. The most common weapon in the North, and arguably Creation-wide. (Melee specialty: formation fighting)
      3. Seax, the seax is the knife found across almost all Northern cultures. Single-bladed with a thick back, the seax is both a useful utility tool as well as a weapon, especially in the press of a shieldwall or inside a building, and in most Northern cultures signifies freemanship. (Melee specialty: close quarters)
      4. Axe, the francisca, a light axe popular across most of the North and Northeast, suitable both for hand-to-hand combat and as a throwing weapon. The weapon has a fearsome reputation for causing damage even through armor and helmets (Melee/Thrown specialty: penetrating armor)
      5. The Javelin: the angon, the Northern derivative of the Shogunate pilum, the angon is capable of punching through a wooden shield and wounding the enemy behind. In addition, the butt-end of the javelin often gets caught on the terrain, effectively removing the enemies shield from the equation even if the head doesn’t penetrate. (Thrown specialty: shield disarming)
    5. Activities as a young adult: Before your current age of 26, you spend your youth and early adulthood at:
      1. Training at war and drilling tribal levies: Part of being a ruler, even a barbarian ruler, is that you lead the people in both war and peace. You knew what your role would be, and you prepared for it. While training, you made friends with the sons of other noble families, and using your families name and resources, raised them up as your beaduprat (roughly, brotherhood, similar to the Realm’s Sworn Brotherhood), giving you not only skill at war and arms, but a small group of warriors loyal only to you. (War 2, Melee 2, Presence 2, Command 1 [Size 1 elite battlegroup)
      2. Experimenting with cavalry: The Swadians, like most of the Northern peoples, lack much in the way of cavalry forces. The lack of strong, fast mounts (most Northern horses are little more than burly ponies) and a propensity for men with large frames of heavy bone and meaty muscle mean that Swadian warriors tend to function better as infantry. What little cavalry the tribes have is exceptionally light, serving mainly as scouts, screens and javelin-armed skirmishers. You, on the other hand, noticed how well the Shogunate cavalry performed, and over the years bought, bred and raised horses and trained men in their use, until you had a small force of heavy cavalry at the tribes disposal. (Ride 2, War 1, Lore: Horses 2, Backing 2)
      3. Playing politics in the Confederation Althing. The Althing, or the Thing of all Swadians, is the assembly of all tribal chieftains, nobles and religious leaders. While the individual clans and tribes of the Confederation have their own Things, to elect chieftains and deliberate on laws and judicial cases, the Althing decides the path of the Confederation of a whole, and much like its “civilized’ counterparts in the Threshold, it is a morass of factions, backroom deals, bargaining and grandstanding. You took to the political life as soon as you became an adult at 16, not only casting your votes but also bargaining and making deals. You are confident in your ability to navigate the political parties of the Althing, and can safely say that some of the decisions reached not only ensured the Swadians continued survival, but even earned a measure of relative prosperity. (Bureaucracy 2, Integrity 2, Performance 2, Presence 2, Socialize 2)
      4. Received a classical education. Contrary to the popular opinion of the average Shogunate citizen concerning the ‘barbarian peoples’, your people greatly valued education, literacy and the written word. Almost every person in the Confederation is literate, in Skytongue runes at least, and you took that drive to greater heights. When you were a young child, your father took on the services of a tutor from one of the Shogunate successor-states, who taught you the languages of the Threshold, mathematics, engineering, history, medicine, and other liberal arts. As you grew older, you used your education to serve as a diplomat for the Confederation, where the recipients of the diplomatic missions were often surprised to find that the author of the well-written missives with graceful penmanship was none other than a barbarian in furs and wool. (Speak Low Realm, High Realm, Dragontongue, Lore:liberal arts 1, Linguistics 2)

                              To clear some things up:

                              The Swadians (and most tribal groups in the North) do not practice primogeniture. Instead, they elect their leaders. Generally, the smaller groups elect their immediate leaders, then these leaders elect their leaders.

                              So, in the Swadian Confederation:

                              -Each clan elects their clan-chief
                              -The clan-chiefs of a tribe elects their tribal chief
                              -The Tribal chiefs elect the King

                              Of course, the general way of things is for power to stay in the hands of established families, according to tradition. For example, the clan-chiefs of a clan are almost always going to be the oldest, most-well-established member of the clan. The Tribal chieftains are almost always going to be the most-well-established war-leader among the clan-chiefs. And, your family has held the Kingship for close to 200 years at this point.

                              This tendency to concentrate power within families is what led to the formation of nobility amongst the Swadians (and, indeed, most of the tribal groups of the north), and the nobles keep their position through deeds, namely war, and through the giving of gifts and grants earned through those deeds.

                              For example: a clan-chief owns all the land of a clan. In return for the continued support of their followers, the clan-chiefs divy it up to their followers, as well as giving them booty and other gifts from wars and raids. They attract an armed retinue of loyal followers who support them in war (though, well, fighting under their leadership) and in peace (by electing them clan-chief in the first place, supporting their decisions in the clan-Thing, and giving them tribute in the forms of crops or goods)

                              When a leader dies, the clan/tribe/kingdom comes together in a Thing to elect the new chieftain. The choice of the now-deceased chief often has a great deal of influence on the final decision, but people are allowed to throw their support behind those they prefer. Prospective leaders will often try to attract new followers through promising them more land, more cattle, wealth from raids or trade, gifts of weapons and armor, the fostering of children, so on and so forth, anything that will cause the prospective votes to come their way.

                              Generally, the tendency is for established families to keep their power, often for the sole reason that they are able to give the most gifts and fulfill the most promises to potential followers. One particularly common method of 'creating' new followers to support you is to set up one of your armed retinue as a landed tenant/clan-chief/new tribal chief.

                              For example: Most chieftains (in Swadian Skytongue, "dryhten') attract armed retinues, called "Hirdmenn" (in Swadian Skytongue, literally 'house-men, but im going to refer to them as 'retainers'), usually the younger sons of landed tenants that wouldn't have much of an inheritance. These younger sons engage in acts of bravery (raids to obtain booty, or heroics on the battlefield) in order to attract their chieftains eye, and if they get chosen, the chieftain gifts them weapons, armor, and freedom from a life of toil, in return for their 24/7 armed support as retainers (Chieftains and their Hirdmenn are semi-professional, compared to the as-needed tribal levies). The retainers live in the chieftains hall, on their dime, and when having Hirdmenn becomes too expensive, many chieftains release some retainers from their service, granting them land and status in return for political support. The retired warrior gets land and status (and therefore, more of an opportunity to start a family, and attract followers of their own), while the chieftain gets support that is loyal to him.

                              The sons of nobility usually have the greatest impetus to go out and make names for themselves for that reason, compared to a commoners son. It isn't certain they will receive their parents social status, so they often feel the greatest drive to go out there, win reknown and booty, in order to attract followers and make it more likely they get elected chieftain.

                              What does this long rambling mean?

                              So..... that means that there is a chance, however slim, that Rechila doesn't get elected as King once his father passes or abdicates. That last bit is especially important: his father is still kicking, and will for a while. In Quest terms, it is going to be a couple chapters until it happens, and Rechila (through your decisions made) will have plenty of chances to either cement his future ascension or send it down the toilet.

                                  With regards to how i view, and will portray, the Great Contagion, since i know the question will come:

                                  I, personally, have always found the canon "90% of EVERYTHING died" aspect to be, honestly, vaugely ridiculous. It sounds more like something that belongs in the grimderpier editions of WH40k.

                                  Instead, i portray the Great Contagion as 'only' killing somewhere around 60% of Creation's human population. Apocalyptic, society-crumbling, yes, but Creation wouldn't be left a barren wasteland. Other animal life and plants were unharmed, by the Contagion at least.

                                  Generally, my population dieoff percentages runs like this:

                                  1) East: 90% in the least, with some areas entirely wiped clean of human life.

                                  During 'my' Shogunate, the East, or more accurately "The River Province", was the most heavily-populated area of Creation, comparable to modern-day China. Cities grew on the banks of the Yellow River and River of Tears like fleas on a dog, and even where the area wasnt urbanized, it still had re-goddamn-diculously high population densities.

                                  This meant the Great Contagion (and secondary pandemics) ripped through the region like a scythe through grain. It also meant that, once everyone was dead, there was a "reverse migration" of people back into the region, exactly what the Swadians are doing.

                                  2) The Center (the Blessed Isle and the inner Threshold), about 75%, from the Great Contagion and civil war.

                                  After the River Province, the Blessed Isle and coastal inner Threshold were the most heavily urbanized areas in Creation. They too suffered from this, as well as an influx of refugees from the rest of Creation.

                                  3) The North and the South: about 50%. Away from coastal cities, most deaths occured more due to logistical interruptions (not enough people to bring in crops, mainly) than due to sheer doompunching via Great Contagion (although the GC still hammered both Directions). The climate change also caused a lot more deaths than in other Directions, with the North getting colder and wetter (during the Shogunate, the North was capable of growing wines and other fruit. In the 'modern day, not so much, asides from apples and other hardy things), while the South got more arid, leading to the destruction of the rice industry in the region as well as the switching of barley for wheat.

                                  As an aside, the North fared better than pretty much every region except for the West, largely for the same reason: geographical isolation.

                                  4) The West: about 30%. Then as is now, the West was the most geographically isolated region of Creation. The dieoff of trade across Creation was a mixed blessing: cant catch the Great Contagion if nobody brings it to your shores, but many islands were semi-dependent on that same trade in order to, you know.... Not die. Famines and 'lesser' diseases wiped out entire islands, and when the Great Contagion managed to get introduced, it ROFLstomped everything just like everywhere else.

                                  I also portray the Great Contagion as, well, just a regular disease, albeit a disease with a supernatural source. That means, while it is absurdly lethal, if you are fit abd healthy and have people to take care of you while you fight it off, you stand a decent chance of surviving.

                                  It is just that, well.... Not too many people in Creation had those perks. The Interregnum (the definitive Shogunate Civil war that ended the Shogunate for good in 'my' Creation) was in full swing when the Great Contagion reached its stride, and between the breakdown of public infrastructure (namely, sewage removal and access to clean drinking water), the lack of trade (namely, not enough food) and the cramming of refugee populations into urban centers, most people werent in tip-top shape.

                                  It isn't their fault: the Deathlords deliberately released the Great Contagion st the worst possible time in order to have the greatest effect. If the Contagion happened to Creation during the Principate (the early Shogunate, when life was actually pretty nice) or even the Dominate (the late Shogunate, when things were still ok, even if the government got more dictatorial. They made the aqueducts and sewers run, at least), things probably would have worked out okay. Probably between the Spanish Flu and the Black Death.... So, still pretty bad, but not apocalyptic.

                                  Vectors and Symptoms: the Great Contagion was passed from person to person via body fluids, meaning it ripped through cities like a wildfire. Any drop of fluid from an infected individual could lead to more infections, and there tended to be a LOT of body fluids.

                                  Picture the unholy lovechild of ebola, influenza, malaria, and diarrhea, and you get a pretty good idea: coughing, sneezing, sweating, vomiting and shitting, all involuntary and all capable of passing along the disease.

                                  The worst part is is that you can catch the Contagion and not realize it (as in, not show symptoms) for about a week, depending on how strong your immune system is. You can travel around and pass along the all the while..... Which is part pf the reason why people got so xenophobic during the course of the Contagion.

                                  Generally, the symptoms proceeded like this:

                                  1) initial infection. Over the course of a week or so, the infectee will gradually develop quasi-flu-like symptoms, such as runny nose and eyes, headaches and muscle pain, coughing, stomachache, etc.

                                  2) as the disease progresses, the infectee develops a fever, which quickly increases in severity. In addition, the infectee suffers increasingly severe nausea and diarrhea, eventually unable to keep down food or water. Most infectees will bleed from the mucus membranes at some point, but this is a byproduct of the irritation to the body than a function of the disease.

                                  This stage lasts for as long as the patient does, usually only a few days at most, as most people either get fried from the fever or die from dehydration.

                                  3) it -is- possible to survive the Great Contagion, albeit extremely difficult. If an infectee manages to break the fever, the other symptoms will fade quickly. Of course, the infectee will need to be administered to in order for this to happen, as most of the time the fever will render them unable to take care of themselves (aka comatose).

                                          Chapter 1: The Wheel and the Road


                                          “So I, wandering, Bereft of my homeland, Over these long years, And from there, fully, Went winter-weighted, Over the icy waves, Far from my kinsmen…”

                                          (fragmentary part of the Northern saga-song “The Wanderer”)

                                          >Ascending Water, RY-138<

                                          You ride to the crest of the ridgeline, and even the spreading vista of the River of Tears, your destination for the last month, can’t do very much to mitigate your black mood.

                                          As you turn back to the head of the royal column, you reflect back on what your father has said in the last few weeks, about how the salvation of your people lies on its banks.

                                          You can’t quite see it, but you share his determination. At the very least, getting down out of the Northern mountain valleys is a top-priority. It is but the first month of autumn, barely out of summer, and yet the northern winds already carry a biting chill, and the upper slopes are thick with frost in the early morning. Pulling the tribes down into the river valley will give you fodder for the livestock, and your people won’t starve, but the winter will be hard nonetheless…..

                                          Shaking your head, you banish the conjecture from your mind. You have always been confident, determined, but ever since you started taking an active role in the migration of the Swadians, your mind has been plagued by increasing worries. The weight of leadership is increasingly heavy, and you now understand why Father looks so drawn. This is your duty, however, as a prince of your people, and you will take your duty seriously.

                                          Your face evidently betrays your inner mood, and one of your shield-brothers, Hildebald, knocks you from your reverie by an elbow to the ribs, hard enough to feel it through layers of boiled wool and mail. Shooting him a quick look, he responds,

                                          “Don’t look so concerned, Rechila! We made it to the River of Tears, in, what, 6 weeks? We will be fine. It isn’t even the middle of autumn, yet!”

                                          His enthusiasm breaks through and gives you newfound confidence, and you grin, “Good point. We still have a few weeks left before it starts getting actually cold, and that is plenty of time for the wagon-trains to move their lazy asses out of the hills.”

                                          Another of your shield-brothers, Lugios, chuckles, “I’m not too concerned. The commoners have plenty of fat on them, enough to keep warm. My ass, on the other hand, is worn bare.”

                                          You interrupt, “Oh, shut up. Your squeeze has enough ass for the two of you. Her hips are as wide as a wagon-wheel!”

                                          He sniffs, “You are just jealous of Yrsa’s ‘expansive tracks of land’”, to the roaring laughter of the rest of your beaudprat. The good mood and the banter continues all the way back to the outskirts of the royal pavilion, where one person in particular is waiting to kick the feet out from under your cheer.

                                          Your younger brother, Adelmar.

                                          “Where the hell have you been?”

                                          Your temper flares at the needling and assumption of authority, but you restrain yourself with some effort. Getting into a pissing contest with your brother won’t solve anything…. Although it would certainly be satisfying to put him in his place publically. Instead, you dismount your horse, taking the moment to compose yourself, then reply with all the dignity a prince of the Swadians can muster,

                                          “The King sent me ahead to scout the road, brother. Was there something you needed?”

                                          Adelmar shifts, rankled at the put-down. Still annoyed, “ Not I, but the King has need of us. (He says this last bit with smug pride.) I was to fetch you as soon as I found you.”

                                          You raise an eyebrow. Him having more work for you isn’t unexpected, but…. Adelmar? He hasn’t been much help, not without a lack of effort on his part. He has a tendency to cock things up, asides from debating in the Thing. What could your father possibly want him to do?

                                          Shrugging, “Very well. Let us go to the King,”, and after dismissing your retainers, you stride off in the direction of the royal pavilion, Adelmar hot on your heels. As you walk, you idly think about your younger brother, and just why the two of you no longer get along.

                                          It didn’t used to be this way. When the two of you were younger, you were inseparable, and he worshipped you like one of the heroic ancestors of old. It was only when he entered manhood, and realized that he had a chance, no matter how small, at the Kingship, that your relationship soured….. and you fully admit, with some regret, that it wasn’t entirely his fault. You were long-used to being in command, and his attempts to bolster his own name (sometimes at your expense, albeit inadvertently), on top of your naturally-dominant personality, led you to have several private disagreements and one very-public argument. It didn’t help that he wasn’t very good at military command nor civilian organization, having drastically messed up both battlefield command and the ordering of several wagon-trains, requiring either you or your father to actively step in and render aid. In the beginning, you tried to gently offer advice, but he evidently took these as criticisms and shut down. Now, the two of you can barely stand to be in the same room as each other, something which, when he isn’t irritating the hell out of you, find to be rather sad.

                                          You sigh out loud, and he hears it, smirking, “Have a lot on your mind, brother?”

                                          “ Yes”, bluntly. “There are still 12,000 people stretched back 5 leagues, almost back to the mountains, and am not sure when they will wander their way down to the plains. The weather is getting colder, and I am worried about the amount of fodder we have for the livestock, in addition to, well, the gods-damned winter on top of that. There are rumors of a blood-feud brewing between the Salians and the Ripuarians, over a fucking cesspit, of all things, and….. I have fears for the future, as well.”

                                          Adelmar stays silent, then , “What do you mean?”

                                          You shake your head, alternately amused and annoyed, “We are moving into a region that our ancestors fled out of, Adelmar. They left for a reason. On top of that, we don’t know what we are walking into. For all we know, the entire Eastern bank of the River of Tears could be a gigantic Shadowland. Or worse.”

                                          He is silent again, “…. You know, this is the first time in a long time that I’ve seen you show doubt.”

                                          You stop walking, turning to look him full in the face. Is Adelmar….. showing concern? Empathizing with you? Or is he just looking for something to get an edge over?

                                          Firmly but quietly, “I do not doubt, Adelmar. Concern is not doubt. I have every faith in our people’s perseverance, in our father’s plan, and in myself. We will succeed, I am just unsure of how yet.” He doesn’t reply to this, but his face grows sullen. After a moment, you turn and continue walking.

                                          Once you reach the royal pavilion, two of your father’s retainers hold open the doorflap for you to enter, and you stride into the tent without slowing down. It is sweltering inside, the air heavy with the smells of woodsmoke and wool and flesh, and you unpin the fibula holding your cloak around your shoulders. Handing both your cloak and your helmet to a servant, you and your brother both walk forwards, dropping to a knee respectfully in front of Hermeneric, King of the Swadians.

                                          He is deep in conversation with the chieftains of the Salians and the Ripuarians, evidently trying to head off any potential blood-feud between the two of them. After a few moments, he notices the two of you kneeling in front of you and rises to his feet.

                                          “My noble sons!” Turning to the chieftains, “I have to discuss something of great importance with my sons. May we continue this settlement another time?”
                                          Evidently, the men agreed, and after nodding respectfully to their princes, leave the pavilion quietly. Your father waits until they are gone, then falls back onto his bench with a groan, putting his face in his hands.

                                          You rise up to your feet, grinning, “Was it as stupid as I heard, father?”

                                          “Fuckin’ nonsense!”, he mutters, voice muffled behind his hands. “Two old, established families, about to go to war because the one family used the other’s cesspit by accident and broke the retaining wall. And, their leaders, instead of sitting down their clan-leaders and smacking some sense into them, egged it on!” He shakes his head, “I need a gods-damned drink.”

                                          You step up to fix it for him, making one for you and looking over your shoulder at Adelmar, offering a peace treaty. He sourly shakes his head. Shrugging, you bring the mead up to his bench, dragging another over.

                                          He drains the cup, then turns to you, “So, how did the scouting go?”

                                          You shrug, “It is just as we were told. On the other side of this ridge is the River of Tears.”

                                          Your father relaxes, something you don’t see him do very often. Resting his head on the tent-pole, he closes his eyes. “Did you see any smoke, any roads, anything that says the land is inhabited?”

                                          You frown, thinking back, “No, but I could only see this bank of the river. The other side is too far away to make out any details. I saw some woods, lots of reeds and marshes….. a couple of villages, but there wasn’t any smoke, so I don’t think they were inhabited.”

                                          “Good, good.” He stays silent for a long time, and all you hear is the crackling of the fire.

                                          “I have a very important job, for the two of you.”

                                          You and your brother lean forward, and he continues, “ It is going to take some time before the rest of the wagon-trains make their way out of the hills and into the valley itself. At least four weeks, by my guessing, and probably more. As important is it is that we don’t get caught with our pants down now, it is far more important that we don’t get ambushed on the other side of the river, or, gods forbid, crossing the damn thing….. which I still don’t know how we are going to do. Build rafts? “

                                          He is silent again, and Adelmar asks, “You are asking us to scout the other side of the River of Tears?” It isn’t really a question.

                                          Your father nods, “Tomorrow, I am giving each of you command of part of my retinue. Figure out some way to cross the River, scout around, and try to figure out just what the hell is going on over there.”

                                          He opens his eyes and sits up, looking at the two of you sternly. “This is, by far, the most crucial task in the history of our people’s migration. I cannot state this enough: All of our efforts will go to naught if I pile 15,000 people onto shitty rafts, only to find out the entire Eastern half of the River of Tears is a mass-grave, or home to something equally awful.” He sighs, “ Each of you will have total command over the mission. No oversight, no advice, and, most importantly …. No relief. Once you cross the river, you will be on your own.”

                                          He looks at each of you, catching your eyes in turn, “Do you understand?”

                                          You reply, “I do, Father.”, and Adelmar nods as calmly as he is able.

                                          “Then go, with my blessing, and do your duty as princes of Swadia. Come here at dawn tomorrow.” With that, he closes his eyes again, deep in thought. After a moment, you stand up, bow respectfully, and leave. Adelmar follows by your heels.

                                          You leave the pavilion and head back to your own tent, thinking hard about what tomorrow will bring. As night falls, and the wind blows cold off the hillsides, you gather together your beaduprat, discussing with them your quickly-made preliminary plans, what you saw from the ridge, as well as what they think the best course of action would be. These plans go on into the night, and you awake bleary-eyed just before dawn with a muttered curse. Getting dressed quickly, you hurry to the royal pavilion.

                                          Once there, you find about 200 of your father’s retinue loosely assembled, gathering their gear and packing food and other supplies into wagons. It only takes an hour or so to get everything squared away (you are good, but your father has been doing this since before you were born), and you set off with little fanfare. Behind you and your retinue is a warband of 100 or so fighters, leading a wagon loaded with food, although you hope to sustain your scouting foray off the land as much as possible. Your father’s only instructions were to return to the landing site in 6 weeks, and mark said site with a pillar of smoke.

                                          As you leave, you take care to give your brother a small nod. As sour as your relationship is, he is still your brother, and you wish him success. To your (pleasant) surprise, he actually returns it, although he looks more than a little distressed.

                                          Almost immediately after cresting the ridge that conceals the royal encampment, your warband starts to descend down into the river valley. Between the two mountain ranges (the ones to your back and the distant ones floating on the horizon), the valley of the River of Tears is broad and flat, with copses of trees and fields of waving grass above the banks, and long, seemingly-never-ending stands of reeds and marshes along the waterside. Upon cursory inspection, the soil appears to be very rich, firm and spongy to the touch and as black as night. You idly wonder out loud to your retainers as to why your people don’t just settle here, on this side of the River, but you already know the answer: your father, and the Althing through him, want a mile-wide river between them and the rest of the North.

                                          You can certainly understand their desire for safety. The few villages you saw yesterday from the ridge are long-abandoned, with the people either drive off from scavengers (on two legs or four, you don’t know, but this region certainly has a lot of wolves.) or dead from disease. The roofs of the houses are long caved-in and fields and streets are filled with weeds and overgrown grass, and what humans you find are long-dead. Clustered in what look to be religious buildings, or huddled in the corners of homes, they are almost omnipresent. You order your scouts and warriors to dig graves as you scout the area, both as a sign of respect to the dead, but also to prevent them from rising up and ripping your guts out. You have never seen the Restless Dead personally, but your father has told you gruesome stories of encountering them in the past, closer to the Threshold, as well as how to mitigate their potential fury. You aren’t taking any chances.

                                          While the silence in long-dead villages and fields is uncomfortable, it is almost preferable to the sound of the wind. It moves and breathes like a living thing, moaning through the branches of the trees, whispering through the grass and reeds, and shrieking through the gaping windows and doors of the buildings. Even though you know it is just the wind, it sets your teeth on edge, and it certainly has an effect on the men of your warband. Jumpy and unsettled, they are as eager as you are to find a way to get across the river.

                                          The one reason you brave the abandoned villages at all is to search for supplies. Not food, you have plenty of that from both what you brought and what you can forage (the land here is unbelievably rich. While the farm-fields and gardens of the villages have long been swallowed up by the forests and grasses, the livestock are roaming wild and seemingly unimpaired by the decline of humanity, asides from the hungry wolves, lynxes and bear. Your foragers bring in feral goat and even cattle with welcome regularity), but supplies for boat/raft building.

                                          Obviously, asides from a few rare exceptions, most of the boats that did exist in the waterside villages have long been rendered unusable through age or neglect, and the small dinghies wouldn’t carry a warband across the mile-wide River of Tears anyways. So, rafts it is! Now, while the Swadians hail from the “interior” of the North, away from the coast, and it has been years since you have seen your cousins among the Eridanian river-traders ….. you are a prince, you can manage to build a gods-damned raft. So, your men tear apart the old buildings for the sturdy beams, cut down the conifers for logs, and cut down entire acres of reeds for floatation. Dividing your warband into teams, it takes about a week to scout the immediate riverbank, as well as build enough shoddy rafts to ferry your warband across the river.

                                          The time has come to push your rafts out into the brackish water of the River of Tears and strike out for the other bank. Standing on the first raft to head out, you take a deep breath and try to calm your nerves, taking care to keep your nervousness hidden from all but your beaduprat. They are nervous as well: Swadians are not a water-going people, more comfortable with both feet on dry land than on a heaving deck. But….. this is an order and a mission directly from the King, and nobody in the warband would dishonor themselves or their families through cowardice.

                                          Looking around at your beaduprat, you give a nod to Hildebald, who pulls a horn from his belt and lifts it to his lips. Blowing a long blast from it, you listen to it recede into the distance, then give the order to the warriors on the raft to push off.

                                          Within but a few minutes, you learn (to your great relief) that the rafts you have ordered built are, well, not shitty enough to tip over and send you to a watery grave. However, the seemingly-placid surface of the River of Tears concealed a hidden fury, and the water rushes quickly beneath your feet. In addition, the wind roars across the water, unimpeded by hills or forests. Ultimately, for every quarter-mile you put between you and the initial shore, a full mile whips by upstream ahead of you.

                                          Well, shit.

                                          There is nothing you can do asides from paddle, and paddle you do. It takes several hours to paddle across the full width of the river, and that was more helped by the fact that the river-water swirled and ebbed, bringing you close to shore of its own accord. Thankfully, all your rafts got swept up in the same current and by the same winds, and didn’t get too separated. It only takes a few hours to bring your entire warband together, at the mouth of a tributary river feeding into the River of Tears.

                                          Unfortunately, there is a problem.

                                          On the banks of this small river, there lie the ruins of an abandoned town, larger than the villages you encountered on the other side of the river. There are a few hours of daylight left, but not enough time to sweep the town for the corpses that are sure to be there, much less dispose of them properly. The land itself is nice and flat, evidently old farmland from the stone walls and old ruts, meaning it will be easy to march through, with hills and forests to the north and south, and mountains rising several miles away to the East.

                                          Sighing, you think on the best course of action:
                                          1. Spend the night in the town. You didn’t encounter a single draugr (Skytongue: restless dead, revenant) on the other side of the River, not a single one, so if your Fate holds out, there won't be any here, either. On top of that, your men are tired from the River-crossing, and it might be beneficial to morale to sleep in an actual godsforsaken building for once. It has been years since you have slept with a hard roof over your head….
                                          2. Push to the north, back to where you originally hoped to land. This was just a little setback, nothing more.
                                          3. Push to the south, where the River decided to take you. The gods work in mysterious ways. Maybe they wanted you to go this way?
