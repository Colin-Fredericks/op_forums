        Originally posted by Rochedo View Post
        Including states, churches, nations and businesses.
        I don't think nationalism has quite been invented yet in the setting.

        Moving on, I'd be fine with a more robust "Bureaucracy system" if these things didn't tend to reduce people to interchangeable cogs in said organizations' machines, kept running smooth regardless of context by the Solars' Bureaucracy Charms. Since that seems to keep happening each time these kinds of rules get introduced (see the Appendices in Storyteller's Guide and Masters of Jade in 2e), I'm happy with the Leadership rules we've got in 3e (the ST decides on the possibility of and how long a project might take, as well as complications that can threaten its success, the player can say how to deal with said complications, and then the success or failure of the project might create further complications).

        Especially given the way that the leadership rules in 2e present a situation that kinda resembles the Hacker Problem in modern RPGs; the one player with Bureaucracy ends up directing the empire's policies, while the other players sit and twiddle their thumbs, waiting for when they can go back to doing stuff. Except where the Decker's player in a game of Shadowrun might take half an hour of the table's time to resolve his hacking, but only have it take eighteen seconds of in-game time, the Eclipse commands the table's time to resolve stuff that takes months of in-game time.

                                                        But basically I think is an argument about semantics because you're talking about different things.

                                                        I think Grod and Alucard are talking about the academic poli-sci definition of Nationalism and Nation-States (which is a distinct ideology), while I think John and Sunder are talking about the non-academic definition where Nation is synonymous with Country or State. Like when you say the Nation of the USA or the Nation of the United Kingdom (neither of which are Nations in the academic and ideological sense, but are clearly things people call Nations, hence the United Nations).


                                                        Saying this, while nationalism might only exist in a proto-form in the Time of Tumult, I can actually see quite an interesting (if morally complex) game about building a (academic-definition) nation. And I can see some parts of that being part of a Bureaucracy system (such as building a... well, bureaucracy. Maybe creating firm borders. Maybe less so spreading a unified group-identity, promoting a particular view of history, etc. Those could be done via bureaucracy I guess... hmmm, I suppose in practice they are).

                                                        (I mean... obviously some would argue that Nation-Building by its very idea is attacking the basis of Nationalism, but Exalted is quite happy to deconstruct ideologies!)

                                                        In fact, one of the PCs in the essence 5 game is doing this. (And it's almost as bad as you'd expect. I mean, there hasn't been any actual genocide, but it's caused a lot of civil and external war, an inquisition, purges of priests and nobles, etc, etc.)



                                                        Anyway...
                                                        Certainly I can see why people argue for a system about running a State. Though I can also see Alucard's worry about it, certainly previous editions had their issues. And I personally enjoy the personalisation of power within a monarchy, in terms of running games. (Ie, you bring your State together by going and socialising with regional nobles, assassinating your enemies, etc. Rather than rolling dice to represent administrative skill. Although of course if you don't what's the point of the ability and charms?)

                                                            Originally posted by wern212 View Post
                                                            I believe the Dev's have said that you can't roll for social actions with Bureaucracy, even bargain?
                                                            If they have then they need to tighten up the Social Influence rules, because the book states "(Charisma, Manipulation, or Appearance) plus a relevant Ability" suggesting it's more the individual Storyteller's call.

                                                            The bargain action is actually based on something Bureaucracy did in 1st edition before 2nd edition made it almost exclusively about organisation management
                                                            Originally posted by 1e Exalted Core
                                                            BRIBES AND BUREAUCRACY Bribery and corruption are the way of the world. Often, characters will not just need to bribe officials to circumvent the law or have a special exception made, but will be forced to pay off officials to get them to do their jobs at all. Paying off an official who wishes to collect money to do her job requires no roll but may be a Resources • to •••• expense, depending on what it is the character wants. Convincing an official to accept a bribe she isn't expecting requires a Manipulation + Bureaucracy roll. The difficulty of this roll depends on the probity of the official and how severe the punishment will he if she is caught accepting the bribe. Obviously, such bribes also have a Resources cost, and the bribing character must he able to pay it. Note that, in many cases, an official who chooses not to accept the bribe will still take the money and simply not honor the agreement.
                                                            Finally, my group used it like that in the playtest and reported such.
