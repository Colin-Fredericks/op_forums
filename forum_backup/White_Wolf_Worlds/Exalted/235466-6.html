    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post

    ... John, you don't come across like you're interested in debate or like you respect my opinion when you start taking shots like that.
    I apologise for any offense caused. My intent was only to contextualize the conversation -- wasn't meant as a personal attack. Likewise I'm sure your points should be read as criticism of the work and not of the writers or fans of the character. I'm notoriously bad for conveying meaning -- just had an argument with someone in Ask a Question whom I agreed with in every respect. So let me kick this off with "my bad", and "I'm sorry".

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    Your points, as far as I can tell, don't remotely track with any of my complaints. But eh. Let's adjourn to that other thread. You've already necromancied it, I feel like cutting loose 100%
    Those points weren't really meant to track with what you said. It was just my take on the character -- didn't want to argue in someone else's active thread.

    That said, I think most of your criticism is valid. You're entitled to your interpretation and opinion. It's different to mine but I respect your right to hold it.

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    So yeah. Bhagwei. Dude's a poorly written jerk and I hate his guts and don't remotely get what you guys see in him.

    Granted, I mostly hate his guts because of his actions as master of the Heptagram. But taking a step back here, let's consider how stupid his story in Aspect Book Wood is on every level. The secret incest baby thing comes off like the writer just discovered Game of Thrones for the first time and was feeling all edgy and hardcore (this is early White Wolf after all ) The most baffling part however is how completely irrelevant it is to Bhagwei's story. It's basically there to give him a claim to the throne... so he can ignore it and be all 'I'm too good for secular power'. You really didn't need incest to give the guy who personally trained almost every active sorcerer in the Realm a claim to the throne. So yeah, not only incest, but stupid pointless incest there to make us be more disgusted at the Dynasty. Yaaaay.
    You're not wrong.

    But why make Mnemon the daughter of the Empress? Why not have her be the second most powerful sorceress and leader of a politically powerful block?

    Because it's a dynasty. In real life as well as fantasy, royal bloodlines and inheritance are important. Political power needs to be backed by a claim. (If we're talking Game of Thrones, Rob became king because he had a blood claim which Ned lacked.)

    And sexual deviance is House Cynis's whole thing (well that and slavery).

    The Dynasty aren't nice people. They're twisted, imperialistic, slaveholders... who are also the foremost heroes of Creation.

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    (It's small potatoes compared to that and what comes next, but the Empress killing her newly healed lover is also very stupid. I get that it's supposed to be subversive, remind us 'oh yeah, the dynasty are the bad guys' but it didn't have that vibe to me at all. It just felt stupid, a capricious act to try and shock me, the reader.)
    The Empress gives her reasoning here.

    She expects the lover to die -- given what we know of Mnemon's lovers, it's probable the Empress poisoned her herself.

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    So yeah. Following that, we get the high crimes stuff. Ragara Bhagwei, dominie of the Heptagram, takes advantage of his position to initiate a sexual relationship with a student, Tepet Oshina. Due to the course of these 'experiments', she is killed.
    Yup. The Dean of Magic College abuses his power and asplodes a student.

    Sex and politics is what a Dynastic game is all about. Throw in a little magic and death and you've got yourself a Heptagram game.

    It's evil. It's full on Guide to the Sabbat, trench-coat and katana evil.

    That's why it's fun.

    It's not fun for everyone. Some people are going to be upset or disturbed by this. Like any group activity, basic human decency means you need to treat the other players with respect when dealing with these sorts of themes.

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    *breath*

    You guys are gonna fan for this? Really?

    Because that's my biggest issue with the point. Yes, it's sodding tasteless in isolation. But the presentation of it is what runs beyond irksome into frothing anger for me. The book seemingly, bafflingly doesn't regard this as a villain moment. There's a sidebar encouraging PCs to continue these experiments. It's one rejoinder is that PCs " should opt to begin their experiments with bisclaverets or neomah as guinea pigs instead of the daughter of a powerful Great House!" The tone reads as comedic to me - as if Bhagwei doing this to the daughter of a Great House was the one mistake he made. Bhagwei is not an obviously unreliable narrator like Deled, he's presented as more detached and analytical, and he's given an excuse that Oshina hadn't done the preparations he'd asked of her. Further the text sets her up with mild slutshaming, Bhagwei noting her "appetite for rich, old Exalts near death from old age". Painting her as a gold digger to try and ensure the reader isn't sympathetic to her fate.

    The whole passage isn't written like the act of a villain, it's just 'a thing that happened'. And yeah, that's kinda opinion. But look at the OP.
    The Solars aren't Lawful Good to Realm Chaotic Evil.

    But the Realm are the default bad guys. Your Realm PCs are evil and need evil plot hooks.

    "My Realm PC isn't evil! They just benefit from a system of slavery and war!"

    Well good news! This sex magic isn't just exploding people for fun -- it's a practical study of mysticism!


    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    And I'm not trying to get at Omicron here, just use their words for illustrative effect. That's absolutely how this bit was written. It's not given gravity akin to Deled's fanaticism, or Lahor's brutality. Bhagwei is presented as a 'good Dynast', fighting Abyssals and being aloof from politics.
    Bhaghwei was written to appeal to the people he appeals to. He's exactly what I want from a mythic hero in a game with questionable morality.

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    Oh, and lest we forget, he then proceeds to skin a little girl and wear her as magic clothing.
    Better or worse than a Lunar ripping out her heart to drink of its blood?

    Originally posted by Croakamancer View Post
    ... I'd be lying if I said that disgusts me as much as I find it... grimly hilarious, in the 'wise old sorcerer' writeup. It definitely helps me not be able to take him seriously as any kind of positive, goodly mentor figure? I'd rather use Sesus Chenow in that role - at least I can try and argue old age mellowed him out a bit.
    That's how magic works.

    Want to summon a Blood Ape? Murder a cat. Want a courtesan? Trade flesh for flesh. Looking to destroy your enemies? Rain poison down upon the land.

    A "wise old sorcerer" is a lot closer to Voldemont than Dumbledore.

    I want my characters morally ambiguous at best. Especially my Dynasts. The things that seem to turn you off are the things needed to counterbalance Bhagwei so he doesn't outshine the PCs.

    Bhagwei the White as a shining paragon of good, curing the great contagion, teaching Dynasts to use sorcery responsibly, solving crises between peach bites, with a good claim to the throne? Blah.

                    Bhagwei is interesting because he manages to do some fairly disgusting and horrifying things, while honestly being almost certainly a net force for good in the world. The book outright admits that he is a megalomaniac with a messiah complex, but his whole thing is curing diseases. Really, how many elder Dynasts are there who would personally visit a small town to cure all the diseased mortals? You probably wouldn't want to hang around him if you weren't sick, though.

                    Skinning the Shoat of the Mire is a pretty disturbing thing to do, but let us keep in mind that from his perspective, he was dealing with a monstrous demon that merely happened to resemble a little girl. It's fucking gross, but who did he hurt exactly? (I mean, other than the Shoat herself. But he and his bodyguards were hardly obligated to concern themselves with the deathknight's tragic backstory while she was trying her damnedest to kill them.) From a detached perspective (probably normal for Exalted sorcerers), he's just making use of available resources after winning that fight.

                    Killing one of his students with careless tantric experiments is pretty goddamn inexcusable, though. I'm guessing the Scarlet Realm doesn't have the same taboos against sex between people in imbalanced power situations that we do IRL...
