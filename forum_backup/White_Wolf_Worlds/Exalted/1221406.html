    How long did it last?
    Who were the major Heroes and Villains?
    What were the major Factions?
    What famous events occurred?
    What, if any, important changes happened in this era?
    Was it "global" or localized?
    and most importantly...

      Let's talk about the Shogunate, bay-be; let's talk about you and me... :P

      Well, you know there's little firm canon on this, right? The Shogunate has some stuff in the Aspect books, but it's deliberately fragmented. Because getting players to piece together setting elements rather than having something coherent is fun. :P

      The Shogunate 'canonically' I believe lasted about 500 years? Somewhat less than that, it was brought up in another thread recently. I prefer to think it went closer to a millenium: one thousand years of rule is just a fun figure. Not much changes for the timeframe.

      Heroes/Villains/Factions for the Shogunate itself probably shifted a lot. It's gonna be pretty intricate about specifics, lots of independent lords and governors all manoeuvring against each other while a Shogun tries to rally them into a more unified whole. You're likely to have pro unity elements who favour a stronger role for the Blessed Isle and its bureaucracy, and separatists, who want as much independence for local lords as possible. Who is good and who is bad probably depends on both era and perspective. You can have a tyrannical shogun trying to extend his grip across the land, or a weary one who wants his squabbling children to unite to deal with something serious. All perspective.

      Famous Events... well, I believe Arms establishes some kinda revolt by fire elementals at one point? Beyond that, we don't know much. Which frustrates me: the Shogunate isn't supposed to be a lost era in the way the First Age was, yet we have little idea of how it shaped the map. The inspiration suggests that there was lots of internal fighting, but AFAIK we have very little information on that, even in the Aspect books. The closest thing I can remember was one Shogun getting assassinated by a youth movement loyal to his son.

      Important changes... well, it's the solidifying of the Dragon Blooded as the Rulers of Creation in most people's minds.It leaves a wider legacy than the Realm, and since it's centuries in the past, I imagine some people are nostalgic for it, even outside of successor states like Lookshy and Calin.,

      Global, but not in a unified sense. It affected everyone, but the degree and nature of that effect varied. Some areas were caught up in outright wars, others were probably managed by sleepy eyed daimyo wh Don't Want No Trouble.

      And the Shogunate can provide almost anything the First Age can. It was still a time of wonder, with a unified well trained Dragon Blooded host in command of marvels. Relics of a lost time, adventure sites, a historical origin for an area's political situation, all sorts of things.

              It's a hassle at the moment for me to go back to my Aspect books for the specifics, but I can answer some things fairly broadly.

              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post

              How long did it last?
              Writing on that has been inconsistent, but the current developers seem to want to stick to a figure of a bit less than 500 years.

              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord
              Who were the major Heroes and Villains?
              It depends on what you mean by "major", but between the Aspect books and Arms of the Chosen, we've got examples of particular Dragon Blooded doing noteworthy things such as going into battle with Fair Folk invasions, creating exceptional works of magical art, forging powerful weapons, desperately attempting to negotiate for the cooperation of gods (and sometimes turning to demons when that doesn't work out) and engaging in strange experiments with human beings. Plus the Sidereals observing the whole situation and trying to keep things together.

              ​On the "villainous" side, apart from those Dragon Blooded that would be in rebellion against the government, there would have also been personalities among the aforementioned Fair Folk, gods with particularly unpleasant conditions for their service, and Third Edition can add the most extreme of the Silver Pact Lunars.

              ​
              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord
              What were the major Factions?
              ​One of the Aspect books has a noteworthy excerpt describing how a group of generals assassinated a shogun in protest of his policy of trying to compromise with rebellious regional governors rather than bring them in line with the central administration. It's a protest rather than a coup, because the generals in question immediately surrender themselves and are prepared to commit suicide, and the policy in question is also one that the shogun's successor happens to disagree with. That's an example of a political division that might have occurred during the period.

              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord
              What famous events occurred?
              I think one particular one was a raksha breakthrough in a certain Southern city. It was a place in which Shogunate policies about water distribution had been causing tension for some time beforehand, to the point where people were openly distributing anti-establishment pamphlets, and ultimately performing reality destabilizing rituals in the streets. When the Fair Folk came through, numbering about a million, they actually had collaborators among the human population, doing things like sabotaging communication centres and skyship docking stations. The Shogunate attempts to contain the invasion were described as having extreme casualties, including more than a hundred Exalted equipped with Dragon Armour. It was described as being the largest breakthrough in a hundred years, in terms suggesting that smaller ones happened frequently.

              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord
              What, if any, important changes happened in this era?
              Do you mean within the era?

              ​Hmmm... I've often inferred the presence of policies intended to bring mortal populations increasingly under the control of the Exalted and harnessing them for hard labour in an attempt to keep systems going in the absence of the First Age's magic. Also, even before the Great Contagion, economic breakdowns are periodically causing portions of the world to disintegrate entirely.

              Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord
              How can it affect my current game?
              ​Probably mostly in cultural background for people and practices, similar to what House Tepet has (as well as unnamed cadet Houses). Perhaps occasionally as a basis for stories with which one can consider their current plans and actions. And a source of clues about where the look for Artifacts.

                  It's a deliberate blank spot in the time-line for the most part. We know roughly the shape of the political situation, a few curt references to events that were important at the time, and that's about it. It serves the same function for Dragonbloods as the First Age does for Solars: a golden age of lost wonders when they were united (nominally) as a political body and their rule spread over the length and breadth of Creation, before a calamity brought it crashing down.

                  It can affect your campaign exactly as much or as little as you want. It's a source for lost manses and artifact caches that isn't the First Age. It's the historical narrative that unites and divides the Realm and Lookshy. It's when a few of the popular operas playing in the Imperial City right now are set (and/or were written). To those with no knowledge of the First Age, it is the mythic past. To scholars who know better, it's a hotly debated political clusterfuck.

                  If you're a history buff and feel like fleshing it out, you could plunder the Warring States period for historical figures and events and translate them into dragonblooded feuds. That's something I'd love to see but don't have time for right now. If I ever get around to running the early-Realm dragonblooded game I've had kicking around in my head for years, I'll flesh it out for my campaign since it'll be within living memory for dragonbloods. Until then, I'm happy with it being an unconnected series of mostly local events that explain cool shit that crops up in my games.

                  "Measure of Hope is right about everything." - Wise Old Guru

                  Currently running an Exalted 2.5 Abyssals game in a homebrew modern shard because I value neither my time or my sanity, and I'm loving almost every minute of it.

                      I believe the writing on the history of Marama's Fell takes place in the Shogunate. In the aftermath of the Usurpation, the created races of the previous Deliberative were suspect for continued loyalty to the fallen Solars. So instead of doing something reasonable, the Shogun/ate decided that genocide was the answer. Unsurprisingly, a shadowland resulted. It sheds light on how paranoid even the victorious Dragon-Blooded were in the early Shogunate, and as for affecting current games, resolving that ancient atrocity could be key to healing a scar upon Creation that has lasted through the Time of Turmoil.

                                  The disposal of Anathema “pets” and slaves has proven more complex than we originally anticipated. While it was easy enough to dispose of the obviously nonhuman ones or those who attempted to defend their masters and mistresses, we eventually found ourselves faced with problems of, to be frank, pure morality. Moral dilemmas came up when considering the unwilling slaves (or those who claimed to have been taken against their will) and the near-human beastmen who had been bred in servitude. Simply slaughtering them wholesale would have been morally reprehensible. Having the troops put them to the sword would have been setting a poor ethical precedent and creating butchers rather than warriors. We want our soldiers to kill on command, but having them slaughter innocent creatures who look mostly human and who are screaming for mercy — well, anyone can see the potential problems there.
                                  We were faced with a difficult situation, however. The battles with the Anathema had damaged a lot of arable farmland, and often, their stored food and grain had been destroyed along with their Manses or looted by the mobs, despite the military’s best attempts to maintain public order. As such, releasing large numbers of slaves into the general population — especially when most of them were only trained for military or personal service and, therefore, would have been little use in farming or harvesting — was decided by our commanders to be unwise. There was also, and I admit that I had strong feelings on this point, the question of the beastmen breeding with the normal humans in the area. We managed to resolve matters to some extent. The beastmen were seconded to frontier territory garrisons, where I understand there have been some incursions by the Fair Folk. Their leaders have been given some guarantees about territory and founding their own tribes, but frankly, I wouldn’t be surprised if they all die tragically with arrows in their backs. We both know how this sort of thing works out. Of the human slaves and servants, the ones who’d been trained as soldiers were drafted into the army and will receive full citizenship after a decade’s service. The concubines, minions and the like were divided among officers and staff with the position to warrant personal retainers. In some ways, it’s the last that actually disturbs me the most. How can we guarantee that these apparently harmless servants aren’t actually traps waiting to be sprung? You and I both know that the Anathema were capable of twisting a person’s mind like paper between their fingers. You know better than I how many of their toys and tools have proven to be booby-trapped in some way, usually lethally. Your division’s already lost several sorcerers.

                                                  Due to the way mortal lifespans and Dragon-blooded lifespans work, through generational anecdotes the Shogunate is simultaneously as far removed from the current setting as WW1 or the Holy Roman Empire are from current life. My Grandpa used to tell me stories about where he was when JFK was shot (I actually live in Dallas, he was actually there at the parade grounds), now imagine that was the assassination of Archduke Ferdinand, or Abraham Lincoln, or Louis XVI.

                                                  With Tepet dead and the Empress missing I don't think, other than a few Lunars and Sidereals, there are any Exalts alive who experienced the Shogunate, but we are sometimes only one or two generations separated from those events. Granted there was a cataclysm or two that makes this transfusion of information from person to person a little less certain, but if you assume one or two max DB lifepans, there could be someone whose grandfather was there during the equivalent of the discovery of America.
