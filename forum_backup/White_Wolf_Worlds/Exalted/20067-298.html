                            There were sort of two layers of perfects.

                            The simplest was "always win a contest". So if you made an attack, that attack would automatically succeed, because the guy can't successfully dodge it no matter how high his DDV (often it would specify something like 'succeeds with 0 threshold successes'). Unless he had a charm which meant he always succeeded when trying to dodge.

                            The next was "render something inapplicable/succeed even if it is inapplicable". So in this case the defender couldn't even try to dodge it because it's an inapplicable defense and his "always successfully dodge" charm wouldn't help ... unless he had a charm which allows you to dodge what cannot be dodged. This is what most people mean when they talk about perfects, as it's the scale that Solars tend to work at.

                                            09-01-2016, 11:12 PM
                                            I have a feeling there are more than two potential STs here, but that's neither hither nor thither. If it were my table, I'd say that it'd be more practical to have a single Intimacy with multiple contexts, only breaking them up by intensity. So someone might have Defining Ties to X in a given set of contexts (e.g. Hatred, Wary Respect), Major Ties in another set (Lust, Obsession), and Minor Ties in another (Admiration, Fear). This allows you a wide variety of applicability without redundant filler.

                                            The key to Intimacy definition is that the object of the Intimacy must itself bear on the subject's life in some significant fashion. So while X might elicit feelings of Admiration, Lust, etc., is it a specific action that X performed that creates the ongoing influence in the subject's life, or is it the idea of X herself? It seems to me that the influence comes from the latter, which is why I'm forwarding the condensed Intimacies with multiple contexts approach. And for the record, that's an approach that another ST suggested to me first; I'm just breaking it down here.
