            You join the back of the line. You wait.

            Every now and then, the line shuffles forward.

            Your stomach growls. The people ahead of you in line chatter-it’s a mother and her two children, both only about four or five years old. They keep whining about how slow the line is, and she keeps shushing them and telling them to be grateful it’s raining, otherwise the line would be longer.

            Speaking of rain, you are cold. And wet. And the rain keeps pushing your hair down into your eyes so you have to wipe it away. You wish you had an umbrella or something. A couple of the people ahead of you in live have beat up old parasols to keep the rain off, and one guy has a heavy coat he’s got draped over his head.

            You wait.

            The line shuffles forward, a little bit at a time. You consider again how easy it would be to just take your new knife and force someone to give you a rice ball after they leave…but no. You’ve had your food stolen before, you’d feel guilty…and perhaps more importantly, you’re not sure you could get away with it. Now you’ve been in line bored for a while, you notice that this area appears to have some guardians-there are a couple local mercenaries idling under awnings out of the rain, and a couple monks chatting with them. Probably not a good place to start something.

            The line shuffles forward. Your stomach growls. The interminable wait continues.

            Finally, after what seems like hours, you reach the gates of the monastery. The sun is low in the West and the shadows are growing longer. The sky is a strange dull yellow-orange color, as the brilliant sunset on the other side waddles its way through heavy rainclouds and thick Nexus smog. You enter the gates of the Immaculate Monastery.

            Inside, there is a large courtyard and a couple of tables. Large red-orange umbrellas shield huge pots of sticky swamp rice from the pouring rain, and two old bald men and a middle aged bald woman in Immaculate robes are handing out rice balls. There’s still a lot of courtyard to cross, but you’re inside the Gates! They’re probably not going to kick you out now without feeding you!

            The line is still moving slowly, so you take in your surroundings. Past the tables are the buildings of the Immaculate Temple. There are still no statues, but the Temple is adorned with gleaming bronze and gold inlays that remind you of shimmering scales, and you can make out the bright colors of tapestries inside. There are several large trees surrounding the courtyard close to the walls that’d make climbing in or out pretty easy. There are also some well-trimmed bushes that’d be good for hiding. As you wait in line, you watch a young monk trimming them with a pair of clippers. There are about a dozen young men and women in the courtyard sitting cross-legged in the rain meditating in front of the temple. Their heads aren’t shaved.

            You arrive at the table where the food is being handed out. All this time waiting in line you’ve been thinking, and now you’re in front of them you decide to ask. As you reach the middle aged bald woman you thank her for the food and offer a prayer to the Dragons…and then ask her a question.

            “Pardon me, Ma’am, but…how do I become an Immaculate Monk like you?”

            She gives you a big smile (ignoring the frustrated groans of the old woman behind you in line) and puts a rice ball in your hand.

            “It’s not easy,” she tells you. “It’s a long and hard road, and most give up on it. What makes you want to become a monk?”

            What do you tell her?

            Lie: “Sextes Jylis and the monks is the only ones who ever really looked out for me. The Dragons are the only family I’ve got.”

            Truth: “I already never get to eat any meat, and sex is dumb. And you folks seem to eat pretty well, and your temple is all rich and fancy."

            Truth: "It seems like a better life than being a beggar."

            Truth: "It seems like a better life than being a prostitute."

            Truth: "It seems like a better life than being a thief."

            Lie: "It seems like a better life than being a thief."

            Lie: “I really want to help people, but I can’t afford to do anything for anyone. Becoming a monk seems like a way to be able to help people. Like giving out rice!”

            Truth: “I really like coming to weekly sermons and hearing stories about the Dragons, I love when they read out of the Immaculate Texts to us! I want to learn to read them for myself!”

            Lie: “My dad got killed when the river flooded last year. But I heard you Monks beat up the river god and stopped the flood and saved everyone!”

            Truth: “My dad got killed when the river flooded last year. But I heard you Monks beat up the river god! I wanna be able to beat up gods and control the weather!”

            Lie: “Aunt Dwi is real mean and beats me and makes me work at her brothel. But if I stay here at the Monastery, I’ll be safe!”

            Truth: “I..I don’t think I’m good enough to be anything else.”

            Truth: “Mom died of the Black Lung three years ago, and I don’t really have anywhere else to go.”

            Truth: “I want to learn martial arts! Wo-Pah!

            Lie: “I need help with my meditation. Going without food and suffering is supposed to make your more enlightened, but it just makes me tired and hungry. I’m never going to achieve enlightenment like this!”

            Truth: “If I’m in training to become a monk I’ll get food and a dry place to sleep and learn to read and write and treat the sick and do martial arts! Even if I change my mind in a couple years, I can use those skills to get a real job!”

                                            You take a deep breath.

                                            “Mom died of the Black Lung three years ago, and I don’t really have anywhere else to go. And, yeah, I want to learn martial arts and keep gods in line so they don't kill people like they killed my dad. And I like your stories, and enlightenment sounds really good, but I got no idea how to get there. Going without food and suffering is supposed to make you more enlightened, but it just makes me tired and hungry. I’m never going to achieve enlightenment like this! And if I’m in training to become a monk I’ll get food and a dry place to sleep and learn to read and write and treat the sick and do martial arts! Even if I change my mind in a couple years, or I just can't cut it, I can use those skills to get a real job, instead of winding up a beggar or a thief or a whore. Or worse!"

                                            “Oh, shut it,” the old woman behind you growls, shoving you so you splash down into the mud and grabbing a rice ball. “Sun’s getting low and people back here’s gotta eat! Get out of the way!” There are grumbles of agreement from the people behind you in line.

                                            The middle aged monk you were speaking to chuckles and comes out from behind the table. A young bald man steps in from behind her to take her place handing out rice. She pulls you to your feet and walks you away from the line.

                                            “Working in solitude and striving to surpass your lot in life, you draw away from the perfection of the Elemental Dragons. Only by working together and accepting their present incarnations,” and here she gestures at the people who were in line behind you, “can all beings mimic the Elemental Dragons and so approach their perfection. That is the wisdom of the Third Noble Insight.”

                                            You nod your head in what you hope is an approximation of understanding. “See, that’s what I’m talking about! How am I ever going to get enlightened like you without somebody to teach me?”

                                            She shakes her head. “Don’t worry about achieving enlightenment, child. It takes many lifetimes. Instead, strive to help others. You seek to better your station in life by becoming a monk-you seek food and shelter and knowledge and power. But the way of the Dragons is not about improving your lot in life, or even improving your birth in the next life. It is about learning to serve the rightful order of Creation, about helping others. Don’t assume that you must be a monk or a whore. You can be many things, even without learning. A humble street-sweeper or painter or rat-catcher can find holiness in service to others.”

                                            “…so…I can’t become a monk?”

                                            She gives you a warm smile, puts her arm on your shoulder, and walks with you ask she speaks. “I never said that. If you wish to devote yourself to the Order you will need to receive permission from the monks of the Procession of the Gray. You can see the men and women here in the courtyard meditating in the rain? Some of them have been meditating there for weeks. Only a handful will be chosen. They have forsworn all worldly possessions and will leave Nexus wearing only simple robes.”

                                            She gestures to her own robe and sash. It doesn’t seem all that simple to you. It’s a nice hemp robe with a red linen sash.

                                            “They will travel by ship to the Blessed Isle to present themselves to the Paragon of Sextes Jylis. In her courtyard they will wait without food and without sleep until she notices them and invites them to join the Order. There are thousands waiting there, in rain or snow, and many are never chosen…and the Imperial City is no friendly place to foreigners who have failed to impress the Paragon of Sexted Jylis with their virtue.”

                                            She frowns for a moment, lost in thought, and her hand idly goes to a scar you hadn’t noticed on her arm. She closes her eyes, and then looks down at you seriously.

                                            “You should not go. It is very rare for Sextes Jylis to accept anyone as young as yourself-children are not often wise enough to understand the commitment they are making. But if you return in a few years, after you have tasted wine and red meat and love, and you still wish to become a monk, then I will be happy to recommend you to the Procession of the Gray.”

                                            “What’s your name, then?,” you ask her. “ I’ll want to know who to ask for if I come back.”

                                            “I’m Banked Coals,” she tells you. “And now it is time for you to go home.”

                                            You realize she has led you to the front gate. “But…I don’t have one!”

                                            “I am sorry,” she says. “But if you have truly gotten by for three years on your own, you are strong enough to get by for a few more. The winters are always the hardest, and you’ve managed at least two. I’m afraid we need to save our spare beds for the very old and the very young, and the ill who cannot care for themselves, child.”

                                            You frown. “Well…thanks for the rice ball, anyway.”

                                            “You are welcome to it. And if you truly wish to learn, please keep coming to our weekly sermons. It is good for the soul to read the Texts together…and if you do ever find yourself tempted towards unworthy behavior, consider the stories of Sextes Jylis. They were a great help to me in my youth.”

                                            And then you are outside the monastery. You are about to say something else to Banked Coals-but you are interrupted by a great cry from the crowd on your right. The gates are closing, and many left outside weren’t able to get in and get rice in time. The monks are apologizing and telling them there will be more food tomorrow…but they are closing the gates nonetheless, and there are large burly monks with staves eyeing the crowd for troublemakers.

                                            Well.

                                            At least you got your rice ball.

                                            What now?

                                            Go Give The Rice Ball Away: Banked Coals speech has moved you! You’re hungry, sure, but there are little kids and old people in line who didn’t get fed. You can give your rice to somebody who needs it more. You’ve still got a rainy muddy night ahead and some fishhooks, you can catch fish or birds, and there’s always Aunt Dwi to fall back on…helping someone else is what Sextes Jylis would do!

                                            Scarf Your Rice Ball Down Real Quick And Get The Hell Out of Here: Before somebody less noble comes and takes it away from you!

                                            Start Your Stakeout: Becoming a monk and getting free lodging and food every day was a bust. But you got a good look at the inside of the Temple while Banked Coals was walking you through the courtyard, and those trees look pretty climbable. If you find a good vantage point to look down into the courtyard from and see where people are moving around, you should be able to figure out what areas are less well-guarded so you can rob the temple under cover of darkness.

                                            Find Someplace to Sleep: It is now dusk and you’re a long way from your safe nest back in the Nighthammer District. You’re not going to try and rob anybody, but you’re not going to try and head back home until morning. There’s raptor cats and worse out in Nexus after dark!

                                            Head Home: This isn’t your neighborhood. Back in Nighthammer at least you know all the dangers and how to avoid them. The trip back to Blackhand Foundry isn’t going to be easy…but it’s safer to keep moving and hopefully get home in a couple hours than stay in unfamiliar territory all night.

                                            Get More Food: Rice ball or no, you’re still hungry! Dusk is when the fish bite, so you should gather some worms and head down to the canal. Maybe if you offer one of the boatmen half of anything you catch they’ll give you a ride back to Nighthammer. A few of them were friends of your father, after all…

                                            Something Else: The night is young!
