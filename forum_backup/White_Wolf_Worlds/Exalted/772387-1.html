                Sure they can be unmotivated. They can be generally unmotivated or lounging around between engagements with no driving force behind them other than "have a good time". Maybe the newly exalted fire aspect ran away from the buck ogre attacking her home village instead of fighting it, maybe the air aspect was willing to just stay married to the sky god that ruled over his people that requires the most handsome youths to be offered to him every year. Maybe the water aspect is perfectly content with spending her days and nights gambling in Nexus, only working long enough to have more money to gamble - the only reason society won't call her a wastrel is because is because some people who have ended up mysteriously drowned. Maybe the wood aspect is perfectly happy being a Guild Factor's concubine with no greater aspirations, the factor is perfectly willing to drape her arm candy with jewelry that matches the flower blossoms that grow in his hair.

                It's just that "Cowardly" isn't the same as "unmotivated". Being brave is not really a requirement for exaltation. It's not like in 1st or 2nd edition there was a minimum Valor requirement for exaltation. I've always assumed that, considering how much pressure the children of the Realm go through, that most children that survive to exaltation age aren't exactly what you'd call meek. I mean, based on 2e's minimums, every single one of them is at the minimum, educated to a degree most of Creation is not, they are trained in courtly manners and maneuvering and they've been trained to not only defend themselves with blade and bow and fist but to lead others in war. Lookshy's people have even more stringent requirements for their children.

                I find it difficult to believe that anyone who has spent their formative years being pushed so far and so hard to have that particular level of skill when they are 18 years old will be very unmotivated. Their society is set up to produce young men and women who have the same skills as Alexander the Great, even if they aren't backed with the blessing of the elemental dragons. For those that are, they will very likely be swept up by the ambitions of their more motivated peers - an unmotivated exalted dynast is a good 1 dot Ally for a PC Terrestrial.

                        This is a really old and really contentious issue. I suspect that in 3e, Essence Fever will address it to an extent, but there's no perfect answer. It's a matter of which assumptions you want to violate. The problem comes from the combination of the following:

                        1. For a DB with perfect breeding, all their children will Exalt.
                        2. To be a candidate for Exaltation, you need to be intrinsically worthy in some way, even before you Exalt.
                        3. Therefore, DBs with perfect breeding never produce worthless children. Their genes are just that awesome!

                        This leads to unfortunate implications -- it's one thing to say that your genes give you superpowers, but it's another to say that they prevent you from ever producing lazy, cowardly, unworthy heirs. It also discards a major part of the issues with real-world inheritance; when the Immaculate Faith says that DBs are intrinsically worthy to rule because they are superior people (rather than just having more raw power), they're not supposed to be right! The idea of superpowered genetics always carries a risk of unfortunate implications, but this particular issue with the idea of Exalted as "worthy" on some level combined with the idea that, under some circumstances, the children of DBs will always Exalt tends to throw it in stark relief.

                        The best solution is probably just to ignore the issue -- to not call attention to it -- because any solution is going to break some key aspect of the setting or involve stating something undesirable. But you can finesse it in a few ways. You can wrestle with the concept of "worthy". You can say that the process of Exaltation and Essence Fever tend to shock even the most slovenly heir into something more grand. Or you can discard the idea that it was ever true that all children of DBs would Exalt -- turn it into a mythological thing that was never really accurate, a legend of a time when people were better and the world was whole and everyone was in their proper place and so on.

                        It still sits on an awkward intersection of nature-vs-nurture, though. Most other Exalted are candidates because they're worthy, so it doesn't come up outside of in-character fretting over whether you were really worthy or why, which can be suitably ambiguous (and Sidereals are centered around Fate, which makes the distinction of nature vs. nurture totally meaningless.) But if you take the various things said about DBs at face value, it makes the statement that "your genes can make you so fundamentally worthy that it is impossible for someone with genes like yours to be unworthy."

                        You can also just roll with that statement, of course, and focus instead on what "worthy" means and whether it really gives the DBs the rights they say it does. But it's awkward.

                        (Incidentally, 1e had some bits that implied that the "Essence Fever" explanation was the truth and that DBs could be average people pre-Exaltation -- Mnemon, for instance, in one of the Aspect Books, is described as having been average and shy prior to Exaltation and as having changed radically in personality afterwards. But this answer has its own issues.)

                                        Squandering your potential isn't the same thing as not being ambitious or not doing things.

                                        Like, if you want to keep a Dragon-Blood from doing anything of particular note, you might get him into the equivalent of extreme sports or video games or thrill-seeking on the Threshold, but trying to get him to just sit around eating bon-bons and screwing whores all day is probably going to be a hell of a lot of trouble - his nature is to strive, and if the thing he has to strive toward is more bon-bons and whores by gum he is going to go whole hog with it! (Hence where you get the Sesus Nagazzer in the middle of spectacular debauchery going "I am so bored right now" meme)

                                        Exaltation pushes you toward extremes - if you're a mighty general, you're going to be a spectacular mighty general. If you're a cunning dick, you're going to be a spectacular cunning dick. If you're a fuck-up, you're going to be a spectacular fuck-up. A Dragon-Blooded who is straight-up a failure will approach Carly Fiorina levels of failure. (That's a lot.)

                                        I would say there are definitely things Dragon-Bloods are lazy as fuck about and in those things you would be flabbergasted with the extent of their laziness. You might even be impressed at the sheer chutzpah of their laziness (like the one story about the navy captain who radioed a new ship heading so the sun would stop being in his eyes at breakfast).

                                        And if you actually want a Dragon-Blood to sit down and not bother the Empress? I would say that's actually something that happens regularly in the Realm, but the constantly-striving nature of the Exaltation means that controlling the more unsavory parts of the Dynasty is an exercise in continually shoveling food into the mouth of a ravenous Dragon. If you want to keep a DB busy with debauchery they're never going to be satisfied, so you have to throw more delights and more exotic fare at them constantly so they'll stay out of your way. That actually makes the Realm a worse empire than an ordinary bunch of rich decadent elites constantly trying to one-up each other in resource expenditure, because the hunger only gets worse with time.

                                        Until the Empress disappears, and the Dragon starts eating itself.

                                                As with all issues of definitions of heroism, analysis of phenotypes, or... basically anything to do with human beings, diversity is your friend. "Hey, is this Exalted person worthy/well bred"? "Well, they're Chosen, so within the boundaries of our limited understanding of how that works... maybe." "But they have significantly different personality and phenotype traits from certain other Exalted people!" "I suppose the logical conclusion is that there's significant variety to what constitutes worthiness and good breeding, and that it's such a complex, intricate thing that trying to distil it down to some singular, essential traits is an exercise in futility." "But then, how am I supposed to make a singular label with which to make my club exclusive along lines of personal bias and familiarity?!" "You're heading a right way for a smacked bottom".

                                                        Hmm, the more I think about this "in the blood" thing... not just here, but in any kind of old timey "the daughter of a traitor is probably treacherous as well"... the issue isn't really the assertion of personality traits as arising from genetic predispositions whatsoever, it's just the assumption that these are simplistic, singular things that always occur to the same degree and never have any circumstantial and environmental input. I mean, genetics is already operating at a level far below the factors that produce what we perceive as personality, but to the extent that it does in the form of the guidance and management it provides to the development of our neurology and general anatomy (since at any given time our moods can be affected by, say, input from our musculature or digestive tract, which are themselves dependent on genetically guided construction and management), it's a far more complex and versatile thing than certain Metal Gear Solid-isms. What I'm getting at is that even if one assumed something approximately similar to genetics as being the basis for that Exaltation, it would hardly simplify the issue. Even then... all of the Dragon Blooded from the most reliable group in Creation exist in the same social context, how can one isolate any external factors that may be contributing there? Are any Dynasts going to volunteer to give up children so that you can raise them in complete isolation, such that you can thoroughly determine whether or not the causes of Exaltation are purely internal? How many test subjects would you even need to ensure it was comprehensive? How can you be sure that the isolation itself could not be an environmental factor that provokes certain forms of heroism? There are so many variables...
