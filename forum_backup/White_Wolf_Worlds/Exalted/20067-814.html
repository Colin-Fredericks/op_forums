    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    Similarly "re-roll 6s" is literally just re-roll 6s, etc.


    I have a couple of questions about the social system, specifically about intimacies supporting social influence, which doesn't seem precisely explained (unless I can't find it).

    People often say you need "tag" a specific intimacy. Ie you need to say "I'm using his intimacy of I Love Dragonblood to persuade him to help", you can't just throw a social influence and hope they've got something applicable. Or even that you can literally only do this when you actually know their Intimacy (via Profile Character or something). But I can't actually see that anywhere. It just says "When a character is targeted by an influence roll that opposes one or more of his Intimacies, add a bonus to his Resolve based on the intensity of the most applicable Intimacy. On the other hand, if one or more of a character’s Intimacies supports an influence roll applied against him, then he suffers a corresponding penalty to Resolve based on the most applicable Intimacy." And that "Without exploiting an Intimacy, persuasion isn’t possible." So I can't actually see anything that says you need to know what their intimacies are.


    Secondly, there doesn't really seem to be much deeper explanation of what "Intimacies support an influence roll" means. For decision points, it says "In the Decision Point, the player must choose an Intimacy and explain how it justifies resisting that specific influence.... If those requirements are met, and if the Storyteller accepts the player’s argument for why one of his Intimacies would
    make him reject the influence..."
    And then there's a whole long example of using different intimacies. But intimacies supporting an influence roll don't seem to have the same level of detail asked; does this mean you don't have to justify it as much? Or they just didn't bother writing it? Is there some part of the social rules I'm missing?
    For your first question : I think it varies between ST, but I consider the player doesn't have to "know" the intimacy, but it needs to be present in its argumentation. If you're trying to obtain something from the local Prince, and everyone knows he is very proud, and you didn't profile him, you can still try to convince him by praising him and hoping he has an intimacy of "I have a high self-esteem", even though you never checked if he actually had it, but your argumentation has to present how you flatter him and try to play on his arrogance.

    For your second question : I think this is the broadest I've seen between players. Some will be extremely flexible, some will be not flexible at all, sometimes the ST will rule that an intimacy apply when the player do not think it applies, etc ... I think it really depends on who you are playing with. I think the best solution is usually to directly explain on what intimacy you are relying on to convince the person (or supposed intimacy), and then the defending player and the ST have to discuss if it applies (in case it's not obvious).

    But this can lead to very strange cases : For example, a player was arguing to someone with an intimacy of "Peace must be preserved (Defining)" that, in order to preserve peace for a specific location, he had to declare war on some opposing country adjacent to it. We all had a headache trying to figure out if the character could be convinced to act by this argument, and none of us was acting in bad faith or trying to exploit the system, it was just a logical situation that happened in the game

        My reading is that you do not have to know about an Intimacy to target it. But also that successful social influence isn't Obvious in-character.

        Out-of-character there's a degree of system transparency. But IC you are going to want to be Reading Intentions.

        And if my players started metagaming Intimacies rather than trying to work them out in play, I'd hit them with the unexpected.

        I would apply the Decision Point ruling to influence in general. Otherwise you get weird sideways "technically your intimacy is that you love your wife, not that you don't want your wife murdered in front of you".
