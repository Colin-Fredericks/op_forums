                                                My internet went down, so I didn't get round to responding to this, but there's probably some interesting things to discuss.

                                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                                I would not be surprised if Sidereals literally end up with a social Charm to get people to take seriously something that hasn't happened yet.
                                                That would be very useful.

                                                But there's barely a hundred of them, and it's probably easier for a god that a Sidereal never got around to talking to to rationalize not taking them seriously.
                                                True, but in this case I was thinking about the Dragonblooded, of whom there were thousands to convince, and who had social defences against Sidereal charms. As you say, it's easy to rationalise not taking them seriously if you don't hear them personally, but I think it's also pretty easy to rationalise ignoring a Sidereal who says "I want you and your family to murder your really nice Queen who you've served loyally for generations, who gave you birthday presents every year, built you a lovely house with their own two hands, and who trusted you with important jobs, because I've foreseen that she'll murder your family."

                                                Whereas I think the Dragonblooded would be much more likely to respond to "I have foreseen that your increasingly erratic boss, who's become obsessed with their strange esoteric research, and responds with irrational anger to attempts to force her to actually administrate her kingdom properly, but also refuses to let you do it for her, is going to get worse until eventually she flips out and murders your whole family."

                                                Essentially, I think it's easier to rationalise the Dragonblood's decision if the Solars were noticeably worse (as a whole, not necessarily individually) than in the past.
                                                Also, of course, it motivates the Sidereals themselves. First, to cast the Great Prophecy, an unprecedented act. Secondly, that they eventually decided that revolution was the solution, as they believed restraining the Solars was too difficult. If things had been pretty fine, they probably would have believed they could maintain it. If things had been getting worse and they hadn't been able to stop it, then that explains their desperate choice.

                                                Earlier, you said something about the depiction of why the Unconquered Sun turned his back on the Solars, and how this lead you to have a more subtle view of the Solars? Could you say more about that?

                                                Eh, it depends. I once had an image in which that Lookshy weapon to turn everything to crystal was made by a Solar who had become obsessed with the idea of fixing everybody into place in highly durable forms, as that was the only way to keep them all safe. Her Limit Trigger was related to people suffering while she wasn't able to keep an eye on them, and she'd had a long battle against trying to keep everything under as strict an observation as possible, struggling with the difficulty and complexity of that when they kept moving.
                                                I think that's a great concept. But surely it's of the "worst excesses of the Solars" school, which you were arguing against?

                                                (So many Limit Triggers have always been about innocents suffering, why do so few people incorporate that into their image of the Great Curse?)
                                                I know DotFA and its Signature circle get a bad rap, and it does have its problems, but I always thought that the Heirophant's curse made sense; that, unable to bear the suffering of the world, he'd just convinced himself that everything was great, and he stayed in his ivory tower where everything seemed perfect, because viewing real suffering was just too depressing.

                                                One of the players in my group maintains that the best example of the dangers of the Great Curse on the Solars is Salina. She wasn't evil or corrupt. She wanted to help humanity. And so, in her compassion, she reshaped the very nature of reality to make it easier to acquire dangerous weapons. Unsurprisingly, the Five-Score Fellowship, whose raison d'etre was to maintain the nature of reality, were horrified, and terrified.

                                                I think though, there is a reason why people rarely think of compassion when thinking of the Great Curse.
                                                Once our characters were in a foreign city trying to track down an ancient Dragon King necromancer. We failed to find him quick enough, and he unleashed an army of ghosts, zombies, and skeletons, in the middle of the city.
                                                My No Moon, his daughter the Changing Moon and the Slayer ran towards the middle of the city to help. We two Lunars went into DBT and battled ghosts and zombies, while the Slayer reached the middle of the city, turned into a 30 foot monster, and fought the Kaiju-sized Dragon King necromancer.
                                                The Night Caste... sat in his apartment weeping and crying because he was overwhelmed by knowing that hundreds of people had been killed by monsters and he'd been unable to save them.
                                                Which was very annoying for the rest of us, because he wasn't saving more people.
                                                But it's not the kind of thing that'd make you want to kill him.

                                                Whereas the Twilight's occasional habit of emotionally shutting down under great stress and acting on callous logic (like the time he murdered our injured prisoners), is a pretty obvious reason why, were he a King, his underlings might want to kill him.

                                                Saying that...
                                                We had a Zenith who had Red Rage of Compassion. We went to negotiate rescuing captives from a Wood King. The Zenith flipped out and attacked the Wood King when he saw the miserable captives, and several captives died in the fight. Another time, we found a woman who'd been cruelly exiled from her village for some venal reason (I forget what). The Zenith flipped out and wiped out the village. There was a couple of other things he did while limit breaking, but I don't remember what.
                                                They were bad enough that he eventually killed himself.

                                                So, I think the Great Curse can of course be something that twists people's own good motives (compassion, virtue, etc) and ends up causing problems for society with their extreme actions. Whether that be liberal interventionism or putting everyone in crystal.

                                                But it's the actions that led to the Usurpation, not the Solar's motives (I mean, many Sidereals considered the Solars insane rather than evil), so I think it ends up much the same. "The Solars of the First Age became mad and committed atrocities" is still the same if some of those Solars were doing it for good motives.

                                                STing Bronze Age Exalted
