                Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                Does GET destroys permanently "any" spirit killed by it ?
                Yes. That's its purpose. As the charm itself notes, it was used to kill the primordials in the War of the Gods, and it can certainly kill all other spirits as well.

                Originally posted by Chausse
                I'm also indecisive in considering this technique is basically evil (it simply and purely destroys a soul, which are apparently in limited number in Creation) or not (it's one of the first Charm of the Solar, usually the good guys, at least when you look the other starter Charms in many abilities). Any advice on how to handle it ?
                Like most Exalted charms, and in fact most tools in general, its morality isn't inherent, but rather depends on the use you put it to. There's a lot of spirits, including some ghosts, where if you "kill" them (i.e. reduce them to incapacitated with lethal damage) they'll simply reform at a latter time, angry at you and wanting to take revenge. If a monstrous ghost is preying on the inhabitants of a village, devouring their souls and flesh, is it evil to destroy it in such a way that you ensure it can never return, rather than temporarily inconvenience it?

                One of Exalted's big themes is exploring the difference between being a powerful protagonist of an epic story, and being a good person. The game wants you to explore that question, to ask "If I was given all this incredible power, how would I use it. What is a moral way to do so?" In order to facilitate that question, the charms can't be designed with an imposed morality. This isn't D&D 3rd, with an alignment system, where spells can come with a [Good] or [Evil] tag that assures you that using them is always in line with the relevant moral stance. Whether or not you use Ghost-Eating Technique only to permanently defeat monstrous spirits who would otherwise return again to prey on you or the innocent, or whether you use it to rip asunder the higher souls of humans who were only doing their job when that brought them into conflict with you, is entirely up to the player, who must make the decision.

                            Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                            I'm looking for insight of the meaning of "definitively destroying a spirit" in Exalted.
                            I mean, it's pretty much what it says - it destroys the spirit (which can be more than just ghosts, remember - "spirit" is a broader category of being in Exalted), in such a way that only the very most powerful spirits can continue to exist in any form. Eric's already noted that the slain primordial titans didn't quite die completely, but were rather reduced to the Neverborn. And in 2e, if you used Ghost-Eating Technique to kill a Deathlord, it could still come back, albeit lessened in power, unless you found its secret, unique weakness and used that to permanently kill it. I'd suspect that 3e's treatment of the Deathlords will probably have something like that as well. Similarly, if you used GET on a god at the power level of the Incarnae, to off the Unconquered Sun or something, I'd imagine they'd still come back, in some horrible twisted form. Likewise extremely potent elementals, on the level of the Kukla, the greater elemental dragon of earth from 2e. But any spirit of lesser potency than those ones is just destroyed totally, snuffed from existence. If you slay Ahlat or Ligier or Fakharu or the Prince of the Red Chamber using Ghost-Eating Technique, that spirit is dead, forever.

                            Originally posted by Chausse
                            if you take most Solar Essence 1 Charms, you'll notice they usually address the Solars as the slayers of darkness, and I think GET stands a little apart into that because, well you just slay anything.
                            Note that while the fluff of the Solar charms may talk about slaying darkness, they are generally not limited to doing that. They'll work just fine on non-darkness too. (The sort-of exception are charms that explicitly target creatures of darkness. But those are relatively rare, and even that category, if you examine it closely, doesn't necessarily equate to "evil". It's apparently not set by the Unconquered Sun as it was in past editions, so it's not purely subjective, but it still appears to be a basically Creation-centric designation, given to spirits and other supernatural creatures that are basically inimical to Creation. But a creature that spawned in the Wyld and was totally non-sapient, with no ability to choose its actions, just a mad urge to rampage, could probably still be a creature of darkness. So that doesn't really line up with much that modern morality, at least, would call "evil").

                            Originally posted by Chausse
                            Since the topic of souls is furthermore quite touchy in fiction, I was wondering if there were insight as to what it means in the world of Exalted to definitively destroy a soul (apparently they are not in limited numbers, but there is the topic of reincarnation, etc ...)
                            I think this is one of the questions that Exalted wants you to examine. Is it wrong to permanently kill something, if it could otherwise return to hurt you or innocent people again? To use a real-world analogy, imagine you were in charge of defending a town against wild animal attacks, and you discovered that the last member of a nearly-extinct species was stalking townspeople. You know that now that it's learned to hunt humans, it will likely continue to do so, and even if you just capture it and move it to a wilderness area, you're not really ending the threat to people's lives, just making it a bit more unlikely (plus, if it lives around where the town is, moving it to another location may well mean the extinction of the species anyway, since you're moving it away from the extremely limited breeding pool). What's the right decision? Kill the creature, and defend the lives of the people you're supposed to protect? Or let it live, preserving a species at the potential cost of human lives? That's the kind of moral dilemma that charms like Ghost-Eating Technique exist to foster, and can't if they sugar-coat the effects.

                            Originally posted by Chausse
                            Do ghosts reappear when slained even if they have no cult ? Or is it just gods and demons that reform only this specific condition ?
                            3e hasn't given us too much info on ghosts yet. In 2e, every time a ghost was "killed" (again, meaning incapacitated with lethal damage), it vanished, and had a chance of losing some or all of its Passions, the strong emotions that kept it linked to existence. A ghost that lost all its Passions and was killed could go into Lethe, but might also go into Oblivion. Ghosts who had heard the whispers of the Neverborn and been infected by that could treat that as a Passion, preventing them from completely vanishing on death, and if they did finally completely end, they'd almost certainly go into Oblivion. As has been pointed out, in 3e it's just "oblivion", no Significant Capital Letter. Oblivion is not a cosmic force, it's just the end. But I'd still think that things work in roughly the same way - ghosts will still have passions, the emotions and principles they cling too, and killing them may cause them to let go of those. And we know the whispers of the Neverborn are still a thing, even if, again, there's no capital-W Whispers trait, and I expect that sort of madness will still serve as an attachment for some ghosts too.

                            And a cult can probably serve as a connection for a ghost too. It might not be quite as simple as a god or demon's "have a cult, have a chance to resurrect". Instead, the cult, if it was connected to one of the ghost's passions, might serve as the fetter (the physical representation of the passion), so that destroying the fetter would require finding all the cult members and getting them to stop worshiping the ghost.

                                                            Originally posted by Jen View Post

                                                            What would those reasons be?
                                                            Different reasons for different people.

                                                            Some might do it for a family member, as a way of upholding their family bonds, but might not do it for a stranger.

                                                            Others might do it for a stranger, treating it as an ethical stance - 'you help people when they say they're in need, even if you don't really think it will matter in the long run.'

                                                            Others might have a religious obligation that places the emphasis on the intent of their actions, rather than on the strict result.

                                                            Others might believe that doing something kind for those in need will cause the cosmos to 'pay it forward' somehow.

                                                            Others might see something in the old man that reminds them of someone else they failed to help - and making this effort will help them to deal with the guilt they feel.


                                                            If it helps, think of a war movie where someone lying with a soon-to-be-fatal wound says "please don't go - I don't want to die alone!" There are always some people who, when push came to shove, might get up and go - this guy's not long for the world, best for me to live for myself! And yet, some people are going to stay with the wounded soldier until the light dies, and might never really be able to articulate why.
