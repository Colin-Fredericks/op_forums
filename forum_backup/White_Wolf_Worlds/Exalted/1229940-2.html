    The concept is a work in progress.

    Ideally, the assignment of "temporary" would be in terms of impact on the story rather than strictly mechanical, but I recognize that there is still a pretty blurry area there.

    My instinct would be to fall back to what the player intends. Putting more decision weight on what level of impact that player wants to have on the story helps reduce the feeling of the base line mechanics as "restrictive." Is the player making a meal to get on the NPCs good side, but expecting this to be the opening for the circle to convert that NPC to their side, or is the player trying to knock the NPC's socks off so hard that they're forever fond of the PCs?

    If I was writing this up more fully, I'd certainly go more into story and frame of reference. What's a temporary change from the perspective of the story the PCs are in, could be a life changing permanent change if we were telling some random NPC's story instead. I mean, to go back to my own example, if you make a temporary replacement sword for your Dawn, it's not really all that temporary for any guards that get sliced to bits with that sword.
