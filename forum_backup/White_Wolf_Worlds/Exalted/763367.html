    [3E Homebrew]Lightbringers: An alternate Solar creation/advancement system, 3E update

    11-11-2015, 03:51 PM
    When I was doing my first ST for 2E, I stumbled upon Mengtzu’s Lightbringers system. It was pretty much exactly what I needed without even knowing that I wanted it: Simple, straightforward, easy to keep track of at all times (as this was my first campaign and there was a waiting list of players, the ability to swap new people in seamlessly following an accidental culling was glorious), and with an eye towards ensuring the play experience was fun for all levels of player knowledge.

    As I start brainstorming my first 3E campaign I’ve decided to take a crack at updating Lightbringers for the new edition. Of course, I don’t have anywhere near the skill or knowledge of Mengtzu, so I was hoping I could tap into some of the forum braintrust to help me out and point out any glaring errors.

    The following is a proposed Lightbringer setup for 3E. I’ve spoilered my thoughts and concerns at the very bottom.

    This package of house rules has a number of related goals:
    - Providing a structure for relatively short campaigns (less than a year of weekly sessions).
    - Supporting a mode of play in which all PCs participate equally in combat and non-combat scenes.
    - Providing rapid advancement that nonetheless has a sensible learning curve.
    - Improving balance between PCs at the table, both to make the game friendlier to new or less system-expert players, and to make it easier for the ST to provide appropriate challenges.

    These house-rules should be used in proportion to how useful you find these goals.

    -Character Creation-

    Caste – Choose as normal.

    Circle Goals - The group should decide together on a mutual goal for the Circle that will form the backbone of the first ~10 sessions of play. This should be at the national/regional level and pose a good challenge for Essence 1 characters.

    Character Goals – Each character’s individual goals should be on a national/regional level achievable within a relatively short time-frame (think “local epic”). Each Player should have a Defining Intimacy related to that goal, whether a general Principle or Tie to someone/something associated with it. Players wanting a Creation-spanning goal should choose an achievable subset and upgrade as the campaign progresses. Personal goals should be compatible with that of the Circle (dramatic tension is good, having no reason to work with the Circle is bad).

    Attributes:
    - All attributes begin with one dot.
    - Set Dexterity to 5.
    - Choose one Social and one Mental attribute to be rated at 5.
    - Distribute 6 points in your primary category, 4 in your secondary, 2 in your tertiary.

    Abilities:
    1. Choose a Supernal Ability to be rated at 5. If your chosen Supernal Ability is one of the choices listed in step 2 or 3, you may skip that step and instead choose an additional ability, for a total of 3, during step 5.
    2. Choose one of Archery, Brawl, Martial Arts* (any subset), Melee, and Thrown to be rated at 5.
    3. Choose one of Integrity, Presence, Performance, Investigation, and Socialize to be rated at 5.
    4. Choose three of your Caste abilities to be rated at 5.
    5. Choose any two other abilities to be rated at 5. If you do not have Brawl, Martial Arts or Melee rated at 5, you must ensure that Dodge or Resistance is amongst your abilities rated at 5.
    6. Distribute 12 points amongst the remaining abilities, no more than 3 points to any one ability.
    7. Craft* is a single ability for Lightbringers.
    8. Choose a specialty for each ability rated at 5.

    *Craft - Craft is a single ability to the Lightbringers - their understanding of one field translates effortlessly to another. However specialized supernatural crafts require an appropriate specialty, or the crafter suffers a -2 internal penalty. Obtaining such a specialty requires an in-character justification, but not a particularly robust one - transcendent skill doesn’t require much to go on.

    *Martial Arts – Lightbringers do not need to purchase any additional Merits to get dots in a Martial Arts style. They must still obtain dots in each style separately.

    Merits:
    - Gain Artifact 5. This must be distributed between artifacts with a rating no greater than 3, ideally weapons and armor. This represents spoils from unsuccessful Wyld Hunts, past life tomb raiding, gifts from mysterious benefactors, etc.
    - Distribute 10 points amongst other Merits, except Manse.

    NOTE: For Lightbringers, change the text of the Martial Arts Merit to the following
    Martial Arts (••••) Purchased - The character had a innate understanding of the underlying values of the martial arts before being Exalted; The awakening of her Essence has only caused that familiarity to blossom. Treat the character’s Essence as one dot higher for the purpose of purchasing Martial Arts Charms.

    Charms
    -Choose 5 Charms useful in martial combat.
    -Choose 5 Charms useful in social or non-combat scenes
    -Choose 5 additional Charms of any type.
    -Spells may be substituted for charms according to their function (ie Flying Guillotine may replace a combat charm choice, Infallible Messenger a non-combat choice, Summon Elemental either, etc.).

    Finishing Touches:
    - All Lightbringers start with Essence 1.
    - All Lightbringers start with Willpower 6.
    - Calculate essence pools as normal.
    - Gain Health Levels as normal.
    - Lightbringers do not receive bonus points.

    -Advancement and Campaign Structure-
    A Lightbringers campaign is intended to run over arcs of ten sessions each, up to five times (for a total of 50 sessions). Character advancement does not occur with XP, but on a schedule over the course of these sessions.

    All PCs are always at an identical state of advancement. No character is deprived advancement if their player missed a session, nor can any bonuses be earned. If a new player joins, or an old character needs to be replaced, the new character joins at the same state of advancement as the rest. This not only naturally preserves balance, but explicitly distinguishes character advancement from reward - new charms are not a privilege awarded for good play, but a periodic milestone in the player’s learning curve.

    Campaign Arcs
    Each arc is intended to last ten sessions, allowing a five-arc campaign of weekly sessions to fit comfortably in a calendar year of real time.

    Each arc deals primarily with the Circle’s shared Goal. That Goal should be resolved - by success, failure or perhaps a dramatic change in the Circle’s perspective - by the end of the arc. As a result sessions should be fairly focused and fast-paced, with some sort of decisive change to the local status quo by session end, in order to build steadily towards the arc’s climax. That doesn’t mean the arc or sessions should be linear - just that even in pure sandbox mode with the PCs striking out wherever interests them, something should happen as a result.

    Once the arc is finished, the group should assess the story and then choose a new or evolved Circle Goal that represents an escalation over the one just resolved (this is also a good time to re-examine personal goals and Intimacies, as they were hopefully a big part of the arc themselves).

    Sample Arc Progression

    Sakuya, Reimu, Marisa, Arisu and Sanae are a Circle of Lightbringers arising in the Dreaming Sea. Their first Circle Goal is to win a Sea-wide race presided over by Mercury herself, building up their ship while sabotaging others. After winning the race, for Arc 2 they take their prize, a treasure map leading to the hoard of a First Age Pirate Exigent, all the while dodging the enemies and rivals made during the race, as well as the Sidereals and Abyssals who have designs on the treasure. Upon finding the hoard, they discover that it was actually a prison for an unstuck in time Balor, who is released during an epic battle. For Arc 3 they must unite the warring Prasad, Volivat, and Ysyr to combat a brewing Second Balorian Crusade. After repelling the Fair Folk, the next Arc finds the Circle then setting out to formalize their new Dreaming Sea Confederation, overcoming centuries of hatred just in time for a two pronged attack from The Realm and Ixcoatli. During the war the portal to Zen Mu is opened after an errant shot from the Realm Defense Grid, and during Arc 5 the Circle must find a way to stop or control the portal's inhabitants from wreaking mass havoc and destruction.


    Advancement during the Arc
    -At the end of every session, each character receives one charm. The group together decides whether combat or non-combat was the most decisive arena in the last two sessions (ie whether they used their combat or non-combat charms more). The charm gained is of that type. Alternately, groups wishing to avoid a positive feedback loop can make the gained charm of the opposite type, creating an incentive to bounce back and forth between violent and non-violent solutions to your problems as you gain new charms.
    -At the end of every odd session of an arc (eg 1st, 3rd, 5th etc), each character receives 1 ability dot to assign as they please. They may exchange this instead for two specialties. This generally should have some fictional justification, even if it’s just a note of the character taking an interest in the ability during downtime.
    -At the end of every even session of an arc (eg 2nd, 4th, 6th, etc.) each character receives a Martial Arts charm, spell, Evocation, or (if Eclipse) spirit Charm they qualify for. If the character has no such Charms they want or qualify for, they may instead gain 3 dots of Merits (usually these will be Purchased Merits only, although the ST may allow some Story Merits to be purchased this way at their discretion).
    -At the end of every fifth session, add 10 XP to a pool shared by the group. This XP may be spent by the group on anything that has an XP cost, such as sorcerous workings, familiar training, etc.

    Advancement at the end of an Arc
    At the end of an arc, each character gains:
    - 1 Attribute dot
    - 1 dot of Essence
    - 1 dot of Willpower
    - 1 new Defining Intimacy (whether completely new as a result of the arc or an existing Intimacy increased in strength)
    This should be accompanied by some downtime narration of how their experiences have caused them to grow as an Exalt and a person (particularly the double-edged sword of a new Defining Intimacy).

    Gaining Merits
    Story Merits should be very easy to acquire in play, to the extent there are no rules for them (save for the exception covered above for every odd session of an arc). Players should naturally assume that easy come means easy go.

    Manses are a somewhat more difficult case, and players should assume they are both harder to obtain and less likely to be taken away by the winds of fate. However the ST should allow every reasonable opportunity to gain possession of Manses in play.

    Gaining Artifacts and Evocations
    Artifacts can be gained organically in play by normal means - seeking out First Age ruins, taking them from the bodies of foes, or crafting.

    However, once the Lightbringers have established their mark on the world, their artifacts will begin to thrum alongside their wielder’s legend. During the downtime between the first and second campaign arc, each character’s artifacts begin to develop free Evocations that apply benefits upon attuning. The player may narrate how these Evocations arose however they please (e.g. from the weapon gaining further sentience, the lingering effects of a defeated foe, blessings from a powerful spirit, etc.). Characters that do not use weapons or armor (generally martial artists) should negotiate with the ST an appropriate substitute useful in combat.

    Every third session thereafter, each character comes into possession of a miscellaneous artifact or hearthstone with a rating equal to the character’s essence. Most such artifacts should be of non-combat utility. The ST and player should work together for the appearance of these artifacts making sense in the fiction, including breaking the regular three-session schedule when needed (so long as it works out roughly equivalent over time).

    In the downtime at the end of arcs 3 and 4, each character’s weapon and armor are upgraded to have an artifact rating equal to the character’s Essence (4 and 5 respectively) with the potential for Evocations to match. This may entail discovering a new panoply and setting the old gear aside, the gods of the artifacts receiving well-deserved promotions and unlocking more of their ancient power thereby, or the discovery that their ‘mere’ daiklave is in actuality the Volcano Cutter of legend.

    Arcs of irregular length
    Campaigns tend to find their own length, and resist planning. It’s fine for an arc to take as long as makes sense for your table (and in particular much longer if your group prefers to climb the Essence tiers more slowly and has time).

    Rebuilding
    Players should be allowed to rebuild their characters between sessions within reason. Enjoyment of the character and opportunity to exercise a deeper understanding of the system outweigh any minor continuity problems. No character should be haunted by an unwise charm selection for their entire career.

    However Solar Exalted are not Alchemical Exalted; such changes should be for the purpose of becoming more comfortable with the character, not adapting to local tactical conditions. Furthermore, particularly radical changes such as changing caste or retconning the entire character concept will not seem appropriate to most groups. In such cases it’s probably better to retire the character and create a new one.

    -What does this mean?-
    All Lightbringers start with very high attack and defence dice pools and 5 combat charms. The whole Circle are first-class citizens in action scenes.

    All Lightbringers start with a strong mortal-level package for social combat, and 5 non-combat charms. The whole Circle are first-class citizens in non-combat scenes.

    Optimised mortal dice pools is something a Lightbringer Circle gets for free. Their strategy and growth is intended to be almost entirely in the realm of Charms.

    The cost is in flexibility. Without bonus points, many mechanical character arrangements become impossible, and the specific structures used above prevent some valid builds (such as having all Caste abilities rated at 5). While most of these expressions are suboptimal and not appropriate for a balance-focused campaign, the group/ST should be open to moving around ability and attribute points somewhat to better fit a concept.



    Circle and Character Goals
    The original Lightbringers referred to 2E’s Motivation, the system term. I switched that out to goal, just the simple noun. Even though it has no system meaning, I still think it’s a good idea for players/the group to consider going in. Requiring a Defining Intimacy seems like the closest I can get to the original Motivation concept.

    Attributes
    Unchanged from original Lightbringers. Dex might not be the god stat it once was, but it’s still the Prince of Earth of attributes. Setting it to 5 is also useful for maintaining the optimized fighting pool.

    Abilities
    Addition of Supernal is a simple update. Lightbringer wrinkles for MA and Craft are covered later, hence the asterisks. Not sure if I worded step 1 super clearly; Trying to avoid a situation where, say, Supernal Melee is chosen first and then the player thinks they have to add another combat ability at 5 even if they are fine with just Melee. The original rules allowed three specialties for each ability rated at 5, thought that might have been overkill with the new 3E specialty rules.

    Craft
    This was the original rule hack. I think it still works for 3E, although it would require shooing a player away from any Craft charms that worked on switching Craft dots around. It’s also possible that this is the worst mistake ever with horrible knock on effects.

    Martial Arts
    The elimination of the Merit will probably be relatively uncontroversial. Having one MA skill to cover all styles still feels too strong, so buying them separately is kept in. Again, this feels like something that feels ok but might be catastrophic/overpowered in practice.

    Merits
    The Artifact rules in the original Lightbringers were oriented around Jade artifacts, when each MM had different stats. Now that stats are standardized it probably doesn’t matter the Artifact’s composition. Since the Martial Arts Merit is no longer necessary just to get MA dots, I tried to spice up the Merit for Lightbringers.

    Charms
    The original Lightbringers made sure that every character had at least a perfect defense. That’s not really necessary in 3E, but I kept the minimums for in and out of combat Charms. I thought about giving 10 Charms of any type, but choosing 20 Charms at chargen might be pushing it.

    Finishing Touches
    Original Lightbringers started at Essence 2, because that’s what chargen Essence was in 2E, and Willpower 7. I lowered it to Willpower 6 because my Lightbringers campaigns envision an additional arc.

    Advancements During the Arc
    This is by far the biggest divergence from the old Lightbringers, where a Charm was given out only at the end of the even sessions. However, due to the much expanded library of Charms in 3E and the increased focus on combining multiple Charms rather than obtaining and mastering fire and forget Charms, I think it justifies increasing the player’s Charm intake?? From my back of the envelope math, if you spent regular XP just on favored charms you’d end up purchasing ~ 37 Charms when you reached Essence 5 by RAW, whereas here you’ll have 40. Of course, you’ll also be doing that in 40 sessions here compared to 60 sessions by RAW…

    The odd session/ability dot section hasn’t changed. The even session charms are intended to reflect expenditures of Solar XP, with the Merit rider due to the fact that if you aren’t a MA/sorcery/Evocation user you shouldn’t be punished. Might need to clarify the Story Merit aside some; Intention is that the player may want to get, say, Contacts or Resources, but not have the justification for it currently in the campaign. By negotiating with the ST to ‘purchase’ the Merit the ST agrees to fudge or add a situation to the campaign to accommodate acquiring the Merit for the player.

    The pool of community XP is to cover the fact that there are a few more sources of XP cost requirements this edition, while Lightbringers envisions XP-less play. Not sure if that amount should be upped a bit, maybe to 15-20 XP every five sessions, since the pool has to cover everyone.

    Advancement at the End of an Arc
    This is mostly from the original Lightbringers, although I swapped out a new Virtue dot for a new Defining Intimacy. That seems like a crude approximation, and I think I might just axe the new intimacy stuff.

    Gaining Artifacts and Evocations
    This section in the original Lightbringers was very focused on upgrading the jade chargen artifacts to orichalcum, as MM mattered a great deal in 2E. In 3E, with its standardized weapon stats, I’ve tried to keep the same feel of an upgraded panoply with free natural/attuned Evocations. It’ll probably take until Arms of the Chosen is released before this won’t require homebrewing, but seeing as they don’t start appearing until 10 sessions in hopefully the players and ST will be comfortable enough to come up with their own.

    Another thought I had was just giving the character’s artifacts uniform stat/number upgrades, like additional accuracy dice or Hardness, to convey that even among artifacts the Lightbringers have something special.


    The last two sections are unchanged from original flavor Lightbringers.

    The original system included some additional mechanical tweaks, but were mostly oriented around 2E concerns I don’t think are present in 3E. The only ones I might consider bringing over unchanged is doubling base Strength for Feats of Strength purposes (but my perusal of 3E Athletics suggests that’s not as necessary now) or the prospect of getting Stamina/2 as base Hardness that can stack with armor. Mengtzu also had some fun flavor bits and helpful tips on constructing arcs and campaigns that I might also just yoink and reproduce.

    For now though, I’m just looking at the above and hoping I didn’t miss something that’ll cause it to fall apart when the rubber hits the road.

              It might be because I didn't include the original reasoning and goals of the system (which I'm working on working into the original post), but you're not wrong (although I don't think your criticisms are completely founded).

              Lightbringers is intended for new players and is designed so that at all times each member of the party is capable of contributing to the action. So you get things like everyone having Dex 5 and a combat ability (so that everyone has at least mortal optimized pools in battle), but also required to have a 5 point social ability and Charms capable of helping outside of combat. Each member of the Circle can be confident that they can be proactive no matter where they can find themselves ("Invincible Sword Princess and her squishy friends" isn't a major problem in 3E

              Now, to accomplish this is does mean that certain builds are shut out. You can't go 15 Charms deep into your Supernal from chargen, you can't play the dedicated pacifist nor the monofocused combat beast, etc. That's the cost of making Circle-balance the overriding concern, but I see it as a feature not a bug. (Additionally, there was also a part in the original I haven't ported over yet that mentioned that switching around dots and fudging things for a given concept is possible, as long as it didn't result in something too suboptimal compared to the rest of the Circle.)

              In that vein, your point about how the Physical Attributes are now more even in the new edition is very true. The original said that Dex should never ever go below 5, but I think in the new edition if the player came to the ST and could petition as you did for opting out of Dex 5 and making either Stamina or Strength 5 instead that could be an option. However, I think it would require a bit of non-newbie system knowhow to realize those were reasonable builds to short Dex somewhat to obtain; Meanwhile, having Dex 5 never really hurts and is usually a great boon, so making it a mandatory opt-in for players is the simple option.

              And finally, being honest, not sure what the exact problem with the Artifact requirement is? I always liked it for how it gave players fun toys right from the get go.

                  My issue with this for new players is that, while no one will have an ineffective character, character creation is a lot more intimidating in a way that could carry over into the game. It sends the message that 5s are necessary because the system is so brittle and because organic choices will ruin your character. Lightbringers helped in 2e, where it really did protect people from a lot of bad surprises.

                  I wouldn't want to make a player think, "Oh shit, I've only got 7 dice in Charisma + Presence. I should shut up and let the good characters handle this," or "My score isn't 5 in this ability, so picking up charms is a waste." Of course you can communicate to your players that they don't need huge dice pools to succeed or to invest in an area, but anyone willing to say that probably wouldn't use this system...
