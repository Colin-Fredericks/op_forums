            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post

            Spring Razor is just three dots despite being made from a Dragon-level Wood elemental that required a sworn brotherhood of Dragon-Blooded to kill, and the Terrestrial crafter devoted a significant span of her life to creating while also using lots of other poisons and special firewood at great expense from across Creation.
            My read on that is that a more powerful Artifact could have been made, but the crafter opted for something more within their wheelhouse rather than let the concept and materials go to waste by risking failure on something more powerful.

            Similarly with the time, given how in the Artifact crafting rules, one isn't exactly bound to keeping at it after the mandatory first interval has been made; I would see the time and care as somewhere between a representation of needing to do other things to build up the craft points, and stunts to maximize the dice rolls.

            Originally posted by Jen
            Since the crafting rule doesn't require you to have materials from apocalyptic creature to make high rating Artifact


            The Artifact crafting rules don't lay out specifics for the necessary components, sure, but they do say that the Storyteller needs to adjudicate what the necessary tools and materials for any given project are, and that a project cannot begin until they are acquired.

            And I feel as though a Storyteller's surest guidelines on what the necessary materials are for any given Artifact are the stories describing other Artifacts.

            So I'd say that, while something like an ancient and powerful demon or the prison of an eldritch horror from before history or a powerful volcanic manse aren't necessarily the kinds of things that you need for a five-dot, it generally warrants something of approximately equivalent grandeur; I don't think that the Storyteller should allow somebody to run with a project for such a high level Artifact if all they have is a cave and a box of scraps.

            If nothing else, given how a consistent element of five-dot Artifact weapons appears to be "this is an immense power that has its own ideas about how it should be used, and effectively using it can be a struggle even for the Exalted", I absolutely think that what it's made of (or the circumstances in which it was made) are at least as, if not more, important to the process as the skill of the maker, and that they should never be allowed just because the player has the gold points and the stats and they just want to pop out a thousand-year daiklave.

                                Now now, Ardor-Sharing Technique is only bad if you don't want to screw with people's emotions mid-fight. If that's your thing, Heartsong is the bow for you!

                                Your target is an Earth Aspect Immaculate Monk with Resolve 5 - pushing the very limits of human stoicism! The difficulty of a Passion-Inducing Gambit is Resolve-2. Ardor-Sharing Technique adds one automatic success, meaning you only need 2 successes to Inspire love in him - or force him to spend a willpower against it. You can't decide how he reacts to getting love fired into his heart, but once you get to Lover's Quarrel, you can!

                                Plus, being Archery attacks, you can combo all this with Solar Archery, using Arrow Storm Technique to fire hatred (or smug indifference) into the hearts of six different opponents and then breaking off to let them deal with it.

                                That probably doesn't improve its chances of being your bowfriend, (neither the weapon nor the wielder are beefy enough, I think), but it's not as bad as you'd think. Being able to just shoot emotional lleverage into your rivals is a neat trick, if you're playing that kinda guy.

                                At the risk of explaining the joke, "Lover's Quarrel" is a pun that a non-native speaker might not get. A quarrel can mean both "an argument" (usually between people that are otherwise friendly) and "a crossbow bolt", which fits nicely for an Eros-bow.
