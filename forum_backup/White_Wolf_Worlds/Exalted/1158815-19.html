    I do like the idea of Adamant being "sharpness"

    Like, it's one word. But so's "fire" and so's "mutability"

    Sharpness can mean a lot of things. Sharp stuff is clear, direct, it is clean and pure. Sharpness means clarity. Sharpness turns effort into result with maximum efficiency. Things that are sharp are often very hard, and very strong. Scalpels that cut to heal are sharp, swords that kill, minds that create, eyes that see, these things are all within the purview of Adamant. I am writing artifact for my next campaign at the moment and I feel like each magical material has a personality which can be transcribed into evocations of various types with a mechanical expression of that personality, and I feel that "sharpness" does that for Adamant very effectively.

                            Incidentally, "how the hell did anybody figure that out given the materials and circumstances involved" is a fairly good way to approach the difficulty of recreating the ancient mysteries of how somebody might have created something like Beloved Adorei or Sozen, the Cataphract of Keys that I was talking about before. Like I said, you need to already know quite a bit about the underlying structure and properties of various materials before you can even do something like hypothesize how they might interact under extraordinary and/or artificial circumstances. That's the kind of thing that took quite a while to figure out from scratch (not least because the investigation existed in circumstances that weren't quite amenable to dedicated scientific research, even if they were rarely really actively hostile to them; progress is slow when one of your only real sources of scientific inquiry is bored and curious rich people). So a major loss of something like scientific literacy would conceivably take a while to recreate a lot of that knowledge so that things like research can be resumed and certain technologies can be applied again.

                            Think about the chain of information that would be needed to get everything required for the kinds of experimentation that let you find a stable oxide of gold, and then imagining it all being destroyed, and you could have an answer to the question of why it might take so long to rediscover secrets that the First Age took for granted.
