    On the subject of trans representation, did the prior Editions have anything to say about it one way or the other? It seems to me like in the past, they were more or less invisible. I'm hard pressed to think of a way of making it quite apparent that they exist in the setting, and are recognised, and that the recognition is not altogether negative, that doesn't seem very abrupt, but obviously I'm biased.

    Certainly, if the idea of making changes to familiar characters in the setting was a concern (which... ehhhhh, wouldn't exactly bother me), it doesn't seem like something that should apply to figures that, in-character, are entirely fictional. Even if Danaa'd was still the name of the Elemental Dragon of Water (which I don't think it is), trans woman Immaculate Dragon Danaa'd is entirely fictional (while retaining significance for what it says about their society to include such a person in their mythology).

    Suddenly, I'm remembering how, when I met Omicron, JimB and Sikker, and they were all playing Burn Legend, I came up with the idea of a hyena totem Lunar that was a trans woman. In retrospect, that was possibly a bit on the nose.

                                                There are... three considerations for adoption that I can see.

                                                One is a means by which a Dragon Blooded family can extend its authority over people that are not blood related, as well as a political operation to extend the rights and privileges of being your family member to an outsider. That kind of adoption has been utilised by the Scarlet Empress, as well as the occasional consolidation of a new Great House such as V'neef, while being heavily restricted for most Great Houses doing on their own initiative (although they get to do it with Exalted patricians).

                                                One is a process by which a Great House keeps children within the family when their mother dies, preserving the matrilineality, and the process by which widowers are separated back out. It doesn't matter if the mother from whom you get you name and who was responsible for your upbringing dies, the House will provide you with a new one.

                                                Last, it's a method by which couples that insist on being together (or that are economically or politically expedient to preserve) despite being natively incapable of conceiving and bearing children can be validated within the default premise that the Dynasty has for marriage. I'd say your point is half right; they maintain the narrative that the central purpose of marriage is the creation of new Exalted children, but there's clearly recognition that the reality does not always strictly align with it, even when it would be desirable. Adoption provides an out.

                                                Note that the last has a certain crossover with the prior two; if some patrician Exalts or somebody's mother dies, then those couples within the House that are not capable of making their own children for whatever reason are a very convenient place to put them (after all, there's probably not a concern that their own income is already invested in a child), while the purpose of validating the marriage is useful in its own right.
