    I'm creating a Black Market Exigent for a 2.5 game

    11-02-2013, 02:29 PM
    I posted this on the old forum, but I may be able to find more interested posters over here. Who knows?

    I'm creating a Black Market Exigent for a 2.5 game. Who wants to help? Has anyone else done this? Anyway, here's the background: A couple of years ago, I was running a solo Lunar game. The PC's antagonist, known as "the nobleman," had ran and ruined the PC's life from the age of toddler to adulthood. After barely surviving the nobleman's attempt to end the PC's life, the PC exalted as a lunar. The PC dropped off the map and let the nobleman believe he was dead. He received training and tats from the Silver Pact. A few years later, the lunar sets out to free enslaved peoples, beginning with the Djalas. His plan was to establish an independent Djala nation. However, he discovered that his plans crossed the nobleman's as the nobleman had become a major slave trader of djalas. Then an event happened wherein the PC was sidelined for 100 years. Now he's back, but to his astonishment, the nobleman is still around. Unfortunately, the player had to move and our game stopped there. However, the player has recently moved back and has been interested in resuming the story.

    Two years ago, I wanted the nobleman to remain an effective and challenging adversary after the PC's exaltation. I wanted him to become some kind of Exalt. However, I did not want him to be a Solar, Lunar, or Sidereal. I was thinking GSP, Abyssal or even a dragonblood who had made some kind of deal with the Yozi to force a terrestrial exaltation. Maybe even make him a godblood with all the godblood abilities, so he'd be as powerful as a low level DB. But none of those seemed to jive with who the nobleman was. Fast forward two years later and the devs announce the creation of exigents and black market exaltations. This sounds like a perfect fit.

    The black market exigence is built around the concept of leadership of city-states. The exaltation will favor the abilities: Bureaucracy, Performance, Presence, Socialize, and War. The out-of-caste favored ability will be melee. The nobleman is a renowned fencer. I'll give him the basic abilities all current exalts share plus some anima ability that jives with the leadership vibe (if anyone has any suggestions on this, I would appreciate it). I'm thinking the DB's Ox Body, and a lunar or sidereal's essence pool. I'm not looking to reinvent the wheel; I plan on stealing liberally from the existing exalt types.

    The charm structure has me stumped though. I know I want it to be ability based, but other than that, I don't know. Is it going to be Ability + Attribute, or permanent Essence, or Specialty, or Virtue? A little more flavor; when the PC returns to the nobleman's city, he will find that 95% of the people are gleefully enslaved to doing the nobleman's bidding. He has used his Charms to make the people love him and strive their utmost to serve him and his needs. This kind of mind-smurfing sounds like it belongs in the Solar Charm realm, but I don't want him to be that powerful. Most of his power is going to be directed at making the city run effectively and making his serfs want to work themselves near to death to please him. The people adore and almost worship the nobleman (hm, perhaps some do). We don't really need to make the social type Charms. That will all be in the background. It's the final showdown I'm more concerned about. He will only have a few combat Charms and those will be related to swordplay.

    Any suggestions would be appreciated. Thanks

      Unfortunately, creating an Exigent at this point will be exceedingly difficult, considering Exigents are based on ideas and mechanical constructs that simply don't exist in 2E.

      That said, it might be worth it to check out the Roll of Glorious Divinity I for their spirit Charms, as well as the Scroll of Fallen Races for some non-traditional Charm models (thanks to the Dragon Kings and Mountain Folk). It provides some ideas for how to work a non-traditional Exalt in the context of 2E. Sad to say, that's the best I can do until the actual Exigents hardback comes out.

                              Gaius wrote: Suleri is mostly in the right. As I understand it, creating an Exigent to create an Exigent is normally accomplished by a god requesting and receiving permission from the Unconquered Sun to access the Divine Exigence. A Black Market Exaltation is when that access is granted, but sold or stolen, such that it is ultimately used by a deity who may not be in the Unconquered Sun's good graces.

                              Here's John himself on Black Market Exaltations and "dirty bomb" Exalts:

                              John Mørke wrote: As some people have begun to speculate, the Black Market Exaltations refer to Exigents made illicitly, somehow bypassing the Unconquered Sun's erm firewall, so to speak.

                              In the most literal terms, there were raw Exaltations granted by the Unconquered Sun which were then not used by the petitioner, but instead passed into the world of spirits, where it cycled the realms of gods and men until a bidder was finally able to trigger it.

                              A "dirty bomb" (not actual game term) Exaltation is a similar concept, only the Exaltation has passed through the hands of multiple spirits who have modified the power inside. This only happens for nefarious, terrible reasons, as the resulting Exigent is always an aberration of enormous power.


                              Gaius wrote: Legitimately acquired or not, creating an Exigent still involves some non-trivial sacrifice on the part of the god. Perhaps "dirty bombs" require lesser sacrifice, shouldered by several deities as it is?

                              I doubt The Guild is alone in being able to buy them, but doing so would certainly demand throwing around a ton of moolah to make it worth it to a deity to brave the risks. A god with access to creating an Exigent at all isn't the sort of thing one finds everyday. Your mortal nobleman even without deep pockets could conceivably get such a god to Choose him: he'd just need to find the right price.

                              Or, possibly, convince the deity the price is much lower than it actually is. Perhaps he's persuasive enough to get a god to believe that he has come into possession of a wondrous object of great power. This potent tool "supplements" the process, ensuring even a weak deity can survive the ordeal with minimal loss. Naturally, he will only use this great gift to carry out the god's will in ways only the Exalted can. Said deity is completely fleeced and goes for it. The nobleman thus becomes an Exigent by tricking a god into obliterating itself. Illegitimate enough for ya? Bonus points if the god gained Exigency access in the proper fashion and was legitimately trying to do right by its worshipers when he was conned -- perhaps the god really was revered and loved, such that becoming said god's Chosen (and keeping the true story under wraps) gave the nobleman a population that adored him he was happy to exploit. Not all impressive feats need to be achieved through Charms, after all, though such adoration would probably grease the wheels on whatever Social Charms he has.

                                  Limited Reagent wrote: If you want to go weird, have his dice cap based on the Magnitude of whatever group he leads, up to a max of 10. This also gives the PCs something interesting to do if they find out about this: if they start removing people from his group (by whatever means) they weaken him personally.

                                  A good way to do Charms, if you want simple, is to take a Solar Charm and cut it down in scope. Like take Husband-Seducing Demon Dance and cut it down to just being loyalty to the city-state. But as you said, you want this to be background stuff.

                                  For combat, it might be worth looking to Sidereals, if you don't want just melee Charms. They have some interesting social combat stuff like You and Yours Stance that could pretty easily be refluffed to work. As far as those swordplay Charms, think about making them work only within the laws of the city-state. I mean, like say the city-state has a dueling culture. His Charms, then, only work (or work to their full potential) when he's officially initiated a duel. He might also have Charms that allow him to shunt damage or attacks over to underlings, either supernaturally (the man is stabbed but it does nothing; his servant collapses in blood) or they just reflexively leap in front of the attack and take the blow. Sort of like granting underlings limited use of Virtuous Negation Defense from the DBs. Something similar to Throne Shadow Charms would probably work well too, though likely more overt so they're based around swordplay and command rather than martial arts and power behind the throne.

                                  ---

                                  Another interesting twist you might want to consider is if this Exaltation is a one-time deal, or if it has any type of reincarnation built into it. Ie if/when this guy is defeated/killed, who if anyone will the power go to next? It could go to the next ruler of the city-state, maybe, or this guy's heir. Maybe there is some symbol of office that, whoever holds it next Exalts. There are a lot of ways you could do it. Make some shit up that you think will be fun.™

                                          		This is for an NPC, right? Not a full-on player character?

                                          That makes it easier, as you don't have to work out a bunch of the "make this kosher for PCs" stuff for his powers - they can be more like a panoply power in one of the NPC spirit writeups. Prerequisites don't really matter - either he has the power or he doesn't.

                                          As for thematics, I'd figure out how esoteric you want to go - is he a straightforward "control the city" sort, with relatively logical ties, or is he a more philosophical sort like the Sidereals, interpreting cityhood though the lens of something else and vice versa in his powerset?

                                          Specific power ideas:
                                          Look at the world through the windows of his city's buildings (Awareness)
                                          Mystically mess with traffic in his city, increasing/decreasing the travel times for his foes or allies (bureaucracy or something)
                                          Make the walls and streets of his city resound with his words, transmitting social attacks (Performance)
                                          Reshape the city itself, leaving unwanted visitors hopelessly lost (Crafts or Survival)
                                          Wrap himself in armor (or transform himself into a creature) of brick and stone, making the walls of the city his bones and the cobblestones his skin. (Resistance or Crafts)
                                          Subsume his consciousness entirely into the city, letting him control the things within while leaving his body comatose.
                                          Transfer knowledge among those in his thrall, so that if one member has a given skill at 4, *everyone* has that skill at 4, while motes are committed.
                                          Destroy part of the city to heal himself, or sacrifice those under his thrall for the same.

                                          Maybe use a dice cap of (Ability + Level of the Followers background)?
