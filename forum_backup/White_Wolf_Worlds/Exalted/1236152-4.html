                Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                That, I grant you, may be a flaw in the design, but at the same time, maybe it isn't? Because if everyone can easily recover from being disarmed it kinda cheapens the gambit.

                "Oh, yeah, you can knock weapons out of people's hands. Don't bother though. It's just a waste of a turn 90% of the time. "

                Yes, this is actually a valid line of argument - but I think it's balanced out by the anima requisite (forcing characters who don't juggle out of aspect aura states a lot to give up free damage) and the relatively high cost (4m versus CtB's 1m).

                (Incidentally, this is why I really like Root-and-Hand Merging: it's expensive enough that you can't afford to always have it on, and it doesn't just undo the effect of a Disarm gambit - but it makes the option difficult and expensive enough to force your opponent to think deep and hard about whether they want to risk it. Too bad it's at least 32 sessions until you can pick it.)

                Another insidious effect of the Melee 5 prerequisite on Dragon-Graced Weapon is how it funnels weapons-only martial artists towards IMA. "Welp, I can't just dip into Melee to defend myself from being disarmed, I clearly need a secondary combat ability I can rely on... and I already paid for the Martial Artist merit... and unlocking the ability to ignore Terrestrial restrictions would be really really nice..." Whether that's a good or a bad thing is left to the reader's imagination.

                (It's the same issue with Elemental Sheath, actually - the set naturally funnels the players towards the most effective builds, but it has the side effect of screwing over players who knowingly forgo their most powerful options twice as hard.)

                                            Without Aura, you have the Dragon-Blooded of previous editions.

                                            With Aura, you can achieve more.

                                            When you play Warframe, nothing demands that you learn how to slide into a super jump followed by a double jump, or how to use your three special abillities, or even just your melee weapon. You could use nothing but your firearm and be just fine. Treat it like a cover-based shooter game.

                                            But achieving the absolute best performance with your character requires mastering her resources.

                                            Dragon-Blooded are the Exalted who have to spend the most time learning how to be awesome. They start early as kids and go to school specifically to learn from other Dragon-Blooded.

                                            Having a deeper and more complex meta-game to their Charms than Solars honestly seems to fit. A player starts out powerful and learns through experience (or the wisdom of those who came before) how to maximize their potential.

                                            Also, the Dragon-Blooded are fueled by five different, conflicting elemental Essences. Naturally you can’t always blend Air and Earth seemlessly together.

                                            Usually, “achieving balance” is about doing two, irreconcilable things in turns, rather than at the same time.

                                                        Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post
                                                        Why is a Dragon-Blood's ability to express her swordsmanship skills by arming herself with a spontaneously created weapon more strongly tied to her ability to wield weapons than that of the splat whose entire powerset is based on their expression of skill?
                                                        You forget that Dragon Bloods are just as much ability-based as solars are. They express this power differently but being the best in [ability] still defines everything that Dragon Bloods can acheive, just as it does so for solars.

                                                        Various quotes from the Devs on the design philosophy of solars vs DB:

                                                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                        as Abyssal Charms branch out and develop, they emulate the themes and powers of the Deathlords, much as those of the Solars emulate the Unconquered Sun. An Abyssal who serves the Mask of Winters (or no Deathlord at all!) is free to take Charms that invoke the themes of the Silver Prince or the First and Forsaken Lion.
                                                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                        From a thematic perspective, Abyssals have less need to rely on non-soulsteel Evocations to access "out-there" effects. Unlike the Solar Charms, which progress towards the singular point that is the Unconquered Sun's mythos, Abyssal Charms spiral out in a bunch of thematic directions as they build on the themes of the many Deathlords.
                                                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                        Lunar Charms are manifestations of superhuman power of the Attributes, often expressed through their shapeshifting. As their power progresses, it diverges from pure applications of Attributes to emulate the miracles of the witch-goddess Luna, much as Solars move towards emulating the Unconquered Sun.
                                                        And perhaps the most important one:
                                                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                        The aesthetics of a Charmset are typically gradated through its progression. For example, both Solar and Dragon-Blooded Charms at the lower end begin as expressions of superhuman skill, but Solars progress towards embodying the divine perfection of the Unconquered Sun while the Dragon-Blooded progress towards overt manipulation of elements and embodiment of elemental Essence.
                                                        Solars in 3E are not about 'being the best X in the world anymore. They may definitely become the best in the world in their field of expertise, but they're more focused and channeling and emulating the Unconquered Sun.

                                                        It's not about being based on expressions of skill in either case. Or at least, no more for one than the other. I think this is something that a lot of people have missed about 3E and it is SO IMPORTANT!

                                                        You can set out to be the greatest swordsman in the world as either a solar OR a Dragon Blood. Both will be able to do things that the other cannot dream about. Will a solar defeat a dragon blood in one-on-one combat in a duel, at Essence 5 with an even playing field? I think it's likely but honestly I don't know. I know it would be a hell of a closer match than in any other edition, and that it would be a hell of a show, regardless.
