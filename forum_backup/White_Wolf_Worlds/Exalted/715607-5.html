    Eminent Paragon Approach is an E3 Charm to let everyone know you have a Principle. Other methods of achieving this end: announce “I feel really strongly about X! Also, I have 8 XP available!”
    I think you're underestimating this quite a bit. Sure, you can claim that you have a defining Principle of 'I fight for Justice' or 'I must defend Creation/the weak' when meeting a Wyld Hunt, but they'll just shout "Lying Anathema, die!". If you use this charm, then they become aware without doubt that you DO have that Principle. Sure, you could try saying what the charm shows, but the thing is that Eminent Paragon Approach lets you prove it.

                                I play Read Intentions as an attempt to find out how someone feels, not as Stare Intently, which helps me make sure there's risk in any action I ask someone to roll for. Also, I don't set things up so the players can just shotgun rolls all the time, such as for lore checks. Awareness to avoid an ambush is one of the few exceptions. Other than that I tell the players what I think their characters would know, and if someone has a good reason to know more that I forgot about, I just tell them more.

                                Coming back to Read Intentions, I almost always play this as the character engaging with someone to find out how he feels. Typically that means having a conversation, though I'm open to other ways of provoking someone to reveal feelings (e.g. making a pass to see how the NPC reacts) or spying on someone to catch him in an unguarded moment. All of those carry at least some risk - in conversation you reveal that you're interested in a topic, for example, and following someone around or whatever has more obvious risks. Some Socialize charms get away from this, but that's OK since they have a cost. YMMV and all - I really dislike rolling without a meaningful downside, but not everyone prioritizes that of course.

                                            The charm feels almost like a magical version of "It's not what it looks like!" The situation I imagine is something like, powerful noble hears noises and comes running into his daughter's room in the middle of the night, to see her, huddling terrified in a corner wearing only her nightclothes, with a strange man standing over her, weapon drawn. Rather than risk him misunderstanding and calling for guards, you activate this charm, keying off your principle "I will not permit the dead to torment the living," at which point he automatically notices that you're not so much standing over her, as in front of her, and she's not staring terrified at you, but at the opposite corner of the room, and when he follows her gaze, sees two red eyes staring out of the darkness and ghostly claws form out of nothing. It can force people to notice things they might otherwise not have noticed in the heat of the moment by automatically making them pass a check they might not have bothered making at all. But in the situation being described in previous posts, there is no mechanical benefit to the situation. Yeah, you appear trustworthy, but of course you do, you're a deceptive evil demon. The DB doesn't know that he passed a check, he doesn't know that he made the check at all, he just knows that Anathema are evil, and have evil, deceptive magics, so what does it matter if everything appears to be kosher? Maybe if he prided himself on being able to see through any deception, it might give him pause, but without that reason to trust his eyes, why would he?
