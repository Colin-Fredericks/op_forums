        The Fair Folk changed since their earliest appearance. They went from what you have in the 1e core - "However, many of the Fair Folk have entered the world and have taken onshape to do so. Most of these beings are tricksters and hunters of men, but some coexist with mortals, ruling them as lords in some places and living among them as citizens in others" - to the 3e core - "Those Fair Folk who venture into reality survive by eating dreams or souls. Many claim to seek the dissolution of the world, though a few seem happy to play at life among mortals" (and actually most post 1e core, pre-3e core stuff went harder in the direction of insane, inimicable Fair Folk than the 3e core).

        That changed happened quite early in 1e actually (and many would say for the best!), making the Fair Folk less immediately playable as the strange, otherworldly sometime allies of the Exalted and mankind, but Graceful Wicked Masks and Fair Folk as playable were planned before that.

        So when Exalted: The Fair Folk happened it reoriented The Fair Folk to be the players of mysterious world shaping games and wars in the Wyld, so you could totally play down the aspects of destroying reality and preying on humans for courtly infighting among insane, but charming and courtly, Dancers At The End of Time sorts. I think it's an appealing venue to play about in, provided people like playing charming and stylish amoral characters, though that system for playing the reality shaping games and war of the Fair Folk in the Wyld was probably too complex for what it tried to do, being a repurposed multiplication of the 1e combat system (multiple health tracks and all) with a lot of heavy abstraction. It remains to be seen how much of that will survive!

                                                        Personally I love oWoD.

                                                        Mating in human form with an animal, or in animal form with a human? YUCK. That's bestiality.

                                                        But I think Lunars absolutely should be able to make yeddimfolk by shapeshifting into a yeddim and mating with a yeddim. I really don't understand the objection to this existing as an option. When you shapeshift, you become that animal. It's not an illusion, it's not a mind-game. You literally became a yeddim. You're a sapient yeddim with super magic powers and a human soul, but still a yeddim. Even moreso if the yeddim is a true form. To me that isn't bestiality. And for players who find any sex in their game uncomfortable there can be other options too. (see below)

                                                        I really have a problem with beastfolk always or mostly being Wyld mutants. The Wyld is fundamentally inimical to Creation. Anything mutated by the Wyld is probably helping to destroy Creation or at least human civilization. So having the Lunars allies with Wyld mutants who want to destroy civilization or need to taint the land because they can't even survive in Creation, fundamentally makes them villains. That's going backwards to the 1E "Lunars want to destroy cities and aqueducts and farming, Hulk smash." I really am not interested in Lunars as allies of the Fair Folk or out to destroy civilization, but that's probably what you have to do when your allies and armies have to be Wyld mutants.

                                                        It also means that if you want to raise an army, you have to be out on the edge of the map. Lunar games should not be limited to those places. Lunars should be capable of functioning in cities, in the Scavenger Lands, in the Deshan, wherever Solars can go. Part of the appeal to shapeshifting and being so adaptable is being able to adapt to anywhere. So why tell Lunar players that if they go 500 miles from the edge of reality, suddenly all their resources are gone? That's crappy.

                                                        Other ways to get beastfolk faster: Option #1 in my mind is the round up an army of beastfolk from the beastfolk who are already around. The 3E corebook has Prince Diamond helping some random wolffolk tribe and there's no mention of a Lunar anywhere in town (otherwise, the local Lunar probably would have explained to that river god what happens to gods who interfere with a Celestial's worshippers).

                                                        Also, beastfolk shouldn't only be anthropomorphic animals. I like those, but what about hawkfolk who look like baselines but with the eyesight of hawks? Why not camelfolk who look human but need as little water as camels, without the hump? If you can use some Lunar equivalent of genesis crafting/Verdant Emptiness Endowment to infuse animal blood into adults, why not flyfolk who look human but have the unbelievably fast reflexes of flies? Holy crap, flyfolk would be a terror in battle!

                                                        Lastly, Lunars in 3E have a policy of building armies of regular baseline humans, so that'll presumably be an option for PCs now. Yeah, there are a shitton of dispossessed and oppressed people in Creation, because the Realm is a horrible genocidal empire. Anyone a Solar can rally, a Lunar can also rally. Their Charms and methods will be different, presumably the results will differ in some way too. But the fictions says it can be done.
