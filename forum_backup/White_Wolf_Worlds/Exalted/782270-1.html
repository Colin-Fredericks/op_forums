            Lunars are Lunars.

            This is kind of a cheap answer, but it's also true and important because there is a strange tendency to try to answer this kind of question by looking at things that aren't Lunars, but carry a facet that Lunars also share, and say "this facet is Lunars". In truth, Lunars have all those facets, but are none of them in entirety.

            As for what those facets are, well, problem is, at least for the 3rd Edition, we don't have the book yet. This might also seem like a cheap answer, but it's also true, especially given how heavily some Exalt types were changed from their previous edition counterparts (such as Abyssals going from eternally hunted bad decision makers, a story that I adored, to romantic inscrutable emos, a story that I have no idea how to work with until the actual Abyssals book comes out and I see a full picture). You can, of course, try to give your own interpretations based on the limited information we have, but that nearly always leads to disappointment when the final product isn't exactly how you interpreted it would be.

            ((Also, Lunars don't rip out hearts and eat them in 2nd Edition. They hunt an animal (or other entity), kill it, and drink its heart's blood. Given that our "civilized society" regularly does far worse without as much tangible benefit, the distinction should be non-trivial to many.))

                Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                This had issues even in 2nd edition, which was super-hellbent on structuring the Exalted in a certain way in character and out. By the time Glories hit, we learned that the Incarnae couldn't actually design their Exalted to do anything other than be humans with powers patterned after the patron deity in question; this retconned out the "Unconquered Sun forced Luna to make Lunars subservient to Solars" detail as well. Also, in EX3, what Lunars are now is different than what they were in the Old Realm's span of rule or the Primordial War; whatever you see of them now doesn't hold true for any point you might want to make about past ages.
                During development of first edition the Lunars were corrupted Exalted of the Wyld, think Fair Folk exalts. Once First Edition came out the Lunars were the precursors to the Garou and other changing breeds in the W:tA setting. Second Edition comes around and they have completely ret conned the cWoD connection and made Exalted a stand alone line.

                First Edition Luna was the companion of the Unconquered Sun, then Luna became a matahari that seduced Gaia. Second Edition then comes out with concept that really Luna and Gaia were the real pairing and the Sun & Moon combo is almost just for show. The list goes on and on and on. Everything about Lunars has been retconned so many times that in truth none of it makes sense. The Luna is vague and used as filler, the Lunar exalts themselves lack design focus and more importantly agency. It wouldn't be so bad if they actually did stuff during the entire absence of the Solars, but at best they were given a backfilled explanation that some how a few hundred Lunars held back the entirety of the Wyld forces after the Contagion.

                You will note that I gave methods of looking at the Lunars, not canon answers of how they should be used. As for Ex.3, until it has actually come out, been published, finalized and White Wolf is working on the expansions, there is no reason to bear it any mind.

                Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                Furry fandom is something quite a bit different from shapeshifting beast-themed Exalted. It's an old saw that most people who identify with furries and who do play Exalted tend to just make Solar beastfolk, because most of those characters don't really get the same thing out of their appearance that the shapeshifting Lunars would (the fact that Lunars will often not spend time in their alleged "furry" form and tend to use that form explicitly to be a murder machine probably puts a lot of prospective anthropomorphic fans off of choosing them; rather than just being a lion or wolf person, the Lunar is the beast as a raging apocalyptic war avatar, a decidedly loaded feature). Also, it's really easy; you can make Solar beastfolk using just a few merits.
                But with a few merits you can do the same with any exalt type. As I pointed out, Lunars don't have to be anything. Thus on a mechanical and storyline level they work well for people who are in the game for the aesthetics rather than the power gaming, body count or empire building.

                                                I think that this might be overstating the problem (if I'm understanding the post correctly). From what I understand, you're confused as to what Luna might be with regards to her Chosen, such that they might become Lunar? If that's the case, I can say that it's not that difficult to do. Here's something I have thought up recently (actually whilst looking at the moon outside at night).

                                                At the full moon, Luna reveals her full glory, beautiful and terrible. Her power is obvious, and inspires worship, desire, and terror. She shines brilliantly upon Creation, an implicit threat to all those who would defy her. At the full moon, Luna’s power is explicit, and her might shines to all those who would dare to witness it, overpowering all others in the night sky. Full Moon Lunars are empowered by Luna in this state, and oftentimes their power is equally clear, albeit on a mortal scale. They are the fiercest warriors, the swiftest hunters, and can survive far more than any living body should.

                                                During the new moon, Luna’s power is hidden by her mystery. Though she does not seem to be there, her presence is implicit in the night sky. The wise fear these nights more than those during which she reveals her fullest glory, for although she cannot be seen, Luna remains vigilant. Luna in this state is unknown to all but her Chosen, and her mysteries are just as powerful as her glory. No Moon Lunars are granted the power to emulate Luna in this state. Unlike the Twilights of the Solars, No Moons are not revealers of great truths, though they may discover them, nor are they builders of great things, though some may construct things. Rather, No Moons are discoverers of mysteries, unlocking secrets and using them as they see fit. Their power may be subtler than their fellow Lunars, but it is just as real.

                                                In those times between full and new moons, Luna hides her full glory, but remains visible, a gorgeous figure dancing through the night sky. In these states, Luna’s terror is hidden, leaving only her beauty readily apparent, but the terror persists nonetheless, and those who find her beauty will find both. The changing moon reveals only that which Luna wishes, while the rest remains hidden, and what is revealed may mislead those who believe it is the entire truth of her being. The Changing Moons reflect this aspect of Luna. Beautiful, enticing, and/or magnetic, they are often the center of attention wherever they go, and use this to their advantage. Changing Moons reveal what parts of themselves they wish, drawing in those who might have use to them, only for their hidden sides emerge when the time is right...

                                                Obviously, this might not be what the devs do with it, but it shows that Luna can be shown to have concrete themes, even though one of them is change. Also, I'm aware that there's a great deal of flexibility between the castes, perhaps even greater than among Solars (by simple virtue of attribute charms), but I think that cores such as these might be acceptable concepts for Lunars.

                                                All that I write but don't cite is simply my perspective, colored by my experiences and beliefs. I extrapolate a lot, too, so don't take it too seriously. :P

                                                    These threads never sum an Exalt, but! still, my reflections at the moment:

                                                    The Lunars: Mighty heroes of humanity who achieve great deeds of legend for man by reshaping their bodies and minds, to take on the strengths of animals and monsters.

                                                    As for the Chosen of Luna, and what that means, thinking about it for a second, I'm interested in Luna as an outsider hero among the pantheon of the Incarna.

                                                    The Unconquered Sun is the king, which means that, like a king, he expects of obedience, service and domination of his people and in return, his compact is that he acts as a champion and vessel of their spirit. The Maidens are guides, advisors and shapers - they offer only advice, to be taken or left, guidance which can uplift, but to refuse it can come with consequences, and to take it can be to be shaped in secret. Luna's an outsider. She comes as a stranger to the pantheon, to be within it through her role as lover (to Gaea). That's not an essence you can always trust - at times it can be predatory, alienated, self interested - but it does not seek to make itself your master or your shaper (either of those chains, of sorts) and exalt itself into that role.
