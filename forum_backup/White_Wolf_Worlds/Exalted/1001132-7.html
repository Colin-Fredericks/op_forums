    Here is the thing about writing ideas. They always pop up. Always. I got ideas for three different quests brewing, two different letter writing games, and three different real life games. That doesn't include short story/novel ideas.

    Ideas are easy. Finishing what you started is hard. You simply do not have enough time/energy to create every idea you have. Focus on the current project, write down the other ideas and save them for later.

    /soapbox

    Restrain commoners and save them for the magistrate. They are his problem. If you can't restrain them, let them go and let the Magistrate's people hunt them down.

    But... the Oathsworn who violated his Oaths? Challenge him to a Duel. A smear on one Oathsworn's honor is a smear on all Oathsworn's honor.

    I write things.

                                            Originally posted by L'het'esh View Post
                                            You two are counting on Kasai winning. It would be a quick end to the thread otherwise, but that's metagaming. What would Kasai do if he lost? What would nameless do? So why would he agree instead of elevating to death?

                                            It all depends on what sort of duel we challenge him to.

                                            If it is a lethal duel, then the outcome is obvious. Of course, if Kasai gets killed, Blue Pines would certainly not just let him walk away, and neither would the peasants. If he won, he would almost-certainly get lynched, and he knows this. This, coupled with the fact that he doesn't view the killing of a peasant, something which was technically within his rights to do so, as something "worth" fighting a duel to the death over, meaning he wouldn't want to fight to the death, and likely try to refuse a challenge.

                                            Of course, refusing a duel outright would mean he would now get viewed as a coward and a bully, and his reputation in the region would get shot. He doesn't want that, so in that case, he would prefer to duel.

                                            If it were a non-lethal duel;

                                            If Kasai won, the Oathsworn would be honor-bound to concede the point to Kasai, and in this case, submit.
                                            If the Oathsworn won, Kasai would be honor-bound to let him go, as much as he and the commoners wouldn't like it.

                                            The Oathsworn would almost certainly prefer to duel to one or three-wounds, while Kasai (in what I think of him, at least) would prefer to duel to the death. However, cashiering/stripping a defeated Oathsworn of his status would be almost as acceptable, and in some ways more acceptable (it would certainly send a stronger message) , than killing him outright.

                                            As far as possibility of injuries:

                                            In a duel to the death, anything goes. Even victorious duelists can come away with broken bones, severe blood loss, grievous cuts anywhere armor doesn't cover, etc.

                                            In a non-lethal duel, both parties are honor-bound to do their best to prevent actually-lethal wounds, although accidents (and "accidents") are always possible. Since both parties are in armor, they would actually have to put some effort into killing each other, and most injuries in a non-lethal duel are going to be relatively superficial. (Basically, if the Oathsworn killed Kasai, or vice-versa, polite society would have a "what the fuck, man?!" type-reaction, and lead to mild social shunning)

                                            As for the Oathsworn:

                                            He isn't nearly that nihilistic. He is relatively polite, charming and such, it is just that he, like most Lowlander high-nobility (although this guy isn't that rich, but is more affecting the behaviors of the upper class as a front), think of themselves as superior-by-birth to the peasants, and is consequently snotty. Most Lowlander nobility are going to be relatively isolated from the commoners (having Peasants direct Serfs, etc), and that, coupled with the Immaculate Faith preaching "superiority through reincarnation", leads to most nobility treating peasants with mild, if coolly polite, disdain.

                                            Kasai, on the other hand, like many poor Lowlander noble families (especially those living on the fringes of Lowlander society, like in the hinterlands and such), interacted with the peasantry much more, to the point where he and his father helped out at planting and harvesting. They didn't have the money to hire many extra workers, and since their effectively-entire income came from the work of the peasants, they actually got down into the dirt and gave a hand. Even if they didn't have to, Kasai's father likely would have made him, if just to promote the idea that "peasants are people too."

                                            Kasai gets the overwhelming majority of his moral upbringing, and therefore most of his Oathsworn sensibilities, from his father. His father is the most moral man that he knows (something that actively held his father back from promotion, although Kasai doesn't know that), and he tries to emulate him as best he can.

                                            This means that the Oathsworn flat-out doesn't understand why Kasai is being so potentially-pissy about this. Of course, he knows about the Oath of Compassion (he swore it, like many/most Oathsworn), but in his mind his noble status comes first, and he expects Kasai to believe the same.

                                                    So, it appears everyone wants to duel this Oathsworn, with two for “single strike” and 3 for “generic” duel, which I am going to take as “three strikes”. To be fair, I am going to metaphorically flip a coin:

                                                    Heads is “single strike”

                                                    Tails is “three strikes”

                                                    RANDOM.ORG - Coin Flipper
                                                    https://www.random.org/coins/?num=1&cur=60-usd.0025c-md
                                                    This form allows you to flip virtual coins based on true randomness, which for many purposes is better than the pseudo-random number algorithms typically used in computer programs.


                                                    Tails it is!
                                                    ------

                                                    You sigh, rubbing at the bridge of your nose to ward off the oncoming headache.

                                                    “Blue Pines, find some way to restrain these men, until we can find a way to bring them before the city magistrate.” Turning to the gathered vigilantes, “While I appreciate the idea that you hold me as an authority figure, I really can’t pass judgement, not legally. As such, the culprits will be restrained, and turned over to the rightful authorities. Is that acceptable?”

                                                    The leader flushes, and then nods his assent, not very happy with the outcome, but accepting your decision nonetheless.

                                                    Hooking his hands under his sash, the Oathsworn rocks back on his heels, “So, does this mean I am free to go?” His half-smile indicates just how seriously he is taking all of this.

                                                    He is surprised to see you frown, stepping forward in front of him as you do so. “….. No.”

                                                    “..What?”

                                                    “Your behavior, even in light of current events, is despicable. Do you not know of the Oath of Compassion?”

                                                    Annoyed, “Of course I do! What the hell are you going on about?”

                                                    “Then your actions are to be doubly damned! Instead of killing people, you should have been here, helping organize the rescue effort, not cowering in your tent away from the danger. What do you have to say in defense of your behavior?!”

                                                    “I…. is this about the peasant? Listen, I swore the Oath of Compassion, but that doesn’t mean the common rabble can just mouth off whenever they want! He had to be put in his place, made an example of! There are rules about that sort of thing, and I..”

                                                    “Yes, there are, and you broke them. How dare you. How dare you.”

                                                    “…. I don’t really understand why you are making such a big deal about this. In a way, this is a “good thing”, as I am now here, and can help out, therefore fulfilling my Oaths. Is that not correct?”

                                                    In response, you pop the hanguard of your sword from the sheath with your thumb, ringing bronze against brass, a soft sound that nonetheless carries across the sand with all the impact of a temple bell. Even the low muttering of the crowd silences, and the Oathsworn jerks back as if struck, face pale and eyes wide.

                                                    “You’ve…… you’re joking. You have got to be kidding me. This, of all things, is worth fighting over?”

                                                    “Yes. This, of all things, is most definitely worth fighting over! We are supposed todefend these people, serve as examples and role-models, not cow them with fist and blade. Our status is but a means toward that end, not the end itself.”

                                                    “Your behavior and demeanor disgusts me, and your flagrant flouting of your Oaths dishonors not only me, but anyone else who swears the same Oaths, holds themselves to the same standards. On top of that, your lack of remorse for your deed is appalling, and shows your true colors. In my eyes, you have no honor, and pretending to do so dishonors everyone around you. There is but one action possible in the face of such an insult, and I mean to take it.”

                                                    You arch an eyebrow, “Unless, of course, you wish to admit to your failings, and submit for proper sentencing. That would but reinforce your cowardice, however.”

                                                    He stares, and then responds animatedly, “You know you can’t just “have” a duel, you need seconds, and witnesses, and challenges! A bout with honor and dignity, not just some roughs tangling in the street!”

                                                    The other eyebrow raises, “Well, as far as I can see it, we have witnesses, and seconds; my armswoman and the militiaman, who is by comparison a neutral party. You can consider this my official challenge, so all that remains is your answer.”

                                                    Raising your head, to glare full into his face, “Well? If you do not accept, I will have no choice but to consider your denial as submission, and you will be restrained with the rest of the lawbreakers.” Smirking, “I am sure we can find some nice chains to make sure you won’t cause any more trouble.”

                                                    He glances around, defeated, at the angry faces of the surrounding crowd, at you, full of wrath. “Is this going to be a duel to the death?”

                                                    “No. Unlike you, I am not a murderer. “

                                                    He snarls, angry, “ Don’t call me that!”

                                                    “I am only speaking the truth. Win the duel, and I will call you whatever you want. What weapon do you choose?”

                                                    After a moment, he smirks coldly, “The sword, of course. I will have you know that I attended the Seigen school of swordcraft, and knelt on the dojo floor in front of Sifu Seigen himself.”
                                                    You shrug, “I have no idea what that means, nor do I care.”

                                                    “It means you are going to have a bad time.”

                                                    You shrug again, if only to irritate the guy a little more. “Blue Pines, stand over…… here. Haru, you go stand opposite her, about 30 feet away. Everyone else, stand outside of them, and don’t get in the way, or interfere.”

                                                    It takes a few moments for the crowd to shuffle away from the imaginary line drawn in the sand, and soon you are left alone with the Oathsworn, glaring at each other across the empty expanse.

                                                    The air is thick with tension, and on the dropping of a length of timber to the sand, you explode into action. Sprinting toward the man, drawing your blade as you do so, you are forced to suddenly backpedal in the face of several blinding slashes aimed at your face. He is fast: you didn’t even see him draw, and you are soon put on the back foot from his rapid, looping slashes and thrusts.

                                                    Some form of consternation must have shown itself in your face, because the Oathsworn suddenly laughs, drawing back as he does so into a high-guard, blade lifted over his head. In response, you shift into a low-guard, handle held at waist-height with the blade pointed at his knee, in effort to draw him into overexposing himself.

                                                    He doesn’t fall for the bait, instead rapidly stepping forward, stomping hard on the sand and bringing his sword down in a mighty blow. You twist to the side, in an effort to dodge, and try to knock his blade to the side to set up a strike of your own, to no avail. His blade snakes around your own, and your elbow burns with cold fire as he scores a cut on the underside of your arm with the weak edge of his sword.

                                                    He exults, crowing, “That’s one!”

                                                    You give no response as you head back to your starting position; best not to show weakness, and being stone-faced could intimidate the bastard as much as pain could provoke him.
                                                    Back in your spot, while waiting for the start signal to impact the earth, you consider your options. This guy is fast, surprisingly so, and he apparently uses that alongside an aggressive style of footwork and strikes to quickly overwhelm and wound the opponent. You, on the other hand, prefer a more defensive style, probably inherited from your skills with the spear, and lack the speed to really compete with him on that level.

                                                    However, you are fairly certain you’ve got a few inches at least on him, and that means you are probably stronger, with a higher degree of leverage. If you can get him in a bind, or throw some grappling moves into the mix, you could very well defeat him on that front. Sounds like a plan or, at least, something better than just feeding yourself into his strikes.

                                                    This time, when the timber strikes the ground, you don’t rush out of your position, instead stepping carefully forward, blade held in a middle-guard, handle again at waist-height, blade pointed at his stomach. He, in turn, takes up the same stance as before.

                                                    The two of you slowly orbit around each other, neither one stepping forward to test what the other hand planned. This, apparently, psyches out the Oathsworn, who proceeds to taunt and degrade you from across the circle, calling you a coward and other choice insults. You, all part of your plan, don’t even dignify him with a response, instead continuing your slow circling, still in a defensive position.

                                                    After but a few moments, he has apparently had enough waiting, and with a wild yell, charges forward, blade still raised high. In response, you step into his strike, getting inside the reach of his blade, so that the weakest part of the strike lands on your armored shoulder. Your mail crunches, and the impact causes your knees to buckle and you to grunt in pain, but your plan, so far, works flawlessly; his blade is relatively neutralized, and you are up close where you can use your size and leverage to your advantage.

                                                    To that end, you grab hold of your own sword by the tip, and bring the whole thing up into his elbows, in effort to push his arms (and blade) off the centerline and allow you to attack. This takes him by surprise, apparently, and he staggers off to the side, only to shout in pain as you draw your sword across his cheek.

                                                    “One for me”, you say calmly, already turning around to head back to your position. He stands still for a moment; hand over his bleeding cheek, before spitting some blood onto the sand in your general direction and returning to his spot.

                                                    In spite of yourself, you can’t help but feel a sense of satisfaction (?) at how the duel is going so far. Sure, sure, you are both evenly wounded, but your wound is comparatively minor, while he has in return taken a wound across the face, which is bleeding heavily now. The pain from the wound would evaporate any sense of cockiness he might have, and he could be intimidated or even cowed as a result. Both far preferable to his aggressiveness and blinding speed from earlier.

                                                    Unfortunately, it would seem the cut had the opposite effect, incensing him instead. Straight off the bat, he charges in at a dead sprint, chopping down from left to right in great, glittering arcs. This time, you step to the side of the strike, interspersing your own blade at the nadir of his strike. With a push to the side, you divert his blade again, flowing the movement into an elbow strike that impacts with his nose most satisfactorily. With a strangled cry, the Oathsworn staggers, and drops to a knee, only to get cut again, this time on the opposite cheek.

                                                    “Now, does that count for one strike, or for two?” You quip, almost amused, as you turn to head back to your side of the circle.

                                                    “Fuck you!”, through a broken lip and mouthful of blood. “I’ll kill you!”

                                                    “Based on your current score, I don’t find that too likely. What will I bre.”

                                                    With a sudden, furious yell, he lunges across the sand, blade in hand. Before you can turn around, he drives his sword deep into your upper thigh, up under the mail of your thigh-guards. White fire lances up and down the limb and it collapses beneath you, dropping you to the ground with a gasp. It hurts so much you can’t even scream, just curl up into a ball on the sand.

                                                    The Oathsworn draws back in sudden shock, stammering, “ Oh shit!…. I….. I didn’t mean to…”

                                                    Mastering yourself through the pain, you manage to grasp the blade projecting out the back of your thigh with enough leverage to slowly pull the steel out. If possible, it hurts even worse than the initial stabbing, and tears come unbidden, streaming down your face.

                                                    Painfully, you manage to stand up into a sort of half- crouch, enough to look out at the circle of onlookers. Most of the peasants are staring, in various shades of shock and horror, while Blue Pines looks pissed enough to spit the bastard like a pig at a barbecue. None of them, however, move to intervene, whether from fear of reprisal or shock.

                                                    By bracing a hand on your knee, and locking the joint, you are able to stand up to close to full height, shuffling as you do so in order to face the Oathsworn challenger, who flinches in fear and shame, diverting his eyes.

                                                    “… Way to prove your cowardice, striking from behind.”

                                                    He cringes, expecting a blow. Instead, you toss him his sword, where it bounces in the sand at his feet.

                                                    “Let’s finish this.”

                                                    Once you manage to actually put weight on the limb, it actually stops hurting to a significant degree, throbbing instead of burning, and you find yourself actually not too bothered by the blow. Instead, a cold kind of calm spreads through your body, and you shake your head to clear it, the better to focus on the man in front of you.

                                                    You are focusing so intently that you don’t even hear the starting signal (if someone even remembered to set it off), and react to your opponents movements after the fact. He is already half way over to you before you start lifting your blade, and it takes all the effort you can muster to deflect the falling blow from your face. You can’t exactly dodge, so instead you drive your shoulder into his, driving him back and scoring a line across his lamellar as he stumbles. Since you are already moving with him, you continue the check, stumbling forward and carving another sloppy gouge over his breastplate. Again, you follow him back, too close now to actually strike. You actually fall into him, confused, and he drops down to a knee from your sudden dead-weight. Grabbing on to him, as much to hold yourself upright as to hold him in place, you clumsily (your fingers don’t want to work. Odd.) pull your dagger from its sheath and hold it to his throat.

                                                    “… Do you yield?”

                                                    “…. I do! Stop fighting!”

                                                    “G… Good. Give me your sword.”

                                                    Taking the blade, you turn to address the crowd, “Through my victory, I prove my superiority, and uphold the intent of my Oaths. Men, take this miscreant and…an.” The fog in your mind is suddenly overwhelming, and none of your extremities quite feel like working properly all of a sudden. The blade drops to the sand with a distant muffled clang, your vision fades to almost-black, and the last thing you see before passing out is the dim sight of a shape rushing at you in the fog.

                                                    ---------




















                                                    It feels like millennia before you are able to draw yourself fully from your sleep, down on the edge of Oblivion, and even now you are “awake”, you can’t quite see anything. An inky blackness covers your eyes, and you panic, reaching up to your face to make sure your eyes are still there, that the spirits of the dead didn’t steal them when you tarried too close to their realm.

                                                    To your surprise (and immense relief), you find there is a strip of cloth wound about your face, around your forehead and eyesockets. Puzzled, you feel around the winding bandage, searching for the knot, only to hear the sudden rasp of a door sliding back. You jerk your head towards the unseen motion, only to be struck by a sudden, overwhelming sense of nausea and weakness. Choking back bile, you reach out towards the sound, calling out but only managing a croaking rasp. You are rewarded by the sound of shattering ceramic and the patter of running feet. Cursing, you grasp at the floorboards, trying to pull yourself towards the sound, but stricken with weakness as you are, you just manage to effectively pull yourself off your futon and into the middle of the room, gasping and drenched in sweat. After a few minutes (maybe longer. You might have passed out, to be honest), the sound of footfalls return, multiple ones, and you are greeted by the voice of Lord Iysen.

                                                    “Well, about time. You’ve been lying in bed for long enough!”

                                                    Despite the choice of words, there is no chiding in her voice, only relief (?), and she walks over to where you are laying. Kneeling down, she gently places a cool hand on the side of your face, helping you sit up in order to accept a cup full of water. After a few sips, the desert that was your mouth and tongue is capable of speech once more, and you croak out,

                                                    “What happened? Why are my eyes bandaged?”

                                                    Lord Isyen laughs, more of a huff than a chuckle, “Oh, believe me Kasai, plenty has happened. In a way, I envy you. You got to sleep through it.”

                                                    “Firstly, your eyes are bandaged because they were damaged by smoke and heat. My physician thought it best if they were protected from light until they recovered. Do you want the wrapping off?”

                                                    At a nod, she fumbles around by the back of your head, tugging at the linen until; finally, the wrappings come away from your eyes. Almost instantly, even with the low level of light in the room, your eyes tear up and pain, and it takes a few moments for them to clear. Blinking away tears, you gradually make out the figure of Lord Iysen, kneeling beside you, and Airi (you think), her youngest daughter, standing in the doorframe.

                                                    “So….. I take it that there was an assassination attempt?”

                                                    At this, Lord Iysen turns and looks to her daughter, who closes the door and leaves.

                                                    “You would be right. In hindsight, it was so obvious, to the point that I am embarrassed to admit that I didn’t see it happening earlier.”

                                                    She sits back; closing her eyes, and looks every bit of her forty-or-so years.

                                                    “My former son-in-law was masterminding a plot to have me assassinated. As you discovered, in that letter of yours.”

                                                    You stare, considering the implications.

                                                    “…. I don’t want to sound stupid, here, but what would he get out of that? Isn’t your daughter your heir, so she would be Lord Xia after your death?”

                                                    Her lips work,” True, but Mino was absolutely enthralled with Haichi, and would likely have gone along with whatever he wanted. His effect on her was pretty much the only reason I allowed the betrothal to go through, and she was so happy, so comparatively well-behaved after the fact, that I…… well, I must have ignored several obvious signs.”

                                                    “So, there will be war then? The Lord Yue would certainly not take too kindly to his son and heir being killed.”

                                                    “…. Haichi is still alive, Kasai.”

                                                    “Wait, what?”

                                                    “After the assassination attempt, I was in no condition to go chase down a fleeing man,” At this, she holds up her hands, which have dual half-healed slices deep in the palms. “Plus, my bodyguards were dead, and I had to look out for my children. On top of that, the fire he and his cronies set prevented any real sort of effective response. You saw that yourself first-hand.”
                                                    “….I see. I…. I am sorry about Nori. I found him in the audience-box.”

                                                    “Thank you, that means quite a lot. We were…… close.”

                                                    There are a few moments of silence, rather awkward on your part, until;

                                                    “So,… what else happened. How are your children holding up?”

                                                    She stands, walking over to the window. “To say that Mino is taking it rather hard is an understatement. She has locked herself in her room for the last two weeks, an..”

                                                    “Two weeks! It has been two weeks since the attack?!”

                                                    “Yes, Kasai. You have been unconscious for the last two weeks. A little longer, actually. Not surprising, considering how badly-wounded you were. Between the blood-loss, the infection, the lack of sleep, and the dehydration, you almost died several times. I, to be crude, have had to spend a lot of silver, sweat, tears and blood to keep you alive.”

                                                    She grins, “Blood is literal, by the way. You received several blood transfusions, from me and from your page. We both were compatible, and you took a lot out of us. And my Airi, who has all-but taken over as your caregiver while you recovered. Do you realize how much you weigh, and how difficult it is to move you as dead-weight?”

                                                    You stare, confused. “Lord….. if I may ask; why did you go through so much effort to keep me alive? Not that I don’t appreciate it, but…. I am a nobody. Not important in the least.”

                                                    She kneels down close, “You are important, Kasai. Firstly, because of what you did during the fire, and what you have been doing ever since you got here. Saiko told me what you did at her village, and Blue Pines recapped what happened in the square. The people that you saved during the fire have been all-but singing your praises for the last two weeks. I am fairly certain that if you walked into any public-house in the city, you could drink for free, and if I just left you to die, I could have had a riot on my hands.”

                                                    “More importantly, what you did was rare. Most Oathsworn don’t pay nearly as much attention to the intent of their Oaths as you do. Hell, I didn’t, and don’t. Such is the reality of ruling a country, unfortunately. Such determination is worthy of respect, if not outright admiration, and I would seek to cultivate that, if I could. For that reason alone, I would seek to gain your loyalty. I am going to need every skilled warrior I can find, after all.”

                                                    She inhales, then, “On top of that, I owe you a life-debt. Without your interference, I most-certainly would have died in the assassination plot, probably after Mino and Haichi were already married. You, almost single-handedly, saved my life, saved the lives of my children, and prevented my domain from falling into the hands of the Lord Yue. I owe you everything I have at this moment.”
                                                    With this, she bends at the waist and presses her forehead to the floor, in the most formal of formal bows.

                                                    You stare silently, in shock at the sudden reversal of protocol, and after a moment, she raises back into her kneeling position.

                                                    “With that in mind….. What do you want, Kasai?”
