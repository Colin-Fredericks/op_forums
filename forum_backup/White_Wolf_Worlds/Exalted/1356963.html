    The Realm and Creation circa 718

    12-25-2019, 08:54 PM
    Part of the campaign I am currently working on will be set about 50 years before the present, around RY 718 (give or take a few years). This however does present a few things, because the Realm was a different place back then... and a number of events of the last 50 years have yet to happen.

    Some of the highlights are that House V'neef had yet to be established (V'neef herself was only 11 or so at the time, and may not have Exalted just yet); House Iselsi had not yet been stricken from the Imperial ledgers (as the Empress had not yet finished dismantling the house piece by piece for their failed assassination attempt back in 643)... and those are just among the Great Houses. Many smaller events have yet to happen... both within the Realm and outside it. Satraps may have been replaced in any number of places as the Houses that control them shift and change.

    I am trying to compile a list of all the things that may have changed over these 50 years, but could use some help as the majority of my Exalted collection (1E & 2E books) is inaccessible and not a lot of world information has yet been provided for 3E. I have a few 2E books on PDF, but most of them deal only with events of the last 5 years in regards to Creation (Autochthon and Alchemicals). My focus will be mostly the Realm and the Southwest (An-Teng and areas between the mountains and the Western Ocean). There may be brief excursions to other areas of Creation as I catch the players up to the present after the first part of the Campaign finishes, but the major events will happen as presented (the players are adventuring in the past, not changing it as their characters have no knowledge of future events).

    Any help the community can provide in things that were likely different 50 years ago will be appreciated. Of course mortal leaders will be different then than they are now... it is more where Dragon-Blooded commanders, governors and satraps may have been appointed due to various reasons over this time that I will need to know (as well as showing younger versions of those who have held their position for some time).

    For example, I am fairly sure that the current Satrap of An-Teng (at least as presented in 2E) is fairly new to his position while the Garrison Commander has been stationed there for a few decades (I am entertaining the thought that the Garrison Commander in present An-Teng will get the post after the events of the first half of the Campaign, while the current Satrap will be assigned shortly before the second half begins... of course requiring very different Dragon-Blooded to fill these positions for the campaign).

    I wish I knew more about the new locations in the area introduced in 3E, but hopefully Across the Eight directions will be released by the time I start this game and can update my notes before such information becomes important.

    There are three types of people in the world... those who can count and those who can't.
    I reject your reality and substitute my own!

          The Realm conquered Zhaojūn about fifty years ago. During the war, An-Teng would have been a key stopover point for Imperial Navy forces and Imperial legions en route to the conflict. After the war, it would likewise have seen all manner of Realm grandees passing through, as the Realm established its administration in Zhaojūn and the Empress shuffled the leases of other Meiyu Coast satrapies among the Great Houses. Likewise, Zhaojūn's current Tengese underclass would have been formed in large part from Tengese laborers conscripted and transported to Zhaojūn during the postwar reconstruction.

              I am sorry, but the information in Compass of Terrestrial Directions Volume 4: The South must trump that found in Blood and Salt... as the former is 2E and therefore more recent than the latter which is 1E. However, General Shuri apparently only has years, not the decade I originally thought, of a head start in making deals for House Ledaal over the Satrap's atempts to do the same for House Ragara.

              Given that General Shuri sees his posting to An-Teng as a well deserved reward for his service in the Vermilion Legion (which up until it was commanded by the Roseblack was seen as the worst Legion in the Realm), I could see him as preferring to stay where he is, even for half a century. Personality-wise he is described as an individual who was a rather decadent Dynast... so much so that House Ledaal assigned him to the worst Legion the Realm had in hopes of shaping him into something useful. While he did learn a fair bit about command and fighting, he still retained his original attitude. This means that he finds life in An-Teng comfortable to his desires (indulging in all the vices the nation can provide) while only doing the minimum he has to do to maintain his position (set up deals with merchants and make sure House Ledaal is informed about any relics found in the area).

              While it may be uncommon for such positions to be filled for long periods of time, it isn't unheard of for some Dragon-Bloods to set themselves up in positions of power where they can act as the head of their own little empire while paying at least nominal lip service to their House and the Realm. For General Shuri, this means making sure his forces are prepared to defend An-Teng. Until it is shown that he can't perform this duty (or make the deals that House Ledaal desires) there is no real need to remove him from a position where he wants to be and where he's behavior can at least be tolerated. General Shuri simply has no desire to leave An-Teng... and he isn't causing enough problems for him to be removed by force.

              As for the the Realm conquest of Zhaojun, I had missed that information in the Realm book (thanks for pointing it out). It can work both ways for my game... either as the backdrop for the Wyld Hunt my players will be active with in the jungles around the Silent Crescent and the Fount of Mourning (I believe those are located north of Zhaojun and south of An-Teng if the area remains true to previous editions) and could be the reason the Hunt doesn't get the full complement of Soldiers it requires (as they are needed to reinforce the fighting in Zhaojun)... or I can have the Wyld Hunt prove the need for the Realm to have an increased presence in the area... leading to the conquest of Zhaojun at this point in time. In short, this is a fortunate coincidence.

              It can even play into how General Shuri gets his posting in An-Teng, provided he leads the part of the Vermilion Legion he commands well. Hmmm... maybe making him a Dragonlord at this time and wanting to avoid fighting in the Zhaojun Campaign might make him consider "volunteering" his Dragon to the Wyld Hunt while he remains behind to recruit new soldiers to defend the An-Teng garrison... and provide back up to both should it be needed seems like the sort of underhanded thing a Dragon-Blood more interested in their own debauchery might do. It makes him look like he is supporting both problems, thus making him a logical candidate to remain as the General of the An-Teng garrison when both situations are resolved (especially since his is making a "sacrifice" to help the Wyld Hunt... which earns him the support of the Immaculate Order for his appointment in An-Teng.

              Yes, this does work with my plans... better than I originally intended. Got to love how these sorts of things work out. With his success in helping House Ledaal gain influence in the are over the next few decades, it is little surprise that House Ragara will want to curtail Ledaal's influence in the area, arranging for one of their scions to get the An-Teng Satap position some time later (I am thinking that it has only been since the Empress' disappearance that House Ragara has been able to leverage the appointment, especially since House V'neef has appointed a Satrap to Zhaojun... another House Ragara wants watched.

              This is shaping my game in ways I never thought it would... and I am loving the ideas that are flowing from it. I have to thank you for reminding me about this area (I had completely forgotten it had been mentioned).

              As for the Anathema the Wyld Hunt will be dealing with, I am thinking that they are taking advantage of the situation between the Realm and Zhaojun... launching raids against the Realm to help Zhaojun (more an enemy of my enemy is my friend type situation than any real alliance... and the Lunar presence could also be justification for bringing the area under Imperial rule). It is likely done more to keep the Realm form getting a good foothold in the area that would make things difficult for the Lunar (and her new apprentice) in building their own nation than out of any real concern for Zhaojun... but the opportunity is there to make the Realm bleed a little more manpower than it would otherwise, so why not, right? Make them fight on two fronts and weaken them more will only help both sides against the Realm. Of course the Lunar didn't intend to attract a Wyld Hunt to the area, but with it undermanned due to the Zhaojun offensive, it might end up as a failed hunt, given her "secret" weapon.

              So many ways to go with this... it works perfectly with what I originally wanted... better than what I originally planned.

                      Irrelevant and ultimately meaningless... unless you wish to police games to make sure they conform to your preconceptions of what 3E may or may not end up being in a given area.

                      Ultimately how specific details come about in the setting are not usually specified except in broad strokes. We know that the Tepet Legions were shattered by the Bull of the North (and that he lost at least one of his Circle in the battle), but many of the specifics are not detailed. Similarly, we know that in both 1E and 2E General Shuri and the Satrap have gotten their positions for various reasons, though the exact details are glossed over (such as exactly when). We can also assume that, given Realm politics, having anyone in a specific position long term is uncommon, but not impossible (especially for someone good at their job).

                      My only real goal was to make the specific setting my players encounter consistent with what is established... not an exact mirror of what may or may not happen. Maybe General Shuri gets his position 50 years ago... maybe he doesn't. It is unlikely that the setting itself will present his history to such a degree. How and when the Satrap gets the position he holds in the present is also unlikely to be detailed, allowing Storytellers the freedom to justify it as they see fit, should such information become relevant to their games (this detail likely isn't going to be relevant in mine so I probably won't detail it too much unless my players need the information for some reason).

                      Ultimately though, having my players interact in this conflict with younger versions of characters they may encounter later on in the setting (in the present) will help make them feel like their actions have an impact on how the setting evolved. They will remember their interaction with Dragonlord Ledaal Shuri 50 years earlier who has used his aid of the players and their Wyld Hunt to his advantage in getting promoted to General in the An-Teng Satrapy should they learn of his position 50 years later. This will make them feel as if their actions actually had an impact (even though he would have had the position regardless of their interaction). It is this feeling of connection I want to develop in this first part of the game... that the actions of the players did have an impact on the current setting even though things ultimately would have played out the same regardless of their actions. They will feel as if they were a part of history rather than merely adventuring in the past with no effect on how things ended up.

                      So like I said... how 3E seems to be presented in regards to 1E or 2E information is irrelevant and ultimately meaningless. I am not trying to predict what 3E will do, but rather trying to present something that will be consistent with the setting as we currently know it. Until 3E shows otherwise, 2E setting material still tends to trump 1E as it is the more recent canon. It may not be how you would go, but then what you would do is irrelevant in anyone's games but your own (no offense intended).

                      Lastly... I only have access to the 2E information anyways (and not much of that)... so trying to make it more consistent with 1E is beyond my available information regardless of any other considerations.

                          While you can and should do whatever makes you happy with your game, I would like readers to understand that Exalted Third Edition is a different game from 1e and 2e, and while it draws very heavily on previous editions, it ultimately has a different setting from 1e and 2e. Neither 1e nor 2e setting material is canon in 3e. 2e material is not "more recent canon."

                          I want to be clear that I am in no way attempting to discourage you or anyone else from drawing on 2e material for your home game!
