    Um. I might need stats for a Deathlord.

    02-12-2020, 07:28 PM
    My players are escorting a caravan of around 800 living people out of the deep Underworld, and they've just hit the borders of lands claimed by Walker in Darkness. Encouraged by a friendly encounter with one of his Abyssal and some totally trustworthy advice they don't remember getting from the Green Lady, they decided that it would be a good idea to send about half the party ahead to visit him and pay their respects. And my over-dramatic ass decides to have him pop up right next to them while they're staring up at his fortress and wondering "wait... was this a good idea?" And that's where the session ended.

    Obviously, I'm not planning on having Walker try to murder them. He's actually pretty well-disposed towards the party right now; they've killed a couple Abyssals working for one of his main rivals and stopped a major invasion she was launching. But I can't imagine he'd pass up the chance to dribble some corruption into their ears.

    The players currently in his face include:
    • A Presence-supernal Eclipse with a bit of medicine and Silver-Voiced Nightingale. Not so hot at mechanics.
    • A Lore-supernal Twilight with a bit of Sorcery and raw strength. She's got a strong ranged attack (a variant of The Burning Name using longbow stats), but no other combat magic.
    • A Dodge-supernal Night with the usual mix of Rogue-y stuff.

    So, uh... suggestions? I've never done much social encounter building before.

              For whatever you think is in his wheel-house: throw 15-20-ish dice at it (or equivalent DV). If it's not in his wheel-house: 8-12 dice. Pick any set of Solar Charms (or equivalent effects) you think are appropriate for him, don't worry about prerequisites unless they also fit.

              I think nailing his personality in the encounter will be more important than the raw mechanics. I'd spend time thinking about what his goals, ties, and principles are and making some good intimacies for him over worrying about stats and other mechanics.

                  So, if you're not planning on having the Walker attack the party, and I assume they're not planning to attack him, then good news! This should be relatively easy. Not having to worry about combat stats should be a big, big decrease in difficulty.

                  So, what you've got to work out are two main things: first, his basic social stats, and second, any charms he's got. The first is the easiest. Set his Resolve and Guile, give him Willpower, work out his dice pools for actions, and determine his Intimacies. For the numbers stuff, use the sample quick character writeups in the Antagonists chapter of the Core to get a sense of what numbers are appropriate. For a Deathlord, I'd compare stats with big spirits like Ahlat, Fakharu, and any of the 2nd-Circle demons. Then, since I think the Deathlords should be very impressive, the top tier of ghosts, I'd add at least 1 to static values, and 2 or more to action pools.

                  For Intimacies, I'd create at least four, following the guidelines in the corebook about how they're broken down (at least one Defining, one Major, one negative, and one positive). But I find that NPCs intended for social encounters generally benefit from more Intimacies, since they give more "hooks" to hang social interactions on. My own rule of thumb is that there should be at least one Defining Intimacy, which should have at least two Major Intimacies that support it, each of which in turn should have an additional two Minor Intimacies supporting them. Then, if an NPC is supposed to be a very complex character with conflicting, or at least significantly complex motivations, I'll add a second Defining Intimacy, again supported by a chain of multiple lower-level Intimacies. If the second Defining is supposed to be in conflict with the first, all of the supporting Intimacies should be different, but if it's just intended to add some complexity to their characterization, it's okay for some of the lower-level ones to support multiple higher level ones. If I'm not planning on a more complex character, though, I'll probably still add another Major Intimacy and a single Minor Intimacy to support it, just to have an alternate route if all the "main" Intimacies get tapped out.

                  Doing all that ensures that the NPC always has an Intimacy available to bolster their Resolve if someone tries to affect one of their higher-level Intimacies, and a second one available to enter a decision point if they overcome the first one. On the player-positive side, it also ensures that there's lots of ways for a PC to invoke the lesser Intimacies to get bonuses and enhance the interaction through roleplaying.

                  Charms are going to need more work, I'm afraid. Again, I'd look at the existing QCs of equivalent or slightly lower power, and borrow from them. I'd pay particular attention to the Dead writeups here, since those charms will already have the closest flavor. But the Nephwrack, the closest to a Deathlord, only has one social charm, so you're probably still going to need to go further afield. Since this is intended for a primarily social encounter, I'd say you want at least three social charms detailed - one to enhance his ability to persuade, one to boost his Resolve, and one to help him determine others' Intimacies. Going through the social influence actions and coming up with a charm effect for each that you think he could use would not be a terrible idea, though.

                          Update: Here's what I'm thinking. (When I make QCs, I replace number-boosting charms with higher base die pools, and ignore motes in favor of running everything off willpower)

                          Essence 8, Willpower 8
                          Social Influence: 18 dice
                          Deception: 15 dice
                          Read Intentions: 20 dice

                          Resolve 8
                          Guile 6

                          Social Charms
                          • Penetrating Eyes (1wp): Make a Read Intentions check against a target's Resolve. If you beat it, you learn all their intimacies.
                          • Mask of the Grave (1wp, Psyche): When someone attempts a Read Intentions check against you, they see nothing but the despair of the grave. Roll dice equal to their successes on their roll against their Resolve. For every threshold success, they take 1 blunt damage.
                          • Manipulator (1wp): Once per scene, treat a Minor Intimacy as if it was a Major. Reset by instilling a new Major Intimacy.
                          • Hypnotic (1wp, Psyche): Make a social influence roll against a target's Resolve. If you succeed, you may "program" them with one sentence per threshold success that they will speak at the time and place of your choosing. The speech doesn't strike anyone as unnatural unless they succeed on a Read Intentions check against your Guile. Instead of providing extra sentences, threshold successes can be added to the difficulty of this roll.
                          • Pervert (1wp, Psyche): Pick a Major or Minor Intimacy, either a target's or one of your own that's been instilled this scene, and make a social influence roll-- against the target's Resolve if against another person, or against the result of the Instill roll. On a success, change the Intimacy to something that better reflects the influence of the Neverborn.

                                              I think I largely agree with the Wizard - he needs a defensive charm to boost Resolve. And I agree with the others as well, Mask of the Grave is way too nasty. Success shouldn't be nearly so bad for players.

                                              Also, I don't think making them all cost just wp is a good idea. For one thing, wp is your most tight resource on most characters. If he uses all his charms just once, he'll only have 3 wp left over, and that's assuming he doesn't spend wp on anything else, like resisting the PCs' own social influence attempts. He should have a big, big mote pool - at Essence 8, a god would have 130 personal motes, and I think Deathlords are potent enough to deserve that much as well. I'd give most of those charms a mote cost. Wp costs are really for charms you only expect to use once or twice in a scene, whereas mote costs can be a bit freer.

                                              Another thing you might want to consider doing, since you have an Eclipse Caste in your group, is writing up at least one of his charms in a bit more detail, with a fair bit of flavor, and making it Eclipse-keyworded. That way, he has a bargaining chip he can use on the Eclipse: offer to teach them this nifty charm, in exchange for something. The Walker kind of has a "warrior-poet" thing going on, at least in his presentation in earlier editions, and a charm that plays with that could also work quite well with an Eclipse, particularly one interested in oral performance the way I'd expect a Silver-Voiced Nightingale stylist to be.
