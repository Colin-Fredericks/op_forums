    ...I'd say focus on spearmen and halberdiers. They're inexpensive and can be deployed in a variety of contexts. Everything else would either take too much time to train* or costs too much.

That means no bowmen, no cavalry (can we even afford horses?), and no engineers.

    1) Jianghu is both relatively poor and underdeveloped, the first resulting largely from the second. While it has Resources 4 (right now, and can/will change depending on domestic policies, when the land actually gets put to plow, and when trade with the Hill-Tribes opens up) with regards to agricultural output, it is on the low end of Resources 4. In 'my" version of Resources, the system is exponential, meaning a rating can cover a wide range of values.

    It generally goes:

    Resources 1: the farm held by a serf family, generally up to 40 acres, not counting that paid for land-rent to the overlord
    Resources 2: the farm held by a peasant family, generally up to 40 acres. Taxes generally amount to less than land-rent, so peasants tend to have more disposable income.
    Resources 3: the value of the land held by a landed Oathsworn, who usually oversee between 1 and 5 villages (~100 - 500 people).
    Resources 4: the value of the land held by a vassal; several thousand people
    Resources 5: the value of the land owned by the ruler of a kingdom.

    After land-rent and taxes (both of which go to Kasai, as the Marcher Lord/Lord Paramount [so many titles!]), serfs will generally make ~1 dinar a year, peasants will make ~2, Oathsworn will make between 5 and 25, etc etc etc.

    Of course, Resources (agriculture) only really measures the value of food production. Many farmers, both peasants and serfs, have/try to have additional sources of income: taking up minor crafts (basketry, sewing, pottery, etc), grow and sell cash "crops" (tobacco, hemp, linen, etc), manufacture trade goods (locate and fell workable lumber, make charcoal, keep sheep and goats for wool, etc), or work for someone else in their spare time (children are good for this, which is one of the many reasons farming families tend to be large).

    With regards to Kasai's Resource (agriculture) rating, that largely comes from the labors of his serfs, who both pay part of their own harvest in food-rent and work on Kasai's demense for a day a week. It is an immense amount of food..... most of which gets sold for cash(really, shipped downriver for credit. Kasai doesn't actually have Resources 4 in actual specie, it is just what his land is valued at), or stockpiled in case of war or hardship. Also something to remember: the money Kasai gets from agriculture only comes from the lands he owns personally. The produce from peasant farms and Oathsworn estates belongs entirely to them, most of which is used to pay taxes (again, usually in kind, not in silver).

    With regards to Kasai's Resources (taxes) rating, that largely comes from the taxes levied on landowners, aka peasants and Oathsworn. The more of those Kasai attracts, the more money Kasai gets in taxes.

    With regards to Kasai's Resources (fees) rating, that comes from things like road-tolls, gate-tolls, bridge-tolls, docking-fees, inheritance-fees, court-cases-fees, etc. Much of this goes towards actually maintaining the infrastructure of Jianghu.

    If you want things in hard numbers, ehhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh. I am leery about hard numbers, but here goes.

    Look at it this way: Lord Iysen is the queen of Xia. Xia (that is, the entire kingdom) is valued at around 10 million dinar a year....... the amount of actual money Lord Iysen makes a year, after accounting for upkeep (roads, bridges, the harbor at Teriake Castle-Town, which requires regular dredging) and what isn't actually held by her (Wulin, Iruma, Jianghu and Ikawa, as well as her smaller vassals)? Probably around 5000-10000 dinar a year. Granted, she has a lot of expenses Kasai won't. (like road maintenance. It is easier and cheaper to maintain a dirt road than a stone-paved one, with post-stations and such. Or, harbor-dredging. That is expensive as fuck)

    So............... Jianghu is smaller, but it has fewer expenses than Xia, so let us say that it makes around 90,000 - 100,000 a year gross income. Net? Maybe 1000, 2000?

    Of course, that will increase when 1) the region gets tamed, by exterminating bandits (aka encouraging trade, increasing Res(fees)) and putting more acres under the plow. 2) the population increases, both serfs (aka increase Res(agriculture)) and Peasants (aka increase Res(taxes)), 3) natural resources get exploited (Jianghu has deposits of iron, stone and coal, as well as lumber, not exploited due to interference from bandits and the Hill-Tribes), and 4) trade with the Hill-Tribes picks up.

    Jianghu was a goddamn backwater. Lord Iysen gave it to Kasai as a test.

    Also, since Kasai is a landowner with a regular source of income, he could very well just get a loan to cover any costs. Since he, again, has a regular source of income, he could get a decent-sized loan and just pay it off over a few years.

    2) Right now, the Jianghu Marches can support around 500 Men-at-Arms, aka part time semi-professional soldiers of the Peasant class. That would however, strip the region of most Peasant men (think old whitebeards and beardless boys), not just the able-bodied. Most Lords will only levy between 1/4 and 1/3 the Peasant population, leaving behind the too-old, the too-young, and enough soldiers to leave the region with a token defense force.

    Jianghu has a population of about 3000. With a 7 serf-to-3 peasant split, and subtracting the Oathsworn (about 40), we are left with a little over 2000 (2072, to be exact) serfs and about 900 (888) peasants across the region. 1/4 to 1/3 of 900 is 225 - 300 soldiers.

    However, Lord Iysen wants Kasai to be able to levy at least 1000 Men-at-Arms from Jianghu before next summer, and preferably more, meaning Kasai is going to have to double/triple his levyable population. Both Lord Wulin and Lord Iruma have around 3000-5000 soldiers, and Kasai should want to be able to match that, but that is for the future.

    Note: If Jianghu is invaded, the Hill-Tribes will almost-certainly help fight off the invaders. They understand that Kasai is their effective "meal ticket", and that if he gets removed from power, any treaty them have with him is null and void. That would give Kasai at least a thousand or two warriors.

    3) Uh...... pretty much all three of those 'styles" of war is probable.

    The "most famous" (aka the battles stories and songs are sung about) 'type" of war in the Hundred Kingdoms are field battles, where rectilinear blocks of men march at each other over terrain, spears flashing and arrows flying........ except that "style" is avoided wherever possible, as it is exorbitantly expensive both in soldiers lives (which are extremely hard to replace) and in material.

    Most battles start out as skirmishes, where light troops (longbowmen or light cavalry) stumble across each other and fight, while the main armies maneuver and jockey for position. These skirmishes are FAR more common than open battles, which only really happen when an army gets ambushed, forced into terrain where it can't escape (or won't leave undefended, like a bridge or river-ford), or some other unavoidable situation.

    Most wars usually degenerate into sieges, where armies camp outside and inside a castle, waiting for either the food to run out or for reinforcements to come, or into a series of constant skirmishes and raids on border-villages, where light troops attack villages and caravans belonging to the enemy side, until one side can't take any more and capitulates.

    Think about it like the E3 combat system. Skirmishes are like Withering attacks, pitched-battle is like Clash attacks, and Sieges are like Hold-At-Bay. You want to make as many withering attacks as you can, until the enemy can't really fight back (Initiative Crash). You want to avoid Clash attacks, unless you are sure of overwhelming the enemy, and Holding them At Bay is safe.

    Finally, if Kasai decides to try and attract the younger sons and daughters of peasants to Jianghu, to settle and to serve, some if not many of them will likely come with weapons. The river lowland valleys are hideously overcrowded, and most younger sons/daughters lack much opportunity to gain an inheritance worth very much (Try dividing 40 acres between 5+ kids!). These younger people are land-hungry (can't make much of a life, or get married, without income) and bored, for a lack of a better term, and parents anxious to see them succeed [and get them out of their hair] might send them off to Jianghu ("Go north, my son, go north!") with hand-me-down weapons and equipment. Depending on what Kasai offers, he could be flooded with potential settlers and soldiers.

    Please note that I am assuming you will only be giving weapons and equipment to settlers/soldiers that don't already have them. Bowmen and cavalrymen are only going to be "recruitable" from people already skilled in archery or in horseback riding. Those aren't things that can be trained, at least not in a couple months (for mortals, at least).

    And, even discounting weekly drill-musters, most Peasant Men-at-Arms are going to be decent (aka 2's where they count, Melee for melee troops, Archery for archers. Strength and Dexterity). They are used to fighting off bandits and other assorted nonsense, after all. Their children are going to be familiar with weapons, even if just through practice and not combat (aka 1s in abilities).

    Of course, he has to clear out the bandits first...... which would be an excellent way to train new recruits.

                                    @semicasual

                                    The reason crossbows are so expensive is because they require a lot of metal in their manufacture, especially spring steel, which is expensive even when talking about metal. In a "war crossbow" or arbalest, aka the crossbow as depicted in the Corebook, the prod (the bow part) of the crossbow is made of spring-steel, which is steel that, well, "springs" back into shape after being bent. The steel prod gives the crossbow it's power, and an arbalest can have a draw-weight of 600+ pounds.

                                    Upon doing some further research, I came across the existence of other crossbows, with prods of different materials, aka the same materials that are used to make "regular" bows, like wood, horn, sinew and suchlike. These crossbows are much wider (basically a 3-4 foot bow mounted on a stock, as opposed to the foot or so on a steel prod crossbow) and weaker than a steel-prod crossbow, but they are also likely to be much less expensive.

                                    In game terms, I would consider them Light Archery weapons, otherwise with crossbow stats, but they would cost 20-30 dinar instead of the 50 of an arbalest. Would those interest you?
