    [QUOTE=Sorcerous Overlord;n1391571]Those charms keep high numbers from being an automatic win: Debatable
    PCs will reliably take those options: Your Mileage May Vary.

    The ability to keep high numbers from being an automatic win isn't really debatable as much as up to the ST (without as much advice as the game should be giving them on such matters). Picking and/or designing an NPC that can fend off a group of starting Solars is doable, even if it takes some system mastery to accomplish (but that's true of previous editions as well).

    PCs not taking defensive stuff doesn't really impact how hard it is for the PCs to gang up on a single target. Even for some of the nastier antagonists out there, ignoring the Melee Dawn Blender in your face to try to pick on the completely non-combat character isn't exactly a smart idea.

    And while you can't force players to take anything... a game that outright says you should take at least 2-5 combat Charms at start due to the default level of violence means if you have players that aren't even doing that then you should be having a serious Session 0 chat about the expectations for your game, and how dedicated to non-combat characters are going to function in high stakes action scenes.

    I have not disagreed more strongly to any single post on this forum. Exalted isn't about the charms, or the cool battle mechanic, or lore, or the laser kung fu, Exalted is about playing a Legendary Hero. It's Gilgamesh, Moses, Archimedes, Marco Polo, and Hattori Hanzo walking the earth and getting into Larger than Life adventures, where each of their individual super-abilities can help save the day in unique ways.
    This seems a bit hard to square.

    Charms, cool battle mechanics, and so on are how everyone's individual super-abilities let them save the day in unique ways over their larger than life adventures. What the game is about, and how the game goes about manifesting that, are tightly connected things.

        But let me try to explain myself.

        While some of those characters are highly specialized, others have a reasonable spread of different abilities. Hattori Hanzo the actual real life figure had impressive displays of Melee, War, some social skills, and Stealth.

        You can be a legendary hero in Exalted taking 3-5 Charms in a particular Ability. You can be a legendary hero at multiple things.

        If you take 15 combat Charms, you might well feel less like a legendary hero than taking 5 combat Charms, because your challenging combats will be slower, more grueling, and less frequent.

        Your character will also have a bit more room to grow in play this way.

        If you want to play a character who is super specialized in a particular thing, sure, play a character who is super specialized in a particular thing. But if you're not sure what the concept for your character should be like, it might be a better idea to think of a character concept who can do a bunch of different things.

        A separate point: If some of your players don't want to take combat charms because they have noncombat-themed characters, then they probably don't want to participate in combat, and won't mind sitting out for a bit during combat encounters, so combat balance isn't as much of an issue, right?

                        These two posts pretty much sum up my experience running Ex3. I had multiple encounters in a row where I never so much as connected with a single withering attack, much less had my players feeling like they were actually challenged. "Three gods of war vs four Solars" or "one primordial storm behemoth three Solars" should have been good encounters, but the former got smeared across the map in, like, two turns, and the latter (Mata-Yadh with a few tweaks) only lasted as long as it did because the players had to destroy its legs one by one.

                        It's true that Solars, even fresh out of character creation, are meant to be on the high end of the setting's power scale... but it shouldn't be true that five brand-new Solars can curbstomp beings as potent as the god of war for a quarter of the world. That they can easily take Ahlat as presented is an artifact of a combat system that-- from the ground up-- makes being outnumbered hugely disadvantageous. It's not about onslaught penalties, it's not even about action economy-- it's the link between health and offensive ability.

                        And if you try to avoid that and have reasonably powerful foes that actually outnumber the party... running a dozen goblins in 5e D&D can be a pain; running a dozen fair folk cataphracts or something is so much worse. When your combat system already trends slow (both from complexity, and from the amount of back-and-forth involved in resolving a single charm-boosted attack), doubling or tripling the number of actors is just... painful.



                        Don't get me wrong, I really enjoy Ex3, even the combat... but I never want to run or play the game with more than two PCs again.

                                                        I certainly find, for Solars, the main issue is how easy it is to get massive scores, and how the system basically pushes you to do so, and punishes you for not doing so.


                                                        Incidentally, I have not found this to be an issue with Lunars, because a lot of it is simply down to freebies, specifically the fact you can get 5 dots in a favoured ability for 2 freebies, which means you can get 7 abilities at 5 (which is probably enough to cover most of what your PC is going to be rolling a lot, if you stick to your niche).

                                                        Whereas with Lunars, you probably have 5 in your favourite attribute (as a Solar probably does anyway), maybe a couple, and then 3s and 4s. Overall, you can roll decent dice pools in lots of areas. But because it costs 2 freebies to get an Ability to 5, and you don't need them for charms anyway, they're much less appealing/encouraged/pushed.

                                                        Of my 6 Lunar PCs, at character creation across all of them there were three Abilities at 5 (only one was a combat ability), and two Abilities at 4. Across 6 characters. Now they're at 100xp, several have spent xp to get a few abilities to 4, but that's it.
                                                        So, they can generally roll good dice pools for most things, but are rarely rolling huge dice pools except when stunting and burning 10 motes on an excellency, which they don't do a lot.

                                                        Whereas Solars tend to roll massive dice pools for their focused areas (which on about 80% of characters tends to include combat, even if they don't have a lot of charms for combat), and rubbish dice pools for other stuff, which is more difficult for an ST to run.
