        After that it's got a bit messy, admittedly. I'll give it a try.



        Chapter 1: Exalts
        All your old ones are there, but there are new kinds of Exalts. Exigents (made by lesser gods, so you might be Exalted of a local volcano god and have volcano-themed powers, etc), Liminals (products of botched resurrection attempts; think Frankenstein), and some that we know less about (Getimians, who are kind of anti-Sidereals; Dream-Souled, Hearteaters and Umbrals, which are non-canon optional extras).

        Chapter 2: The Age of Sorrows
        The map has been expanded (especially in the corners, for example there's a big sea in the south-east) with lots of new additions.
        More pulp and classical influence, less Magitech.
        You can see it here:
        http://howsfamily.net/Exalted/map/


        Chapter 3: Character Creation
        You get more charms and specialities, and a bit of choice for your Caste abilities (Solars choose 5 of 8; for example Dawns might get Resistance and Dodge. Lunars choose 2 of 3, and 2 favoured). Solars (only) can choose one Caste ability that ignores Essence prerequisites.

        Chapter 4: Traits
        Instead of the old 7 dots of backgrounds, you now get 10 dots of Merits, which includes all the old backgrounds plus various permanent merits (fleet-footed, iron stomach, mutations, etc). Languages are now a 1-dot merit rather than from Linguistics.
        Most anima powers are roughly similar (combat stuff for Dawns, sneakiness and hiding anima for Nights, toughness for Earths, trickery for Changing Moons, etc). Twilights can now teleport to places of power and make spirits into their familiars.
        There's also a bonus XP system (which can't be spent on native charms, just everything else) for stuff like engaging your intimacies in the story or fulfilling your caste's role or helping others with theirs.

        Chapter 5: Systems
        Combat has changed a lot; it seems more complex but I find flows better. It's more cinematic, building up the advantage in combat before unleashing your decisive attack. Movement is completely different. You get 5m a turn instead of getting motes from stunting.
        The social system is more about intimacies than before, and you can't persuade people to do stuff unless they have a supporting intimacy.
        Craft has changed massively, and is very controversial (some love it, some hate it). Basically, crafting artefacts takes less time but requires you to have crafted a whole bunch of regular stuff beforehand to build up Craft points.
        Sub-systems (poison, etc) have mostly changed a little.

        Chapter 6: Charms
        Basically, there's way more but they're a bit weaker. This applies to Solars, Dragonblood and Lunars. Dragonblood charms are a bit more even in power than before, and the Abilities each have charms of different aspects. Lunar charms are also more even (No Moons are no longer rubbish, essentially).
        Lunars can also change the Attribute of some charms with certain spirit shapes (for example, take poison charms as Intelligence and Wits instead of their normal Stamina if your spirit shape is poisonous).

        Chapter 7: Martial Arts and Sorcery
        Martial Arts are now the same styles for everybody, but Dragonblood get weaker versions and Solars stronger versions.
        Spellshave you roll to generate special sorcerous motes, rather than use your own, so essentially it can take longer to cast but is effectively free.
        More importantly, all sorcerers get rituals to generate extra sorcerous motes, that represent the way they've learnt sorcery; perhaps they make sacrifices to demons, or burn prayers to a Efreet, or have a channeling artefact, or absorb power from the elements, or have prophetic dreams.
        Also, there's now a relatively freeform sorcerous rituals system, that lets you spend a bit of XP and a lot of time and rolls to create permanent sorcerous effects. Mutant monsters, magical rivers, cursed forests, flying mountains, etc, etc.

        Chapter Eight: Antagonists
        There's a lot more variation in Antagonists now; more weird and wacky stuff that doesn't fit into god/elemental/demon/etc. Animals have more unique stuff. Spirits don't share a charmset, they have their own. Elementals no longer dematerialise.

        Chapter Nine: Artefacts
        The key, as has been mentioned, is all weapons and armour are at least 3 dots, but come with their own sets of charms. So one Warstrider might have charms for unleashing giant essence cannons, while another has a charmset for fighting underwater. One artefact boomerang inflicts madness, while another feeds on blood and helps track murderers.
        Also, there's less of a magitek aesthetic.


        So overall I'd say:


        Originally posted by The Free Man
        What new toys did we get
        For Solars, a more flexible choice of abilities, a Supernal ability, charms from your artefact, a lot more (and more functional) Martial Arts styles, sorcerous workings, and a lot more stuff in some of the abilities that only had a few charms before (Investigation, Larceny, Socialise, etc).

        For players generally, a bunch of new Exalt types, but none of them have much in the way of published rules yet (I expect the Exigents kickstarter next Spring).

            Oh, speaking of anima powers, one big difference is how the Eclipse thing of learning the charms of other things works.

            It's no longer a free for all. Instead they can only learn charms with the Eclipse keyword. Other exalted's native charms will never have this, and are categorically off-limits. For this reason Lunar knacks are gone, and they just have shapeshifting-enhancing charms. Also charms exist that allow non-eclipse solars and lunars to learn eclipse charms too. Dragon blooded getting the same isn't entirely off the table but they didn't provide any in What Fire Has Wrought.

                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                Craft is... just terrible now. If you want to build a diaklave, first you have to build a dozen horseshoes -- then actually use those horse shoes in play to advance the story. Then you can build a dozen normal swords, which also have to actually be used in play. Then you can attempt to make a diaklave... if you have purchased at least 4 dots in Craft: Swords (not "Craft: Fire", or "Craft: Blacksmithing", but Craft: Swords), and at least 3 dots in Lore and Occult, then you can start to buy dots in Craft: Artifact (and use the lower of Craft Artifact and Craft Swords on your roll). Crafting a diaklave takes three months, during which time you can't Craft anything else, and need to spend several hours a day working on it (not sped up by Craftsman Needs No Tools). And you do this every time you want to make something. Or repair something.
                This description of the craft mechanics ranges from misleading, to outright wrong. I'll aim for a somewhat more objective description.

                Crafting is now organized around a couple of mechanics, "project slots" and "craft experience". Project slots represent the number of different things you can focus on at once, while craft experience represents artistic inspiration or something similar. Craft projects are broken up into tiers, depending on how complex and important an item is. When you complete a project of a given level, you're awarded a certain amount of craft xp for the same tier and the next higher one, based on the number of basic goals you completed and how well you accomplished it (the basic goals are, more or less, "did you impress someone", "did you benefit yourself", and "did you support your own intimacies"). Generally, finishing a couple of projects that meet all three goals will earn enough craft xp to attempt a project of the next highest tier. The project slots mechanic does limit the number of things you can work on simultaneously, but only at the higher tiers, and it's relatively cheap to use craft xp to create new slots, spending your inspiration on doing more work simultaneously, essentially.

                Making artifacts does need a separate Craft (Artifacts) skill. This has prereqs of Lore 3 and Occult 3, just like 2e artifact crafting did. However, those requirements no longer increase for 4- and 5-dot artifacts, meaning anyone capable of manipulating essence can at least attempt them, no special gateway charms necessary.

                Personally, I mostly like the basic craft mechanics (with the exception of having separate Craft Abilities for every type of craft, which I've always disliked in every edition). The craft xp mechanic could be better-named (it leads to some confusion with regular xp, in my experience, and the name doesn't convey the intent very well), but in practice, it basically encourages the craft-focused character to always be looking for reasons in-game to use their craft skills. 2e had a strong incentive to have crafting as a strictly background thing, accomplished only during downtime, and even mundane crafts were usually handled off-screen. The need for craft xp to accomplish higher-tier projects, and the necessity of tying crafts to story-based goals, on the other hand, really ensures that the crafter now actively wants to find reasons to craft stuff.

                                General Setting :

                                -Celestials created their Exaltations on their own. While Autochthon might(?) have played a part in them coming up with the concept, he didn't make the Exaltations for them.

                                -There are no "small gods" anymore, like a god of a single ordinary tree or a god of a single lamp. Gods are all created by the Celestial Bureaucracy or by powerful magic and Charms.

                                -Pure Chaos is no longer a category of Wyld, it stops at the step just before that.

                                -The Yozis can never escape Malfeas under any circumstance. This rule is absolute (you can do what you want at your table, but in the canon it's not possible).


                                Exalts :

                                -Exalt Charms plateau at Essence 5, which is more powerful than it used to be. While Exalts get more motes from going higher, E6-E10 Charms are roughly as strong as E5 Charms and more meant to be signature Charms.

                                -All Exalts are closer to each other in power. While Solars still sit at the top, non-Solars can occasionally have better rolls/effects than Solars for the things they excel at.

                                -Lunars can now gain new Shapes without killing their targets by completing alterate challenges like scaring them to death or beating them at cards.

                                -Abyssals should be less beholden to their Deathlords - while being your deathlord's servant is still very valid, there should be more freedom to go out there and do your own thing if you prefer.

                                -Infernals are moving away from being strictly the Yozi's minions and more towards the theme of being returning corrupt Solars. Their themes will focus on wielding alien powers. Their caste names are changing (i.e. Fiend is now Penumbra).
