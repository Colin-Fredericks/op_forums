                                                        Well, it's good to see that Haunts-My-Nightmares-With-Claws-Of-Black-And-Ivory* is striving to be an upright member of society by merely maiming the children of enemies.

                                                        Wait! I don't mean that sarcastically!

                                                        If you slay some terrible foe but leave their children around, you're just begging to have monster hunters at the gates of your subterranean lair in a few years. But! If you kill everyone even tangentially aligned with those who oppose you, you have to accept that you're a bad person. A moral dilemma! Luckily, by maiming the children of your foes, you can ensure** that at worst they'll become the bitter mentors of monster-slaying heroes in two generations. Not only is that an extra generation, there's a fair possibility that their protegee will only angrily threaten you a few times before they learn your side of the story and ultimately take a middle-ground towards morality.

                                                        As a bonus, now nobody can question that the victim had nothing to do with you. After all, you bit her leg off! That gives them an increased chance of successful re-integration into their native society rather than being driven forth to become an obsessed wanderer!




                                                        *I don't know the Lunar's name, but I know what the Lunar does.
                                                        **With 94% accuracy

                                                            Unless of course they Exalt, in which case you were probably better off just killing them, because now the new hero has extra drama power from the inevitable "overcoming physical disabilities in an inspiring fashion" story arc.

                                                            The only real way to have a positive end to this sort of story is to not seek revenge at all. Instead, seek justice by bringing the villain's actions to light, and punishing them according to relevant and acceptable law and-or tradition. Someone might still seek revenge against you, but this way you retain the moral high ground which will either win the would-be-avenger over, or simply label them a villain and acceptable target when they deliberately seek self-satisfaction over justice.

                                                            Unfortunately it is the nature of good stories to be messy, complicated affairs and our poor heroine was on the receiving end of what seems to be the long-standing literary tradition of cyclical revenge. Ah well. I suppose if this cycle is going to end, someone needs to receive an epiphany on the difference between justice and revenge.

                                                            So, do the Maidens share the load on this sort of thing Lady Mercury? I imagine most good revenge stories include all five elements of fate.
