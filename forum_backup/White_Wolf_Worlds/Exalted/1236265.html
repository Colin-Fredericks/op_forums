    In praise of first edition

    07-17-2018, 10:39 PM
    The frankness of the 3rd ed developers in using 1st edition as their starting point inspired me to start rebuilding my old collection of first ed books.

    It's been ages since I read through them all, and I guess I forgot how great they are! Creatures of the Wyld, Scavenger Sons, Savage Seas, Games of Divinity, the first year of the game came out with a really solid, fun lineup of books that established the mystery and epic scope of Creation.

    The system was far from perfect, but I have to say those early books especially were really fun and engaging to read and packed with inspiration. By contrast I often found the second edition setting and gear books to be exceedingly dry and often bloated with the wrong sort of details.

    Anyway this is a bit rambling but I guess my point is I'm super happy with the direction the devs are taking the current edition and I can see how the original game is coming through in their work.

                          I dunno,
                          I actually liked Return of the Scarlet Empress. It requires modification to really engage with, and I certainly wouldn't make it the default direction Creation progresses in for most campaigns, but for a group interested in doing an apocalyptic style game, it can work.

                          What I got FAR more utility out of were breakdowns of specific regional schemes specific Infernals were engaged in. The plan to wipe out the Confederation Council at Marita, or methodically overextending Lookshy to worsen its standing in the Scavenger Lands. In a modular way, there's definite utility there. You just ignore stupid stuff like the Haywire Nanite-hacking by the Defiler in Chaya.

                          Hell, one of the single BEST ideas I ever saw for Infernal activity was in the comic...with the Manosque Fiend, deep-cover as a senator of House Nellens in the lesser chamber of the Deliberative. The obvious implied plan: Using Poramo Nellens to get to, then subvert his mentor Sesus "the Slug" Negezzer, and subsequently use HIM to get to the Roseblack...so that the Green Sun Princes can lock down the Roseblack, and then turn their attentions fully to eliminating Memnon, so they can deliver a pacified, Civil War-free Realm to Hell's control...That works BEAUTIFULLY, even if you throw out the whole "Returning Queen of Hell" thing.

                          I don't get all the 2E hate, honestly. There are awful parts, so hate the awful parts, but stuff like Thaumaturgy as magical sciences I loved. Take a look at Paragon as Exhibit A for what a creative group of PCs can accomplish with it. Thamaturgy-as-miracles may be more "mythic" (whatever that means)...but it's certainly less helpful to a Twilight PC (someone like Raneth of Diamond Hearth) who wants to figure out how to repair the damage to the Salt-Manse @ Plenilune, and make the other 4 in the nearby city-states begin working again, as part of his larger goal to repair the ecological damage done to the region.

                          Did they maybe go overboard with re-skinning 20th-21st century stuff (like modern appliances) in their definition of "Magitech"? Absolutely, so you hate that part.

                          Don't get me wrong: I love the 1E books too. The Cult of the Illuminated book is one of my all time favorites. Not least because it gives mad details on the Wyld Hunt's actual organization and breakdown by what you can reasonably expect Wyld Hunt's of 1st, 2nd and 3rd-class quality to possess in terms of manpower (quality and quantity), Artifacts, and financial support. I just believe there's a predisposition to despise all things 2E, and that's unfortunate.

                          What do I know, though? I'm a lover of Dreams of the First Age

                          Edit: I realize that the radical changes to Sorcery help address the kinds of things 2E Thaumaturgy did when applied to the civic-works scale, but I truly feel that 2E-type Thaumaturgy, (and by extension mortal Essence-users) was good stuff. Making the answer "We made sorcery more accessible to mortals, so we don't really need it" sort of misses the point for me. YMMV.

                                  Exactly,
                                  If you want to get right down to it, there's something outright deceitful being practiced by some (perhaps even subconsciously) lovers of 1E. They use 2E's worst elements as talking-points for why supposedly 2E sucks, and then leverage the "Well, the Devs have gone back to 1E for their inspiration for 3E. This obviously proves our contention that 1E is inherently superior to 2E."

                                  The Developers made a style-change for EX3, and that's their right. Just like it's my right to buy the book, gut it of their style change for the improved mechanics, and anything else I do happen to like (such as 3E Sorcery), and basically go on playing a hybrid of 2E with a lot of 1E influences. They like a smaller Halta, a weaker Bull of the North, and NO places in the Threshold where a Celestial Exalt can walk openly, so long as they aren't getting Totemic and wrecking stuff. (Instead, as their explanation for what happened when a Solar Exalted in Lookshy's backyard prior to the Empress's disappearance, Lookshy forces teamed up with what amounts to Realm forces invading their sovereign territory, to eliminate the Anathema before resuming hostilities.)

                                  That's a MONUMENTAL change to Exalted. 2E gave us the idea that, in very cosmopolitan, very anti-Realm locales, the Immaculate Philosophy was derided as the BS it was. It replaced the nonsensical "Say a thing long enough, and even those who hate you will come to believe it" explanation for why places that hate the Realm would still retain their view on Solars and Lunars. Umm no, if Russia trumpets that very wealthy people all sold their souls to the Devil, and plan to destroy the world from the time of the Cold War into the 24th century, I as an American citizen am not likely to adopt their position. These people have had their hands on The Button, and would destroy me and my way of life if they could. I know that, ergo I NEVER LISTENED to the bullshit coming from the mouths of Russian-national missionaries handing out flyers at the local mall. No matter how many centuries they repeat it, my parents taught me the missionaries are agents of the Evil Empire and never to be trusted, and I in turn taught that to my children, who will teach it to my grandchildren, and so on ad infinitum.

                                  Instead, we were introduced to the more nuanced view that the Powers That Be in places which have had intensely negative experiences with the Realm actually ( ::gasps:: ) Decided to make up their own minds, regarding the "Anathema."

                                  I don't know about anyone else, but I always rather fancied the East and North as Directions where, due to a lack of Realm-penetration, views of the "Anathema" differed wildly from the Immaculate Philosophy. Whereas the more pacified South and West, due to trade interdependence and the presence of powerful Realm presences in their Satrapies and client-allies, views of Anathema dovetailed with the Immaculate Philosophy. It made sense to me, but now we're back to Solar/Lunar Super Global Fugitives for 3E.

                                  1E did much right, but people forget it also branded Lunars as NOTHING BUT animal skin-wearing barbarians painted with woad, leading beastmen armies in an attempt to destroy civilization. The skinhead-like behavior of 2E's Silver Pact leaves much to be desired too, but at least MoEP: Lunars gave us cool stuff like how to go about a Thousand Streams project, or how to map one in progress for the benefit of PCs entering a custom one. 1E made its mistakes too, it simply benefits from more "Ahh, those were the good ole' days" rose-tinting than 2E.

                                  Edit: 2E also empowered Lunars to say "I'm an experienced Celestial Exalt who is hyper-adaptable as my Schtick. I say to Malfeas with Sidereal efforts to keep track of me, devour the heart of a patrician playing scavenger-lord out of Greyfalls, run Flickering Star Infusion to assume his fate in the Loom, and Evading the Spider's Eye to keep an eye out for any Sidereals/Sidereal agents who pop up in my zone of influence anyways." Implausible Lunar Panopoly disguises my Daiklave as a normal, albeit well-made sword, and Hide of the Cunning Hunter hides my Moonsilver Tattoos. If I am 250+, I can go even DEEPER undercover with Sunset Hibuscus, and hide even from Essence Sight and specific Moonsilver-detection. Voila, I am now a Lunar who could believably penetrate the Blessed Isle for a considerable length of time.

                                  Because yes, there ARE such things as Lunar players who would like their PCs to experience "Anathema No-Go" portions of the setting. 1E made the very idea of that preposterous. Which kind of made it stupid to have such a large static presence of the Wyld Hunt on the Blessed Isle. What are you guarding against with all that force? Newly Exalted Anathema the Black Helms can kill on their own?

                                                  To elaborate on the strengths of 1E then:
                                                  Cult of the Illuminated: Detailed write-ups. Much quantifiable material. I found it completely revamped my understanding of the Wyld Hunt. To the point I think of it as much as "The Wyld Hunt Book" as I do a supplement about Sidereal activities. Honestly, I'd been wanting something like its breakdown of the Wyld Hunt for a long time, and I hope EX3 material on them hews to this sort of mode, when they say "returning to 1E influences." I mention this book as my best-loved, because the Wyld Hunt is an antagonist-body in the games I enjoy. Yet it's also this hugely complex para-military force with religious overtones, and the breakdowns of the ranks versus which levels of "Wyld Hunter" are Dynasts and which strictly Immaculate helped me immensely.

                                                  The Castebooks, as were previously mentioned. These were gold, as much for their write-ups of sample Caste-members as anything. Loved the "How the world sees them/How they see the World" sections. It's the same deeply-quantifiable trait you see in CotI, applied more broadly. As a Lunar-guy, I'd always mourned the lack of Lunar Castebooks, given how well the Solar Castebooks were done. The Sidereals didn't get them either, and I thought that was a shame too. Especially when I would've chosen to do both Lunar and Sidereal Caste-books before heading into Aspect Books. Celestial Caste is a much more profound impact on the Celestials than Aspect is on the Terrestrials. Especially considering many Houses are not one Aspect throughout.

                                                  Scavenger Sons: The Book so good they basically simply reprinted it for 2E. If you don't love Scavenger Sons, what DO you love about Exalted? IMO, Scavenger Sons was essentially the model used for the CoCDs to come, and its worth is therefore inestimable as an example of "How to do a Direction-Guide."

                                                  Savant and Sorcerer: I love some of it, and I hate some of it. The portions of which I love and which I hate do not always remain the same. It's a funny book, but not necessarily a bad first foray in this direction.

                                                  If it's not obvious by now: I prefer the quantified to the vague. The explicit to the suggested. Assume that any supplement that hews to the one over the other is something I approve of. This is also a source of confusion for me when I read the "3E will hew more to 1E" but then also read that the designers want less to be quantified, and more left to be filled in by Storytellers and Players.

                                                  Let's see: I'm personally of the opinion that the Sidereal/Cult-related material which relates to Yu-Shan was basically the turning-point when quantifying the alternate realms of existence was deemed a positive. You'll notice how much of its style recurs, or gets a reprint.

                                                  What 1E did well (IMHO) was limit scope-creep, and maintain a unified vision of Creation. While they quantified, they did not over-elaborate. Story-hooks were presented, but not to the point of trying to "mini-module."

                                                      Cult of the Illuminated is a mixed book for me. I think that it more or less killed the Cult of the Illuminated itself for a lot of games. One of the previous devs called it a roach motel for character concepts, and I think it's not a bad descriptor of it. It also kind of played on a lot of the bad bits of late 1e and through 2e I think in:
                                                      - Takes on Sidereals that depicted them as hubristic to the point of incompetent, Golds bieng hostile to Bronze on a pretty silly level, and so on.
                                                      - The numbers didn't add-up. This is more a general WW problem, but th whole ide aof training schools of Solars, with entire years of students, when there's like, 60 the Cult has worldwide, is a biiiiit of a stretch it felt to me.
                                                      - Solars were just assumed to be going ot steamroll everything an dwere the good guys always in a way. Lot of the NPCs were pretty psycophatnic to Solars, especially Lunar and DB ones.
                                                      - The Lunar material in general was pretty terrible and I think was peak 1e in its Lunar treatment.

                                                      The goal I think was to make a counter ot the Immacualte Order, but in a way it didn't quite work. That said, I liked a lot of the art (Omar Dagon is one of my favorte artists in general and seeing him in an Exalted book is fun, plus Melissa Uran's stuff in the Appendix is stellar susual). But I think it says something, to me at least, that Jak and Pia are the most memorable bits of the first main chatpers.

                                                      That said, the Appendix on the Wyld Hunt is one of the best examples of world-building for the game, helped ot build a lot, and I think was plain awesome. I don't think it made-up for the rest of the book's lacklusterness to me, but I think it's great in part for showing a lot of details, but not the sort that bog down (we know some of its Shogunate style but not like, details that matter beyond its origins and legend; we get a sample Wyld Hunt, but not an exhaustive list of everything ot it or where they all are in Creation, stuff like that).

                                                      Hell, it having a sample Sidereal with astrology blessings helped for if anything having good examples of astrology to work with.

                                                      I'll note though on other books on your list, to me, are a bit why the 2e stuff is probably more maligned. Or at least, bits why I malign them.

                                                      Scavenger Sons is great, in part, because it does in one book what took the next edition five to even get close to, and I think is in the end still more useful than the entireity fo the Terrestrial Compass books. Nearly every single thing introduced in the 2e books were just taking something in this book, and increasing its wordcount a chunk (whether it should have been or not), often with useless "In the First Age X, In the Shogunate Y, contagion, reign fo the Empress" format. In the end, some places didn't need more than what they got in this book. I honestly think there's not really much more needed for Chaya, and what we ended up with didnt' help the setting. And while the Introduction says these are just a sampling, 2e almost never introduced anything actually new to the world save some locations in teh North that had to be curtailed by authors. This is further evidenced I think with even a 1e book, Bastions of the North, which I think its decision to drill down on those three countires, and bascially write-off the rest of the North as useless or not antyhign of note hurting the settin'gs openness more than helping it. And its "revealing the secret" in a couple places (the gods of Whitewall, Vodak) also again, hurt more than helped long term.

                                                      I think there's something to say about when something is first introduced and left open to players, and then coming back to just answer the plot threads. There are a lot of "WHy is this?" in a game setting that's important not to answer by devs. Answering it is a cheap way to get wordcount met, but I think it doesn't do a lot to actually add to games. I think making a setting more a toolbox with things to draw on and plot threads to use is what a lot of early 1e stuff felt like, while later 1e and throughout 2e, there is a feeling that the game was more writing to make a setting for a consistent fictional setting than a useful gaming one.
