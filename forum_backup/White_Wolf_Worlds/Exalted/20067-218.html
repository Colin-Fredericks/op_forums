    Originally posted by EmanantVolition View Post

    Ok, I misunderstood the point you were making in your previous post then. I agree with your argument as articulated here. Although, with harvest days it is again not entirely clear how this all works when plants growth and harvest times may have more to do with the schedule set by courts of field gods than how things would proceed "naturally". It may be that harvest times are the same everywhere regardless of crop-type or environment because that is how the gods associated with the harvest prefer it. As an aside, I would really love a Manacle & Coin style fluff supplement that addresses just how much gods and elementals curate processes we'd intuitively assume were "natural".

    One further point of consideration is that there is an Immaculate Calendar which dictates what datetimes the gods get their "due" allotment of prayer. I've always wondered what this calendar looks like and how it has influenced the Realm calendar or vice versa. I suspect this calendar in particular would be fairly regional, since it's there mostly to keep meddling terrestrial gods in line.

    My argument is that the field gods need to set their schedule by how things happen naturally. The two are tied together, neither one drives the other. The Loom dictates the way things will go in general terms, the field gods need to set the rate at which things ripen and reach readiness for harvest, and then makes sure everything works out ok. If they do a bad job (say, don't properly account for how long it takes a stalk of corn to grow), the harvest will be bad. If the god needs the corn to grow faster, it needs to expend some power in making it happen. It can't just plan for a fast harvest, it needs to actively participate in making it happen.

    The devs have said that most things in Creation operate because that is how they are supposed to operate. Gods don't determine that water runs downhill, or how fast crops grow (without intervention), or how nourishing the soil is when overworked (without intervention). Gods plan and intercede around these things, because that gets them prayer. This causes disputes between courts and offices, because that's where stories come from (and how bureaucracies work. There are a thousand changes I want other departments to make because it would make my job SO MUCH EASIER).

    My idea for the Immaculate calendar is that it is has various 'modules' which are overlaid on the Imperial calendar. One would be nation wide, and include days for the greatest of gods. Another level would be directional, and include a day for that direction's censor, and chief deities (like the directional war god). One would be regional, and finally one local. Each level would have input from those priests and politicians relevant at that scale. For example, no-one gets a holiday at the national level without the Empress' blessing, and the stamp of the highest rank in the Immaculate Order, and probably someone from a Sidereal office as well. The local regions are probably the most likely to abuse their position to bribe certain gods in certain ways, and then try to slip it past regional oversight, or just bribe regional oversight outright.

    The Immaculate calendar probably has no influence over the Imperial calendar, at least as I have defined it. The Imperial calendar says 'the 1st of descending earth falls on a Sunday, and can be confirmed as the moon will be in this phase, and the stars will be in this position.' The Immaculate Calendar says that 'the 1st of descending earth shall be a holiday in which we celebrate the Immaculate Dragon of Earth (Pasiap I think?) and this small assortment of gods of things that are mined. There shall be a procession of villagers, each wearing costumes that represent gods in numbers according to the station of the god, and the priest shall make sacrifices worth no less than X and no more than Y.'

    Kind of like how the 11th of November is the 11th of November. We celebrate the end of World War 1 at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month, but if we were to move it for some political reason to the 12th day of the month, the 11th of November would still be the 11th of November, but no longer Remembrance Day.

                    "There is only one way to resolve this!" someone yelled as he began flailing about himself with his grand daiklave.
