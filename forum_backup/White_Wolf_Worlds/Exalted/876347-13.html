                    It kind of seems like you are a victim of Hype Backlash more than anything else.

                    Part of selling any product or funding kickstarter means you need to generate interest so those fans can get others interested, so you can actually get the money funded to run the product.
                    OPP are presumably on a shoestring budget, with Pugmire possibly being the first product they can run from internal funding rather than preorders and freelancer goodwill.

                    At that level, you need Hype or the product never exists.

                    I've backed many KSers and no matter what the product from webcomic funding to 3d printing to VR, Murphy's law always applies, and often the biggest fans experience the biggest backlash.
                    Am I saying criticism is forbidden? No, but recognising the same pattern is there prevents you from getting overhyped and thus disillusioned, and to take a break from one experience, subscribe to the kickstarter RSS feed and do something else for a bit.

                    In this case, the Backer PDF is in art layout! it's completely optional! it'll be cheap!

                    Fast reasonable updates are exactly what we want now!

                                I'm a bit lost as to where all the hype is coming from that got people's expectations so unrealistically high. Like, OK, some of the promotional material and developer comments, particularly early on, were to the effect of "this will be the best game ever and everything will be fantastic" but that's pretty much par for the course for "thing a developer says about a product currently in development". 3e's certainly flawed in some unfortunate ways, like any other game, but in most respects it exceeded my expectations appreciably, and my expectations would probably have been lower if I'd taken the forum scuttlebutt to heart. I mean, JayTee, I don't really remember you posting a lot about how excited you were and how great 3e was going to be before it came out, which is kind of what I'd expect if you were fooled by hype?

                                It's good; it's probably the best high-crunch RPG I've seen, and easily the best one working under Exalted's design constraints. It is, in fact, appreciably better balanced and playtested than the average OPP product. I feel like the game has too many charms and I'm still enthusiastic about the backer charm PDF adding more, because so many of them are so rad. On the other hand, in the early phase people were free to imagine it being anything they wanted out of Exalted since details were scant, and now that it's a specific thing, a lot of those people find that it's not (or not exactly) the thing they want. I think that's more attributable to the sense of disappointment some people seem to be feeling than "hype" -- the attitude that, "well, given all this time and work, why didn't they make the game the way I'd have wanted it to be made?" But given that there have always been a thousand competing visions for Exalted, it didn't take much perspicacity to realize in advance that that wasn't likely to happen.
