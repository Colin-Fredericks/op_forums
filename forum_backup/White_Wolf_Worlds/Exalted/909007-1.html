                    It doesn't seem to be in the case of this thread, but I think the question touches on the question of whether a person's characteristics can be irrelevant to the question of whether or not they're doing some good in the world.

                    And I'm not saying in the sense of "evil person who ruthlessly pursues brutal means of achieving a noble goal, or is hard enough to make the tough decisions", I'm saying where the associations between who you are and how you govern are non-existent.

                    For instance, there's Frederick William I, King in Prussia, father of Frederick the Great (one of the greatest military minds in European history). As a ruler, he was an autocrat, but highly effective and even equitable, revising tax codes to something more comprehensible and reasonable, building schools and hospitals and encouraging education, resettling land depopulated by plague, ordering reclamation of marshland, stockpiling grain for the public good, and developing a highly comprehensive code for the civil service, which did not suffer laxity in any officials. Although he drilled the kingdom's army to an extremely high quality, he himself hardly ever pursued wars, seeing them as a wasteful extravagance. In his own right, he was reported to be a highly diligent administrator, who worked long hours, lived an austere, unostentatious lifestyle (to the point of limiting use of candles in order to save on money), and is even said to have paid his own consumer tax. As kings go, he was probably not exactly what one would call oppressive.

                    As a person, he was known to be brutal to the point of outright sadism, given to violent tempers and unforgiving of even small discrepancies. Few people experienced this more than prince Frederick, whose interest in things like music and literature were regarded as decadent and unmanly by a father who wanted to shape him into an effective civil and military leader. Frederick was held to a harsh regime of education and military exercises, and would be beaten for the slightest failing or defiance; he was reportedly punished in this manner for things like being thrown from his horse, or wearing gloves in cold weather. The king's treatment was such that the prince actually once attempted to flee into exile with his friend and tutor, and when they were captured, Frederick was forced to watch his friend's execution, which is said to have made him faint and experience hallucinations for days afterwards; some think Frederick William would have killed his son as well if he thought the Reichstag would have let him get away with it (at the time, Prussia was still under a lot of Imperial power).

                    So the point I'm making with all of this is that you can have a person, whether as a ruler or exercising their skill or power in some other way, who does great, helpful, even just things, while being a complete heel in their personal life and relationships. I don't think that even necessarily entails hypocrisy.

                    You might also be able to have the converse, but I'd say that's a bit more precarious.

                                                            Yes. Solars are the good guys. Source: They glow yellow. Yellow. Obviously the most moral color.

                                                            More seriously, though. Obviously nobody is axiomatically good or evil. That said, the Celestials in general and Solars in particular tend to be closer to what we'd consider conventionally good. I suspect this is even more true in Third Edition; Solars are personally selected by the Unconquered Sun and generally represent, at least when they Exalt, something that he considers virtuous or worth Exalting.

                                                            The Unconquered Sun himself is of course not axiomatically good, so you have to take this with a grain of salt... but he's not awful, and I suspect if you wrote down the morality and thought-process of the various in-setting entities with their own Exalted, and had people rank them based on how good they are or whatever, the UCS would rank at or near the top. There would be some people who'd hate him, people who would prefer the Maidens or Luna or whatever, but overall he's the closest fit for what we'd consider good, and his Exalted generally skew a bit more towards that, just like the Lunars tend a bit more towards survival-of-the-fittest and Sidereals skew a bit more towards fatalism and utilitarianism and What Must Be Done and all that.

                                                            You can have awful Solars, but usually you would have to answer the question of "what did the Unconquered Sun see in this guy?", which implies that even totally horrible Solars have a degree more focus on the fact that they had the possibility to be someone better, or that they have or had some aspect to them that the Unconquered Sun found appealing.

                                                            tl;dr: Some Solars are terrible, but almost all Solars have at least the potential to be good.

                                                            (Of course, to the extent that Exalted is humanist -- and I think it is, especially with the Solars as the central Exalt type -- this is true about all the Exalted, too; but Solars exemplify it most directly and have it most clearly as a theme.)

                                                            Like, the central theme of Solars draws heavily on this. And they often fall short of that, but it's still core to what they are. They were created to be humanity's champions and teachers, the people who scout new horizons and find common ground among humanity's disparate parts; they weren't created to be its tyrants, no matter how awful they sometimes behaved.
