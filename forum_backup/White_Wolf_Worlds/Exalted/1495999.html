                          <1>Cervine Kalenders [part 1]

                          In the North, most people know that elkmen often possess mystic powers and arcane lore. Thus, Osman the farmer felt little surprise when the towering figure plodded into the village of Burchin’s Hold. The elkman stood a head and a half taller than any villager even without his heavy, branching antlers. As well as his gray-brown fur, the elkman wore a plain gray kilt and a blanket rolled and folded over one shoulder. Pouches hung from his leather belt. He bore a large pack on his back and a staff of twisted, lightning-struck oak in his hand. Cords bound small mirrors, beads and carved wooden amulets to his antlers.

                          Osman feared the elkman no more than he would any peddler. Everyone knew elkmen were harmless.

                          The elkman named himself as Kemalainen. He traded iron nails for bran mash, turnips and lodging in Osman’s barn. Over the next two days, he sold a few luck talismans and cured Granny Sevda’s cough. The elkman gave her grandson seeds for tonic herbs. With an inscribed iron plate and a spinning spoon, he mapped the geomancy around the village and advised farmers on auspicious locations for byres and wells. Always did Kemalainen speak softly. He let children poke at his antlers and invited them to tell him stories.

                          That was how he learned about the ghost. Little Zulfiye drowned three years ago. Some nights, though, she came back to her former playmates and wanted them to go with her. The carpenter’s son Gunduz did so last week and never came back. Kemalainen became very grave. He spoke long with the carpenter, the huntsman and other folk who knew the hills and forest. The elkman bought five small jars from Unver the potter. The next day, he plodded out of Burchin’s Hold.

                          <3>Wandering Thaumaturges

                          The Cervine Kalenders are a mystic order of elkmen in the North. Not all elkmen join it, but most of them have kin who do. The Kalenders provide minor arcane services for Northern folk: medicines both alchemical and mundane, warding talismans, dowsing, spirit-dealing and the like. For many Northern villages, a wandering elkman is the only thaumaturge they may see in a dozen years. Few people worry about the Kalenders because the elkmen never take sides in wars or other disputes, and their magic seems small and safe.

                          The Kalenders’ code says they should not use their knowledge and powers for worldly gain or for violence. Kalenders can defend themselves, however, and they can accept alms or mystical materials that the order uses in rituals or to make artifacts. Some elkmen stretch these loopholes pretty far. They still avoid curses or other aggressive magic. The Kalenders’ elders fear that if elkman mystics become known for meddling in human politics, war and commerce, they will attract dangerous attention to the order as a whole. It’s happened before.

                          Most Kalenders carry a geomancer’s tools to map Essence flows. These traveling savants compile detailed and extensive maps of dragon lines and demesnes… including dozens of ancient manses that the rest of Creation forgot. Sometimes, Kalenders find other locations of geomantic importance. Now and then, they also need to use higher arts of thaumaturgy.

                              Cervine Kalenders [Part 2]

                              <2>Geomantic Wardens

                              Kemalainen had no trouble finding the small shadowland. He thought the pit where Zulfiye drowned must have been a quarry centuries ago. Many other people must have drowned there, too. The elkman remembered tales of a local warlord who enjoyed mass drownings of his enemies; the remains of a fortress further up the hill made Kemalainen guess he’d found the spot.

                              The water in the pit seemed clear, but the bottom stayed dark even at midday. All the trees nearby were dead. Geomantic readings confirmed the elkman’s suspicions.
                              As the sun descended, Kemalainen drew a solid black circle on his brow. The elkman placed salt-crystals and a small silver nail in each of his jars, scratched Incarnate Names in Old Realm on the sides, and sealed the lids with wax. He then buried the pots around the edges of the pit and piled stones over them. With a silver-bladed knife he carved signs on the trunks of dead trees, then struck a silver nail into the center of each sign. As the last sliver of sun sank behind a hillside he spoke and his voice thundered with the power of Essence and the force of his will.

                              “By salt and silver I abjure, let mighty Zatesh shut the door! By traveled staff of lightning-tree, the road is closed by Mercury! By holy names and dauntless Sun — and by my will, the ward is done!”

                              “But I want my friends,” the ghost-girl said plaintively as she waded soundlessly from the black water and pushed against the unseen barrier of the ward.

                              “And I must have children for my master,” rasped a voice from behind Kemalainen. And the other ghost was upon him.

                              <3>An Ancient Legacy

                              Elkmen claim descent from Cervinus, a Lunar sorcerer of the First Age. Cervinus, the Kalenders say, was a crony of the legendary Salina and assisted in her great Working. This makes elkmen natural thaumaturges. A few elkmen even learn true sorcery, guided by the Salinan Working.

                              Cervinus died during the Shogunate (and the Kalenders say he died hard). Before he died, he charged his offspring with guarding the Working and its expression through Creation’s geomancy. The Kalenders carry on that mission.

                              <3>Salinan Terminals

                              As every professional mystic knows, the Salinan Working wove the trials of sorcerous initiation into Creation so that people could become sorcerers without the guidance of anyone else. There are many tales of Salinan-initiate sorcerers perceiving the secret of their first spell in the flight of birds or the twists in a chunk of driftwood.

                              Places that see great concentrations of free Essence — notably, but not exclusively, demesnes — sometimes catch and materialize such epiphanies, ready for any perceptive sorcerer to learn. No mortal can explain why a particular location becomes an access-point to the Working, or why the terminal stores one spell or body of lore, and not another. (The Maiden of Secrets unhelpfully explains it as “destiny.”) Kalenders say that if no one in Creation remembers how to cast a spell, the Salinan Working writes it into a terminal, but such a claim is difficult to prove or refute.

                              A Salinan terminal holds points of spells and lore equal to twice the demesne’s rating. (Terminals outside a demesne rarely hold more than one or two points of content.) One point “buys” one Ability dot and one Specialty in some arcane subject. Spells cost one point per Circle of sorcery. For instance, a terminal might impart Medicine (Acupuncture +1) in the form of thorny vines that curve in the shape of Essence-flows within the human body, with spines marking the acupuncture points; or it might teach Sapphire Countermagic (costing two of the terminal’s points) through the patterns revealed when a beam of light shines through a mass of crystals. Extracting information from a Salinan terminal involves use of the same geomantic Procedure used to find its Essence token. A skilled manse architect might be able to transfer the terminal into the manse as a built-in archive of mystic lore.

                                  Cervine Kalenders [Part 3]

                                  <2>At The Ashram

                                  Ou Lan took a sparrow’s guise to follow the elkman thaumaturge through the hills. At last the Kalender led her to a tiny hamlet of his kind, and then to the mouth of a cave. She took the form of a rat to scurry after him. The signs carved around the cave mouth did not bar her way.

                                  The passage ended in a huge cavern. Forests of white stone icicles hung from the arched ceiling; mighty pillars of fluted stone rose from the floor, looming in the faint light of scattered oil-lamps. Ou Lan felt the Essence of the place prickling against her soul.

                                  Her elkman guide bowed deeply to an elkman with silver-gray fur who sat in a posture of meditation. In addition to small mirrors, the elder’s antlers bore twisting lines of yellow, red and black pigment.

                                  “Hail, Balimoinen, Voice of Cervinus,” said her guide.

                                  “Hail, Kemalo, and happy your return from walking in the world,” the elder replied. “But you did not return alone.” The elder slowly swung his lowered head back and forth. Ou Lan shrank behind a stone pillar and froze, but the elder stopped his slow nutation when he faced her. The painted antlers looked like two hands stretched to grab her. “Come forth, Chosen of the Moon,” the elder said. “Your kind is not forbidden here.”

                                  Ou Lan told herself she was mighty in her Exaltation and felt only… surprise. Not fear. Fear was for mortals. The young Lunar strode forth in the form of a weasel, then a bobcat, a wolf and finally her half-gull form, blue and silver Essence swirling about her and burning in brilliant spikes along her silver tattoos. “Hail, Balimoinen,” Ou Lan said. “Be not afraid… much. I require your service.” The younger elkman took a step back as Ou Lan approached them. The elder sat unperturbed.

                                  “I may assist the Moon-Chosen,” Balimoinen said. “If I can. But I expect assistance in return. Forgive me if I seem unmoved by your glory,” he added. “I feel it, but I cannot see it.” The elder raised his head, and Ou Lan saw his eyes were blank, polished stone.

                                  <3>The Kalenders and the Exalted

                                  Each ashram of the Kalenders has a caretaker. This elkman may well be a powerful sorcerer (for someone limited to the Terrestrial Circle, anyway). These are the Kalenders’ only officers. Members of the order defer to Kalenders with greater seniority, lore and achievement, but only a caretaker can forbid or command another Kalender — and then only within the ashram itself. Given how easily a discontented Kalender can avoid contact with the organization in the Northern wilds, sensible caretakers rarely demand much of other members. Kalenders call these caretakers Voices of Cervinus, as they believe these elders sometimes contact an eidolon of their ancestor embedded in the Salinan Working. Certainly, long meditation on (and in) Salinan terminals seems to grant Voices unusually sensitive perception of Essence, and sometimes-oracular revelations.

                                  Cervinus gave his children a jaundiced view of the Dragon-Blooded. Events in the last thousand years did nothing to improve the Kalenders’ opinion: Wyld Hunts slew many elkmen for their presumed association with the Lunar Anathema, or as Hundred Gods heretics for mediating between Northern communities and local spirits. A few Kalenders know about the Sidereal Exalted and regard them as similarly hostile to their order. That doesn't make cooperation impossible; but a Terrestrial or Sidereal may have to work at gaining trust.

                                  Kalenders readily assist the Lunar Exalted but do not casually violate their own codes for a Lunar’s sake. Whether such assistance develops into a deeper alliance depends on how the Lunar treats Kalenders. In any case, the Kalenders cannot supply more than two dots of Allies, Backing, Followers, Influence or Mentor — they are too few and too scattered. A Lunar who became a strong and trusted Ally might be allowed to claim a hidden Manse.

                                  The Kalenders hold no institutional opinions about other Exalted — in the case of Solars, for instance, because they have no experience of them, while they know every Exigent is unique. They, as individuals, judge other Exalted as individuals. Kalenders are slow, however, to trust any being of power who lacks a recommendation from another Kalender or a Lunar ally: The elkmen have learned to be cautious with the unknown. Too many times, strange entities have turned out to be Fair Folk playing bizarre and deadly games.
                                  -----------

                                      <1>Riwa [Part 1]

                                      About midway between the Lap and Gem, the Diamond Road crosses the plateau of Riwa. Let the caravan move on; linger instead in the plaza of Qoba, one of Riwa’s seven cities. Listen to the bard, descendant of ancient princes, as he sings the Riwayana, epic repository of the country’s history, heroes and lore.

                                      Four times hath Riwa risen, ruled by mighty kings,
                                      And four times hath it fallen through the faults of men.
                                      An Age of Gold by glory gained, and lost through treacher’s guile.
                                      An Age of Silver subsequent, built and lost for love.
                                      A Dragon Age of toil and greed, by plague its vices purged.
                                      A new law from the Risen King, by zealous pride undone.
                                      A Fifth and Final King shall come: Hear now the prophecy!
                                      When Riwa rises ne’er to fall, from mortal fault set free.

                                      The Riwayana has five books, each taking a full day to sing, but the fifth book is not yet known. Today, the bard sings only a brief passage from the fourth book as a way to introduce Qoba’s king.

                                      <2>Blood and Soil

                                      King Ruhollah stands on the high dais before his palace, his body painted black and a maguey thorn in his hand. He thrusts the spine through his earlobe, smears his hand with blood, and raises it high. A priest hands Ruhollah an ear of maize. The king then drives the thorn through his tongue and spits blood onto the dark soil that covers the platform. Finally, he lances his own manhood. He collects the blood in a small bowl and dribbles it over the skull of his grandfather. Thus does Ruhollah enact the covenant of the Risen King and proclaim himself the rightful heir: He hears the words of his ancestors, repays the earth for its life-giving bounty, and acknowledges that life comes from death. The charcoal on his body shows that his flesh comes from the Black Earth and to that darkness must return.

                                      <3>Colors of Earth

                                      Riwans treat black as the color of both life and death. Strips and dots of Black Earth run along the seasonal streams and ancient qanats that bring water from the mountains. Here the Riwans plant beans and maize. When Riwans die, they return their bodies to the Black Earth to nourish the soil with their flesh and the vital force of their lower souls.

                                      Yellow Earth surrounds the farmland. This land is too dry for crops but supports sheep and goats. The half-nomadic herdsmen are not quite respectable in Riwa, for they do not live on land sanctified by their ancestors. Still, they can return to their native villages each year to perform the sacred rites. Yellow is the color of wild things, the world without humanity. Shrines to elementals are painted yellow.

                                      The Fire Mountains to the west are the Blue Earth, the sky-land that brings water. Blue is the color of Heaven and a suitable hue for shrines to gods; an auspicious color, but not as good as black.

                                      To the East, the plateau ends in a maze of canyons, knife-edged ridges and badlands. Beyond this Ragged Edge stretches the great Southern Desert. Riwans call the desert the Red Earth, the evil land of sandstorms and raiders where death brings no life. Ancient fortresses guard the passes through the Ragged Edge. All seven kings should send troops to the fortresses, for so commanded the Dragon Lords of old. The watch sometimes fails because the modern kings of the city-states cannot agree about which fortress belongs to which king. Some Riwans, especially those living near the Ragged Edge, volunteer. Such wardens cannot be conscripted for other battles.

                                      The seven cities of Riwa are Borsuna, Dolawi, Orzad, Qoba, Resht, Uda and Wegál. Of the ruined cities, the most important is Aman-Ri, first and eternal capital of Riwa. Nothing remains of the Golden King’s palace except the great stepped, circular mound that supported it. Nevertheless, every king of Riwa since his reign — both high kings of old and the rulers of the schismed cities of today — crowns himself on the mound of the Golden King. They could not rule otherwise, or at least not rule in Riwa.

                                      <3>The Pact of Life and Death

                                      Riwans build shrines to placate various useful or important spirits, but they reserve true reverence for their ancestors. They bury their dead in the Black Earth with clothing, food and ornaments for the afterlife. After ten years, Riwans exhume the bones and pack them in clay jars. Wealthier Riwans use urns molded with death masks of the occupants. The jars of poor Riwans merely have a face sketched on the side. In return for prayers and offerings, Riwan ghosts bless and protect their living descendants. Few Riwans encounter ghosts, though. The Risen King taught this covenant of the living and the dead, but also said that each should stay in their own world. Riwa has no shadowlands, so few ghosts have easy access to the living in any case.

                                      Matters are different for nobles. Each city has several noble families. The nobles rise and fall in rank based on the number and power of their ancestral ghosts: The aristocracy of the living echoes the aristocracy of the dead. The royal family just has the strongest cohort of ghosts. New deaths can swell that cohort; Lethe shrinks it. Dynasties shift over the decades.

                                      The ziggurat of the noble dead dominates a Riwan city’s plaza, overtopping the palaces of the living. Each tier of the ziggurat has crypts cut into its sides where black-glazed urns rest on altars of diorite, receiving sacrifices of incense, grain and blood. The royal family claims the higher tiers and crowning spire. The sacred ziggurat enables the dead to visit their tombs when mortals sacrifice to them, and so advise their heirs.

                                          Riwa [Part 2]

                                          <2>Holy War

                                          Tawia of the noble Darhune family dons the regalia of war: a breastplate, a skirt of leather strips, bracers, greaves and helm. Last she dons the silver-gilt mask of a desert jackal. The sisters in her martial order dress likewise, with masks of vulture, lizard and leopard. Tradition says that women must not fight in war, so they wear the visage of beasts instead. In their own chapterhouses, the male nobles don similar panoplies in the colors of their orders: Storm Knights in blue and white, Flame Knights in red and orange, Stone Knights in gray and brown, Maize Knights in green and yellow, and River Knights in green and blue. They paint their faces in their order’s colors instead of wearing masks. The warriors march from their chapterhouses to the plaza and shout, “Riwa and Qoba! Ruhollah and the Risen King!”

                                          Similar ceremonies take place in Dolawi. The kings spent a month scheduling the war. Deaths among the aristocracy left both cities short of slaves, for nobles must not go alone to the Black Earth. A war enables the nobles to re-stock.

                                          Meeting in battle, knight shouts challenge to knight. They wager handfuls of conscripted peasants on their duels. Tawia wins ten soldiers as slaves for the Darhune. Only the bravest dare to wager their own lives: The victor shall sacrifice the loser to his ancestors.

                                          The second day brings the melee. Knights charge at the common soldiers of each city, striving with bola, net and mace to capture additional prisoners. The conscripts bear only padded clubs.

                                          On the third day, the two kings duel. Ruhollah loses and so must give 100 of his subjects to the king of Dolawi.

                                          “My ancestors demand more,” says King Fodjour. “Double or nothing? I know it’s irregular, but what can I do?” King Ruhollah allows that his ancestors say the same, but suggests they simply duel for another 100 soldiers. Perhaps they shall break even.

                                          <3>Secret Conquest

                                          Indeed, lately the ancestors demand more every year: more incense, prayers and blood, richer funerals even for the common folk, more beasts and slaves sacrificed at the death of nobles. More wars. Everyone complies. No king would dare seem stingy: He would offend his ancestors and weaken his claim to inherit the Risen King’s reign. Instead of making war once every few years, Qoba has fought two wars already this year.

                                          Nobody points out that the Risen King’s law does not include Riwa’s ritualistic slave-taking wars. When the Risen King departed, the lords of the seven cities elected a new high king from among their number. Sore losers turned to civil war. Kings and nobles sacrificed prisoners to strengthen their ancestors, which the ancestors endorsed for their own benefit. The Riwans eventually gave up on high kings, but the wars continued.

                                          Limited contact between the living and the dead also means that no Riwan has yet figured out that the new demands don’t come from their ancestors. In fact, the First and Forsaken Lion conquered Riwa-of-the-Underworld four years ago. Most of the Riwan ghosts now labor in the Deathlord’s prayer mills. He destroyed any noble ghosts whom he could not break to his will. Many of the ghosts who manifest in the mortuary ziggurats are imposters.

                                          In each city, bogus ancestors tell kings that ceremonial wars are not enough. They should train their knights and soldiers for real wars, marching north and south along the Diamond Road. Dutifully, the kings obey. Already, some launch raids against nearby tribes and villages, capturing entire populations for slaves and sacrifices. They want to be ready when the Final King appears to forge Riwa once more into a great nation… from mortal faults set free.

                                              <3>Tephra, That Was Anadem

                                              The nearest shadowland to Riwa lies almost 100 miles south of the plateau, along a now-disused spur of the Diamond Road.

                                              Once there was a valley between two knots of mountains, its rich soil watered by several small streams. Here was built Anadem, City of Flowers. A high volcanic mountain overlooked the flower-scented city. The folk of Anadem prayed to Heaven in gratitude and joy.

                                              Then the volcano erupted so violently as to destroy its eastern flank. A deluge of burning ash swept over the vale of Anadem in minutes, killing everyone and everything. The people had time only for brief prayers. None were delivered, for the gods are weak and inattentive; but many of the people became ghosts. Their death-prayers carried Anadem into the Underworld.

                                              The city’s name died with its inhabitants. By day, the valley remains a desolation of barren gray ash where nothing moves except the wind. At night the city of Tephra appears, gray and white gables and towers rising in the starlight.

                                              Many among the living and the dead believe that someone in Anadem must have sinned a great sin to offend the volcano’s god. Others suggest the god envied Anadem and destroyed the city out of spite. No one can ask the god, for no one has seen him since the cataclysm. The remains of the mountain, half a mile shorter than before, snarl toward Tephra like the jaw of a skull half-buried in the ground. Its former name is cursed and unspoken. Instead, men call it Anadem’s Pyre.

                                              The ghosts of Tephra do not cultivate their ghostly ash-fields or otherwise pretend that they still live. They know they are dead. They could scarcely pretend otherwise, given the seared and seeping ectoplasmic flesh exposed by the blackened, flaking remnants of spiritual skin. The ghosts hate the living out of envy. They still pray, but now they have only one prayer that they offer to Oblivion: May all gods burn.
                                              [END BOXED TEXT]

                                                  <1>Tombolo [Part 1]

                                                  The free city of Tombolo lives by trade. Most of the goods and services are the same as any coastal city in the West: The same fresh-caught fish and squid at the quays, the same coir mats and copra from Wavecrest, the same armor and weapons from Coral, the same nacre buttons and turtle-shell combs from the Neck; the same net-menders, chandlers and whores.

                                                  Five bazaars stand out. Tombolo’s Spice Market is better than most, though Azure’s is larger. The Slave Market is similarly well stocked and a favorite place for the Lintha to sell their surplus. The Cloth Market is a marvel: Here see cottons and linens dyed the famous Tombolo green, as well as Wavecrest silks and Coral woolens, Delzahn mohair and Realm brocade — even the rare byssus of far Nathir, worth its weight in jade. The Wine Market sells vintages from throughout the West as well as other forms of alcohol. At the Bright Market one may purchase bangles and beads, poems scribed on plaques of agate and abalone, Gateway sets of gold and alabaster, caskets of seed-pearls and similar trinkets and treasures.

                                                  But one who knows and has the right introductions may attend other markets and join in stranger commerce. In unnamed back-room shops, smiling vendors sell the skulls of ancient Dragon Kings, grimoires scribed in human blood, black death-roses that never wilt, and years of life in gossamer flasks. One may buy a new face, or a new fate. Spices and liquors of Heaven and Hell are for sale, and cloth woven of jewels, voices or dreams.

                                                  Tombolans call this secret commerce the Hell Market. While some commodities come from spirits or the Fair Folk, the Underworld or Yu-Shan, much of the stock in trade comes from Malfeas. Demons walk openly in Tombolo — as befits a city ruled by a demon. Brocard, called the Hyacinth of Truth, rules the sea-girt city as its absolute master.

                                                  Learned folk understand this is very wrong and someone should do something. But the Great and the Wise endure Brocard’s presence in Creation. It is too useful.

                                                  Like the city it rules, Brocard lives by trade.

                                                      Tombolo [Part 2]

                                                      <2>The Most Important Commerce

                                                      Nelian entered Tombolo with a group of farmers. Once across the Conqueror’s Causeway, she donned a messenger’s kepi and the farmers promptly forgot her. She trotted past row houses and warehouses, up stairways and across bridges as she ascended to the city’s higher tiers. Glancing down at the harbor from the Grand Rampart, Nelian saw ships from Coral, Wavecrest, Gannet and the Realm lining the quays. Further out, an inbound ship from Skullstone passed a fortress-crowned sea stack while the city’s fishing fleet receded into the distance.

                                                      Past the palazzos of citizens and the shrines of gods, Nelian reached Tombolo’s central peak. Only two structures occupied this topmost tier: the palace-manse of Tombolo’s master and the seven-sided, roofless structure that honored his ultimate progenitor. Its azure-framed doors stood open. Glancing inside, Nelian saw a Guildsman in red velvet lighting incense on one of the altars, but no one else. That was good. The day this fane had a priest… Nelian shuddered a moment, then hurried on.

                                                      A brooch enameled with a hyacinth flower got her past a side door and its blood-ape guard. Once in the palace, Nelian doffed her cap and snagged a feather duster from a closet. Servants, clerks and guards ignored Nelian as she walked through the palace halls, since she obviously belonged there.

                                                      In this way, Nelian reached the salon where a merchant in Ragara livery and a local slave-dealer sat opposite each other at a small table. Small violet flames danced a finger-length above each man’s head. They glanced curiously at Nelian. So did Brocard, who stood at a third side of the table. The master of Tombolo tapped its lower left hand on the table to reclaim the traders’ attention while Nelian dusted the blue porcelain medallions set in the room’s white marble walls. The merchants turned back to their host and resumed their negotiations. Now and then they flinched and flickers of violet light escaped their mouths as their words strayed from exact and literal honesty. At last, the clerks waiting on a side-bench wrote out a contract in violet ink, the traders signed it, and Brocard sealed it with azure wax. The small flames went out. Another servant led the merchants from the salon. A gesture from Brocard sent the clerks away, too. Nelian sat in the chair last used by the Ragara trader. Brocard nodded in greeting.

                                                      “Well, demon, what do you have this month?”

                                                      Brocard tented the fingers of its upper pair of arms. “My informants say the Quarter Prince declared war on the Player of Games.” Nelian waved it on. She’d get the details, but the demon Octavian was always at war with someone. “The Apothecary of Stench found a patron in Creation.” That was more interesting. The alchemist Mestrolian was less terrible than most Second Circle demons, but none could be ignored. “And the Manse of Echoes Ascending..." Brocard paused expectantly.

                                                      Nelian leaned forward. “Yes?”

                                                      The demon tapped its fingers together, amethyst nails glittering, and smiled. “Ah, fate-weaver, news of an Unquestionable costs more than the Bureau’s forbearance. What news do you bring from Heaven?”

                                                      <3>The Free City…

                                                      The city of Tombolo consists of a small but mountainous island half a mile from the largest island in the Tidemont archipelago. During the Shogunate, the little island was carved into a city of narrow streets connected by stairways. Bastions, gates and ramparts made the entire city a fortress. Outlying reefs became breakwaters for a great harbor. Smaller forts crowned steep islets even further from the shore. At this time, the city’s name was Tenshu-Daketsu, the Fortress Mountain-Harbor.

                                                      In the Six Island War two centuries before the Great Contagion, the daimyo Chagan Ugona conquered Tenshu-Daketsu. He could not take the city by sea, so his forces built a causeway from the main island. Over the centuries, silt accumulated along the causeway to create a narrow strip of land. The poorest folk of Tombolo live on this low-lying isthmus. Every few years, storm surges wash away dozens or hundreds of shacks and hovels; but there are always more poor people ready to rebuild.

                                                      The Scarlet Empress occupied the city, by then called Tombolo, as one of the Realm’s first conquests in the West. She lost it during the war against the Anathema Jochim when various Western satrapies took the chance to rebel. In the complicated peace negotiations that followed, Tidemont became sovereign but its new Dragon-Blooded aristocrats agreed to a power-sharing agreement for Tombolo. The co-dominium broke down within a decade. When the fighting stopped, there was a demon in charge.

                                                      <3>…And Its Master

                                                      The Hyacinth of Truth should not have compelled certain citizens of Hell to speak without lies. Its own Third Circle progenitor Erramun, the Dust Ulema, sought to destroy it and grow a new Messenger Soul. Brocard fled Malfeas and took refuge in Creation. The Bureau of Destiny soon found it — but Brocard made a deal. In return for sanctuary, it provides information about the Demon Realm. The Sidereals even let it occupy and rule the war-ravaged city of Tombolo.

                                                      Mere forbearance by the Sidereals would not protect Brocard from its progenitor’s will and assassins sent from Malfeas. The Hyacinth of Truth buys allies back in Hell by providing information about Creation and — rarely — Heaven.

                                                      Brocard’s power to compel honesty goes beyond forcing people to tell the literal truth (as best they know it). It can hallow a bargain so that those who break their side of the deal wither and die with dust in their veins. Merchants usually settle for assurances of an honest intent to fulfill a contract.

                                                      The Hyacinth of Truth can also stride through the air, extending its six spider-like legs into stilts of violet fire hundreds of yards long. Pirates who raid Tombolo quickly find their rigging aflame. Thus does Brocard assure the safety and prosperity of Tombolo.

                                                          Tombolo [Part 3]

                                                          <2>Powers and Players

                                                          Vozor tapped a plump finger on the azure-edged papyrus. “Make your marks here by your names. Your lovely daughter is mine, confectioner, and my protection is yours once more. So long as you pay on time.” He smirked at the woman who wore her smock half-open, with a ribbon in her hair. “Smile, my dear. Your family will be safe. And some girls learn to enjoy the sesselja’s changes.”

                                                          The gray-haired man bent over Vozor’s desk — but instead of grasping the proffered pen, he flicked his hands and transparent chakrams of Essence-hardened air slashed into the throats of Vozor’s guards. Before the dying guards hit the ground, the man slammed Vozor face-first into the wall. The confectioner’s image unraveled in threads of Essence. In his place stood a younger man clad in saffian, silk and silver-blue mail. Mela’s blue-white light shone about him, and a breeze ruffled his blue-black hair. Meanwhile, the young woman barred the office door.

                                                          “The Exigency, whoremaster,” Ledaal Torino said pleasantly. “You know who’s selling it. Tell me or die.”

                                                          Vozor struggled in the Dynast’s grip. “I am a citizen!” he ground out. “Brocard will avenge me!”

                                                          “Only if I’m still here when he finds out,” Torino replied. A dagger of air appeared in his free hand. He pressed below Vozor’s double chin. “Speak quickly, demon-f***er.”

                                                          “Please, Prince of the Earth,” Vozor whimpered. “I am watched. I will die if I speak to you!”

                                                          “Which way do you live longer?” Then an eye opened amid the oiled ringlets at the back of Vozor’s head and glared at Torino. The Dynast felt faint as a cold, alien will pressed against his mind. His grip slacked for only a moment. I bear the blood of dragons. Such deviltry does not defeat me! Cold air swirled around him, blowing papers through the room. “Not that easy, whoremaster.”

                                                          Vozor screamed. Torino swore as the eye sank into Vozor’s head and blood gushed from the hole. The Dynast stabbed into the wound, hoping to kill the thing before it killed the Tombolan. Too late.

                                                          Guards pounded on the door. “Get the blood-ape!” one called. Torino’s lips tightened.

                                                          “Sorry, sweetling,” he told the young woman. “Saving your family might be a little harder than I thought.”

                                                          “But a deal’s a deal,” Nelian replied, making her voice shake a little. “Maybe Vozor has something written about that Exy-thing. Maybe we can find it before they bring the demon.”

                                                          “Brave heart!” Torino said, smiling, and began tossing the room.

                                                          Nelian did not smile. Bureau policy said to keep Torino thinking she was a simple tradesman’s daughter he had used to reach Vozor — and that his learning of the Exigency had been chance. The eye in the back of Vozor’s head, though, was pelagial work. If those creatures have an Exigency… For stakes this high, she might need a partner, not a dupe.

                                                          <3>Citizens, Clients, Strangers and Slaves

                                                          Tombolo has a simple government called the Dominate. The law is whatever Brocard says at the time. Seven human deputies oversee particular fields of concern such as state violence (defending the city and arresting lawbreakers) or collecting and disbursing funds. Brocard endorses everything its deputies do — until it doesn’t, at which point deputies may be summarily executed. That was the deal Brocard offered.

                                                          The Deal underlies Tombolan law. No Tombolan has any obligation to any other, except what they spell out in a signed or witnessed contract. Even rights to life and property come only through purchase from Brocard (costing Resources •••• per year). Such persons become citizens. Everyone else can be robbed, raped, cheated or slain with impunity… unless they find a citizen willing to subcontract their protection. How /much/ protection a citizen grants, and what he demands in return, is up to the parties involved. Who does not buy protection, or have the strength to protect himself, becomes a slave to whoever has the power to take and hold him. Tombolans call this “Liberty.”

                                                          Visitors can buy temporary protection at a weekly cost of Resources •. In return, they can buy and sell in Tombolo’s markets, and shall not be enslaved — but Brocard promises nothing more. A pin or patch of a hyacinth flower marks such protected strangers. Forging a pin infringes on Brocard’s rule and carries the death penalty.

                                                          The law of Tombolo extends to all. Citizens can summon demons as they will, but demons can buy citizenship or patronage just as humans can. So can gods, elementals, Fair Folk or anyone else: The Hyacinth of Truth treats everyone the same.

                                                          <3>Watchers

                                                          <n>In a squalid waterfront tavern, a Guildsman and a Lintha captain plot a slave-raid on the Gannet Isles. The sodden, snoring sailor in a corner booth is actually wide awake and memorizing every word for the All-Seeing Eye. The rat scrounging crumbs under the bar is a Lunar who seeks the identities of the Realm’s agents in Tombolo. The bartender shivers briefly as he steps through the ghost that has followed the rat for days. Unseen by all but the ghost, an undertow wends its way through the tavern, sensing everyone’s Essence as well as looking for merely mortal spies. The small god works for the Guild: The Guildsman cares less about the slaves to be gained than about who responds to the information he planted.

                                                          Tombolo is packed with spies. Many interested parties want to trade with or spy on Malfeas, the Bureau of Destiny, and each other. The Great Houses of the Realm place agents in Tombolo. So does the Guild, Skullstone, Coral, the Pearl Court and every major Western nation or supernatural faction that’s organized enough to have agents. Pelagothropes, People of the Sea and pelagials rise from the water on moonless nights to pursue their own secret dealings.

                                                          In a city where loyalty is just another commodity for sale, very few people are only what they seem. Artisans, laborers and guards spy on their citizen protectors and sell what they learn to secret patrons — though a prudent citizen can use such channels to spread disinformation. It’s also rarely in a spy’s interest to be too rigid in her loyalties: So often, the person best able to help a mission is the enemy of an enemy… until the inevitable betrayal or discovery of true identities and agendas. In the city ruled by the Hyacinth of Truth, the games of deceit never end.
