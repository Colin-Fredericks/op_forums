                    Originally posted by Ketrus View Post

                    Ah, right. I forgot about that sidebar, and couldn't search for it because consistent verbiage is hard.

                    This still does not precisely answer the question of how CoCT interacts with battle groups. Any particular mortal soldier would probably be a trivial opponent, but there could be enough of them to form a size >= 1 battlegroup that actually rolls into play. In that case, what happens when the secondary effect of CoCT (automatically doing double damage to insignificant opponents) hits the battlegroup? Nothing, because the battlegroup is significant? Or complete dissolution, because the insignificant opponents comprising it are all pincushions?

                    And, really, that assumes that the developers meant for "insignificant" and "trivial" to be synonymous.
                    If you face a battlegroup with a size > 0 then it is significant by definition. The rules on P. 206 say that a size 0 BG is one where all members are either dead or have fled. Everything above 0 has a magnitute track and is still in the fight.

                    The way I read it, CoCT has two elements and functions. The first gives you a free full excellency against a single target and is a normal combat charm. The second copies the projectile and acts as a scenery destroyer. Note that it mentions "all significant objects and insignificant opponents" in one sentence to ensure they are treated equally. Also note how it specifies significant objects. This means it affects things that are not just flavor (which can be destroyed by stunting) but things that actually have some sort of combat function, such as a wall or crowd blocking your way.

                    It makes little sense to do more damage to secondary targets than it does to the primary one if both are actual combat participants.

                    From a mechanical point of view, Battlegroups are a single target, so CoCT would strike the group and do normal damage and the copies would strike scenery and trivial opponnents around the battlegroup. Narratively that makes little sense, but I'm pretty sure that is how it works.

                        Originally posted by ObsidianHeart View Post
                        I'm a new ST and I don't really understand how lying works.
                        What do you roll? Against what? And another thing, can multiple character try persuading or instilling(degrading a defining intimacy or instilling a new one) in the same scene?
                        Lying is an Instil Action:

                        "The instill action is used to change the feelings and beliefs of others. When a player takes an instill action, he declares what he wants to make his target feel or believe." (P. 215)

                        If you succeed, the target forms an initmacy of "I believe x on issue y" or "z has done/believes y". Lies are the basis you build social influence on. They are not an influence in themselves. Social influence is about getting people to do something, which means you need to not only tell the lie but also incite action.

                        As for what to roll, there are no hard rules because it depends on how you tell the lie. If you simply talk to the person, use presence. If you make a grand speech and accuse someone publicly, its performance. Use Linguistic for written lies. Generally Manipulation sounds like the go to for lying but I could see charisma working out if you are relying on your reputation and personality to make the lie believable, rather than outmaneuvering your target's brain. Either way, you're targeting resolve.

                        A person with sufficient facts to contradict your lie would likely get a [appropriate ability] + wits/intelligence vs your guile to see through the lie, either by catching your intention or seeing the contradiction of facts. Use read intentions unless there is a better suited combination.
