        Originally posted by MoroseMorgan View Post
        What breaks if you allow massive strengh differences to penalize Parry?

        I always come back to Dex being god stat for to hit, and then also contributing to damage via threshold successes (for withering at least). Combine that with the fact that in fiction big strong heavy hits are harder to block, and I thought of a houserule. When parrying, compare strength and damage values, and if the attacker has 4+ more damage potential, then the defender is at -1 penalty. Granted this is one more step in an already complicated system, but this way, Strength 5 Heavy Weapon negates the +1 Parry of Strength 3 Medium.

        Phrase it as "If the attacker's strength damage total is 4+ greater than the defender's, then treat the defender's Onslaught as 1 greater for this attack", that way this would fall under an existing penalty, and any powers that negate it.

        The only part I haven't worked out is how charm damage should affect this, or if it even should. As it stands, it squares up neatly for you have to have 2 orders of magnitude in your favor for this to apply. 2 strength, weapon category, or artifact. Forex- 5 strength and an artifact medium levies a penalty against 3 strength and a mundane medium.
        Dodge becomes absolutely necessary for any combat build that doesn't focus on a heavy weapon. Certain combat abilities, especially brawl and martial arts styles that focus on lighter weapons, are significantly penalized. Non-combat focused PCs with the most affected combat abilities are even further penalized, as they're forced to move away from their non-combat focuses of choice and pick up enough Dodge Charms to remain combat viable. The biggest advantage of medium weapons (their defense value) is often negated, leaving the category decidedly unappealing. Play slows down as every potentially parried blow needs to compare the relevant damage values to determine what the parry penalty should be.

        Meanwhile, Dex remains the most important combat attribute, it just requires a much higher dodge, since it isn't impacted by the house rule.
