    I'm the ST of a circle with a Sail Supernal Eclipse. We just had a naval battle that may help shed light on the situation. First, though I want to address this:

    Originally posted by limaxophobiac View Post
    I'll just note that with the rules such as they are, it also means artifact warship like the one in Arms really aren't worth much compared to being the guy with Salty Dog + Immortal Mariner against someone without. Some extra hull levels and extra damage on broadsides aren't going to save you if you can't get the momentum to fire them and wouldn't hit anything even if you did.
    Person with a Daiklave but no combat charms loses to person with combat charms but no daiklave seems like a pretty fair comparison. It makes sense that the one with charms and excelencies will pull out a win. In fact, I hope that paradigm is true for every subsystem.

    So my Eclipse has finished the Sail tree, has an artifact imbued ship (NOT an artifact ship). He was on one ship, the other 3 solars were each on their own ship. They were attacked by 4 Imperial Triremes in the open sea, no weather effects or hazards, no rocks or coral or whirlpools. A total white room scenario. He destroyed one with a Broadside followed by a ram, destroyed the second with a ram quite easily, and by that time the Non-Sail Solars had boarded one ship and broadsided the remaining one.

    Momentum is gained by Positioning, which is just Wits+Sail+Maneuver (a stat boats have), its an opposed roll. You gain momentum from the difference in your rolls. It takes only 5 momentum to broadside your opponent, which is absurdly low for a Sail focused character who has 11 dice before charms and excellencies come out to play. A broadside attack does a flat 1 or 2 damage depending on your weaponry, and ships only have 3, 4, or 5 hull points. Two Broadside attacks is almost a certain victory for a Sail user.

    Ramming costs 7 momentum and it deals 1 damage + 1 for every 3 successes you beat your opponents roll by. This is the best tactic for great sailors to beat lesser sailors because of the bonus damage potential. The Eclipse scored 22 successes on this roll, and dealt and extra 5 damage for 6 total hull points sundered. That will instantly destroy and non artifact ship.

    But if you don't want to deal with that, just use a basic Boarding action for 8 momentum. Now its just a regular fight. Sail over.

    Honestly, where can I find this "100 turns battle"? I find it hard to believe that rolling two equivalent dicepools against each other cannot get to a difference of 5 so one can broadside the other, then the loser has a dice penalty and its basically a finished battle. Hell, at 8 momentum it literally is a finished battle because the ships stop and the melee starts.

    EDIT: Jeez, forum, help a Solar out a bit.

    Im gonna run the Mortals battle. Wits 3 Sail 3 Specialty 1 Maneuver 2. Dice pool 9

    1) A: 3 successes, B: 4. Net 1 to B.
    B has 1 Momentum

    2) A:1 , B3, net 2 to B
    B has 3 Momentum

    3) A: 4, B: 5, net 1 to B
    B has 4 momentum

    4) A:2 B: 1, Net 1 to A
    B has 3 Momentum

    5) A:1, B: 5, net 4 to B
    B has 7 momentum.

    B attempts to Broadside

    6) A: 3, B: 4
    B Broadsides, does 2 damage.
    B has 2 momentum
    A is at -2 dice

    We can see that B got a lot luckier with the dice then A, but turn 5 roll really handed this to B. This all took me about 5 minutes to roll, type, roll, type, etc. this would have been easy to do one on one in 2 minutes tops, and even still, Exalted is not a system designed for Mortal to Mortal fighting. How well are two men with swords in heavy armor going to slug each other back and forth before someone builds up enough to successfully decisive? How boring is it to run a no-charm, no-mote rolloff?

    I get where people are coming from- if you can't sail then you aren't going to be good in a naval fight- but this is Exalted. Someone is going to have a Sail pool with excellencies of 15+ if you are sailing. Someone is gonna have weather sorcery. Someone is gonna jump 2 range bands onto the enemy ship. Someone is going to set a sail on fire with a flaming arrow. Someone is going to swim up to the enemy ship and rip the rudder out. You are Exalted, you do the incredible everyday. Complaining that a Mortal v Mortal white room is unfulfilling like complaining that a Ferrari doesn't make a good tow truck.

    EDIT 2:
    Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
    Hey has anybody used the naval combat system? To me it looks like the person with the biggest dice pool basically emerges unscratched while everyone else is at the bottom of the ocean. The solar sail charms seem to make this vastly worse.

    Is this inaccurate? I'm interested in play experience.
    So, going back to the first post: yes, this is basically true. A Sail focused character with a big dicepool and charms is going to devastate anyone else on the sea, and quickly. Just like how a Dawn with combat charms is going to roll a bunch of mercenaries, a Night with Larceny charms can rob anybody, and a Twilight with Medicine can Heal anything. Exalted are really good at what they do. That's the point of the game.

    Before anyone @'s me though, Sail is opposed by Sail, so yes, it absolutely is unfair to anyone without Sail, unlike Larceny-Awareness or Brawl-Melee-Archery-MA-Other, there isn't really any variety in Sail builds for combat.

    HOWEVER, you said one really interesting thing: [The Sailor] emerges unscratched while everyone else is at the bottom of the ocean.

    Unlike Initiative, Momentum is non-transferable. Any you build up attacking the Sea Sprite is lost if you switch to attacking the Wave Dancer. And, for every ship that gangs up on you, you lose -2 dice beyond the first on your opposed sail rolls. So 2 ships on 1 is going to be really rough for the Sail Solar, and 3 on 1 is going to cause you to tap deep into your charms and motes to avoid that -4 penalty! It's much harsher than Onslaught penalties. A Dawn can easily roll 3 regular soldiers, an Eclipse is going to have to buck up to beat three ships.

            So here's something else to consider re: dawn vs sail against three opponents- the dawn is going to be at -2 onslaught a good chunk of the time. The ship is at a -4 penalty.

            Both can spend Excellency to negate that penalty pretty easily.

            The fundamental difference between the systems is that if the dawn's opponents get good rolls, they can reduce the dawn's initiative even if the dawn consistently rolls better than them. The sailor's opponents must roll better than the solar on multiple different turns to be able to do anything to him. Plus every single one of the solar's Opponents have double the number of health levels as the ships, and are thus more difficult to kill.

            The difference in charms is that the sail charms are vastly cheaper to gain the same dice tricks, and the sailor gets to activate them AFTER the roll if they want. (There is no melee equivalent to salty dog method that permanently gives you exploding 6's on every sail roll forever. As far as I know, no other ability has this dice trick for free, since it's ridiculously OP )
