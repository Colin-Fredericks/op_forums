    [Ex3] How to challenge a full solar circle

    02-23-2016, 04:00 PM
    I'm finding that it can be very difficult to challenge the might of a full circle of Solar exalts, so I'm making this thread for STs to share their ideas and experiences. Feel free to post non-combat challenges as well, anything to break up the often boring steam-roller sensation of besting obstacle after obstacle without even busting out a full excellency.

    Observations I have made so far:

    1. Don't let them all get in the first hit; my party has won fight after fight by crashing every enemy in round one, hoarding their initiative, then ending the fight in one round of Decisive attacks. This is an extremely effective tactic, so don't let them use it freely. Ambushes and initiative-modding charms for your antagonists will help.

    2. Always out-number them; with the new system even just one more combatant on the field makes all the difference. That's one more attack the party can use each round to crash an opponent and prevent them from launching a decisive blow. If you often use battlegroups, I recommend splitting as many "heroic" characters off as needed to keep the numbers at least even. Never write in a final boss fight with only one antagonist.

    3. The higher the enemy's Soak, the longer the fight; a high soak is just about the only thing besides the Legendary Size ability which I've found can counteract the advantage of wolf pack tactics, even more so than an onslaught negater.

    4. Beware of initiative hoarding; one of my players regularly builds up to 30+ initiative during combat and never unleashes a Decisive attack because he knows that resetting to base initiative will leave him vulnerable. These players are effectively "tanking" for their party, and the best solution is to avoid them and focus on players who might actually get crashed.

    5. Assume someone is going to pass every check; in a group of five Solars you'll probably see full coverage for every kind of skill proficiency, so avoid writing in plot elements that assume every member of the party will fail that check. Awareness is a good example: if even one person in your party makes that obscene roll to notice the ninjas approaching from the South, then the ambush you just wrote in isn't going to go off unless you first split the party or prevent them from communicating.

          Dragon-blooded in numbers, particularly with summoned/browbeaten/allied supernatural support, are brutally effective.

          Fear Sidereals.

          Almost anything without a dice-adder or equivalent effect is going to have a bad time.

          Take advantage of the flexibility offered by opponents possessing sorcery/necromancy and artifacts. Although, obviously, be careful with the latter getting into the hands of your party before you're ready for it.

          Remember, Solars reshape shit and derail plots and that's OK! If they're steamrolling almost everything, well...they're Solars. That's their schtick. If it didn't take an epic effort to seriously stymie them, the setting wouldn't make sense.

                  Don't play the game, play the players.

                  I know my players as people. Over time, I have gleaned insight into how their personalities influence their play style. When I am designing anything, I first consider their reactions to previous similar encounters.

                  It's also worth noting that in a game like Exalted sometimes the best way to handle the might of a party is to deny them the opportunity to use it. Sure, they can probably take a few months and steamroll the local horse tribes, but the Wyld Hunt will have caught up with them. What they really need is to use diplomacy and guile to convince the tribes to unite against the Realm's oppression.

                          I would argue that a Decisive ought to be considered (note: not necessarily selected, but considered) at any Initiative value above 6. It can be tempting to just save up until you can one-shot somebody in a single splattering blow, but at 7 or more you have a reasonably good chance of rolling at least two HLs of damage and knocking an opponent into wound penalties. That tilts things permanently against them. At 10 or higher you have a decent chance of knocking someone straight into their -2's, which is enough to put most folks on the back foot for good. You don't have to kill an enemy to defeat them - if they're worried about actually dying and looking for a way out while nursing a serious injury, they're probably not a threat any longer.

                          Obviously there are a lot of tactical items to consider when choosing how to attack, but it really doesn't take a whole lot of Initiative to make a Decisive worth taking. If a good opportunity presents itself (i.e. their current Defense is low and you're not in immediate danger) it's probably worth jumping on.

                                                  TBH just sometimes going "ok fuck it, you're strong beefy Solars, you can handle it" and dropping a Nemissary or Octavian in there, backed up by a couple war ghosts/blood apes and shit gets real. I think I'm personally beginning to grasp what's dangerous, and can adjust accordingly. Just write up a powerful spirit if your Solars thump everything.

                                                  The best advice in this thread so far, IMHO, was the "play the player"-line. Look at your players tactics and usual approaches, and counter them. If your most deadly guy is a Single Point stylist, swarm him, if he's a White Reaper stylist, don't swarm him. If he's a melee tank, whittle him down from distance, etc.; of course it's never that simple, but if you players specialize, have the antagonists counter-specialize. But don't overdo it or you get a min/maxing arms race. If your players specialize hard in something, they have to feel their character being great at it, or they'll be driven to up their numbers even more, that's unhealthy imho.

                                                  Luckily in my group nobody's a combat supernal (3 characters, no Dawn, heh.) making balance easier.
                                                  But still, if your Solars curbstomp everything I have to ask:

                                                      A quick way to boost BGs ability to fight without having to add another moving piece into the system is to give them what I call a free-commander. Each BG has a commander you give a command dice pool to. His role will only be to lead the unit, he can be target by the players ofc. Just use the magnitude track for BG for health levels (represented as bodyguards or him just being a bit tough to take down) So if you take him down that might cause a rout check or not, up to the ST.

                                                      Easily ups the danger from facing battlegroups, withouth adding yet another initiative number to track.

                                                          Something else to keep in mind is the kind of game you want to run. If you're like me, and you DO expect Solars to start changing the world (or just their little corner of it) from almost session 1, then you probably want a fair number of conflicts (combat and otherwise) to be dramatic, but ultimately diversions to the story of the characters' success in making change (just because Solars DO effect change doesn't mean they get the changes they want). You can feel free to use other Celestials, DBs, and Celestial gods as necessary for large threats.

                                                          If you lean more to the Zel persuasion, then you need to spend more time investing in tactics and strategies to make it feel gritty for the players. Another way to do that is to be more strict with training time and XP. It's much easier to challenge a Dawn with three named mortal swordmasters BEFORE the Dawn has a hundred XP.
