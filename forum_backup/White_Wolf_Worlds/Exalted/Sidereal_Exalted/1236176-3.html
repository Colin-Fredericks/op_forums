            OK,
            When I said "Worst, most Objectively Evil religion" it was a Damages-based calculation, not a dogma/means calculation. By dogma/means, the Silver Prince's New Order telling everyone living that all their loved ones have reincarnated, when you're actually soul-forging them wins hands down, but the Skullstone Archipelago is one relatively small portion of an entire Direction. Contrast that with the Realm-backed Immaculates hunting down Ahlat's chosen ruler, Blood on the Horns, and shattering the tribes of Harborhead's culture, + the devastation of numerous invasions of the East + the subjagation/re-design of An-Teng +++ The greater hideousness of the New Order, outweighed by Less Intense Hideousness, in varying portions of all four Directions, in concentrated form on the Blessed Isle.

            Before we get too into the Immaculate monk championing the peasants, let us remember that "Peasant eaten by a sorcerer's demon?" Immaculate monk kills demon, then finds an (undoubtedly illegal) mortal demonologist (now that mortals can bind demons), or simply frames a mortal for it, rather than pin it on Irresponsible Dynast Sorcerer. I don't see the functionality of the Immaculate Philosophy changing a great deal, when they've retained the total primacy of the Dynastic Houses in Realm culture.

            I find it less relevant that the Immaculate Philosophy has its good points, and more that Chejop Kejak made the valuation that "Acceptable levels of social control" involved Exaltation-based species-ism.

            As for the "playing Russian Roulette ten times with a five-chambered revolver" characterization of the Prophecy of Gold, that's not a fair parallel. It's never even implied that the failure-percentage of the Vision of Gold was quantified. Let alone that it was this insane long-shot. The Vision of Bronze adherents made their stand on "Any possibility of failure AT ALL is too high." With Chejop Kejak's famous speech to that effect. Neither do I accept the linkage that Sidereal efforts to bring about one Vision or the other could've been factored into the Prophecy, since Essence use makes the decree of destiny uncertain.

            In the same way as I've mentioned the same thing several times, the fact that present/modern Golds are not the individuals who were alive at the time of the Usurpation has been mentioned several times. It misses the point that a lack of Sidereals initially opposed to the Usurpation has impact on them by virtue of the legacy of their movement. Joining a movement begun by Sidereals against the Usurpation has a different connotation than joining a movement begun by those who regret the Usurpation. I'm fine with the Gold being a mix of those who were for it, those against it, and those who didn't even want to be involved one way or another. Just like I'm fine with those going the other way INTO the Bronze.

            I simply don't want the default setting-point to be "The Gold grew out of retrospective of Usurpation consequences." If the Bronze Faction Ideology had total initial primacy, the Gold Faction is only a reactionary group. It's the legacy of the groups I'm concentrating on. Not the perspectives of each individual Sidereal, because the legacy of the initial division either limits or expands the moral connotations attaching to both groups. I LOVE the back-and-forth of "Sidereal X was Bronze last life, Gold this one" and vice versa. I HATE the idea of there being no room for a Gold Faction grown out of more than reconsideration of the initial decision to remove the Solars. You get the least-reductive result from initial dissension.

            As for judging Exalts based on their past-lives, I was under the impression that sort of practice was unique, specific and special about Sidereals. That things like "This new Sidereal, who I believe has been misled and lead astray (because they just joined the Gold Faction) was my Sifu and a loyal member of the Bronze in their last life. What a tragedy this is! (said the Bronze Sidereal) was the sort of story element you found among Sidereals in particular.

            Edit: I believe I also mentioned earlier that I adore Ayesha Uru, and the whole "Began Bronze, and the sorcerous initiations Chejop Kejak pushed her towards were the very thing that drove her to return/resurrect the Gold." I have NO problems with the Gold having "died out" for a time. The faction-swapping-through-incarnations is a lovely element of Sidereals. I simply don't see what the possible benefit of focusing the definition of the Gold on the point of Ayesha Uru bringing it back benefits anything but rationales why the old "Bronze 95%, Gold 5%" should exist.

                                        Wyldwraith I would not say that everyone or even a majority of the Forum hates 2E wholesale. Hell, the VAST majority of my play experience was 2E and 2.5E, including a 2.5E shard I ran for my playgroup which spanned over two and a half years. What people have issue with comes in several varieties from my past several years worth of Lurking on this board. 1) 2E unnecessarily adding squick factor when not needed. I return to the humble Mercury Ant as just one example of many where specificity and an overarching need to categorize motivation took a fun and interesting thing and made it both sexual and confrontational when it was not needed. This was not a singular event, nor just contained within one book, and while it was also in several books (as you pointed to the Lunar 1E book was...very problematic as well) within 1E, it was not nearly as ubiquitous.

                                        2) Over-specificity causing ONE TRUE CANON arguments. This one I think is the other compelling point. You mentioned in another thread a love of specificity/quantification and I can totally understand how awesome it can be to be down and delve deep into the lore. However where it becomes a problem is when it cages the types of stories and narratives that can be told within the framework of the Setting. By leaving the First age to each individual storyteller with some broad guidance, they can frame the story in a manner that best suites the chronicle they wish to tell. This is especially true in online play where players may not have the personal connections that are necessary when a ST moves away from established canon, and can lead to extreme arguments.

                                        3) Related to point 1) but also dealing with 1E, the world and gaming environments have changed. Some would see this for the worse but I would contend that this has been a net positive, the kinds of things written just to be either edgy or to make a work feel ''mature" no longer are written the same way. The introduction of the Red Rule and the alteration of the several items within the canon of 3E such as how the Solar/Lunar bond works is nothing but a net positive for this game, and gives players an environment I feel promotes better play and development than the previous incarnations.

                                        Finally, please continue to love Exalted in the manner you wish! If you wish to keep true to the lore of Second edition, and use that as the framework for where you tell your stories, no one here can tell you not to do that

                                            Originally posted by Manual of Exalted Power: The Sidereals
                                            It took many weeks for the seemingly disparate prophecies that form the Vision of Gold to be acknowledged as a single foretelling. The outcomes were so different and strange at every turn that they might as well have been entirely different fortunes, save for one aspect: all were brought about by the Sidereal Exalted revealing their concerns of madness to the Solar Exalted.
                                            In some visions, the Sidereals revealed their concerns to a few Lawgivers who shared the Sidereals’ concerns. They worked together to create a system by which the Solars might themselves be appropriately policed. Working hard to quell any concerns and to keep their system above corruption, the Solars could address the fears of the Seers, and lead Creation to even greater heights of glory, peace and justice than before.
                                            Other visions gave darker outcomes. In those visions, word of Sidereal concerns reached the Solar Exalted. Some Solars agreed, and cast a suspicious eye on their peers, never once considering that they themselves might be mad. Paranoia and accusations of corruption and madness led to civil war. Sidereals and Lunars were forced to either flee the pogroms or be swept up into them. Solar battled Solar until Creation became a wasteland and the Wyld consumed the ruins.
                                            The Sidereals who chose this vision believed they could manage the revelation and help the Solars regain their sanity. After all, the Sun’s Chosen had succeeded at so much. If they could be persuaded to reform themselves, how could they fail?
                                            Many of the Sidereals were not so sure; indeed, even those who did not wish to see Creation lessened still sided with the Vision of Bronze because it was sure. The Vision of Gold fanned into many possibilities, never settling into a single, stable truth. The future foretold by the Vision of Bronze might take a thousand years or more to fulfi ll, but in the end, a new Realm would rule all of Creation, lumpen, a bit brutal, but unchanging, forever.
                                            My "Ten pulls of a revolver with 5 chambers loaded" is harkening back to the thought experiment I posited. How many flips do you consider it 'okay', to gamble all of creation, the suffering and misery of an entire world being slowly strangled and exsanguinated and burnt and tortured until finally everything lies dead? One? Two? Five? When is it "okay" to bet the house and risk losing it all, losing everything everyone ELSE owns, to you? Some people might not want to risk everything, absolutely everything, on that single d20 roll coming up 1-10 instead of 11-20 (if we're being nice and assuming it's 50/50) just because "You're telling me THERE'S A CHANCE".

                                            My argument is opposition to the fact that there's a lot of Bronze Condemner players who say "Oh, the odds aren't stated so OBVIOUSLY they were like, 99,999,999/100,000,000 that the Vision of Gold goes off without a hitch and only one in a hundred million that something goes wrong and Vision of Darkness happens", arguing that it should be assumed that it felt like a sufficient gamble, gambling away everything for everyone else in Creation, to not be worth it for most of the Bronze Vision supporters.
