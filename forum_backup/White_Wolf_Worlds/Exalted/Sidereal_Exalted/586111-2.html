    Considering that "villain," "villainy," "villainous," et cetera., all came about as words in the first place as yet one means by rich people to demonize poor people.

    Villein (Old French), Churl (Germanic), and Boor (Saxon) all basically meant something along the lines of "farmhand," but these days the words are synonymous with being the bad guy.

    Which is to say, sometimes whether you get seen as the bad guy or not is less dependent on what you do or how you do it, but rather who you do it to and how different you are from them.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            I would posit that this is particularly common of people with criminal or violating proclivities; maybe they technically don't think of themselves as villains, but they don't exactly think of themselves as anything else either.
            Pretty much that.

            For example, if you spoke with members of ISIL who are beheading people, they would likely insist that they're doing Allah's work and are good, just and even heroic and inspiring individuals. On the other hand, if you spoke with a member of one of the Mexican drug cartels who cuts people's heads off, they'd probably be more likely to admit that they aren't a good person and that what they're doing is bad. The latter is just because they can't point towards religious ideals or the idea of a "greater good" and instead have to be a little more self-conscious of their own actions and motivations.

            So some people could do terrible things but not think of themselves as villains or terrible people, but in general criminals often tend to be a bit more realistic about what they're doing and their motivations. Even members of the Mafia (or more modern day Yakuzas in Japan), which often tried to dress themselves up as a protector or champion of the common people, were usually willing to admit that they aren't good people and that what they weren't exactly doing good deeds.

                                            To first address the 'killing each other in the streets' comment...see Page 30 of MoEP: Sidereals. The 'nuclear option' for Siddies who hate each other is to strike at the rival Sidereal's loved ones. Killing each other? Madness! Sidereals killing each other is such a massive deal that the gods in the BoD STILL talk about the Night of Fallen Stars, when five Sidereals were outright murdered because they tried to warn the Solars of the Usurpation.
