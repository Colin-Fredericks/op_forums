                        Well sure, they're so isolated from humanity that they are unlikely to identify even remotely with mortals, or be at all concerned with what happens to them.

                        That said... I can't find where you said it Isator but you recently posted that you thought, in spite of how awful the Deathlords are and the horrible degree of atrocities they commit, they have probably been responsible for less human suffering (in scope and scale) than the Realm and Guild. That might be true of Deathlords (especially if we leave out the Great Contagion and the Silver Prince's industrial soulsteel production) but it appears to me that it is 100% true of the ancient Solars. The worst of them committed horrible atrocities... but did not systematically reduce 99.99999% of humanity to grinding poverty with a huge side-helping of widespread chattel slavery. (They also never did anything remotely as destructive as the Great Contagion, or it would have been mentioned in a book by now. So the entire notion that the Solars the biggest possible existential threat to humanity and Creation is already utterly absurd.)

                        It's also important to realize how unsustainable it actually is to build a society that revolves entirely around an insatiable empire that will never be satisfied with zero annual growth, whose economy is by design dependent on rapaciously draining lives and wealth out of the finite Threshold (and systematically eviscerating the capacity of Threshold civilization to defend the borders of Creation, even while being demonstrably incapable of defending the borders in their place.) The Realm is inherently unstable and unsustainable, and puts Creation into a situation that will inevitably collapse. It's no different in this regard from any imperialist or growth-dependent regime in real history, with the added problem that destabilizing, depopulating, and un-developing the Threshold is a giant, neon-sign-waving invitation to another Balorian Crusade.

                        The only way I can actually imagine a worse situation, existentially, is to replace the Realm with people who explicitly want to destroy the world (Deathlords) or whose prime goal is to make human suffering as awful as possible (characature? of the Yozis). Though the Yozis, ironically, would be more than capable of defending Creation from (other) outside threats.



                        It isn't reasonable for any informed Exalt, who realizes what's actually happening, to be content with Creation being destroyed unless they're spiteful and selfish enough that if they can't rule the world, they'll make sure nobody does. However, it is reasonable for somebody to believe that Solar or Lunar rule would be equally bad as the Realm, and want to try a new approach.

                        The question is whether the Factions are monolithic and able to manipulate/deceive/mind-control/whatever their recruits into total ideological "purity", or if there's room for differences of opinion and method within them. I would prefer the latter -- I don't know how the books currently present them.

                                            I was thinking about Dune and Leto II and comparing him in my mind to Chejop Kejak, and I realized... Leto II is a monstrous, horrific person who does monstrous, horrific things, but he's compelling and even in a way admirable because he's competent enough to actually save humanity... and even the horrid things he did were part of a longer-term plan to teach humans to stop doing those same horrid things. Kejak wants to be Leto II, but is so effing incompetent that he fails miserably over and over again, and too narcissistic/stubborn/dumb/not-sure-what to admit he made any mistakes, so he keeps on robotically repeating the same thing over and over like he's already dead... it's not compelling, it's pathetic.                                                             I was thinking about Dune and Leto II and comparing him in my mind to Chejop Kejak, and I realized... Leto II is a monstrous, horrific person who does monstrous, horrific things, but he's compelling and even in a way admirable because he's competent enough to actually save humanity... and even the horrid things he did were part of a longer-term plan to teach humans to stop doing those same horrid things. Kejak wants to be Leto II, but is so effing incompetent that he fails miserably over and over again, and too narcissistic/stubborn/dumb/not-sure-what to admit he made any mistakes, so he keeps on robotically repeating the same thing over and over like he's already dead... it's not compelling, it's pathetic. Worse, it's patently obvious that all the monstrous, horrific things he's done have accomplished almost nothing, which makes him despicable.

                                                            And the option for Sidereals who don't want to march along in lockstep is... join Team Solar Yay Nothing Can Possibly Go Wrong Here!, or become an independent and accept that your goals and plans for fate and Creation will have approximately 0 impact on anything that happens anywhere, ever, because the others hate you now.



                                                            If they're going to have only two factions, at least those factions ought to have varied beliefs and methods and disagreements within them, and darnit, at least a few of them should have a plan that isn't pants-on-head idiotic once in a while.

                                                            But this goes with my idea that Creation shouldn't be Doomed by default, and the PCs should not be obligated to be the Only Sane Ones who can save the world because everybody else everywhere is stoned and/or moronic.

                                                            It's also not really believable that everyone in each faction would agree on all things, and not be scheming against each other.
                                                            I do broadly agree with you.

                                                            But there are a couple of points. First, to be fair, Chejop has, so far, saved Creation. The Solars didn't destroy the world. The Deathlords didn't destroy the world. The Raska didn't destroy the world. The Lunars haven't even managed to destroy the Realm.

                                                            The issue though, is this is largely portrayed as not much to do with Chejop. The Empress saved the world, with Chejop just helping a little. Before that, the contagion failed because of the Raksha. If, say, Chejop had gone "there's one way to save Creation... we need to provoke the Raksha to invade. It's a hard and desperate choice, but we need to do it to save Creation" or something like that. Followed by him perhaps showing the Empress how to get into the Imperial Manse, or something.

                                                            The other thing, of course, is that Creation is basically presented as being doomed. So, although in fact Chejop's plan does seem to have worked, you're basically told "it's about to fail unless someone else saves the day".


                                                            I'd also make a point about the Gold Faction, which you described as Team Solar Yay Nothing Can Possibly Go Wrong Here! Now, if you read Cult of the Illuminated, that is how Ayesha Ura is. But Lupo, her second-in-command, does actually remember the first age, and knows that it absolutely could go wrong. That's why he has a different way of training Solars, and a different view of them, because he wants to make sure that this time it'll go right. So it's not like everyone in the Cult is a blind idiot.
