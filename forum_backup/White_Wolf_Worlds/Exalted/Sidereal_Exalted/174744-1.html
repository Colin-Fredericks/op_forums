                            Sidereals worked as PCs in First edition because unless the ST was either inexperience, lazy, or personally hated the idea of sidereals (Which why would he allow such a character in the first place) their abilities were checked and balanced by the ST.                             Looking at the current situation, they'll probably have a limited charmset. I think we'll probably be able to keep a number of old effects while adding new ones. However, sidereals will probably be developed last again because there charms more than others have to be balanced around other charmsets because of it's nature.

                                            I always felt that the Sidereals closed charm set was a brilliant way to engage the feel of them. First of all, have a look at the Charms in the Presence tree:

                                            Force Decision: If a target is trying to make a decision about something, he chooses whichever option the Sidereal wants.
                                            Impose Motivation: The target gains your Motivation in addition to his own if you win a roll.

                                            Those are amazingly powerful charms, and they aren't exactly high essence: Both are Essence 2. I'd almost call them broken, but they are a little tricky to use. There's another charm where you convince someone that you are lying. That charm is also incredibly powerful, if used properly.

                                            Part of the feel of the Sidereals is the feeling I have every day working in business: I don't have the tools I need, and I can't just go and get new software without running it through just about every damn department, and by the time they are ready to move, the software is obsolete. It really feels like I am an overworked celestial bureaucratic fate ninja trying to save the world despite itself, and that's how the Sidereals are supposed to feel.

                                            Remember that episode in Babylon 5 where Sheridan is trying to convince the League to let him use the White Star fleet to protect them, but he knows if he just says that, they'll refuse? So he tricks them into basically forcing him to put them there, while he resists it at every step, and then at the end capitulates just a little bit too easily, and then is screaming YES! at the end of the episode? That is how playing a Sidereal is supposed to work (not saying that is how it actually works). Oh, and the part where he says 'There is no invisible fleet threatening your territory.'? That's where he TOTALLY used the Sidereal 'I am lying' charm.

                                                I think the closed Charm set gets a bit over thought sometimes.

                                                From what I can gather of Jenna Moran's comments, I get the impression that the original design mandate of "short, powerful trees, but that's all you get" was primarily just for the sake of making Sidereals mechanically distinct, in a manner reasonably informed by their setting place and function and genre aesthetics. The degree to which they're quirky and intricately interlocking is a matter of the touch of Jenna Moran, both in terms of her usual artistic flair and computer science background. The idea that they're designed by the Maidens, who need to be petitioned to for new ones, is a mostly incidental, if fitting, little mythology quirk.

                                                I have started to think that the degree to which they're about "solve problems with toothpicks and duct tape" is also overstated a bit as well. Between comments from Jenna and John, I'm in a mindset that the limitations of the Sidereals have more to do with what they can't do than what they can; as she said, sometimes you had Sidereals who had to face the Ebon Dragon armed with nothing but their own Charms, and they were generally screwed. If their Charms are toothpicks and duct tape, then they're probably mostly about picking teeth and patching things up; maybe sometimes you can be all MacGyver, but I think most of the time when facing that kind of situation you're supposed to leave the duct tape and toothpicks in the box, and instead pull out the prescience and influencing people.

                                                    In character, I think because Sidereal Charm trees are mystical, skill driven expressions of a short, finite, interpretable poem on the nature of reality, expressed by the Maiden. Each Charm is part of the poem (and the poem explains part of the nature of the Maiden). That's just what they are.

                                                    You can't really glue or grow new magic on the edges of that. You have to go and write a new poem, and that's what the Sidereals do with their martial arts (as much as that might, arguably, be a bit ropey for social and mental skills).

                                                    So it's not like "Sidereals are banned from making new Charms" as such, just making new Charms doesn't sit with the form of how their magic works (and could the Maidens have made them another way? who knows, the Maidens are pretty inscrutable that way).

                                                    Out of character, because Sidereals are quite a bit about being awesome because they use their power more efficiently and are about working deftly under the constraints of Fate, to make things work out how they want anyway (or not). It probably helps them feel this way if they have *some* restrictions on pulling out new magic, even though with martial arts and sorcery and astrology, these are obviously far from total.
