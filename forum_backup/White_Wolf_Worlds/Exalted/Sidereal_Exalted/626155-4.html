    Originally posted by Kallipolis View Post

    Welllllll. Even goddesses of fate and destiny aren't always 100% on the mark. Maybe Mars was too busy winning the Games of Divinity to personally pick Siaka for Team Red?
    Sidereals are not chosen. They are destined. Specifically, their exaltations were put onto people with great and appropriate destinies. Iron Siaka is perhaps destined to bring great happiness* to the world, not to bring great battles to the world (bearing in mind of course, that the job of Sidereals is to oversee destiny, so she may not actually do so: destiny doesn't always happen as planned. It's just that she's supposed to). Possibly she'll do so by winning a war and thus bringing peace. Perhaps she'll get kids into martial arts, and they'll enjoy it a lot. Possibly she just really enjoys hitting people, and coincidentally this has nothing to do with her destiny.

    *I recognise this is a simplified version of how the Joybringers work.

    As Isator said, the primary job of Sidereals is to oversee things, not be good at them or necessarily do them themselves. A lot of the time they will be good at them because it helps, but not always. A Chosen of Journeys has a knack for travelling, but her actual job isn't travelling, it's to make sure that people in Creation travel as Fate decrees.* So she might encourage the Guild to do more caravans, or encourage a road-building company, or encourage the Realm to invest more money in shipping. It probably helps if she has, say, Sail, so she understands how ships work, but it doesn't necessarily mean she actually will need to do a lot of Sailing. However, she can use her Sail charms to make deals with Water spirits to ensure the smooth operation of a shipping concern (this why Sidereal charms are not necessarily very direct applications of that ability).
    A Chosen of Battles has a knack for fighting and war, but his job is primarily to make sure wars happen at the right time, between the right people, and the right people win. He might do this by spreading rumours or propaganda. He might do it by sabotaging supply lines or assassinating generals that are too good/bad. He might get some war gods to send dreams to their worshippers encouraging war. He might pay mercenaries to attack a neighbouring country dressed in fake uniforms. Only very occasionally is he likely to actually lead a company in a war between mortals in Creation. He's much more likely to pretend to be a regular soldier, and use his War charms to buff the side that Destiny has decreed should win.

    *This is rather complicated by the fact that the gameline has never quite agreed on what Fate and Destiny actually involve, but there you go.

    The secondary job of Sidereals, of course, is thwarting the enemies of Fate; demons, fae and ghosts. So Iron Siaka's pretty damn good at that, which makes Venus's department look better (most Joybringers have no particular knack for combat, so when it comes to rounding up a posse to put down a second circle demon that escaped from Hell because some idiot had sex with a virgin in her weaving room, or whatever, Iron Siaka can represent her bureau, so the departments of War or Endings don't complain that the Joybringers don't pull their weight and demand its funding is reallocated to them. So Iron Siaka probably makes the other Joybringers happy that way).


    Oh, lastly, I want to address Kallilopolis's point about caste stereotypes. I'd say the ones that adhere least to their archetype is Dragonblood, because they don't have Castes, they have Aspects. Their aspects are just determined by their blood (I'd say it's genetic, but that'll annoy people: still, you know what I mean). There's some effects on their personality, but not massively (there's more cultural pressure).
    Think of the signature characters on the front. Pelleps Delled is an unbending fanatic, not what you expect from a Water aspect. There are two master generals as signature characters: an Air aspect, and a Wood aspect. The arrogant sorcerer who does whatever it takes to get power is an Earth aspect.
    Essentially, you can take any personality or character type and put it in any Aspect. Whereas a master doctor who's dedicated his life to researching medicine is not going to exalt as a Dawn, nor is a tribal chief who loves war and fighting but is as dumb as a pile of bricks going to exalt as a Twilight. After exalting, they're likely to train in those things they suddenly find easier (a bookish nerd who exalts as a Wood Aspect might start horse riding and playing music, because he finds them easier than he did before... or he might not, because he still doesn't like them).

                                Incidentally, some of the topics brought up in this thread complement a few other things I've been observing lately that have caused me to have an increasing sense that typical seduction narratives (or even "distract somebody with pretty face" ones) are kind of... stupid.

                                It starts off with something as low as a Friends episodes in which a couple of guys can't manage to leave a gym or a bank because a pretty woman is rolled out for them, and goes up to more run of the mill spy fiction in which groups of characters form elaborate plans that hinge on how their one female member will absolutely stand out in an environment in which pretty people tend to be standard, with a mark who can then be distracted or drawn away or whatever.

                                The types of that latter one are especially stupid when they take the form of "yeah, just walk into the club/ballroom/pool party and wait to fortuitously catch the eye of the target". It's... pretty lazy.

                                It also crossed with me having seen A View to a Kill a couple of days ago, with the one part in which May Day goes to have sex with Bond. Not even seduce him, really, just go to his bedroom, undress and get under the covers with him. I mean... why? What does that actually accomplish for anybody? I know somebody will say something to the effect of "oh, it's to get him off his guard or establish a kind of power dynamic or something", and y'know, in real life that kind of thing tends to be just done with prostitutes. It's weird for a person in anything like that position to do it themselves. Perhaps May Day just wanted to get a piece of that sexy Roger Moore action...

                                But that's just one part, it's mostly just the whole "draw or distract somebody with sexual flaunting" thing, which the more I think about it the more distractingly improbable it seems to me. The whole thing seems so haphazard and impersonal that it makes me wonder exactly what kind of person could ever fall for it. It's not just about when it's at some kind of party in which there's a whole bunch of attractive people who the one character stands out amongst for some reason, it's also when targets are often wealth and influential people who really don't need to exert much effort to have attractive company without being fleeced, or the possibility of coming on too strongly in a manner that puts people on their guard.

                                I feel as one of the more egregious examples was in that dumb GI Joe sequel.

                                "All right Adrianne Palicki, you're the woman in the team, so you're up for getting this one guy we need. Just slap on some shorts and a sports bra and walk up to him, and let nature take its course."
                                "That sounds good and all, but what do we do if he's ever seen a woman jogging ever?"
                                "Then may God help us all."

                                See, doesn't it sound incredibly stupid when it's just mildly framed in terms of what a person's actual context would be?

                                Or the annoying Friends thing... has anybody ever known somebody to actually act like that? To be rendered incoherent and easily plied just by a generically attractive person (as opposed to somebody specific whom they've formed an attraction to, probably after some extended contact) standing in front of them? I can't really picture it.

                                I'll be interested to see what the social influence system makes of things like this; it would probably have to make a few allowances for genre conventions, but as it stands the implications of needing setup and to interact with existing Intimacies (or work to build new ones) seems to have promise in making something a bit more grounded and real. I'm mostly okay with the idea of Solars having something to abstract in the idea of having some indefinable eye-catching quality, to command the attention of a room (and especially its most prominent members) just be walking in.

                                Doesn't really seem to be part of the Sidereal milieu, so they have to take the long route (if it's even a situation where that could work).

                                Just as a final note to this rambling piece, I recalled that thread from a while back about seduction, and of how towards the end somebody asked how it would work in a manner that wouldn't come across as skeevy.

                                At this stage, I think the answer to that question is "when the people involved are already openly interested in having sex with one another". Seduction in terms of playing up a scenario in which you use cues and visuals to entice a partner, of relaxing and stimulating them, I'd say that's all right.

                                Seduction in terms of kind of luring and manipulating a person with whom you are otherwise uninvolved into sex with you is the one I think is creepy and weird.

                                This having nothing to do with casual sex, which as far as I understand tends to be... casual. Something the people involved don't make a big song and dance over at any stage.

                                        I also have to wonder at that point about spies seducing people for information, given how the most well known real life example of such a method, Margaretha MacLeod, aka Mata Hari, was kind of infamously ineffective, acquiring little information of worth and being exposed in fairly short order.

                                        I suppose that, given some of the wording used prior, one might have been thinking of occasions in which an operative goes for a deep cover, with an unassuming family identity that can be used to hide in plain sight, which is pretty different from being seductive and usually implies an investment of decades (also is usually confined to the periphery of what is being watched; it tends to actually be kind of difficult to spy on somebody you live very closely with).

                                        That there is another rub to the idea of a Sidereal having a child for the sake of getting in on the life of a ruler or whatever; the bizarre assumption that the actual pregnancy is the only matter of concern, rather than everything that needs to happen afterwards.

                                        "Aha, I've managed to convince this monarch to take me on as consort, and have even sealed the deal by shooting out a kid. Glad that year's over with, time to get back to Heaven and report in."
                                        "What, you're just going to disappear? Right after having a monarch's child?"
                                        "Yeah, so?"
                                        "And you think that you can pop back in five or six months and pick up where you left off without anybody having any questions? Do you not know how little privacy consorts tend to have, both for security purposes and the traditional mistrust of rulers? Do you know how difficult it is to have an afternoon to yourself without somebody marking your absence? I mean, I know you're a Sidereal, these guards and walls aren't much of an obstacle to you, but when you're gone what's the explanation going to be? 'Oh, Mommy just wanted to pop out for a postnatal spa treatment for most of a year, but we'll be keeping her chair and tiara waiting nicely for when she gets back'?"
                                        "Hmm, I may not have thought this plan to ingratiate myself with the local powers through, considering everything else I've got to do. I mean, I started a revolutionary group in Great Forks that I should probably check in on."

                                        It's not really an effective application of resources. Not unless there would be some specific quality of the Sidereal's child that they want to insert into any given situation (to once more reiterate what part of the point of the Bene Gesserit was, it wasn't just about ingratiating themselves with the Landstaad and the House of Corrin, it was about being able to control their lines of descent; Lady Jessica was assigned to Duke Atriedes because she was a child of Baron Harknonnen, and the group wanted a merging of those bloodlines which couldn't happen any other way on account of them being sworn enemies. This is a conceit that is largely absent from Exalted, and to the extent that Sidereals would ever need to arrange for any family lines to cross over they would have much better luck with just manipulating other people into doing it).

                                        This all goes back to what I was saying from the beginning, about how even if it's useful to examine the influences of Exalted, it still needs to be assessed according to its own context, rather than being a generic slate to just project whatever on wants onto. That was the problem of the Lunars throughout Second Edition.

                                        Yeah, when you lay it out like that... the concept is rather stupid, and if writers mostly use women for it it's sexist too. The woman's "job" is to get laid ... because she has nothing better to contribute to a mission.
                                        It makes me think of Gangs of New York, in which Amsterdam Vellon's plan to work his way into the inner circle of the man he wants to kill is based on gradually building himself up, of doing some audacious jobs and putting himself out there when they're offering tribute and an extended series of alternating between deference and speaking out with advice, as well as seizing on unexpected opportunities (namely saving his target from another assassin). Like, he's setting himself up as a criminal protege rather than a false romantic interest, but it's still something more developed and interesting than "walk through the club and wait for serendipity to draw the target to you".

I'd say there's even a point about trying to look innocuous and be ignored prior to the sudden reveal, so that his marks feel more clever for figuring it out.

                                        I guess on that note I'll acknowledge the likelihood of confidence tricks that are at least partially built on feigning interest, but any good confidence trickster knows that it requires a lot of set up to lure people into thinking that the scenario is plausible and making them comfortable with giving something up, that it's generally something that you have to keep an eye out for particularly susceptible folks and adjust for opportunities rather than focusing on particular targets, and that you can't keep the con running too long and need to get out of there quickly before the mark starts really examining it or somebody smarter who is close to them catches wind.

                                        It's more complicated than "be attractive, lure to somewhere unseen" or "have sex, get information/keys".

                                                            After reading over all this, it strikes me that Heaven wouldn't really want Sidereals to have connections like children. Sids are expected to be able to allow (or force) the massacre of entire cities, to preside over the death-pangs of empires. if you go with the 2E interpretation, that included literally planning human history on a large scale (hope that gets changed).

                                                            People who make those kinds of decisions don't really benefit from having kids. It's one thing when you are a leader in your own nation. Then making your nation powerful helps protect your kids. In the case of Sids, they may be called upon to bring about the genocide of their personal dynasty. In the real world, we call that a potential conflict of interest.

                                                            This sounds like really good story fodder to me. You can play it one of a few ways.
                                                            1. Heaven has directives specifically forbidding anyone working with the Bureau of Destiny from reproducing. In the event they do, the child is killed/taken away. Lots of interesting "going rogue to save my kid" stories there, as well as "to save my friend's life, I must murder/kidnap their child." Good stuff.

                                                            2. Heaven has no policy, but the Sids "police" themselves. In some cases, dark, horrible things happen to children of Sidereals who choose to have them. If you want an especially dark take, operatives tend to only find this out the hard way.

                                                            Or, if you like your Exalted with less grimdark:
                                                            1. At some point, lots of Sids make babies. It just kinda happens. Off the books, there is a village in Creation for the mortal(ish) children and spouses of the Maidens' Chosen. Fate always ensures this town enjoys great, if boring, fortune. Marauding armies miss it. Expanding empires never add it to their maps. Fair Folk who try to raid it find an increasingly improbable amount of opposition and happenstance turning against them. It's accepted between all factions that this place is simply neutral. But what happens when someone decides to break that truce?

                                                            2. Heavens' gods, not really understanding the nuances of human reproduction, and the utter helplessness of children, just assume the Sidereal can take it along with them. There are no policies, no daycare, no maternity leave. Just...figure it out. Shenanigans ensue.

                                                            3. Sids just never have kids. This departs from canon, but it could be interesting. Somewhere along the way, someone built into the Loom that it never happens. Until it happens to a PC.

                                                            And if you like things light-hearted and kinda funny.
                                                            1. Sidereal children reflect the Caste of their parents, ensuring that whatever the Sid brings to Creation, they will find at home (Even Serenity can be a bad thing when it's your teen that's into it).
