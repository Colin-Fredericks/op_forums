                  Let me see if I can come up with a few.

                  Riding the Dragon: This transforms a person into a Lesser Elemental Dragon, and leaves them either an invalid for the rest of their short life, or permanently changes them into a Lesser Elemental Dragon. Yes, the Sidereal has to pay quite a bit for this to happen. It is still a very powerful effect. I'm not saying its a play balance issue, because the actual result doesn't seem that bad, but in character? That's some pretty serious mojo there.

                  Avoidance Kata: Wow, I just lost that argument? You're really upset. Well, good thing I was never here to have this argument. I'm just walking in the room now. Let me sympathize with you. Or even better, let me lead you into this obvious deathtrap, which I can prove is NOT a deathtrap by gong first and showing you its safe. Oops, it was a deathtrap? Good thing I was never here.

                  Neighbourhood Relocation Scheme: That's some pretty heavy mojo. I mean seriously heavy. I have no problem with the play balance, but that's like bringing a nuclear bomb to a sword fight.

                  Many Missiles Bow Technique: Best healing charm in the game. One arrow heals five boxes of damage? Pretty awesome. Plus ending drought, making people fall in love, sowing fields of grain... That's a lot of stuff for one charm to do! Especially since it is scene-length. Again, I love this charm, but that's some pretty heavy magic. I mean, its bringing an orgy to a swordfight and ... winning?

                  Efficient Secretary Technique: Who does research in libraries? Armies of grad students filing their findings so the Sidereal can have their secretary pull up facts as needed. Inventive use of this charm can do amazing things. Especially when you are trying to act like a secretive, all-knowing fate ninja.

                  Avoiding the Truth Technique: Properly used, this is a convince anyone of anything Charm. It works so much better when you try to support your enemies than it does if you try to help yourself, of course.

                  These are just a few of my favorite charms that I don't have issues with in terms of play balance, or play effect (so long as my PCs tell me they are buying them, so I can plan accordingly), but are just such heavy-handed effects.

                      OK, 1e Sids: Combat-wise, Sidereals were intentionally given the best parry charm in the game, and the only perfect defense that worked against anything, not just attacks. Blinding the Boar was absolutely fantastic...for a few rounds. They had a number of "near-perfect" effects on contested rolls that other Exalted had difficulty pushing through, and many of their prayer strip charms counted among the game's strongest buffs -- Charms like Earth and Sky Bargain or Five Ordeals Odyssey* were ridiculously amazing. Many of these effects were, honest to goodness, too good to give to Solars. Like other splats, 1e sids had no Excellencies, but they had one charm that gave them TN reduction for all attack abilities, and another that gave it to them for all social abilities, and TN reduction was scarier in the pre-I(A)M world. Unlike other splats, they had a lot of coercive social magic, like Force Decision, End Debate, and the entire Socialize Tree, that just worked without a roll.


                      *2e butchered Five Ordeals Odyssey, which was one of the coolest charms in the entire First Edition. Here is how it works in 1e: when confronted with a serious threat to a sea voyage, you pay (I think) 10m, 1wp, 1hl, and the prayer strip floats there for the scene, doing nothing. If the actions you take in the scene make it so that the threat cannot plausibly prevent you from completing your voyage, the charm instantly annihilates what is left of it, and you and your entire party gain a dot of Essence for the remainder of the story, which stacks and can take you up to Ess 7 and your allies up to Ess 5. This does not require you to maintain commitment to the charm.

                          Back in 1st edition the capstone charms to their charm trees were, almost without exception, nuts. And some of the smaller charm trees had Impeding the Flow or Efficient Secretary Technique or Avoidance Kata very low down the tree so you could fill up with a lot of cheap and useful effects with your 12 char. gen. charms. Playing a default starting Sidereal in a mixed game was practically cheating. Layer onto that VBoS being just as crazy as 2nd edition and you can see why Sidereals were perceived of as powerful. Then second edition introduced things like keywords and totally changed the combat system at the same time it gave Solars 3m perfect defenses.

                          There was a weirdness component, but that was just to add a stylistic flourish to things like Easily Accepted Proposition, Five Ordeals Odyssey, or Transcendent Hatchet of Fate. That way when you slap someone down it takes them a few seconds to realize what just happened. Saturday morning cartoons might be a good source of inspiration here.

                          SMA were just straight up powerful. Pre-errata BotBM wishes it was 1st edition SMA when you had charms like Full Moon Ways in the same style as Soul-Fire Shaper Form and could combine that with the capstone Melee charm. I think that may have been possible at character generation, but I may be mistaken, it might have required about 20-50xp first. That's not even getting into CMoS, Unnatural Many Step Stride alone ... but with the form charm active, holy shit.

                          edit: Just forgot to add that Chosen of Serenity got to add [Essence] successes to Performance rolls. There was a charm - maybe two charms into Performance - that allowed you to use Performance when making Dodge rolls (defenses were rolled back then) and you got a reflexive Dodge at your full pool against all attacks. I think there was a Performance TN charm too, but my memory is a bit hazy.

                          edit 2: for those who don't know this: you had to declare how many defense actions you would take in a turn. Dipping Swallow Defense and Shadow Over Water allowed you to activate a full dodge or parry (as in, at full dicepool) for the cost of the charm and thus, were actually very good. Otherwise, when it was your turn you had to say something to the effect of "I take one attack at (you) and two dodges". This would count as 3 actions, so your attack would have a -3 penalty and the dodges would be -4 and -5 dice each. Fivefold Bulwark Stance and Flow Like Blood granted you full dodges at your full pool against all attacks. So, they were basically mandatory charms if you were a Solar because wow.....that's just nuts... if you think the 2nd edition versions are too good just imagine what they were like before. That Sidereal Performance charm combined with the Serenity Aspect power was like a free I(A)M on top of whatever else you could muster because dice pool and DV caps didn't exist (or in the case of DV, the concept itself didn't exist). Except, no one had I(A)M. That's the big thing to remember, there are no generic excellencies and there are NO generic I(A)M charms. Oh, and if someone beat you on initiative then you had the choice of going either full defense for the turn (you get to defend against each attack with a successive -1 penalty applied to each roll) or you got to eat an attack with no defense. Similarly, if you got attacked and didn't have a prepared defense you ate the attack. So, unless you wanted to get stomped by high initiative opponents* then I hope you have FFBS or FLB.

                          DV, for all its complications, was actually an improvement to the game.

                          *the opening charm to VBoS doubled your initiative. Yes. Doubled.

                                      On Sidereals -> 2e, it's been a while, but as I understood it, in 1e, Solars filtered attacks very reliably with stacked persistent defences then used expensive perfects when attacks popped through.

                                      So basically the same deal with using strong passive defences to make yourself statistically difficult to thwack, then pulling out a perfect when they get breached, so the game is making your opponent run out of motes. A mote drain game. As perfect are more expensive and stacked persistants were a stronger filter there was less constant (at least in theory) stunting to maintain resource economy in 1e.

                                      I think Sidereals 1e strategy used a much more expensive persistant (Blade of the Battle Maiden) combined with a cheaper perfect (Impeding the Flow) for essentially a similar effect (but worked out worse than Solars, because duh, they're less powerful). Which is more of a 2e-ish way to have that same problem (although 2e does it because the making PDV value work formally anything like stacked persistants would've been obviously both dumb and annoyed all the fans who already hated the stacked persistants game.) Plus 2e introduced all this "Soak is cheaper but bad touch is bad, so best to have a 2/7 with perfect soak and another defense" stuff, right? Which just added complexity without adding interesting tactics.

                                      I can't recall if 1e was as bad for high damage weapons so all this may have ante-d up less rapidly in that edition.

                                      Essentially both issues have the same problem where there is one tactic and one response, and no real element of reading your opponent and gambling on a strategy of moves, and the game is just draining resources and making sure you have more by various means that don't really interact with your opponents' choices. That's a major problem to be mastered by the new edition's design, so this state of affairs doesn't just re-emerge through negligence in testing or lack of thought in design.

                                          My 2 cents:

                                          Sid charms, even in 2.5, can be VERY powerful. Depending on how ruthlessly they're used they can be dangerous cheesy and broken - but in moderation, they can be ok. However, that depends on both the player and ST being willing to cooperate and basically limit themselves... and not everybody can agree can agree to that.

                                          That said, sid charms are also VERY limited and specific in scope. Like the capstone thrown charm, where you throw a prayer strip at a target, who then gets an insane compulsion to kill the sid, but every time the sid is hurt a magic throwing hatchet appears before the sid that when thrown auto-homes in on the target as a perfect attack - and heals the sid for every level of damage done.

                                          Comparing that to the solar thrown charms, then they have vastly more flexibility in what they can do: They get sneaky attacks, they get flurry attacks, they can conjure essence weapons, they can greatly extend range. It just seems as if a lot of sid charm-trees are missing some basic 'enhancement' charms, instead having gone straight to the cool and unique functionality charms.

                                          I mean, even DBs get a "conjure thrown weapons out of essence" charm - so it just seems to be like a lot of sid trees do not have that much in basic ability enhancements.

                                                          Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                                          Oh dear, it's seem that they went from "Oh my glob, the Sidereal is killing my whole Circle!" to "It's cockroach-Sidereal stomping time ~ <3 " when the Edition changed.
                                                          This is very true. A few reasons:

                                                          In 1e, Sidereals had a huge number of charms that you flat-out couldn't defend against (including most of their mental / social stuff, as well as some of what we now call Shaping.) The also, as mentioned above, had early versions of the paranoia combat suite.

                                                          2e was designed with late-1e Sidereals in mind; hence, it not only had defenses against social and shaping effects, it also had a rule specifically intended to avoid people accidentally making impossible-to-defend-against social effects -- any social effect that listed no WP cost to resist defaulted to 1 WP.

                                                          Then 2e Sidereals was handed off to someone who either didn't understand that 1e Sidereal mechanics didn't work in 2e, or who understood and didn't care. Large amounts of stuff was just copy-pasted over; many of their best charms were deliberately nerfed, but a huge range of other stuff (like all their social effects) became useless by accident because 2e had gone from "social effects with no listed defense are insane game-breaking powers" to "social effects with no listed defense are a sign the author was an idiot, assume they can be resisted trivially."
