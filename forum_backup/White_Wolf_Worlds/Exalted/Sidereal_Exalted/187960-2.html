            Originally posted by notthepenguins View Post
            Um. Chung hated paranoia combat and filter builds. We call it Chungian Combat because he pointed out the problems that led to it, and talked about how to adapt to it, but he didn't actually you know. Like it. It was a response of "Well, do this or die randomly and horribly," not a response of "Clearly this is the way a game system should run." It's a lot like blaming von Neumann for Mutually Assured Destruction - technically accurate, but missing the point.
            Yes, I'm aware. But his efforts had the exact opposite effect -- they made those things prominent in the minds of the many Exalted players who had been largely ignoring or working around them, they spelled out as explicitly as possible the ways to break the game as hard as it could be broken while also arguing that doing those things was a practical and intellectual necessity, and they established a library of memes and archetypes people could use to construct their opinions on the matter and gesture toward in lieu of actual discussion.

            They also subtly promoted Chung's favoured playstyle, which was heavily focused on simulationist optimization and abdicating decision-making to the game rules. Regardless of its individual merits, this happens to be the style that most severely exacerbates the problems Chung was railing against. Chung's perennial response to "I haven't found this to be as serious an issue as you're making it out to be" was "that's because you're playing the game wrong." He was very much willing to make other people's play experience worse in order to get them on board with the problem of his bad play experience, and he was successful in that at least.

            The fact that you're arguing against something and want it to go away does not immunize you to the accusation that your arguments encourage that thing -- on the contrary, that's very common.

            Originally posted by Zironic View Post

            What kind of weird, side-ways, group-attack, BADWRONGFUN post is this?

            Chung and people that share his game style preferences like to believe that there is an active living world outside the Player Characters. In their style of game it's not about the Storyteller and Players weaving a narrative together, it's the Storyteller managing the world sandbox and reacting to the actions of the Players. In that style of game if a player character is hit by Shun the Smiling Lady, then that is not a plot hook to investigate some sidereal conspiracy, that is just any of the 100 Sidereals deciding the punish the PC for something he did. Sidereal charms often ruin this style of play which is why Sidereals are often either ignored completely or hit with a nerfbat.

            In third edition this style of play will be explicitly unsupported however in first and second edition it was supported except for Sidereals.
            This isn't the objection I'm making. See how I'm not attacking the style of play you're defending anywhere in the quoted passage? The scare-quotes on "sensible" are meant to imply that it's not a plausible reading of how people would behave in-setting, not that worrying about what's sensible is BADWRONGFUN. I will say this, though: you're seriously overestimating the extent to which the Sidereal charmset represented a philosophical break from the rest of 1e.

            My point is that people's arguments about how things like Shun and AK damage the internal consistency of the setting generally require an extremely questionable form of simulationism -- as a rule they tend to show player-perspective bias in how they think it's "logical" for people in the setting to act, they elide things that don't interest them personally even if those things are real (especially if they're not mechanical), and they often rely on bad-faith readings of the powers in question in order to bulk up their case. This is because, most of the time, the underlying cause is actually that powers that they can't resist causes them a "bad feeling" that involves their sense of fairness and is difficult to quantify, and they don't want to argue from that angle because they value objectivity and are wary of putting their feelings on the table where others can pick at them.

            There's a real discussion to be had about the Sidereal powerset and I have criticisms of it myself, but the tenor of the discussion generally made it impossible to usefully discuss it.

                                                Are we doing this? *checks the thread topic* Okay, we're doing this. 1e version, obviously, since that's what we're talking about.

                                                So Shun the Smiling Lady has two modes. It seems like we're on the same page about the fact that the committed version, while a powerful nerf for specific types of social builds, is prohibitively expensive to maintain just to fuck with people. So we're dealing with the "activate and immediately release" version, which costs 7m and "instantly severs" any "romantic feelings" that people of lower Essence have toward the target. It's not spelled out explicitly but the implication seems to be that this only affects feelings with sexual undertones, and that you can rebuild them through normal means.

                                                So, okay, I'm Asshole Sidereal who wants to hide out in Heaven mess with people's love lives because fuck those guys. I use the charm before I go to sleep, generally, because I'm going to be getting all my motes back anyway and it's expensive. I'm a representative character rather than a PC, so I'm not optimized -- at Ess 4 (pretty high for 1e), I have something like 14 Personal Essence and 40 Peripheral, less commitments. Assuming these pools are completely full except commitments, I can Shun one or two people with no big effort, and if I want to take full advantage of the full regen I'll get from sleeping, I could nail 6-7 people in exchange for putting myself at some risk and being enveloped with a burning halo of Essence that broadcasts my spending and lingers for some time after I lie down. Still, that's pretty good -- I can do this every day so long as I'm not wrung out or away on fieldwork.

                                                Now, there are a lot of potential targets I could hit with this, because as Asshole Sidereal, I have a lot of enemies or people I just happen to hate. Definitely the list of people I might like to peg is going to be 10x as long as the people I actually hit. If I repeatedly hit the same people, I'm going to want to choose which ones to target on the balance of how much I hate them, how much I expect the charm's effect to get in their way, and how much I can spin it as doing my job if it comes up. If I wanted to make a concentrated effort I would maintain a list and ding people on a schedule, but that's a hell of a lot of trouble to go to. Either way, for this to have a reasonable chance of hitting any given Anathema I'm going to be making something of a production of it, and since it's Simple it requires my full concentration for anywhere from 3 seconds to a minute per use. So this is on par with brushing and flossing as a nighttime ritual, except for the part where it requires spending a crapload of Essence and maybe hitting full anima. So while it's not a hard thing for me to do, it's a nuisance and isn't completely cost- and risk-free.

                                                But it's worth it given how far the effect outstrips the cost, right? Well, let's see. I go through my nighttime ritual of mashing the Fuck You button, and...what? I know in your heart of hearts that a thing happened, but it was a small, subtle thing, and the good-feeling I get would be no different if the charm were a sugar pill. Over time the initial thrill of "I'm fucking people over!" is going to fade, because it exists entirely in my own mind, and eventually I'm going to want some kind of feedback that all this dedicated spite is actually accomplishing something -- which is difficult to get in Heaven. Moreover, even as an Asshole Sidereal I recognize that using this charm can steer people's actions and shape Destiny, since that's what it's for, so I probably want to make sure I don't accidentally fuck up any important Sidereal projects, because that could have actual serious consequences for me.

                                                So, I hear that my buddy Warbert has just returned from fieldwork in Icewalker lands, and I take him out to drink that night, and while we're chatting, I probe, real subtle-like -- "So you met the Bull of the North, right? Did he seem unhappy?" "Well,he usually seems a little surly except when he's been drinking, but he did a lot of drinking after the campaign." "Ah, well -- did he seem like maybe he had blue-balls?"

                                                The romance-severing effect of Shun is extremely subtle, enough that not only is it hard to tell if it's working when you use it yourself, it's hard to even notice anything has happened when it's used on you. It doesn't end relationships, it doesn't cause Capgras Syndrome, it doesn't remove the social context of a previous romance, it doesn't have any effect on one-night-stands or make it so the people you loved from before it was used will withhold consent to sex. It does a thing, but that thing is not very dramatic or noticeable, to the point where I could point to most of the Exalted PCs I've ever seen and retroactively say "yeah they were totally having Shun spammed on them." To get substantial effect out of it you need to use it surgically and with specific, up-to-date information about your target's activities and relationships. The upshot is that this whole Shun-spamming ritual is mostly about generating personal amusement, and the bottom is going to fall out of that rapidly unless I can get some kind of feedback that I'm palpably harming the targets of my spite.If I begin to doubt that I'm genuinely making my targets unhappy through my policy I will feel like a huge chump.

                                                Shun feels easy and consequence-free relative to its benefits from the perspective of the target's player, but from the perspective of a Sidereal using it just to be a dick, the benefits are sufficiently trivial that the consequences and cost actually matter, and trying to get better value out of it increases your attack surface. Occasionally a Sidereal will come along who actually does this, but it's going to be pretty rare, for the same reason that most people don't block off 10 minutes a day to anonymously smear random people they dislike in blog comments.

                                                            This seems like a goalpost shift to me, though. Subject to its constraints, AK is an excellent way to extract yourself from a situation you don't like, but setting aside the fact that its requirements make it somewhat unreliable, the more serious issue is that it requires abdicating all control over what happens in the scene and any responsibility you might have for being there, in exchange for an unknown which is almost surely better but also outside your control or ability to plan for. Which reinforces my sense that the underlying objection is that the powers circumvent "proper", "fair" dispute resolution procedures, and the whole argument about them being game-warpingly broken and encouraging Sids to use them all the time in a cavalier way is just an attempt to justify that instinct in terms of game balance.

                                                            I think there are problems with several of the charms people perennially complain about, including Shun and AK, but as long as people's complaints rely on hyperbolic misreadings and on ignoring any part of the charm's consequences and implications that doesn't contribute to the argument, I'm going to end up over here defending them. Bad criticisms erode the potential for good ones.
