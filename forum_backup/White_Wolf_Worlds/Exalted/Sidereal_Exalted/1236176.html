    What's Your Read on Sidereal Control of Heaven Re: Factions?

    07-17-2018, 06:32 PM
    So,
    MoEP: Sidereals, and CoCD: Yu-Shan seems fairly schizophrenic to me regarding the relative levels of Bronze versus Gold Faction control and influence over Heaven. On the one hand, we hear again and again how influential Chejop Kejak is. He never gets audited, managed to pull off a pogrom of pro-Solar deities by having Ryzala frame them for Yozi-worship, and has his hand so far up the ass of the Wyld Hunt that when he wiggles a finger, an Ostiary in Cherak commits ritual suicide for the glory of the Dragons. On the other hand, we ALSO hear all about how Kejak's nearing expiration-date and the return of the Solars is causing many previously staunch Bronze Faction loyalist-deities to waver, reconsider their positions, or in some cases simply go off the Bronze script (like Hui Dai Ling, Director of the Crimson Panopoly, who wants to kickstart the Realm Civil War to get it over with.) Other previously neutral or Bronze-loyal deities are quietly giving their support to the Gold Faction (like Nara-O, whose conversation in a fluff comic with Kejak seems very indicative of his/it having lost faith in Kejak). We read about Ayesha Uru, expected to become the dominant force in Sidereal politics, once Kejak expires...and about the handful of prominent, pro-Solar deities who have survived every effort of the Bronze Faction to eliminate them, such as Lytek, the Golden Lord, Lo Ping, Uvanavu, and Luraname (the Syndics of Whitewall) who are doubling-down on their Solar commitments. Nysela, Charioteer of the Daystar, has become more active of late re: Solars/Abysalls/Infernals she calls to righteousness, than she has in well, ever.

    A lot of that seems to say the notion of Bronze dominance isn't as well-supported as is implied in places. The draw-down of the Wyld Hunt due to the incipient Realm Civil War gets mentioned frequently, but other than mentioning most of the errant Shikari have been recalled by their Houses, taking their House-troops with them, and the number of Wyld Hunt Chapter-Houses dropping from fourteen to six, just HOW drawn-down the Wyld Hunt has become by Direction, and therefore what level of resources the Bronze has to call on in the Threshold is something I find difficult to quantify. MoEP: Sidereals gives us a general idea of which directions are more concentrated examples of Faction-prominence to some extent, but again, not in the most quantifiable ways.

    What's the general consensus then? Beyond the ubiquitous "Whatever you consider is most appropriate for your particular game" that is always the cardinal rule. Realizing of course much of the reason for the vagueness is precisely to allow that selfsame Storyteller flexibility. Canonically, what are your thoughts?

          My understanding is that Sidereals tend not to get overt control of things. They're Merlin or the Leverage Crew. When they want something to get done, they can't just go out and tell everyone what to do. Instead they have to go out and arrange things so that things happen the way they want, via "luck" and "coincidence" and a Sidereal smirking into their tea 100 yards away.

          This can be a very effective strategy if you have a firm grip on the ear of the boy king, a long period of time to arrange things, or other useful levers. However, if your puppets start ignoring the strings, everything needs to be done right now, and many of your other levers are starting to rot away, the weakness in the Sidereals' strategy becomes obvious. And right now, the DBs in the Realm are ignoring the monks calling for a renewed Wyld Crusade against the Solars, there is an order of magnitude increase in the number of Solars presently at large in Creation, and all of their enemies are pouncing on the opportunity.

          Chejop is something of an exception, since he's the nominal "head sidereal" and has been accumulating direct political power for thousands of years. But even if he called in all the favors he's collected and bought as many as he could, he still can't stem the tide.

          Sidereals spend their time gently redirecting rivers.... which doesn't help much against a flash flood.

                  I think Fenrir and Brilliant rain have made pretty reasonable points. It was a good one about speed.

                  As the masters in shadows, the Bronze faction is owed favours, knows how to blackmail or manipulate gods and elementals. But their only real institutional power is the Bureau of Destiny, which they have about 80% control of.

                  I think it's important to the game that the Gold Faction is not too big. Maybe 5 Elders plus another 5-15 young Sidereals who are motivated by dislike of the Realm, anti-establishmentism, or seeing an opportunity for the future.

                  If the Gold Faction is too powerful, it makes it too easy for Solars, frankly, and detracts from their struggle. Though as the Solars rise, more Sidereals might flock to the Gold Faction.

                  Saying this, I've always seen the Scavenger Lands as kind of "easy-mode" for Solars. You don't have to fight the Realm so much. So I think the idea of the Gold Faction being the local Sidereals, so PCs don't have to face much in the way of Sidereal assassins, is pretty reasonable.

                      Ya - Sids are agents of heaven, not lords of heaven. There is a BIG difference.

                      A sidereal can't go to god X and say "You have to do Y"

                      What a sid can do, is go to god X with a a scroll where a higher ranking god signed off on some orders, and go "You have to do Y, because I have the paperwork that says so"


                      oh sure, sids can have sneaky things going off on the side - loads of sneaky things - but unlike the solars, lunars or even DBs, then sids only officially have in-direct power.

                      And yes, they have a nice charm-set that can do loads of weird and magical things - kick plenty of butt - but I'm talking besides that.


                      Ultimately this should reflect in the power of the sids in Yu-Shan. Blackmail or not, favors to call in audits or not, the power of the various sid factions ultimately depends on how much the other gods want to cooperate... because as noted by other posters already, then some gods in Yu-Shan are powerful enough (or have sufficiently powerful friends) to avoid getting into trouble no matter what is thrown at them.

                      That said, which faction is in power should ultimately - IMO - depend on the ST of a given story. Wizard of Oz has some good points too.


                      One of the things I like to do, is have a sid NPC try to set up circumstances to make sure that a prophesied event happens (or doesn't happen) - and then get really annoyed when stupid mortals screw things up - then subtly recruit the players to try to fix things, off the books of course. That's in my experience a fun way to start a game that only spiral out of control (which of course is all the fun)
