                    Oh man are we discussing the Usurpation and the Contagion as they relate to Sidereals again? Alright let me get this warning for what you're about to read out of the way, given numerous problems that have happened when the game approached it badly:





                    Why? It's the same problem the game has had with a lot of other things; it's taken a decent idea and portrayed it without nuance.

                    The core concept of hubris is pretty central to Sidereals; even if you think that everything they did was 100% totally awesomely correct, the specter of it hangs over everything they do. When you're the secret masters making decisions for everyone in the world based on your Objectively Correct Analysis of the Inevitable Truths You've Foreseen, the doubt that you might be overestimating your own abilities or overstepping your right to make choices for other people hangs over everything you do. The Usurpation is just the most obvious example; simply being a Sidereal involves making decisions for other people, constantly. It's a big part of what a Sidereal is and what it means to manipulate Fate.

                    This is sort of core to their story, so the game was never intended to say "the Great Prophecy was absolutely totally reliable and 100% correct with no chance that it allowed any other options" (obviously.) Nobody gets to know that they made the Objectively Right Decision. But it's stumbled repeatedly on how exactly to portray this uncertainty.

                    Problem 1: If the prophecy isn't reliable enough for the Sidereal's decisions to make sense, then you make them look like doofs. Honestly I think that this is the easiest problem to solve, which is why I'm baffled that the game has repeatedly stumbled over it -- I think they wanted to tie it into some sort of mechanics for how Sidereal prophecy works? But it's not really hard. What I would say is that prophecy (even flawless prophecy) involves interpretation, which is inevitably subjective and flawed and never completely correct. The Sidereals are the absolute best people in the world at interpreting prophecy, and pretty much all of them got together to interpret this big prophecy, so their interpretation is as good as it was likely to get. But it's not perfect. And they knew that, and decided that that uncertainty was acceptable given the seriousness of the problem they faced.

                    Problem 2: If you approach it badly, you end up with an irritatingly oversimplified Trolley Car Problem choice. This is my problem with most of the discussions people have had about it so far and the Bronze Prophecy / Gold Prophecy way that the game eventually went to resolve the problem. All of Creation is tied to the tracks with a trolley heading towards it, and you can pull a lever to redirect it to 300 Solars and their magical empire... no. That's dumb. The choice wasn't made in some sort of rarified vacuum of Freshman Philosophy logic. It involved deeply personal bonds of fear and love and loyalty, temptations of power and sentiment, their responsibility to Creation and its people (and what exactly it meant), and so on. Each individual Sidereal had their own reasons to end up with the answer they did.

                    The Great Contagion (and the idea that the Sidereals didn't see it coming) does serve at least one important purpose in dragging their decision-making out of that Trolley Car Problem space by making it clear that they did not and could not see everything. It sort of pulls the rug out from under you if you tried to approach it in the simplest way possible.. but it doesn't really make it that much more interesting if you stop there. It's ultimately just a cheap twist, used like that, so I think that it sort of overstayed its welcome and became stale as The One Problem With the Usurpation. (I mean, you can lump the other outside-fate stuff the Sidereals didn't foresee into here, it doesn't change anything.) "Maybe you didn't see everything" is one thorny issue raised by the Usurpation, but hardly the only one, and the important question isn't really whether they did or not -- because the game obviously requires that their visions and ability to interpret them have limits -- but the human feelings and loyalties and ambitions and fears that drove them to decide that what they'd foreseen and the reading they'd managed was accurate enough.

                    Or, in other words, it doesn't really matter whether their prophecies are 99% accurate or 98% accurate, or whether the exact reason for the flaws is because of Fate or because they chose to ignore an alternative or whatever -- those are distractions, we already know what they decided. What matters (and the reason why those flaws are necessary) is why they made the decision they did. That's what really characterizes Sidereals. The Usurpation happened a long time ago, so the details are really only important in terms of history; but the kinds of human bonds Sidereals wrestle with when they're forced to decide whether to interfere in a fate that would kill this guy or that guy, depending on their choice? That's what's really matters.

                    Asking whether the Gold Faction or the Bronze Faction are right or not (or whether the details of the Contagion or whatever prove one side was objectively correct) is silly. I mean, we can argue over it, sure, but if you can find something in the book that seems to make it 100% clear what the right decision is, that just means you've found something that shouldn't have been printed. I think that, on the balance, the game is generally meant to portray the Usurpation as a failure on the part of the Sidereals (as part of a tragedy where all the Exalted failed.) The backstory is meant to show all the Exalted in the past ages having somehow fallen short of their full potential so that you, in the present, can look beyond them and do better. And -- to the extent that there's a default right or wrong way for characters to approach the setting, which obviously isn't really a thing, but to the extent that the writers inevitably have assumptions like that -- I'm pretty sure that "get stuck on this old decision, keep repeating old squabbles forever" means you're probably screwing things up somewhere. The game is at least a bit more upbeat than that.

                    (You might consider exactly when and where they failed, though. The First Age shouldn't have gotten to the point where the Great Prophecy was even necessary; and the Sidereals bear some responsibility for that, too. Maybe if they'd seen the direction things were headed in sooner, they could have repaired the world's fate without taking such desperate measures? The Usurpation itself attracts a lot of attention because it's the crux where everything came to a head, but ultimately it was the result of over three-thousand years of accumulated bad choices by the Exalted as a whole. Even if the Usurpation was necessary, fixating on that choice tends to miss the bigger picture.)

                    But that's all very subjective, and my point is you're not going to find an answer to that kind of question by analyzing the details of the game's history, by trying to read too much into the mechanics of Fate or the Contagion or anything like that. It's more useful to think of the kind of overarching backstory Creation's history is meant to give you -- imperfect but hopeful, with generations of Exalted past who were heroic and larger than life and who earnestly fought for their ideals but whose actions ultimately fed into a tragedies that gave us the Age of Sorrows.

                        As I understand it, the Contagion hitting was one of the biggest selling points of the Gold Faction for a long time... the idea that the peerless physicians of the Solar Host "would have found a way".

                        And they're probably right, since the best medics in the universe have always been Solars. Dragon-blooded medicine looks a lot more like battlefield triage, in a lot of ways, though vastly more effective obviously. Lunar Medicine focuses a lot more on curing the LUNAR than it does anyone else, and even if the Lunar could protect a small group of people Lunars don't really seem to do vast-scale effects well, they focus more on the personal. Sidereal Medicine is wonky as hell.

                        The Alchemicals probably could've figured it out, if they were around at the time, though whether they could've done so in time might be debatable. An Infernal solution to the problem might look worse than the Contagion itself. Abyssals would've obviously had less interest in solving the issue but theoretically could've found some way to kill the heck out of the Contagion, maybe. Probably a way that also killed everyone afflicted by it. Which wouldn't necessarily be better.

                        But the Solars, presumably, who're by far the best at vast-scale far-reaching effects that draw upon their peerless skill and unfathomable might to envelop the world in their golden solutions, probably would've been able to do the job. At least in time to stop it from hitting 90% of everybody. Presumably it'd've involved the Wyld somehow, since a Solar researcher who discovered the origin of the Contagion (as some Solar Circle of heroes may well have done, in this case I'm referring to the Well of Udr and not the Dowager herself) could've theorized that infinite entropic possibility might be countered by infinite creative possibility.

                        But that's just a theory.



                        EDIT: I'd like to specify of course that none of this necessarily speaks to the Solars being "automatically better" than everyone else, for those who might be inclined to think so.

                        Just that in this case, they really were the best option for dealing with the problem at hand, and that they were, for obvious reasons, not available.
