    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
    Okay. If I didn't concede that earlier, I'll concede that point now.

    But the poem could be talking about kittens or apples for all I care, because that's not the point I was making there.

    The Drowning Maiden is struggling against her descent, the water, the dark, the cold... The children have nothing to do with any of that, so if her circumstances represent the opponents that the Brawler is struggling against to survive, then the children can't represent some of those opponents. They would more logically represent bystanders.
    Crowds of folk who are not skilled in combat tend to be treated as environments, and environments tend to take collateral damage, sometimes in very extreme fashion, when the Exalted take to the field. Anyone who doesn't clear out quickly enough at the earliest signs of trouble, and sometimes even those who do, are going to suffer for it, possibly at the hands of the one that didn't start or want it but who isn't going to sit back and wait to die. This is the use of unrestrained force in a nutshell; a vast sea that washes over everything and spares very few, if any, from its touch. If this seems callous or horrible... the description of the constellation is pretty much in line with that, since it seems to be all about the cliche of "hard men making hard decisions".

                                                    I think Exalted has acquired an odd standard of how much collateral damage is necessary or to be expected from fights. People often talk about levels of destruction that don't quite track logically.

                                                    Originally posted by HamSandLich View Post
                                                    If you're talking about combat use a combat imagery, not this obscure bullshit you can spin to blame a dude taking a swim for mass murder.
                                                    I feel as though this is a perspective informed by lack of familiarity with English language poetry.

                                                    Mind, I think poetry in many languages also has a tendency for more abstract and metaphorical imagery.

                                                    In any case, the constellation under Battles that Sidereal Brawl powers is not about combat. It's about the role of the proverbial dice determining where the cannon shell lands, and who will be helplessly blasted to smithereens by it. The Sidereal in this case being the shell.

                                                    Originally posted by HamSandLich View Post
                                                    Which isn't very healthy for the gameline.
                                                    Sidereals arguably have a more consistent focus on doing things intended to be for the benefit of people at large than the Scarlet Dynasty does, and we've gotten two books concerned with how to play them with more sophistication than "tear the whole system down and replace it with liberal democracy".

                                                    I suppose that another way to answer the question of Accelerator is that one of the themes of Sidereals is having responsibility for the well being of a universe that, in the grand tradition of pulp fantasy, is unsympathetic to human life (although not strictly brutal to it either), with the ruthless calculation of a Chinese mandarin. It's not for everybody, but the priority is on making it compelling by presenting strongly motivated characters with elaborate methods and world views more than it is on creating good people.
