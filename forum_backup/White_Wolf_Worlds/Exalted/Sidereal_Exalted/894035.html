                      You're very ambitious! I had a plan once to write an entire splat book for Werewolf... it's been on my back-burner for years now.


                      Edit: Uh... I wasn't going to mention Factions, but since you are planning to talk about them... I would love to see a vision of them in which they have coherent, plausible plans for keeping Creation intact, especially against threats outside of Fate, instead of "Uh, just keep doing what we did in the Shogunate, nothing can go wrong!" and "Uh, lie to the Solars and just assume they won't go crazy again, nothing can go wrong!" Concrete plans for monitoring and combatting the three realms outside of Fate (Malfeas, Underworld, Wyld) without needing Lunar or Solar help, put into place and given significant priority and resources at least since the Great Contagion. "Sword of Creation >>> Fair Folk army" is a good start but not enough, IMO. By plans, I mean long-running and demonstrably efficacious activities/institutions to actively counter forces outside of Fate, that have been practiced for centuries alongside the Immaculate actions against the Celestials, that PCs can participate in. Also, the Gold Faction should be trying to study if Solars inherently go crazy and if so, why. And really shouldn't be something founded only after the Jade Prison broke. There's no plausible way that there was no dissent whatsoever against the Bronze Vision for 1500 years, even after the Contagion, even from Ayesha Ura. There was stuff they could do before the prison broke (find/break the Prison, gain enough political power to end support for Immaculates and open the Prison, study/protect the remaining Solars, diplomacy with Lunars...) that PCs in historical games ought to have as options. Having no institutional existence before 5 years ago also sets up Gold Faction PCs to have almost zero political backing, monetary resources, mentors, etc. rather than just having significantly less than the Bronze faction.

                      I would also be interested in a take on their Great Curse that gives the sense of elders becoming insane and carrying out plans in ways that end up with terrible consequences either for Creation or for the mortals whom they hold themselves aloof from... that somehow doesn't give the impression of stupidity or continue the tradition of the Blind Seers sidebar. Can everything inspired by that sidebar or sharing an aesthetic with it please die in a radioactive green fire? I admit I don't have a good suggestion of how to depict characters so that they come across in the way I want Sidereal elders to come across... insane, but not dumb. Plans that attract younger ones not just through institutional inertia and political power, but by having an internal logic as well. And younger Sids who are markedly less insane-and-out-of-touch-with-mortals than their elders.

                      I feel like the Curse of the Noldor in The Silmarillion might show the sort of thing I'm thinking of -- especially the sense of what went wrong leading to the Fifth Battle, that it wasn't because the elven princes fucked up by stupidity or not considering the obvious, but by making fairly small strategic mistakes in the context of being fated to be betrayed by their followers, and fated to see everything go wrong with their plans that could go wrong, all at once. This should be doable for Sidereals without huge setting changes, I think -- the Empress vanishing without warning is a good example of "everything going wrong that could go wrong," coupled with the problems that arise when you make an entire society dependent on one person with a finite lifetime and no heirs, even though there were cogent reasons to set up the Realm that way that made an internal logic, in the context of the Shogunate infighting that weakened the Dragon-Blooded host.


                      I wonder if anyone else would like to see some of the fanmade Celestial and Sidereal MA converted to 3E. Somebody made a full set of 5 styles (including a tweaked Violet Bier of Sorrows) and there's also a set of 5 "House of [astrological house]" styles created by Sidereals instead of the Maidens. Not having a play-group I wouldn't be using them so I don't feel I can request them, but I wonder if anyone else uses them. I thought they're pretty cool, at least on 'paper.'

                                  So, my thinking on the Factions goes along as follows;

                                  The Sidereals have always tended to splinter along 'pragmatic' vs 'idealistic' lines. Well before the Usurpation, these had already earned the monikers of 'Bronze' and 'Gold' factions- although any given Sidereal could and would swing between their stances. As the First Age wore on and the madness of the Solars became more and more evident, the Sidereals cast the Great Prophecy- and the informal factions became hardened. The pragmatic Bronze could not budge on the idea of risking all of Creation; the idealistic Gold could not budge on the idea of ruining Creation to save. Things moved from debate to arguement to a very quiet, very cold civil war. And we all know how things moved from there.

                                  Since then, only a shadow of the hard faction lines that drove the Usurpation still exist- even ten years ago, the terms 'Gold' and 'Bronze' much more spoke to the idealism / pragmaticism divide again. The return of the Solars has changed that. Sidereals who generally favored idealistic solutions could still look at any given Solar and shudder at what they might do. Pragmatic Bronze Sidereals still might think that the Realm is the right idea- but perhaps with a thin layer of Solar 'puppet masters' whom the Sidereals then manipulate, giving them an extra layer of protection.

                                  Additionally, a hard core of 'no compromise' Sidereals, calling themselves the 'Copper Dragons' have begun to form- mostly of young Sidereals born on the Blessed Isle after the rise of the Realm, they are only ones who have a 'Kill all Solars' belief verging on the religious.

                                  (This is also why there's no meaningful discussion of a 'silver' faction, an 'iron' faction, a 'brass' faction- the terms are legacy and refer to ancient yet ongoing political divides, rather than a singular inciting incident over who should rule the world as the Sidereal's puppets)

Some must have remarked on the limit breaks of Lunars and Dragon-Blooded. And a current of awareness of this would probably be present also in the Second Age, leading not just to a sense of smug superiority over all other Exalted, but also to honest attempts to figure out why this was happening -- including to Solars, since some still existed.


                                          I've also been wondering if the Great Curse, as applied to Sidereals, really needs to take the form of insanity at all. They don't have limit breaks the way the other 3 original Exalted hosts do. Depicting them as making bad decisions in groups can so easily lead to accidentally making them dumb or blind.
                                          In The Silmarillion, the Doom of the Noldor didn't lead to Noldorin elves acting in any way that deserved to be called insane or stupid, even when they made unwise or unethical decisions. They all knew about the curse, yet it brought their utter ruin anyway. They couldn't just go around it because there was usually no obvious, Point A to Point B, way in which their own actions directly fulfilled it. It influenced their behavior (some elves more than others) but also manifested as things that were fated to happen to them -- their allies betraying them, their plans going awry in the worst possible way, etc. And these things came apart because of their own actions, but in subtle and indirect ways that they didn't easily recognize.

                                          The Great Curse turned the Solars' great virtues (or great idealism, in 3E) and heroic drive against them, twisting the very qualities for which the Unconquered Sun Chose them into something ruinous. Perhaps it would be appropriate for it to turn the Sidereals' relation to Fate against them by subtly leading to things happening to them, or cascading from the results of their actions, indirectly arising from their own decisions but not manifesting primarily in their plans being bad ones, or their behavior being insane. Part of it would also be the aloofness from human concerns that is a danger for all Exalted, compounded with alienating effects of the Arcane Fate.

To them, the Solars will always be the World's Worst Threat (tm). They grew up (relatively speaking) with idealized versions of the Great Prophecy- they don't internalize the compromises, the philosophical struggle, the urge to reconcile, only the bold speeches of Chejop Kejack and the cowardice of those Goldies that want to compromise with the devil.

                                              2. What, practically speaking, can "gold"/"idealist" PCs do in a game set before the Jade Prison broke? What form did dissent against the Chejop Kejak plan take? What agendas other than "focus on promoting the Realm and Immaculate Order exactly as it is now, as the top priority" were being persued?
                                              First, the goal in shifting the faction focus a bit away from the Great Prophecy is to lessen the 'Gold faction Sidereals always oppose Kejak and the Realm, Bronze faction Sidereals always support Kejak and the Realm'. All Sidereals see the Realm and the Immaculate Order as a damned useful tool.

                                              The difference between the Gold and Bronze Sidereals in the time between the Usurpation and the vanishing of the Empress tends to be... well, more situational. Bronze Faction sidereals tend to prefer the most expedient route to resolving their missions. The sterotype of a Bronze Faction Sidereal is someone who is willing to burn the village to save it; they'll make short term sacrifices for long term gains, is very loss adverse, and prioritizes the goals of the mission over the suffering of Creation.

                                              Gold Faction sidereals tend to prefer resolving their missions in such a way that it makes the lives of those they touch better- even if they are forgotten, even if the life they touch doesn't matter in the long run. The sterotype of a Gold Faction Sidereal is someone who will gamble the fate of the kingdom on the roll of the dice; they have martyr complexes and will sacrifice themselves to save a handful of mortals, and hate seeing those same mortals suffer. They miss the big picture- they're so concerned with the sickness of a child they overlook the plauge ravaging the city. They're also more prone to going off-mission, taking fate into their own hands, and suffering from Paradox as a result.

                                              Spying in Hell (in particular, running covert ops out of Vempura's embassy) and the courts of the Fair Folk. Cultivating contacts with the Liminals.

                                                      I believe I'm not conveying my ideas well then.

                                                      The core thought is 'move away from the Sidereals being defined by the Usurpation, while still keeping the familiar language'. To me, the idea that Sidereals are motivated and defining themselves by something *other* than the Solars is important. This implies that the Gold and Bronze factions predate the Great Prophecy. They need a divide deep enough that people will recognizably self-sort, but shallow enough that you can believe they'd work together instead of engaging in bloody conflict. It needs to be political, but rooted in philosophy.

                                                      The pragmatic / utilitarian vs idealist divide seemed, to me, like a good candidate for a fit for that.

                                                      The Bronze faction is the faction willing to compromise, to face reality and take things as they are, not as they wish they were- and that often means making hard, ugly choices; it means spying on your friends because one of them is spying on you, and removing the diseased limb to save the patient. Because they play the realpolitik game well, Bronze Sidereals can often accumulate more political power and capital- but at the cost of growing tarnished and crossing lines they swore they would never cross.

                                                      The Gold faction is the faction that is willing to place their faith in things working out. They take great risks which frequently pay off, but when they fail, they fail *spectacularly*. They aren't stupid, but they are trusting in the better angels of everyone's nature, and that no one is so far lost to the darkness that they cannot find the light. They will tend the ailing patient, and many times save both life and limb- but sometimes, the patient dies despite their best efforts. Because Gold Sidereals are more likely to reach a line and say 'Thus far and no farther', they often hit stumbling blocks in their careers, making enemies when a more flexible person could have made an ally- but the Gold Sidereal would rather have a monster as an enemy than an ally anyways, even if that monster does happen to get to decide your operations budget for the next decade.

                                                      The two groups don't come into violent conflict often, because both are ultimately part of the 'Manipulative Secret Masters of the World' club, and both have far more in common with one another than they do differences. Both factions will use any tool they believe they can control- the Realm, the Shogunate, the returning Solars -to achieve their goals, and both believe that their goals will lead to the best Creation possible. Few Sidereals until recently- and them, almost all survivors of the Usurpation -would define themselves as members of their faction... but everyone would know that you could count on certain people to act in a certain way most of the time.

                                                      I mean... I suppose I don't grok what you're looking for, if you don't see how you can have political conflict out of that. From your 'even a single voice of dissent against the Immaculate Faith / Wyld Hunt', I get the feeling you want a more agressively confrontational form of faction politics than I'm envisioning. Which, if so, cool! Not my direction, but that's fine.

                                                      But you would absolutely have Gold faction Sidereals who argue for letting this Solar live a little longer because they're fighting a plague and no one else can do it during the Shogunate. You would also absolutely have Gold faction Sidereals who use the Immaculate Order to push back against the worst impulses of the Dynastic aristocracy in defense of the common folk. And you would have Gold faction Sidereals leading Wyld Hunts against Solars and Lunars that have gone mad and spread death and war like waves on a beach.
                                                      You would also absolutely have Bronze faction Sidereals who would love to find a peace with the Lunars, but don't think it's achievable any more and every time someone tries, they *fucking die*. You would have Bronze faction sidereals who disagree about the value of the Empress as an ally, who might have arranged her 'disappearance' to put their own puppet on the throne, or who think that the problem with the *old* Vision of Gold was that the Solars were too old and powerful- but now, with these new, younger, inexperienced Solars...

                                                      At the end of the day, the Sidereals (in theory) value the Fellowship above any single faction dispute. The violence that preceeded the Usurpation was an abberation, because the stakes were so high that the pragmatics felt they had no choice. That doesn't mean they don't politic against each other- but it also doesn't mean that they won't use the best tools they can to save the world from itself.
