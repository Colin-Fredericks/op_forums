        When reading something, it's worth keeping in mind who it's written for and why it emphasizes what it does. Some of the things you're reading are aimed at different audiences or are intended to serve different purposes, which is why their emphasis differs and why they seem so different in tone. Roughly, there's three viewpoints here that the text takes at different points:

        1. Broad setting coverage intended to set the backstory for the game and lay out its general tone. These tend to put an emphasis on how the Bronze faction has ruled the world, especially up until five years ago. When reading these it's also important to remember that the return of the Solars changed some things. Some of the stuff you're talking about - like Kejak purging Solar loyalists - happened a long time ago. He absolutely couldn't pull that today. The return of the Solar Exalted made supporting them an actually viable position rather than (to most of the gods) a point of meaningless doctrine, so most gods are going to be more cautious about who they back until the dust settles. The fact that he's nearing the end of his lifespan has also somewhat weakened his influence. It's worth pointing out that this emphasis that the Bronze Faction's grip is weakening isn't necessarily because the authors are "biased" against Solars - it gives players breathing room to tell their own stories. And not just Solar players or Gold Faction Sidereals - unrest in Heaven and threats to Bronze Faction supremacy are obvious plot hooks for Bronze Faction PCs in the same way that eg. the Bull of the North's main purpose is a plot-hook-slash-boss-encounter for dynastic Dragon-Blooded.

        2. Things written for Sidereal players. These tend to emphasize the cool parts of being a Sidereal (ideally), but also the dangers and threats that the Five-Fold Fellowship faces and enemies suitable for you to go up against.

        3. Things written for Anathema. These emphasize potential plot-hooks in Yu-Shan, as well as potential enemies within the Bronze Faction. So, naturally, you get two things - lots of Solar friendly gods for your ST to use as NPC supporters, and lots of emphasis on Kejak Chejop and a few other Bronze Faction Sidereals or affiliated gods as bosses.

        The last one is important because it's what gave CoCD: Yu-Shan the feel that there was a Solar-friendly deity hiding under every rock. That's not intended (and it's not how the overall setting is described), but the book is written from the assumption that running an Anathema game in Yu-Shan is going to be intrinsically difficult without a friendly deity to provide you a foothold, so several are provided.

        I do think that the a lot of the 2e write-up of Yu-Shan lacked nuance. It isn't a matter of it being biased towards one thing or the other, it's a matter of everyone and everything being written too heavily towards one role. I think that the book both over-emphasized Sidereal influence in Yu-Shan and over-emphasized how many gods still care about the Solar Exalted one way or the other, even now that they're back. The vast majority of Yu-Shan is corrupt and decadent. The politics there need to be deeper than just "Kejak uses Ryzala as a puppet", too. (And, generally, there needs to be more focus on what the gods care about other than Solars, what political factions they break into and so on. It's bad enough to have Sidereal politics completely centered on the Solars, but at least they have the Great Prophecy. Most gods are going to have other concerns.)

                I have to disagree with your theory Aquillion, concerning the pro-Solar, neutral, or previously staunch Bronze-loyalist and now wavering deities being simple hooks/resources for a game foothold.
                1) There's a rhyme and reason to the pattern of weakening Bronze influence. Example: At one point, Ryzala was willing to engage in framing pro-Solar deities for Yozi worship, and not flinching when those deities got smelted into Starmetal on those bogus charges. Cue the Solar Return, and five years later she won't even allow a Solar in Yu-Shan to be attacked by the Bronze. That's a paraphrasing of a direct quote. Nara-O, Kejak's direct (theoretical) superior, is shown in several phases of his weakening faith in Kejak's vision for Creation. 2E CoCD: Yu-Shan made a deliberate choice to retain/re-iterate his support of the Solars, in exchange for Lupo bringing a Solar to visit him once a year. It wasn't a copy-paste mention, but something written by someone who read the CotI info, and chose to work it into the CoCD: Yu-Shan treatment of Nara-O.

                2) A DISTINCT portion of the pattern present is that those pro-Solar deities who HAVE managed to survive all this time in a Bronze-dominated Heaven are showing distinct signs of their stars beginning to rise again. Taru-Han is growing more and more obsessed with her illegal soul collection, and her relative indifference to anything beyond simply retaining her position is making "some Gods wonder if standing by while Lytek, the Right Hand of Power was supplanted was truly the right decision." Add this to the text talking about the Gods/Goddesses attempting to badger the Golden Lord out of retirement in An-Teng, despite the Golden Lord's protests he's happy where he is.

                3) In counterpart to #2 above, you've got all the stuff about various Bronze deities making uber-illegal moves. Ruvia's lieutenant wants to assassinate him, but Ruvia has learned about it. Laughing Ragamuffin, Uvanavu's subordinate is manipulating fate to hide the Heroin-producing Beasts of Resplendent Liquid belonging to the Guild, and is getting blackmailed over it. Hui Dai Ling wants to kick off the Realm Civil War, etc etc etc.

                4) Talking about where all Sidereals have been pulled from, who controls what Direction, which Factions control what Committees...it seems like some developer started trying very hard to quantify how much control each Faction had, but then someone else continued adhering to the "The Bronze controls 90%, the Gold 10%" while ignoring stuff like Kejak using both the East as a "dumping ground for Gold-Faction malcontents" and the Sub-Convention on Essence Wielders as a drain on Ayesha Uru's time and resources, and how much Kejak now regrets those decisions in 20/20 hindsight due to the Solar Return. Stating Chejop Kejak not only can't get a Bronze seated as Chair of the Convention of Air, but is burning all kinds of favors simply to keep the Chair open was CERTAINLY intended to be a statement concerning weakening Bronze influence. Especially when taken in tandem with the repeated mention that more Sidereals are opting-out of Factional politics in favor of Nazri's Independents.

                As for a lack of nuance in CoCD: Yu-Shan, I thought the detailed write-ups of how the various Bureaus prey on one another, which Bureaus are allied, which enemies, and which Bureaus have how much clout relative to other Bureaus were excellent. I do DEARLY wish that the Secret Societies written up had been linked more meaningfully to the Bureaus those Gods must be part of. Using their jobs, and the favors their positions can grant, while wielding those favors received to advance their agendas is what I'd expect those Gods to care about, and a big opportunity was missed by the Devs not doing that, at least IMHO.

                Regarding Chejop uses Ryzala as a puppet: I thought there was a tight narrative about this. Kejak used his Bronze Faction to help a then-very minor deity Ryzala a) Get assigned to the Yozi-worship-investigating position, and then b) Helped Ryzala use that position to conduct a pogrom against pro-Solar deities, so the executed gods could be replaced with Bronze-loyal deities. That devil's bargain quid pro quo welded them together into the partnership which allowed the Bronze Faction to dominate. For me at least, it captured that "Characters you love to hate" sort of magic, which pushed my dislike of Kejak as a Hard Man Making Hard Choices into an outright fanatic more interested in being right than doing right.

                I prefaced this with saying I found the Sidereal/Yu-Shan material to be schizophrenic in result, but I don't believe that's all attributable to "They meant the Bronze Faction really does dominate everything, and all this Gold/Pro-Solar stuff was accidentally blown out of proportion. There's simply too many examples of it for it to be that, again IMHO.

                After all, the return of the Solars was supposed to have given the Gold Faction new life. As for their only being 3-5 Elder Sidereals in the whole Faction, there are literally that many Elder Sidereals simply running the two written-up Cult training camps, and the 1E Cult book says it left the third training-camp, the one in the East unwritten so Storytellers could create it themselves. There's no reason to believe that camp doesn't have its own Elder Sidereal running it. More than that, I believe it strains all credence to believe there are NO Elder Gold-Faction Sidereals beyond those specifically written up. Especially since the logical inference of the statement that the Gold Faction is gaining ground because of the Solar Return would be that some of the previously middle-of-the-road Independents have drifted into the Gold. It's a lot easier to drift into the left aisle from the middle, than it is to "turn traitor" and cross to the left from the right.

                I'll freely admit I don't know where the % of Bronze-Vs-Gold control of Yu-Shan should lay, but I believe there's too much material piled up to say "Nope, the Devs meant the Bronze control everything, and all this Gold-increasing/Bronze-weakening material just fell out of a gremlin's ass and landed on the printing press, to accidentally get published." Why repeat the number of Wyld Hunt Chapterhouses are down from 14 to 6 in CoCDs: North, South and East, otherwise?

                One of the things I MOST hope to get from 3E is a much more quantifiable answer to these issues. Leaving things open enough for Storytellers to put their own stamp on things is one thing. Not being able to tell what the canon intention actually is, well, that's something else altogether.

                Edit: I do think the story-foothold/plot-hook angle has something to it though. Mentioning things like the reason Yaogin is always dosing himself from the Golden Ewer is because he's in agony ever since an Elder Lunar poisoned him. Or any of the other tidbits about the various seedy things Gods are doing that they could be busted for if discovered...It's this stuff I think they intended to be the plothook fodder that answers the question "What do the Gods care about, other than Solars and Sidereal Politics?"

                                                            Apologies,
                                                            I realize I wasn't at ALL clear for my reasons why my ancient history-centric view of the Sidereal divide is relevant to modern Sidereals. If the Great Contagion and Balorian Crusade are retained, then it follows even the most foolish, oblivious Sidereal would be able to recognize the removal of the Solars and the exiling of the Lunars essentially opened the door for both events. By going with the idea that the Sidereals were united behind the Usurpation, no one in Heaven is essentially responsible for those catastrophes, because they all are, meaning either a) The write-up will be none of the Sidereals accept responsibility for the Contagion/Crusade being their fault for eliminating the Solars, which kills story hooks, or b) The write-up becomes those who perceive their responsibility are only diverging from the Bronze out of guilt and horror for what they helped usher in.

                                                            Essentially, achieved consensus among Sidereals results in a) Deletes the entire "The Sidereals own Great Curse lead them to usher in the very horrors they sought to stave off by eliminating the Solars" tragedy. Which kills in one blow the tragedy of the Sidereals having been just as corrupted by the Solars, but ironically the Viziers were too blinded by their own corruption to see it, b) It eliminates the moral implications, which lead to brother-against-brother, lover against lover conflict between not Villain and Hero, but two groups who both perceive themselves as heroes trying to save Creation, and willing to sacrifice even their bonds within a brotherhood/sisterhood so strong the other Exalts gave them their famous name. If it all just diverges over 20/20 hindsight, there are no Sidereals who are in the slightest bit heroic. Just a group who lost their stomach for the choice they made.

                                                            It moves the Sidereals down to be on par as a whole with the Abyssals and Infernals, instead of being a complex, nuanced splat. I was just reading the Dev Q&A, only up to part 2, but I've read 4 times where Holden repeats how EX3 is supposed to have this optimistic vibe in a world with very real, very dark problems. The corruption of the Celestial Bureaucracy and the indifference of the Incarnae, coupled with ALL the Sidereals having no moral leg to stand on doesn't have the slightest optimistic feel to it, IMHO. The first two do an admirable job of the real, very dark problems, without dragging the entire splat down into negativity.

                                                            More importantly, the Gold decision to keep the new Solars in the dark regarding the Sidereal role in the Usurpation takes on ENTIRELY different connotations in a United Sidereal Usurpation. Right now, it's at least morally ambiguous. The Gold having been willing in the past to try and redeem the Solars without resorting to the Usurpation gives them the credibility to say that keeping the Solars in the dark IS simply trying not to complicate the present with a past no one can change. Yet in a United Sidereal Usurpation, the Gold Sidereals keeping the returned Solars in the dark is simply conspirators still engaged in a conspiracy to continue perpetrating an Age-long deceit. It darkens the Gold agenda into something just as ugly as the Bronze agenda, and THAT I find exceptionally off-putting and repellent.
