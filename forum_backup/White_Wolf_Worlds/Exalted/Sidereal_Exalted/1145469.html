          Originally posted by Volivat View Post
          If a solar lay extensive plans to ambush and destroy several legions of prasad + annihilating the wyld hunt hq, would this come to the attention of sidereals monitoring fate for prasad? (Ignore the difficulty of the task)

          There is a lot to unpack here, Volivat, depending on if you're asking the question from the point of view of a Sidereal-centric campaign, the point of view a Solar-centric campaign, or a "no PCs, completely in-setting use of powers" neutral point of view.

          It also depends on how precisely you asked the question; that "would" means something radically different than "can" does.

          I'll try and answer as much as I can, with the proviso that while the Sidereals are my boys, there are those who know more than me and I'm capable of being wrong or mis-informed. Also, this is a very, very weird question. Prasad is far, far away from the Wyld Hunt's HQ. I'm having a hard time envisioning a scenario where a Solar wants to eliminate several Prasadi legions and at the same time annihilate the Pinnacle of the Eye of the Hunt, which is like four thousand miles away. That's a very odd pair of things to have going on simultaneously. But like I said, I'll do my best.

          First we'll go with "can."

          The Sidereals absolutely can discover this is going to happen. The Lore tree contains a whole mess of Charms for discovering shit like this in both 1e and 2e, and the Sidereals also have access to the Loom, which if they're experienced enough at viewing and interpreting can totally tell them "a Solar is currently planning on how to fuck Prasad and/or the Wyld Hunt up really, really bad" and even give you a decent idea as to how. This is well within the Sidereal wheelhouse.

          But "can they" is easy when it comes to finding out secrets and reading the future when it comes to the Siddies. That's never the interesting question. The interesting question is always "will they."

          And that's a whole different kettle of fish.

          If you're running a Sidereal-centric campaign, especially one involving Sidereals who take the extracurricular activities of their faction banner seriously, and the Sidereals in question have a specific interest in Prasad such that the PCs dedicate time, effort, and resources to keeping an eye on it, then the answer is yes, they almost certainly will find out. They'll make the time to hit Prasad with "Of Things Desired and Feared" one or twice a year or so and they'll consult the Loom with regard to it regularly, as well as investing mundane, non-Fate-y resources into monitoring the place. They'll probably know if a Solar is about to roll over them, and depending on their specific politics towards the Solar Exalted and the Wyld Hunt, they might help, hinder, or ignore it.

          If you're running a Solar-centric campaign, then the answer is a much weaker "maybe." If it's Prasad, then the Sidereals likely don't notice it unless the GM specifically has a Sidereal-centric plot hashed out, and even if they do their vision of events is probably rushed, distorted, and incomplete. But more likely they don't notice at all until it is way, way too late. So with Prasad, it's "they probably don't notice, but maybe they do." And even if they do notice, Prasad is in the southeast, which means it either falls under the Convention of Wood, which is a Gold Faction stronghold and thus likely to be sympathetic to the Solars and their goals, or Convention of Fire, which is run by a hardassed advocate of neutrality who has a reputation for not putting up with faction infighting in his bailiwick. So maybe they notice, and do jack shit about it.

          The Wyld Hunt is trickier. That's important to the Bronze Faction, very important, which means they probably have people keeping an eye on the place. The answer there is the flip-side of the Prasad answer: "they probably notice, but maybe they don't." And the Pinnacle is up north, traditionally a Bronze stronghold, and near the Realm. So the Bronze have a freer hand to act.

          It's going to depend a lot on the players, and on how involved you want the Sidereals to be in the game.

          Which brings us around to "what about in a non-game context?"

          And the answer there is an even more ambiguous "maybe."

          The Sidereals are overworked and under-resourced, and on top of that they have their Faction cold war (that occasionally turns hot) eating up a lot, I mean an absolute lot, of their time. There might not be anyone specifically detailed to monitoring Fate specifically for Prasad. The people monitoring the Loom might not notice anything significant happening down there. Even if they do, they might not be inclined to do anything about it. Even if they are, the other faction might stop them.

          Conversely, its also entirely possible that the Sidereals find out about this eventuality early and in massively complete detail, even knowing all of the Solars plans before the Solar themselves lays them. The Sidereals get lucky sometimes and major future events simply land in their lap in the form of super-accurate prophecy and insight. That's on the outside of the possibility curve but it could happen. Equally possible in the other direction is "the Sidereals have no clue, none."

          With the Wyld Hunt, that's different. The Bronze Faction, I would say, would have a very high probability of finding out about a major threat to one of their major tools and instruments of policy. They're gonna notice a threat like that unless the Solar in question has taken steps to hide it from them. But the question here is "can they do anything about it?" Maybe the Gold Faction trips them up constantly. Maybe the Solars are just so puissant and have laid such good plans that even if the Bronze Faction takes action, the Solars win anyway. And hey, maybe the Bronze Faction doesn't find out either. That's unlikely, but possible. The Wyld Hunt is important to them but a lot of things are important to them.
          How early and how likely are they to discover this?
          This is too uncertain to have a definitive answer. Depending on the vagaries of the situation, it could be anywhere from "decades in advance" to "literally the day before." Most likely, if they're actively monitoring, they find out about the same time the Solar seriously commits to their own plans.

          How soon will they learn of it if they fail to discover the plans but the plan starts to be executed with success? Will alarms go off for a sidereal following the prasad empire when the wyld hq is destroyed and/or a legion is wiped out?
          I note again that these are two very, very weird things for a Sidereal to be monitoring at the same time, given how they have basically nothing to do with each other. A Sidereal monitoring the Prasadi might not give any kind of a fuck at all about the Wyld Hunt.

          But to answer this question... they're likely to find out, but not by arcane means, by mundane ones. If Solars destroy the Wyld Hunt's HQ every single interested party is going to know very, very quickly, because that's a huge blow to the Bronze Faction and they'll know and react quickly, and from there word will spread.

          With Prasad, it might take longer. If someone is actively monitoring Prasad and they're simply bad enough at reading the future in the Loom they missed this, they'll for damn sure pick it right up when Prasad's fate suddenly takes a hard Solar-inflected swing. If they're not actively monitoring and Prasad is simply one more checkbox on a lengthy to-do list, they might have to get the news at the same time mortals do.

          "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."

                      Honestly, Sidereal mechanics have been such a mess over time that it's probably best to wait for the 3e book.

                      The general "fate monitoring" you're talking about has always been really poorly defined; some things treated it as this nearly omniscient power that would let Sidereals instantly find and annihilate anyone they pleased unless they were Outside of Fate, while others treated it as much more limited. Compounding this, 2e tried to resolve it by saying that the actions of high-essence characters couldn't be predicted, which caused all sorts of problems if you think about it too much.

                      My reading of the original intent is that there are really three ways Sidereals interact fate and the future.

                      1. Sidereal charms (and Astrological effects.) These do exactly what they say, no more and no less. They're spelled out in the most detail and are generally far more limited than Sidereals are portrayed in the lore.

                      2. Reading the loom directly. This is the really vague one. Generally speaking the books agree that it's a huge pain in the ass and is rarely done. In theory, if you perfectly read the entire Loom, you would have a flawless prediction of the future (aside from the actions of a few things, like Yozis, that are not governed by the Loom, and the actions of a few people, like Sidereals, who are capable of limited rewrites.) However, in practice nobody can read the Loom flawlessly outside of a tiny number of Sidereal charms, all with severe restrictions or costs. It's possible to predict your ambush this way, but extremely unlikely unless a Sidereal already knew to search for it in particular.

                      3. Relying on knowledge of plans set up by the gods in the Bureau of Destiny. This is rarely actually elaborated on, but it's actually a very important power, and probably the thing Sidereals lean on most frequently in day-to-day operations. Essentially, the Bureau of Destiny has specific plans for stuff (wars, when people live and die, etc.) These things are not, themselves, Fate in the sense that the first two things are - rather, they represent the plans and preferences and desires of the various gods (and high-ranking Sidereals) who have the ability to influence and interact with Fate either through the Loom or by directly influencing Creation. A Sidereal can generally predict what the weather is, for instance, by asking the gods in charge of it what the plan is. If a Sorcerer in Creation then casts a spell to make it rain when there was supposed to be a drought, those weather gods are going to be pissed and the information the Sidereal got from them will be wrong, but that sorcerer didn't, strictly speaking, defy Fate - what they did was ruin the plans of some of the gods in charge of Fate.

                      So to answer your original question, whether or not a Sidereal could predict that ambush would depend on three factors:

                      1. Is there a Sidereal using a specific ambush-detection charm or astrological effect to look for it? If so, it works as described. (We don't have 3e Sidereal charms, though, and the 2e ones had issues.)

                      2. Is there a Sidereal specifically searching for the ambush on the Loom? If so, they might be able to detect it, but often won't. The difficulty is poorly-defined, but generally looking things up on the Loom directly is a pain in the ass and requires that you devote lots of time to it.

                      3. Are there gods in the Bureau of Destiny (or related bureaus) for whom this ambush is part of their plan? Often the gods won't know about it at all (they don't normally track individual Exalted in depth that way), but it's possible that it's actually planned somehow. If it is, a Sidereal could know about it long in advance with little effort (although it'd require politicking and asking the right people.) But if they learned about it like this, they have a secondary problem - if it's part of some god's plan, thwarting it is likely to make them enemies in Heaven.

                      Anyway, that's my thoughts on how it works.

                              Going by 2e metaphysics (only because I don't know the interaction of Essence usage and Fate in 3e), I'm going to say the Sidereals have very little way to find out ahead of time because the Loom of Fate itself has very little way to notice. Bear in mind that the Loom of Fate is quite far from being an all-encompassing system - there's rumored to be this Samsara thing that only the Maidens can perceive that's more infallible-like, but the Loom is riddled with imperfections. One of which is that it only accounts for the patterns of existence as they are, and Charm/Essence usage by definition defies those patterns. Fate might have a way to tell that the Solar will develop a particular plan in advance, if that particular Solar is being tracked, but Fate will only make predictions based on the mundane capacity of the involved parties for actual events, and if a Solar is coming up with a plan to destroy a large number of enemies, my money's not on them doing it without Charms. And if the Wild Hunt HQ/Prasad wouldn't be threatened by whatever the Solar is doing without those Charms, or if the Solar's plan wouldn't bear any fruit to begin with if not supplemented by Essence, Fate's not going to register that the Solar's targets are en route to destruction until it's already happening.

                                              Kinda. But all three of them serve an important role for Sidereals:

                                              The first one is obviously a core part of what Sidereals are and can't be removed.

                                              The ability to read the Loom could be welded into some Sidereal charms, maybe, but there's always going to be epic acts of fate that aren't covered by Sidereal charms in any easily-repeatable way. What I would suggest is something like... access to the Loom of Fate counts as means for Sorcerous Workings based solely around reading fate or the future, and allows you to do so without spending XP, plus maybe with some sort of roll to try and accelerate it. Multiple Sidereals working together can attempt Ambition 3 Solar-tier workings of reading the future when using the Loom, or even things beyond that in large enough numbers. That maybe undersells the loom a bit, but it gives an easy and straightforward way to govern it if you want to do the full reading thing. You could then also have some Sidereal charms give benefits to this basic mechanic.

                                              The last one isn't really something that can be merged with charms (or which needs specific mechanics), and follows somewhat logically from what the Bureau of Destiny is and how it works. "Talk to a weather god to find out what the weather will be tomorrow" is always going to be an option. It's also important to have it there for games where players want to fight over fate - it lets you finesse the question of whether and to what extent players can defy fate by shifting the focus from an abstract (which can be hard to tell stories about) to personified gods in the Bureau of Destiny and elsewhere, who your players can argue with and struggle against and so on without necessarily undermining the mechanism of the Loom or Sidereal charms by making them too easy to thwart. Think something like the gods in the Iliad.

                                              I think the books would be better off touching on all three, with a note that the first one is the one players are most likely to be spending their time doing (and with most of the text devoted to it as a result.)

                                                      Sort-of, but in the past, just sort of vaguely saying "there's other epic feats you can do with the Loom, too, but we're not gonna talk about them" has caused problems for players, groups, and writers who wrestled with how much Sidereals ought to know and how difficult it'd be for them to know it. A lot of the problems we have with Outside Fate and players freaking out over Sidereals are because of this and the somewhat inconsistent view it gives of Sidereal capabilities.

                                                      I think that tying it into the Sorcerous Working system, while a cheap hack, is the easiest way to deal with the issue. It takes one paragraph and still leaves some things open-ended (especially with the last bit about exceeding even the highest-tier workings if you have enough Sidereals), but it neatly buries a problem that has caused endless issues in the past.

                                                      Yes, I know it's tempting to say "sometimes characters do things that exceed the rules; deal with it", but this in particular has been such a pain that I feel the game is better off using a paragraph to handle it.

                                                      (Astute readers will notice that using Sorcerous Workings to divine the future is almost entirely undefined anyway - this is a bit like 2e's "objects are things smaller than a Yeddim" and then not saying what a Yeddim's size is. People will still fill in the blanks as appropriate to their game. But it avoids the dangerous extremes of "Sidereals can glance at the loom and automatically know everything" or "everything that matters is in an Anti-Fate Field that thwarts absolutely everything, even your charms" or "reading the Loom is a thing on paper but you can't really do it without basically just making puppy-dog eyes at your ST", all of which have plagued the game up until now.)
