    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
    It's particularly galling to me that they didn't even plan for the threats that had already harmed the Old Realm: faerie invasion (which had happened before) and a free Primordial attacking (there are still 1 or 2 hostile Primordials out there). The Shogunate wasn't equipped with any way to counter known fae and Primordial threats -- even the Sword of Creation sat unopened for centuries. Likewise, having faced the Great Contagion and now knowing that's also a threat, they have devised... zero ways to prevent another one. That's not smart planning.
    First off, humanity is not full of "smart planning." Look at Venice. No, really, look at it.

    Second, these things you describe, they're apocalyptic in scope even with the Solars there. One of the things I love about Ex3 is the return to uncertainty - having a Solar on your side is no longer a guarantee that you're going to win. If another Primordial came crawling out of the woodwork, yeah, maybe this hypothetical United Exalted Host as a whole could ride him down and murder him before he rendered Creation so uninhabitable that it has to be abandoned. Likewise the Solars may have been able to contain the Great Contagion before it took billions of lives, but I feel like a setting where there was no chance of them not doing it would be a pretty boring one.

    You might as well call us shitty planners for not being able to prevent another mass out of space striking the Earth out of nowhere hard enough to gouge out the Moon - sure, it's happened before, but that in no way implies that anything we have is big enough to throw at the problem. Now pretend that we've had to destroy, decommission, or outlaw our most powerful weapons to prevent the psychos in charge of the world from turning them on each other and destroying the Earth as collateral damage (you don't even have to pretend on that one, because we're already in the process of doing so)! Losing the ability to maybe break up a potential Moon-gouging mass by bombarding it with space lasers is an acceptable sacrifice for keeping space lasers out of the hands of people who would definitely turn them on our own planet.

    For that matter, how about the Sun? No, I'm not talking about the god, for a change, but rather, that bright and hateful thing that stabs me in the eyes every time I leave work! We had a geomagnetic storm back in 1859 that was paired up with a nasty solar flare; this caused wires to be overloaded with current, knocking out communications and even setting some equipment on fire.

    So what have we done since then? Covered the Earth in wires, of course! Millions of them! Billions of them! If the 1859 storm hit us today, we would be fucked, with the damage to the U.S. alone being measured as, potentially, trillions of dollars.

    But I guess we're just "poor planners," right?

    Also, just to clarify, "Doylist" versus "Watsonian" refers to the difference in analyzing elements of a work of fiction from the perspective of someone writing it (Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, author of the Sherlock Holmes stories) versus someone inside it (Doctor Watson, a character in the Sherlock Holmes stories).

        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
        I'm not sure entirely what you're saying (I don't know "Watsonian" and "Doylian").
        Alucard's already addressed this, but hey. "Watsonian" refers to the use of a fictional work's internal or in-universe logic, where "Doylist" refers to a logic from reasons outside that fictional work. Consider a C-movie's last-minute reveal that the protagonist pulls superpowers out of nowhere. A Watsonian explanation could be that the protagonist was actually an angel all along, who needed to do everything the way it did because of some big unexplored cosmic plan. The Doylist explanation could be that the writer just stopped caring and/or just found out there wasn't going to be the sequel that was originally promised and needed to wrap things up.

        This is a good point. However, it won't be the case in every circumstance or for every goal, only some of them. If Faction A wants Option A and Faction B wants Option B, independents get to play tiebreaker (or even coalition builder) between Options A and B... assuming one of those two options appeal to them. If they actually want Option C, and Factions A and B absolutely won't consider it, then the independent's goals are a lot less relevant. The two factions can mostly control what options are on the table in the first place. (This is also common in U.S. politics.)
        Strictly speaking, it's not between Option A and B. It's between (Option A plus whatever that side's offering in exchange for the cooperation) and (Option B plus whatever the other side's offering). Depending on the situation and Sidereal, all options could be found odious on the face of them, but the deal one side is giving is sweet enough to the Independent to sway that character.

        You're correct that it's not going to happen every time. It does still give them a certain contextual and circumstantial amount of power that's not to be had as a member of either Faction. Power which might be seen as more of a bonus than anything else, if the Sidereal in question disagrees strongly enough with both Factions' agendas to not want to work toward them, or, say, has some other agenda from which the Sidereal would rather not be distracted to the degree both Factions would demand. It depends on the character.

                                                            Right. Which can easily take the form of something like...

                                                            "Okay, we both know you couldn't care less about this decision. I noticed that three of her recently recovered volumes are on track to be cleared for Bureau use, but the earliest is at least eighty years down the line from being inspected. If you'd be so kind as to lend your support on this little matter, we can get them bumped a ways up the schedule. Unless they find something really dangerous in there, I could guarantee you could be digging into them by this time next year."
