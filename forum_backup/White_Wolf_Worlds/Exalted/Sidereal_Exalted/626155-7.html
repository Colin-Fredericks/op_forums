    I don't know enough about Irish politics to think of an equivalent example.
    Maybe... Protestantism in Ulster? Protestantism was the religion of the ruling class of Anglo-Irish nobles. But then suddenly all these guys with a newer, even less Catholic version of Protestantism turned up, and so the old Protestant nobles found themselves siding with the Catholics against the Imperial power and the Presbyterians who supported it.

    Maybe this search for an apt simile is just doomed to failure.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
        I don't know enough about Irish politics to think of an equivalent example.
        Once there was Sinn Féin, the Republican party, that after the Easter Rising was filled up with all the disaffected elements of the country (including many former Home Rulers, which left that party a rump of what it once was). It came under the leadership of one Eamon de Valera. Then the whole civil war thing happened, and the party split into two, with the... more respectable side becoming the party that we call Fine Gael today, and the other side still Sinn Féin, who abstained from political participation and continued to associate with the IRA; de Valera took their side. Eventually, de Valera became distressed by the violence of the IRA and the increasingly apparent uselessness of a party that wouldn't take part in politics, and when his suggestion that they end abstention was rejected by the IRA elements, he left the Sinn Féin name and took the majority of the party with him; the party he founded out of them also had the name of the Republican party, as it still advocated for many of the same things (particularly an Irish republic). In the modern Republic, Sinn Féin still exists, still advocates for the integration of Northern Ireland (among other issues), and has a direct line of continuity to the party that de Valera led, but is a lot smaller than the new one he made.

                        Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                        I don't want there to be a whole splat where having children is illegal. At the worse, Kejak should be mumbling "Haven't these people heard of Maiden Tea?" when he gets a maternity/paternity sabbatical request.
                        Yeah, I'm not sure why this point is still in discussion. Sidereals are still kinda human, so human things—like wanting to have a family—are likely still going to be there. Part of having a family is having kids, either through the regular means or adoption, so why wouldn't some Sidereals have children? Granted, their jobs and the way Arcane Fate works makes it much harder, but that also lends itself to interesting stories. I can certainly see stories of Sidereals that have a child and lose contact with it because of those two factors, vowing never again to subject themselves to such pain.

                        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                        Regarding the Wyld Hunt specifically, I don't care for the 2E notion that everything with a caste mark counts as Anathema. I think even the Bronzes had enough sense to limit the definition of Anathema specifically to Solars and Lunars, and in 3E that'll be specifically true. A dude who glows blue with a strange forehead mark is a weird and possibly dangerous supernatural that merits investigation, and that'll inconvenience them, but it's silly for Immaculates to consider them Anathema.
                        Given the distinction that the Solars, Lunars and Sidereals have regarding the fact that their caste marks appear on the forehead, and the lack of such marks from other supernatural beings, I can see how at least some Immaculates would be quick to label such beings as "Anethema" or possibly Anethema-touched. There would likely be similar reactions to Green Sun Princes—probably even stronger, since they tend to have more obvious demon-touched features.

                        That being said, there's likely nothing in the text refering to the Maidens regarding what is considered Anethema, which an astute study of the text would likely know (which is something wonderandawe also played with in her hero quest).

                                                I suspect that the higher-order Immaculates, the upper crust of the Realm aristocracy, and the learned would be able to judge the factors that distinguish "Anathema" from other phenomena in Creation (and outside of it). But for the country priests, peasantry, and common labor that makes up the lion's share of Creation society, would they have the knowledge to distinguish between the different Exalted, the fae, and other mysterious beings?

                                                I mean, would French peasants in the 13th century be able to distinguish between miaphysitism and Arianism as two specific and mutually-exclusive theological branches of Christian belief? It'd all be "heresy" -- and therefore "dangerous" -- to them, and to be reported to an authority as soon as possible.

                                                Now, the Exigents are going to shift the categories a little bit, but in general, I think that 90+% of the population of Creation would see a Sidereal caste mark and not sit around to ask questions about its provenience. And I think that's perfectly fine for Exalted's narrative. The hubris of the Sidereals screwed them over, and now they have to deal with it.
