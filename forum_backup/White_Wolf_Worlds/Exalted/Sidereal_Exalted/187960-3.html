                Look, leave aside for a moment whether you think it is plausible, in general, that some significant fraction of Sidereals will go around orbital-nuking relationships entirely consequence-free. Is it good to have a statement that they can do this, and they just don't feel like it? Is that an appropriate statement to make about the breadth of Sidereal power: "Yes, a Sidereal absolutely could make you unlovable from another dimension, with no way for you, Solar God-King, to track it back to him or prevent the effect"?

                Because if that's a statement the game makes because it is supposed to happen to you sometimes, with no plausible chance of reprisal, well, that's kinda stupid, right? It doesn't even have the excuse of generating plots, because, again, another dimension, with no way to track it back to him. And so it's incentive, as others have noted, to play someone with no romantic attachments, which is a bad thing.

                And if it isn't supposed to happen to you sometimes, then the Charm doesn't really need to work that way, does it? If it's only really supposed to enable interesting mortal-manipulation strategies, it could have been written to allow some kind of meaningful defense.

                Either way, it's a bad Charm, and noting that it was a bad Charm was not unreasonable on Chung's part. I think it's a bit uncharitable to read his position as "This will inevitably happen to your PC, specifically, or you're cheating"; I've usually seen him argue something closer to "Game-wrecking powers should not be contingent on an NPC not being in the mood to wreck the game with them," which again, kinda reasonable.

                (The response to people who haven't degenerated into paranoia combat is also much less "You're playing wrong" than "You're playing with a very congenial set of social contracts that I would find unpleasant to play with, and/or you've been lucky enough not to hit a mine so far.")
