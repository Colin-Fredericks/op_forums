            Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
            Very cool idea, though I'd think some disease gods would not be too pleased with such a development, possibly resulting in the Sids having to beat some ass to protect him.
            That just makes things more awesome, though, does it not? In my mind (and in my games!) this is how conflicts within the Celestial Bureaucracy should play out, with a combination of cutthroat office politics in Yu-Shan and "controlling the facts on the ground" in Creation.

            But seriously, this is a much, much better idea than the things the book suggests, especially the "snap baby's neck in its crib" mission.
            That actually seems like at least some Sidereal elders and/or sifus are going to make new Sidereals do, not because it's an important mission that requires a Sidereal, but in order to bind them tightly to the Bureau of Destiny.

            Charlie Stross wrote a novella once, called "Palimpset," which featured a kind of time-ninja society, and their organization had an explicit indoctrination method, that in fact exists in the real world, called "bonding through shared atrocity." Basically, they made all of their new recruits murder their own grandparents, and then they made them murder their own past selves. Once you've crossed that Rubicon, you're a lot less likely to walk away, and you're tied very tightly to your co-conspirators, who you all know have committed the exact same atrocity. And if you tried to walk away... well, you're a guy who slit the throat of his own grandparents. Who is gonna trust you, you fucking murdering scumbag?

            It really enhances organizational cohesion; you get the same thing in the real world with organized criminal communities who make prospects commit a murder to obtain full membership. So yeah, I can totally see Sidereals making people do this. Just not for the reasons detailed in the splat.
            Look, I just have trouble suspending disbelief that the overworked Bureau of Destiny would assign one of its most rare and precious resources to that kind of drudgery, even during downtime.
            A Sidereal would probably have to be, not just a fuckup, but an ongoing enormous discipline problem for that to happen. It wouldn't just happen in a fit of pique; you'd need a Sidereal who was willfully, repeatedly, and flagrantly derelict in their duties in order for the Bureau of Destiny to decide that kind of hardcore remediation was necessary. Said remediation also wouldn't just be drudge work, although that would certainly be part of it on account of that way you're at least getting something productive out of them, it would likely involve drudge work underneath some gods who are really good at reforming hard cases.
            Is it the kind of job that 3-5 Sidereals should be put toward? Probably not, unless the ST pulls out the stops to make it worth the attention of such significant assets (the above case being a possible example).
            This is something I've always struggled with.

            A circle of 3-5 Sidereals who are all in the same place, doing the same thing, is an enormous commitment of BoD resources. Four Bronze Faction Sidereals all in one place at one time is probably either literally every Bronze member in an entire directional Convention, or merely most Bronze members in an entire Conventional direction with some high-powered ringers diverted from other assignments. It's a little better if this is official business, not faction work, because that lowers the ratios, but that's still an enormous number of Sids. Even if all those people are starting characters, if four Sidereals are being thrown at a problem, that problem is probably a really big problem. Like, "you guys are four-fifths of the entire Convention on Deathlords, except for your boss, who is an Elder who basically camps out in Yu-Shan and serves on four other Conventions" big problem.

            That can make running group games tricky, because you whipsaw between "why is every problem we face a world-shattering one" and "it strains believability we're being allowed to idle this long." When of course the reason, out of game, is you have four people who all need to sync their schedules and whose PCs all need to remain more or less together.

            Originally posted by Stephenls
            We're adding more obviously interesting and engaging sources of time-consuming bullshit for Sidereals to deal with, so that it's more obvious why the death squads aren't working as fast as Chejop sometimes wishes (while sighing wearily, making a note, and grabbing the next form from his in tray).
            Good lord, I remember reading that for the first time and dancing a fucking jig.

            There were loud, long, and bitter arguments back in the day on rpg.net involving people who were entirely adamant in their belief that Sidereals made no sense because "they can just find and murder everyone, endlessly, if you attract any attention at all you should die, see, look at all these resources they have, if I had control of all those resources I could take down all the Solars no problem, Bull of the North should have had his throat slit ages ago, you fail at splat Creation writers, you fail forever."

            And, well... no. Both editions haven't made this as clear as they could, but... the Sidereals have day jobs. All that stuff Chejop Kejak does with shadow puppeting the Realm and working hand-in-glove with the Empress and suchly? That, from the formal perspective of the Bureau of Destiny, is his hobby. Stuff he's expected to keep off the books and not allow to interfere with his work. And when he deploys official resources in pursuit of Bronze Faction interests, from another formal perspective, that's a willful act of corruption that really shouldn't be happening. He gets away with it because he's a huge political power player, but he has to do a lot of heavy lifting to make that work. When he dispatches Ahn-Aru to take care of Lilith, he can't just sign the death warrant; he has to cover all the bases. He needs to expend political capital to justify the hit as an official BoD op (which involves lying and then making sure Ayesha Ura can't poke through the lies and get the op cancelled) or he needs to do things like arrange for Ahn-Aru to be on furlough. He has to go work the Immaculate Order to shake loose some more prayers for Jorst, which, again, he has to do off-book, because that's not part of his day job. And he has to do all that while still doing said day job.

            People seemed to have a really hard time getting that.

            Originally posted by Zelbinnean
            Right now it feels very much like Sidereals skew between 0 and 10 pretty quickly on the "mission importance" chart, and I guess I'd rather have the low end shored up so that even the most 'banal" mission is still at least a 4 on the "cosmic import" scale.
            In my experience, the easiest way to make banal missions work is for them to be part of overall bureaucratic infighting. Sort of like how if you're playing Dragon-Blooded and your House sends you to do something seemingly mundane, it is probably part of the great game of jockeying for power and position, because you're a Dragon-Blooded and unless you're a willful fuckup, you are probably too valuable to waste sweeping floors.

            I once ran a game where my three players spent a month and a half's worth of sessions wondering why their assignments had suddenly gotten so disjointed and trivial. (They were having fun, they were just confused.) And then suddenly one of them went "Wait a minute. I recognize the co-signatory on all of these assignment orders. Didn't he work for that guy we got audited, censured, and demoted?"

            Why yes. Yes he did.

            Things stopped being banal rather fast after that.

                                                        Speaking for myself (as someone who likes the Sids and may not be the target audience) Sidereal missions should involve

                                                        - A level of logical sense to the mission : "We want this kingdom here, because we've predicted this great person will arise in it if it does (and will not if it does not), which will mean..." more than "This guy must die. Because Fate... Why are you still in my office?".

                                                        - A goal to change Creation beyond simply maintaining the status quo: "If we do this, new agricultural methods will be invented and used, the population of the region will grow, the city gods and the people will grow fat on the flow of money, Creation will move forward to a more glorious state", rather than "There are exactly 200 peasants in this hamlet; predictions show there we may be at risk of there being 500 peasants in this hamlet in 100 years. Rookie, you need to fix this situation. We do not want any change, ever, under any circumstances."

                                                        This doesn't actually have to be altruistic - it can simply be "This will make the Bureau stronger." Whatever, maybe your mission is to capture new secret martial arts techniques. It just has to achieve something meaningful in changing the status quo, something new, rather than makework or just perpetuating the status quo ad infinitum. You're part of a functional bureau who achieve things and change the world. You build and create and enable and recover and restore. You're not just maintenance men.

                                                        - A degree of latitude by the Sidereal in the field. The Sidereal gets a broad goal to achieve, and then uses his or her ability to accomplish the goal. The Sidereal does not get a series of micromanaged instructions that have no real input other than "How do I apply my weird Sidereal magic to these ends?". Comms with your bosses should be hard, and they should be busy, to create a feel where you're not on your cellphone to your mentor all the time and are working off your gut in a situation where intel and your briefings (which really resemble a meeting between the wizards of Middle Earth as much as an intel briefing) haven't prepared you enough for what you face and the actual issues in the field.
