                    Regarding the changed relationship between Sidereals and the Gods of Yu-Shan: I suspect this is the Devs reacting to a common misconception that Sids can't walk down a heavenly street without every god there hissing and booing them, throwing divine turnips at them and overcharging them for every little thing. The books don't actually support this reading, but it wouldn't be the first time the echo chamber blew things out of proportion.

                    It's possible that, in order to win over the fans, the Devs are fixing a problem that isn't really broken, but I hope they read the actual source material clearer than that. In any event, even if the masses of gods now enthusiastically support the Fellowship, that doesn't mean that they won't still have problems with the Gods, esp the ones they work closest with! They still have to produce results, and will get in trouble if they don't, or if they cross the line on what they're allowed to do to carry out their duties. Temporary exile from heaven will probably be something we see a lot of, esp as it allows the Seer to conduct extended activities in Creation with his bosses having Plausible Deniability.

                    I think hostility will still exist between gods and viziers, but it will most likely be for personal reasons, some insult or inconvenience that you (or your previous incarnation!) caused a minor or major figure in the Bureaucracy that they intend to return to you, for ex.

                    Incidentally, as to the Realm officially executing Dragon-Blooded: Yes, this is a thing that happens. There was an incident where no less than a full *General* and all his senior officers were found guilty of Jade embezzlement and publicly beheaded in front of their own troops!

                                    Actually, it doesn't surprise me that the occasional Sidereal gets executed, now and then. Sure, Heaven is corrupt as heck, in the Exalted era. But there's still the rule of law (just a very corrupt one).

                                    If a Sidereal actually gets caught, dead to rights, committing a serious offense, there's not much that Kejak or the other Sidereals can do to help him. Sure, they could close ranks, help him escape . . . but then their entire faction becomes accomplices. Even if they get away with it, that's going to break down the fragile fabric of peace in Heaven. And once that happens, its only a matter of time until there's blood in the streets.

                                    And with all the other problems going on, the last thing Creation needs is a civil war in Heaven. Better to sacrifice the stupid (or unlucky) Sidereal, and maintain the fiction that there's still a veneer of civility and rules. After all, the unfortunate Sidereal will reincarnate, sooner or later. And the other Sidereal can always settle the score, later, quietly, after the crowds have gone away . . .

                                    Think of Milady du Winters, from The Three Musketeers. She was a very loyal henchwoman to Cardinal Richelieu, and a talented one to boot. And yet, when she's caught in the act, she's still executed. One word from him, and she'd be spared. But then he'd have to answer dangerous questions about what she was doing . . .

                                    And du Winters' has a daughter, who's an even more promising agent . . . and now has a very personal reason for wanting the Musketeers dead.

                                                        Originally posted by leo1925 View Post
                                                        Really? Why they were called that?

                                                        Because in Werewolf the Apocolypse you had the evil "demon" realm called Malfeas (which served as the inspiration for Malfeas in Exalted). Then in Wraith the Oblivion, you had the Malfeans which were these dead-creepy monsters that hung out near Oblivion and were pretty much copy/pasted into Exalted.

                                                        In Exalted, the idea was that Malfeas had been the general of the Primordials, and the Malfeans were those Primordials who swore their loyalty to him and fought to the death. I never had an issue with it, but some people found the terms incredibly confusing and complained about it, so the authors dropped that name in 2nd edition and the Malfeans only went by the name "Neverborn" which had been one of their titles in 1st edition (like the Yozi being called the Demon Princes).

                                                        Post 2nd edition the term "Malfeans" becomes even more confusing as we suddenly find out Malfeas is actually pretty young and didn't came into being after he made the surrender oaths. Prior to that he was essentially an entirely different being. So the idea of the Neverborn calling themselves Malfeans definitely no longer works, as they would have already died before the being known as Malfeas even existed!

                                                        But you still see the term tossed around occasionally as it's been with the gameline since before day zero.
