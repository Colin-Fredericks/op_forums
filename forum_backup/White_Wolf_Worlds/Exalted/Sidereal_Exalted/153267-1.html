                            Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                            This is basically the same argument people make for scrapping Craft, rather than address the decker problem the whole subsystem should be cut and the creation of artifacts left in the hands of dedicated NPCs who never leave their workshops.
                            Seeing a variation of it coming from you it is hilarious.
                            This is kinda part of why I suggest that Sidereals should be able to put a couple of epic twists of fate in their back pocket and save them for later. The idea of Sidereals having this greater perception of the world and the ability to make plans accordingly is great, but if you are playing in a mixed game the GM will often just roll their eyes at you when you suggest using these powers because it can be so out of character with what the other splats are capable of doing. I can see why some people would suggest that Sidereals in some ways work better as NPCs, but I think changes could be made so that instead of being the splat who always has the plan, they could be the splat who always SEEMS to have the plan.

                            That's why I like the idea of having these useful and slightly flexible outcomes held in reserve, from the Sidereal's point of view they just know they really need this situation to turn out differently so they change it, from the Solar's point of view it looks as if the Sidereal had a plan all along, and from the GM's point of view they didn't have to share their campaign notes with a player just to make their powers work. Another example I have been thinking of would be the situation where the party is hopelessly lost, the Sidereal could use an instance of "Hidden Path Revealed" they had motes committed to and suddenly they have inserted a fate into reality where they know of a secret trail around the corner that will get them to where they need to go. The sidereal is relieved that they spent the motes on that snippet of fate a couple months ago, and the rest of the party is amazed at this mysterious Sidereal who always seems to know more than they should.

                            If there were one or two of these sort of "emergency charms" in each charm tree I think the Sidereal's could have a play-style somewhere in between "Prepared for every outcome" and "It's uncanny how things always work out for them" without them becoming the spat with metagaming built right in.

                                                        Originally posted by turkeygiant View Post
                                                        This concept came up in a discussion about how the celestial splats can be balanced against each while still maintaining the concept of Solars being the best at what they do in a very straight forward sense of achievement. We were trying to come up with roundabout ways in which the other splats can work around or, even negate the power a solar gains from just “being better” than everyone else.
                                                        That's backwards.

                                                        Sidereals aren't distinct from Solars in order to "let" Solars be the best at what they do, or to find ways to sneak around and beat Solars anyway.

                                                        Sidereals are what they are in order to be Sidereals, which includes being meaningfully distinct from other splats (including Solars.)

                                                        There's a host of implications stemming from what Solars are and what they do, or from the fact that Exalted is a game and therefore requires some degree of balance; but using those implications to inform core Sidereals powers is putting the cart before the horse. Decide what Sidereals should be and get a general concept for how they'll work mechanically, then figure out how you'll balance that into the game.

                                                        Or, in other words -- when you're designing a splat, you start with some basic concepts about how they'll work and what their charms will do, then you implement those with an eye towards overarching balance and how they interact with other splats. If you can't resolve it, you go back to the drawing board; and obviously your basic concept needs to be meaningfully distinct as a whole; but if you start with the presumption of "how can I avoid stepping on these people's toes, while still beating them" you will end up with awkwardly-designed splats that will feel like they're assembled out of other people's scraps.
