    The way I see it, and I'm not in any way withdrawing my wish that it were possible to make a fun, playable Sid character without diving headfirst into MA, SMA is more appropriate for Sids than Sorcery because to me, Sorcery seems to be about knowing whereas SMA is about understanding.

    Sorcery has things like, you know the secret Sign of Peace that prevents people from fighting in your presence. You know of the horrible Eyeless Face and know how to exploit the cracks in the walls of his prison to call his minions to your service. You know the names of ten thousand demons and the ancient surrender oaths that let you enslave them.

    With SMA, you understand a fundamental truth about the nature of the world and reassert said truth through your martial techniques. You understand the underlying patterns of Creation's essence and thus you can use the techniques of Prismatic Arrangement of Creation Style. You understand that identity is just a glitch in your essence pattern and express said understanding through Kaleidoscopic Borders of Logic*. So on, so forth.

    Sorcery is very thematic for Solars, as it is about imposing your will upon the world. SMA is very thematic for Sids, as it exploits the esoteric system of Creation while still working within it.

    If having access to the highest tier of sorcerous power was to be the Sids' shtick, it would have to look and feel quite different from what we know as Exalted's Sorcery. Likewise, if Solars had their own way of achieving blatantly magical effects through martial training and philosophical understanding, it'd have to look quite unlike SMA to remain in theme.

                                    Okay! I'm a full supporter of SMA, but envisioning a 'Sidereal Sorcery' isn't too hard at all. In fact, you can throw one together in about five minutes by taking the entire Astrology system, translating the Resplendencies into minor sorceries, creating a spell to replace the basic Destiny system and making the rest of the spells more-or-less Greater Astrology charms. High power is balanced out by Paradox costs, slap a coat of paint on it.

                                    If you wanted to take more time to create a more intriguing system, you should look into removing the idea of 'Sidereal Sorcery' as a separate system and instead adapt Astrology so that college dots = dots in the related ability and give a player who invests in a Sidereal tree the ability to create greater astrology 'spells' of that ability's college. This is a notional drop in strength for the Sidereals, but a practical increase in power- since each Ability dot gets you more sorcerous options.

                                    I know this relies a lot on the Greater Astrology stuff- but that really is exactly the sort of stuff one'd expect to see out of 'Sidereal Sorcery'. I think adapting it toward a new end beats creating a redundant system.

                                    Originally posted by Gonzo View Post
                                    I know a lot of people appreciate Sidereals the way they are. IMO, I'm not entirely comfortable with Sidereals being based on the "kung fu hermit" or the "legendary kung fu master". I think that those archetypes may end up forcing them as NPC's. If the solar is Hercules, and the lunar could be the nemean lion, who would be the sidereal within this same myth?
                                    Gaius had a good post addressing that point, but if I might expand upon it:

                                    Focusing too much on pithy descriptions is dangerous, even if it's a great way to communicate a high concept. Sidereals have a very natural entry into the 'legendary kung fu master' archetype, but that's in the same way that a Solar has a natural entry into the 'Master craftsman of Creation' archetype. It's not actually a defining characterization! Instead, let's look at what Hatewheel likes to describe them as, since he's been working on this whole 'quickly and effectively describe the Exalted' pitch:

                                    'Sorcerer-Bureaucrat agents of Heaven'

                                    This is it, really. Solars are the god-kings of old, returned from banishment to bring glory to a fallen world. Lunars are heroes with monsters within or monsters with heroes within. Sidereals are sorcerer-bureaucrat agents of Heaven.

                                    This is why I like that turn of phrase so much:

                                    Sorcerer - In this case only nominally refers to Sorcery. Even in 1e, the author of the Sidereal charms considered the high-end charms essentially self-contained sorceries rather than the expansions on ability which were most common. An unusually large percentage of the Sidereal trees are hugely externalized effects- moving cities, vanishing from timelines, cursing someone to unlovability, firing an arrow which becomes grain, sacrificing an army to bind a god. SMA fits here too; while it might technically be an expansion on martial skill, it manifests in things like the ability to slap the illness out of someone or re-align your essence to create a hearthstone. You cannot make a Sidereal who has no strange and powerful sorceries, even if you never buy a dot of Occult.*

                                    Bureaucrat - This one's more obvious, but no less important. Sidereals are part of a greater bureaucracy, and are connected to ten thousand potential friends and foes through it. It's a source of vast power- access to the illimitable powers of Heaven- and a restraint, since you can never stop being part of that bureaucracy. A Sidereal can move a mountain with a single Charm, but he can't do it without explaining himself to someone down the line.

                                    Agent - Here's a secret: 'Agent' in the Men In Black sense is not a secondary archetype which applies to some Sidereals and not others. It's a description of how you play Sidereals, a characterization which you can only avoid by willfully subverting it. There's always been a lot of arguing about how one 'fixes Fate glitches'- well, here you have it. You go to the source, investigate what's causing the problem and you neutralize it, usually with extreme prejudice. You have some discretion, of course- that's part of the driving story behind any 'elite secret agent' story- will you follow your orders even if you must betray your principles?

                                    Of - Oh-ho, I bet you thought I wasn't going to include 'of' in my word-by-word analysis! Well you are totally right.

                                    Heaven - This is the last piece in the puzzle. What does a Sidereal serve? The Exalted of the Maidens change the story of Creation around to match Destiny's plan, but they also hold a red pen which they could use to make edits to their orders. Is it right to bring two realms to war to prevent catastrophic loss elsewhere in the system? Is it right to watch the scions of the Great Houses tear a teenaged Solar apart? Do 'right' and 'wrong' even matter when the stakes are so high? Is a Sidereal loyal to their own morals? To the system? To a god? To a nation? The Sidereals are the Exalted of Heaven- but have a look at oriental emperors in the real world and see how fickle Heaven can be.

                                    When you're inside, looking out, playing a Sidereal feels a little like being a mechanic. You've come down into Creation because something has gone tits-up, or is otherwise going to, and you're the only man for the job. You've got a toolbox with a portable hole, an infinitely-extensible piece of chalk, an indestructible length of hose and a fighter jet (it's collapsible), and you will damn well find and fix that problem with this extremely-powerful-if-somewhat-confusing arsenal. Sometimes you come across other people with powerful tools and no direction poking about, and you have to decide if you're going to recruit them, point them to somewhere less risky or break out the fighter jet.

                                    When you're outside, a Sidereal is like a bizarre combination of Gandalf and Agent K. He shows up, declares that the ring which has been in your family for generations needs to be in a volcano on the other side of the world ASAP, and suddenly the adventure begins. Gandalf K is at once incredibly powerful** and bizarre in his priorities. He's clearly a powerful sorcerer, yet he insists on avoiding all notice and wipes the memory of anyone who notices that something's up.

                                    ...mind you, this is all for PC Sidereals. Most people who meet a Sidereal do indeed encounter them disguised as someone's evil vizier. That's just a product of limited perception, not a completely different archetypal role.

                                    Anyway, I think that's cool. Actually, I think it's so cool that I have a hard time playing anything else. Some people feel the other way, and I am happy to let those people play monster-heroes and glorious returned kings while we are sailing through hyperspace on my rowboat and the Exigent is asking me why the fuck we didn't just take the hyperspace-rowboat to Mordor.***


                                    *Well, I guess you could just not buy charms? Or maybe only purchase TMA? I digress.
                                    **You can't help but wonder why he doesn't just use that fighter jet to fly the Ring to Mordor. You've got a sneaking suspicion that he hasn't quite told you everything.
                                    ***Because there would have been insufficient time for character development, obviously.

                                                            I think it's important to note that if we gave 2e Sids access to SCS, either alongside or instead of SMA, it wouldn't change anything for them - Solar or Celestial, Sorcery is cumbersome to deal with and not that useful in the long run.

                                                            Basically, there are two layers to the problem Sids have in 2e. The first layer is having a closed, limited native Charmset in a game where only Charms matter. The second layer is none of the options they have for circumventing the first problem being well written or especially useful. Sorcery is only good for cherry picking a few spells, Martial Arts were for the most part broken to the degree of being unplayable, and Astrology was too much buck for too little bang. All three were notorious exp sinks on top of their other problems - but you already know all of that.

                                                            What matters is, with the exception of Astrology, these are universal problems with universal subsystems, and we only associate those problems with Sids because they're saddled with this first problem of their native Charms having too many holes in a system that doesn't forgive having holes in your set of magic tricks.

                                                            Each of those problems requires it's share of attention, but for Sids in particular, it's imperative to make them such that their native Charmset doesn't leave them wide open in situations they are supposed to handle. From what we know so far, the solution proposed by 3e seems to be shifting the focus of the system away from Charm interactions and letting things work without Charms. If this works satisfactorily, then the Sids will be fully playable, pretty much regardless of the shape of Sorcery and MA (which, hopefully, will get satisfactory solutions for their own problems).

                                                            If all these problems are solved, there is no need to change the basic setup of Sids.
