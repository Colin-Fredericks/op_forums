                Well,
                I've been doing my reading, and it turns out you guys are right. It seems the Devs are going with a "The First Age/Usurpation is unexplained, and it isn't productive to write material that judges past actors, instead of material about present actors who still possess agency to affect Creation."

                So, it looks like they're just giving the issue a whitewash. Disheartening, but not that unanticipated after "Thaumaturgy is no longer a supernatural science, and mortals no longer access Essence, because we wanted to limit "casual" magical penetration of societies. Now, please ignore all the customized, specific magical penetration we wrote into societies that contradicts this."

                As for whether the Contagion was the Bronze's fault: I don't consider it an out-of-game meta thing to make a decision to eliminate 6/7ths of the Celestial Exalts, and then somehow expect Creation to be OK defended by 1/7th of the Celestial Exalts and the Dragon Blooded. Anyone with even human foresight could have divined the eventual result. The Dragon Blooded would do fine with the day to day, week to week stuff, but the first really major existential threat to Creation to emerge would overwhelm them. It didn't have to be the Contagion. There was that last enemy Primordial never accounted for, for example..By definition, take ANYTHING that the Solars/Lunars/Sidereals were collectively needed to deal with post-Great War, and a repeat of that would overwhelm Sidereals + Dragon Blooded alone.

                Not that I can't see the design cleverness in obfuscating the details of the First Age for 3E. Without the players having the meta-context concerning antiquity, it leaves the door open much wider to toss in things at a later date. The Devs seem to have a real hard-on in the Q&A I'm reading against story-elements in 2E that "collapsed the waveform." Examples: Return of the Scarlet Empress, the five listed options for the potential identity of the Emissary, and the "truth" of what makes the Chayans go nuts.

                It's a valid position, and it's theirs to take, but there are a LOT of people like me who enjoy lore being definitive. If I wanted to fill in all the major mysterious elements with my own unique stamp, I'd simply heist the Attributes + Ability or a D20 system, and build my own game.

                You make my point for me though Epimetheus , the Bronze believe that, no matter what terrible fate befalls Creation, by definition the Solars would've done worse. That's absolutist thinking, and a wonderful exemplar of the Great Curse at work. Blurring out the details of the First Age serves to relieve the Bronze Faction of the villainous cast to their actions. If its just "Yup, we all agree the Solars were nuts, obviously they have to go" then there's no room for "What if the Bronze was wrong? What if, as happens in so many epic myths when someone tries to Cheat Fate, the actor only served to bring about the Fate they feared? What if all this suffering was unnecessary, and there was another way?" Nope, ALL the Solars were mad with power and hopelessly corrupt. Let's just kill nuance like the fact that some Solars, like the Queen of Whitewall (I can't remember how to spell its First Age name) hadn't social-fu'd her Dragon Blooded at all, and her people loved her so intensely the Bronze Dragon Blooded armies had to butcher all of them to get to her.

                As I said, it can be characterized otherwise, but after-the-fact reconsideration of the Usurpation is a Utility Calculation, not a moral stance. I was always most interested in the Gold Faction because, for all their many, many faults, they'd at least put their money where there mouths were. The original members fought and died, rather than becoming Brutus & Cassius: The Fellowship. They endured nearly two millennia of political marginalization in Heaven for refusing to cave and profess "Yes, of course your slaughter of the Solars, all your fellow Sidereals who would not bend their necks, the many thousands of Dragon Blooded, who chose to fight and die in battle against their brothers and sisters, rather than become back-stabbing slaves, and all those Lunars who wouldn't abandon their mates, Chejop. Oh wait, I forgot all the Gods and Elementals you had smelted into ash-trays and toilet seats on trumped-up charges."

                Instead, it's "Well, 90% of Creation died, and we lost 40% of Creation, period. Perhaps we shouldn't have been so hasty in assuming we could overthrow the natural order without horrific consequences. WAIT, LOOK! The Solars have returned! Quick guys, if we hurry, we can scurry down there and cultivate them. We can press the reset button! Remember everyone, Ix Nay Usurpation-ay."

                I get (and I agree, so far as many elements) that it's better to focus on the agency of the PCs than spoon-feed characters-on-rails, but the Usurpation and its context has always served to inform the canon agenda Re: Yu-Shan. Drawing a curtain over the First Age with regards to the Usurpation is simplistic, and creatively reductive. YMMV, of course.

                Then again, I ADORED Lords & Lands of Creation, which I gather are only slightly less despised around here than the Underworld Rapist-Rape Brothel, and Lillun (Relax, I utterly despise both of those things.)
