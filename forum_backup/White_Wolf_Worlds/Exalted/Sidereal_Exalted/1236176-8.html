    Manacle and Coin noted that the process of the Great Houses taking increasing control of the peasantry amounted to reducing them to serfdom, so one could see that there's at least a distinction in character between where they've been and where they're going.

    My read on the situation of the Realm's peasantry is that they have obligations to the state representatives, including needing permission for travel, but are otherwise relatively autonomous, and have concerns about the possibilities of being tied in fiscal responsibilities to the local Dynasts.

    Mind, European serfdom came in several forms and with varying nuances, and was not universally a terrible situation. You got Medieval peasants who would opt for serfdom over being Freedmen, because it could carry certain economic stability and responsibilities from the landlord. I would say that the Realm situation is more concerning because of the specifics of how Dynasts would relate to peasants that they have a more direct control over.

                            I wasn't saying that Chejop Kejak isn't a totalitarian moral utilitarian. In the passage you quoted, I was referencing the LACK of counter-examples in the character's known canon behavior. I was also allowing for the possibility such a counter-example might well exist, that I am not aware of, and suggesting if anyone knew of one, I would be extremely interested in such as a broadening of my understanding of the character. A character who has a significant, yet rarely expressed, view is fundamentally different from one who has never offered such a view. Everything *I* know of the character Chejop Kejak is indicative of his considering the GC & BC extremely regrettable, but ultimately preferable, to continued Solar rule...and personally, I'm giving the character the benefit of the doubt on his considering the GC & BC extremely regrettable, because there ARE inferences one could draw to the contrary from the character's cited statements. I tend to think no one except Deathlords/Neverborn, and some of the hardcore true-believing, outright Oblivion-worshiping DKs could look at either catastrophe and not feel SOME level of horror.

                            As for Sidereal influence over the Realm, I tend to believe they COULD have done more to rein in what would later become all-but-unstoppable cultural and economic forces, but early on, the focus was very much on whipping the Realm into the centralized power required to rapidly reconstitute the Wyld Hunt and assist the Realm in sinking its hooks into three of four Directions before humanity got back on its feet.

                            Oh, and you guys REALLY preferred a Sword of Creation that operates optimally for a Terrestrial Exalted, to one she can get to work, but with consequences? Why? How does that improve the setting, in other than a "DBs RULE!" kind of way? If the Empress could just fire the Realm Defense Grid however she liked, as she liked, there's no reason for her NOT to have fired it at any target she wasn't going to get anything out of economically, but might pose a threat to Realm interests. The Skullstone Archipelago was a piracy problem long before the Silver Prince reemerged, as a shadowland it's essentially worthless as something to conquer, and obliterating it would likely go a long way toward reminding Coral whose boss. Firing on the exceptional conglomeration of Lookshy/Nexus/Confederation forces marching against the Thorns forces pinning the rest of the Confederation at Mishaka could have broken the East forever. Especially since the Lookshy forces had brought out a significant portion of their First Age goodies. Losing at Mishaka was a FAR more severe blow to Imperial power than even losing the Tepet/Linowan/Talinin/Roka-Jin forces would later be, after her disappearance. Limiting the Sword when operated by a non-Celestial was, IMHO, one of the best setting-point decisions 2E made. Someone as utterly ruthless as the Empress required there be a material reason the Sword didn't become the answer to thorny military problems.

                            Since it's been mentioned several times though, how do you guys envision the Sidereal capacity for perceiving what's going on/will go on in Creation with the Loom in EX3? Specifically, stuff like tracking individuals by use of essence, determining significant events in area X in say, the next year, and with what level of precision locating and identifying damaging Out of Fate (or whatever demons/Fair Folk will be with regards to destiny now) beings?
