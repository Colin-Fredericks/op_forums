                            Blind Seers is emblematic of a problem that Exalted has struggled with since the beginning, which is that things that require subtlety and nuance are instead taken way too far.

                            Like, it makes total and complete sense that the way the Great Curse manifests within the Sidereals is via hubris, arrogance, and an inability to admit to themselves that they done fucked up. That makes sense for them, because it perverts their intended purpose in the grand scheme of things into something ultimately pretty horrific, as a good curse should. Great! Good stuff.

                            It also makes sense that the Sidereals didn't see the Great Contagion and other major disasters coming because those disasters originate outside Fate and despite working really hard it, the entire Sidereal toolkit isn't designed with that in mind. So far, so good. Things make sense, and this is a good reason the Sidereals haven't seen this shit coming and stopped it, so we still have a setting.

                            Really, what makes an otherwise serviceable sidebar completely break down are a bare two sentences:

                            Their arrogance and hubris have blinded them to even the possibility that their prognostications may be in error or that there are things of which they are not aware
                            And
                            It simply doesn't occur to them that they could be wrong.
                            Emphasis mine in both cases.

                            And that's shitty. That's a shitty curse. It precludes a lot of interesting possibilities, it makes the Sidereals less awesome, and in fact it contradicts greatly other parts of their splatbook, where it is pointed out that the Sidereals are very concerned and work very hard to gather information on shit they know they are ignorant about. It also makes them seem like idiots within the setting, which should never, ever be the case.

                            Because you know what? It is true that there should be some Sidereals in whom their version of the Great Curse manifests as a stubborn, arrogant refusal to admit that they could be wrong and who meddle with things they don't understand because they're 100% convinced they do understand them, because they're so wise and powerful and control the world. Oh, sure, when pressed they'll admit they've fucked up in the past. But they won't really believe it. It was always somebody else's fault. Their plan was perfect. That should, certainly, be a thing.

                            But there should also be Sidereals who are so consumed by doubt about ever having enough information, about knowing enough about everything, that it wraps them up in bureacratic paralysis, and they order study after fact-finding mission after reconnaissance report after committee meeting after analysis after analysis after analysis after analysis until the time when they should have acted, could have made a real difference, could have saved the world, has long since passed them by.

                            It should also be a thing that there are Sidereals who for the most part have a realistic understanding of their own strengths and weaknesses, and carefully balance acting decisively with holding back until they're sure, but occasionally they get... nudged. They step back, and examine their plan, and think Maybe I am wrong. And the Great Curse slithers up to their ear and whispers No, you're totally right and super awesome. Sometimes they listen to that voice and don't scrap a plan they really should have.

                            Those should all three be things! They make the Sidereals nuanced and interesting while still preserving the fact that the Great Curse has its hands around their hearts.

                            All it would have taken to make that sidebar a thoroughly unremarkable information dump is just the tiniest bit of nuance. Instead, whoever wrote it got super overexcited.

                            It's not the only time that had happened. "The Gods are actually metaphysically incapable of ever respecting any Dragon-Blooded, ever" is perhaps the masterpiece of that genre.

                            "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."

                                Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
                                Like, it makes total and complete sense that the way the Great Curse manifests within the Sidereals is via hubris, arrogance, and an inability to admit to themselves that they done fucked up. That makes sense for them, because it perverts their intended purpose in the grand scheme of things into something ultimately pretty horrific, as a good curse should. Great! Good stuff.
                                On this, my opinion is that it's OK, but a little narrow.

                                Compare the Solars - their Great Curse leads them to tyranny and misrule, yes, it subverts their role as champions and leaders. But the way it does so is through a number of different routes, through the twisting of the four virtues in a bunch of different ways depending on the individual. Wrath, depression, bitterness, pride, anger, paranoia, alienation, paralysis, contempt, fear, cruelty, and so on - the whole human suite.

                                It'd be nice if the Sids had a bunch of different Compassion, Conviction, Valor, Temperance related curses that led to the place that they were supposed to go, to being advisors who give corrupt advice or make hubris fueled errors, ultimately, as a broad effect of what happens when these individual curses come together over time without that being spelled out mechanically.

                                I think another advantage (beyond for its own sakes) of going through that idea of their role as advisors becoming corrupted through multiple routes and multiple emotions is it prevents that one note picture of Sidereals as just arrogant as forming.

                                Just my feeling on it.

                                            It was asked: Was the reign of the Solars really that bad, such that the worst excesses of the Scarlet Dynasty are worth propping up?

                                            I imagine every now and then, an Elder who lived through that time is asked this by a younger Seer. He looks them right in the eye,and says "I know what you're thinking. That we are exaggerating how bad things were to justify our secret control. Or we're just delusional or outright lying. I understand why you might think this, and applaud you for being skeptical. But you'll just have to take my word for it when I tell you: Yes. Yes, it really was just. That. Bad."

                                            And it was. DotFA is set before things got really bad and became a dystopia, so details are still sketchy, but one book tells us that, by the time of the Usurpation, something like one in every 100 mortals living on the Blessed Isle knew someone who had been murdered or molested in some way by a Solar. Out of the millions of people living there, that's horrifying!

                                            Before they acted, the Sids discovered a conspiracy of mortals who were going to attempt to overthrow theirrulers; they covered it up, not because they felt they could use them for the Usurpation,but because they were genuinely afraid of what would happen to all those people when they were found out!

                                            Even in the Dreams time, we see Solars who do such things as mutate the humans living in their principalities into new races of inhuman creatures against their wills. And there are laws protecting mortals, specifically Accords against mutating,mutilating or otherwise altering their souls (which is well beyond DB capacity), which many solars were willing to break, either openly if they ruled a Tributary, or covertly, which some others covered up because they didn't want the Deliberative telling THEM what not to do.

                                            Don't forget, the Soalrs also invented teh Soulbreaker Orbs. In a world where the soul is a verifiable thing, guaranteed to survive any amount of violence to the body so someone can start over witha fresh life, think of how horrible such a thing is! No wonder that Lookshy deploying one against the REalm troops is considered oneof the most unforgivably heinous war crimes of the Second Age...

                                            Then there's the Agony Organ, a torture device that applied pain to wrench a single perfect note from the mortals inserted into it; the Solar who created it played long symphonies of complex melodies composed of the agonized screams of his victims...Apparently, a big hit among his fellow Lawgivers! Now, can you imagine a Dynast openly trying to get away with some shit like that??

                                            On a larger scale, Solars were fucking around with the Sidereals,gods and heaven in other ways. Not only were they imprisoning Spirits for minor "offenses" (like sorcerously binding that Elemental Dragon under An-Teng and binding a Spirit insidethe Brass Leviathan, both just because those spirits didn't want to hang with those Solars), and experimenting on the Pattern Spiders (only stopping when the Maidens themselves asked them to), but when the Sids warned them that the large amounts ofEssence they were using was having unforseen effects on the Loom of Fate, their reaction was to deliberately *increase* the amounts of Essence they were applying, just to see what would happen!

                                            If the worst started to happen, many didn't care, as they were planning to reshapethe entire world into a more pleasing shape, anyway. Something round, perhaps, or maybe cubical? Seriously,there were entire political cliques centering around what shape they should force the world into! And don't even get me started on Operation: Wyldhand...

                                            One of the creators once stated that the Usurpation went down because the Solars were increasingly coming to the same answer to the question, What need have we for Man and Gods?: None.
