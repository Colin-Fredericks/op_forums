                                Originally posted by The Revenge of TV Head View Post
                                Oh, so you subscribe tot the theory that the law in Yu Shan is absolutely fucking batshit. Okay, carry on.
                                Forgive the long lapse in replying; I was at the Toronto Comic Arts Festival all weekend.

                                And... hmm, maybe? I do think the law in Yu-shan is batshit, but not in this specific case. Rather, in the context of this discussion, I think that it, like most bodies of law, doesn't cover edge cases.

                                Let me toss out some examples. Nazri convenes the Convention of Fire for their once-a-month staff meeting. The third item on the agenda is that there's a guy in the Lap who is alive when he isn't supposed to be; his thread in the Loom ran its course and was duly snipped, but he's still walking around and breathing and suchly. The Pattern Spiders are worried that random orphan destinies are going to start attaching themselves to him, and would like it "taken care of." Dude is just a mortal, so this is the equivalent of paperwork for the Sidereals; one of them will simply knife him the next time they're in Gem and the body will never be found.

                                The Sidereals have committed a series of rather serious crimes here, up to and including murder, but I don't think Heaven is going to hold them all that responsible for it, because they're doing the job they were given. It would be illegal for them to just randomly kill people without orders, in the same way Her Majesty's Government would be okay with James Bond blowing away a British industrialist they'd specifically tasked him to assassinate because he was going to hold the world hostage with his space lasers but would be less cool if he killed the same guy because he just didn't like his hair.

                                Let's scale that up. Would you say the Sidereals are permitted under their ambit to do things like rig the outcome of battles? That probably involves ensuring hundreds of people die. Would you say they're permitted to actually arrange the fall of entire kingdoms and noble houses if all the paperwork is in order and word comes down from on high in the Bureau of Destiny that the time has come?

                                Because, if so, then the Usurpation may very well have been entirely legal. Remember, the higher echelons of the Bureau of Destiny have a fair number of Sidereals in actual planning, as opposed to simply execution, roles. The law codes of Yu-Shan probably were not ever written to consider the possibility the Sidereals would decide that the time had come for the Old Realm to perish in fiery destruction, so when they did that, there wasn't a legal venue of recourse available, because the Sidereals can just piously say "we had a planning session, it generated a number of possible destinies, we picked the best one. We do that all the time. That is our job. You got a problem, talk to our bosses."

                                I am not saying this is canon. I am saying that this seems like a reasonable explanation to me.

                                                        Originally posted by goldfaction View Post
                                                        For that matter, how does the Bureau of Destiny's gods planning Creation's future interact with the Creation-Ruling Mandate, which is all about gods not messing with Creation & leaving that to the Exalted?
                                                        "Planning Destiny" is supposed to mean figuring out the logical consequences of actions and motivations that people are taking, bound within the Sidereal conjunctions of the constellations, and instructing the pattern spiders to weave in those processes and outcomes.

                                                        A god who plans the day and the moment when some mortal dies isn't really interfering in anything, because somebody writing out the day and cause of your death is just how things die, and that person is mortal.

                                                        It becomes interference when it becomes about not sticking to operational procedures and instead inserting personal agendas.

                                                        Which gods have probably always done to some extent, possibly even by necessity to keep things actually running smoothly, because it's not actually possible to have a complex system consisting of large numbers of individuals follow the rules all of the time, both in the sense of "people won't be so rigid" and "there are too many unpredictable variables that people will need to break rules to fix".

                                                        (Although the need for improvisation and cheating when things aren't going by the book is probably largely supervised by Sidereal Exalted, at least for the important things)

                                                        People talk up "Creation-Ruling Mandate" a lot, and many books don't help, but Games of Divinity was clear that even before the Usurpation there were various levels of gods not following the rules.

                                                        But yeah, non-interference is supposed to mean things like Ahlat not favouring the side that makes the biggest cattle sacrifice and not taking the souls of fallen warriors to adorn his cloak, not that he never contributes to the overall essence of how certain kinds of battles and wars play out. At least that's how I see it.
