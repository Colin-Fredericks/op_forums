    Actually my first second edition character for that matter, played an Alchemical, Lunar and a Solar in the first edition.

    So as someone who read most of the first edition stuff, I'm pretty good as far as the lore goes. Well, excluding any changes from 1st to 2nd, of course.

    Anyway, I was wondering if anyone had helpful information on what to pay attention to when building a Sidereal. This is the first exalted game I've played in about 6 years, and the majority of my memory is "martial arts and dodge are both great and vital."

    Almost every link I could find is from the now defunct/hopefully reemerging Wiki or otherwise so old as to be dead. Kind of frustrating, but I suppose it happens when they get ready for the new edition. So any information would be appreciated!

      The thing with Sidereal abilities is that they have a great deal of cross pollination. Their Melee charms work with Martial Arts, and one of their best social attacks is in Larceny, for example. So, as long as you take a pretty decent spread of abilities, you'll be good, for the most part.

      That said, Martial Arts and Dodge are both vital. Martial arts helps with the closed charm set, and Duck Fate works on ANYTHING.

      If you gave some more information on what kind of character you were intending to play, I could give you some more info. Speaking in generalizations, favoring a wide range of abilities is about as much advice as I can give.

          Dabble in some martial arts, a siddie should always know ~SOME~ kung fu, but other than that, it doesn't matter how you take your siddie. How you act is more important than how you're built ! One of the most important and hardest to grasp challenges is seeming to be in control and in the know of all the things at all times. Ideally, you should be as prepared for a situation as possible and pass off every coincidental success as part of being of the plan. Get used to thinking about everything sideways. Be the http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.ph...TheChessmaster

          I'll give you a fun example in a mixed environment chat server I played on. After a big snaffu, some starmetal fell from the heavens over the populated district of Whitewall, controlled by the syndics and well known supporters of the gold faction. As a bronze chosen of serenity, it was my job to secure as much of the precious stuff as possible while the dragon blooded envoys and solars there fought over things. There was technically a abyssal caste in the mix too, but that was a setting specific detail, a sort of renegade black guard loyal to the syndics. Regardless, using favors I hadn't yet earned in exchange for the star metal I was about to collect I got myself a Pectoral of Resplendent Speech and a Celestial Lion escort, along with an Oricalcum Infinite Resplendence Amulet (conned off of Plentimon in a "fair" bet) I set the stage for a grand and elaborate farce ! Utilizing Arcane fate's ability to disguise yourself so trivially, I adopted a "mundane" disguise and descended from the sky atop the lion to act as a representative of the heavens in order to soothe the panicked masses at the mini meteor shower raining from on high. The Lion, by no means in my (or anyone else's) pocket, was there merely as an impartial NPC tasked with my defense should things turn violent and to ensure the star metal promised to heaven was delivered as I had promised (and secretly to add credence to my diefic farce).

          With a little manipulative phrasing, I had the players of the solars and abbysal squabbling over the largest chunk of the star metal while I went off and manipulated the pacified crowd (thanks to my performance) into collecting the far greater wealth of metal. The solaroids, as expected, broke into squabbling and infighting under the eye of a watchful Lion and refused to return the property to heaven. Once the fight broke out I faded to the background and let them take the negative spotlight in the eyes of the people. In doing so, mostly through planning, some suggestion and one performance roll, I achieved the following goals:
          - I performed my role as a chosen of Serenity by ensuring the peace and safety of hundreds in the light of a supernatural event.
          - I got far more star metal than anyone else competing for the bounty, hence earning political favors
          - I snubbed the nose of the syndics for showing up in their city with such a public display, and did so in a fashion that would either expose their identity (which was suspect at this point in the game) or run contrary to their core nature if they tried to stop me.
          - I earned a special place in a celestial censor's heart for the solars, a place reserved for child molesters and people who talk at the theater. Big plus given a bronze faction.
          - Bonus: my elaborate farce as a god was similar enough to an existing god, the divine mother of a god-blooded friend, that when arcane fate kicked in any prayers for the appearance would swing her way and fit within her portfolio quite nicely !

          This is what I consider to be a huge win. With only a little nudging and a show, I manipulated the situation into several gains and had contingencies just in case it all went to hell. This is how your scenes should go: A lot of planning, using the full resources of the heavens to your advantage, approaching the problem from odd angles and working toward more than one agenda. Don't forget you work for the most powerful network of spies ever to exist and you personally know the movers and shakers that manipulate creation through yu-shaun: use it !

          Also: Make sure to put some background dots into retainer gods to do your paperwork >.> there's always paperwork and someone's gotta do it, why should it be you ?

              My Sidereal-Fu is weaker than I'd like, but Noodle's advice is good.
              EDIT: As is kawaiiwolf''s, which has appropriately Sidereal'd me.

              From a more practical perspective, one thing you'll want to have very clear is whether you're playing 2e by the printed books, or using the Scroll of Errata (aka 2.5). This matters to the mechanics of every splat, but none more so than Sidereals (with the possible exception of the Fair Folk). Basically, the 2e Sidereal hardback was not handled especially well. For instance, many of their Charms were just copy+pasted from 1e, without consideration for changes in the overall system. The Scroll of Errata, written by the current EX3 writing team, gets every Charm completely up to snuff. If you're not using 2.5 -- which is especially plausible if there are Infernals in the group -- then you may want to use Loom 2.0 instead (written by one of the big writers on the Sidereal Errata back when he was just a fan).

                  I'm burnt out from final exams, but let me try to throw out a couple of three things:

                  1) While kawaiiwolf is giving you the 'traditional' Sidereal chess master, don't feel that you're limited to that style of play. If there's something strange in the neighborhood (like a demon), and you want to beat its face Iron-Siaka-style, you can do that. Don't listen to the haters; pick up your starmetal reaper daiklave and proceed to beat face.

                  2) You really have to go all over the Sidereal charmset and Astrology to find things. If we're talking about combat, here are a few places to look:
                  • Resistance: Optimistic Security Practice and Shield of Destiny... great charms for helping you not die, and Shield of Destiny is all about what a fight against a Sidereal should be (that is to say, shit is going to get weird for the opponent)
                  • Craft: World Shaping Artistic Vision... go with swords and work a symphony with your daiklave (through the magic of lower TNs)
                  • Dodge: Absence, Avoidance Kata, Duck Fate... because Sidereal... these are high-priority charms to keep you alive in general
                  • Awareness: Prior Warning, Expected Pain... because Sidereal... more future-looking shenanigans to keep you alive
                  • Integrity: (Virtue) Essence Replenishment... good for smallish Sidereal mote pools
                  • Martial Arts: Violet Bier of Sorrows... it's a fun style and very Sidereal...
                  • Medicine: One of the best Sidereal charms for surviving a fight is at the top of the tree
                  • Astrology (Resplendencies):
                    • Heroe's Iron Skin under The Shield... +9L/B non-armor soak for the scene... yes
                    • Dignity of the Dead under The Corpse... your perfect social defense
                  3) For a few more Sidereal shenanigans, consider:
                  • Investigation: Efficient Secretary Technique... because I know kung fu (do references to the Matrix now count as dated? Am I old!?)
                  • Larceny: Lots of Sidereal weirdness lives here... sometimes, you just have to steal someone's name... also Sidereal Shell Games is another instance of combat against a Sidereal getting weird... in a good way.
                  • Stealth: Walking Outside Fate... so you can be sneaky
                  4) I'm assuming that you're using 2.5 errata. Also, note that most of the charms I mention are relatively low on their respective charm trees (as is the way of Sidereals)

                  5) TN reduction is powerful and very Sidereal... do it when you can

                  6) Don't ignore Astrology. The rules are a bit of a pain (look to Shards of the Exalted Dream for a simplified system), but you can do some cool things with it if your group/ST is willing to work with you.

                      Chessmastery is fun, but I'll be the first to tell you that it's exhausting. Great when it's pulled off successfully but you put a lot of mental effort into getting it to that state. There are other ways to go about things, however, if you want to "Beat it's face", you still have to be smart about it. Realize that in a lot of straight up fights, you're at a disadvantage, which is why you cheat, and a lot of your charms facilitate this. Your kung fu is strong, but you won't outlast a lunar or overpower a solar.

                      A note regarding excellencies: At first it might seem like your first excellency is quite weak, being capped by your essence. Fateful Exellencies change this however, by lowering your target numbers (making each die that much more effective) or simply convert dice to successes: Using your manipulation of fate to cheat away any chance that you perform less than you could have. At this point, first exellencies become more cost effective per mote than second, although second has better potential when used alone.

                      Astrology is strong ! If you build for it you can use it to great effect, if you don't it becomes an excercise in unnecessary accounting. You should work with your ST to see if you can't simplify the process. Petitioning friends, getting cosignatories and a countersignitory, composing plans and computing horoscopes while gathering a cabal to ritualized the affair to gain sufficient access to your spat specific powers can make for an interesting adventure .. but after the first time this trail of paperwork becomes just that: unnecessary paperwork. If you think I'm exaggerating, take a look at Siddies p207. That kind of bureaucracy loses its appeal midway through the first attempt at it for me.


                      Regardless, the goal is to have fun ! Your approach to problems should very infrequently be head on. Get used to thinking sideways about things; it's part of the reason that the charm sets are set up so bizarrely ! Their charms are designed to be versitile and applicable to more than one situation, but not a direct answer to a problem. Some examples: Their charms for hiding lies instead gets others to believe that the truth is false ("Psh, yeah ... I don't belong here, I'm probably just here to steal all your secrets, look, I even have the same costume as you. Really ? .. have you been hitting the qat too hard ?") an invisibility charm that functions by virtue of social bearings (in bureaucracy I might add, "Ohh him, he cleans chamberpots, ignore him") or shunting off an unfortunate fate, such as poison or a pesky stab wound, to someone else; you didn't block that attack, you were stabbed, it just so happened that the consequence of that got written to someone else's fate instead of yours.

                      Also when consulting charms, I've found it's best to reffer to the scroll of errata BEFORE the core book, as stated earlier, siddie charms were given a major overhaul in the errata. Also yes, "Efficient Secretary Technique" is a must have ^^ It's one of those things that lets you know all the little details. Ohh you know that guy's uncle, who's a baker who happens to be suffering a case of the gout ? How'd you know that, well, you've got a magical little friend who can google all those pesky details for you when you've only got seconds to come up with a convincing excuse.

                                  It's hard to give general advice because it really depends on the character.

                                  I have a 2.5 Sidereal character. A socialite who has virtually no combat skill. Ie no Martial Arts, no Melee, no Dodge. (Well, partially untrue. I recently picked up a couple Firearms Charms. But for the majority of this character's existence, she's had jack shit for offensive capability). That said, she's quite good at what she does. Sidereal Performance, Presence, Bureaucracy, and Socialize all have extremely good Charms.

                                  Performance has things like, as kawaiiwolf said, Heart-Brightening Presentation Style, allowing for cross-use of Excellencies. It also has Perfection in Life, a nice all-around social Charm, Song of Spirit Persuasion which is amazing toward any spirit, and the ridiculously powerful Harmonic Completion. Oh, and of course how could I forget Defense of Shining Joy, allowing the socialite to pull off a very good DDV despite having no Dodge.

                                  Socialize has the classic Shun the Smiling Lady, along with Life Without Compuction which allows you and your friends to get away with nearly anything socially. You and Yours Stance is a good combat and non-combat Charm, and Cash and Murder Games is a potent UMI. Fortuitous Fellowship is great during downtime. Bureaucracy has Icy Hand which is cheap and useful, Underling Invisibility Practice to splash into stealth, and Terminal Sanction to fuck up gods and demons.

                                  Presence has the ever-useful Force Decision if you're a bit creative with it, and the extremely powerful and subtle Presence in Absence Technique. Easily Accepted Proposition Stance is crazy as basically a deus ex machina power.

                                  As for other Charms I've dipped into: Optimistic Security Practice in Resistance is easy to get and provides good soak and UMI combat defense. Efficient Secretary Technique is amazing as others have said. I've also gotten great use out of Auspicious Prospects for (Caste), though it's a little hard to parse and your ST has to be good with it. The early Survival Charms are also fun, as Adopting the Untamed Face allows you to speak to and command animals, and Becoming the Wilderness negates a lot of environmental penalties.

                                  ---

                                  One general thing I'd like to mention is Excellencies. You can buy up to (Essence) dice with the First, or (Essence) successes with the Second. The Third is a reroll for 3m. You'd think that makes the Second the best, but the First has its place as synergizing with the Fateful Excellency, effectively turning into 1m per success when you go with an auspicious success. Keep that in mind.

                                  ---

                                  Oh, and finally: Don't forget about Resplendencies! You can have up to (Essence) of them at a time, and each one essentially gives you three Charms that never use motes but have a limited pool of points of their own. If you've got a good Performance (ie the Fateful Performance Excellency and a good pool) you can get tons of endurance points and have some very good extra powers at your disposal. For example, summoning the wind of Adorjan, shapeshifting into a bird, negating any social attacks, getting big soak buffs, Training effects, stealth effects of all kinds, and non-Charm dice bonuses to many things.

                                  ---

                                  Also on the subject of Astrology, I like Simple Astrology from Shards. It might not make it easier, but it definitely makes it simpler.

                                          I'm hardly the most system-savvy person out there, but I've been playing a Battles in the same game as Limited Reagent for, um, a while now, so I do have some insights.

                                          - WATCH YOUR MOTES! As best I've been able to find, by Raw, Sidereals have no mote pool expansion charm. You'll need to think very hard about any charm use that costs more than the 4m you can recover with careful stunting, and even harder about whether the benefits of that artifact you're eyeing are really worth the bite out of your limited pool.

                                          - Connected to the above, and to the nature of the Sidereal Ox Body, your survival state is always going to be binary - you're either fine, or you're toast. Bring a panic button; I use Duck Fate.

                                          - Sidereal Firearms can bring the pain. Steel and Smoke Mandala (the Firearms multiattack charm) is expensive, but worth it if you've got a sufficient firearms pool to use it unexcellencied. Combining it with Storm Clouds and Ravens Prophecy (lowers the target number of damage rolls) and an excellency to increase damage dice, it's quite possible to wipe out entire groups of significant opponents in one round. Although trying to leverage this all the way will run you flat out of motes, see first bullet point above.

                                          - Excellencies. If you can get it, go for the 4th. If there's any charm that sums up Siderealness in my mind, it's their Fourth Excellency, and it's pretty mote efficient to boot.

                                          - Emperically determined by my own biases (I hate wading through page after page of nitpicky charm writeups), you can get a long way with a Sid on nothing but a handful of excellencies and a relentless concentration on Ability dots.
