                        Sidereal are tied heavily to the idea of the Loom of Fate and working to control destiny If you tried say, dropping them into (I don't know) some Forgotten Realms-esque setting, there's going to be some issues there because you'd also have to import the entire concept of Fate, Colleges, and all the other stuff that their mechanics key off of if you want the full "Sidereal experience."

                        Fair Folk are tied fairly heavily to the Wyld, so if you dropped them into a setting without something comparable to the Wyld, you'll again run into issues, though it's possible for them to skate by via harvesting gossamer from dreams and in 2.5 most of their charms work in Creation and the Wyld. Still, all of their Wyld-only charms would be totally useless in a setting without the Wyld.

                        Alchemicals are tied to Autochthon in a fairly similar way (to the point that often times just bringing them into Creation proper raises certain issues) - they require taking their infrastructure with them. So if you bring Alchemicals into another setting, they'll also need to bring all of their steam-punk-magical-technology to create Vats, new charms, etc.

                        Compare that to Solars or Abyssals or Dragon Blooded. You can drop them into pretty much any other setting, without worrying nearly as much that their mechanics are drawing on setting material unique to Creation.

                        Basically Sidereal, Alchemicals and Fair Folk mechanics are heavily linked to setting-specific elements (Fate, Autochthon, and the Wyld respectively) that you would need to create analogues for those elements in your new setting, if you wanted them to retain the flavor that they are supposed to evoke.

                        I mean, you could certainly make a setting without the concept of Fate, without colleges linked to specific star formations and have Sidereal without those elements, but you'll be cutting out a fair amount of their character and power. And the same is true for Fair Folk without the Wyld, or Alchemicals without their Vats and such.

                                    Just stop and look at how different Alchemical Exalted are in the Modern Shard vs how they are officially. They had to be changed rather dramatically to fit in a setting that didn't have any of the magical steampunk elements or Autochthon.

                                    If you introduce Sidereals (or Fair Folk) into a world without the setting elements that form a core part of their mechanics and themes, you'll either have to do similar rewrites to them, to strip those themes and mechanics out, or you'll have to rewrite the new setting to include the things that will allow the Sidereal or Fair Folk to work as they normally do.

                                    Again, Modern Alchemicals is the perfect example of this. You can drop many other Exalted (like Solars or Abyssals or whatever) into new settings and only need to make a few cursory changes (change out the Holy keyword, or let Abyssals regenerate motes normally). Dropping Alchemicals into a new setting requires much more profound changes (as seen in Shards), and the same is generally true for Sidereal and Fair Folk.
