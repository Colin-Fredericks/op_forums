    Originally posted by Gayo View Post

    SMA did this a bit, because SMA were always terrible. But most people played below that power level and it didn't come up. Aside from that...well, I respect many of his insights and ideals, but Chung is one of those people who thinks that the "sensible" thing for Sids to do is to constantly spam Shun the Smiling Lady on everyone they dislike, so that's the angle he's approaching them from. Above all else, the #1 thing responsible for encouraging paranoia combat and filter builds in Exalted was Chung himself.

    There's a genre of person (not necessarily Chung here) who goes absolutely apoplectic about stuff like Avoidance Kata because it violates their sense of fairness and how conflict ought to play out. Rather than arguing directly from this stance, which is difficult and puts their feelings and preferences on display, they justify their distaste with arguments that take the most extreme and abusive interpretation of the charm they can possibly contrive, positing that not only is this interpretation obviously correct, but it will define all dealings with Sidereals everywhere in the game. This is, by and large, the kind of person who has traditionally been unhappy about Sidereals' supposed game-warping effects, and who has driven the fandom meme about unstoppable Sidereal hit squads ruining everything.
    What kind of weird, side-ways, group-attack, BADWRONGFUN post is this?

    Chung and people that share his game style preferences like to believe that there is an active living world outside the Player Characters. In their style of game it's not about the Storyteller and Players weaving a narrative together, it's the Storyteller managing the world sandbox and reacting to the actions of the Players. In that style of game if a player character is hit by Shun the Smiling Lady, then that is not a plot hook to investigate some sidereal conspiracy, that is just any of the 100 Sidereals deciding the punish the PC for something he did. Sidereal charms often ruin this style of play which is why Sidereals are often either ignored completely or hit with a nerfbat.

    In third edition this style of play will be explicitly unsupported however in first and second edition it was supported except for Sidereals.

            Jon Chung is a "simulationist" true.

                    For Jon Chung's specific comments

                    On how much 1e Sidereals is to blame for 2e:

                    http://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php?...its-peak/page3

                    Originally posted by Chung
                    "on Sidereals: Some people think Sidereals was the pinnacle of the line, and I still think it's the best standalone subgame in 1E, but I saw in that book's mechanics the foundation of what would later turn into some of the more egregious problems in 2E, as well as a highly annoying set of assumptions as to what the GM role was required to do regarding NPC usage of their RAW capabilities.

                    Scenelong full pool combat dice adders and dirt-cheap absolute defenses hiding behind a passive filter. High-essence geometric inceases in charm power progression making the Elminster problem look trivial. Easily accessed and consequence-free "fuck you from orbit" buttons that expect the GM to solve the natural NPC-on-PC usage issue of such things by shrugging and fiat. Feh."
                    That's an argument where, like I've said upthread, I think it's arguable, but questionable to what extent dirt-cheap absolute defenses exist in 2e as a result of 1e Sidereals.

                    Instead, they might just what happens when you have the level of damage that 2e had, and the insufficiency of passive defenses 2e had, by the very nature of DV, and last but not least, the fandom's glorification of the "statement" that perfect defenses made.

                    On the Sidereals Charms within their own context:

                    http://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php?...its-peak/page6

                    Originally posted by Chung
                    Excellent, possibly the best subgame out of the set, defining this as an all-Sidereal game, sure
                    ...
                    Looking purely at mechanics, the main problem was introduction of mechanics with no equivalents beyond learning said mechanics yourself (a thing the GM is supposed to heavily discourage) or inventing a custom equivalent (and/or counter-mechanic/defense) in your native sets and trying to get it designed properly and past your GM, a highly undesirable situation. This is a general trend, of which Blade of the Battle Maiden was merely an example.
                    And also :

                    Originally posted by Chung
                    Mechanically speaking, when looked at as a general MA Charm for everybody, yes, it was. This is a matter of observation, not opinion. Solar, Lunar, Abyssal or Dragon-Blooded, if you could get it, you took it, because it was just that much better than every other option.

                    Was it balanced as a Sidereal Charm? Arguably. But it wasn't a Sidereal Charm.
                    This is not exactly the idea that the Sidereals' low Essence Charms and strategy broke the game within their own contexts (as I understand it, they weren't either dominant to or less fun than the Solars' stacked persistant plus perfects madness).

                    The general trend of high essence is bad, but again, I see that as inevitable given the fans attitude towards power and elder essence in the game, and Exalted the Sidereals is just a consequence of that.
