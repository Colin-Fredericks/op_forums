    Hi Jen,

    I think that when people consider antagonists, they should take into account a rule of thumb: "Very few people consider themselves the villains in their own stories."

    When we talk about the Sidereals, we need to start at the beginning, at what Confucius would call the "rectification of names" (invoking Confucius feels appropriate when talking about the Sidereals anyway). The Sidereal Exalted are the agents of the Five Maidens of Destiny specifically, and of Yu-Shan in general. They're usually chosen as infants or at a relatively young age compared to other Chosen because unlike the others, their Exaltation doesn't depend on making a choice -- in a sense, the Sidereal Exaltation has always been a part of them, in the same way that having two arms and two legs is a part of their bodies.

    You consider the raising and training of Sidereals to be "brainwashing", but maybe that's a perspective that only comes from outside. Consider that the Sidereals are raised in the territory and ideologies of Yu-Shan, and learn at the feet of their sifus. In the 2e corebook, Ahn-Aru was described as "[having] lost those discerning traits that might have once betrayed her Haltan heritage". I think that line describes most of the Sidereals: They consider themselves ethnically of Heaven now, and their immediate clan is made up of the other 99 Chosen of the Maidens. And like everyone else, their ethnicity marks their perspective for the rest of their lives. I'll give you an analogy. To grow up in Vietnam is something that would shape your ideals and perspectives differently than someone who grew up in France, for example. And even if you "rebelled" from your Vietnamese ancestry, it would still mark you forever -- you would still know the Vietnamese language. The stories of the Trung Sisters would still color your perspective on heroism and nationalism.

    The Sidereals don't see themselves as brainwashed. They see themselves as a family. There are so few Sidereals, and they have to spend so much time together, that any given Sidereal likely knows each of the other 99 on a first-name basis. They know each others' names. They know each others' birthdays, hobbies, favorite foods, favorite colors, sexual orientation, fears, motivations, et cetera. The information about them hints at the fact that there's even a big web of romantic and intimate interactions between the Sidereals, too. This means that unlike other Exalted, the Sidereals see themselves as Sidereals first, and all other considerations come after that. So when we look at the dispute between the Gold Faction and the Bronze Faction, it's perhaps inappropriate to see it as a "shadow war". It's more like a difference in political opinions between family members -- the arguments may become heated and ugly, but at the end of the day, a Gold Factioner would happily attend the birthday party of a Bronze Factioner; a Bronze Factioner would buy a package of rare tea for a Gold Factioner who liked that kind. For inspiration, look to the relationship between Supreme Court justices Antonin Scalia and Ruth Bader Ginsburg. The two are on opposite ends of the political spectrum, and their rulings are often bitterly opposed and combative, but in their personal lives, they go to the theater and eat dinner together.

    Chejop may regard the situation in Creation to be too critical to allow Gold Faction Sidereals access to the levers of power in Yu-Shan, but I have no doubt that Chejop genuinely loves the members of the Gold Faction as friends and his children, and if it came down to it, he would do nearly anything to protect them from harm. I think that the Sidereals regard the ronin in the same way. Imagine that your brother or sister said to your family one day, "I'm moving far away because I hate you all. I don't want anything to do with you." You'd be hurt and confused. Angry too, but that anger would come from hurt and confusion, rather than moral outrage. The reaction in Yu-Shan to these prodigals would depend on what kind of Sidereal you're talking about: More direct ones would want to talk with their wayward siblings to figure out what went wrong, and the pensive ones would give the ronin their space to let them work through their issues. But either way, the reaction would have its roots in familial compassion. So when would the Bureau of Destiny send a hit-squad of Sidereals after a ronin? When answering that, consider when you would take the horrible and drastic step of shooting your brother or sister. Would you shoot them for shoplifting? No. Would you shoot them for joining a questionable political party? No. Would you shoot them if they were about to bomb a school? The Sidereals only kill their own when they find them about to do something so breathtakingly evil (from the Sidereals' perspective) that it threatens the stability of Creation. But when you're writing a story, ask yourself if a Solar and a Sidereal (or a Sidereal and any other Exalt) would consider the same acts to be "Creation-threatening".

    But how do Sidereals interact with Creation? We're inclined to see the Sidereals as mustache-twirling villains or Gestapo, but that's from the perspective of most Solars and Lunars, who have a bone to pick with the Chosen of the Maidens. I think that eight times out of ten, the Sidereals are quietly maintaining the stability of Creation, doing real, tangible good for the people. Journeys maintain Creation's communication and transport, ensuring that existence doesn't dissolve into petty fiefdoms easily conquered by outside forces. Serenities cultivate harmony, peace, and goodwill among the people to prevent the spread of nihilistic or self-destructive practices. Battles actively take the fight to the enemies of Creation, like the Deathlords and the Rakshas. Secrets maintain stockpiles of information so that Creation never forgets its failures, which motivate it, or the victories, which give it new weapons against its foes. And Endings assassinate tyrannical beings (as well as organizations and concepts) that have become rotten and need to be pruned away from the healthy "tree" of Creation. Eight times out of ten, they're behind the scenes, doing the thankless work that keeps Creation running, but as talented as they are, the Chosen of the Maidens are overworked, irritated, and frequently emotionally exhausted, because 100 of them have to contend with millions of gods, billions of people, and hundreds of thousands of miles of Creation. Those who could aid them are either gone (the Solars), enemies (Lunars and Abyssals), incompetent (the Realm), or too caught up their petty matters to care (the lion's share of the Celestial Gods, including most of the Incarnae). In those last two times out of ten, people tend to see the Sidereals as enemies because the Sidereals regard most other Exalted as dangerous. But from the Sidereals' perspective, they're just being reasonable: The returning Solars are tearing down stability that took centuries to build. The Abyssals are burning Creation to the ground. The Terrestrials are mere foot-soldiers who shouldn't do anything more than serve a greater good. The Infernals are the servants of unimaginable, insane ur-gods. The Alchemicals are communist robots from another dimension. Even the Serenities would be hard-pressed to see those archetypes and consider them positive for Creation's long-term stability. So the Chosen of the Maidens do their job. And their job is to protect existence from threats.

    Still, that gets to the heart of the Sidereals' strengths and weaknesses. The strength of the Sidereals is that they're a family. You fight one Sidereal, and you fight them all. The weakness of the Sidereals is that they're a family. They see Chejop as their metaphorical father, and they give him filial devotion regardless of his wisdom in this matter. Even the Gold Faction refuses to openly fight against Chejop, even when the safety of Creation may demand that they should. The Siderals probably should be openly asking dangerous questions about the Usurpation and Lytek. They probably should be confronting the Incarnae about their addiction to the Games of Divinity. They probably should be defying tradition to offer overtures of peace to the Solars, Lunars, and maybe even the Alchemicals. But their paterfamilias has put his foot down, and the tragedy of Yu-Shan is that so few of the Sidereals are willing to defy him.

            I think Kalli's got it spot on as far as the 'brainwashing' goes.

            Not to mention....Siddies have the potential to be the absolute longest lived of any of the Exalted (something like 5000 years). So....18-25 years of brainwashing. Several thousand years by which to form your own opinions on the world, while possessing powers that basically let you go 'lolnope' to anything that tries to force you to change your mind.

            Granted, when I write or play around with Sidereals, some of them are true believers in the factions, to the point of fanaticism...others are more casual about their commitment (Y'know...like people actually are). Yeah, they think the Solars are bad news, but they aren't going to hurt a fellow Sidereal over it, and they aren't going to let it interfere with their job. Sure, if they see an opportunity to make life miserable for a Solar, or get them killed or driven off, they'll take it. But just because they disagree with Waving Palm over there in the Gold Faction doesn't mean he's going to sabotage Waving Palm's work...that might make him miss their planned Gateway game! I do try to portray characters in a reasonably realistic and intelligent fashion...Level 1 Intelligent, at least.

            Yes, they are very close-knit...they are the only 'humans' in all Creation that can reliably remember each other. Other than that, their companions are Celestial gods. If you were a being that would live for thousands of years...and there were only a max of 100 other people who could reliably remember you for more than a few days...wouldn't you tend to gravitate towards them?

            To first address the 'killing each other in the streets' comment...see Page 30 of MoEP: Sidereals. The way it talks about inter-faction sabotage makes no mention of outright murder. Sidereals are far too valuable to kill. To quote the book directly: "Physical confrontation is absurd, particularly within the gaze of other Sidereals or anywhere in Heaven." The 'nuclear option' for Siddies who hate each other is to strike at the rival Sidereal's loved ones. Killing each other? Madness! Trusting Siddies to not kill their rivals is a plain enough sentiment that the Chair of the Convention of Fire (Nazri) takes great delight in sending Sidereals from opposing factions out on missions together, then interviewing them separately afterward to make sure nothing happened. If a Sidereal murder happens in Heaven, you're facing an immediate Criminal Audit for a Severity 4 or 5 Offense. Sidereals killing each other is such a massive deal that the gods in the BoD STILL talk about the Night of Fallen Stars, when five Sidereals were outright murdered because they tried to warn the Solars of the Usurpation.

            Now...if you want to play a Sidereal that was not raised by the Siddies, that is explicitly allowed for in the MoEP: Siddy, page 49. It talks about how, commonly, a Sidereal's death can be predicted and their replacement identified. And to quote...

            In recent years, however, too many Sidereals have died unexpectedly, centuries of millennia before their ordained times. In such cases, the Five-Score Fellowship often has no time to target the Exalt-to-be for preliminary training. In a few cases, the deaths were so unexpected and so soon in the Sidereal's lifespans that the inheritors cannot be identified at all and the newly Exalted Sidereals run amonk, ignorantly displaying their powers and drawing the unwanted attention of the Wyld Hunt--which treats them just as any other Anaethema.
            Right there, you can see the allowance made for Sidereals who gained their powers without any preliminary training. Naturally, if they survive long enough for the Sidereals to locate them, expect a circle to be dispatched to try and recruit them. In general, this is not a hard thing to do. Every mortal you know has started forgetting that you exist, all your stuff is going missing and your house just got sold because the deed vanished. Then someone shows up out of the blue with the same powers that you have, can explain what you are going through, and offers to train you, let you live in a mansion in heaven, pay you tons of money, and give you tremendous amounts of power. You may say no the first time...but they'll give you advice on how to keep yourself hidden from the Wyld Hunt, give you a bit of cash to get by on for now, and tell you they'll come see you again soon. How long are you going to keep turning them down?

            The only point at which an Ronin may be executed is if they become a threat to Reality. A Sidereal aligning with the deathlords or the Yozi, for example, would probably be contacted to see what the hell they were doing (it might be a clever plan to sabotage their efforts. This is probably why no other Siddie tries to stop The Green Lady, they aren't certain she's turned on them.) and possibly charged with treason against Heaven in-absentee...and sentenced to execution. Then the Reckoners get sent out.

            A Siddy working with a Solar (as your post implied)? Well, Bronze Faction may still try to get the Solar killed, or manipulate things around such that the Sidereal gets to "see the Solar's true colors" and abandons them, or otherwise makes life harder on them...but you just don't kill another Sidereal. It just isn't done except in exceptional circumstances. Add on that the Gold Faction would actively be trying to protect this Sidereal...talk them into joining them, convince them to be the 'power behind the throne', try to lure their Solar into the Cult of the Illuminated, and so on.

                                Originally posted by Kallipolis View Post
                                Hi Jen,
                                unlike the others, their Exaltation doesn't depend on making a choice -- in a sense, the Sidereal Exaltation has always been a part of them, in the same way that having two arms and two legs is a part of their bodies.
                                Yes, exactly. It's not like you walk up to a random teenager and say, 'hey, come and fight terrorists with us'. Sidereals are very clearly distinct* from normal people. They have a subconscious knowledge of fate, of how to manipulate people, of their unearthly identity- even before Exalting. No Sidereal actually wants to stay on the farm and plow fields for the rest of their life. They're not like you or me- we've put down roots, we love our mundane lives, we don't (usually) feel that urge, that inexplicable pull to go, to do big things, to be someone more than we are. We are complete, content. If someone offered us radical change, we would refuse it if only because it's change.
                                Sidereals aren't. They are chrysalae, adolescent. They haven't yet become what they are, and they know it, and feel restless. A Sidereal may love his family, a Sidereal may love their farm, but they don't belong there.
                                And then the time comes, and everything falls into place. They have always felt different- not because of egoism or oppression, but because they are. The Exaltation proves this.
                                And now they get to meet, for the first time, people who are like them. Who can see exactly how important fate is, how it was always influencing them and everyone else. Who give them knowledge beyond what any human will ever get or be capable of using. Who are perpetually curious, always watching, always tinkering with physics, always manipulating others- and yet so utterly dedicated to the grand design, the only work really worthy of their powers and personalities.
                                Fate has always been directing Sidereals toward a goal, and now they can see what that goal is. It may be hard to achieve that goal, it may be exhausting, but these are Sidereals- they were literally born for heroism. When the opportunity comes for that heroism, they don't falter like normal people would.

                                * not better or worse, but different

                                            I'm Sid til I die, so I'm hardly unbiased, but I Think Sids are the least brainwashed faction in Creation. They regularly hobnob with enemy gods, ancient supervisors and endless paperwork yet walk the mortal lands just as often. Sure, they see everything through the lens of workaholic masterminds spinning the plots and counterplots that keep Creation unblowed-the-hell-up like an An Teng juggler spinning plates on spears, but Kejak doesn't brainwash his fellow Viziers--he expects the rightness of his vision to do all the convincing for him, eventually. (This in no way prevents him from giving the more annoying missions to factional fence-sitters, of course.)

                                            Sidereals don't have any misconceptions about Solar rule, Lunar stewardship, the victory over the Primordials, the First Age, the Usurpation, or the Immaculate Philosophy. Bronzes are sure that they did the right thing, Golds not so much, but that's not brainwashing, but an effect of the Great Curse.

                                            As for the "indoctrinated in childhood" part, remember that there are only 100 Sidereals at most, less the un-Exalted child that's carrying the untriggered Exaltation. Sidereals run missions, engage in politics to stay afloat, serve on multiple committees, and get contract-mandated vacations. The absolutely do not have time to play Mary Poppins to each pre-Sidereal, and no Sid worth her salt gods would consider handing over indoctrinating duties to some god, elemental, or demon-- that's waaaaay too much influence for a non-Sidereal to be trusted with. Besides, flexibility of mind, as well as of morals and ethics, are crucial to the whole "Viziers running missions" business model being effective. Rigid, doctrinaire thinking would doom them faster than any other Exalt splat, something that any comparison of Charmsets (and dice-caps) will quickly bear out.

                                            The "undergrad" period of a pre-Sidereal's life is probably designed to ensure certain core competencies (remember, every Sidereal has a job s/he's expected to step into as soon as their Exaltation hits), to prevent them from becoming dependent on a tight-knit social circle for their well-being (since Arcane Fate will traumatically sever any such interconnections), and inculcating the service mentality necessary to get a newly-Exalted demigod to show up to Convention meetings, submit paperwork, and accept that occasional explosive comas inflicted by invisible omni-spiders are just part of the job.

                                            Oh, and prepping them so that when they are first taken to witness the Untempered Schism-- er, the Loom of Fate in all its terrifying glory, they don't psychologically snap and become driven mad by the sound of drums, or somesuch.
