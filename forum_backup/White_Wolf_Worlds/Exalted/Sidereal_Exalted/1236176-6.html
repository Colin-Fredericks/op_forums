                Originally posted by Verzio View Post
                The problem with treating it as a completely dead issue is that the setting has characters on both sides who lived through it -- thanks to the Elder Lunars. Even if the Sidereals are satisfied with "We all thought it was for the best, but now some of us are having second thoughts", the Lunars certainly aren't. Whether the Usurpation was heroic or villainous needs to be something debatable for setting purposes, or else you're declaring the default/majority faction for a PC splat to be unambiguously wrong.
                The Elder Sidereals are an even more important issue. As a Sidereal "what was this choice, why was it made, and what does it mean" is often going to be a vitally important question because it still defines the Sidereals in the present day. It's meant to speak to their themes in the same way the position of their previous incarnation in the First Age speaks to Solar themes, or the Shogunate speaks to Dragon-Blooded themes. Is it going to be relevant in every single game? No. But it's absurd to say "oh, it's just a dead issue."

                Here in the real world, with people who live far shorter lives, no magical reincarnation, nobody still alive from that era and so on, there are still plenty of people who care very very intensely about events that happened hundreds or even thousands of years ago. I just don't see how you can say that "what sort of choice did the Sidereals have, exactly, and what did it mean to them?" isn't an important question.

                And, on top of that, as a Sidereal you have to answer the question of "what did my previous incarnation think of the Usurpation?" Disagreeing with the choices your past incarnation made are pretty common for the Exalted, but I still think it is absolutely, 100% essential that PCs have the option to say "no, my previous incarnation didn't go along with it." Establishing that there were no Sidereals who rejected the Usurpation takes that option off the table and therefore has serious implications for any Sidereal PC who cares a lot about their previous incarnations - and sets a meaningful constraint on what stories you can tell.

                (Thinking about it, I suspect the latter issue is the main reason the books unambiguously established that there were Sidereals who didn't go along with it.)

                        A major reason that I like the premise of the Gold Faction as a relatively new thing, under the terms that writers have often referred to the idea, is that I like the idea that the condition of the prophecy in which a saved Creation would be diminished is something that has been examined and questioned within the setting, and ultimately judged to be not worth it.

                        In retrospect, I feel as though when the only voice for the Bronze ideology are people that more-or-less accept the diminishment whole-heartedly, it makes the whole situation look obtuse and incompetent, particularly in the face of things like the Great Contagion. I'm not put off by that solely because it prompts people to form conclusions that the Bronze Faction is basically evil (and stupid to boot), although those are a significant part of it.

                        Mostly, I think the Gold Faction is more interesting if it consists not of people on the sidelines tutting at their colleagues and repeatedly remanding them with "I told you so"s, with the security of a clearly defined alternate path to which they've been attached for a thousand years, but of people who once went along with the party line, some out of genuine belief and some out of group solidarity, who have seen the results, and come to the conclusion that no, a diminished Creation is not an acceptable end state.

                        That's not to say that no part of the prophecy had the high-risk idea of helping to redeem the Solars.
