    Exalted 2.5E — Custom Sidereal Charm: Invocation of Zen-Mu

    01-13-2018, 12:46 PM
    What follows is my first custom Sidereal Charm. It was inspired by the subtle, yet significant mechanical differences between White-Wolf’s various d10 games. I hoped that a Charm that forced an opponent to play under the most restrictive rules from each WW d10 game system (OWoD, NWoD, Exalted 1e, Exalted 2e, Scion 1e) might have both a solid enough effect and Borgstromian enough flavor to feel properly Sidereal.

    The result is admittedly klunky, but I do like the flavor. And besides, this Charm is strictly for Exalted 2.5e only, and nobody ever accused 2.5 of being elegant and intuitive. Please let me know what you think.

    ON ZEN-MU, SIDEREALS AND THE SPELL THAT WAS TRADED FOR A CHARM


    Before the Primordials ever wrenched form out of chaos, before the inspiration of Yu Shan and the engineering of Creation, the Ancient Ones dwelt for a time in the realm of Zen-Mu, built as a bulwark against the annoyances of the Wyld’s endless hunger. They eventually grew weary of Zen-Mu, and despair led them to abandon the realm they had built, to seek the Shining Answer elsewhere. Abandoned Zen-Mu became Forsaken Zen-Mu, and then Forgotten Zen-Mu.

    But Zen-Mu yet exists. Ever since the wake of the Time of Cascading Years, the Sidereal Exalted have suspected that the engineering of Creation was informed by lessons learned from some sort of earlier prototype of reality, and their Theanoan statistical modeling supported such theory. Intrigued, they pored over every account of conversation with Autochthon, Gaia, and the lesser souls of the Yozis. Gradually, the Fivescore Fellowship pieced together an understanding of Zen-Mu, its antagonistic metaphysical principles, and how residing there drove the Primordials to despair.

    By the time the Sidereals began to contemplate the Usurpation, the mathemagicians of the Forbidding Manse of Ivy’s school of Sorcery, Dept. 137, were nearing completion of a Sapphire Circle spell that would render a fragment of Creation temporarily coterminous with the laws of Zen-Mu, tainting chance and probability for whatever poor wretch that suffered its effects.

    When the Maiden of Secrets herself manifested before the Sorcerers, demanding that they cease all such research and surrender the unfinished spell to her, one among the Viziers was ready. Prydonian Scholar, widely regarded as the craftiest of Jupiter’s own Chosen, had anticipated her objection to crafting such a spell, and was prepared with a counter-offer: Allow her Chosen to manifest the effect as a Sidereal Occult Charm, and they would surrender the spell, along with all memory of how they developed it. Thus, the ability to summon Zen-Mu into Creation would remain only with the Sidereals.

    For her own reasons, the Maiden of Secrets agreed Thus was born the Charm: Invocation of Zen-Mu.

    Only a few Sidereals were taught the Charm prior to the Usurpation, and in the chaos that followed the Breaking of the Mask, Jupiter quietly sealed the Invocation of Zen-Mu away from the Sidereal Charm set. All those who had been taught the Charm had perished in the Usurpation, and all knowledge of the Invocation’s existence faded from memory as completely as the spell-turned-Charm fell from use.

    But the Time of Tumult has engulfed Creation. Factions and fellows who have long stood in opposition have begun to relinquish old enmities in the face of threats, both new and resurgent. Mighty beings, long accustomed to unilateral action, have been compelled to seek counsel and share troubling observations with others of like stature. And ancient prohibitions, once held to be absolute, are beginning to be lifted. In particular, revelations regarding the existence and nature of the Infernal Exalted are the things most likely to spur Jupiter to restore to the Sidereals the Charm known as Invocation of Zen-Mu.

    ——————————————————————

    Zen-Mu is not in any way sentient, but it has strong antipathies nonetheless. Just as Creation itself abhors any vacuum, literal, symbolic, or metaphorical, Zen-Mu despises accomplishment. Where most actions in Creation spawn equal, opposite reactions, achievements in Zen-Mu spawn repercussions. Its elements of randomness lead to stagnating sameness, and yet its unyielding laws infect every principle with uncertainty.

    All of this is both by design, and entirely unintended. The Primordials had never before considered working together in any sort of productive harmony, for the very concept was antithetical to their nature. And so the crafting of Zen-Mu, the first joint effort of the mighty Primordials, largely consisted of each of them adding one unyielding principle to its design, without any thought of how their own absolutist metaphysics might interact with the those of their siblings.

    The result was...not what it might have otherwise been.

    The Primordials were beings of the purest power, and yet existence within Zen-Mu drove them to despair, eventually sending them fleeing into the Wyld. And if the Primordials and their most treasured souls could find only misery in Zen-Mu, then the suffering of any lesser being that might labor under that realm’s restrictions would be legendary...even if they were Exalted.

    Invocation of Zen-Mu
    Cost: 15m, 1wp
    Minimums: Essence 5, Occult 5
    Type:Simple

    Keywords: Combo-OK, Obvious, Theanoan
    Duration: One scene
    Prerequisite Charms: Sapphire Circle Sorcery

    The Sidereal brings to mind the metaphysical principles that defined Zen-Mu, visualizing the geometric patterns formed by the interconnections between them. She then rotates this pattern, attempting to find an angle that allows it to fit over the pattern of Creation’s principles.

    The Sidereal rolls (Intelligence + Occult) against a difficulty of (target’s Essence), adding her own Essence in automatic successes. If successful, the user clicks the theoretical model of Zen-Mu’s principles over Creation’s laws, imposing the harsh, unforgiving strictures and demands of that forsaken realm upon her for until her DV has refreshed a number of times equal to the successes rolled. This is not a Shaping effect, and cannot be resisted with Shaping defenses.

    Players whose characters who suffer this Charm’s effect apply a number of changes to how they roll dice, count successes, and interpret results.

    Apply the following changes to every die roll made by a tainted character, including damage rolls:

    * Raise the standard target number of all rolls to [8].
    Reason: In walling themselves off from the Wyld, the Holy Tyrant knew that his brothers and sisters would have only each other to struggle against. Tyrant though he was, he was also their King, and and he alone bore responsibility for the well-being of his subjects. Therefore, he ensured that his siblings’ Primordial might could not be turned against their fellows without focus, effort, and intent.

    * Count any die that rolls a [10] as only one success, not two.
    Reason: The Principle of Hierarchy could not countenance the thought of those with less raw ability and inferior skills matching or outperforming their betters due to luck, or mere affinity. Zen-Mu was designed to always give those of greater power the greater chance of success.

    * Every die that rolls [1] subtracts one success from that roll’s total successes.
    Reason: Cecyline knew that she and her siblings were perfect, and therefore the refuge they built would be perfect. As such, she contributed the decree that all attempts to enact change upon such perfection, or even do anything but exist within Zen-Mu’s blessed borders, should be opposed by Zen-Mu itself.

    * If more dice show [1] than [8+] on any roll, that action botches.
    Reason: The Dragon’s Shadow knew he could not attain the transcendent state of antithesis he sought while limited to the stifling confines of Zen-Mu. For his contribution, he poured into Zen-Mu’s design his own hatred of the accomplishments of others, in the hope that the resultant frustrations would eventually drive the Primordial host to abandon it for a more desirable home.

    * Effects that normally grant automatic successes instead add two extra dice per automatic success.
    Reason: Isidoros, who despises the thought of relying upon anything other than his own might, rammed and trampled the conceptual pillars of Zen-Mu until he had beaten all traces of constancy, reliability, and other such impurities out of its very design. Such absolute uncertainty forced its inhabitants to actively pit their might against every challenge that arose, making existence in Zen-Mu a constant series of tests.

    * If an effect allows a re-roll, any dice that showed [1] on the original roll carry over to the re-roll, and are applied to the new roll.
    Reason: To Adrián, the River of All Torments, the thought of abandoning existence on the razor’s edge of chance, action and consequence, in favor of cleaving unto the stale predetermined outcomes of Essence Resurgent was horrifying. Determined to spare her siblings the deprivation of existence without peril, she scoured all elasticity from Zen-Mu’s meta-structure, eliminating any possibility of second chances.

    Note that the universal principles summoned via the Invocation of Zen-Mu override the Sidereal Astrology of Creation. A victim tainted by the Invocation cannot be the target of any Ascending or Descending Destinies, and any pre-existing Destinies are suppressed for the duration of the Charm.

    The Theanoan keyword indicates that only Sidereals who have studied Sorcery under the paradigm of Theano may learn this Charm. Currently, the only place Theanoan Sorcery is taught is Dept. 137 of the Forbidding Manse of Ivy, in Yu Shan.
