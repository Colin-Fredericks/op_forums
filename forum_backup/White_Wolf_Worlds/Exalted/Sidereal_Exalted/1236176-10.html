    Originally posted by Epimetheus
    In most cases, basic laborer would produce easier results.
    I'm mostly coming from a place influenced by a time I read Geoff Grabowski say that the First Age was not dependent on utilising the labour of vast numbers of people. It was a premise that sparked my imagination and drives me to envision scenarios that fulfil it. That and a general sense of trying to picture the anthropology of the setting in which human civilization has always included the Exalted.

    So, as an example, I would imagine how the First Age, in its earliest stages, would have managed some great feats of ancient (or early modern but in a technologically distinct part of the world) engineering, such as canals across the length of China, or mountain road networks connecting the Inca Empire.

    In real life, these things entailed corvee labour; not quite slavery, but still a form of bonded servitude, making providing a bit of time and service in a major state infrastructure project an obligation of existing within the state.

    As an alternative in Exalted, I see a few Solars, some accompanying other Celestial Exalted, a few dozen associated Dragon Blooded, a sorcerously bound additional force of demons and elementals, and maybe a few hundred paid professional or volunteer mortal labourers, and they go at it with gusto.

    Sure, it would be easier on those Solar Exalted if they just commanded several hundred thousand people to work on it instead, but you weren't bestowed the Twilight Caste because you're work averse, and it's a pretty worthwhile thing to devote some of your creative talents to.

    The idea that the Solars as fully corrupted by the Curse actually do become the kind of indolent and lazy that would create a preference for the alternative is not my favourite, but is kind of plausible... but for them sitting on the legacy of several thousand years of an alternative work ethic. I presume that they've gotten in a fair amount of automation by then. Certainly enough that it can then do things like go violently out of control in response to the Solars being betrayed.

    It's not referred to as the Era of Light so Limited that you need Large Quantities of Bonded Servitude to Harvest your Cash Crops.

                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                    Real life never had human civilization originate with the presence of mystical demigods that were around for the vast majority of it.

                    I want to make a joke about the Usurpation happening, and all of a sudden there's a pressing need for cotton, but... that doesn't actually seem like an inaccurate take on it. The worldwide market is still huge, a lot of the old infrastructure has broken down, they need some means of harvesting that cotton.

                    Unless it's to say that, for all of their immense power and variety of innovations, the preferred method for harvesting cotton in the First Age was to acquire massive work forces of slaves to do it?
                    It's not like it's a bad take, and I'm not objecting on common sense grounds or infeasability - what you describe is a staple of post-apoc, after all; you see it about in Mad Max to Fallout.

                    I just have a personal preference for slavery emerging from the kind of mechanisms that it emerged from when practiced by real world slave societies, which were more in the phase of the waxing of societies; as the Roman Empire's machine allows it to conquer, they take slaves as war booty; as the Islamic World's economies allows it to export trade goods, members of Slavic tribes captured in internecine warfare are traded to them; as the Atlantic European economies expand, they can sell trade goods to people who have not much more to offer than slaves, and an opportunity presents itself in transporting human lives to a place where European workers die in droves.

                    That is, as something that emerges from economic expanse and the lack of any real check on the institution, more than a substitution of labour effect

                    As to the question of whether the First Age Solars would ever have had need of slavery, there is the character of general abundance about which would limit *need* to resort to slavery, but, at the same time, I would point to the stance that the institution emerges more because of a lack of a sufficient check and incentives than from necessity.

                    And on whether the Solars would ever have had *need* of slaves, to use in their magics, to perform ritual labour that free people probably would not do of choice, I think that seems reasonably possible. Factory cathedrals, "through a mirror darkly", suggests some images. Especially given the caprice that certain magics show for people who meet a particular intersection of qualities ("This ritual must be performed by a member of this specific bloodline"). And whether malice is intended, there is probably a fair bit of moral compromise about in the wars of Solar Empires and between them and other things. (Though of course, much of depends on how much your First Age is defined as one of grandeur vs one of justice.)

                                The Order Conferring Trade Pattern and the dependence of good functioning of the world of vast quantities of mortal prayer also suggest that in certain ways, the Solars were never able to completely dispense with the need to organize vast swathes of mortal society towards activities which were somewhere between ritual and organic, and between centrally planned and spontaneous.

                                (He's also not totally consistent to my mind on the implied economic basis of the First Age in his comments, here: http://exalted.xi.co.nz/wiki/Thus_Sp...ologicalLevels

                                On the one hand he notes, in response to a comment that the Dragonblooded would suppress printing on the basis that; Commentor: "Peasants are peasants and they shouldn't be allwoed to rise up. It distrupts the order of society and causes chaos and all that", Grabowski: "I think that you take a little more cynical a tone than the average Dragon-Blooded would. Consider that you can go outside and see the ruins of multiple destroyed high-tech civilizations. Would it be hard to keep a straight face when pushing a society based on idealized peasant farming?".

                                On the other states "Society also has a different focus too. The self-envisioned idyll of the world of Exalted is, I think, very much like the funeral scene at the end of Kurosawa's "Dreams". A few Exalted trouble themselves so that the masses might live in peaceable ignorance." where the end of Kurosawa's Dreams having watched the thing, is, in effect, some kind of idealized agrarian village!)

                                *More on the tangent about industrialisation, even then, for Grabowski's statement, maybe refers to a personal theory of industrialisation where industrialisation was driven front end by social changes to recruit large amounts of the population in projects that societies viewed that they *needed* to achieve.

                                My understanding is that's probably valid to some degree in later industrialising societies, who viewed it as a need to remain competitive in a dangerous international system (it probably tells you about German or Japanese or Soviet industrialisation) and engaged as a more top down process.

                                But possibly less valid for primary industrialisation, in which putative theories revolve around where increases production of secondary goods (and so machines to do that, e.g. industrialisation), are a more organic aftereffect of things like a high agricultural productivity and a large share of goods production, which builds high demand, and a high wage level, which together with high demand and disposable income for non-food goods makes machine substitution and mass production attractive.
