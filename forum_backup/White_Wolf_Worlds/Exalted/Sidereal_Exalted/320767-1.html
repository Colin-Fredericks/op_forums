        Honestly, I would characterize the main constraint of Avoidance Kata as "it is a form of emergency retreat", which means that using it a lot has the same downsides you'd expect of doing a lot of emergency retreating. Not being there is often a really significant drawback in itself, particularly for people as busy, regulated and infrastructure-bound as Sidereals, even before considering that the place you are instead is unpredictable and unplanned. AK is nice because once you realize that this nefarious psychic foresaw your arrival and slipped out in advance, and this is a thing they can do whenever because they're a nefarious psychic, the naive way you would handle that in character tends to also be an effective method to deal with Avoidance Kata from a mechanical standpoint.

        I think it could stand to have its limitations explained a bit more clearly, but AK isn't really what I'd call a "problem" charm, even from the angle that the character is an antagonist the PCs are chasing.

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                    The distinction seems largely arbitrary.
                                                    The distinction is not even remotely arbitrary.

                                                    A hard counter is like this. You push a button and whatever your opponent was doing, it is foiled. Playing large, expensive cards becomes functionally impossible because playing a large card will always be responded to with Counterspell. Judge's Ear Technique is a hard counter: you make it impossible to do anything involving lying and your opponent has no practical way of defeating this. Dragonblooded Charms failing against magic is another hard counter: there is no way to get around it with planning or luck, just suddenly none of your applicable Charms work. Even if Charms were not as foundational to your character as they are, this would still be poor design because it declares the outcome in advance.

                                                    A soft counter is like this. When you play Force Spike, your opponent has two options. They can either choose to let you counter the card you used it on, which will leave you down 1 mana and the Force Spike card and them down the mana cost of the card as well as the card itself, or they can choose to pay an additional mana, which will leave you down 1 mana and the Force Spike and them down the mana cost of the card +1. There are situations in which either one might be a preferable outcome, such as if they have a stronger card coming up on the same turn and they need all of their remaining mana to play it. In general, Force Spike makes it more expensive for your opponent to use single large cards against you, but does very little to thwart an attack based around using lots of small creatures. There are other soft counters to deal with that kind of attack that don't do much to hinder single large cards. More importantly, Force Spike makes strategies built around large creatures require more foresight from your opponent - leaving a bit of mana in reserve in case you play it, for instance - but it does not make such strategies impossible.

                                                        I want to preface by saying that Solars and Sidereals are both constantly fighting for the place of my favorite Exalt, for pretty much opposite reasons. I love Solars as the shining hero who shapes the world through bold words and deeds just as much as I love Sidereals as the FBI/KGB/CIA agent of heaven who shapes the world with secrets and subtlety.

                                                        With that said, I believe that Solars having a hard counter to Sidereal shenanigans makes a degree of sense from a narrative standpoint. When you get fate and destiny involved in a fantasy setting, there are usually two outcomes: Either the hero is guided by fate to fulfill his ultimate purpose, or the hero subverts fate to write his own destiny.

                                                        Sidereals, being agents of fate (and also it's master) fall in the former category. They are guided by Fate in the form of their charms towards success in whatever goal they choose. Solars on the other hand are in for latter category. They shrug of the threads of fate and ignore destiny to pick their own path in life.

                                                        Given the usual conventions of the hero/fate narrative, it makes a degree of sense that Solars, being the Heroic Archetype Exalted, have some hard counters against some Sidereal powers, who are the Agents of Fate Exalted. This isn't to say that Solars should be able to no-sell ALL of a Sidereal's spiffy powers. The struggle of the hero vs his destiny (as assigned by the Sidereal) is also a common plot. I suspect that Solars will have hard counters to the more overt examples of what I affectionally refer to as the Sidereal's cheaty bullshit fate powers, which will represent the Solar's ability to define their own fate. At the same time, Sidereals will still have a number of options to force the Solar to conform to the fate the Sidereal picks and chooses.

                                                        Tl;DR: I believe Solars will be able to hard counter some Sidereal abilities, but I think this is unlikely to invalidate the Sidereal's ability to do the cheaty fate bullshit that I love.
