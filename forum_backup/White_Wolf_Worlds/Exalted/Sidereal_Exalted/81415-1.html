    Originally posted by MOK View Post
    Regarding how he could have a charm within onieromancy, despite not knowing the charm - well, it wouldn't be the first time the player has overlooked details such as that. Frankly that part doesn't bother me, though.
    As far as I can see this is not an issue. A raksha doesn't need to know a particular Charm to have crafted a similar Oneriomancy or vexed one from some other foe somewhere along the line.

    Originally posted by MOK View Post
    I hear Sidereals are, comparatively, less fond of the fair folk.
    Sidereals are not great fans of anyone from beyond the borders of Creation, be they outside fate or not. One imagines there's a circle of neophyte Sidereals watching newly-integrated Autochtonian mortals and just waiting to erupt into a 'no-one-told-us-there-would-be-a-Solar' chronicle. That said the Sidereals do broker 'asylum' deals and there are Raksha within Creation who are tolerated as long as they stay within the guidelines imposed on them. Such deals are tensely watched by both sides, perhaps having the Sidereal appear as the Raksha's 'parole officer' would be an opportunity for storytelling and character development.

    Originally posted by MOK View Post
    Regarding how a resplendent destiny 'looks' from the outside ...
    Anyone within fate (suffering the -3 dice to their awareness rolls) sees what fate tells them to see. If Sidereal Chris Hemsworth has a resplendent destiny as tiny, pregnant Natalie Portman then they see a slim, gravid woman. Beings outside of fate see whatever their roll without the penalty dictates. In this instance it really depends on how much effort the Sidereal has put into his guise ... the separation between high-Stealth and low-Stealth Sidereals being their ability to conceal themselves from targets who see through their Resplendant Destinies.

    Originally posted by MOK View Post
    Regarding the rationale of differentiation between the prior listed categories, I see the basic premise that each is derived from a particular attribute. Though, they still feel slightly arbitrary - If I can countermagic Cup magic, why should I not be able to countermagic Staff magic, Sword magic?
    Why can you not countermagic an artefact? Or a Charm? Or a familiar? Because countermagic opposes Sorcery. Specifically, not all magic in general. It happens that in their practice of forging magic out of Graces the Raksha have happened upon one type of magic that is vulnerable to Creation-born countermagic. They've also happened upon one form of magic that is vulnerable to stabbing, it doesn't follow that all Ring or Staff artifacts can be killed any more than all Manses or Past Lives can be.

                            RE: RDs and Arcane Fate

                            You're right that RDs are not terribly specific. They don't specify the creation of any illusion effect, but the text of the segment says that people 'see only the false identity'. My refuge has always been back to the 1e logic of Sidereal charms which involved Fate, like Avoidance Kata. It's not that you don't see that the pregnant woman is actually a guy with a pillow stuffed under his shirt, it's just that your eyes are getting outvoted by how obviously he's a pregnant woman. You can feel that with a total certainty!

                            (This also fits with the trend of using those as disguises since the Breaking of the Mask. Arcane Fate makes the actual Sidereal difficult to spot, or focus on, or even remember. Resplendent Destinies give a very powerful impression of a specific sort of person, so it's very easy to just go with that instead.)

                            ...but that's not actually spelled out. I just think that's the best interpretation of the mechanics which exist.

                            And this does mean that someone Outside Fate will look at the Sidereal and see a guy with a pillow stuffed up his shirt. If the Sidereal's bothered with a good disguise, they might not notice anything wrong at all, although a properly paranoid Sidereal might leave a small-but-explicable inconsistency just to identify people who point it out.
