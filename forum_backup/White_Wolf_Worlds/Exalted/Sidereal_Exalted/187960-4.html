                "Be weary when drinking tea, for hidden inside the cup may be a Sidereal prepared to punch you into a duck." - old First Edition Exalted saying.

                It wasn't called "Paranoia Combat" back then, but the concept of being utterly destroyed by a ninja Sidereal in a tea cup was a thing back in 1st edition, and that was the basis around which Paranoia Combat formed; have a perfect defense combo'd with a Surprise Negation charm and use them all the time, or a hidden Sidereal will punch you into a duck.

                As for Shun the Smiling Lady, it quickly falls apart in the game setting as there's no reason why the Bronze Faction Exalted wouldn't frequently use that charm on the Anathema targeted by a Wyld Hunt. When you're on a Wyld Hunt, you want to cut the target off from his allies, and Shun the Smiling Lady is a wonderful charm for cutting off his closest and dearest friends. It will also cause a fair amount of confusion and worry in the target, as the love of his life suddenly rejects him - that coupled with the knowledge that he has transformed into one of the Anathema, would be a wonderful way of mentally breaking your target. "I've just turned into a monster and my wife and children all dislike me now!"

                As PC's are frequently Anathema and would often be the targets of a Wyld Hunt, that means that they would logically be the victims of StSL. Oops.

                    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                    As for Shun the Smiling Lady, it quickly falls apart in the game setting as there's no reason why the Bronze Faction Exalted wouldn't frequently use that charm on the Anathema targeted by a Wyld Hunt. When you're on a Wyld Hunt, you want to cut the target off from his allies, and Shun the Smiling Lady is a wonderful charm for cutting off his closest and dearest friends. It will also cause a fair amount of confusion and worry in the target, as the love of his life suddenly rejects him - that coupled with the knowledge that he has transformed into one of the Anathema, would be a wonderful way of mentally breaking your target. "I've just turned into a monster and my wife and children all dislike me now!"
                    But StSL doesn't do...that. It doesn't make your friends and traveling companions abandon you or reject you (in all ways) or hate you. It makes you ugly, terminates romantic feelings towards you and prevents such feelings from being started. But it says nothing of feelings of friendship or loyalty - so the Solar's boyfriend might not feel romantic towards him anymore, that hardly means he rejects him and casts him out of his life and calls the cops on him. It creates a simple variable in the relationship, "lack of romantic attachment," it doesn't inject a statement into the relationship that says "you now despise your boyfriend, abandon him and sell him out!" That's more of an ED charm, and these are very different things. At the least it terminates romantic feelings from the Solar's bf to the Solar, but the Solar's bf may have feelings of platonic affection or loyalty, and StSL does jack to those.

                    If we're talking the 1e Charm, as was said, Appearance wasn't the biggest deal. Yes, in 2e, it affects your MDV when targeted by social attacks by pretty kitties, and being super ugly probably helps defenders of your social attacks resist them. I mean at least a little, StSL doesn't do much to hurt your social attacks if you're already built to throw 20 dice on an Excellency roll and have UMI, but for a Solar who is mostly about punching and relies more on unaugmented social rolls, it can be a pain.

                    Really, "romantic feelings" and later "romantic intimacies" was terrible from a clarity standpoint of what the Charm will actually do to someone's social life, even if it's an evocative phrase.

                    Also, wouldn't the Sidereals working with the Wyld Hunt have to know who you are to hit you with it? At least a little Just "that guy we're chasing, whom we know exists but have never seen!" shouldn't cut it.

                        Originally posted by Ejtaka View Post
                        Let me see if I can come up with a few.

                        Riding the Dragon: This transforms a person into a Lesser Elemental Dragon, and leaves them either an invalid for the rest of their short life, or permanently changes them into a Lesser Elemental Dragon. Yes, the Sidereal has to pay quite a bit for this to happen. It is still a very powerful effect. I'm not saying its a play balance issue, because the actual result doesn't seem that bad, but in character? That's some pretty serious mojo there.

                        Avoidance Kata: Wow, I just lost that argument? You're really upset. Well, good thing I was never here to have this argument. I'm just walking in the room now. Let me sympathize with you. Or even better, let me lead you into this obvious deathtrap, which I can prove is NOT a deathtrap by gong first and showing you its safe. Oops, it was a deathtrap? Good thing I was never here.

                        Neighbourhood Relocation Scheme: That's some pretty heavy mojo. I mean seriously heavy. I have no problem with the play balance, but that's like bringing a nuclear bomb to a sword fight.

                        Many Missiles Bow Technique: Best healing charm in the game. One arrow heals five boxes of damage? Pretty awesome. Plus ending drought, making people fall in love, sowing fields of grain... That's a lot of stuff for one charm to do! Especially since it is scene-length. Again, I love this charm, but that's some pretty heavy magic. I mean, its bringing an orgy to a swordfight and ... winning?

                        Efficient Secretary Technique: Who does research in libraries? Armies of grad students filing their findings so the Sidereal can have their secretary pull up facts as needed. Inventive use of this charm can do amazing things. Especially when you are trying to act like a secretive, all-knowing fate ninja.

                        Avoiding the Truth Technique: Properly used, this is a convince anyone of anything Charm. It works so much better when you try to support your enemies than it does if you try to help yourself, of course.

                        These are just a few of my favorite charms that I don't have issues with in terms of play balance, or play effect (so long as my PCs tell me they are buying them, so I can plan accordingly), but are just such heavy-handed effects.

                        Sorry but I see some exaggeration here :

                        Riding the Dragon: even if you sacrifice the dots (4xp in 2.5 IIRC) you don't get to "keep" he familiar/aquaintance. He becomes a real lesser elemental dragon and isn't your subordinate anymore, probably quite the opposite actually in the heavenly hierarchy. Though he may have/keep intimaces towards you. But don't forget that the tranformation itself is extremely painful (the target has to be restrained in order not to tear the prayer strip) and even if the recipient acquires divine immortality he may not feel in debt for your "gift". Also the basic effect in itself suffers the same problems tan a lot of sumonns : fixed stats. It makes them powerful if you rush to get the charm early, but as your character progresses the impact is less and less important.

                        Avoidance kata : it explicitly says that whatever effect the sid suffered remains. If you react before the deathtrap mortally wounds you good for you, but if you activate it too late, you'll be teleported but still die unless using other powers. Also if you activate it after "losing" to an opponent's argument you'll still be affected by the mental influence either natural or unnatural. Also it's limited to the first 2 actions after join battle/debate/war and creative storytellers may define other limitations for "out of direct opposition" situations like traps.

                        Neighborhood Relocation Scheme : Like a lot of sidereal charms, extremely strong but narrow charms. It's up to you and your wits to put them into good use and adapt them to the situation, just like all the UMI that rolled against the target's essence instead of MDVs which is way easier, but way more narrow and limited than solar UMI.

                        Many Missiles Bow Technique: rightfully nerfed. 1arrow and 1mote to heal many LHL , anybody can see the OPness.

                        Efficient Secretary Technique : 2 motes for every question is quite a high tax, especially for sidereal mote pools. Believe me. It can be nice to fetch situationally crucial information like "what's the name of this town's baker?" so you can pose more convincingly as a local, but it can't find you anything really secret or "lost" on a large scale (IE only a few people still know the piece of information). So if your looking for a specific book quite "popular" in a frequently visited library it will work, but for rarely consulted books or books in an abandoned library, unless you stunt your way based on information you have (like "which wing in the one dedicated to books about anathema's occult powers?") you hardly get anything out of the

                        Avoiding the Truth Technique : actually it's a kind of specialized excellency plus it turns the social attack int an UMI costng 3wp to resist. I don't know solar UMI charms very well but i'm quite sure there should be something along those lines and less restrictive than having to work your way around the fact that your target(s) will be convinced you are lying.


                        Now even if the "narrowness" of the charms is a tradeoff for their power it doesn't always mean the overall result is balanced. But except a few charms that make sense for sidereals (particularily pinalcle charms) or charms that are cornerstones designed to compensate for some of their huge weaknesses (avoidance kata, heartless maiden trance are perfect exemples) I don't think there is a sidereal charm that is blatantly on a higher power level than your typical solar charm.

                        The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.

                                    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                                    Really, "romantic feelings" and later "romantic intimacies" was terrible from a clarity standpoint of what the Charm will actually do to someone's social life, even if it's an evocative phrase.
                                    Yeah, I spent a lot of time reviewing the wording and hashing over readings as a result of this thread and I definitely hope that the 3e version talks over its implications a bit more. Even "romantic" is an incredibly loaded term.

                                    I've said in this thread that I have problems with Shun and mainly object to criticisms that seem hyperbolic and off-base, so putting my money where my mouth is, I think what I'd like to see done with Shun is something like this:
                                    • As long as we have varying levels of Intimacies, make it easier to shake off its effects, but not automatic, if the Intimacy is Defining. Compromise between the "love is a fickle amusement" and "love conquers all!" camps. In particular, it should probably take less work for a strong intimacy to rebound if you just ding it and then immediately drop the commitment.
                                    • While it's active, astrology (the mundane kind) and other kinds of fortunetelling can detect that the character is destined to never find love, maybe with a sufficiently boss roll they can detect that it was done on purpose.
                                    • Sidereals have a lot of narrow hard counters and that's OK, but a long-term hard counter to a specific character niche is kind of a bad design space even when the character type is something very narrow. It needs to be calibrated such that it feels like an interesting challenge for a PC who relies heavily on their looks and sex appeal.
                                    • Making it a Touch effect, as in 2.5, might be too extreme, but the idea that you have to actually be there to use it seems like a reasonable edit.
                                    • Clarify the wording and implications.
