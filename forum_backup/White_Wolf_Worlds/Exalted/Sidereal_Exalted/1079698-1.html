        I'm just thinking that in general their prediction charms have always been more about pushing actions rather than negating them.

        To use the Project system of 3e for an example, Sidereals get to know about the complications before they happen - and their objective is thus preventing rather than counteracting or ameliorating the problems: A project is happening. A peasant revolt will disrupt the project.

        A Solar goes in as or after the revolt happens and quells the populace by solving their problems as they happen, or stomping out the revolutionaries.

        A Sidereal goes in before the revolt is happening and quietly ensures the sparks of unrest never occur - preempting a starvation-based revolt by bringing in food, discrediting or assassinating the malcontents before they Make Great Forks Great Again, etc.

        The Sidereal's foreknowledge didn't let him sit on his butt and just handwave the revolt out of existence, taking no action. He just took different actions, in a way that made him feel like a master manipulator predicting a possible future and taking pains to bring about the future he desired, rather than a Solar running to catch up and fix problems as they occurred.

                            Sort of. Ronin are a thing, so Sidereal definitely do have the option to mostly cut ties to Heaven. The problem is that while you can do that, that's not what most people have in mind when they want to play a Sidereal.

                            I think their book needs more guidance on how to play a Sidereal who maintains and plays out their connection to Yu-Shan, while in a mixed circle that isn't really focused on it. Currently, the Sidereal books basically go into a huge amount of detail on playing a Sidereal in a Sidereal game with large parts of the action focused on Yu-Shan politics, a tiny bit on how you can cut this out entirely and play a Ronin, and nothing else.

                            Whereas I suspect the most common thing people want out of Sidereals (and the most common situation Sidereals find themselves in) is somewhere between those two things - playing a field agent who checks in occasionally via one method or another. I'd also add a bit about how Sidereals check in with the Bureau of Destiny (and how they can do it without actually visiting Yu-Shan) - maybe it's just because I was rewatching FLCL, but I'm thinking back to the scenes of Haruko Haruhara communicating with her superiors via Naota's cat. Give players clear guidance on how they can tie their character to Yu-Shan, conceptually, without having to physically go there or shift the entire story towards that sort of thing.

                            (Actually, Haruko Haruhara would make a good Sidereal. She's good with Resplendent Destinies, she works by advising or guiding people to achieve their potential for her own personal ends, and her habit of pulling giant robots out of people's heads seems similar to some Sidereal Ride charms.)

                                            All else aside, Kejak probably does way way way more office work than the typical Sidereal. He pretty much runs Creation; his situation shouldn't be seen as normal.

                                            Either way, the ideal solution is to give Sidereals more ways to communicate with (and, sometimes, visit) Yu-Shan. I like the idea of them using weird things to communicate - again, I have the image of Haruko Haruhara using Naota's cat as a communication device in my head, which seems like a very Sidereal-ish thing to do. They could have some sort of ability to eg. seek out objects or locations that are tied to fate in certain ways and send communications along the threads. Writing up your report and tossing it in a puddle where it disappears, for instance. Or just letting the wind catch it and carry it away. Or giving your debriefing to a particularly angry squirrel. Or grabbing a random leaf out of the air without looking at it, then reading some important message from another Sidereal off of it. A big part of Sidereal thematics is this idea that everything is connected via these hidden links that most people can't see and never know about, which provides a lot of options for interesting methods of communication and staying in touch with the Bureau of Destiny.

                                            I also like the idea of Sidereals getting missions and sending in reports using messages that just seem to naturally appear, like WoD hunters - like, say, the wind blows the leaves and dust into a pattern that you instinctively recognize as a message telling you that such-and-such a thing needs to be done; or the raindrops on the balcony tell you who has to die.

                                            Of course, they've always also had Summon the Heavenly Portal. But I think it would be best to reduce the necessity of physically visiting Yu-Shan, which can be disruptive to some games. I'd make actual visits to Yu-Shan into fairly rare things for many Sidereals.

                                            Most fictional superspies don't regularly spend large amounts of time dropping into HQ - I mean, real-world ones do, but let's be honest, playing a real-world spy is often pretty boring! That's mostly not what Sidereals should shoot for, no more than eg. a Dawn deals with realistic injury.

                                                        Exalted suffers from one of the drawbacks of White Wolf's desire to have entirely playable game universes which, in the world of darkness meant multiple game lines in which you could play good, bad or grey. But each character type had their own game universe in which to function, and each universe/game line had a fairly distinct style and play.

                                                        In exalted (which you may remember was originally sold as a kind of proto-WoD) tried to combine elements of all of the major WoD game lines into a single, playable universe. But, each of the exalted splats was still very much aligned by the feel of the WoD game line the exalt type related to.

                                                        What this means is that when you play one of the other splats in Exalted, you're effectively engaging in a different style of game - integration is not only a problem of mechanics but also one of genres and sub genres.

                                                        I think this is one of the reasons that Lunars were so... disappointing, because what was created didnt meet a lot of people's genre-based hopes for shape shifting exalted heroes. And for sidereals, I think its a similar clash of genre expectations in the playong of it.

                                                        If you're playing a solars game, sidereals are the mysterious strangers who occasionally turn up to help/make things worse, and to walk in to the final act of the story and claim credit/give praisr for things under the rules of fate and hand waving.

                                                        As has been said elsewhere, Sidereals make interesting PCs in a sidereal game, otherwise, let them fade into the background for the most part.
