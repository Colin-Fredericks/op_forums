                    I disagree with the original post.

                    To me there is a noticeable proportion of charms that clearly fall into one fo these categories :
                    - apply the "concept" of their original charm tree to another purpose
                    - Have a deliberately narrow but strong basic function, causing them to be substantially useful only of you get creative in their use and build intricate plans around them
                    - Charms that require you to have prior information on the opposition and/or topology and/or course of events to be substantially useful

                    Underling Invisibility practice is one of the perfect exemples for the 1st category, but there are quite a few others :
                    - "thought-swiping distraction" applies larceny to thoughts where it would qualify as reading intent which falls classically under investigation.
                    - conning-chaos technique has a double use, une which "cleans" wyld from a land which would naturally fall somewhere between lore, occult, integrity or even craft. And the "curse" part would also fit somewhere in lore or occult (manipulating the wyld) or integrity (reversing the effect)
                    - Invocation of the Storm-Following Silence (in 2,5) damages/kills creatures of death and creates a temporary zone that forces the rules of creation upon a shadowland. Applying the theme of "cure" to the malediction of zombies and ghosts and to creation itself.

                    The most obvious exemple of the second case is Shun the Smiling Lady :
                    The charm is extremely strong, it requires only a touch, "hits" automatically without a roll to resist, last as long as you commit essence, can only be "cured" with emotion-protection charms, affects anyone with a love-intimacy for the target without needing the sidereal to interact in any way with that person, forces to pay 1wp or slowly lose the intimacy.
                    It's extremely narrow : it works only on intimacies of love. Not friendship, not loyalty, not family bond, not respect/admiration, not hate.

                    One of the many uses that can redeem this charm is to build up a plan around it as it can be a very efficient willpower-draining charm. Say you will socially interact with a king to convince him of taking some important decision. You only need to manage to touch his queen to automatically curse her and force the king to spend 1wp in every scene he interacts with her. After a few days the king would have few remaining WP to resist your UMI. Allowing you to easily win him over with charms like favorable inflexion procedure or trick him into a Perfection in Life.


                    For the 3rd case there are quite a few obvious exemples :
                    - force decision requires you to set up a decision for your target. you can't just have the king sentence death to his brother, you have to set up some crime punishable by death and frame him for it.
                    - Presence in absence technique also works best when you know the intimacies of your target to benefit from the social advantage
                    - Walls of Salt and Ash takes a dramatic action to activate so it can hardly be activated right before a battle. You have to know which kind of opposition you will face.
                    - Abandoned Words Curse (version 2,5) requires you to know about the social environment and possibly specific circumstances (pass phrases, specific catchphrases or information to try make the victim look like an impersonator)

                    Now It is true some people push the interpretation too far. Not all charms work weirly, actually probably less than 50% are. That doesn't mean it's 0% either.
                    Sidereal charm description are far from the basic solar "i am awesome at [stuff]" or lunar "I shapeshift to do [stuff] or i mimic [this animal] to imitate his faculty to go [stuff]". They inspire on the ways of fate and potentially long term consequences of a tiny event. That's why you can often naturally build up on the "cascade of events" these charms tend to create.

                    The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.

                            Originally posted by MrInsecure View Post
                            Oh yes, one more irritating meme I'd like to address:

                            Infernals Are Transhuman

                            This is mostly a case of "You Keep Using That Word," since it's about the least useful and most off-putting way of describing how Infernals work without using the phrase "mandatory rape." Yes, Infernals are transformed by their Charms, in ways that other Exalted are not- hell, they Exalt by emerging from a Chrysalis. If the metamorphosis symbolism were any more obvious, Infernals would all come out with butterfly wings and be greeted by Ovid himself.

                            The issue is that the word "transhuman" has a lot of real world baggage that comes with it- transhumanism is a real philosophical movement with real adherents and detractors, which has nothing to do with what the Infernals are doing. And unlike the use of "demon" or "god" in the context of Exalted, it's not a deliberate invocation of that real world philosophy, it's just fans being lazy and jumping on a meme because they forget that newbies will have exactly zero context for what they're talking about. So let's just kill this meme dead in the water and figure out a better way to talk about our favorite proto-Primordials, shall we?
                            Alternatively, I get tired of people telling me not to use a word when they know quite well when what I mean when I use it.

                            If you *actually* think that I'm saying that Infernals are adherents of a modern socio-philosophical movement, you might question me about this, but no one ever does. Rather, this is just a cheap way of saying "Stop enjoying a splat in a way I don't approve of" or "Stop seeing Infernals differently than I see them."

                            I like that Infernals are transhuman and I like their body horror. I'm not going to stop describing them as such, because those are the elements I enjoy.

                            But hey, since you're a rigorous, intellectual fellow who is totally not lazy and doesn't jump on memes unlike my sorry ass, how about you propose an alternative word?

                                        Originally posted by hippokrene View Post

                                        Alternatively, I get tired of people telling me not to use a word when they know quite well when what I mean when I use it.

                                        If you *actually* think that I'm saying that Infernals are adherents of a modern socio-philosophical movement, you might question me about this, but no one ever does. Rather, this is just a cheap way of saying "Stop enjoying a splat in a way I don't approve of" or "Stop seeing Infernals differently than I see them."
                                        No, no one is going to mistake an Infernal for Kimiko Ross (although she would make an excellent Defiler). But at the same time, "transhuman" is not a particularly apt way of describing Infernals, since in this context, it's generally used to mean "becoming more/different than human." Which... describes every Exalted, to varying degrees.

                                        I'm not trying to claim badwrongfun here. It's more like... well, once you call them transhuman, you have to describe how they're transhuman. Which defeats the purpose of using "Infernals are transhuman" as a shorthand in the first place.

                                        I like that Infernals are transhuman and I like their body horror. I'm not going to stop describing them as such, because those are the elements I enjoy.
                                        So do I! Hell, I wish there was more body horror involved in Infernals. Let's go full-on Event Horizon with this shit.

                                        "Where we're going, you won't need eyes to see," and all that.

                                        But hey, since you're a rigorous, intellectual fellow who is totally not lazy and doesn't jump on memes unlike my sorry ass, how about you propose an alternative word?
                                        Okay, going back a few steps- I used the word "meme," but that's not really accurate. The term I actually wanted to use (but didn't because most people here probably don't have the right context for it) is "buzz word." The term comes from politics, and refers to a word or phrase that both encapsulates and simplifies a collection of ideas into easily-recited "codes". Such phrases act as ideological identifiers that encompass a world view without forcing someone to actually explain their ideas (who may not have the time or understanding of the issues to do so).

                                        For example, it's like when moral conservative politicians talk about "family values"- the term refers to everything from a desire to preserve the traditional nuclear family structure, to a desire to restrict children's access to violent and sexual content, to a desire to prevent the legalization of gay marriage. It is simultaneously very descriptive (marking its user out as a moral conservative) and completely meaningless (which family value are we supposed to be supporting, and why?), and after a while one runs the danger of allowing the buzz words to replace, rather than represent, their ideas. (Don't think this is limited to one side of the political spectrum, however- see American Democrats and "the war on women" for another example.)

                                        This is what has happened with "transhuman" in the context of Infernals: it's become a buzz word to represent the metamorphic nature of the Infernal Exaltation and Infernal Charms*. Now, in case it hasn't been clear from my spiel, I hate buzz words. They make my eye twitch. I hate seeing ideas oversimplified down to their most basic elements, then repeated ad nauseum by people who either can't or won't explain what they mean.

                                        At least buzz words make sense in politics (annoying as they may be), since most politicians are thick as bricks and need bite-sized catch phrases to throw to their constituents in interviews and stump speeches. In this community, though? I have a higher standard for the level of communication.

                                        *Metamorphic, incidentally, would make a new potential word to replace "transhuman," if you insist on needing a replacement.

                                            Originally posted by SuddenThunder View Post

                                            For those of us who know very little about transhumanism and would, frankly, have preferred that Infernals never got further than Dukantha ... could you elaborate some?

                                            Because for all the savage awful that seems to have come with them so far it would be nice if there was, you know, a point?
                                            "Transhumanism (abbreviated as H+ or h+) is an international cultural and intellectual movement with an eventual goal of fundamentally transforming the human condition by developing and making widely available technologies to greatly enhance human intellectual, physical, and psychological capacities.Transhumanist thinkers study the potential benefits and dangers of emerging technologies that could overcome fundamental human limitations, as well as the ethics of developing and using such technologies. They speculate that human beings may eventually be able to transform themselves into beings with such greatly expanded abilities as to merit the label "posthuman".
                                            The contemporary meaning of the term transhumanism was foreshadowed by one of the first professors of futurology, FM-2030, who taught "new concepts of the Human" at The New School in the 1960s, when he began to identify people who adopt technologies, lifestyles and worldviews transitional to "posthumanity" as "transhuman".This hypothesis would lay the intellectual groundwork for the British philosopher Max More to begin articulating the principles of transhumanism as a futurist philosophy in 1990, and organizing in California an intelligentsia that has since grown into the worldwide transhumanist movement."

                                            From Wikipedia. To put it simply, once we are able to make something that can increase it's own intelligence (AI, man/machine interface, gene splicing, etc it doesn't matter which one) there is nothing stopping that thing from increasing it even more. At that point, all the models for predicting future actions and reasoning of this thing break down because those models are based on human level intelligence. This is known as the singularity because we have no way of looking past this point. If this is a person then it would be classified as a posthuman because one of them trying to have a conversation with one of us would be like me trying to explain Chemistry to my dog. If you want a RPG about posthumans, Eclipse Phase is a good start. I personally think that Nova Praxis does transhuman better though. In this context, however, MrInsecure is talking about how the Infernals start becoming more alien in both mindset and body as they go down the charm trees. At the end, they basically become the Yozi's or ..... something else. That's not really transhuman in the sense that the concept is normally understood. I normally understand what the person means, but it's understandable if a person who is unfamiliar with the base idea gets confused. Metamorphic works also but I prefer alien.
