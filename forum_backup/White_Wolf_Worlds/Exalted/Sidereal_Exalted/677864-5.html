                                        Part of my ideas for how Destiny works do have the idea that during the First Age there were various factors, such as the management of the world and its ontological issues by the Exalted, the relative restraint of the Celestial gods, operating budgets that were a lot higher (you had billions of people and a lot of holidays sending constant streams of Essence and cash in huge quantities to Heaven) and the Sidereals being aided by all kinds of wondrous tools they could get the Realm to produce for them, it was a lot easier both to make plans for unfolding history that could be adhered to a lot more smoothly, and kind of make the world... nicer. People would still have to put some of their own work in, but the world could be a bit less arbitrary about some things, so people who were really ideal for each other didn't need to have their happiness prevented by little things like being on opposite sides of the world from one another.

                                        When I say tools made by Solars, I'm thinking of things like thinking automatons optimised to helping manage the unintuitive workload of destiny management, engines capable of modelling all kinds of life intersections and alternate futures, and even things like writing kits made to allow Sidereals to write out all of the necessary orders and directives to precisely manage enormous numbers of destinies per day in a manner that transcended the physical limits of how much paperwork a human shaped thing can do in that time. Not things that replaced the powers of Sidereals, but complemented them; the kinds of tools that would be useful to somebody with World-Shaping Artistic Vision in a similar way to how a daiklave is useful to somebody with Hungry Tiger Technique.

                                        I don't see Chejop Kejak as having been a very sentimental man even in the First Age (I've got this image in which Chejop has incidentally had numerous children over the centuries, and isn't very interested in any of them beyond doing a bit to give them destinies to insulate them from some arbitrary disasters; I don't see this as necessarily being a flaw), but I can imagine some old Sidereals who occasionally look at the work they have to do now (not in terms of "we must make Hard Choices for the Greater Good" so much as it just being a bit... grubbier), and look back with nostalgia on the times when being an agent of Fate meant you did a more meaningful equivalent of ensuring that it never rained unless everybody had an umbrella.

                                            So, Mercushio, I may be misunderstanding your post but you appear to be saying that the majority of humans in Creation don't live in poverty? Let's see... they live in a world of constant warfare where warfare is apparently necessary just to find enough to eat, nobody has access to clean water and most don't have a reliable food supply either (hence the disease and warfare), they live in mud huts or wooden shacks or at best small urban hovels, most roads aren't paved if they exist at all, starvation and disease are rampant and uncontrolled, chattel slavery is all over, the weather is totally out of control.... everywhere in the entire Threshold. Not to mention the Fair Folk, Wyld mutants, and undead marauding everywhere and killing people constantly. The only humans who are slightly better off are the wealthy, who are a vanishingly tiny minority and still don't have clean water or peace. You don't consider that to be a low standard of living?

                                            Your post also looks like you're saying that the extractive empire set up by the Empress does none of the things that absolutely every single extractive empire in all of human history has done to every nation they conquered.

                                            You seem to have a very rosy, idyllic view of empires in which everybody is happy and healthy and exploitation never causes any actual harm, either at home or in the conquered countries. (You also seem to say the Isle and Satrapies never had any rebellions, which is not what the books say.) I don't think you realize how structurally destructive empires inherently are to the conquered societies. The "order" they impose is solely the order of external hierarchical obedience (because anyone who disobeys is killed). Everything else is systematically chopped apart. The society and culture and civilization conquered and subjugated to extract material wealth and human slaves inevitably crumbles into poverty and dysfunction -- this has been demonstrated, historically, in every single empire for which we have any historical data. Look at the Third World today -- those countries didn't start out dysfunctional and broken since the dawn of humanity. That chaos is the result of centuries of slave trade and extractive imperialism, which destroyed the cultural institutions that once existed and plunged formerly prosperous countries into poverty and chaos. Now imagine that everywhere there isn't a stable government, everywhere that disease or war temporarily wipes out a big chunk of the population (such as the recent Ebola epidemic) the Wyld or undead can move in and take over permanently.

                                            I have also read explicit statements -- I think by authors and devs on this forum -- that every time a civilization independent of the Realm becomes large or prosperous or unified out in the outer Threshold, the Legions go and utterly raze it to the ground because it's a rival and might be infeseted with Lunars. This probably means only polities that are getting quite large and independent, since they haven't wiped out Halta, for example.

                                            The Scarlet Empire is also very foolishly and short-sightedly designed to depend utterly on the immortality of a being who cannot possibly be immortal because no Exalt is immortal, and everybody has known this since the Thousand Struggles Era. And Oh Look! as soon as she inevitably went away her supposed "orderly utopia" immediately collapsed. That is not a sustainable society even internally, even ignoring the way the empire is designed to eviscerate human society in the Threshold.

                                            And Sidereals live long enough to witness the decay firsthand, and realize what is happening, in a way that mortals and even Dragon-Blooded cannot.



                                            The problems with the Visions of Bronze and Gold started from the moment the Great Prophecy was cast. Every Sidereal present assumed three facts that were utterly false:

                                            1. The Bronze and Gold are the only possible solutions that can ever or will ever exist, therefore we should never attempt to have any other ideas.
                                            2. This Prophecy accounts for all the known and unknown threats from outside of Fate, and therefore we can trust the Visions to be 100% accurate and trustworthy.
                                            3. If either Bronze or Gold Vision succeeds, it will have succeeded for all eternity, because we can see infinitely far into the future. Neither Vision will ever come to an end in all of eternity, or require a new prophecy, let alone a new Vision.

                                            Every Sidereal should have known better than 2 (which is contrary to the known laws of Fate) and 3 (which is contrary to the existence of Endings) but from the very start, they failed to realize this. And in all the time they've been watching Creation disintegrate, the faction elders still haven't guessed that 1, 2, and 3 are not true. Due apparently to the Great Curse, they took leave of logic and sense, and the elders who are still alive are stuck that way.

                                            It's pretty obvious to me, for example, that the Vision of Bronze only actually predicted the course of things until the Great Contagion and that it no longer has any relevance to what's happened in Creation since then. The Bronze Faction is currently flying blind, on utterly outdated and irrelevant information.

                                            (To Mercushio, I simply disagree with your opinion that the Great Contagion and Balorian Crusade were not failures because a small, crumbling, very doomed remnant of Creation still exists for now, but we'll have to agree to disagree there.)




                                            And I think that the younger Sidereals, who are not chained to the Great Prophecy because they weren't there, would notice that 2 and 3 are BS and 1 is looking mighty dodgy as well. Most are probably smart enough to notice that the current state of Creation is bad and getting worse, and that repeating the same old plans is not working. They would not blindly obey Chejop and Ayesha and agree with everything the leaders tell them. There are going to be disagreements because they are going to notice how often their leaders' plans are out of touch with reality.

                                            I think there must be a lot of younger Sidereals who agree only loosely with the broad thrust of one of the factions, but have their own very different goals for how to carry them out because they disagree with their leaders. There are young Sidereals who only pretend to belong to a faction, who are actually independents who quietly discuss among themselves what Third Option would be best. There might be a few independents who really don't have any opinion about what to do, but most of them are likely to care enough to have their own idea about what should be done. I think very few of the younger Sidereals, possibly none of them, are so blinded by the Curse that they think the current plans are working perfectly and requires no changes.

                                            And by "young" Sidereals, I include those who were born during the Shogunate and witnessed the Great Contagion, too. I mean pretty much everyone younger than Ayesha Ura. I think the majority of Sidereals have ideas of their own and do not agree with every single thing their leaders tell them to believe and do. They are arrogant and often callous, but most aren't stupid.

The Great Prophecy was a thousand thousand prophecies, cast by the entire Fivescore Fellowship, to the furthest reaches of the Loom (remember this was written when most Sidereal prophecy Charms required at least three Sidereals of different castes to work, and were more effective the more Sidereals present. Even that presupposes that the best way to mechanically represent the Great Prophecy is through Charms, or that it should be mechanically representatable at all)

                                                        If anything, the three Visions are amaglamations of hundreds or thousands of visions that follow the same theme; the Vision of Gold in particular is called out as having taken the longest to form because the smaller prophecies that would eventually comprise it were so varied that it took the Sidereals a while to see any commonality between them. Hell, you could make a decent argument either way that the Visions are distinct things that exist in setting and that the Visions are constructs for us, to players, to sum up the Great Prophecy without it having to be mindmeltingly complex or the length of an entire fucking fatsplat.
