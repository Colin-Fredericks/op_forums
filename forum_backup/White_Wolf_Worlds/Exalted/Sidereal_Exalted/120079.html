    Sidereals and focus change

    04-26-2014, 09:39 PM
    So I'm reading the original games of divinity and this is a passage I've read in several places.

    "They made the sidereals, who were wise and mighty seers and weaver of spells but few in number and weaker in battle than the solar and lunar exalted"

    So my question is when did weaver of spells change to martial artists? I mean the implication is that they were more traditional sorcerers compared to other exalts but none the less they were less about fighting and more about mystical might. When did that change and why use Martial arts instead of sorcery?

          The Martial Arts were also written in a very... wonky manner. When Sidereal Martial Arts came out, Jenna Moran and the other writers for the line DID say they were inferior to how powerful you could get with native Charms of Solars and Lunars (you can find plenty of old, old writer quotes to that effect); they weren't meant to be the ultimate combat skill. Problem is, Jenna tuned them so damn high that it was basically impossible to write a stronger combat suite than them unless every charm read 'I win'.

          Perhaps Jenna herself could have written Solar and Lunar charms that could have beaten SMA, and could flow from the written Charms for both splats, but she is a goddamn genius. For everyone else, it just did not seem to flow, and Sidereals became the combat monsters, the absolute Exalted-killers of the setting, when that wasn't the intention.

                              04-27-2014, 12:40 AM
                              Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                              Yes but why make them combat abilities if they were supposed to be weaker anyway? Why not just keep them to something without that focus? If you are going to make them physically weak making a set of abilities that are focused on combat seems like a shitty thing to do.
                              Well, first of all, I would be cautious about taking any broad statements of "weaker in battle" too literally. It might be technically true after a fashion, but "battle" is a pretty broad term. Exalted is a heavily Wuxia-influenced game, which means the mechanical differences between splats include a lot of words devoted to "what is their approach to fighting?"; "they are bad at it" is not a very useful answer.

                              However, Sidereals are supposed to have a sort of idiosyncratic approach to combat. A Lunar or Solar who chooses to specialize in combat is going to be straightforwardly good at beating people up and will generally do well in situations where you'd expect beating people up to be a useful solution.

                              While Sidereals aren't bad at that (in that your SMA master is never going to be a slouch in a fight), their focus is more on esoteric, indirect powers, or monofocused high-risk high-reward strategies which require planning. They're heavily technical fighters, while Solars and Lunars tend to be more balanced. The perception of Sidereals as "arcane" ties into this -- attacking indirectly or using complex plans is a staple of that sort of character.

                              It might help to realize that because of that, it's (generally) a mistake to compare Sidereals to Dawns. Sidereals don't usually fight like Dawns. I mean, they can, and they won't fall apart, but it's not their forte. Thematically Sidereals are a lot closer to Twilights. Every Sidereal, after all, is going to have arcane trickery of some sort up their sleeves, even if it's from their astrology or native charms or martial arts rather than sorcery; and they leverage that in order to win.

                              So with that in mind, what does it mean for, say, Pattern Spider Touch to be an MA charm (whereas the Twilight is probably getting that kind of polymorph-other effect from Sorcery, if they want it?) What it means is that for that sort of character -- for an arcane-mystical-seer sort of archetype -- Sidereals are actually very good at combat, because their punching-people skill and their strange-mystical-enlightenment skill come from the same place; Sidereals (and ancient-martial-arts-sages in general) are about blending mystical enlightenment with the power to beat up an entire tavern full of people at once if they set you off.

                              I suspect that the bit you quoted was written under the mindset of "well they usually can't leap at a Dawn with no plan beyond murder and expect to win", which is technically true but misses the point.

                              Also more importantly (most importantly) it's just a throwaway line in a book that was not about Sidereals, so it probably didn't have as much thought put into it as the stuff in the actual Sidereal books.

                              Also also, belatedly: I just realized that in context, it's describing the creation of the Sidereals. That is to say, the idea was that Sidereals were not created for combat, but they said "screw that, the Maidens are insane" and invented SMA anyway. As I recall, 1e was clear on the fact that Sidereals invented SMA specifically in order to jailbreak their way past the not-intended-for-combat thesis the Maidens had built them with. So the actual answer to your question is that it changed, in-setting, when the Sidereals decided that they weren't happy with their limitations and found a way to break them by practicing MA.
