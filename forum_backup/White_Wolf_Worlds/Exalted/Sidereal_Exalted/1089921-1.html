    I always thought there were some weird allocation. in my mind you have 5 archetypes. The Intrepid Messenger, The Consummate Socialite, the Tactical general, the Secret agent and the Implacable assassin

    Journeys: Ride, Sail, Athletics, Survival, Dodge, Archery, Brawl
    Serenity: Bureaucracy, Socialize, performance, Craft, Linguistics, Occult, Presence
    War: Presence, Melee, Archery, Resistance, Lore, Ride
    Secrets: Stealth, Larceny , Investigation, awareness, integrity, Lore, Occult
    Assassin: Medicine, Stealth, Resistance, Dodge, Thrown, Melee, athletics

    They are pretty radical re thinks but I think they create 5 really distinct classes of Sidereals that really feel like they have the skills related to their fields. Obviously they all have access to martial arts anyway so Serenity and Secrets not having a combat affiliation is hardly crippling in a combat scenario plus they are also both sorcerous now.

                            I'm going to look over your Brawl Charms, since it doesn't look like anybody has so far. Let's get to it!

                            Horrific Wreath: +2 damage and automatic Aggravated seems like a little much for an 1 mote charm. Ghost-Eating Technique costs 3m and also deals Aggravated against Spirits, but it's got some other effects too. I think this charm could do with having its cost raised to 2 or 3 motes, just to keep it balanced with other Withering damage-adders.

                            Tolerant Strife: A little more broadly applicable than Dipping Swallow Defense, but also a mite more expensive. I think this charm is alright. 3 motes means that it's expensive to keep using when one is suffering from wound penalties or other debilitations, but it's still pretty strong.

                            Fateful Feint: This one is pretty unique and fascinating. Recouping the motes spent on an attack is pretty useful, and the automatic success is a nice reward. I think something like this might be a bit too all around useful. Maybe it could have a minor Initiative cost and the Perilous tag? The Willpower cost might be enough, but it can easily be regained through stunting or other means.

                            Shooting Star Suplex: Perfect, I love it already.

                            Wait, I need to mention the mechanics, don't I? So, it's 3 motes for a decisive counterattack that takes the form of a grapple gambit followed up by an immediate Slam. That's pretty hardcore. This is a fascinating counterattack Charm, very different from Solar Counterattack or Cross-Counter. I think it's alight as is. It resets the Sidereal to base Initiative, right?

                            Judicious Use of Leverage: This is a pretty interesting mishmash of mechanics, allowing waifish little Sidereals to toss around big guys and then later huge monsters. The Essence 3 Repurchase seems on par with Titan-Straightening Method. I feel the 'must have a lower Strength than your opponent to sub in Wits' might be more trouble than it's worth, but I don't know enough to be sure.

                            Ripples in Force: This charm has some badass imagery, but it's a little bit unclear. Mentioning Aim actions paints this as changing the way the attack works. It's still a melee Withering attack, right? Does it use its normal accuracy, or does it recalculate based on the distance between the Sidereal and their target?

                            Unobstructed Blow: This as interesting armor-piercing attack, with some unique rules that set it apart from other similar charms. I like it.

                            Broken Wing Atemi: Breaking limbs is awesome. This charm is great.

                            Crimson Palm Counterstrike: So, it's just a reflexive decisive clash with some extra damage? I feel like this charm could use some extra bells and whistles, but the cost seems pretty in line with other counterattack charms.

                            Mutually Dissatisfactory Compromise: So, a clash enhancer that basically means that both attacks hit and deal damage simultaneously. I don't think DV is the right terminology here. Defense seems to be what the book uses when it's talking about Evasion and Parry simultaneously.

                            Flexible Fate Reversal: Oooh, a charm that lets you gain more control rounds mid-grapple. That Initiative cost makes this thing kinda risky, though. This charm seems interesting, especially with its multiple uses.

                            Beatstick Encouragement: This charm's name doesn't really jive with me, but I love the effects. The 'damned if you do, damned if you don't' nature of the Intimidate action seems fun to play with. I'm unsure of how someone might react when faced with this charm, though. It's similar to other followup or secondary attack charms, but it seems a bit more broadly applicable. One Weapon,

                            Two Blows has very stringed activation requirements, but I think the Willpower and the fact that the Intimidate has to be successful offset this.

                            Forcing Hand Incantation: Yeeeeeee, prayer-strip charm! Hot damn, light artifact fists is a huge upgrade! That last sentence, though, that's absolute madness in a can. Double raw damage, or reroll failed dice on the attack roll? That's an absolutely brutal effect. I like it. This definitely seems like a great capstone, not to mention a great way of capturing how RIDICULOUS some Sidereal charms are meant to be.

                                Hey I'm really glad to hear your input and have a few responses.
                                • Going to definitely take your advice over Horrific Wreath and Fateful Feint
                                • Shooting Star Suplex does reset you to base initiative. It follows the same rule as a normal Decisve Slam/Throw including adding your rounds of control to your damage and ending the grapple with your target prone.
                                • Ripples in Force has almost the same text as the Solar Charm Iron Raptor Technique, barring its unique capability to ignore cover. The Aiming thing is just a fact that all ranged attacks made at medium range or longer need an aim action before them unless otherwise said.
                                • Crimson Palm Counterstrike is kind of boring, especially compared to all the rest, but in first edition, Crimson Palm Counterstrike was a clash charms before clashes existed. I figured leaving it as a sort of bland clash charm would be a nice way to hearken back to its original incarnation. Adding bonus successes to damage (Albeit a limited amount) hearkened back to 1e and 2es way of dealing damage.

                                All in all I'm really excited to see you like what I've written. Love to hear every bit of criticism, advice and general interest this project has
