    Hello all,

    I'm currently ST'ing a game in which Sidereals make semi-frequent appearances, often as Resplendid Destinies.

    I'm sure that given time and experience I'll understand why it's bad, but I need to correctly ST a Sidereal for the time being, so... why does the internet think Paradox is a bad mechanic? What was so bad about it?

              I'm sure there's a bunch of other backlogged negative feelings deeply embedded in the culture, but the primary issue I've taken with it that I've seen echoed elsewhere is the ability of the game to outright murder your background (or even your character) for interacting with the mechanics. If I knew that someone I cared about could die, or that I could just straight up take more damage that I have life, I would be pretty reluctant to gain any Paradox.

              To that end, in most cases the carrot was not juicy enough to justify the stick. Astrology offers a lot of potential for Paradox, but it was cooler in concept than practice in most cases. Punishment mechanics suck. You should always be trying to encourage players to do interesting things. That's my 2 cents, (before this turns into a Limit conversation) hope it helps.

                          Yeah, the problem with Paradox was that it was so harsh. Sure, some of the options on the roll weren't too bad. Suffer a -3 would penalty for 1-10 weeks is harsh, but livable. Being reduced to zero Appearance for 1-10 months sucks, but again it's not necessarily game over.

                          But lose all motes and can't regain motes for 1-10 days? So you don't get to use Charms? What is your character supposed to do if you end up in a fight? Or how about losing your ability to perform Combos, which under the 2nd edition paradigm was a death sentence? Losing all Willpower, again, meant no combo's. Even something seemingly minor like Falling into a coma for 1-10 days is terrible. If the PC's need to stop Raksi's plan in 3 days, but your character is in a coma for the next 7 days, you're in trouble, and your ST will either have to handwave things, ask you to sit out of the next session, or have you play an NPC character instead. And, of course, Essence Venom is potentially just an auto-kill effect if you roll high on the damage and don't have Ox Bodies or a high Stamina.

                          Roughly half of the Paradox effects would either directly require the PC to sit out of the game for a time, or would basically force the PC to do that. These effects were not "fun" or "interesting" or "engaging." They were just Fuck You effects.

                          The only other Exalted type with something similar was Abyssals with their Resonance. But if the big difference is, the player was allowed to choose which Resonance their character would suffer from. So if they didn't want a particular Resonance effect, or if that Resonance effect would have an intensely negative impact on the play experience, the player could just not select that effect. While Resonance had problems, Paradox was much worse in every respect.

                              Originally posted by wern212 View Post
                              The latter. I understand Paradox is a "negative" mechanic, but why is it's inclusion bad for the game?
                              To be honest, most of it is this fandom's usual propensity for throwing the baby out with the bathwater.

                              That's not to say Paradox is a good mechanic as-is. It's overly punitive, which feels particularly bad and unwarranted since Astrology that causes it is neither that powerful nor that fun to use even without the added disincentive. It's also pretty random, which many people feel clashes with the themes of the Sidereal Exalted. With Sids being the hubristic manipulators, you'd think Paradox is about their meddling in the Loom causing unwanted tangles in the tapestry in a way that's directly relevant to what they were meddling in, not unlike what botches do to sorcerous workings. But no, it's Pattern Spiders getting pissed at you for daring to increase their workload and biting your fate strand to make a random horrible thing happen to you. Clearly you can see the wasted potential here.

                              But that's the thing - the idea has potential, and if handled properly (and coupled with otherwise more inviting Astrology mechanics), would make a great tool for adding interesting complications to a story involving fate manipulation. I agree what we have in previous editions is pretty weak and you're probably better off not using it should you choose to play either of them, but I don't think the idea should be scrapped from the game entirely, at least not without attempting to remold it into something useful and fun first.

                                      Originally posted by Morangias View Post
                                      I don't think the idea should be scrapped from the game entirely, at least not without attempting to remold it into something useful and fun first.
                                      I don't recall ever hearing people claiming that Paradox as an idea was bad. Just that the implementation of it was. And, yeah, as people have noted... The Paradox Effects aren't fun. They don't really contribute to a good story, which in some ways is worse than them being harsh.

                                      Like, a curse of "You respire Motes at 5 every TWO hours outside of combat, until you find and apologize to the God who laid it", THAT can be worked into a story. That can lead to potential events and some fun.

                                      Just "Welp you can't respire Essence for 1d10 days" means either your Sidereal has to go sit on the couch for a week and change, potentially missing out on some sessions, or it'll lead to the ST saying "... Right let's just skip forward a month". Nothing's really gained with the latter, and the former... Well few things suck more at a game session than "Sorry but my character isn't there... I guess I'll pull out my DS or something, don't worry about me". These DON'T lend themselves well to enriching the play experience.
