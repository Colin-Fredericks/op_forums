            Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
            What calls to Sidereal fans?

            /is Solar fan
            To me at least, a big part of it is the being part of something greater than yourself, being an agent enacting more than your whims. Solars work from a very self-focused position - they are all about their own ideals, though sometimes they might have one of these ideals be the following of some other person. They are classic western heroes, beholden to nothing but either themselves or their own loyalty to their beloved lord. Sidereals, on the other hand, start out with a baseline assumption of being agents for cosmic forces and committees that deliberate how to make the world keep turning. Where Solars are Kings and Heroes, free to go around and happen to everyone else, Sidereals have a job to do, and are given enough knowledge and perspective to understand how horribly important this job is. They are Men in Black, toiling away unknown in the shadows, so that there is still a sunrise tomorrow while the Solars in the daylight shake the realms and change the lives of men.

            Basically, all Exalts can come to bear the shackles of understanding the fragility of the forces that keep Creation as we know it working - but only for Sidereals are these shackles quite literally part of the introductory package and come with a specific job to keep it going. This creates a certain agent of heaven/MIB/SWAT role and narrative that I rather enjoy over the kingdom building of other splats.

            It's related to why I enjoy Alchemicals so much as well.

            And there is also the more... low-height feel of them. It's hard to explain, but a lot of Exalted feels like you are supposed to be visibly above things, being always a big player and such and if you aren't you're doing it wrong, while with sidereals it feels like you're encouraged to solve your problems with the absolute minimum of fuss - a perfect Sidereal is the one who, like Lu-Tze, can change the course of history by breaking off one lever with nobody even knowing or helping a single man come to an epiphany. They feel like they are more encouraged to get a bit more involved in the details. And I like going down to the details.

                                    Originally posted by Couch View Post
                                    The thing about "Sidereals as MacGyver" or "Sidereals as Bond/Maidens as Q" is this: MacGyver never has exactly the right tools for the job, but he also always has exactly the right combination of random objects to do what he needs to do the job. Similarly, every gadget Bond receives from Q, no matter how bizarre, will always be used at some point before the end of the film.

                                    The difference is that unlike MacGyver, and unlike Bond, the guys writing the Charmset are not the same guys writing the script. Sidereal Charms can be weird and wacky, but they also have to actually cover all of the things that they need in order to do their job in a generally applicable manner, and I would say that in cases where these functions collide the latter should win out over the former.
                                    Originally posted by Cenobite451 View Post

                                    True, but only to a degree. MacGyver's challenge is picking the right combination of random objects to do the job, even though individually it generally seems as though they really shouldn't. The way the Charmset facilitates this sort of thing is largely by giving us a big toolbox full of Awesome-but-(often wildly)-Impractical random shit.



                                    I guess all I can really say is that I disagree. "Generally applicable" is the sticking point really; a version of the Sidereals that doesn't force the Sids (and by extension their players) to employ a degree of lateral thinking to solve their problems is a version that completely loses my interest.

                                    Also, one must remember that even if they somehow find themselves without a single Charm applicable to the situation, a Sidereal's still no less capable than a heroic mortal, and this is supposedly the edition to finally terminate the tradition of mortals-can't-do-shit. At the end of the day, you can get a whole lot of general utility out of just, y'know, being human.



                                    ...also also, apparently italics are my new semicolons.

                                    These issues are exactly why I stressed on the two charm types that synergize with each other :

                                    - you can't just give a lots of impractical charms for each situation like MacGuyver gets a lot of random objects to tinker with. Because it would make their trees a mess and also because each charm costs just as much so having to buy multiple charms just to have tools for a few situations means character development will be way slower for sids. Creating once again a frustrating power gap.

                                    - the solution is to make charms that cover a wide set of situations , or flexible or that can be stunted towards different outcomes. And because of that, those charms can't be "powerfull" at the same time or they would be unbalanced (think BotBM : boosting all MA rolls and static values, providing an enhanced dice cap AND lasting a scene)

                                    - on the other hand you can't just make trees only with flexible but "weak" charms. It would be just like going to war with a multi-tool knife, a first aid kit but no rifle. Sure you can do a lot of things but you'll lack raw firepower. That's when the narrow but strong charms come in : they allow for strong thematic impacts and particularily reward you if you manage to manipulate the situation to fit the restriced use cases the charm works in.

                                    The end result is you deal with each situation by fiting them into patterns specific to each ability/constelation.

                                    For instance I have though of a verison of cash and murder games where you don't just wish to craft a relationship and pays the mote and magicaly create it. But instead he would need to create a situation where everyone else acknowledges the relationship and the target would become forced by fate (or peer pressure or whatever) to fit into the pattern. Of course to counterbalance the required efforts compared to the original charm, the created relationship would be stronger than "pay a few willpowers to break it".

                                    Exemple : you want lady [x] to marry with the king. you disguise as her and seduce the king and convince him to get married. The day of the ceremony you arrange for the real lady [x] to be present and activate the charm so she feels a strong pull forcing her to wear the dress, accept the marriage, and live the rest of her life just as if she lived the moments with the king when it was actually someone else posing as her.

                                    The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.
