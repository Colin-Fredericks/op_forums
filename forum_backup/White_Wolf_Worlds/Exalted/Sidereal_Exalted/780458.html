    Loom and "Reality" glitches, Sidereal's everyday life problems.

    12-05-2015, 05:33 AM
    OK I know one of Sidereal's jobs is to maintain the Loom of Fate and make sure the things that will happen will basically happen. Assuming 2e lore, Essence users can disrupt the Loom of fate and cause threads to be tangled while beings outside of Fate (EX: Demons, Fey) can cause merry hell on the Loom itself. This I get, I get the causes of how the Loom can basically bug out.

    But my question is the effect, what effect does this have in the world? What are some good "reality hiccups" that can be show in an area which has the Loom of fate tangled up in an area. I mean there has to be something between "Reality + causality is in full force here" and "Everything unraveled into Deep Wyld."

                          Trying to sum up how I'd think of random Fate glitches, I guess I'd:

                          a) Just make something exist which shouldn't exist - Why is this city here? History says it ceased to exist 200 years ago. What effect does this have on the region? How do you smooth this over, by travelling around and persuading the "citizens" of the city that they've actually had lives elsewhere, and creating a campaign of information that the city never existed, and then faking up the city as more aged and weathered than it actually is, etc.

                          b) Give something the properties of something else (including a destiny) that it oughtn't have - following a surge in the Wyld and certain bizarre occult convergences, the Linowans have suddenly assumed part of the destiny of the local birds, and so use the power of flight to make ceaseless warfare on the Haltan. Someone oughta fix that, as it's obviously not what we want to happen.

                          c) Something has happened which ought to happen but in the wrong order (the historical cart has come before the horse), and you have to flex wildly to put it in the right order

                          (Wasn't there a random generator for these someone built, or am I misremembering?)

                          I think the difficulty here for me with these has always been how these weave as part of a compelling larger plot, driven by heroic passions and other such things of heroic wuxia epics, rather than being random world-goes-derp that the Sidereal character has to fix.

                          You can make a lot of how demons and fae and undead exploit the opportunities created by fate glitches (that city that wasn't supposed to be there is a fine place for demons to cross over into the world of Creation, etc)... but the Sidereals aren't really the Watchers or Libra of Kekkai Sensen (secret organisations whose mission is to fight and manage demons), so there's a limit to that.

                          In the fictional antecedents of the Sidereals, The Traveller in Black fixes glitches which are really the suffusion of ancient primal magic into the world, driven by foolish mortals seeking easy power and mischief by petty chaotic godlings - but it is kind of a loosely held together collection of short stories, so a bad example of how these can hang together into a great narrative (and the Traveller is more of forger of natural law in the world and banisher of the Wyld, something Sidereals seem less set up to be in Exalted, for whatever reason). While the Otherhood of the Great Work of Time don't really fix glitches so much as adjust history towards what they think it ought to be.

                          Not that fixing Fate glitches could be fun (because dream up a pretty crazy situation, and then have players apply their characters skills to it fixing it should be), I guess I'm saying it seems too reactive and status quo oriented and random to really make too much of as substance beyond challenge of the week parts of the character's Sidereal career which building up to the *real* arcs of the story, and to establish the sort of person they are before introducing your Sidereal characters' *real* proactive plans to change the world and the real interpersonal dramas and rivalries and Celestial politics that actually motivate them (like a substory that introduces a boss or rival or lover who can become more important in the story than they appear to be at first).

                                                          Otherwise perfectly mundane spiders start weaving messages into their webs that foretell the planned fates of the locals in several villages. This breach of security is ostensibly a minor one, but knowing one's fate with such ease can sometimes put a snarl in the most delicate threads of destiny. Farmer Jeng might learn that he will be attacked by bandits the next time he goes into town. By delaying his trip, the bandits move on a day early and end up pillaging another homestead a week early, killing the wrong people. Currently the consequences are very minor, but solving the problem is more difficult than killing all of the spiders in the area and your superiors are being pressured by Gods getting kickbacks from local divinities to fix the problem now before it gets to be a bigger problem and the area gains a great deal more official scrutiny than they are prepared to deal with..
