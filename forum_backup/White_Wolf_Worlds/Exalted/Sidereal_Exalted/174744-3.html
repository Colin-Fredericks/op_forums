        Originally posted by MrInsecure View Post
        Okay, before we continue, can we please get the "Sidereal Charms are sooooo weeeiiiiirrrddd" thing to die in a fire? I made an entire thread about this before, but with only a few exceptions, 2e Sidereal Charms
        For the record, I also thought that that you were mostly wrong in that thread, or saying things that didn't matter, but I avoided it when I realized you were arguing about the 2e Sidereal charmset. Arguing about that is a waste of time; everyone knows it sucks and fails at just about everything it sets out to do.

        Mostly the Sidereal charmset was designed around this idea of giving them an unusual competence grid overall, including holes in certain areas and unusual spikes of power elsewhere. The 2e charmset misunderstood all of this, and also failed to adopt them for the changes between editions, so none of these things really showed through the way they were intended to.

        For an example of what I'm talking about -- one thing Holden has said a lot is that he feels one of the big mistakes the 2e Sidereal charmset made was giving them Excellencies, since doing so essentially removed a ton of the holes in their charmset and made them play a lot less interestingly as a result. That one change negated a huge amount of what made Sidereals what they were, since in 1e most charmsets had a bunch of different dice-adders, but Sidereals did not. (This isn't to say that 3e Sidereals will necessarily lack them -- I've got no idea, and really given the vast differences in the system there's no reason to think that any of the old mechanisms for hitting that general mood will be coming back -- just an illustration of the mindset behind how they were originally designed and which is likely to inform their design in 3e. Understanding how a fan of 1e Sidereals could feel that carelessly giving them excellencies in 2e 'ruined' them tells you a lot about their intended design.)

        My feeling is that when you say "Sidereals are not weird!, especially in the context of what you're saying in this thread, the subtext of what you're saying is "I feel that designing them in this way has been a failure, and I don't want to see it in the future, especially not if it means them having a closed charmset." But that's not a particularly compelling argument when you're basing it on experiences with their 2e charmset, because their 2e charmset was crap from start to finish and has no lessons to teach anyone beyond "don't totally screw up a splatbook, lol." I guarantee you that 3e Sidereals will be designed, as much as possible, without the slightest thought towards the mechanics, themes, or "design principles" Sidereals used in Second Edition; they're going to be based on the overall concepts of 1e Sidereals. (Obviously, very different based on both the changes between editions and attempts to fix some of 1e Sidereal's flaws -- 1e Sidereals weren't flawless -- but I'm 100% certain they're going to try and shoot for the same general goals.)

        So if you want to understand where they're heading, you have to look at that.

                "Hey, Sidereal trees are just a bunch of utterly random stuff with no constraining rhyme or reason and are balanced because of careful, and disguised / hidden holes in their set, so they'd better not be able to get any new Charms or they become easy to accidentally or intentionally break (either unfun or too powerful for the setting)" - I think that's wrong (although perhaps a strawman position, I have certainly seen comments in this vein).

                While there are carefully placed holes and spikes, Sidereals do have a constraining and structured design - short trees, themed under a Maidens and a prayer strip, generally focused on the ability and never really totally contrary to its nature, with particular Sidereal type patterns of strengths and weaknesses (see the start of the 1e Sidereals Charm chapter for starters and other patterns can be intuited from reading through the sets).

                But at the same time, this design does mean that opening their charmset in the sense of letting Sidereals build potentially endless ad hoc extensions to their sets probably doesn't work.

                If their set were to be opened, in the sense of players able to innovate new Charms, rather than the retro-active modding Fata-Ku quoted - then the design would have to be stuck with, new complete ability cascades, not new ad hoc charms.

                And all this would probably be so much work for designers that we would, at least, probably not see many new published Sidereal trees, other than homebrew. So the situation likely wouldn't change *that* much, except for very good homebrewers or people enthusiastic about using others homebrew.

                                It's worth remembering here that in 1e, Sidereals were encouraged to stunt using their charms in ways that technically broke the rules of the charms, and they had considerably more latitude to do this than other Exalted. 2e removed this, I think, because 2e tried to make Sidereals less weird and dialled back the "rule-breaking" aspect of stunts (a shame, as it was my favourite part -- but I digress). However, it's a useful illustration of the difference between Sidereal design philosophy and everyone else's, and it was meant to provide some counterbalance the scope limitations of their charms without throwing open the gates or allowing you to "cheat" in a reliable and repeatable fashion.
