    Sidereal and Fey - Rules and possibilities

    02-28-2014, 12:49 AM
    Right now I'm running a gDocs game where one of my players is a raksha, and a Sidereal's presence is coming due. I only barely understand some of the raksha rules, and am spotty on Sidereals too, so I'm attempting to troubleshoot ambiguities, only to find more in the process.

    A) In what circumstances can a Sidereal's Resplendent Destiny be rendered invisible/inactive?
    My raksha player carries two.. uh.. items... that confer(?) the power of Mad God Mien(GWM pg 160). Presumably that means that as long as the character 'holds' the things, he's considered outside of fate. But I'm not even sure whether that means he does not see a Sidereal's resplendent destiny. And what about other entities - Demons, Abyssals, Infernals?

    B) Emerald or Sapphire Countermagic can render some raksha stuff temporarily inert. Or destroyed, barring the inclusion of Mad God Mien. What are the categories of stuff countermagic affects? Or doesn't affect?
    Are the affected categories Oneiromancies? Adjurations? Behemoths? Treasures? Any/all? And what's the difference between those labels, anyways? Seems like fluff. They all just seem like items. My player's character sheet has some items listed under a category of "Dreams," which I don't think is an official term, so now I'm unsure whats what. A scarf and a mask that appears to confer charms. What categories might these be accurately called, and what differentiates it from the other confusing crap in GWM?

    C) Can these varied raksha items be transferred to others, to confer benefit? If I decided that an NPC had a history with a raksha(NPC or PC), could it be possible that the non-raksha took posession or ownership or ... whatever... of any given [insert raksha buzzword]? Therefore allowing a non-raksha access to raksha power? It seems oaths need to be acquired from someone/something else before getting .. er, empowered or whatever. So concievably someone could steal my players oath? An exalt perhaps? And what about the scarf, or mask? Or beyond these, are there any limitations as to what a mortal(for example) could gainfully take from a raksha?

    Ugh, this stuff is definitely not so convoluted as 1E(as much as I admire R.S.B.'s creative mind, yikes). But it still seems a bit excessive and confusing.
    Any help on the subject, broadly, would be beneficial. Thanks all.

              Behemoths are, essentially, mortal creatures that you stack mutations on top of. So you take a common wolf, you stack the Huge mutation on it a couple of times, then you stack two Extra Head mutations. You give it the Breath Weapon mutation. You got yourself a fire breathing Cerberus. Pretty straight forward.

              Adjurations are a way to add Mutations onto yourself. You make an oath and in exchange you get certain mutations. In Creation, an Adjuration doesn't really exist and doesn't have a form; just like real oaths, it's an ephemeral concept (though in the Wyld they are "things" that can be taken from other people). Again, fairly straight forward.

              Oneiromancy are magical spells. These are vulnerable to Countermagic. Essentially they tend to produce long lasting effects. Basically think of them as granting the character bonus Charms, which tend to be limited in how you can use them (for example the Paupers Mask allows the owner to cast Hateful Coin Curse, but only against people who see the mask). You build them using Mutation Points to grant the spell both charms (effects) and vectors which allow you to affect people with those charms.

              Finally you have Treasures, which are a bit more like standard Creation Artifacts, each with their own powers and abilities.

              Regardless, you can give Behemoths, Adjurations, Oneiromancies and Treasures to other people. They still have to attune to them to be able to use them. Many of these tend to be fairly expensive to attune to, and Raksha have fairly small mote pools. So it's common for a Raksha lord to give some of his stuff out to his servants and have them attune to those for him. So a Raksha lord might have a Stablemaster who attunes to his Behemoths or a Vizier who attunes to his Oneiromancies, When the Raksha needs to summon forth his Cerberus, he summons up his Stablemaster who then summons Cerberus.

              Your players Scarf and Mask (which provide Charms) are probably Oneiromancies.

              And yes, Fair Folk Oneiromancies, Treasures and Behemoths can be given to or stolen by other people, including the Creationborn (Adjurations can be stolen too, but as they don't have a physical form in Creation, they can usually only be stolen in the Wyld).

                  A) Just as the Mercury says, although I'd point out that just because the Raksha isn't effected doesn't mean the Destiny is inert. Other characters will still interact with the Sidereal as though he were his assumed identity, Resplendancy powers are still available to him, and so forth.

                  (As an aside, I'm not a Raksha expert but I'm struggling to see what those items would be. The usual means used to bestow long-term Charm effects are Oaths, but they only bestow mutations and by my reading Mad God Mein isn't eligible. They could be Treasures but a Treasure that just replicates a Charm is uninspired, to say the least, and carry two would be fairly odd. Could be worth touching base with your player and finding out just what he sees these being.)

                  B) Only Oneriomancies are effected by countermagic.

                  The different categories of Raksha Artifacts vary in a few key ways, their composition, their Shaping function, and their effect in Creation.

                  Every Wyld Artifact is made from a Grace, and each type of Grace can only be made into it's associated artifact. You can't take a Sword Grace and make a Treasure, for example, or forge a Ring grace into a Behemoth. Likewise each artifact only helps in it's associated type of Shaping combat. A Behemoth is a powerful tool for Sword Shaping, but useless for Staff Shaping.

                  Treasures (Ring) are the most straightforward. Anything that might be an artifact for an Exalt could be a treasure. A Noble's glass sword or spiderweb armor would be Treasures, possibly also your scarf and mask (though I wouldn't really be a fan of a player taking two artifacts that just replicate the same Charm).

                  Oaths, also called Abdjurations, (Staff) grant bonuses over the long term in the form of mutation points, which can be taken either as Wyld mutations or certain types of Raksha Charms that are eligible to be taken as mutations. In Creation they tend to manifest as intangibles, lights, sounds and will-o-the-whisps.

                  Behemoths (Sword) are monsters and Wyld creatures, a sort of Rakhsa answer to Familiars if you take into account that Behemoths can get massive and dangerous and familiars generally ... don't. Uniquely among Raksha Artifacts, Behemoths can be slain by the Creation-Born.

                  Oneriomancies (Cup) are the Raksha Equivalent to Sorcery. The are the Raksha's primary means of using reality-shaping on others in Creation, since free form shaping is prohibitively difficult there and also a means of imposing effects over a wide area with spells build around Assumption of the (Land's/City's) Heart. Oneriomancies can include Mad God Mein (so your mask and scarf could fall here) but in this case they wouldn't confer the power of that Charm on the Raksha character.

                  C) Yes, non-Raksha can steal or be given all of these items, and can use them in some circumstances. Anyone who can attune an atrifact can use a Treasure. Anyone who bears a Grace (usually meaning they've had the various Forging the [Grace] Grace Charms performed on the by a Raksha) can swear to an Oath or bond to a Behemoth. Only characters who possess gossamer can wield Oneriomancy spells. Any suitably daring Creation-Born can steal a Raksha artifact and just hold onto it, to deny its former owner it's use.

                  Hope all that helps!

                                                          Originally posted by MOK View Post
                                                          Regarding the rationale of differentiation between the prior listed categories, I see the basic premise that each is derived from a particular attribute. Though, they still feel slightly arbitrary - If I can countermagic Cup magic, why should I not be able to countermagic Staff magic, Sword magic?
                                                          But that's a minor quibble, more like a semantics argument, easily ignorable.
                                                          It might help to remember that they all actually are artifacts. Weird artifacts, but artifacts. Indeed, Treasures are functionally Artifacts by another name.

                                                          Countermagicking away a raksha's attuned giant monster Behemoth (made from a Sword Grace) would be analogous to countermagicking away someone's daiklave.

                                                          Adjurations are more internalized. I can't check my books, but I recall they mostly give mutations, and Permanent Charms that are treated as mutations, to one who swears the Oath. Maybe think of them as working actual, lasting (albeit conditional) changes on the one who swears them.

                                                          Oneiromancies create external and active magic in the world under preset conditions, so they're vulnerable to countermagic. Also analogous to Sorcery spells, Oneiromancies require some time (albeit in scenes) and effort (ill-defined, other than the mote cost to cast them, which comes on top of the attunement cost) to get them going. The other Grace artifacts are good to go the instant one has them attuned (other than swearing the Oath, in the case of an Adjuration).
