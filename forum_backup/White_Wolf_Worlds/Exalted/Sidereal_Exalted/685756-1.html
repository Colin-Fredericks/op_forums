    I don't think the Loom of Fate should be treated as some kind of control object rather than the thing that actually produces the world, and I'm worried that postulating Sidereal Charms in terms that try to disregard the Loom is just heading back into the same problems as samsara (even if there are ideas that the Sidereals are not powerless against things like the Design, I think that can be done in terms of Sidereals naturally linking to any such thing, rather than transcending all of them). I don't even think it's necessary to explain how something like Prior Warning would work; if I was following Jenna Moran's point that demons can't be utterly disconnected from Fate, since people can still see them and stuff, I don't think it would be too difficult to go "hey, the destinies of the plants and rocks and stuff are telling you that there's something dangerous with no destiny just over that hill" or even something like "you feel an inexplicable void in the threads where a warning of impending danger should be". I think any kind of "transcends the awareness of fate" thing is ultimately a Voodoo Shark solution.

                The Sidereals are fate's agents. Not fate's.....how to say it....gardener? farmer? something like that, point is they are not bound by the Loom's power. just as the Infernals are not bound by the Yozi's power and thus lose it when they leave Malfeas or the Abyssals when they leave the Underworld. you can argue that the Alchemicals lose the ability to change charms when they leave Autochthonia, but only because there are no vats constructed in Creation yet, and they still have some form of power, even if its unchanging.

                The Sidereals, while having themes of being constrained to limited forms of power and capabilities, are also all about finding ways to go beyond with what they have. They need every little bit of power they can use outside of the Loom of Fate. making them more bound to it will only hurt them and what they are meant to do. they are supposed to be the ones separate from the Loom and empowered by the Maidens who while manipulate the Loom, are not OF the Loom. the Sidereals are not The Chosen of Pattern Spider. the Maidens came before the Loom, Autochthon made it, and the Sidereals fix the problems with it. it is the Pattern Spiders who do the day to day work on the Loom, it is the Sidereals, who while use the Loom a massive resource, also in many ways must go beyond the Loom to make sure things stay on track. they are not the civil servants, but the black ops agents. their job is not "Loom technician" thats the Pattern Spiders, their job is "do whatever needs to be done to make sure reality stays stable. no matter what."

                and sometimes, black ops agents do some really shady things that are not legal and within the law. not everything a Sidereal does can come from within the system. they sometimes have to take measures to make sure the system is upheld even if that upholding requires something the system wouldn't use or praise. that said, I find such charms that predict the future without the Loom to be perfectly in theme for Sidereals.

                                        Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                        How do you think it would impact those Charms if the Loom of Fate were removed from the setting?
                                        Do you mean like it was in the Modern Age and Heaven's Reach, or the existing setting having an event that removes the Loom?

                                        Assuming the former: I assume that the heptarchy of disaster gods functioned differently, or that that fluff didn't apply to the Charms at all, although I probably wouldn't sweat it too much.

                                        I just think that when is describing the cosmology in terms of this big Loom, and describing certain cosmological underpinnings to the Charms of people that work with that Loom, it's unintuitive and overly complicated to not really have them interacting with one another.

                                        I'm still not entirely sold on the idea of examining the Loom as a function of anything other than associated Charms in the first place even being a thing. I mean, I think one could easily get by with saying that unless you're using magic involved with doing so, looking at all of the gossamer threads strewn throughout the big frame is not really revelatory of anything. I'd say people only think of the Loom as something you can examine in that matter because it was written that way, that if you showed the concept to somebody new without that reference they wouldn't independently draw that conclusion.

                                        Because that's another thing, besides the point of "why have a Loom and Sidereal Charms and that have them function along the same principles"; why is it necessary or useful to create a form of Sidereal prophecy that works from their Charms and from looking at the Loom that function according to different rules and don't really interact with one another?

                                        I'm not necessarily saying remove that aspect of the Loom entirely, I just think it's worth examining. For one thing, the standards of prophecy created from the one have had a tendency to crowd out the other; you're here saying that Sidereal prophecy Charms transcend the limitations of the Loom and that you wrote later stuff with that assumption, and I see all that, but every single discussion about Sidereals has a lot of people assume that Sidereal Charms can't foresee things like demons and can't reliably predict the Exalted, because that's how prophecy from the Loom of Fate is described, and nothing ever really suggests that their Charms are different and there's not really much inclination to assume it.

                                        For the Loom from herein... I could see something useful from the idea that examining it directly let's one know straightforward, utilitarian things about the future, the things that are matters of simple causality, things like the idea that a person's current course will bring them to Nexus in a week (with that as the kind of stuff where it can be difficult to see far for the Exalted and demons don't show up at all), while their Charms are more things like omens, ways of receiving prior information about important or dangerous things, things that rely on too many complex factors to be determined from examining single threads, or a sense of ways that the future needs to be adjusted in order to fit into an existing wider plan or a general metaphysical precept. A possible difference between looking at the Loom and being warned by the Loom.

                                        I've got my own idea that for regular people, when they're on the path to something significant and improbable (like encountering a person with whom they could be happiest in all the world), the world generates ambiguous signs and omens that can warn the person beforehand and give them a reasonable chance to engage with these things rather than be subject to arbitrary circumstances. So I've got an idea of relevant Sidereal Charms as being a bit of a more specific, potent and deliberate form of this.

                                            My question is, if their prophecies don't come from fate, then where do they come from? It must come from somewhere.

                                            If Sidereals prophecy mechanics truly transcend everything, my difficulty with that is it would come from nowhere.

                                            In myth, when somebody predicts the future by omens or oracles, it's because they consulted the gods. And the gods know the future because they decide everything that will happen. It's not fate, it's Destiny. In fantasy and scifi sometimes people can predict the future by looking in the direction of time or something, like in Dune, but they're still learning something from an actual source that has a reason for providing that information. Or there's again an indication that they receive visions from the gods.

                                            To me it would be incoherent if Sidereal knowledge of the future literally had no source at all. They need to have a reason for how they got it, that's part of the cosmology. Just saying "it's magic!" is a lazy way out, and doesn't satisfy my suspension of disbelief. In 2E the Maidens have samsara, which transcends fate and is more all-encompassing of reality in general. I don't really see the problem with keeping samsara as a source of information greater than fate. It doesn't necessarily have to control Sidereals' actions, maybe not even control the Maidens' actions, if they consult it. It can just be a source of information, since basically it's like the Loom of Fate but for all of reality, and not directed by anybody. It doesn't have to be described in detail, other than being part of the fabric of all reality and present in every real world.
