                    Preptime is nice as far as it goes, but you don't want to overly focus a splat on preptime or it makes them unpleasant to play (see, for instance, the 15-minute working day in D&D), and it pushes the game toward something closer to modern war, where there's a lot of tedious set-up and double-checks, and actual conflict is usually sudden, brutal, and one-sided. SIdereals as "preptime Exalted" is fine when it's a matter of making sure that some of their key effects are only accessible via weird shenanigans like Earth and Sky Bargain, but trying to expand astrology heavily into that role would, I think, have deleterious effects on the splat. You don't want to overly incentivize the Sidereal to noodle around in Heaven 90% of the time and rely on Scry and Die as a general methodology, because although Sidereals are "NPC-flavoured", they are a PC splat, and they're a lot more interesting as a PC splat than they would be as pure NPCs.

                                        Honestly, if anything, they have the opposite problem -- Sidereals have traditionally had a huge number of charms that are fine when used by PCs, but cause problems when used by NPCs. (Transcendent Hatchet of Fate, Of Things Desired and Feared, Five Ordeals Odyssey, Shun the Smiling Maiden, etc.) -- charms that operate on a narrative fiat level that works fine for PCs and has enough restrictions so it doesn't break the game when they're the ones using it, but which is generally not fun when it's being used against you. One symptom of this is that Sidereals (especially in 1e, where they were otherwise well-designed) had a ton of charms with no clear way to defend against them.

                                        The 2e Sidereal Astrology section straight-up says "do not actually use this for NPCs; just pretend you did." (Not that 2e Sidereal Astrology was very fun for PCs using it, either; but that illustrates the problem. It is fun to weave the fates of others, but much less fun to feel that your own fate is being weaved by a distant NPC.)

                                                            Originally posted by Demac View Post
                                                            I never understood the argument that they weren't intended to be a PC splat. I get that much of their charms and the things they do are just not fun to play with and should probably be changed, but given that Exalted was supposed to be a prequel to classic WOD and the Sidereals are meant to be the mage analogs, they should totally be PCs.
                                                            The problem is one of execution, rather than intent.

                                                            Sidereals were supposed to be the Mage analog, but the "best" sorcerers in the game are the Solars (the Hunter analog for some reason), this left the MA connection of "Do" from the Mage system which becomes the fluff basis for all the SMA.

                                                            Essentially Sidereals are bits and pieces of "cool concepts" that didn't add up to much on their own, but when lumped together almost make a functional splat. It just also happens that most of those bits and pieces tend to be the exact same qualities which are normally associated with NPCs, specifically ST-NPCs which are always one step ahead, always have foreknowledge and always have a plan.

                                                            Never mind the fact that the spiritual enlightenment of MA, the tough as nails CIA spook and divine cubicle worker shticks don't mesh well on any level. Basically they are an ST NPC race that can fit a broad range of rolls, rather than a PC race that can be played in different ways.

                                                            Each splat can be broken down into some core concepts, some mechanical, some fluff and finally some storyline based which make up the feel and direction of said splat.

                                                            In the case of Sidereals they have the following:

                                                            Fate & Astrology:
                                                            As a replacement for total mastery of Sorcery, the Sidereals have control of fate. But if you think about it, the very notion of being exalted is all about carving out your own fate. Of over coming the situation by your own will and changing the course of history. Thus Fate becomes a paradoxical concept that controls everything in the setting and nothing at the same time. Does anyone really feel like the Fate powers are on par with Sorcery in Exalted, to make up for the fact that the Mage analogs are worse at magic use than the hunter analogs?

                                                            Xanatos Manipulation:
                                                            With the aforementioned control and foreknowledge of Fate comes not just the ability, but in many cases the expectation of Xanatos level manipulations to steer the course of the situation in games. But since all the important characters (PCs and high end NPCs) are either outside of Fate or have the power level to simply change the course of history via their actions, then it is not believable to say that Sidereals can actually control Fate. This conclusion then undermines the first concept of having control over Fate in the first place. Thus it is a function of the ST saying so, rather than a function of the class.

                                                            Sidereal Martial Arts:
                                                            So while all Exalted are by definition an advanced integrated weapons system, how is it that the smallest mote pool Celestial Exalts, who are not regarded as front line warriors or even functional spec-ops combatants, are given the most powerful MA combat charms in the game? The answer is game balance, not because it makes thematic sense or even logistical sense if you think of all the Exalt types forming a cohesive army, which the backstory of their design implies or outright says.

                                                            Trainers and Wise Elders:
                                                            Currently Dragon-Blooded are better trainers than Sidereals, mechanically speaking. That alone should be enough to say that something has gone horribly wrong. Lastly the whole concept of knowing forgotten knowledge of ages past is not a class specific benefit, but a background perk. If you swapped things around so that the Lunars or Solars ran Yu-Shan rather than being hunted, they would have said knowledge and surviving wise elders. The whole trainers & Elders thing is a fluff contrivance based on the Sidereals being the basis for the Usurpation, just like the good little plot devices/NPCs they truly are.

                                                            Hmmm, I might have to explore this further.
