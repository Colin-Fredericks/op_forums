                                    I feel like Resplendencies would actually be a decent starting point. You buy the one College and get a number of effects, all of which provide specific bonuses in specific circumstances, helping you conform to your college's archetype. -1 TN is a pretty dull effect; "You are guaranteed to find a demanding job that provides just barely enough to live on, and nobody will be able to track you if you work your hardest at it" is more interesting. It's also way more situational: As a Charm, it would just be too narrow to work (even for a Sidereal Charm), but as an Astrology, it's just one of a number of thematically-related abilities that you buy in a single package.

                                    Now, toss on the ability to attach Resplendent powers to other people (though not other Sidereals), requiring you to specify what conditions will activate the abilities (since non-Sidereals can't consciously activate their destinies). That gives you the ability to play Sidereals as advisers: They can craft up an appropriate destiny and then give the appropriate advice ("I recommend taking a menial job to blend in. Trust me, just keep your head down and be a good little worker, and nobody will look at you twice").

                                        We always end up talking about the closed Charmset a lot in these threads. It does seem arguable that beyond the soft limits of "homebrew is hard" and "People playing Sidereals generally want their characters to come up with Sidereal Charms, which includes not being exactly what they want", the hard limit closure of the Charmset doesn't really add much. As much as it does add to their cool as a mechanical statement of them working under limits and their cool of using MA as an cunning solution to their "problem", is it worth it? (Open question). I actually don't get the impression actual Sidereal players have a strong consensus on how much it matters.

                                        With Astrology and the desire to have it be an open system, I'm more interested in whether the designers can actually knock it up as a balanced open system that's fun and inspiring to use, more than a desire to have curses and blessings not look like Charms or spells in their rules format.

                                        Like, the Curse of Blades (I can't remember if that term was actually in the comic or not, but it's cool either way) is like a social influence towards a prophecy declared to be that lives in the structure of Fate. It is not exactly like the existing base Sidereal Astrology that makes shifts the structure of the work to make auspicious and inauspicious events more and less likely, which is a fine effect. If coming up with effects like that works better in a Charm or spell format that trying to write a general system of mechanics, then they should be written that way. Mechanics follow function, right?

                                                            Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                                                            So I'm reading the original games of divinity and this is a passage I've read in several places.

                                                            "They made the sidereals, who were wise and mighty seers and weaver of spells but few in number and weaker in battle than the solar and lunar exalted"

                                                            So my question is when did weaver of spells change to martial artists? I mean the implication is that they were more traditional sorcerers compared to other exalts but none the less they were less about fighting and more about mystical might. When did that change and why use Martial arts instead of sorcery?
                                                            Esp as the design intent is still clearly that Sids are weaker in direct combat than other Celestials (and probably well-built Terrestrials!)...

                                                            Well, you know that I already agree with this, from that prior Sid thread. Look, we can argue current mechanics on the forum all day, and it's clear that rejiggering 2E Sids to favor Sorcery over MA would be problematic. But I still maintain that this could be made to work starting fresh from scratch in 3E! The big problem wouldn't be reconceptualizing the narrative/mechanical themes and frames of Sidereals, it would be getting the fandom to accept these changes, which is probably too great a hurdle to even attempt. Which is why I said before that this is almost certainly never going to happen, even if it should, but it's still fun to ask "what if"?

                                                            I know people love the concept of SMA, but frankly, I don't see why that needs to stay around. Sidereals have the "old kung fu master" as part of their narrative, but it's hardly exclusive to them. There are probably more DB "old masters" out there, Immaculate and otherwise, than there are Sidereals as a whole! And Lunars can fill that role easily, as well. It's pointless to build an entire splat around that theme. Esp as the Seers do have other things going on, narratively...

                                                            Here's an idea: "Secret Styles" instead of "Sidereal". These were created by the Solars, with Sidereal guidance, in the First Age. When the Solars died, their knowledge would have died with them, but for several martially-oriented Seers, who kept their secret arts alive (think of the Arabian scholars who kept the learning of the Classical world extant through the European Dark Ages until the Renaissance). Now that the Solars are back, the masters of one faction seek to return the lost arts to those Solars who are worthy...while the other faction finds it poetic to kill them with their own mastery.

                                                            Of course, this may bring up cries of "Solars aren't weird enough to come up with SMA!", but that's all an issue of flavor text. If the current writers really wanted to spin it that way, they could do so in a way that we could accept (with lots of grumbling, of course). The mechanics follow the narrative, not the other way around...

                                                            ​Besides, it's not as though mystic sorcerers *can't* be the hidden master that one must seek out! That's pretty much Dr. Strange's origin in a nutshell! Make Sidereal Sorcery something that only Solars can also master, with extr difficulty, and there you go...
