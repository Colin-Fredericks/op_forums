    Ehhh.

    I honestly find it hard to accept that the Usurpation could be pulled off without the Sidereals having to murder some of their own members immediately after the Great Prophecy. And no, I don't think it could have been over the years of prep after they decided on a course of action - I think that immediately, as soon as the prophecy was finished, they would have realized there were people in the room who couldn't be safely allowed to leave alive. What they were proposing involved killing some of their closest friends (in some cases, spouses or lovers), starting an enormous war with an inevitably massive death toll, and overthrowing their entire society.

    I flatly don't find it credible that all 100 Sidereals could completely trust each other to go along with the plan - and all it would take is a single one who refused to accept the necessity. All Tammiz Ushin has to do is say "welp, I informed twenty Solars, now the decision is set and we have no choice but to work with them!" Note, he wouldn't even have to inform the Solars that the Usurpation was a potential thing - he can present the prophecy as something that the Sidereals and Solars will work together to fix, and the other Sidereals will immediately be forced to go along with his plan as if the Usurpation was never an option. He knows that. The other Sidereals know that. No Sidereal whose willingness to go along with the Usurpation was in question could be allowed to leave the room alive. (And it's silly to suggest that the Sidereals would hesitate to kill him in that situation when they were already preparing to kill so many others.)

    That said, I think I started to diverge from the old devs' views on Sidereals and the Usurpation when I started to seriously consider the narrative implications of allowing Sidereal prophecy to be absolute in the sense that it is 100% narrative-fiat perfect when used at that level and every Sidereal has 100% absolute narrative-fiat understanding of this perfection. Nothing in the real world works that way, and while you can say the same thing about a lot of stuff in a fantasy setting, I feel that taking that route with something so central to the motivations of an entire faction undermines their usefulness for storytelling. Sidereals who have that level of guaranteed certainty for the most important and controversial decision they ever made cease to be people, as I understand the term - they're just logic-robots. It also leads to really unfortunate implications when you start connecting it to any sort of comparable real-world hard decisions - like I mentioned earlier, everyone who ever seized power in a coup has given the exact same justifications about why it was Absolutely Necessary and how Terribly Hard it is to rule on their own etc etc etc.

    I feel like it's a position taken in response to a lot of bad forum arguments and the like, with the intent of annihilating particularly bad takes on the Sidereals using a blast from the authorial canon, so to speak.

    The reality is that it's fine for the Great Prophecy to be potentially fallible as long as the Sidereals were confident enough in it (and had sufficient grounds for that confidence) that their decisions made in response to it at least make sense given the potentially-catastrophic outcome it predicted. This doesn't even require the possibility of a literal "oh it was just wrong" failure - the Sidereals are the best prophets in the setting, but even if Fate is absolute it still requires interpretation, and there's always room for aspects you didn't see. I don't think anyone ever gets to know with absolute certainty that their interpretation is correct, and I think the Great Prophecy is more interesting if it was a process of continuous refinement and interpretation on their prophecies until they finally said "all right, we know enough; it's never absolutely perfect, and yes, there's a chance there's something we're not seeing, but we have to make a decision based on the information we've gleaned." Because... that's how decision-making works, in reality. The urge to try and make their decision Absolutely Definitely Justified isn't healthy for the game or for storytelling in general; it just has to be a decision they could reasonably make at the time.

    Which touches on a lot of stuff in this thread, but it's also why I think it's important that, yes, there were a few Sidereals who, no matter how much time they spent refining and interpreting the prophecies and getting their accuracy closer to 99.99999%, were always going to say "no, fuck that, fuck this prophecy" and kick over the table at the end of it, because there's always some chance, however slim, that something has been overlooked and that they were willing to risk the universe on that chance rather than do what the prophecy implied they should. And the other Sidereals (who had worked with them for a long, long time) would have known who among them was like this and where it was headed, near the end.

    (I also think that this is honestly a better approach than the fixation on the Prophecy of Bronze vs. Prophecy of Gold thing. Having two competing prophecies doesn't even make as much sense, mythologically. I would generally prefer to frame the Gold Faction as Sidereals whose response to the Great Prophecy was "fuck that, we'll find a better way anyway." This sets up an actual serious philosophical conflict between them and the Bronze Faction - who went with the safer option or who viewed the Gold Faction as insanely, dangerously idealistic / blinding themselves to the truth because they didn't want to see it. If you give everyone their own prophecy it's not as interesting and doesn't really compare as much to real-world philosophical / political arguments.)

        Having re-read the entire thread, and seriously reexamined my position, this is what I'm left with.
        1) If you keep powerful God/Goddesses from the time of the Usurpation active in modern-day Heaven, you always keep the Usurpation politics alive, to at least some extent. Unlike the Sidereals who are moving on to new lives, and may develop wildly different political stances from life to life, a deity's politics are only going to change if they are given reasons to change them so compelling as to be equivalent to convincing the deity to alter its very nature. The relatively inflexible, unbroken memory-possessing, examples of extremes related to their domain coloring their views of everything they view through a pretty mono-focused lens has been a staple of Exalted forever. Uvanavu, God of Health, is rampantly pro-Solar because the level of good health among all living things in Creation is vastly lower than it was under the Solars. Even the wildlife was healthier, because optimum weather and such benefited them. Unless he's persuaded some force besides the Solars can (and will) restore the abundant good health the living things of Creation enjoyed in the First Age, his good health-lens will always make his view "I want Solars back, so we get the good health of the First Age back." To change this, without giving Uvanavu that alternative source of increased good health for Creation, you'd have to convince him the Solars will never raise Creation's health level as they did in the past. A daunting prospect, because you're trying to prove a negative, against the positive bias he associates with the Solars' First Age achievements.

        Any Sidereal (like Chosen of Serenity), who work in the Cerulean Lute of Harmony where he's a major player, are going to hear all about how the health of Creation was so much better in the old days, when the Solars ruled. As the God of Health, Uvanavu isn't just spitting rhetoric either. He's got the hard numbers for a detailed comparison/contrast of any year you'd care to name, with context-specific bias allowed for. The Chosen of Serenity can tune him out, but Uvanavu raising the issue in a hard-numbers way that checks out if the Joybringer wants to do any independent research in the Department's files, will certainly make the issue one that is real for the Joybringer, whatever side of the Solar issue they come down on. Even if they decide his position is irrelevant, because health is irrelevant when the Solars were piloting Creation towards total annihilation, this part of the Usurpation will be alive for the Joybringer in a way it wasn't for them before, because a living, breathing, sentient relic of the Usurpation-era is right there, co-signing reports on the latest plague that broke out in Town X, with a "See, what did I tell you?" expression.

        2) If you don't like two competing Prophecies, and you don't like the absolute precision of Sidereal prognostication (which, incidentally, I agree with you about) make it one Prophecy, with wildly diverging probabilities entirely open to interpretation. The Bronze didn't need confirmation to take the stand that ANY chance of Creation's total annihilation was too large. Half of this thread has been wildly divergent opinions on the interpretation of the Factions interpretations. While I love me some quantification, if you quantify this, even vaguely like "This result more likely, this result less likely" it always seems to jump to players trying to nail down the quantification to a percentage. I would be perfectly fine if the Prophecy said "The Solars may destroy Creation" and have the rest be those who became Bronze looking about at the Solars current sins-in-progress, the general state of the world, and concluding "May = Will." Then those who become the Gold don't even weigh in on the prognostication angle. Where the Bronze takes its stand on "Any % threat to Creation in its entirety is intolerable" the Gold goes "Prophecy isn't an exact science, and beyond that, Exalts have always tangled Destiny into a mess, ESPECIALLY when you try to parse a totality of an Exalt group in one sweeping prediction. The not-insignificant margin-for-error means you would be committing mass murder, and plunging all Creation into civilization-cracking conflict for something you can't even be certain WILL happen!

        I agreed in spirit with pretty much everything you said in your most recent post Aquillion , and I realize it sort of sounds like I'm disagreeing with you, while saying the same thing you are, but I always look at the fine-detail for abusable print. If a nuanced approach to the setting-point is shaped in such a way it can be brute-forced into an absolute, it will be. I've been guilty of that pretty much all week, and so has Epimetheus. We're essentially Exhibits A and B for why the Sidereal Prophecy needs to be one of the most carefully considered, reviewed and re-reviewed thing the Developers do for EX3. A bunch of people aren't going to be happy, whatever they do. Just like a bunch of people aren't going to be happy with the Lunar treatment, whatever it turns out to be (Because somehow it seems us Lunar fans want Lunars to be All Things to All People), but if they botch it, they'll be botching a lot more than the Sidereal splat.

                    The Gold Faction as the Sidereals who were willing to say "fuck destiny, we'll find a better way" even in the face of a seemingly clear prophecy to the contrary is more powerful and evocative than them just being people who preferred a different prophecy. It establishes the two factions as having fundamentally different outlooks on fate and necessity and on the role of Sidereals in relation to these things, rather than just having different views on Solars.

                    The way I see it is that the Bronze Faction are the Sidereals who believe that their role is to "choose the best option out of the ones available." The Gold Faction believes that their role is to "find the ideal option no matter what." These are similar but fundamentally irreconcilable views that make for an interesting philosophical and ideological conflict suitable for a heroic fantasy game. If you make it two prophecies then it ends up becoming "do they like the Solars or not?", which is much less interesting.

Most prophets in fiction are not perfectly accurate at reading and interpreting their prophecies, after all. And in the real world we make important decisions on much weaker information all the time. A prophecy that is 99.9999% accurate (with the 0.00001% chance probably being the possibility that the Sidereals have misunderstood it, rather than "lol fate was just wrong") still has more than enough weight to make every decision the Bronze Faction made at least defensible. That's a false dichotomy. There's room for a setting where the Gold Faction can say "they should have taken the risk; they should have been willing to keep looking for a better future" and the Bronze Faction can say "no, that's too idealistic; at the end of the day we had to take the information we had and act on it in order to avert the worst possible outcome", and have both arguments be defensible and interesting.

                                                            I was paraphrasing a quote, when I said "Any non-zero probability, rises to a 100% certainty, given an unlimited amount of time." It was said by a Nobel Laureate, in the context of talking about the nuclear arsenal, and how many close-calls to nuclear deployment have occurred due to errors in launch-detection systems. A scandinavian rocket launched near Antarctica was mistaken by Russian early warning systems with an (at the time) recently inspected and quantified error-level of .9% He was saying that since the error-level is non-zero, and since nuclear deployment can occur based almost entirely on what the Russians computers are telling them, eventually the point nine percent of the first level of redundancy will line up with the fractions of a percentage error-levels in the second and third layers of redundancy, and how even if it takes ten centuries, so long as the nukes stay pointed the way they're pointed, eventually BOOM.

                                                            I was drawing a parallel between that, and the vanishingly small chance of a being equivalent to a Primordial rising up once more out of the Wyld. The whole point of THAT being the Solars, Lunars and Sidereals were all, at least at one time, very concerned about such a possibility. A possibility that worried the Gods as well, which informed their decision to tell the Exalts about the Sword of Creation at all. Which was part of my pointing out how drawing a curtain over the OTHER threats Creation had faced during the First Age serves by default to strengthen the Bronze argument. Ie: If you remove the existence of any credible threat to Creation in its entirety BUT the Solars, that in itself is story-reductive.

                                                            Also, the "This is canon, and that's that" argument has never been a terribly persuasive one. Stupidity has a long history in Exalted, and trust me, we haven't seen the end of it just because a new edition is arriving. Anything that 1) Doesn't make logical sense, 2) Isn't considered fun by the majority of players who look at it, and 3) Storytellers find more reductive than additive generally gets binned.

                                                            As Aquillion said, the elder Lunars are still there. Add to that, the GODS who lived through it are still there (and Gods without agendas aren't Gods). You can't just say "Event X Happened" and leave it at that, and I think you know that Epimetheus. Nice attempted deployment of the turn-what's-a-question into a statement, however.

                                                            The developers ARE going to have to do better than the EX3 Core blurb on the Usurpation when they get to the Sidereals book. Word-count and book focus explains a general statement in Core, but once you reach the higher Yu-Shan resolution of the new Sidereals book, it's going to take more. It's not just explaining why the Sidereals would be united on the Usurpation. You have to explain what created the same unification of conviction among the divinities as well. They'll need to nail down what really triggered the point of dissent, etc etc etc.

                                                            To me, making the Bronze position the only logically defensible one by, as Aquillion stated, a blast of the authorial cannon would do the setting an incredible disservice. It's an approach with an inherently lower utility.
