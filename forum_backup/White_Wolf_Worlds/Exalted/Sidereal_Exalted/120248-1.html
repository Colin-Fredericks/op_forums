    Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
    As long as we're talking about annoying fan memes about the Sidereal Charmset...

    The Sidereal Charmset is not actually closed. The Maidens can add to it whenever. But for some bizarre reason fans seem to think it's impossible to make new Charms. It's as if they think the ST can't control the Maidens.
    Here's the thing. The book mentions that yes, technically it could happen, that a Sidereal could get some new Charms approved by the Maidens, but also that nobody has managed it in roughly forever despite constant attempts.

    When I'm playing a Sidereal, neither the ST nor I expect me to be able to casually do something that smarter, more capable, more influential, and just plain better at their jobs Sidereals have been trying to do for the last fifteen hundred years with a 0% success rateeasily. Things that nobody has been able to do before are arc seeds, not givens. Except that at some level nobody wants to bog the game down with the "not easy" path, because putting the entire circle through an arc just so I can buy some Charms (that aren't even going to solve a poblem because these are still Sidereal Charms) is boring for other players. So, end result: "I'll get to it later" and then the campaign ends before you ever got around to making new charms.

    In fact, funny thing - if the book simply had read "Sidereals can't make new charms, at all", especifically stated to be impossible, I'm pretty sure there would be MORE Sidereal custom charms being made. The fact that there's a theoretical escape clause, even if it's a clause that basically makes it completely impracticable for most real games, gets it enforced a lot harder.

                        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                        I get why that stops players, though I do dislike the way that people often see anything which hasn't been done before as impossible.
                        There's a difference between "nobody has done this before" and "people have been trying to do this continuously for centuries and nobody even got close to succeeding". The first one is a decent call to trying and seeing what happens. The second one is telling you that you're not supposed to succeed unless you make a really good effort. But starting a plot solely so you can have some new Charms that, again, aren't even really going to solve problems because that's not how Sidereal Charms work, is not a thing most people want to interrupt the ongoing game for.

                        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                        What I don't get is why it stops STs. They can just say "Mars has seen fit to grant you and your comrades a new Melee Charm". The return of the Solars and the disappearance of the Empress and the creation of Infernals and Abyssals is a great excuse, but you don't actually need an excuse.
                        As said, it's most often a psychological matter. People have less trouble waiving impossibility than "really hard", because if something is stated to be technically possible but really hard it feels like there's an "out" and if you really want it you could do it.

                        You need to actually sit down and think deeply about sidereals in an actual game instead of theorethical Sidereals to realize that in this case, this "really hard" and "impossible for all practical purposes" are the same thing in 90% of cases, and react accordingly.

                                Sidereal Charms aren't weird, not individually, but in a collective sense and for the sake of orienting them towards various functional goals, they often incentivize Sidereals acting in a weird manner.

                                I would also say that there are some Charms and synergies that reward a bit of context control, sometimes in ways that aren't readily apparent. Like making sure that you have vaguely defined appointments all over the place for the sake of Yellow Road, or using some of those Socialize spirit-controlling Charms to define any random room you own as a personal library and then arrange for information drops by locally recruited spies that you can constantly check in on with Efficient Secretaries.

                                Any of those Charms can be, and readily are, used exactly as they're written, but how they're written (interpreted through a lens of assuming that Sidereals are naturally about a bit of pedantic cheating) can let you use a bit of lateral thinking to apply them in non-obvious ways.

                                I would also say that "preparation" is not always strictly necessary, but can be useful (off the top of my head, I'd say one would prefer to use Avoidance Kata when one's approach to a situation gives them a bit of foreknowledge about where they'll end up), although I think it's mostly just about prayer strips.

                                        Originally posted by MrInsecure View Post
                                        Oh yes, one more irritating meme I'd like to address:

                                        Infernals Are Transhuman

                                        This is mostly a case of "You Keep Using That Word," since it's about the least useful and most off-putting way of describing how Infernals work without using the phrase "mandatory rape." Yes, Infernals are transformed by their Charms, in ways that other Exalted are not- hell, they Exalt by emerging from a Chrysalis. If the metamorphosis symbolism were any more obvious, Infernals would all come out with butterfly wings and be greeted by Ovid himself.

                                        The issue is that the word "transhuman" has a lot of real world baggage that comes with it- transhumanism is a real philosophical movement with real adherents and detractors, which has nothing to do with what the Infernals are doing. And unlike the use of "demon" or "god" in the context of Exalted, it's not a deliberate invocation of that real world philosophy, it's just fans being lazy and jumping on a meme because they forget that newbies will have exactly zero context for what they're talking about. So let's just kill this meme dead in the water and figure out a better way to talk about our favorite proto-Primordials, shall we?
                                        I agree wholeheartedly, especially since Infernals will be different to some degree in the new edition. Infernals weren't transhuman, they were alien.
