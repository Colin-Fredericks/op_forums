                Not saying that this needs to apply to Exalted, but... The classical concept of the hero is 'paragon of his society's virtues'. That can make a lot of classical heroes seem ... pretty not-moral. Achilles was a killer and a rapist, with a major case of the rages, but he's considered a hero by the ancient greeks because he was an awesome fighter who swayed the course of a ten year war, and when motivated by the loss of someone he loved, tore down an army. Odysseus was a philanderer with a cruel streak, but his cunning won a war, and his journey home threw him into conflict with sorcery, monsters, and gods which he prevailed against through an application of that same intellect. A lot of the things we see as 'evil' in these heroes are simply not important to the greek culture because their values are different from ours. They didn't consider women to be... well, people. They saw martial skill and loyalty as important, intellect doubly so. The other things didn't factor so much for them as a simple matter of values dissonance.

                When it comes to Solars, I'd say in some sense they should reflect that. Creation is a big place, there's room for a broad range of cultures, each of whom have their own values, their own perspectives, and their own dissonance. Some Immaculate-raised son of a Great House is going to have vastly different opinions about what 'good' or 'virtue' means, as compared to the various threshold societies.

                Again, not universally applicable here, but worth considering.

                    I agree, and it's also why I think it would be very difficult, if Solars had to be good, for STs and players to decide whether they are good or not. I mean, what if they make a character that seems okay and are then a bad person during the game, does their exaltation fall off? Are players going to write up characters who want vengeance for their dead sifu, and then the ST says "no, you're not good"? It doesn't really work.

                    Solars are supposed, as people have said, to emulate mythic heroes, and a lot of mythic heroes weren't that great by modern standards (don't ever make fun of their baldness!).

                    Every last Green Sun Prince, meanwhile, is one of the fifty most horrible humans alive in Creation. At best, you have high-functioning sociopaths who can be reasoned with so long as your interests and theirs align and you plan to betray them before they betray you. If your Infernal Exalted don't feel like Apostles or the God-Hand from Berserk, they are very little like my Infernal Exalted. The Yozis, you'll recall, have only 50 Essences with which to turn Creation into Hell. They would be super selective about their recruiting.
                    Yes, but those aren't the sort of people they would select, because they're too untrustworthy. They're likely to betray the Yozis. The Yozis after all, are not simply "evil".

                    What I'd expect them to do with their selection is to choose people loyal to the Yozis. For example, the signature Defiler was a member of a Yozi cultist, while the signature Malefactor hated the Unconquered Sun (which makes sense, being a dune person).

                    They want committed fanatics, not arsonmurderrapists.

                                            This came up in an exploration of the Zenith caste, but I think it applies to Exalts as a whole. Basically many a Zenith's message boils down to "do the right thing and be righteous." Problem is Exalts are still human and it's not like they're given a device that lets them know if they're about to do something wrong. So an Exalt will do what they think is right, but that in no way means that will be moral, right, just, or not horrifyingly evil acts.

                                            For example there's a character mentioned in the history of "Wiseman's Fear" who apparently did more to advance medicine than anyone else in the history of the setting. Of course he did this by doing such things as dissecting people alive, peeling off layers of the body to see how they work, and all kind of other horrible and monstrous things, but there's a note in his journal that implies he did all of this because he felt that these actions were for a greater good. (He's only come up once and I'm still reading no spoilers if anything else comes up on him please.) Or there's a character in the Stormlight Archives series. Due to a number of events apparently the guy has a plan to save the world (He's pretty sure anyway he's still translating the plan and he wasn't all there when he came up with it.) This plan involves basically the assassination of a large amount of the settings world leaders in very specific methods; this plan involves starting wars; this plan involves a room full of people of all ages having their blood slowly drained out of their bodies, because sometimes a person will give part of a prophecy or something in their dying moments. All of this he feels is justified because it will save the world. That's part of the conflict in Exalted. When you have the power to change the world, how do you exercise it, and should you exercise it?

                                                        Hello, noble peepsicles of assorted flavours. After searching deep within myself, and on this fine forum, I've been unable to find an answer to this simplest of questions. Are Solars the good guys?

                                                        I've played exactly one session of Exalted, although I'm more familiar with the setting and mechanics, because A: I'm the only player in my group able to read the goddamn book apparently, and B: Our ST is a giant Exalted fanboy. I created a character in the expectation of !Heroic Endeavour Style! and !Innocent Saving Prana!, but after our first session, I'm finding that my party are... not the good guys. Maybe that's just the people I'm playing with, but we've got one devious snake-tongued trust-betraying sidereal-in-solar's-clothing, one Lawful Evil Bureaucrat, one straight-up rape-and-murder pirate and one ex-mercenary bent on killing every dishonourable employer he's ever had (does this count as good?). In the middle of this party, I'm playing... basically Jesus. Am I doing it wrong? Are they doing it wrong? Are we -supposed- to be actual Anathema, or is there simply something I'm missing?

                                                        I really need help with this problem before Sunday if possible, because I like my character, but if evil Solars are totally legitimate, i'm going to have to roll up a new character.

                                                        "Evil" Solars are legitimate. No faction of Exalted are "good" or "evil" by definition. Free will is a central component of Exaltation, so how people chose to use their power varies mightely. The Unconquered Sun's will that drives Exaltation (whether he's consciously guiding it or not) obviously leans away from Exalting someone who wants all of Creation to be destroyed - but his definition of "heroism" beyond that can definitely have a Bronze Age selfish-but-awesome flavor to it, or ends justify the means hard-man-of-hard-decisions etc etc (he's ineffable like that). Work out with your Storyteller and the other players what type of campaign they want to run and proceed accordingly.

                                                        If another player is actually intending to have his character commit rapes, that is definitely something I would personally look askance at, but not something that inherently contradicts canon. Just good taste.
