    Originally posted by Elfive View Post
    That said you'll probably get away with a speciality that's in theory hyper specific but in practice will always apply like melee (my daiklave).
    Absolutely. Your Speciality is supposed to basically always apply. But if the ST wants to catch you out they should have a vector.

    Like, if you're a brain surgeon then you're going to have a specialty in brain surgery and probably won't ever be carrying out OBGYN exams, using your brain surgery specialty on every roll you make because that's literally what your job is -- all day every day. But if you unexpectedly get trapped in a ski lodge with a pregnant woman giving birth and you are the only doctor available then you're going to have to roll Medicine without your Specialty.

    A really narrow Specialty that basically always applies is fine -- but it can be pretty broad. Taking Melee (Unarmored) and refusing to ever wear armor is fine in my book. You just can't take Melee (Whilst breathing) or Melee (Whilst armored or unarmored) or Melee (Any-weapon-but-swords, or Swords). There's got to be some reasonable position the ST can force you in.

                                                Originally posted by Reminiscent Oasis View Post

                                                I'm thinking it would be something akin to the Order command action (directs actions, gives bonus dice), but could benefit other PCs and characters of equal importance, rather than just a mob of NPCs. I don't think a direct port of the Order action would work since, hypothetically, a War-centric character could be tossing out a bunch of bonus dice if they brought an excellency to bear. Maybe if they had to divvy up the bonus dice equally to all affected characters (minimum 1 perhaps?), that'd help balance it out.

                                                But I'm still getting a feel for the balance of 3E so I could be way off.
                                                As cool as that would be, the balance point would have to be pretty low, especially if this was just an action and not a charm. Ordering somebody to attack and then having them activate Invincible Fury of the Dawn and get like 60 free attack dice would definitely be imbalanced. There’s some charms somewhat similar to what you might want though. Sold Melee gas Guard Breaking Technique, which lets you perform a distract gambit and in addition to initiative transfer the benefactor gets 3 bonus attack dice. There’s also With One Mind in Dragonblooded Linguistics that makes it so when you use a distract gambit, even if it fails, the benefactor gets double 7s on their next attack, that’s only at Essence 5 and it’s Perilous, which means it can’t be used in crash, and it’s a simple activation.

                                                The most balanced way to do it as a gambit is probably like a distract but instead of you losing initiative and adding to theirs to increase damage, you lose initiative and add to their attack pool. I would also say stipulate that it only adds to their next attack, so it doesn’t multiply to insanity with multiattack charms.
