    Alchemical Cities in Creation, Level 5 Demenses, and geopolitics

    10-03-2020, 08:14 AM
    So I'm trying to create a shard where the majority of the Exalted are Dragonblooded and Alchemicals. Autochthon is down for the count, either dead, in hibernation, or missing. Thankfully the blood of the Dragons are as fecund as ever, and Alchemicals can be built even outside Autochthon.

    The issue is that I now have to build a world where there are at least several hundred Alchemical Exalted. Several hundred Celestial Exalted, all of whom are at worst apathetic and at best actively helping humanity. Hobbled somewhat, yes, and somewhat having limitations that other Exalted don't have. But still exceptionally powerful.

    Ignoring the wider setting questions...

    Let's say that you have a level 5 manse and you plop down an alchemical city down into Creation, somewhere in a place similar to the Threshold. Loads of nasties, Raksha, cholera, and all that stuff. You're counted as lucky if you have a horse or ox to bring your cart to the market, and you are near an urban area if you have a cobblestone road. The old technological primitiveness. And then this Alchemical city smashes its way onto the nearby level 5 manse, with a population of Autochthonians, and a dozen or so Alchemicals. After they finished the shakedown and secure for themselves a source of food and water and essence, they start affecting the world around them.

    The city has municipial charms that help it adapt to the surroundings. A shield against inclement weather. A method of dealing with sewage and fast-growing food and water. And a working knowledge of how Creation works. The question is how would it affect their surroundings? The city has multiple factories, high level crafting and bureaucracy charms, as well as access to champions who are stronger than Dragonblooded if slightly more limited.

    So far I have them as a the equivalent of city-states, with chemical plants inside to create reagants and alchemical products that serve as fertilisers and explosives for mining. Bureaucratic plans are drawn up within the city-states, mean to maximize productivity from the mines and farmlands and trade routes, while the specialized forges within create Superior and Perfect equipment that are the envy of any other craftsmen in the lands. The entire thing is like a bronze age command economy, with a centralized city state taking in the resources from the country side and pumping out more complex products. The only difference is that since its a Celestial Exalt with charms made from the magical materials, the scale is way bigger and the effects are a hell of a lot more effective.

    The political structure is that of City-states. City-states descended from one-another are generally friendly but its not unheard of for them to have a fight. Nation-states defined by borders are rarer now, since there is far far more concentration of power inside individual cities.

                      I'm trying to flesh out how a setting with more alchemicals inside would look like. Definitely more technology. But by how much? After all Alchemical cities are high essence elders who are capable of crafting arifacts far more easily than Dragonblooded. Even ignoring the Artifact question, Alchemical cities have better resources, far better education (there's several homebrewed mass training charms), and probably far more bureaucracy. Farms in the vicinity of an alchemical city might suddenly transition fast to mechanised harvesters and large numbers of specialists that bring in the expertise to build bridges, construct manses, and deal with diseases, there by making shit go far faster.

                          Hey Accelerator hope your feeling better. Anyway, Ecoonnooomicccs Nerrrrrds, Assemble!

                          You've just created a Boom Town. All of the local communities and the nearest City States now have a new weight on the planets-and-trampoline gravity analogy, in an Economic sense. You have a city capable of producing Desirable Goods and everyone around is gonna start doing mental money math to see if they can blow this whole farming thing for a load and become a middleman or merchant at your doorstep. More on that in a second: lets look at your citystate first.

                          Since we're living in Fantasy and command economies work (joke of the week, Sors), you have a set of bottlenecks and time constraints. You need X food for Y people which take Z farms W days (assuming food production tech exists and your not just ordinary farming). Well, now you have a population limit, which is a worker limit, which is a goods produced limit. So any trade opportunities work out on a simple Algebra format: Is it beneficial to trade 10 clockwork crossbows (100 manhours) for 50 bushels of wheat (300 manhours?. It becomes a fun little exercise for your economists to work out trade ratios, and then the needs of the Market and surrounding geopolitics changes that valuation. Its basically Recettear/Stardew Valley meets Sim City.

                          But of course, you have to take care of those silly people and their dumb wants and needs. Sure you can make wine, tea, and tobacco in your Glass House of Heat for the plebians to have their Luxury Rations, but those ingrates are gonna want to have this thing from the South called Chocolate and so Chocolate merchants come in and people spend their Resource Tokens on outside goods. And then their gonna want different wine, tea, and tobacco from Other Places because its Different. (IMPORTANT jokes aside, don't fall into the idea that Export Good Import Bad. This is probably the biggest giveaway that someone with an opinion on Economics has never studied Economics) This triggers the next thing: Mass Merchant Migration, aka the Boom Town

                          So now your Shining Tech City has its staple foodstuff growing and its buying goods from other people and selling them Finished Products (and even Raw Materials like gold which they can't get themselves), so Jan Ni, the ex farmer, is now doing quite well for himself turning Crossbows into Wheat, buying low and selling high. He gets rich. Now everyone who sees him thinks "I bet I could do that too". So Gre Gori the carpenter who is sick of making furniture all night after a 10 hour day farming decides to open a shop outside Technopolis fixing wagon wheels and selling caravaner stuff to merchants to make their life easier. He buys food with his money. same goes for Blacksmiths, Leatherworkers, Horse.... people, and tailors and everyone else with a side hustle. But they need food since they stopped subsistence farming so now there's a market for other mechants to go farther away and buy food from distant villages and bring it over to feed these people, and they are gonna carry goods back with them to trade for the food, and maybe you can see how this whole thing grows.

                          So you have Technopolis chugging away like a (literal) machine while 50 feet out the main door is a boom town of people selling things to Technopolis, and people selling things to them, and distant villages opening new fields and hiring on laborers to provide more food for all these people trying to get rich. Technopolis, like any good communist state, becomes capitalist ('in secret"), because it turns out making Steampunk Gizmos is basically the equivalent of growing food where Trade is concerned, and you can make 100 Light Crystals a day and that becomes Rice measured in tonnage. This is pretty much the point of City Skylines or Stardew where everything is basically solved and all thats left is to spend your money on getting more money and you get bored.

                          That's not what happens in here. That's where the swamp gator in the river of trade comes out: Politics.

                          So now what you have isn't a city, it isnt a factory, it isn't a utopia of the working man, what you have is a Threat. A threat to people who live in a place called Status Quo and don't like what you're doing to it. Trade isn't a 0 sum game (each person's gain is another's loss) but it can be detrimental to others in the short or even long term. Merchants who used to go to Dynastopia aren't going there, because Light Crystals and Auto Crossbows are more profitable than Candles and Longbows. Technopolis will sell a pound of gold ore for 1000 bushels of wheat, Dynastopia will sell a pound of gold ore for 5000 bushels of wheat, and you have to be an approved gold buyer, which takes political connections, bribes, a daughter to marry off, etc. So in a single year Dynastopia has lost more than half of its merchants, which isn't looking good for a city with landed aristocrats obsessed with status. So they decide to open a frank discussion on trade, productivity, brainstorm new avenues of resource gatheri no I'm fucking around they go to war.

                          Dynastopia doesn't really produce paper, ink, wine, tobacco, and rice. Dynastopia produces Violence. and they are quite happy to sell you a receipt for Violence, in the terms of "keep buying our other stuff and we won't sell you Violence". And you know what? this system works! its great. Nobody else is making Ink, Paper, or Whatever because nobody else can outproduce violence. This is true of real life, but its extra-double-triple true of Exalted which has hereditary super powers and a distinct lack of indiscriminate artillery fusillades. Nobody can compete with Dragonbloods showing up in Magic Armor with Magic Sticks and killing people until peace is declared. So Dynastopia send Technopolis a little friendly note that says "Literally stop existing or we're going to kill the shit out of you and take your stuff" But what's this? Technopolis has it's own domestic production of Violence. Surprise! its war time.

                          Dragonblood are a renewable resource but they really don't like being called that. So Dragonblood have to decide if its worth spending Dragonblood ... blood on stopping the Alchemicals but for basically the first time in their life they have opposition, and that opposition has Shiny Fancy Things called Weapons and Dragonblood have been having a great go of other people not having those (in a way that works against artifact armor, etc). So now its gonna become a Siege and people are gonna have to actually Die and if you thought losing merchants was expensive, let me call your attention to how Nation In Bankruptcy level expensive War is. This is where Even Bigger Politics comes out. Essentially, Dynastopia has to get other Citystates and Great Houses to pay for their war, while Technopolis can maybe convince other places to help on Defense. Eventually this is handled between the Major Movers and Shakers and suddenly a ragtag group of homeless omnisexual idiots with golden weapons show up and decide this is Their Problem Now, and yeah, you're in an Adventure Scenario now.

                          To sum it all up: 1)City starts making Things, 2)People come for Things, 3)Other people get mad about this -> 4)War? Remember also that up until the very end, the exalted played no role in this scenario. The beat of this drum is made of human desire, and exalted can interfere or assist with a lot of stuff, but they ultimately only change the speed of whats going on.

                          And that's Economics for you, folks.

                          Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.
