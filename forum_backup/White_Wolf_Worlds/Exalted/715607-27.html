            Originally posted by Irked View Post
            The things that trouble me about 3e - and the concerns that, left unchecked, are going to be a mounting problem as the number of moving packets in the game increases - are the following, in roughly decreasing order of severity:

            1) The rules fundamentally fail to describe common, basic interactions. These aren't weird, niche, edge-cases - they're your meat-and-potatoes concerns. In what order do you declare Charms, at the start of an attack? What are the rules for counterattacks - when are they made, by default, and can you use multiple counterattack Charms in response to a single provocation? Are gambits Decisive attacks, or are they similar to Decisive attacks? What happens when a magical flurry Clashes with another attack? What happens if you Crash (or die) partway through an action? When do you actually start suffering from Onslaught penalties? What happens if two effects conflict, and neither has explicit conflict-resolution rules?

            2) The rules that do exist are sloppy and unclear in numerous places. The lack of an explicit sequence of combat steps leads to painfully vague resolutions, in places. Charm types are used inconsistently, with identical effects written with multiple different types - Reflexive/Instant, Reflexive/Turn, Permanent/Permanent, and so on. Numerous Charms don't actually follow the typing rules; there are, for instance, many Reflexive Charms that neither create a reflexive action nor enhance an unrolled action, and yet, by the book, that's all they should do. Worse, this often makes it unclear whether a given Reflexive Charm is actually creating a new action or not. Several Charms introduce notation ("recurring 10s") that is not picked up on or repeated by any other effect. Some Charms seem not to know that "3a" is a legitimate cost, rather than something that has to be described in text; others seem to think you can spend yourself into negative Initiative as long as a Charm lacks the Perilous keyword (you cannot). Penalties are introduced only in effects that remove them (the Ambidextrous merit, Steel Devil Form), and general system effects (training animals, for instance) are described only inside Charms that modify them. On the other end of the scale, some Charms lack their own rules - Fivefold Bulwark Stance will never tell you what "certain penalties" it removes unless you read its sidebar; the rules for activating multiple Awareness Charms are also tucked away on a different page (rather than, say, Keyworded into the relevant Charms), as are, say, the rules for determining whether you need to Aim with a Charm or not.

            3) Even where the Charms are not unclear, they are too often overwrought, wordy, and uninteresting to read through. Numerous Lore Charms include special call-outs that say, basically, "Supernal works as it always does for this Charm." That's both unnecessary and confusing; it causes the reader to wonder, "Is this not true of the Charms that lack this clause?" Literal dozens of Charms provide only 10% die boosts or 1-2m cost reductions, backed by descriptions worthy of the Princes of the Universe lyrics - and virtually none of these effects are standardized, to cut down on the mental overhead. By far the worst thing about reading the 3e Solar set is that it isn't fun, because for every power that made me drop my jaw and exclaim, "Wow!" I was reading a page or more of Charms that tweaked the rules slightly. I'm not saying the latter aren't powerful - they are! - I'm just saying that they're a poor argument for 200+ pages of rules. Exalted has to sell itself, not just to me but to any new player looking to pick up the game, and too many of these Charms do not do that.

            4) The game doesn't know what it wants to be. Is it a competitive thing, where the Storyteller throws Limit Breaks at you at dramatically terrible time, or is the ST dialing that back carefully? Are we trusting the player to invent facts and use the powerful XP-generating engines he has, or do we need numerous sidebars tut-tutting at players for using mechanics in too effective of a way? Is this a tight, rules-heavy CCG-style engine, or is it a natural language, "do what feels right" system? Are we relying on carefully-specified gamist-style systems, or is this kind of a Fate-ish "Eh, your background gives you different aspects you can tag to know things?" The answer seems to be, in all cases, yes.
            Please, please submit this on the KS. I truly doubt it will make a difference, but at least they will have it and consciously be saying, "no, we don't consider these problems to fix," if they ignore it.
