    Okay, here's something I can use some clarification on. Can Glorious Solar Arsenal be used multiple times while still having Motes committed? Like, can I use it to summon SunSword in turn 1, and then SunArmor in turn 2?

Does that proc Golden Champion Glory every time? Could I theoretically dump half my motes in one turn into Glorious Solar Heavy Mob Cemetery and gather more power than god?

    However, my understanding is that yes you can have it activated more than once to have both a weapon and armor, for example, but you commit essence both times and it takes 2 turns (and uses your step 1 charm activation in both). Best to do it before starting the fight, if you can...

            Originally posted by autXautY View Post
            My impression is that actions have steps, not turns. So it would be step 1 of an attack, or of a social roll, or another action.
            Agreed. Normally you only act once per turn, but you're right; if I flurry or otherwise make 2 attacks then each action will have a step 1 where I can activate a charm. Although the rules do say:
            Originally posted by Chapter 4
            No matter what, a character can only take two actions per turn. This includes actions taken in response to things, like counterattacks.
            That explicitly says actions taken in response to something, like counterattacks, are limited that way too. I think that means if you want to do a standard attack and use this charm on step 1, and that gives you the Build Power action, those are your two actions for the turn. So my reading is that we couldn't flurry the original action and also get the bonus action from this charm in the same turn, because then we would be having 3 total actions in the turn.

            Originally posted by autXautY View Post
            But Glorious Solar Arsenal isn't linked to any action - it's reflexive, which in previous versions meant it creates its own action. Which it's presumably step 1 of?
            The rules for reflexive charms in update 18 say "If it creates an action, it will specify." So, the use of a reflexive Glorious Solar Arsenal doesn't say it creates an action for its main effect of creating the weapon/armor, but it does say (at higher prereqs) that it creates an action for Build Power or the other choices. I think that action would have a Step 1, and you could then use a charm for it's step 1, but even if you used Glorious Solar Arsenal again at that point you couldn't get another Build Power in the same turn because you are limited to a max of 2 actions per turn anyway. But it would be a way to get both armor and a weapon in one turn, if you wanted to commit 2 motes.
            Originally posted by autXautY View Post
            Even if it doesn't create it's own action, my impression is you can activate a charm during a step of someone else's action - you have to, or you wouldn't be able to use charms to defense - so you could possibly activate it every time some took an action you didn't have another charm to use during, which should be fairly often (every action an ally takes, every time an enemy attacks someone other than you, etc)
            Originally posted by autXautY View Post
            Also, If you can do this, it doesn't even use up all your motes - you can reflexively de-commit them all to regain the motes
            I know you can activate charms on specific steps of someone else's action, so to use a defensive charm you need a charm that works on step 2 of their combat action (and probably 4, 7 and 8 too). But I don't think you can activate a charm on step 1 of someone else's action, that is their declaration phase, not yours. The defender gets to declare their response in step 2. Could be wrong, but letting every character act on every step of every other character's turn seems against the intent to me. I think you act on your turn, and you react on other turns only on specific steps of their action if you are their target.

            The rules for un-committing motes say that you can't use that same charm through the next turn, there is a cooldown before you can use it again. This was discussed with the devs on discord today, and they confirmed that you could only use this tactic to try to build power once every other turn. You'd be able to use the weapon or armor only half the rounds, and couldn't use excellencies or other step 1 charms on those turns either, but you could get an attack and a build power every other round as a way around the rule against flurrying them together.

                        Hard Bargains are definitely a cool idea, but I'm reserving judgement until I see them in play. I like to think I'm a reasonably creative person, but I'm just not sure whether I'll regularly be able to thinki of something on the spot which seems to be (1) in the spirit of the original influence, (2) not quite as bad, and (3) not totally toothless. I would argue that, in the example, Heather's metafictional Storyteller failed at the second point: "sell out the other real-life people I'm playing with" is usually a much harder sell than "sell out this minor NPC". Would I have done better?

                        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                        "I am Gazban, Merchant of Hearts, Fourth soul of Orabilis, End of all Wisdom, Sixth soul of Cecelyne. A somewhat pathetic creature for a second circle demon I admit, but I have this....oh yes, the Pictarious Rex, the badge of kingship for your home, the item you've sought as its rightful heir for these past 90 years. It's yours, but only in trade, in return you must tell me the location of your uncle's hidden manse. A warning though, in case you were thinking of simply taking it from me, the instant we join battle away it goes to my vault in Malfeas, never to be seen again."
                        The Exalt takes a Hard Bargain. Now what?

                        Offering up something else in exchange for the Pictarious Rex isn't a Hard Bargain, because it would be literal bargaining in-character. That's no different from refusing outright. I suppose that I could offer to let them refuse this offer only by personally deciding that opposing Gazban matters more than being the rightful heir, renouncing that Intimacy, but "radically alter your character concept" isn't a much better ask than "betray the PCs." Reducing an Intimacy from Major to Minor (as the book seems to suggest) has the opposite problem: it just doesn't seem to change very much, even assuming I'm insistent on them not increasing it again the next time they do something king-ey.

                        Maybe in actual play, really ambiguous cases like that don't come up often. Maybe I'll quickly start presenting nastier social rolls with fallback positions already in mind. I don't know. I hope so, because as I said, they're a cool idea.
