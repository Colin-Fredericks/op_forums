    Can a human change? Yes. Is it difficult? Yes.

    Can a (non-asexual) human deny their sexual urges and live a life of celibacy? It's very difficult, but there are people who've done it. It's not really how we've evolved, but it's possible. They will still have the urge to act a certain way, but it can be resisted. Of course, some people are better at this than others.

    Can a human deny their urge to eat and drink? No, that'd kill them. But it's possible to restrict your urge, in fact most people do. Some are better or worse at it.

    Can a human deny their urge to socialise? Live an entirely solo life? Again, very difficult, but some people have gone off to live as solo hermits, etc.

    Can an Agatae restrict their jealousy of other steeds? Can a spider-demon put up with the barking of dogs? Can a Neomah not collect samples of organic matter to babies? Can a Blood Ape be a pacifist?
    These are all possible, but very difficult. Most demons are just not going to want to, and even if they did, most will fail. Humans can barely manage to be pacifists, let alone Blood Apes.


    We actually debated this in our last session. We're having a Conclave of the Cult of the Illuminated, and one member of the local Cult is Mara, the Shadow-Lover, who was converted by the Lunar social character in the party. So the question was whether it's possible for a Demon to genuinely convert to the Cult of the Illuminated, or whether they'll always be untrustworthy. Experts on demonology (like the Zenith and Eclipse solar-sorcerer) were consulted, and it was decided in the end that while most demons were fundamentally unhuman (there was a debate about whether the alien nature of demons is evil, or just different) and so would seem evil to humans, not all demons were definitely evil.


    Of course, this is my opinion, but I think the redeemability of demons is something that can be altered to your table. I know people have mentioned Jenna Moran quotes, but I think 3rd ed has moved away from these hard-and-fast rules about "heroism" as a hard-baked quality.

    I'll also point out that the line has always had Primordials and Devas that switched sides to the Incarnae; Gaia and Autocthon of course, but later there was stuff like the Deva who became one of the Gods in Luna's court.

    And one last thing; while first-circle demons are in some sense bred for a purpose, they're living beings, not robots. They're not absolutely programmed, they're not perfect automotons for the will of their creators (who are not, after all, all-powerful).
    Except Baidaks, of course.

                    Okay, to rephrase it; humans have various instincts and urges. It is very difficult to completely resist these instincts and urges, but it is possible. Most people are able to resist them to some extent when it would cause them problems (for example, people can resist their hunger pangs when they're worried about getting fat, or can't afford enough food but believe that solving this issue via crime, etc, would be worse than the hunger pangs). Only a few people can resist them entirely, but such people do exist.

                    So I'd think all the same things would apply to demons. Most Teodozja can resist proselytising about the Yozis when necessary (for example, when a monk threatens to kill them if they keep doing it), but only a very small number can decide to live a life where they never proselytise about the Yozis. But such Teodozja do exist.
