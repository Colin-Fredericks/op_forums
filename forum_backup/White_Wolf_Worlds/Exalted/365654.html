                                      I highly doubt kilomotes are still a thing. Of all the ways to represent vast unfathomable power in the setting, "1000 motes" is about as boring as it can get; and the whole conceit behind them was based on Yozis using Infernal Exalted charms (intended for PCs) and therefore needing something else on top of them.

                                      The Yozis in 3e probably don't have kilomotes because they probably don't have mechanics for mote pools or anything of that nature. If so (assuming you haven't given them equally outrageous charm costs, which defeats the purpose), why are you making it such a boring protracted process? If not, why give them any mote pool at all rather than throttling their powers in other ways?

                                                          Okay. I'm seeing a number of questions along these lines, and I understand. The book says things like 'Essence use disturbs destiny' and all that, so it's pretty easy to make assumptions along the lines of 'The raw power radiating out damages the giant fate machine!'

                                                          What you have to understand is that 'What happens if 1000 motes' isn't an interesting question because the volume of essence is only tangentially related to the Loom. The Loom of Fate has descriptive and predictive functions, and it's totally capable of tracking enormously powerful things. Greater Elemental Dragons, mountain-sized monsters, entire cities of sorcerers- those aren't automatically disruptions in the lines of destiny.

                                                          What disrupts the Loom of Fate is when a things happen which had basically no chance of happening. If your Solar casts Rain of Doom and melts a city to nothing, that came out of nowhere. The disruption is equal exactly to the Loom being wrong about everything in that area suddenly melting in a torrent of acid. Using the proper magic, or planning, you can mitigate that entirely by letting Heaven know that someone is going to melt everything with acid rains.

                                                          If you use those thousand motes to temporarily look really cool, then you'll get virtually no discernible disruption at all. If you use the thousand motes to transform an entire nation into a pillar of fire which spews out fire-birds to fly around and drop fire-shit on people, it will be a massive disruption (although not something the Loom hasn't seen before). If you use it to create a creation-spanning megaplague which kills 100% of everyone, you essentially break the Loom.
