                                    Originally posted by Greyman View Post
                                    The who, what, where now? Oh, Fair Folk Lorelei, Beguiling Idyll charm, page 536. Why didn't you say so?

                                    ​Well, it supplements the inspire action, and if successful, instils an emotion and makes the target ignore danger related to it. So, the target can be inspired to want to kiss the Lorelei and will be beguiled into ignoring dangers associated with that (like the Heart Eating Kiss charm for instance), and, under that influence, then might be persuaded to "kill the guy" to get a kiss; but would still more probably be inclined to go straight for the kiss unless something else is in the way.

                                    ​Though, yes, when evaluating what's the easiest and best way to obtain the kiss, danger isn't a consideration.
                                    Remember that an Inspire action creates a foothold similar to a Major Intimacy for the purpose of persuasion. You cannot use an Inspire action alone to make someone turn traitor to everything they know, but it's enough to help you clear the final hurdles. The benefit of Beguiling Idyll is that it lets you ignore the implicit Defining intimacy of self-preservation / the unacceptable orders rules that usually prevent people from doing something openly suicidal.

                                    This is of more use against ordinary people than player characters, generally, because heroes are used to acting according to their ideals rather than according to what would keep them alive. In other words, they probably have intimacies that would get in the way. Take the example in the book! A normal mortal captain might be persuaded to crash his ship into the rocks because he no longer perceives any danger for him in doing so, but the last Eclipse captain I ran never would. She loves her ship; it's a significant intimacy! Never mind whether or not anyone would survive, you just don't destroy quality craftsmanship like that. It's disrespectful.
