        Originally posted by MoroseMorgan View Post
        Also, doesn't TAP require you be able to move, so wouldn't it only work on the first attack?

        Funnily enough, going by a strict reading, the answer to that question is "yes, except if your opponents are in the air at less than short range". I originally wanted to make that point, but the "if the Exalt uses this Charm to move to another range band, it counts as her movement action for the round" clause tripped me up - I thought "so if the character uses Invincible Fury of the Dawn's movement clause to propel herself towards her foes, she doesn't move with TAP, therefore her move action isn't used up". However, I forgot that it specifically says "aerial targets up to short range, or ground targets at another range band", which means you can't use TAP after moving into close range with movement granted by IFoD.

        So, going by strict RAW, you can only use TAP once per round unless you have access to powers that grant you extra movement actions (such as One Extra Step in Solar Athletics) or you're attacking aerial targets who are less than a range band away from you (in which case, you can still activate the Charm due to the "up to" clause, and you're not using up your movement action because you're not moving to another range band).

        This is weird and slightly funny emergent behavior, but I think it's legit.

                                                Originally posted by Phoenix_Kensai View Post
                                                I would like to see it, if you ever feel like writing it up. This topic has been interesting so far, and I haven't been in many games, so I feel like your posts have helped me understand how the game works in play and what players might be capable of.
                                                So, lemme flip the script. How about I just go through each ability, give a little description on the themes, the coolest things you can do with each, and contrast a bit with the Solar version, and why you might want to play them.. Let's do this and get to sleep at a reasonable time.

                                                Archery

                                                A theme that pervades this charmset is a reminder that the wood in your bow and arrows was once alive and can be again. Your bow can become a tree for you to have cover behind, turning any plant into arrows, and having arrows explode into grasping vines. Speaking of Life Swelling Sap Strike, that's something else that comes up a fair bit in this tree, special shots. Where a Solar has like 1 or 2 of these that supplement attacks the Dragonblooded ones instead form the base for an entirely new action. Oftentimes totally modifying the purpose of a shot into a social counterattack that deals physical damage, or an arrow that ignites and explodes into a fireball on impact.

                                                Another theme present is the military sniper style guardian angel. They can snipe incoming archery attacks out of the air, both protecting her allies and even punishing the attacker. Awesome teamwork.

                                                From a mechanical point of view they differ from Solars in another way, and that's aiming. The Solar charmset has a lot of ways to enhance aim speed, or attack without aiming at all. Dragonblooded on the other hand are the exact opposite, a lot of the special moves require aiming, with the only way to shorten it being a signature charm that locks you out of all the other signatures.

                                                Overall, very exciting and fun!

                                                Athletics

                                                What does athletics do? It lets you run fast, lift things, swim, jump, all that stuff. The Solar charmset is really focused hard on just increasing the scope of those abilities. You run faster and faster, can leap multiple range bands, and eventually just ignore the strength requirements for lifting literally anything, deal damage.

                                                Dragonblooded get to do much the same, just not quite half as good and often with elemental restrictions, like needing to stand on the ground. On the other hand they can straight up fly. The closest Solar analogue requires a combat target and doesn't let you do anything except fly to it and stick to it, you can't even change targets until you touch the ground again. So yeah, here we've actually got a charm for the Terrestrials which is (kind of) in theme with an ability that's a more powerful effect than the Solar version.

                                                There's one other theme present here, burning the crap out of people. There are no less than four charms that set your opponent on fire when you run toward/past them. The teamwork charm here is the climbing one, it lets you carve handholds for your self with the option to leave them for your allies.

                                                Very unique, even more mobile than Solars and totally destructive to enemies and environments.

                                                Awareness

                                                Solars again, super straight forward. They can see the beating of a fly's wings on the horizon and feel the vibration of a spider crawling on the roof.

                                                Dragonblooded this time around are very different. Instead of boosting their senses in general they're good at picking out specific things only, namely threats. People who mean to do her harm, disguised people, poisons, specific chosen sounds. There's also an interesting mechanic of moving your point of perspective, either letting your senses ride along with somebody else, or allowing you to send them anywhere along a body of water that you're touching.

                                                Of course, it also lets you kick ass. Both by disabling enemy's senses, and eventually destroying their motes of essence.

                                                Very evocative of an intrigue laden game, where the most important thing you need to see is an assassin.

                                                Brawl

                                                Back to combat with Brawl, Solar brawl is full of powerful and kinetic attacks, onslaught penalties, and crushing grapples. Stuff like Heaven Thunder Hammer flinging enemies around, to going super Saiyan and firing a Kamehameha wave.

                                                The Dragonblooded ones have the crushing grapples and onslaught too, just way less powerful. What they lack in power though they make up by allowing you to use elements to make grapples and attacks at range, before Solars get the Kamehameha and easier to activate even. It's also got a thing for dragging your enemies all over the place.

                                                Teamwork plays a big role in this tree, enhancing distract gambits and doing such devastating things as setting your target's hardness to zero. With a full circle of allies around that means that your foe is taking a lot of damage from the animas and can just keep getting hit with lethal damage until the thousand cuts disintegrate him.

                                                Very much like the Solar version in mechanical theme, but loving the cooperation opportunities.

                                                Bureaucracy

                                                Solar bureaucracy is a really varied and broad tree. It gives bonuses all over to social transactions, protecting your organization, and, of course, boosting project actions.

                                                Terrestrial bureaucracy is cool because rather than assume you're the head of a big organization, it has a lot of stuff that seems to imply that you work within a larger organization. Stuff like finding factions and gauging votes. It's also got something else too, resources. With a few different charms you can seem more wealthy, get access to allies, contacts, retainers ect. You can also leverage your wealth directly with charms.

                                                The teamwork here is actually in partnering with an allied general, your projects give them sweet strategic bonuses. It's also got a really sweet charm that lets you flash a blank piece of paper and force people to see it as official documentation and/or a badge of your authority.

                                                Pretty straightforward and definitely applicable to high civilization games that DBs will be involved in most.


                                                And I think I'll leave that for now, I need to get sleep tonight. I'm really enjoying this though! Most of the charm trees so far have personality, very distinct from Solars. It's actually making Solars more cool as well, before they just kind of did stuff super well with no comparison. Now that we have one the ability to do things like lift a house is actually novel, since it's something that's beyond the other exalted (so far). At the same time though I can definitely see a Solar player getting just a bit jealous of the DB in his party who gets to use his brawl charms to fight enemies in a courtyard while he coolly struts along the ramparts.
