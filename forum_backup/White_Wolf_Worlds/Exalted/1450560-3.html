                                            I actually had a thought regarding Getimians: Is it possible for them to emerge from discarded destinies, regardless of whether Heaven wanted them discarded or not? I'm asking because of how prior editions talked about Essence use messing with how Destiny is supposed to go.

                                            Here's a scenario: A Getimian and a Chosen of Serenity have a talk. The Getimian is fairly belligerent, upset that due to her parent's never meeting in this world, she was never born here, and thus wasn't able to raise the family business to the mercantile empire it should have been. Having spoken (but not joined up with) Thulio before, she's aware that Heaven is generally responsible for discarded destinies, hence her situation. The Sidereal however clarifies: The Get's parents were supposed to get together, that was actually the original plan. However, due to the actions of an Anathema (presumably Lunar), the Get's father was seduced away, leaving the family business to rot and never meeting the Get's mother. While the Bureau of Destiny could have forced the issue, to do so would require active (and dangerous) Sidereal involvement, possibly risking an agent dying to a frenzied Lunar's claws. So, they let the situation be and focused on cleaning up the area. Thus, the Get's parents never met and had a child (her), the family business died on the vine and was never able to grow, and the Sidereals lost a prime "investment" for the future.

                                                Finally getting some details about the Getimians has me thinking back to those three sample characters we heard about back in the day - so long, I can't even find where they first posted, just seven years worth of quotes:

                                                Minuend used to be the feared and worshiped sorcerer-queen of a wealthy, upstart kingdom in the deep South, until she woke up one day and wasn't. She aims to reclaim what was taken from her, and to punish those responsible.

                                                An-Ko-Shi has dedicated his fists to justice. He particularly focuses on delivering punishment to the wicked in absence of a derelict Heaven. He eagerly builds his strength for the day when he can cast down Fakharu, the corrupt censor of the West.

                                                Apkallu was a gambler who lost and won his freedom, won and lost a fortune, then gained Exaltation. Despite his roguish background, he holds the preservation of Creation against the vices of the mighty as his most pressing concern, and so has forsaken Rakan Thulio's dream. He works as a special consultant for the Bureau of Destiny.
                                                In those long years of periodically seeing this, it oddly never occurred to me that Minuend's situation was a defining through-line of the splat. Hindsight rerolls 1s until they cease to appear, I suppose.

                                                With what we've learned...

                                                Minuend, as a Sorceress, sounds like a Winter (though she might be a Summer). It's obviously vague, but she could indicate a less-sympathetic version of Getimians: they're repeatedly called "heroes" of their timelines, but I don't see any indication that Minuend's deeds (or those of Getimians as a whole) were necessarily righteous. Heaven could avert the rise of a terrible tyrant... who suddenly shows up, wanting to take back all their old power and more. I wonder how some of the other Getimians would respond to that? It might raise some friction with the likes of...

                                                An-Ko-Shi is a classic Heroic Warrior, so probably a Spring. I wonder what his old timeline was like? There's interesting possibilities: maybe the cut Destiny wasn't meant to be "about" him, but was instead an experiment in casting down Fakharu, which allowed a good (and, presumably, pretty badass) life for An himself, but caused larger problems (as far as Heaven is concerned, anyway), so now he assumes he's in the Dark Timeline where Fakharu got to keep messing stuff up. At the opposite extreme, it might be pretty cool if he somehow became an agent of Heaven (if you want to bend Getimian lore a little, maybe even a God, Talespinner style), and had cleaned house in the past. There's something appealing about a Getimian with firsthand knowledge of Yu-Shan's inner workings, now using those skills to infiltrate and oppose the Sidereals' stronghold... honestly, maybe even moreso if it's someone who sounds like they're not really cut out for skulduggery. In a way, that would make for the exact opposite of...

                                                Apkallu isn't just the clincher that these guys don't go for easy-to-remember names. (Really, not even a single Broken Thread or Seven Deadly Honors. Such defiance of Creation's order.) No, Apkallu is also the concept here that I like the least. Personally, I feel like Getimians who walked away from Thulio, and maybe even provided some covert aid to Heaven, are fine, but if one of them is actually going to work directly with the Bureau of Destiny, that should happen during play, not before. A big part of what makes Getimians scary is how strange and unknown their powers are, so I wouldn't give the Sidereals a chance to study those in a safe setting unless and until the PCs make that happen. Still, the indications of his past are intriguing - I've seen people musing on the idea of Getimians who didn't really like their original version of Creation, and here we seem to have one that at least views his as a mixed bag - and that does seem to have made him less receptive to Thulio's sales pitch. I'd speculate that Apkallu is a Summer (there isn't much to go on, but "iconoclast who upends order" seems as good a summation as any), and hope he still breaks out the old trickster Archetype habits by using Impossible Conquest Authority to make minor Spirits tell their bosses exactly what they think of them.

                                                I was really only interested in An-Ko-Shi before Essence (that "heroic antagonist" energy), but having more context clearly gives me several potentially-intriguing takes on each brief sketch, which (like this thread itself) probably speaks well for the versatility of the new Exalts' themes... not bad, for something so odd and specific that those "in the know" famously struggled to come up with any analogues from other media!

The show never went into any more detail about them, but the names are suggestive.
                                                        Honestly, now that we have a more complete picture, I think the mistake is looking for a specific character. Getimians exist in the form of a classic stock situation:
                                                        • Wolverine in House of M.
                                                        • Marty McFly in Back To The Future 2.
                                                        • Nodwick in A World Without Piffany.
                                                        • Bishop in the Age of Apocalypse.
                                                        • Captain Kirk in Mirror, Mirror.
                                                        • George Bailey seeing the world where he was never born.

                                                        You know, the one character who remembers a better world than the awful version which everyone around them accepts, and is constantly freaking out at everyday stuff for being upsetting and wrong. Of course, those examples all share one key difference from the Getimian situation: the point of each and every story is to fix the world back to what the character remembers.

                                                        Getimians don't get that. They're stuck in the wrong world forever. That's incredibly tragic, but also it may provide the best context for the idea of them being fundamentally antagonistic: deep down, I think that most Getimians would choose the annihilation of every being in Creation to bring back "their" version of it. On a philosophical level, the fact that the Getimian exists at all seems to imply that this is not an inherently greater crime than the simple existence of fate itself, which is maaaaybe doing that to countless "potential destiny" people all the time. On a practical level, if you live in this version of Creation, it's virtually indistinguishable from the Neverborn.

                                                        ...I really like all the possibilities of this new splat, is I guess what I'm saying.


                                                        (If you want to keep New Who in there, I'd actually lean into The Doctor in The Long Game and Bad Wolf, when he keeps going on about how this supposed to be "the fourth great and bountiful human empire," much to everybody's confusion.)
