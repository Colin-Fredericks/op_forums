        I took some time to be sure to express clearly my thoughts about it, and in the mean time mercury already said much about what I wanted.

        I'll just add that, a few exemple charms to that I consider, in their design, essential to the sidereal style :

        - narrow and strong, like someone else's destiny. A charm that works only for poisons (+diseases and crippling effects in 2.5 IIRC) which are not really common except in specific campaigns, but allows for totally reverse some situations unlike any other poison-resisting charm (there isn't even a resistance roll I think).

        - "weak" charms (meaning charms that don't do extremely potent effects) that comboes well with others or opens interesting stunts, like presence in absence technique or sidereal shell-games.


        I like the sidereal style to be like "playing" a game of pachinko (i guess that's how it's called) :
        the events are like a ball, and you want it to fall in one of the desired outcomes
        first, you use wide but weak charms to influence the events slightly untill they fit the situation where your narrow but strong charms give you a strong impact.

        Just like if you want to make a king change his mind on a decision, you can use and abuse presence in abscence technique and avoiding the truth technique until he no longer trusts the gounds on which he based his decision, then you can introduce another option and seal the deal with force decision.

        That's the "trinity" of sidereal gameplay for me, opposed to solar's straightforward charms : influence the events by small bits like a buterfly effect and then with one or 2 powerful but situational charms reap the effects.

        As a side note, this style sure works well with plaing, but it could also work well with improvising plans. I say that because I don't think sids should always be weaker in combat (for instance) if they didn't plan it ahead. Instead I find they should be weaker only if they fail to adapt and improvise a plan to the situation. That's why i'm not as attached to avoidance kata as other players.

                        Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                        But does every single Charm need to require weird lateral thinking to facilitate application? Some of that is cool but after a time I can't see that requirement for all Charms as being anything other than exhausting.
                        I think what you're worried about is nothing more than a bit of meme expansion.

                        If we ignore 2e Sidereals, as is right and proper, 1e's structure should give us some idea of how the devs will approach 3e. In 1e, Sidereals had short trees with very deliberate holes, but they didn't exactly have 'a rubber band, a toothpick and a zippo' or anything like that. Sidereal charms were enormously powerful. The 'McGuyver' aspect, such as it existed, came from manipulating the situation such that one of your ridiculous charms was applicable to it. Lateral thinking was an element, but not something which was unduly burdensome nor universal.*

                        The archetypal example from 1e was always Avoiding the Truth Technique. 'Sidereals don't have the ability to make someone believe that what they're saying is the truth!', a commentator would announce, 'You've got to make them believe that you're lying in a way which accomplishes your goal!' I'm here to tell you today that this is and has always been really easy. A simple inversion often accomplishes it! Look:

                        Solar "Know that my words are truth! The Immaculate order is evil and the Solars are magnificent god-kings!"

                        Pitiful Mortal "Right-o!"

                        Sidereal "You've heard of the Cult of the Illuminated? Murderers and thieves! The Solars are not magnificent, nor are they god-kings! The Immaculate Order is truth and light!"

                        Pitiful Mortal "Theoretically I could believe that you are lying and yet still incidentally correct. In practice, however, my powerful belief that you're telling lies inclines me strongly toward a positive belief in the converse. Still, as I am a worthy philosopher-peasant, I shall carefully weigh the possibilities and make a concerted cho-"

                        Sidereal "Force Decision, you believe the opposite of what I said!"

                        Philosopher Pitiful Mortal "Oh, balls."

                        There's another thing, of course. Sidereals get charms like Force Decision, or Unweaving Method, or Duck Fate- powerful, straightforward and with wide applicability. There's a reason that a number of people in 1e thought that Sidereals were far too powerful.

                        Not that I condone such blasphemies, you understand.




                        *I mean, sure, There's some lateral thinking involved in making Neighborhood Relocation Scheme solve a problem, but I've never seen anyone get frustrated and shout 'How am I supposed to work with only the power to re-shape the face of Creation at my will?!'

                                        When you had an obvious problem that you couldn't finagle with, or needed to approach something head-on and all your native tools are circuitous, the traditional way for a Sidereal to handle it is by application of a Martial Art, probably one of the weirder ones that feels less like an "I fite u" style and more of the "other Ability crossover" stuff like Ebon Shadow or SVN, to say nothing of the weirdness possible in SMA.

                                        While I understand that's part of their designed play experience, I just wonder if that paradigm is desirable. It seems to take some of the "cleverness" out of the Sidereal Charmset when the "easiest" solution to a problem is just using Martial Arts. You set up a situation where you cannot deal with a problem in the same way as other Exalts because your Charms don't work that way, then give the loophole that with the right MA, you totally can deal with a problem the same way.

                                        Perhaps this will no longer be the case in 3e, but there are going to be a number of the more esoteric non-SMA Styles released as early as the Core (SVN, Ebon Shadow, Black Claw, and Crane come to mind). So, I just wonder if the old paradigm is desirable because it basically undercuts itself and the exception swallows the rule.

                                                        Ghosthead:
                                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post

                                                        Personally they called to me based on nothing more than what was written up about them in the 1e Core and early books... spirits, prophecy, martial arts mastery, mysteriousness, declaring to fate "thus it shall be", making hard decisions and beating the impossibly perfect Exalted. I like characters who have uncanny psychic powers to know, who are weak but know where to push at the right time and place.

                                                        So not really, primarily, any of the Sidereals hardback stuff about them having to deal with paperwork, or living in Heaven, or fixing causality errors, or having a lot of limited, finite effects they had to use cleverly and have eureka moments using.

                                                        Not that I hate that stuff, just it's... distinctly... secondary. Very secondary. Not the main taste. I don't really get jazzed up about office politics mixed with Spirited Away, or fixing random purposeless temporal anomalies in the spacetime continuum, or killing random innocents because Destiny Says So. It just feels empty and unsatisfying on its own - Just too much backstage maintenance. It's the ketchup. You don't just eat the ketchup or eat 90% ketchup, 10% food.

                                                        Realistically, that ketchup probably what makes the "play experience" of an all Sidereal group most distinct, and more than a matter of style. Which may be why I like better the idea of Sidereals in a mixed group, with Solars and Lunars and all that, or with Dragonblooded defending the Realm. Away from being Heaven's Task Force and very much in the thick of Creation, salvaging from the ruins of what came before, exploring the vistas mortals live and thrive in and building a new (and if you're an idealist, better) human political order in the world (or upholding the one that's there)., Only using the distinct capabilities of the goddesses of Fate to do so.

                                                        Mercury:
                                                        Originally posted by Mercury View Post


                                                        I apologize that this response took so long- I was busy elsewhere for a while.

                                                        Anyway, I'd ascribe it to a few factors:

                                                        1. Agency - specifically that a Sidereal is, by default, an agent of order. Heaven in the specific sense, sure, but even if you wipe out Yu-Shan you can still fit them as agents of fate or destiny as you'd like. Among my favorite set of short stories are the Traveler in Black sets, which is an archetypal inspiration for the splat, When I was a student of Christian mythology, my favorite stories were always the ones featuring the angels rather than the ones with more direct interaction with God. There's something very satisfying about service to a higher agency.*

                                                        2. Reality-Bending Magic - one could say that all magic bends reality, I suppose. People have noted before that Sidereal magic is less about who you are and more like a set of special sorceries; I quite like that, at least to a point. You really feel like you've been given a set of cheat codes, or perhaps access to console commands (but you only know so many commands!). I've seen people build entire characters around having Avoidance Kata, and I've built characters entirely around both Mirror-Shattering Method and Godly Familiar.** There's a sense of significance which Jenna Moran tried to imbue the Sidereal charms with, and I personally think she succeeded to some extent.

                                                        3. I'm a Massive Asshole - If I could be that guy in shows who appears and knows everything and vanishes if you try to grab him, I would do it. Fuck, that sounds like fun. Anyway, even if you don't actually know everything, there's nobody like a Sidereal for looking like an omniscient dickbag.

                                                        4. Spirit Moderation - There's way too much redundancy in 2e, but I always loved the idea of being a moderator between the spirits and the world. Or, since the Immaculates mostly have that covered these days, the spirits and Heaven. Second role gives me way more opportunity to threaten spirits anywho.

                                                        5. Philosophy - the Exalted aren't tied to philosophies by any means. The Solars don't have to be virtue ethicists, the Alchemicals don't have to like socialism and the Lunars are thankfully free forever of mandatory worship of pulp-barbarianism. Yet there are connotations in the gameline, and the Sidereals are the obvious consequentialists. We all know which side of the Trolley Problem Chejop Kejak finds himself on. I have great sympathy for their anti-heroic image of the Men Who Did A Terrible Deed Because The Ends Do Justify The Means, even if there's a very real question about exactly how justified they were.

                                                        6. Story - as I hinted in the first paragraph, I like playing as the agent of a higher power. While you can certainly finagle that as a Solar or Lunar player, the background's set up differently and I think the game tends to push people in other ways. Solars are the returning god-kings, come back to save the world! Only I have an aesthetic dislike for 'returning god-kings' and I don't really feel qualified to save the world. I prefer to moderate the attempts of others to do so! Abyssals are the vengeful champions of the dead, destined to end all Creation! But I don't want to end Creation and I don't like Abyssal thematics! Infernals are the last hurrah of the scheming Primordials, who may return Creation to the days before days or spur it to new and undreampt-of heights! Meh.***

                                                        But Sidereals are the overworked, harried overseers of Creation since the dawn of Ages. They've achieved jurisdiction over all things and everything is slipping through their fingers! They are bound by strange laws, but wield power which the world does not even suspect exists! They're the servants of gods and the masters of gods! They work with supreme dedication even when all their hopes have perished! I like all of that. I like it a lot.




                                                        *As someone who has worked for several different government bureaus, I really like how Exalted took inspiration from the Chinese conceptualization of Heaven and melded the numinous with the mundane. It echoes the feeling I've had while working for agencies which have goals I respect and yet require a certain amount of soul-crushing work and banal office politicking. It's a beautiful mixture of determined pride and weary cynicism.

                                                        **I had an idea for an Oracle-hacker in space-Shards who would be built around using Willing Assumption of Chains to hijack gods, but I never got to try that and I think it would have needed a very careful ST to moderate.

                                                        ***I don't know what the shit Lunars are supposed to do, so.
