        Investigation

        Number of Charms: 17
        By Ability requirement: 1/1/1/2/12
        By Essence requirement: 6/3/5/2/1
        Nonstandard dice tricks: Free full Excellency
        Themes: Detect crime automatically; compel the truth of who a criminal is from objects, recreations of the crime, or his own lips.

        I’m looking forward to this tree – “Solar Sherlock Holmes” has always sounded cool, and the trees have never quite measured up. Let’s see what we’ve got!

        (Well, first, we’ve got a Divine Mantle sidebar that’s about a page too late.)

        The Charms:
        1. Auto-detect “There’s something fishy here; roll to try to notice it.” Nice!
        2. Auto-detect “Larceny is being used nearby.”
        3. Case a scene in seconds, with bonuses.
        4. 1/scene, free full Excellency.
        5. Profile criminal based on crime scene; auto-detect characters matching profile.
        6. Tell whether one statement is true or false.
        7. 1/scene, roll Investigation with double-8s.
        8. Get an alert (and an explanation) when a character acts against your profile of him.
        9. Compel a character to honestly answer questions.
        10. Infinite Investigation Mastery, roughly.
        11. Momentarily restore destroyed evidence.
        12. Auto-succeed on a case scene action. Make Holmes-style logical connections between objects and events.
        13. Reenact the events of the crime from the perspective of one of the participants.
        14. Sense the emotions of the person who left behind a piece of evidence.
        15. Reset any Investigation Charms with limited use frequency (#4, 7, 9, and 17).
        16. Understand the perpetrator’s motivations, as per read intentions.
        17. Enter your “Mind Manse” and solve some piece of the mystery.
        This is our shortest tree so far, isn’t it? And over two-thirds at Ability 5. Hooray.

        I really like Watchman’s Infallible Eye (#1), which codifies a basic “I’m a great investigator” competence.

        Judge’s Ear Technique (#6) is nerfed hard – one statement, not a scene of truths. It also points out one of the things 3e seems to be outright missing: a generalized Charm-conflict resolution system. This Charm calls out how it, specifically, deals with conflict, but there’s no general-case solution. I didn’t expect to miss the UFvIO table, but I think I do!

        Evidence-Restoring Prana (#11) is great – I love the idea of a Solar investigator plucking evidence from the air.

        Ten Magistrate Eyes (#12) really feels like it ought to upgrade Evidene-Discerning Method, i.e., the other “case-the-scene-in-seconds” power. Well, or else ditch the “in seconds” part from this one, or something – it just feels redundant as-is.

        Er, why does the Solar investigator in this drawing have wings? I kind of like the picture, in general – the fish-eye view works for it.

        Unknown Wisdom Epiphany (#13) suggests someone has been watching Hannibal; this might as well be called This Is My Design Prana. Which is in no way a negative.

        Yes: I think my favorite Charm in the set is also the first – “Hang on, my crime senses are tingling.” Unknown Wisdom Epiphany is also pretty great, though.
        No: Mind Manse Meditation (#17) is waaaaay too much flavor piled on top of a simple idea: the Storyteller tells you how to solve a piece of your mystery, or at least how to make progress on it. I don’t think that’s a bad space for a Charm – it’s a good anti-frustration feature, at a minimum – but it’s hard to suss out of the text. “Mind Manse” is also a little too cute for me, Holmes allusions aside.
        Just Plain Weird: Empathic Recall Discipline (#16) is kind of odd – what is this doing that Unknown Wisdom Epiphany isn’t? They’re both very heavily fluff-texted Charms, and I’m just not parsing the distinction between their effects. (Of the two, UWE is the cooler description by a fair mile – is it just supposed to grant actions, without motivations?)

            A few months ago, someone asked me: "If you had to choose between a politician you respected and trusted, and one that you thought could get the job done, which would you choose?" My response was that if I'm trusting politician X, then X is probably just very good at manipulating me, and I'm better off with Y.

            Then they stated which two politicians this exercise had been based on, and I realized that I did in fact trust X more than Y, and did in fact want X to get the nomination more than Y. The belief that I couldn't fully trust any politician was not sufficient to overwhelm my belief that X was being genuine. Now, it's possible that somebody more cynical would have stronger protective Intimacies to preserve their distrust, but the fact of the matter is that I was convinced of X's sincerity, and since I was convinced, the general rule stating that I shouldn't trust politicians seemed less applicable.

            All that to say: people are able to hold contradictory beliefs, at least for a time. Eventually, one may win out, but that doesn't necessarily happen immediately. "All Anathema lie" can and will coexist with "this Anathema's all right," because that's how people's minds work. Then you can spend WP to ignore one of the things you believe, but which one you choose to ignore depends a lot on circumstance and the social atmosphere.

                Side note/Callback:
                About the "Seeing in darkness"-charms discussed on page 4 (of this topic, not the core book!) - they're useful if you want things to stay dark. I mean, it's all fine and dandy if you don't mind announcing your presence to everyone with a line of sight to you, but light sources tend to be very easy to spot. If you want to hide, better learn to stay in the dark.

                Last but not least, there are things like heavy fog/smoke that can impair vision, and no amount of glow can help you there. Also, who knows, magical darkness may be a thing, too.

                                    Larceny

                                    Number of Charms: 29
                                    By Ability requirement: 1/3/5/8/12
                                    By Essence requirement: 9/9/7/2/2
                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Bank successes for later use, spend Initiative from your next Join Battle roll, add opponent’s 10s to your roll, treat two numbers 2-5 as 10s

                                    Themes: Be a criminal, steal anything, be anyone, go anywhere, avoid all suspicion.

                                    Charms:
                                    1. You can attract criminals, avoid the attention of magistrates, or appear to be involved in any crimes in the area.
                                    2. Appear to belong, wherever you are; boost your Guile to resist detection.
                                    3. Treat another’s uncertainty as a Minor Intimacy.
                                    4. Claim ownership of a thing.
                                    5. Read Intentions better against someone while gambling.
                                    6. Cheat at a board game or poison a drink.
                                    7. Infallibly steal a thing.
                                    8. Automatically pick a lock.
                                    9. Create a perfect disguise.
                                    10. 1/scene, mute five motes.
                                    11. Convince a target you’ve always owned something you steal from him.
                                    12. Bank successes for future Larceny actions.
                                    13. Add successes to a Larceny roll, at the cost of future Initiative.
                                    14. Build points to spend on retroactive(?) larceny actions.
                                    15. Perfectly palm an object.
                                    16. Steal someone’s pants. Or, well, something else in plain sight, but probably pants.
                                    17. Steal from several yards away, even with obstacles in the way.
                                    18. Pretend to be another person, including special effects.
                                    19. Treat some number from 2-5 as a 10.
                                    20. Plant evidence on a target.
                                    21. Steal from someone trying to pickpocket you.
                                    22. Steal your enemy’s weapon mid fight.
                                    23. Teleport through a door. But, like, skillfully, not with magic.
                                    24. Adopt a disguise instantly.
                                    25. Wear two disguises at once.
                                    26. Prevent criminals and creatures of darkness from attacking you, briefly; enable a number of weird theft possibilities (dreams, Initiative, etc.).
                                    27. Make your disguises “retroactively harder to pierce.”
                                    28. 1/scene, free full Larceny Excellency.

                                    Seasoned Criminal Method (#1) is a really great plot-hook generator – it guarantees your ability to Do Crimes anywhere you go. Swift Gambler’s Eye (#5) is just the opposite – an extremely narrow effect, that’s still only a little better than just using your Excellency.

                                    Flawless Pickpocketing Technique (#7) says that, when opposed by magic, it “adds the Solar’s Essence in successes.” To the pickpocket technique, I guess, but again, some kind of general rule would be nice here. (An Excellency counts as magic, right?)

                                    Actually, wait, what’s going on here? It gives +Essence successes, okay. It can’t be detected at all. Then we’re told it can’t be detected with Awareness. Then we’re told magic gets -4 dice, in addition to the bonus successes this gets. Then we’re told Larceny-detecting magic can’t detect this, unless the user of said Larceny-detecting magic also knows FPT specifically. What? Are Abyssals and the like just automatically out of the running, then?

                                    Flawlessly Impenetrable Disguise (#9) is proof against human senses, and inhuman senses, but not supernatural senses. I guess I get that one, but it took a couple readings to click.

                                    Doubt-Sealing Hiest (#11), i.e., “What, this thing I just stole? No, I’ve always had it,” is just a great image.

                                    Unshakeable Rogue’s Spirit (#13) requires you to keep track of how much Initiative you will have paid from your next Join Battle roll, which just makes me shudder a little.

                                    Master Plan Meditation (#14) doesn’t actually specify when you can start spending the points it collects. The implication seems to be that you can destroy evidence after the fact – but how far after the fact? After it’s been found? After it’s been put in the police locker? I don’t find either of those implausible, per se, but they would make it clear that this is another of those reality-hack Charms (and not just flavor of “wooOOOOOooo, I am sooooo skillful”). Ahh, okay, there’s a sidebar, which clarifies that it’s not retroactive – it’s just declared when someone would reveal a piece of evidence. How does that work, though? The cops show up to arrest you, start proudly declaring evidence, and halfway through everything turns out to have never existed?

                                    Proof-Eating Palm (#15) seems weirdly dedicated to not storing items in Elsewhere. Why?

                                    Fate-Shifting Solar Arete (#19) is a favored target for questionable balance so far. With the upgrade, it’s an 80% increase in your expected number of successes – which has dramatic effects for, say, Crafting, sorcerous workings, or anything else that would assume a sane and fixed pool distribution. Alternatively, given its 1/day usage, it comes darned close to guaranteeing a Decisive attack lands. This would be a devastatingly powerful Charm in any one Ability – by far better than almost any dice-adder that’s out there, except in usability. (And a number of the existing dice-adders are unlikely to pop more than once a day, if that.) Cross-Ability, it’s kind of ridonkulous.

                                    Skillful Reappropriation (#20) is pretty fun – I know of characters who would get a lot of use out of that Charm. Reversal of Fortune (#21), by contrast, is ridiculously situational – how often do characters expect to get pickpocketed, that counter-theft becomes superior to just walking around using your normal super-pickpocketing?

                                    Null Anima Gloves (#26) seems to have no resistance mechanism. I’m just not following this one: my hands start glowing (which seems odd to be “null anima”), and the Mask of Winters can’t attack me for thirty seconds… because…? (Or at least, I assume the Mask qualifies, since Creature of Darkness isn’t defined.) Shouldn’t this have the Psyche keyword, given that it irresistibly compels behavior?

                                    Yes: Again, I think some of the simplest Charms are the best – “I’ma be a criminal now!” is pretty great. Most of the stuff that’s more-or-less lifted from 2e is solid.
                                    No: Fate-Shifting Solar Arete just feels completely out of place, and particularly out of proportion.
                                    Just Plain Weird: Null Anima Gloves is this massive grab-bag of effects – there are half-a-dozen or more sub-powers you can choose with it. (And what’s null anima about the gloves, anyway?) Also, why does this edition have beef with “Elsewhere?”

                                                    Linguistics

                                                    Number of Charms: 33
                                                    By Ability requirement: 1/0/5/4/23
                                                    By Essence requirement: 10/7/11/3/2
                                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: None that I caught on first pass!

                                                    Themes: Conceal messages, speak and be understood, understand when others are speaking, compel seemingly-nonsense behavior from your targets, write quickly.

                                                    Charms:
                                                    1. Copy a book in seconds.
                                                    2. Forge handwriting; render a perfect copy.
                                                    3. Hide a message to a specific individual in another written message.
                                                    4. As #3, but spoken instead of written.
                                                    5. Double-9s (or 8s, or 7s, with repurchases) on written works.
                                                    6. Interpret one statement in a foreign language, or parse “abstruse language.”
                                                    7. Communicate via body language.
                                                    8. Fuse two languages into a new language that speakers of either tongue can understand.
                                                    9. Automatically sense the Intimacies behind a written work.
                                                    10. After using #9, raise Resolve by Linguistics/2.
                                                    11. Write in the style of another person.
                                                    12. Write an anonymous letter, which readers will assume was written by a specific person.
                                                    13. Compel the reader to announce the contents of the message.
                                                    14. Prevent the reader from repeating the contents of the letter.
                                                    15. Speak a language that everyone can understand.
                                                    16. The character’s Guile cannot be pierced while she’s reading.
                                                    17. Read faded scripts.
                                                    18. Copy any magic embedded in a document.
                                                    19. Edit a document without trace.
                                                    20. Target forgets all known languages.
                                                    21. Kill someone who has no sensible Intimacies.
                                                    22. Convince someone of the truth of an assertion – but then have them do the opposite of that.
                                                    23. Learn a new language.
                                                    24. Maintain a perfect memory of a written work.
                                                    25. Read a book at superhuman speed.
                                                    26. Write a book in your miiiiiiiiiiind.
                                                    27. Reduce time required to compose a work, though not the time to write it.
                                                    28. Combine Simple Charms on a single work.
                                                    29. Hold people spellbound as long as you speak (or as long as they read what you have written).

                                                    Why are Letter-Within-A-Letter Technique and Subtle Speech Method (#3 and 4) two different Charms? Is there a meaningful difference here that wouldn’t be covered by “When used on a letter, ____; when used in a speech, _____”?

                                                    Sagacious Reading of Intent (#9) is narrow, but powerful – automatic knowledge of Intimacies offers some interesting possibilities. … And then we chain into Word-Shield Invocation (#10), which (1) only works after using SRoI, and (2) is a cheaper Excellency. Pass.

                                                    “That Burning Question:” says the sidebar, “What happens if you combine Essence-Laden Missive and Voice Caging Calligraphy? A: You tell me.” Guys, if the rest of your systems were clear and unambiguous in their interaction, this might be funny. As is, the humor’s a little too dark for me.

                                                    Essence-Laden Missive (#13) states that it compels the character to announce the message aloud, as an Inspire action. But inspire actions are emotional triggers, aren’t they? Shouldn’t this be a persuade action, since it’s compelling a specific behavior?

                                                    Single-Voice Kata (#15) includes callouts about how spirits and fair folk get mad at you for using it, which is an interesting flavor note – I’m not sure whether that makes the Charm more or less useful, but it’s definitely more interesting.

                                                    Power-Snaring Image (#18) solves an old problem, where the Solar can make an amazing magical document and perfectly copy it, and yet the copy isn’t magic. (Surely this implies that the Solar’s writings really are magic, in any event, and not just very skillful – a copy would catch that.)

                                                    Twisted Words Technique (#22) is… either crazy-powerful or not, I’m not sure. The Charm itself suggests that it acts as an Inspire action, creating behavior – like the monk going to live with the prostitutes. But the sidebar seems to be explicit that it’s only modifying Intimacies, kicking them up or down a level (in the direction opposite what it appears to do). If it works in the former way, it seems trivial to convince an Immaculate to abandon the Wyld Hunt (by getting him to agree that Solars must be hunted). In the latter case, you might weaken his Intimacy, but he’s still probably going to try to kill you. The verbiage in the Charm talks about “persuasion caused by this Charm” – but persuade is a system term, meaning “induce behavior.”

                                                    Rereading the opening parts of the Charm, it does seem like this is behavior-creation – “the target will feel overwhelmingly compelled to act…” So that is persuasion, and the Charm is correct. Does it also function as an Instill action, or is this an either/or? Does this trivially dispatch Immaculates, given that you’re appealing to a very strong Intimacy and they can’t spend Willpower to resist?

                                                    I can’t help but see Unbreakable Fascination Method (#29) as the old Metal Gear Solid “leave out a magazine, totally distract the guards” thing.

                                                    This is just not an immediately inspiring tree. Lots of utility, but not a lot that fired my imagination – though that part may be me, rather than the Charms themselves.

                                                    Yes: Sagacious Reading of Intent.
                                                    No: Mind-Scribing Method (#26), i.e., “Write a book in your head and copy it down later.” That’s a stunt, not two sessions’ worth of XP.
                                                    Just Plain Weird: Cup Boils Over (#21) just feels… well, honestly, it feels like it’s there so that if someone ever says, “It seems like Intimacies are more liability than help,” that the internet can reply, “No, because Cup Boils Over will kill you if you don’t have Intimacies.” If that’s accurate, that’s a pretty obnoxious treat-the-symptoms thing; if it’s not, and it may not be, I’m not sure how else to use this.
