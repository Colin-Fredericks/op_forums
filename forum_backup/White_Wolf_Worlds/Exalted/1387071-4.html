                                    So everything else aside, I think we can reach an agreement on the following: Sail is used in Naval Combat to kill other people on boats (among other things). It is both extremely powerful at this, and this power can be attained very cheaply. It is opposed only by other people with Sail. Solars are overwhelmingly better at sail than Non-Solars, in large part because of how powerful and cheap Sail charms are. There are not enough strong naval enemies to make heavy investment in Sail useful.

                                    So we're all clear on those bits?

                                    I could go on and on about What Specifically Constitutes a Combat Skill, but that is a subject for its own thread. ​I'm sorry I got a little heated. I got wrapped up in the oneupsmanship of forum debates.

                                    So, Sail is exclusively the skill to use in Naval Combat. There are a couple of ships provided, There's 2 Artifact ships- one Warship and one Flying ship. For Sorceries there's a few to choose from: one makes a small boat from a peach (Floral Ferry), and one lets you sail on the clouds (Keel Cleaves the Clouds). I wouldn't say either are great for combat, Floral Ferry is really fast and has +3 maneuver so its probably best used for boarding actions.

                                    Sorceries don't really matter in Naval Combat because they are just lumped in to dealing Hull Damage. I had a Twilight Sorcerer use Death of Obsidian Butterflies on an enemy trireme which was cool, but it didn't do any more or less then the artifact harpoon on another ship. Also, considering how... vague a round of positioning is it was really hard to rule on how fast a Sorcerer could summon Sorcerous Motes. Still, 2 damage is better than 1 damage. It requires Resources 3 to obtain first weaponry of similar power, but since you need to own a ship and have crew for any of this to matter, you probably have Resource 3 already, and if you don't that's what piracy is for.

                                    The artifact warship is called Horizon Endeavor, its in Arms of the Chosen. It costs a whopping 10 motes to attune to, but has 11 hull points. Eleven! the other ships are 3 or 4. It has a max speed of 14 which is insanely better than the next ship of only 4 (the sorcery Floral Ferry is better at Speed 5 but your call if that's a boat or a spell). It has two special attacks- Red Jade Cannons deal 1 damage in a broadside attack, but each subsequent turn the opposing ship has to roll to put out the fire or take one hull damage; and Tidecutter Bolts which are basically torpedoes and get bonus damage after a concealment roll.

                                    The flying ship is the Chariot of Aerial Conquest, which is THE fastest ship in the game at speed 15. It has 6 hull points. It has no special armaments and can hold barely 40 men in tight quarters so it is more like a flying troop transport than an actual ship. It's nigh-invisible in flight (cool), and the soldiers inside have arrow slits to attack from. YMMV on whether this counts as a ship or not, and since it flies and is nearly invisible it probably will never be used in Naval Combat.

                                    Just as an aside, a ships cruising speed (as in, miles per hour) is its Speed Rating x2, so the super ultra fast Chariot of Aerial Conquest goes along at 30 mph (almost 50 kmph), the Horizon Endeavor is 28 mph (basically the same), both of which roughly tie the spell Cirrus Skiff cast at Essence 5 (30 mph). The sorcery Stormwind Rider is moves one or two people 100 mph, making it the fastest vehicle in the game.

                                    There's a charm in Sail called Blood and Salt Bondage (Essence 3, 3 prereq charms) which give you and the entire crew a bonus Health Level for every point of Hull that your ship has. Paired with the Horizon Endeavor this will make you terrifyingly powerful. This is the equivalent of almost 4 purchases of Ox-Body Technique at Stamina 5. You get the Health Levels of the Ship as your health levels*. The prerequisite charm, Hull-Taming Transformation, lets you take 3 health levels of damage for in lieu of your ship taking 1 hull point of damage. All said and done, you can use your own health to buffer the hull integrity of the Horizon Endeavor from just its own bonus to get 4 free hull points of damage. I'm not kidding when I say nothing in the entire game can rightly challenge your ocean supremacy at this point. Even Fakharu would struggle to harm you, and he can heal himself 3 times in the open ocean.

                                    The highest level of Sail charms kinda suck by virtue of being sheer overkill and or dicetricks. Death-Daring Maneuver is really cool, but its got the Apocryphal tag because the devs consider it OP. You basically get to invent a natural hazard and then do a straight Wits+Sail roll off to see which of you eats shit. It doesn't specify what exactly it means to "wreck spectacularly", whether that's instant failure or not, but if you fail you take 2 hull points of damage.

                                    Ship-Razing Renewal is a full free excellency after wrecking an enemy ship. Rail-Storming Fervor gives you three more Initiative to a JB roll and explicitly can be combo'd with other JB boosters. Interestingly, it doesn't say it has to be used boat-to-boat but I assume that's the intention. The ultimate charm, Black Fathoms Blessed lets you speak Mysterious Ocena Language at other Ocean things and gives you +Maneuverability to Defenses. You also can't drown. eh, kinda weird? It's more for fighting on your ship and dealing with ocean spirits than actually getting into Sail challenges. Special mention to Ship Rolling Juggernaut Method for letting you keep your Momentum after killing an enemy ship, and it gives you a bonus equal to the enemy's Essence. I don't know of any other charms that give you a bonus equal to the enemy's essence, although their probably are many, but that's cool. Its a charm which let's you just steamroll ship after ship, as if you weren't OP enough. This is probably the best charm in all of Sail to just wreck an entire enemy armada. Against an E2 or E3 enemy DB Sail Captain you have basically a free Ram which, at this point, will probably turn out to be an instant death attack. and then you can do it again if you have more momentum. Considering how ludicrous momentum rolls get I wouldn't be surprised if a Sail Master queued up 4 of these in a row: 7-Ess each means you could conceivable hit 4 Wyld Hunt ships one after the other like some kind of ship eating demon. Maybe what they say about anathema is right after all.

                                                I made (with help) a first draft of an alternative naval system.

                                                Design goals:
                                                1. The system should a little more complicated than the existing system, but not a huge amount more.
                                                2. In a fight between opponents that are evenly matched, or close to it, there should be some nonobvious strategic decisions.
                                                3. A fight between evenly matched opponents should not last forever.
                                                4. In a fight against a weaker opponent, it should not be trivial to shut down their attacks and emerge unscathed. Doing this requires some combination of luck, good strategy, and a substantially larger dice pool.
                                                Not design goals:
                                                1. I didn’t try to make the system compatible with existing Charms or naval enemies - largely because most of the existing content (the Solar charmset and the Armored Kraken) do not seem very well balanced.
                                                2. I didn’t try to simulate the complexity of running a ship, including the reliance on mortal crew, or ensure every player has something to do. This is not designed for campaigns with huge amounts of naval combat.
                                                3. I didn't try to make rules for all the cool stuff that could go in the basic combat system (like concealment or staying upwind), although I did try to build a framework that this could be added to in a reasonable way.

                                                I playtested it once, between Exalts with Solar-like Excellencies but no Charms, in a total white room with no narrative enhancements, and had fun. That's not much data but

                                                The system:
                                                Naval combat proceeds in rounds. At the beginning of each round, each ship’s captain declares an enemy ship to target, and then makes a Naval Maneuver roll (Wits + Sail + Maneuverability). They declare Charms associated to this roll publicly, before rolling, but keep the successes secret. They take the obtained successes and then divide them up between Attack, Defense, Strategy, and Positioning pools. All captains simultaneously reveal their allocations, and the round plays out.

                                                Naval combat uses a resource, Momentum, as well as a range band system - ship can be at a close range, normal range, or long range from each other. Combat usually starts at normal range.

                                                To put any points in their attack pool, a captain must have at least 1 Momentum. They also must declare what weapon they are attacking with. See below for typical ship weapons.


                                                A naval combat round proceeds to Attack, Positioning, and Strategy steps.


                                                Attack step: Every ship that has more points in their Attack pool than their target has in their Defense pool makes a successful attack. They add their Momentum to their weapon bonus, divide by four, and round down. The defending ship takes that many levels of hull damage. Their Momentum is then reset to zero. Every ship that has no more successes in their Attack pool than the target has in their Defense pool misses, and loses half their Momentum, rounding down.


                                                Weapon types:

                                                Catapaults and ballistae may be used at close range or normal range. Their weapon bonus is 0 for average quality, +1 for high quality, and -1 for low quality. They also get +1 for a great number of weapons, 0 for a usual number, or -1 for a small number. Artifact weapons can have a higher weapon bonus.

                                                Ramming prows may be used only at close range. Their power comes from speed, and their weapon bonus is the number of extra successes in the attack poll beyond the target’s defense pool (thus, it is always at least one). A ship without a ramming prow may ram, using the body of the ship as a weapon, but it is a desperation move - their ship takes equal damage.

                                                A sorcerer with appropriate training is a weapon in themselves - their weapon bonus is their Occult rating.

                                                Boarding is also treated as a type of attack, which can be taken from close range. It requires 10 Momentum, minus the target’s current damage penalty.

                                                Positioning step: In a duel between ships, whoever got a higher Positioning pool, if any, chooses how to position their two ships. They may move their ship one range band closer or further. They may instead take advantage of a terrain feature, such as concealing their ship in a fog bank, which has its own special rules.

                                                For two or more steps, whoever has the highest Positioning pool chooses a direction first, and then the next, going through everyone who had a success in their positioning pool, with the restriction that later choices must be consistent with earlier ones. For instance, if the captain with the highest Position roll chose to move closer to an enemy ship, that enemy ship could not choose to move further away, or maintain their distance, but they could go into a fog bank, and the captain of another enemy ship would be unaffected.

                                                Ties are broken by the average Wits + Sail of the ship’s crew, and then randomly.

                                                A ship at long range from all enemy ships that chooses to move further escapes the combat. However, they must have 8 Momentum to do this. If they are pursued, they roll opposed Wits + Sail + Speed with their pursuer, and if they roll lower, must begin naval combat again. This resets Momentum, and combat begins again at normal range.

                                                Strategy step: Each ship’s Strategy pool is added to their Momentum. Unlike in normal combat, motes are not recovered in naval combat.

                                                Notes on ships:

                                                All numerics very subject to change but a standard combat ship has the same health levels as a normal human and maneuverability +0. Lighting ballistae have a +4 weapon bonus and implosion bows have +5.
