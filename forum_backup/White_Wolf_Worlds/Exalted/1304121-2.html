    Generally there are limitations on the defenses. If my attack that always hits goes up against your defense that always protects than you win that clash of unstoppable vs immovable. But neither of us can do this forever. In practice you can't perfect defend against every attack. At least with traditional attacks. Now with Shaping it's a bit easier, Destiny-Manifesting Method is permanent after all. But even there there is the idea that if you go long enough without using your guaranteed means of ending the effect, which is not guaranteed to be easy, you will eventually die from lethal things like being turned to stone.

    So to answer your question. They're not actually invulnerable. They are just capable of parrying a mountain that is thrown at them if they exert themselves. Or similarly impressive feats of defense.

    And no generally these are not area of effect. And with shaping defenses in particular they have frequently been explicitly stated to not prevent environmental changes that put you at risk. Only direct changes to your person.

    And the primacy of defense over offense is explicit in second edition. I can't find it explicitly mentioned in third edition. Though many defenses in 3rd edition do say thye defend against the indefensible in various ways. (Eg Block the Unblockable, Dodge the Undodgeable, etc.)

    It's also worth noting here that the phrase "perfect defense" is not used in third edition and there closest equivalents are a lot less perfect than in previous editions. Though they are still exceptionally good against "uncountable damage" and "shaping effects."

    The Dreaming is indeed the best analogue in WoD to the Wyld but even then it's not a perfect one. For a lot of reasons. Both in terms of how it operates and just in general what it is. The Wyld is not a different realm of Existence like Heaven, Hell, or the Underworld. You can just walk there with no magic involved. But moving away from metaphysics, the Amnionic Winds thing seems like a good way of dealing with it's mutagenic properties.

                        A few points that seem to be missed about the Exalted setting:


                        In Exalted, there is no Gauntlet, and no spirit world/umbra at all. Spirits exist fully in whatever location they are at. In creation, the spirit may be dematerialized, but it is still physically present within creation. The Underworld, Autothonia, Malfeas, and Yu-Shan are completely different planes of existence that have gateways between them and Creation. Same with spirit sanctums. Don't even get me started on Elsewhere! The Wyld is a physical location. There is no outer space, and the sun and moon are physical objects that actually fly between the surface of creation and the adamantine dome upon which the stars are mounted. So MtA mechanics concerning the Gauntlet and Umbra simply don't apply in Exalted as written. There's nothing wrong with homebrewing rules to use a similar system. But the difficulty to travel between planes of existence should be pretty darned high in most places. Correspondence and Spirit should both be required for most travel of this kind.

                        (The following is based on Ex2 material, all my be changed in Ex3.) In Exalted, the rules of reality are defined by the Loom of Fate. While there are MANY uses for the Loom, it was created to define the rules of creation. Because of the Loom, gravity works, fire is hot, etc. The only way to bend or break the rules of reality is using essence or disrupting it with the Wyld. In the time of Exalted, the Loom is MUCH stronger than in the OWoD, the Incarnae that created it (the Maidens) still take a hand in maintaining it, and there are exponentially more pattern spiders running around keeping all those f^&%ing essence wielders from ruining the Loom. The reason that the world of Exalted appears to be less stable is because of all the essence being used everywhere. Spirits use essence. Exalted use essence. Mortal sorcerers and martial artists use essence. Manses use essence. Shadowlands leak underworld essence. So the Loom is under constant stress and the greater number of pattern spiders are still stressed out keeping things together.

                        (Based on Ex1, where Creation was an ancient precursor to the OWoD) In the OWoD, the spirits have been mostly locked away in the spirit world, and there are no other forms of essence wielder to mess with the Loom. The Wyld is sealed away from creation by the Gauntlet and the Horizon. Malfeas and the Underworld are also sealed away. Gods no longer interfere in the daily operation of creation. So the greatly diminished Pattern Spiders can manage to keep the Loom from failing. The Mages of the OWoD are really the last big annoyance to the Pattern Spiders, and their are enough Spiders around to respond when they catch Mages tampering with the Loom. .)

                        So the real question is "How does MtA magic work in terms of Exalted?" Is it all shaping? Is it a form of essence manipulation? Does the Mage have a bit of raw Wyld in his soul, allowing him to manipulate reality? Are Mages within Fate or outside of Fate? Obviously there is no cannon answer, but you need to decide the answer for your story.

                            The last post kind of gets into a fundamental thing to remember with anything crossover-related. That being you have to kind of establish what your goals are (like, what do you wnat Mages to be, how much do you think they should warp the setting), and then you have to no matter what comrpomise or change one or both settings involved in order for the crossover to function.

                            In the case of a Mage and Exalted, there's a big range between them being able to manipulate the world, even setting-rules-brekaingly, on one end. And on another, it could be argued that the nature of reality in Creation is just not compatable with the WIllworking of a mage, and that their insights on the Spheres doesn't actually matter as they're in effect applying the wrong programming language to the wrong system, to use the metaphor. There's a lot inbetween, but I think regardless something often gets compromised in crossovers, especially when they ahve pretty deep setting mechancis that fundementally don't just take things differently, but in contradicotry ways.

                            I think that this is a bit that the OP kind of missed. It just kind fo starts dropping Mages in and making changes. So far, most seem to be towards the Mage side, so I'm guessing Mage is the "right" one when possible, yes? Is the plan to recast Creation in a Mage-compatable light, or is it to make a new setting where Mages can operate in Creation? Those are going to be different results and I think probably needs to be pointed out to help on this stuff, since I see a lot of trying to explain Creation in light of assumptions that shared terms mean th same thing accross settings.
