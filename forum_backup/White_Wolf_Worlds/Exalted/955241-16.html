        Track Down Grinning Eel 2
        Sell River Dragon Bits First 1

        You scrub your teeth with your finger and a twig from the potted plant on the windowsill, spit down at the cobblestones, and gather some water from the dripping of the eaves over the window. It’s a bit gritty-it’s apparent that Gleaming Smile hasn’t invested in roof repairs in some time, and you’re getting a bit of clay dust. You leave your spare clay jugs out on the sill to collect more rainwater-it’s the Season of Water, it’s likely to rain again today or tonight-and filter the water through one of your rags to get rid of the detritus. While White Moth continues to snore, you poke at the dim coals in the stove and heat up a small pot of water, and make use of your new chamber pot while it slowly comes to a boil.

        Watching White Moth sleep, you briefly consider going back to bed yourself. It was so warm, and soft…but even though the sun’s not quite up all the way, you’re feeling oddly energized. You’ve been blessed with a lot of good fortune over the past couple of days…and part of you is afraid that the other shoe is going to drop suddenly and it’s all going to get yanked out from under you. You shake your head as you prepare breakfast, and when it’s ready you bow your head over it and offer a silent but sincere prayer of thanks to the Dragons for offering you the chance at a better life. You tell them, and you tell yourself, that you intend to seize it.

        Breakfast is a bowl of White Moth’s rice, with just a splash of vinegar for flavor.

        By a warm stove on a cold wet foggy morning. Under your own roof. It tastes… heavenly.

        After briefly considering buying some tea or herbs or spices to mix up your diet, you decide to save your money. Instead, you really ought to go find Grinning Eel and take him up on that apprenticeship. You’ve been given a windfall, but regular work is the way to keep making money. If you can only keep making money, you can keep putting a little bit into your savings with the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants and hopefully build up enough money for…something. Buying Grinning Eel’s boat? Buying multiple boats and leasing them to boatmen? Raising chickens and selling pickled eggs?

        …something.

        You finish your breakfast.

        Knowing Eel, he’s probably been up for an hour or two already, and he’s currently ferrying the morning-shift folks down to the Nighthammer foundries. They usually arrive a half hour or so after sunrise. From there he’ll probably pick the graveyard-shift workers up and ferry them back up the canal. That doesn’t give you too much time.

        You throw on your new outfit. You did good work last night, the stitches in the back are only visible with close examination, and White Moth’s suggestions on removing bloodstains did wonders-there’s a little discoloration, but not so much you’d notice it without looking hard. You take a moment to look at yourself in the little mirror Moth has in her makeup kit (you already know where she keeps everything and which of her possessions are valuable…it’s not that you necessarily plan on robbing her and selling all her stuff, but it’s a reflex born of long habit). Your face is still covered in little scratches, and your hair is a big frizzy black mess. You grin. You’re not sure you could pull off “cute kid” if you wanted to, but you’ve definitely got “roguish young street tough desperate enough to do something stupid” covered. You suspect if you showed up looking as mean as you do right now, Rat’s new friends might let you join the Sluice Gang. You can see why Moth was so scared of you last night.

        If all goes well, Grinning Eel is going to put you to work today, so you pack light. You grab your knife, tuck your remaining fish hook into your pocket along with your roll of twine so you’re equipped to go fishing again if there’s time for it…or so that Eel can fish while you steer, if it gets to that point. You can’t live on rice forever! You also grab your bag, so you have something to carry fish in. You sort of wish you hadn’t bought such a nice bag-it’s waterproof, but you keep filling the interior with stinking meat and fish, which sort of ruins the point. It still smells faintly of smoke and fish from last night…but it’s sturdy and reasonably light, and more importantly it’s the only bag you’ve got, so you bring it. Hopefully the smell will wash out…
        You head out, and lock the door behind you. Moth is still snoring gently.

        The Blackwall neighborhood is quiet around sunrise. There are a few drunks asleep on stoops, and a few house swallows fluttering about below the clotheslines hung overhead catching bugs as the early morning sun starts to burn off the morning fog. As you head south towards the foundry districts of Nighthammer, the street markets are just getting going-vendors are putting up their stalls, laborers are trundling up and down the streets on their way to their various places of work, carts are being loaded…the city of Nexus is waking up. Not that Nexus sleeps…but there’s a sense of early-morning calm coming from the knowledge that most of the thieves and ruffians and knife-wielding “Neighborhood Watches” are still sleeping off a long night.

        Your trip down to Nighthammer is largely uneventful. You rush through shortcuts and back alleys you’d avoid later in the day, you clamber up swaying bamboo ladders and dash along the roof ways you’d avoid in the rain or after dark, and once or twice you gingerly step over sleeping men you’d give a wide berth if it weren’t for all the empty bottles around their snoring forms. Several times, you spot raitons perched on clotheslines or rooftops. They're not exactly uncommon around here, but you get the distinct impression they're watching you-mostly because when you stop and watch them back they always look away and stare intently at something else for a while before flying away. Can a bird look sheepish?

        Finally, after about forty minutes of urban navigation, you reach the Nighthammer docks. The sun is peeking over the rooftops now, and the streets are bustling as always with the eternal scurrying of wagons and carts and merchants merchants on their way to and from the barges moored in the Nighthammer Pool. You follow the canal until you reach the forge-district where Eel and the other boatmen usually drop off the morning shift workers. It looks like you’ve made it just in time-dozens of canal boats are in the process of unloading their packs of passengers. You shield your eyes from the morning sun with one hand, looking for Eel’s distinctive hat-broad-brimmed and black, with white koi painted on it…

        But you don’t see it.

        As the workers are clambering up the docks, you weave your way through the crowd.

        “Hey,” you call out to one of the boatmen. “Have you seen Grinning Eel?”

        “Not this morning,” he calls back.

        “Old bastard probably skipped the early morning shift,” another of the boatmen chuckles. “Too busy counting his coin from yesterday!”

        “Lucky codger,” the first one sighs. “Wish I’d been out the other night.”

        “Don’t.” Another, older boatman chimes in. “Red Dog and Singing Fish got eaten the other night, and a lot of other folk got boiled alive. It was a nightmare.”

        The younger boatman looks a little sheepish. “Yeah…Emissary doesn’t mess around, I guess…”

        “Why you lookin’ for Eel, young man?” The old boatman eyes you suspiciously.

        “He was thinking about taking me on as an apprentice,” you shrug. No reason to lie. “Says if I’m any good maybe he can retire and lease me his boat.”

        There’s a general nodding of heads.

        “Yeah, Eel’s been complaining a lot about his joints lately…if you’re looking for him, I’d try the Drowned Feather. He usually drinks there when he’s in a good mood…and his mood yesterday was good enough I’ll wager he’s sleeping it off there this morning.”

        “Thanks,” you nod your head. “I’ll go see if I can find him.”

        “Good luck, kid.”

        You make your way back through the oncoming rush of foundry workers on their way to the docks, tired and groaning and chattering about how much they’re looking forward to getting home and going to bed after a long night of pumping bellows and pouring molten iron.

        ***

        The Drowned Feather isn’t too far, it’s right on the canal, but it takes you a while to get there anyway-the place obviously caters mostly to boatmen, and while it’s easy to access from the canal the streetside entrance proves a little difficult to find. You inquire with the proprietor, a middle-aged woman cleaning up after last night’s festivities, and she tells you that Grinning Eel is in fact sleeping off a raucous night in one of the guest rooms upstairs.

        “He was up until well after midnight, throwing Guild scrip around and buying drinks for everybody and dancing like an idiot. Apparently he sold a River Dragon’s heart to an alchemist and walked away a small mountain of coin. Pretty sure he’s got a couple of girls with him, too,” she glowers. “I swear, a man his age…”

        “I see.” It sounds like Eel hasn’t been as…frugal with his windfall as you’ve tried to be with yours.

        “You want me to go wake him up?”



        What Do We Do With A Drunken Sailor: You want to be a boatman. It’s steady work, the pay is good, there’s a canal right by your new apartment…but you can’t start a career as a canal boatman without a teacher, and Eel is gonna be that teacher. Splitting headache or not. You're going to wake him up, and he's going to teach you a lucrative new career, whether he wants to or not! You're pretty sure standing in a rocking boat in the canal surrounded by the smell of dead fish and Nexus sewage is the perfect hangover cure anyway.

        Let Sleeping Eels Lie: Leave a message with the owner of the Drowned Feather asking her to let Eel know you stopped by and asking him to get in touch so you can get him to mentor you when he’s in full possession of his faculties. This frees your morning up to do something else! (If you vote for this option, please suggest what else you wanna do this morning.)

        The Tide Waits For No Man: Your few memories of your father includes lots of stuff about tying knots and currents and tides and canal gossip and lore. You watched Eel the other night poling his boat through heavy traffic in the pouring rain and, to be sure, you wouldn’t want to try that on your first time…but the river is in your blood (you do have Sail 1), and today’s weather is clear and lovely. You can see Eel’s boat moored at the dock right there. You could take her for a spin, maybe take a fare or two, learn a bit and make a bit of money…if you move quickly, you’d probably be back before the old man even wakes up!

                                    Let Sleeping Eels Lie: Unanimous!

                                    You ask the innkeeper to let Grinning Eel know you stopped by. You briefly consider just stealing his boat and learning by doing…but no. The other boatmen would probably come after you, they look after their own, and besides there’s no need to antagonize a man who has offered to help you…and now that you think about it, while the rains are a little late this year, it is still Resplendent Water: the rivers haven’t flooded yet, but the water is high and fast this season. Maybe practicing on your own isn’t the best idea anyway.

                                    You decide instead to head to the markets and see if you can find a buyer for your River Dragon parts. Might as well spend your morning trying to make some more money! This area of town doesn’t have too many alchemists, it’s mostly merchants focused on trading with the docks, so you head for the Nexus district. It doesn’t take too much asking around to figure out where Blue Root, Aunt Dwi’s alchemist, does business. His shop is in a slower neighborhood near the bustling Tailor District. Most of the folks you ask for directions seem to assume you’re delivering an order for one of the Guild artisans in that district-apparently Blue Root does a lot of work manufacturing unusual dyes for the various textile companies in the Tailor District. You suspect Aunt Dwi probably made his acquaintance while shopping for clothes-it’s just like her to run into someone by chance and then get her hooks into him in search of a discount on herbs and medicines...

                                    Blue Root’s shop is clean and professional, with honest-to-the-gods glass windows and a Guild seal posted prominently on his sign. You’re not sure exactly what the rest of the sign says (you’re guessing it says he’s an alchemist), but everyone in Nexus knows the difference between a Guildsman and a free merchant. The Guildsman will charge you more…but he’ll still be around tomorrow if your wares are defective, and he’ll usually have some sort of return policy because he’ll pass the cost of the defective merchandise on to his supplier. Free merchants aren’t usually able to offer that sort of protection.

                                    As you enter, the opening of the door pulls a cord and rings a small bell. It’s a clever contraption, and you find yourself thinking about setting something similar up around your apartment building so you’ll know if anyone’s around. Just to be safe. There’s only a wooden door and a lock between you and everyone else in the Blackwall neighborhood, after all…

                                    There’s a desk, and the woman behind it has turned from organizing a vast array of bottles and jars to look you up and down. All the walls of this shop appear to be covered with little square shelves with small labels on them and little glass jars full of powder or herbs, or small clay jugs, or occasionally packets of parchment. There are dozens of powerful smells mingling in the air-you can make out several dried herbs and something like vinegar or brine…but there are many more you don’t know. It reminds you a bit of the New Friends Curry stall that sometimes operates on the west side of the Nighthammer market and will refuse to serve you until someone sits next to you to order, even before sunset.

                                    “Can I help you?” The woman behind the desk raises an eyebrow, interrupting your thoughts. She is strangely pale, with black hair tied tightly in a bun, and she’s wearing a leather apron and gloves. The left side of her face has…writing on it, on the forehead above her left eye and down onto her cheek. Some kind of swirly foreign lettering in deep blue ink, it’s definitely not Riverspeak.

                                    “I hope so.” You smile. “I have some supplies to sell, and I’m looking for a buyer.”

                                    She seems dubious. “Oh. You're selling for yourself? I assumed you were a runner for one of the tailors. Blue Root doesn’t normally purchase at the shop, he goes out to the Guild market for ingredients…what are you looking to sell?”

                                    “You’re Blue Root’s apprentice?”

                                    “I am.”

                                    “I’m the guy who got Dwi the River Dragon eye and the teeth.”

                                    She leans over the counter, suddenly interested. “I see. And the supplies you’re looking to sell..?”

                                    “I went to Aunt Dwi because she knows some alchemists, and I figured she’d get me a fair price. But I didn’t give her everything-I kept some of it to sell on my own once she found me an alchemist, so I wouldn’t have to split my profits with Dwi.”

                                    “I see…what’s your name?”

                                    “I’m Tavi. And you are?”

                                    “Golden Smoke. Tavi, what parts of the River Dragon do you have?”

                                    “Several parts.”

                                    “…do you have the liver?”

                                    “I’m not sure-it was dark when we butchered the thing, and I’m not really an expert on River Dragon anatomy.”

                                    “It would be about…this big?” She holds her arms out. “Darker in coloration than the other organs, sort of oval shaped and flat? Very tough and rubbery?”

                                    “…yeah, I’ve got something like that. I thought it was a spleen, it looked a bit like the pig spleens kids blow up and make balls out of…I cut it open and stuffed a bunch of other bits inside it, then tied it up with twine. Been using it as a bundle to keep the rest fresh.”

                                    “I see.” Smoke takes a deep breath, composing herself. You can tell she’s trying not to get excited…or trying not to let you know she’s excited. “If it is the liver, and if it is still fresh enough that it’s usable...we would be willing to buy it from you.”

                                    “I’ve also got three more teeth, two small ones and a big one. I have a section of scales and hide about…about this big, roughly rectangular. And I have assorted smaller sections of meat stuffed inside the liver.”

                                    “I can buy the teeth as well, we’ll grind them to powder for strength draughts, but I can’t offer you very much for them, the market is a little saturated right now. The hide…honestly there’s not a lot I can do with River Dragon scales, you’d be better off taking it to the Tailor District and finding a good tanner who knows how to work with crocodile hide. I’m happy to buy assorted bits of River Dragon flesh, and for the liver I can offer decent money, but I’ll have to actually see what sort of shape it’s in.”

                                    “It’s…it’s a little smoked. I kept it in a chimney to keep birds and bugs from getting to it.”

                                    “Hm. That shouldn’t hurt it, not if the inside is still good…what kind of smoke?”

                                    “What?”

                                    “What were you burning under it?”

                                    “Oh. No, I hung it over a smokestack at the foundry. Cheaper than burning a bunch of stuff myself.”

                                    “I see…one of the foundries over in Nighthammer?”

                                    “Yeah.”

                                    “Was it the big blast foundry powered by a waterwheel?”

                                    “No, I’m pretty sure they use oxen turning a crank.”

                                    “Hm. Probably some phosphorus and sulfur…maybe some lime…no spiritual contaminants or anything that would interfere with the process, probably…”

                                    “The process?” This is sounding like money. “I’ve heard you can use the bile from the liver to make a potion to make yourself invulnerable.”

                                    “Not quite...invulnerable. Just…it hardens your flesh, so it’s like River Dragon hide. Swords and arrows will slide off you, but your inner organs are still soft and things like hammers can still break your bones. There are a number of undesirable side effects, but it’s something a Guild Merchant might like for his bodyguards or gladiators.”

                                    “Sounds expensive.”

                                    “It is. For the liver in perfect condition, fresh out of the beast, we’d probably get enough bile to make a couple batches...we'd be willing to offer…say, two and a half Dirham.”

                                    "I...see." You do some quick mental calculations. That’s a thousand Dinar! You’re now the one taking a deep breath to try and appear calm. It’s difficult not to tremble.

                                    “That's fresh, though, Tavi. It’s been a couple of days now, and I expect it’s been considerably dried out by the smoking, and possibly contaminated…we’d offer much less, if we bought it at all.”

                                    You scowl. “If I put it in a jar with some vinegar to keep it from going rancid, would that count as contamination?”

                                    She shakes her head and sighs. “Yes, that’ll make it more expensive to purify, and I expect it’ll reduce the potency…but it could be worse. If it’s soaked in vinegar, say…one Dirham.”

                                    “One and a half.”

                                    “Sorry. It’s one Dirham, and if you wait to sell it the price is just going to keep going down as it gets more pickled and less fresh.”

                                    You grit your teeth, trying not to burst out giggling. A Dirham is four hundred Dinar! That is still a laughably vast amount of money! That’s enough money to pay rent every month for…well, for practically the rest of your life!

                                    “Do you have it with you?”

                                    “I don’t. I left it back home for safekeeping.”

                                    “Hm. It’s quite valuable. If you’d like, I’d be willing to come pick it up personally. I will need to get my Master to approve the expense, and hire a couple of guards to escort me, and that may take some time anyway. Would it be alright if I come by this afternoon to pick it up and deliver you a Cheque?”
                                    Okay, hold on. Deep breath. Slow down. How are you going to play this?



                                    Show Me The Money: You’re not handing your goods over for a slip of paper, Guild-backed or no. That Cheque might bounce! And more importantly, Guild-backed currency is easier to track, and that’s enough money that the Council of Entities is going to start getting interested in your taxes. Best to avoid a paper trail, for you and for the alchemist. You want your money in silver. Sixteen pounds of hard silver, half up front and half upon delivery of the liver.

                                    Have Your People Contact My People: That’s way more than enough money to kill someone over. Waaay more! You are not just telling her where you live or where you’re keeping the goods. It would be way cheaper to hire ten guys to kill you and rob your place and bring the liver back without knowing what it is than to actually pay you a whole Dirham. You need security. Go to the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants, talk to Fifteen-Fingers Liu, and have them set up the deal as a middleman. They’ll probably take a hefty cut, but this way it'll be a deal between Guild companies-you can get it in writing and make use of the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants' security.

                                    I Am Altering The Deal: If she’s willing to pay a Dirham for it…that means Blue Root will be able to sell the resulting potions at a significant markup. Probably for at least three or four Dirham. The right move here isn’t to sell it-it’s to use theft or the threat of violence to get the recipe (and maybe even the other alchemical ingredients-you ARE in a fully stocked alchemist's shop!), and then go make that potion yourself. Then you can sell it for more money than you’ve ever even thought about! Or maybe drink it yourself, and never worry about guys with knives in alleys again!

                                    Sure, We're All Friends Here:                                     This isn't just some random stranger. This apprentice’s Master is a regular client of Aunt Dwi, and she’s probably bringing in regular business in herbs and medicines…not to mention something of an intimate relationship with Blue Root and now with his apprentice Golden Smoke. She trusts them enough to do business, and Aunt Dwi is a good judge of character. You can probably trust these people, and if you offend them you may lose your chance at four hundred Dinar...and you've already dropped Dwi's name, so they might get offended at her and cost you an ally if things go badly. Don’t get greedy, don’t get paranoid-agree to the deal, go home and get the liver out of that vinegar to keep its value from going down further, and be ready to take the Cheque when the apprentice arrives.

                                    That's Interesting-But I Have A Better Idea: Something Else?
