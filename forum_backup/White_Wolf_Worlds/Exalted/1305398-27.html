    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
    As a RWBY fan, I've recently been exploring how THIS is the thing that makes Grimm really dangerous.

    That is: Almost any Creature of Grimm has at least as much mass as (and frequently more than) a human being, and Grimm usually try to tackle and pin targets to the ground.

    This is why I've been asking about how to model that capability.


    In the Red Trailer, Ruby Rose made exterminating battle-groups of Beowolves look easy because she avoided all but one attack, and that attack only knocked her back rather than down. Since she consistently avoided getting knocked prone or grappled, she always retained her complete freedom of movement.

    Obviously, this was also helped by her ability to kill the closest Grimm in a single attack, and sometimes kill multiple Grimm with a single attack, thus denying them these individuals the ability to attack her at all. But that doesn't map very well to battle-groups.

    But in the Weiss Short, Weiss Schnee gives a similar performance until she slips up and shows us how quickly Ruby could have died. Even ignoring the exact mistakes leading up to it, as soon as one Beowolf grappled Weiss and bore her down to the ground, she became unable to dodge or retreat, and she could not have easily parried or struck back with her sword even if she hadn't been disarmed. She was seconds away from being dogpiled and savaged to death by the other Beowolves when her sister banished them all.

    I'm away from my book right now, so I can only vaguely recall that being &quot;engaged&quot; by a battle-group isn't as restrictive as being grappled by a single opponent.
    .
    They can not restrain/drag targets, but being engaged prevents movement. Also Engage is actually insanely deadly. Firstly it auto succeeds to lock in the grapple, no initiative roll required. Next the control roll uses their highest combat stat, not their unarmed stat, so if they’re melee focused they get to use melee to determine rounds of control, and also melee to make savaging attacks.

    Here’s the silly part though, command actions add dice to ALL actions that battlegroup makes that turn. So if you u give them +7 dice for a command roll to engage, they get that to make the attack, determine rounds of control, and on their free savaging attack, which with the right weapons can be a smash or pierce or whatever.

            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
            It’s also just awesomely powerful. You could definitely make a gambit that does both grapple and knockdown in one go, maybe a cost of 4-5?

            Having both conditions on you at once lowers your Evasion by 3, Parry by 2 and attacks by either 4 or 6. If they survive the grapple they also don’t get to jump back up from prone either. I like the combo, it’s awesome and also feels appropriate to being pinned on your face, as long as the cost fits I say go for it. It’s just that grabbing and making a smash attack doesn’t require any asking of the ST or anything.
            Not every tackler will have a weapon with the smashing tag, and it shouldn't be necessary to have a cestus.

            Increasing the cost of the grappling gambit could be seen as substituting for the need (and cost) of using a cestus or hammer to make an immediate savaging attack count as smashing.

            And the idea is that this custom gambit WOULD require the attacker to go prone as well. NOT needing to go prone was a factor of using a normal grapple and then using the savaging attack itself to knock the target prone by whatever means.


            But however you pull it off, grabbing and making an immediate savaging smashing attack is indeed a powerful combo, and best of all it's completely legal within the base rules. It's an extremely natural, simple, and effective tactic that virtually any mob of enemies can and should instinctively employ.

            It's also another thing that Brawl boasts over various Martial Arts Styles. An Earth Dragon stylist can try this tactic to render an Anathema vulnerable to his Hearthmates, but Terrestrial Brawl is so much effective at it, whether with the Water or Earth elemental Charms.


            The additional penalties to Evasion and Parry don't actually matter to the grappler, as savaging and throwing attacks count the victim's Defense as 0 to begin with and those values cannot go lower than that. Unless dice-adders can raise the 0 up into positive numbers; then I could see the penalties eating the dice-bonuses.

            But it definitely matters to the grappler's friends.

            Again, I'm away from my materials, so I forget if the Restrain action penalizes Defense against other characters or just prevents the victim from taking actions on their turn (or even outside of their turn, through Reflexive Charms).

            I don't think battle-groups can Restrain, though I imagine a battle-group might have a special trained ability, or being composed of beings with a magical or biological restraining ability, which would Restrain a victim so their commander could attack unhindered.
