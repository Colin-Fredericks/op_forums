      Originally posted by TheBadWolf View Post
      They create a quite bad society
      What? Was Rome inherently bad? Athens? Imperial China? The French Empire under Napoleon? The British Empire? I could go on.

      Sure, in some ways, all of these were regimes that were truly horrible to certain populations whom they intentionally oppressed, and yet we romanticize them all in other ways.

      The Realm is not an evil nation, or at least not in any way that is unrealistic for an oligarchical imperial power to be. The Realm isn't evil in the way that Mordor is inherently, objectively, metaphysically made of evil. The Realm is evil in the way that human beings with power frequently do evil things for their own betterment. Every great empire commits evil; every great empire justifies evil done to the Other in the name of doing good things for its native citizens. Portraying the major empire of the setting in a manner that downplays the terrible things it does for its own gain and continued hegemony would make the Realm completely unrealistic and unrelatable.

                  The Dragon-Blooded aren't the equal of the Solars, but when confronted with something, they rise to the challenge. A DB game isn't a Solar game, and it shouldn't be treated as one. A DB game works really well when done like a war movie, or a samurai movie, or lower power animes (I don't watch anime very much, so ask someone else about this). Just because they are weaker than a Solar doesn't mean a DB game is a fantastic game. I once ran two simultaneous games of Exalted: Solar group with one Abyssal and Dragon-Blooded group with one Abyssal. In the Solar game they cut a swatch through the Threshold while pursuing their goals, changing every society they visited, making alliances (through brainwashing or other activities). The other game had the same overarching plot line, but the method was much different. Politics wasn't about meeting a few people and using a few Charms. They struck alliances through marriage, political favors. Sometimes they had to assist an army. Some of them died. The Solars gave up and founded their own empire and used the Abyssal as an enforcer. The DBs actually completed the chain of purifying an Abyssal. They had to have other people do a few things, and they paid high prices for it.

                  That's kind of what DBs are to me. They are the guys who get the job done. They might not be the lone big damn hero who cuts through the opposition easily, but they are the elite team that gets the job done, even if not everyone makes it.

                  Solars are myths. DBs are Seal Team Six.

                                  Dragonbloods aren't reeeally my favourite... but they were and when I'm in a Dragonblooded mood:

                                  I like anything that was created weak and became stronger than it was supposed to be - like, I prefer, in theory, the Enlightened Undercastes of the Mountain Folk to the Artisans' intentional design. Or where the ability of the Sidereals to create the Sidereal Martial Arts wasn't seen or intended by the Maidens, its an unexpected wrinkle in their plan and it may just make everything better (even though they are, gods of foresight). Subverting divine intentions and order. It's about tenaciously rejecting what Heaven tells you is your place and what your life should be, not about having Heaven tell you that you are the one true king.

                                  (Exalted is designed so all the main splats can be underdogs you root for on one level or another. I really like the divine order subverting one, where the destined king that Heaven sent is not the one who saves the day, other people prefer the one where Solars are just like pulp heroes who rise from anywhere and whose skill and drive is a clear and simple extension of the human in a world of other people who master, understand and turn to human purposes the essence outside the human. And so on.)

                                  I particularly like that rough edged, wild, natural, unpretentious elemental magic is particularly well suited for the aesthetic of overrunning your purpose - elemental power gets the job done where the fancy, yet somehow hollow magic of the Enlightened Ones doesn't.

                                  I like the idea of powers and skills that work with uniting their master and his essence more closely with nature, where the master takes on nature's aspect, rather than merely manipulate it. Be water, my friend. (The Lunars' powers also have some of this).

                                  I like the idea of people who are flawed themselves, and whose personalities are far from all being world shaking and part of a flawed society. I like the idea that many people like this can make real change in the world.

                                  I like the idea of a larger canvas of people working together roughly as equals to change the world than where the broad focus is on a canvas of just less around 1000 (for all that that any game is going to tight focus on a few).

                                      1. Cut-throat Dynastic politics! Other Exalts can get into intrigue, especially Sidereals, but nobody else does politics like a Dynast.
                                      2. Playing the Underdog. Hunting a dangerous Solar or Lunar as part of a PC Wyld hunt is scary and fun in a way that's harder to get as a more high-powered Exalt.
                                      3. Because Being Rich Is Awesome. Sometimes you want to play an entitled rich bastard or a spoiled princess and throw your money and your pomposity around. Dynasts do it best.
                                      4. They've got charms to kill a man from thirty feet away with a sweet guitar riff. I mean sure, so do Abyssals and Infernals, but come on, plucking a string to sever a head is badass.
                                      5. Elemental superpowers: Look, sometimes you want to go all Poison Ivy or Human Torch or Avatar State Yip Yip on somebody, and Dragonblooded control of the elements is awesome.
                                      6. Elemental Supersoldier Noir: The Realm is a place of corruption and intrigue and vice...but it's also a place that encourages honor and service in its upper class, and the conflict between the two can make for beautiful stories about well-intentioned young Dynasts struggling to take down corrupt elders or to protect the weak from the heartless bureaucracy or change the system from the inside. Dragonbloods do this best: Solars tend to tear the system down and replace it, Lunars and Sidereals tend to go all uncatchable superhero, but Dragonblooded would-be reformers rarely have the advantage of magical anonymity and thus you get interesting plots as the system tries to stamp them down. Wandering dragonblooded Magistrates appointed by the Empress but now abruptly without any support from their superiors teaming up to make sure they've got each others' backs makes for a FANTASTIC PC Sworn Brotherhood.
                                      7. School drama: the House of Bells and the Heptagram are bloody brilliant.
                                      8. The Empty Throne: I wanna outfight Auntie Mnemon and outmaneuver cousin Ejava and win the civil war and bloody well SIT on that shiny five-headed chair. And then I want to deal with the trials and tribulations of running the Empire for a while as the Anathema grow in strength on the borders...
                                      9. Bein' Prince of the Earth: It's nice being able to wander around being open about what you are and watching mortals bow and scrape before you without worrying about if you're drawing the Wyld Hunt's attention. Playing an elemental Bodhisattva is fun!
                                      10. The Immaculate Order: Playing devoutly religious characters is sometimes a lot of fun! The Order is pretty well-defined and has lots of fun guidelines for how to live life properly. Trying to maintain your vegetarian lifestyle and keep up your meditation and defend the weak and teach the way of the Dragons while traveling with a party of PCs is tons of fun, and getting your worldview challenged over the course of the game is fun too!

                                      So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                          The dragon-blooded have both a lower power-level, and greater integration with the setting as a whole, this forces them to work within the world of exalted rather than bulldoze their way over it in the fashion of the celestial exalted. The Celestial Exalted, once they surpass the rookie stage quickly pass several tiers of power above many setting elements, making it difficult to integrate any of that stuff into their stories in a meaningful way.

                                          Also, the way the setting is structured Dragon-blooded have far more in group variability, especially in terms of life experience. There are really new dragon-blooded and really old ones, and they occupy all possible roles and expressions of exalted power. Solars/Abyssals/Infernals are almost universally new to their power, and while Lunars have a broader dynamic the existing fluff really scatters the group, while sidereals are limited by their small group size (also a problem for Infernals) and specialized duties. The abundance and breadth of the dragon-blooded host allow for roles and possibilities that don't work with celestial exalted (excepting integrated stories, but the inherent enmity the setting designed makes that tricky).

                                          To me the dragon-blooded just have more possibility (at least within exalted as currently designed) than the other groups. Also I feel the general absence of cheap perfect defenses makes combat descriptively more interesting.

                                              I think I would like DB more if I "fixed" some aspects of the setting that bug me, e.g. the Great Cruse, the so-called 'perfection' of the Solar (the Solar Exaltation may be perfect, but grafting a perfect onto a non-perfect does not make a perfect), the idea that the Deathlords are supposed to be a remotely worrying threat. Not meaning to turn this into a 'bagging on Exalted issues' thread, we all know these exist.

                                              For example, the Great Cruse being a curse laid on the Exalted/Solar, which makes them go bad. If, instead, their madness was the inevitable result of moving further and further away from flawed humanity, then you can view the DB as a kind of 'safety mechanism': from what we saw of the First Age, it's not impossible for a Solar to get it into their head that they should destroy a mountain (and everyone who lives on it) to create a piece of art with the land. When they get that mad, it's the job of the DB to take them down. They'll get mowed down, sure, but enough DB can kill a Solar, even if it's whole armies on one. That would help feed into their military and discipline theme, and also give a better reason for their current anti-Solar vendetta than "the Sids made us do it and we're dumb!" Plus, it'd make the one advantage the DB have over the Solar - the Solars comparatively more difficult dealing with elemental attacks - make a lot more sense.

                                              Stuff like that. Then all the Exalts would work together a bit more thematically: the Solar may be the great rulers, but the Sids advise them, the Lunar support and guard them (both in the sense that the Solar are protected and the world is protected from them) and the DB as a fail-safe. Would explain why the DB are so battle-oriented and comparatively weaker, too: weaker makes them closer to human, and as such the 'drift' would be lesser.

                                              Hell, you wouldn't even really need to change the outcome. The DB may have still overthrown the Solar (maybe reasoning the gains weren't worth the losses), but the problem is they just aren't capable of dealing with the issues. Then you're cooking with gas, theme-wise.
