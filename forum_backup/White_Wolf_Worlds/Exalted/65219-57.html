    I got headcannon for days, son!

    Ommmmmmmn

    The Wyld came to be from a sentient singularity called Omn or Ohm which contained within itself the potentiality of all things and whose only options were to Be or Not Be. A voice called Fear advocated the latter and Hope the former. Omn chose to Be and gave forth the endless Wyld.

    First World problems

    Creation is the First World - It is the very first island of order to arise from chaos whatsoever. While Creation exists as it does, there are only four dimensions anywhere and no alternate universes or other possibilities exist at all. As such, Creation is not actually subject to inexorable entropy on a macro scale and can maintain any given status indefinitely. In Creation, the center can, in theory, hold.

    Glacial progress

    Creation was formed by a proto-Promordial called Ymrran the Demarcating Glacier, who was the first being in all the Wyld to say This can only be Itself and never be That. He carved out a mate for himself from the chaos, who was Cytherea. She birthed for him a Reality, which could be more Itself than anything, and from it he made a home, which was Gaia. The other Primordials were their children, and as each child was born its aspect imprinted on Gaia until Creation was formed from their joint influence.

    We are actually on our THIRD Usurpation: At the behest of his mother Gaia (whom her father Ymrran had raped, by the by) Empyrean slew his father and carved Yu Shan from his bones. Unless you count Ymrran himself usurping reality from Chaos as a Usurpation. Either way, Creation is moving its way through a cycle of kingship: The King Who Tames the Land (Ymrran), the King Who Rules by Force (Empyrean), the King Who Rules by Righteousness (UCS), and the Mandate of Heaven. There are more phases beyond this.

    I Like 'Em Round, and Big!

    In the Primordial Age, Creation was spheroid, and the Elemetal poles were thus:
    • Air: An icy firmament with the stars as defense platforms to ward off the Wyld
      • There were many stars, always an even number, each twinned with another across the firmanent, each named and themed for a great beast. Together they bore the weight of the firmament. Most were destroyed when SWLiHN did that thing she did. The best-known Guardian Stars are Bat, Lion, Bear, Horse, Rat, Wolf, Hare, Eagle, Turtle, Dog, Fish, and Elephant.
    • Earth: Mount Ymrr (of which "Meru" is an ancient mispronounciation) rose from what would be the south pole to pierce the firmament
    • Fire: Creation's molten core, utterly hostile to all life, even Primordial life
    • Water: A great vortex wandering the seas, which would take one through the world and deeper yet, far beyond the diameter and into the Wyld
    • Wood: The First Tree, which Cytherea planted at the roof of the world for reasons unknown, which was on target to pierce the firmament when the Gods rebelled
    Godforge

    The gods were created in a continent-sized temple called Godforge, a new god whenever a Primordial needed one. The Unconquered Sun was the first and last god to be created using fires from the Elemental Pole, and his creation irreparably damaged Godforge, but only for active use -- it now creates gods automatically when they are needed. The Fire of Exigence comes from Godforge. During the Usurpation, Godforge's emergency protocols hid all but its gateway away Elsewhere.

    Games within games
    Creation is not an engine for gathering prayers; Yu-Shan is. Ymrran's bones resonate with his nature: the will for one thing to be and another not to be, the very root of prayer. In the center of Yu-Shan, that concentrated hope and desire congeals into the Pearl of Aspiration, the thing for which all sentience ultimately strives in its secret heart: freedom from impetus, unending joy and leisure, the Unerring Poison, the Games of Divinity.

    Jesus Lizards

    There are two "lost" breeds of Dragon King
    • Brontok: The greatest builders to ever live, these sauropod centaurs (I refuse to say "taur," that's furry talk) built the cycloopean edifices that boggled even the minds of First Age Solars, who could recreate the work, but never with ease. Godforge was just one of their many projects, as were many of the buildings in Yu-Shan, including the Jade Pleasure Dome, which they built in a day AROUND the playing Primordials.
    • Tyrok or Tyranok: The "High Kings," towering and noble dragon kings whose forms inspired the creation of the Unconquered Sun. When Ligier, displeased with his worshippers, scoured the Dragon Kings mercilessly for centuries, the Tyrok grew wings and warred against him -- and won! It was their triumph that led Empyrean to create the Unconquered Sun in their image and for their worship, his sole act of magnanimity. Exaltation was first tested on the Tyrok, and worked like a charm except for driving the High Kings mad. It was this flaw in the Exaltation that the Neverborn used to create the Great Curse. "ONLY ONCE!" the Tyrok cried out as they slaughtered the Brontok, and fled forever into the Wyld.
    That's just the macro stuff. Headcannon for DAYS.

        The Silver-Crowned believe it is their duty to protect all of An-Teng and practice a rite of passage in which their youths are exiled from the dominion and unable to return home until without proof of their worth.

                The Heptagram is not a Hogwarts-style magic school - it's a massive geomantic construct, similar to the Pattern of Amber. In the wake of a magical calamity, it was inscribed by seven powerful Sorcerers; some of them died in its creation, and most of the rest did not survive into the present day - the only exceptions are Mnemon, and perhaps one other character, should I need such a person. Its "graduates" are those who walk along it, transforming their Essence as they do so. Well, other than the people (Dragonblooded included) who attempt that feat and are instead consumed by green fire, obviously. Initiates of the Heptagram make up a plurality of the Realm's sorcerers (and perhaps just barely a majority), and are generally the least-distrusted - their expertise in Geomancy is certainly very useful in raising new Manses in conquered regions of the Threshold. Whether they're from the Heptagram or not, Realm Sorcerers tend to follow a system of Master-Apprentice relationships, as well as highly-formalized exchanges and deals, which (combined with the wide social stigma) link them into a surprisingly tight-knit community.

                Lookshy is austere, conservative, and focused on martial pursuits, so it has fewer Sorcerers relative to its Terrestrial population than does the Realm - and in the Realm, the number is less than 1%, so Lookshy has maybe four or five of them. Playing off of Lookshy's, well, Spartan culture, this is a sect of grim ascetics who brew strange elixirs and master their internal energies. Other traditions of sorcery are more likely to find acceptance in Nexus or Great Forks... but, frankly, were I (1) running a thing in the Scavenger Lands, and (2) wanted a large number of Sorcerers, I'd be more likely to introduce them as Witch-Queens and Mad Lords of their own strange kingdoms than as members of some monolithic power bloc, and most of them would be non-Exalts. I would carry over the "surprisingly formalized network of shared knowledge/power" thing from above, though.

                        Eight centuries is a long time for a Terrestrial Exalt. It's always been a mystery, the way the Empress managed to live so long. By all accounts, she was a century old when the Contagion struck... that would put her age at nine centuries, easily.

                        One century into her reign, the Scarlet Empress began to plan for contingencies. She covertly gathered a core of savants, sorcerers, and physicians. In the course of twenty years of research into Anagathics, they finally found a solution... a sustainable treatment that could prolong a Terrestrial's life indefinitely. All it would require were alliances in the Underworld, and access to its critical Artifact.

                        Once they finished their research, and documented their findings, the Empress had them all killed and buried with lavish honors. They were her negotiators in Stygia. Five years later, she spent one passionate night with her Imperial Consort and departed on the first sabbatical of her reign.

                        She traveled to the Underworld, where her negotiators had laid the groundwork for her arrival. Her emissaries had promising news for her; she already had allies.

                        The Dead Sun Rebellion, fourty five Solar ghosts dating back as far as the Primordial War. The last guard of the Spectre War lingered, watching carefully the manuvers of the Deathlords. Lead by the Righteous Sunlight Martyrs (Primordial War casualties granted the status of Righteous Dead), the Dead Sun Rebellion represents the single largest concentration of remnant First Age knowledge on Stygia. Though she hadn't known it, when the Contagion Struck it was the Dead Sun Rebels that acted to initiage emergency protocols within the Sword of Creation, releasing its firing control systems from the Lockdown procedure it had been left in by the paranoia of the Usurpation.

                        The Dead Suns had military access codes to Solar armories on the Blessed Isle that had been beyond the reach of armed forces during the Shogunate. The Empress needed them. The Dead Suns had long established diplomatic access to the Court of the Dual Monarchs. The Empress needed that access. And so an alliance was struck.

                        The Dead Suns would share select Armory access codes, and negotiate with the Monarchs on the Empress's behalf for continued access to the Calendar of Setesh for purposes of her longevity procedure. In return, the Empress would use her authority over the Immaculate Faith to insert Worship days for the remnant ghosts of the Dead Suns, including arms, armors, and other appropriate grave goods. In addition, they called for regular reports on the deaths of Solars to the Wyld Hunt, and the empress's aid in selectively preparing and equipping one new Dead Sun every ten years. Every twenty fifth Solar confronted by the Wyld Hunt would be brought to the Imperial Manse, offered the Dead Sun's cause privately in the Empress's chambers, and permitted a choice. If they declined, they would be killed immediately. If they accepted, their training would begin. The Empress would have artifacts forged explicitly for them, and they would be trained to the best of the Empress's ability, ready to provide aid to the rebellion.

                        The Rebellion has never been as successful as its leaders would have liked... the Wyld Hunt fodder and even the specially prepared troops that they've managed to recruit rarely cause much more than an annoyance to the Deathlords... but the Martyrs remain a strong influence on Stygia proper. And so, the arrangement continued.

                        The Empress's procedure involves a celestial ritual tapping into the repressed stasis of the Underworld. It would use functions within the Calendar itself to flood her body with the energies of unmoving timelessness. Because those energies are normally innimical to life, the ritual channels the most dangerous overflow through her chakra into her womb. If it is empty, the energy gathers there as a poison, stripping her of fertility... but in the Empress's careful preparation, she only subjects herself to infusions when her body is flush with the quickening growth of new life. The conflicting death and life energies compete within her physiology, and predictably settle into a mutation within the child... a deathly twisting of their Terrestrial lineage, manifesting in ghostly pale animas and associations with the Underworld... and a capacity for necromancy.

                        The Empress would travel to the Underworld once a century, remaining away from her throne for no more than three years. Whichever Shadowland she departed from would be the home for the child she bore, raised in secret as the Ghost Breath Lord of that region, keeping tabs on the situation within the lands of the dead.

                        Most recently, the Empress departed for an Infusion five years ago. As she finished, bound for Thorns, she was accosted by the forces of the Eye and Seven Despairs. The Eye dispatched her Dead Sun escort and fought her to a standstill. Beyond Creation and its nourishing Essence Flows, and depleated from her Celestial magics, the Deathlord forced her to expend all of her magic in combat before finally seizing her and her newborn child as prisoner.

                        Lillun proved to be a useful bargaining chip for the Neverborn, allowing them to seize a superior bargaining position against the Yozi for their Exaltation Restructuring techniques. Now, the Eye takes as his primary mission the extraction of intelligence from the Empress regarding the Dead Sun movements within Stygia. He does this in secret, concealing it even from many of his Death Knights.

                        Meanwhile, direct aggressive actions against the Dead Suns by the Walker in Darkness has thinned their core membership from fourty five to twenty seven... the Usurpation era targets taken by the Ash Covenant so that their access codes could be found... or traced to the ghosts to which they had been entrusted. This has been the core of the Covenant's ability to remobilize the Thousand Forged Dragon silos and First Age armories in Rathess.

                        With the circumstances of the Empress explained, the question must be confronted; who is the Bride of the Ebon Dragon? She has been called the Scarlet Bride, and the Queen of Hell.... and any Intelligence agency would swear upon a stack of Immaculate Texts (and their own reproductive organs) that the woman is the Empress.... How then is this possible? How, then, could this be?

                        Very simple. Like all things of the Dragon, the nature of his Bride is a lie. And the Queen of Fangs has been... strangely silent of late. Of course, most sensible people leave Raksi be in any case, but even her small retinue of sorcerous Apprentices haven't found her... and what's more, her prize posession is missing. Within her chambers (braved only by her most trusted) is a bare lecturn where once rested the Book of Three Circles.

                        Coincidentally, on a similar Lectern in the Conventacle Malfesant rests the Chalcanth formula required to compose a unique Vitriol Etching upon Moonsilver... particularly Moonsilver that has been formed into tattoo artifacts, such as the Caste Fixing, Form-protecting innovations of the Silver Pact. The formula is structured to react with such tattoo's, damaging them in part, upgrading them in other parts.... subverting them by enhancing them. More explicitly, it allows a Lunar to treat shape shifting gained through Infernal charms as components of their natural shape shifting.

                                I have a similar perspective on this, if I may engage in some intra-thread necromancy.

                                As I've run these matters since First Edition, the substance of the Great Curse's actual word for word recitation was a warning from the lips of the dying Primordials. In consistent whispers just loud enough to be heard by their killers, they would steadily turn to the Solars and Lunars and say "You shall be betrayed, as we were." This persisted as one by one, the Primordials were slain or exiled beyond the world by beings they had believed too small to be worthy of notice, save for the few traitors who were happy to simply be forgotten by the new order they had aided.

                                This narrative thread wove its way through the actions of all the Exalted in the First Age, driving the greatest excesses of the Solars and eventually provoking the Sidereals into composing the Great Prophecy. Thus, the Usurpation. Thus, one by one, the Celestial Exalted were slain or exiled beyond the world by beings they had believed to be too small to be worthy of notice, save for the few traitors who were happy to simply be forgotten by the new order they had aided.

                                The teeth of this curse have had their bite. The poison has gone out of it. What lingers is an echo of what the thing was at its height. They might be a permanent mark, but it will never lead to the perpetuating cruelties that were seen in the First Age.

                                    Starmetal is made from fallen stars not dead gods. These stars are formed from the congealed essence of averted fate, when a mortal doesn't quite make the choice that the pattern spiders expect. Minor divergences such as 'I had coffee rather than tea today' congeal with other masses of starmetal and burn up on entry, losing its magical content in the process. Usable quantities drop only when a mortal chooses against a fated world-shaking life in favor of something mundane, such as a great general who would have shaped a nation instead falling in love and choosing to settle on a farm and have a family. The power of destiny is persistent, however, and Starmetal is consequently rare.
