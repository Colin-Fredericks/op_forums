    [White Elephant for Taleksi] Whispers for an Empty Throne

    01-05-2020, 09:24 PM
    Originally posted by Taleksi View Post

    I'm currently planning a Creation-spanning campaign, where a group of Dragon-blooded try to investigate what happened to the Scarlet Empress. The players will be playing both the Dragon-blooded and the Sidereals that are subtly aiding their mission. I would greatly appreciate A) a list of reasons/rumours about Her Redness disappearance (I explicitly won't be using the option given and hinted in 2e). B) Some antagonists or other NPC's with potential to interact with both the DB's and the Sids or C) an interesting location or two and some suggestions, why it would be relevant for the investigation.
    I wrote this more or less went in order, and I was, for the most part, happier with the ideas as I went. I didn't get to locations, but I was dragging my feet, I was running behind, and I was happy to close with Anak Rhum and his desire to see the Realm ended to resolve what he sees as an unforgivable clerical error.

    3 Rumours:

    Among the Legions:

    “Everyone knows that the legion of Mountain Folk returned to the mountain one richer, right? Weird, don’t you think: no army returns home greater than when it was deployed, save the red-piss legion most years. And yet here…

    You know what I think? The Empress has secreted herself beneath the mountain, and for these last five years has worked to secure some great favor from the Mountain Folk. Dragons only know what.”


    Among the Gods:

    “A Prince of the Earth walks our golden streets. Certainly, it’s a common enough rumor, or a story we bat amongst ourselves. I admit to some pleasure in how those stories end. We may here remind the ‘Chosen’ that they are no Prince of Heaven, and one of us is allowed, even obliged to cast them out of Yu-shan. But this one—this story—it does not end so. In fact, it does not end. Not yet.

    This, I must confess, is because it is true. And it is now, not last week, last season, or some time in the beginning of the age. She’s here, somewhere.

    There are those of us that hate her, and others who share her bed. But she is accorded every comfort that Heaven sees fit to give, with no concrete answers given. Why is she here? Most often, I have heard tell that she seeks an audience with the highest of holies, and has instead, each Calibration, been met by one of the other Incarnae in his place. Each one has offered her a great boon to leave, and told her something new of the terrible price for what she wants. But she sits resolute, refusing to state her case to any but the Unconquered Sun himself."

    Among the Dynasty:

    “There’s a child of the Empress none of us know about. I laughed at the idea for years, but now that I think of it…

    The story goes like this: The Empress takes her leisure as she sees fit, and sometimes—just sometimes—she has seen fit to disguise herself as a commoner. Oh, not some beggar or some humble farmer, a merchant or a legionnaire at the least. She’s got to have some dignity. But she sometimes takes a lover in secret for those times, and it seems like you do that often enough, and if they have the right bits, eventually Venus giggles and tells you to either grab your maiden tea or prepare a nursery.

    I’d wager there’s at least one Great house founder who has that going for them, but those kinds of rumors get you a daiklave to the throat, so we’ll let that go there. Just…don’t tell the Cynis that their debasement is something they’ve come by honestly, hm?

    Anyway, halfway between here and Juche there’s a small city where the Arbiters of Purpose hadn’t been for ten years. I mean, that could mean nothing, sure, if it weren’t that the whole area’s a blind spot in their records.

    The Empress has sent messengers, gifts there a couple times through in recent years, though always quietly and through the most secretive of back channels.

    Just yesterday, a report from the Arbiters came in. Nothing big. Nothing special. There’s a scribbled-out mention of rumors of a young woman taking the breath of the Dragons a couple years back.

    Someone is being hidden.

    So if you ask me? The Empress has made her pick. At least, she’s decided it’s not going to be any of us. Maybe she thinks that since she was just a soldier in the Shogunate and none of the nobility there did anything to save Creation, handing the crown of the world to someone born wielding that power can’t lead to anything good. Maybe she just hates us all.

    She’s got this child. And for the last five years, she’s been leading her on, guiding her to the image of herself, at least who she thinks she was when she took the crown: Capable and proud, in mastery of her Essence, but not born to the trappings of nobility.

    And then, when the Realm needs it, up shall rise this child, in the footsteps of her mother, across the sky and above the Imperial Manse, ready to assume control as the new Empress.

    It’s horrible. But there isn’t a night that goes by that I wonder if this should be, should I kneel? I do not expect that she will be any more merciful, and if this bastard urges us to bend the knee to her, a straight spine will lead to a broken neck. I would be dead, but I would die with pride.

    But I would still be dead.”



    Anak Rhum, Most Efficacious Chronicler of State’s Ends

    Despite his aged appearance, Anak Rhum is in fact only a scant handful of centuries old, having been born mortal and uplifting himself to full divinity through sorceries and alchemy. He has regretted this decision ever since: once among the greatest of living sorcerers, an encounter with a Sidereal Exalt shortly post-apotheosis saw his ambitions with Creation rudely cut short and found himself in Yu-shan. Since then he has elevated himself to a middling functionary in the Violet Bier of Sorrows, keeping detailed records of fallen nations. While the wars of the River Provinces normally keep his department busy, he directs his ambitions at a lofty target: the Realm. The Scarlet Empire has fashioned itself not just a successor to the Old Realm, but its continuation, and within the bureaucracies of the Bier this is a legal truth. Rhum, for his part, claims that the records of the Old Realm should rightfully be his, and that these were relocated from his own office shortly before he assumed the position. This is largely disregarded by his fellow deities, and his desire for them to be returned as a sort of ambition unbecoming of a deity.

    The disappearance of the Empress is an opportunity for him to set right this grave imbalance. While he bears the Dragon-Blooded no ill will--the actions of the Wyld Hunt have added to his records more than a dozen times over his tenure--he is well aware that should the Realm break or fall, then he gains a double windfall: whether the present-day Realm is the continuation or successor of the old one becomes irrelevant, and he may file them as he pleases. To this end his traditional loyalty to the Bronze faction has become somewhat shakier, especially with his exaltation of an Exigent champion, but rather than forsake the Realm he tentatively backs multiple potential Dynastic successors, with an eye towards those who would remake part of the Blessed Isle in their own image. Despite his present divinity and clerical position, he is still a scholar and sorcerer at heart, and would love the opportunity to access the histories he is currently denied for his personal edification. Sadly, in this Time of Tumult it is unlikely he’s going to have the free time in the foreseeable future.

    Appearance:

    Rhum appears mostly human; his beardless face is brown and leathery, his hair is long, thick, and bright white, and his eyes are dark and alert. His nails are the deep, faceted red of crystallized cinnabar, however, and the smell of ash and dust and ozone clings to him, precedes, follows, and lingers. Despite being restrained in his gestures and in his speech, neither carries a bit of the infirmity of his apparent age, and the latter still carries a hint of his native Firetongue. He wears the trappings appropriate to one of his divine station, shimmering gray silks trimmed with an unfading violet, and carries with him an inkbrush that writes in quicksilver. While he despises violence as something other, lesser people and gods do, he can defend himself with this as described below.

    Essence: 3; Willpower: 7; Join Battle: 5 dice Personal Motes: 80 Health Levels: -0/-1x2/-2x2/-4/Incap.

    Actions: Research 9 dice, Sorcerous Knowledge 7 dice (Specialty: Alchemy), Social Interaction 5 dice, Read Intentions 7 dice

    Combat
    Attack (Unarmed): 8 dice (Damage 9)
    Attack (Quicksilver Memory) 12 dice (Damage 12)
    Combat Movement: 4 dice
    Evasion 2, Parry 4
    Soak: 6


    Intimacies:
    • I Deserve Better than My Current Station (Major Principle)
    • I Have No Use for Fools (Minor Principle)
    • Compliné (Minor Tie of Resigned Mentorship)
    • The Sidereal Exalted (Minor Tie Wary Respect)
    • I Will Have the Secrets of the Old Realm (Major Principle)
    • Knowledge Is Power (Defining Principle)

    Backgrounds:
    Artifact 3: Quicksilver Memory, the brush with which Anak Rhum writes may be wielded in combat as an artifact short direlance.
    Cult 1: While Anak Rhum is no great deity, a handful of Scavenger Lords and scholars do make offerings to him.

    Charms:

    Materialize (40m, 1 wp; Simple): A sound like the starting patter of rain on broken stone begins to tap itself out, and Rhum fades into being as though through a dusty glass cleaned by the impact of raindrops.

    Measure the Wind: Anak Rhum may divine the Essence of anyone within a place no longer inhabited by its creators. This also applies to bustling, inhabited places built on the ruins or remains of older peoples, as long as he may appropriately stunt it.

    A Historical Footnote (5m; Simple; Decisive; Eclipse): Anak Rhum lays his touch upon a mortal, rolling 8 dice against his target’s Stamina + Integrity. If he succeeds, then for a month and a day the mortal suffers from something like the Arcane Fate of the Sidereals; he automatically uses the same dice pool (8 dice) as stealth whenever he attempts to interact with someone, and when leaves a scene, he uses this pool to make an automatic Instill roll appealing to a Minor Intimacy against all present; If this overcomes a witness’s Resolve, then they forget that the target was present if they ever noticed him, as well as any actions they took that scene. This does not obscure the signs of their passing in any fashion, and this effect only suppresses others’ perception of him in the present and the past for their duration: such a fool may dance into a Guild office naked and take a sack of silver unnoticed, but if inquiries are made next season then any witnesses will be able to describe him without difficulty, save those implicit in waiting months to give a description of a thief.
    An Eclipse or other Exalt who learns this charm rolls their Charisma + Lore in place of Anak Rhum’s 8 dice.

    Consigned to Vellum (+4m; Supplemental; Instant): With one strike Anak Rhum renders a fool a non-entity as far as most are concerned. With a second, he makes it an inarguable fact. If Anak Rhum uses A Historical Footnote against someone already under its effects, he may use this charm to bind them into a scroll. Outside of sorcerous projects or other great magics, the target is lost forever, although those with access to the scroll may peruse it at their leisure as though investigating the victim’s history.

    Millennial Footsteps Echo (8m; Simple; One Action): Rhum can make Investigation rolls without penalty for time passed. Deliberate attempts to obscure or alter details can interfere, but no natural or accidental processes can stop him.

    Anak Rhum was a sorcerer in his mortal life, and he still is, knowing multiple spells of the Terrestrial circle; his work has kept him too busy to effectively seek deeper initiations into its mysteries.


    Compliné, Walker of Ruins

    Touphas, Child of the Tower, was a young anathema who styled herself the daughter of the demon Octavian. She martialed an army to herself and aggressively seized several small provinces in the Scavenger Lands. A temporary coalition of neighbor states attempted to bring an end to her ambitions, but almost failed; Anak Rhum petitioned for, and was granted, an Exigence, and bestowed it upon a member of a resistance cell within one of Touphas’ conquered territories.

    Compliné succeeded where armies failed, striking down the Child of the Tower in single combat. To her relief, no demons appeared in following nights to avenge the champion who claimed their blood. To her anger, the victorious armies redrew the maps while she nursed her wounds, and took the fallen nations (and credit for the victory) for their own. She was furious with Rhum when she realized that his interest in the details was merely for accuracy of his records.

    Since then Compliné has served in a handful of mercenary companies; she has left all of them after a brief, often brilliant period of employment, usually by butting heads with authorities. Some look to Rhum’s domain and his upstart status as influencing her, but the god of fallen kingdoms insists that she’s come by this rebellious streak without his help.

    She has a passing familiarity with the Sidereal Exalted (her patron’s domain has placed her in contact with more than one Reckoner), but is equally aware of their tendency to fade from the minds of most.

    Compliné has little of her patron’s interests in histories or decorum, though her blessing from Rhum gives her some facility with them anyway, and also allows her to temporarily emulate Anak Rhum’s ascension.

    Essence: 3 Personal Motes: 18 Peripheral Motes: 42 Willpower: 8; Join Battle: 7 dice Health Levels: -0/-1x4/-2x5/-4/Incap.

    Actions: Introduce Facts 8 dice(only w/ Sage of the Thousand-year Library) Recall History 6 dice, Intimidate 8 dice, Carouse 5 dice

    Combat:
    Attack (Unarmed): 10 dice (Damage 12)
    Attack (Piercing Gaze, Jade Direlance) 9 dice (Damage 18)
    Combat Movement: 6 dice
    Evasion 3, Parry 5
    Soak/Hardness: 15/7

    Intimacies:
    • The Fallen Province of Usciad (Minor Tie of Nostalgia)
    • “There is *always* an Ending” (Major Principle)
    • Mist, the Eternal Revolutionary (Minor Tie of Kinship)
    • Anak Rhum (Major Tie of Grudging Respect)
    • “I will not be what others want.” (Defining Principle)


    Appearance:
    Compliné is a lean woman, not tall but stretched in a way that makes her seem longer at a distance, almost gaunt up close. Her black hair is hacked short and uneven, its curl making it even more chaotic if she hasn't trimmed it lately with the edge of a blade, and a heavy scar stands out against her tan skin, tracing the left side of her face and just barely sparing her eye, a memory from the days before her Exaltation.

    Compliné’s anima banner manifests as a yellow reminiscent of old vellum. At glowing and bonfire, it presents a moderate environmental hazard, albeit rarely immediately dangerous; on any turn Compliné spends Essence, she rolls seven dice to break objects wholly encompassed by her anima, and not carried by herself or another person. She treats her effective Strength as 1 at Glowing and 3 at Bonfire. On actions where her anima is iconic, she treats her strength as 7 for this purpose. Damage done is not inflicted violently, instead manifesting as ages of use and wear suddenly catching up to it.

    Charms:

    Excellency: Compliné may use her Excellency to add up to her Attribute in dice to a roll. While in an area of Ruins (i.e. a location in which Anak Rhum could feasibly Measure the Wind on a target), she may increase her maximum Excellency by her Essence.

    Sage of the Thousand-year Library (6m, 1 wp; Simple; One Story; Essence 2): Compliné spends a scene in meditation or research; for the duration of this charm, she is treated as having appropriate specialties and prerequisite skills to introduce facts regarding history, ruins, and sorcery.

    An Eye for Yesterday (3m; Simple; Indefinite; Essence 1): Compliné’s eyes cloud over, and she perceives everything that has ever happened in an area at once. As long as she allows this state to persist, she suffers a -1 to general perception rolls, but instead turns this into a bonus to investigate. This state also allows her to perceive immaterial spirits.

    Reaching Across Ages (1m; Supplemental; Instant; Essence 1); Compliné takes an action, and finds anything appropriate to the area where she is, even if the passage of years would render this unlikely: An abandoned barracks has a spear left by one of its former residents, and an old kitchen has fruit that has dried rather than spoiled. Compliné can also use this ability to interact with the immaterial entities she perceives via An Eye for Yesterday.

    Tears Run Dry (3m; Supplemental; Instant; Essence 2) Compliné makes an Inspire roll: any listeners who have Intimacies relating to the subject feel an upwelling of emotion and catharsis. They may accept the weakening of the appropriate Intimacy, but gain a point of willpower in exchange.

    Burning Mortal Emesis (10m, 1 wp; Simple; Keyword: Perilous; One Day; Essence 3): The exigent feels a wave of nausea and fever, abruptly and expels everything living within her, leaving a pile of liquefying bone, flesh, and mercury on the ground. In addition to dematerializing and functioning as a God for all practical purposes, the Exigent suspends any disease and poison within her, and regains [her Essence] in Health Levels. Compliné may sustain this state until the next sunrise, at which point she materializes.

    Second Son’s Ambition (3m; Supplemental; Instant; Essence 2): The Exigent may make Inspire and Instill rolls encouraging someone to better their role and station as though targeting a minor tie of pride in themselves. If the target already possesses such an intimacy, Compliné instead treats it as one step higher than its current level, up to Defining.

    Fallen Crown Finality (5m; Reflexive; One Scene): Compliné designates a target. If she defeats them while this charm is active, she gains three dice to any actions for the rest of the scene which facilitate her leaving the area, be it stealth, breaking down a wall, or intimidating others out of her way. These dice become automatic successes if they are brought to bear upon people who possess an intimacy of devotion or loyalty to her victim. Defeat is usually inflicted martially and often lethally, but a sufficiently caustic incapacitating verbal dressing-down may suffice.
