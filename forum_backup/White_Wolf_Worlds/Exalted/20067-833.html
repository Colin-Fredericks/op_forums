                                                        I feel as though somewhere back there was a premise that Lunars had something that was equivalent to Supernal and people were trying to appeal to you through that, but I think that's the incorrect approach to take. Like, Supernal provides the potential to grow towards Essence 5 Charms from the earliest possible moment, nothing is ever going to be equivalent to that.                                                         The things that Lunars have, the diverse array of applications for a small number of Attributes (and a decent amount of individual Charms within that with versatile applications), the option to appropriate a few additional capabilities as needed through the inexpensive means of shapeshifting, and the occasional Lunar Charm that goes places that the themes of Solars don't really touch such as tormenting people with corrosive madness or sprouting clones of yourself with varying levels of autonomy, these things do not ultimately add up to something that is equivalent in strength to Solars (although it's still pretty close as the Exalted go).

                                                        But they do create scenarios and characterizations and methods of approach that don't emerge so much from the gameplay of Solars, and I think that ought to be seen for its own gratifications, and yeah a not infrequent variety of gameplay styles within the practical constraints of "we only have so many players to encompass so much of the Solar range" and "we don't have infinite time to suss out all facets of overcoming the challenge presented to us by the Storyteller but the Lunar capabilities cut through them quite efficiently" and even just into "the narratives that can be created out of the Lunar contributions are the most compelling at this moment".

                                                        As for the bond between the Solars and Lunars, well, I want to say that is something with a narrative force that is not dependent on them being direct mirrors to one another in terms of power...

                                                        But it's a bit more complicated than that because Lunars now get a number of Charms that have expanded capabilities in relation to their Solar mates. I don't think there's a firm answer to the question of whether or not those capabilities are equivalent in strength to what the Solars do (they probably aren't), but they're another thing that means that the Solar and Lunar working in tandem are capable of achieving desirable things that neither can do on their own.

                                                        It's a complicated thing that is hard to summarize, it really requires somebody to actually have the full range of the Charms in front of them.

                                                            Originally posted by Ranger_Lord
                                                            None of that is enough to make up for the difference in population and resources. None of that is even close to enough. You are talking out your ass […]
                                                            the realities of the real world pre-medieval socioeconomic issues they're inspired by


                                                            I was honestly worried that the conversation was going to lean towards the disparities that exist in the modern day, because sure it's drastically difficult to overcome when one side possesses the Maxim machine gun (and motor vehicles and pressurized, refrigerated storage) and the other does not. But pre-Medieval? When everybody is still dependent on the bow and arrow and spear for weaponry, the horse for transportation and communication, and only as much fresh water and dry tack food as an animal can carry?

                                                            Large systems of interconnected resources can convey advantages, but they can also have a drastic weakness in the form of dependence on those systems, which can experience cascading failures if something goes wrong at one of their vulnerable points. Crops build large populations, but crops that fail or are burned leave you with a sizable population that does not know how to forage for its own food. Large armies that have to chase people into territory where they're dependent on lengthy supply lines and don't quite know the lay of the land while the indiginous people can live effectively off the fauna and flora, know all the good spots to make approaches and fall back into and are more resistent to things like the heat stroke and malaria, well, the army has a more difficult time acting as an enforcer there.

                                                            The ancient Romans frequently failed to subdue Germanic peoples. Imperial China had to stretch its borders really far out of their way to acquire the kinds of horses that would let them compete in even terms with northern steppe peoples, and still frequently experienced defeats to them or were outright conquered themselves.

                                                            Heck, look at the Bronze Age Collapse. Even if we presume that the Sea Peoples were about as urbanised and agrarian as the inhabitants of the Levant whom they overwhelmed, the latter were in a state of severe weakness because the interconnected series of resources they were dependent on had buckled under their own weight. As did the walls of their cities when some bad earthquakes were happening, sooo yeah that's another advantage to living in a yurt, it doesn't smush you when the ground starts shaking a lot and is less capable of turning into a firestorm when somebody doesn't tend to their hearth properly.

                                                            The realities of those socioeconomic issues as they existed several centuries ago where a lot more diverse and evenly distributed. I can't even say that the disadvanated people still had victories, because can we really confidently state which side of that was disadvantaged?

                                                            The magic side of that isn't "brute forces a thing into working where it otherwise wouldn't", it just gives precision and direction to capitalising on advantages and exploiting weaknesses. First/Second Edition Ma-Ha-Suchi who cares about fighting civilization isn't coming from a place of "raw power lets me make this thing work regardless", he's fighting on an arena of "I can point the malaria in the direction of the encamped army and give it some motivation" and being able to sing to a swarm of locusts that sends it flying in the direction of the crops that his enemies are so dependent on. In real life, stuff like that happened and shifted the advantage drastically one way, it just couldn't be done deliberately.
