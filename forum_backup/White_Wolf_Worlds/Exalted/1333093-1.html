        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
        That seems intentional. Demons have not been portrayed in a sympathetic light this edition. From the personalities showcased in the Corebook, to summoning, to dev commentary about slavery -- demons and their Yozi mistresses are no longer recognised as the former masters of the world but are instead &quot;monsters&quot;, &quot;baleful spirits&quot;, and &quot;dread fiends&quot;.
        I don't really see it, and would find it to be a misstep if that was the case.

        I find that they can still be dead fiends while still having a diverse range of personalities. I'm still all for Makarios dealing almost solely in dreams in a manner that causes severe neurological harm. Just that he's basically friendly about it, and that for him it's all about the commerce.

        I don't think their status as former matters of the world enters into it.

        Originally posted by JohnDoe244
        3E has done away with a lot of moral complexity and challenging issues.
        With the Lunar Dominions?

        Originally posted by JohnDoe244
        And if you're going to vilipend The Realm, you can't exactly lionize demons.
        The setting of Exalted is one in which Hell has basically no association with human evil.

        That said, I don't want to lionised Makarios; it's still basically about him trading in souls.

        Originally posted by JohnDoe244
        If you're going to make mortals matter and have Creation as the setting, you don't want the PCs rushing to dream worlds to cavort with demons.
        Oh sure you do; demons are an excellent source of magical weapons and the like that are very useful for evening odds or coming at enemy good or Exalted sideways.

        Makarios' best wares should not come cheap. You want that deadly automaton soldier, that's going to cost you the dreams of a couple thousand for about a year. That's enough to keep these things from flooding the setting.

        The agency still lies with the mortal princes because all Makarios wants it of it is to get paid, and maybe have some return business. What you do with your purchase is entirely in you.

        Lunar Dominions are good for presenting that principle as well.

        Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
        Possibly it’s a word count issue? Because the bits describing him and how he operates seem kinda short, especially as compared to many of the other entries. Maybe cutting or condensing one of his charms would allow for going more in depth into why he does things?

        Alternately, maybe “using dreams as construction material” is a Raksha thing that they don’t want to explicitly give him?
        Word count I can see. I wouldn't like it, but I could accept it, albeit with reservations. If writing mechanics means that there's little space to establish personality or a surrounding tone, that's a problem; it was a real weight on the necks of several First Age Exalted back when they needed those really stupidly written gigantic Charm lists.

        I wouldn't accept differentiating from Fair Folk. There should be enough to distinguish them to keep that kind of approach from being proprietary.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
        And I can see the point that Octavian, whether he's more &quot;evil&quot; than Makarios, is someone you're going to either enslave or punch. You're not really going to be going to dinner to discuss business dealings with him.
        Not with that attitude you're not. Guy has Intimacies, he can be socially influenced. Offer him something that will expand his empire. Appeal to his desire to test his strength against the world. Maybe some food that doesn't taste like his ass.

        When I say he's more antagonistic, I'm thinking about how his aspirations on the whole are focused on conquest and combat. On an individual basis, that can still be engaged with in a number of ways, albeit probably ones that end with somebody bleeding.

        Somebody is likely to suffer out of engaging with Makarios as well, but that's not the point for him.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
        But saying this, I have to disagree with Isator too; I don't think Sigereth or Alveua are really that antagonistic, tbh. Sigereth plays games (scrupulously fairly) and lets you bet stuff. Alveua makes stuff, loves insects, and turns willing souls into items. But if you run into her in a teahouse, you're not immediately going to start a kung-fu fight with her, the way you would with Octavian.
        To be honest, I had forgotten Alveua was in there. That said, I've always seen her as a bit... heartless. That whole attitude of reshaping gods and mortals into shapes that are obedient, and equating this with happiness.

        I could make an argument about Sigereth, but right now it feels a little bit obtuse. The main thing I'll say is that Makarios is more transactional; you pay a fair price (in a horror story currency) and get something in exchange.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
        Mara... is a bit more complex.
        Her defining principle is that she takes pleasure in death and suffering.

        There are other things against her, but I feel as though those are kind of gilding the lily when I start at the point of her defining principle bring taking pleasure in death and suffering.

        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
        On a different topic, has anyone else noticed that of the 5 published Second Circles, 3 are from the Ebon Dragon? And the Third-Circle they said they've written (but it's waiting to be published) is also of the Ebon Dragon.
        I don't think that has anything to do with the Ebon Dragon, just those three happen to be popular demons.

        Mara was chosen by a backer.

                                            Originally posted by Synapse View Post
                                            For all that talk about octavian having intimacies, you are awfully unwilling to see people that are willing to sell mortal souls would sit down and talk to the guy in a dream.
                                            That alone conveys a bias that I'm distressed to be associated with the demon. When Makarios has a persona and assistance that conveys the impression that he's trustworthy and sincere, it more readily cultivates narratives in which people will opt to pay in their own dreams.

                                            The elements together mean that I've always liked the idea that in transactions with Makarios, the human is the one who's going to be the asshole; the one who'll opt to make another pay on their behalf, or even try to cheat out of paying at all. Makarios is ever forthright.

                                            They're facets that broaden the range of interactions with him, the kinds of people that might feel comfortable engaging with him.

                                            That and I just like the demons to cover a variety. There's already too much of a bias towards presuming the wicked and the sinister, some effort needs to be exerted to keep focus on the actual interesting Hell that we've got.

                                            Otherwise you get stuff like people presuming that neomah are definitely going to make evil scary babies, or writing Demon-Blooded as needing to recover Essence in "places of sin".

                                            This would be why I would object if it turned out that the intent is that the Exalted aren't even capable of paying in their dreams. You take away the option to sacrifice oneself, it makes actions harming others a bit more forced and less meaningful. Conversely, if they can make the payments but suffer no deleterious effects, them they're just given an easy out from being morally compromised.

                                            You can engage with Octavian in a variety of ways because there's a social system, but he's still always the asshole. You might be as well, but he definitely is.

                                            Originally posted by Synapse
                                            It's just a punkier appearance. There are plenty lunars and dynasts I'd hesitate longer to sit down and talk.
                                            You know I'm fine with even a fictional human being more than their appearance, but I find that spirits are a bit more archetypal, wear their symbolism on their sleeves, and that it would be especially helpful for conveying this game's novel take on the meaning of the term demon.
