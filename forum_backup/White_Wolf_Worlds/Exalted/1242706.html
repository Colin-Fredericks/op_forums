    Mining and the Threshold

    08-04-2018, 10:10 AM
    So, this has always bugged me about the setting. Hopefully someone can point out something obvious about why it's not an issue.

    At various points in the source books, there are references to mining in the Threshold. But why wasn't it mined out in the First Age, or even earlier? Human civilisation has been ascendant for thousands of years. Did no-one use metal until the Age of Sorrows? What's left of the Threshold used to be the innermost part of Creation before the Fair Folk invasion. Even if we assume that, for some reason, the Solars exclusively mined in Wyld-Shaped areas, why didn't the Shogunate or later civilisations use it?

    Maybe there's an off-hand reference to the Bureau of Nature periodically renewing the ores of Creation somewhere, but if so I've missed it.

          Originally posted by Katamari_Naneke View Post
          But why wasn't it mined out in the First Age, or even earlier?
          The First Age wasn't a period of industrialization in the manner of our modern Earth.

          ​Don't get me wrong, there are probably thousands of places littered around the setting in which the resources from below were exhausted centuries or millennia ago, but the world represented by Creation is plenty big enough for there to still be lots of untapped potential.

          Originally posted by Katamari_Naneke View Post
          Did no-one use metal until the Age of Sorrows?
          If you've been reading the book enough to catch the references to mines, you should also have seen that they used metals in the First Age; the write-ups for artifacts are pretty explicit about what they're made of.

          ​That said, the First Age probably didn't have as great a demand for miners as you suspect; pacts with gods, earth elementals, and various entities from the Wyld could probably supply a not-insignificant bit of the metal needed for the miracles of the First Age without someone having to so much as swing a pickaxe. And while I'm sure metal was mined, the trade networks enabled didn't necessitate that such mines be absolutely everywhere.

          Originally posted by Katamari_Naneke View Post
          why didn't the Shogunate or later civilisations use it?
          I know these days a bored billionaire can throw money to hire a dozen guys to pretty much erase a mountain to get at the shiny stuff under it, but historically, that sort of effort used to take a lot longer, with vastly more manpower and a much higher relative cost.

                                                      ...
                                                      You reacted to someone saying "we're still mining on Earth" by saying "for how much longer?"

                                                      What I'm saying is that depletion of mineral resources is not a current concern, even after all our extraction activity. We have centuries (if not millenia, once it becomes cost effective to prospect and mine the ocean floors) ahead of us on that matter. I would consider our civilization to have achieved a bittersweet victory if we can steer this ship long enough to experience depletion of our crust.

                                                      There is no reason to assume that the First Age was as mining provided resources hungry as our current society. In the real world there is one way to go for a nation to grow, an that way goes through industrialization. Every single nation needs cars, trains, airplanes, ships, war machines, infrastructure...

                                                      Creation's First Age had many kingdoms on many different paths.

                                                      Magic allows things to be diverse, there could be whole nations that were considered militarily mighty without having mined an ounce of iron or smelted a steel bar.

                                                      There could be cities full of glass towers that don't require a steel supporting structure.

                                                      Continent sized landmasses where millions of people lived, being fed by magical fields (instead of modern agriculture, with it's trucks, tractors, harvesters, factories), who got around on baskets carried by giant turkeys (instead of cars, trains, airplanes and ships) who lived in ivory towers raised by sorcery (instead of modern housing and all it's concerns for engeneering and materials).

                                                      So even if there were (and there were) mighty civilizations in which billions of people existed, it is just unimaginative and nitpicky to assume they all developed the same way and went after the same things (like in the real world), and so those things must have been exhausted by now. Like I said before, its magic, not reality.

                                                      And that is besides the magical replenishment other people mentioned, which I don't think is necessary at all to be honest. Nothing I have read in the setting indicates mineral resources exhaustion to be a thing, or that it not being a thing somehow lacks verissimilitude.

                                                      MIneral resources are plenty. It's abundancy or scarcity should only be a concern if it adds to the story being told.
