        Originally posted by Shinjo View Post
        I would argue the Great Curse isn't a thing one should be interacting with in-setting; the parable of the Great Curse is hidden from the actors on Creation's stage because it exists to inform us, the players/storytellers/audience, of the essential tragedy of the Age of Sorrows. Later Exalted books added material that support the "alien transperfect UMI effect that makes you a jerk" read of the Curse, unfortunately, so I can see why there's a visceral reaction to it from the fandom where curing it becomes a priority. A lot of the bad fluff around the Great Curse needs to be stripped out to go back to its roots.
        I agree with this 100%. I feel 3e should word the 'win condition' of Exalted not in defeating the Great Curse, but in succeeding DESPITE the Great Curse. Much like fear and hatred and all other enemies a Solar will encounter, it is to be surpassed, but it cannot be vanquished.

        If they word it well enough, it might change people's minds about it, as the one thing to make it all come to ruin. It should be presented with less subterfuge, as what makes it epic fantasy, and how artificial it might seem if it is removed, even though soul-surgery is how many people will still end their game; their table, their business, and it IS a valid way.

        Originally posted by maelstromseeker View Post
        Having no Great Curse and instead just people going crazy because they have power would make the setting too hopeless. The return of the Solars at the end of the Second Age might doom the world to endless cycles of rebellion and corruption, but it might actually save Creation instead. Just now someone pointed out that the return of the Solars is meant to bring hope. If the Great Curse is not external, then there is no hope for Creation to have a better future.
        Of course there is hope! The same hope there is for mankind. There are several theological and philsophical treatises in the real world that say there is no hope for mankind: that we will bring about our end in folly, being inherently flawed. Some believe the opposite. Exalted is a good stage to play these ideologies, and the one that has more clout on your table ought to win.

        There is a very good anime called Gurren Lagann; at its core it is basically a fight between Sidereals and Solars. At some point the Sidereals tell the Solars they may bring ruin to everything again, so why would they keep fighting? And the answer is basically: because we believe we won't. The choice is not to stop being human, to give up everything, but to keep going the way they are, knowing the danger they represent, and choosing not to end the world, one day at a time. That, to me, is how a good Second Age Solar should behave.

                I want to defend the Great Curse as something people won't notice (as I said, in my group we noticed Solars are crazy, but just assume this is because they can't handle their power), but in the interests of logical debate...

                One point people have made is that people will often internalise things. That if you projected alien thoughts into their heads, they'd rationalise it.

                But...
                After the Tutsi massacre in Rwanda, many Hutu militia members ran away to the neighbouring countries, such as the DRC. Sociologists interviewing them about what they'd done found that a lot of people claimed to have been possessed. They couldn't accept the idea that they'd murdered and raped civilians, it didn't fit their own view of themselves, so they blamed Satan and evil spirits. They claimed (and seemed to genuinely believe) that they'd been possessed by dark forces which had influenced or forced them to do bad things, which they'd never do normally, because in their minds, they're good people. So it's not their fault, it's some external force that has made them do it.

                So maybe, for a Solar who has flipped out and done bad stuff, they might actually go "huh, I must have been mind-controlled by someone", especially as that is something that most people know is entirely possible in Exalted.
                So them going "I went mad and killed people, an external force from some dark power, like the Neverborn or Yozis, must have made me do it, because that's not like me," perhaps isn't that strange after all.

                            At the OP, to me, the Solars represent the will to overcome and remake the world, to make it what you want, not what it ought to be or is, and try to make it better or grander, even if that isn't very wise.

                            The Orichalcum Caste I see as representing the essence of what the Solars are about. They're a "naive", "innocent"/uncynical, bright, self assured power of their own genius and excellence alone. Their essence (like the Orichalcum's resonating attributes) is one is primarily one of power, not precision or endurance. Their essence is one of being unconquered and they confront being conquered by their own fallibility and limits by limit breaking* and heroic madness (like Achilles in the Iliad or the Unconquered Sun under a virtue conflict).

                            The other Exalted represent, to a degree, dealing with limits of the self through interdependence (Dragon-blooded / Jade), adaptability and fluidity (Lunars / Moonsilver), assimilating reality (Sidereals / Starmetal) and living through failure and death (Abyssals / Soulsteel), with associated vices.

                            The Solars being strongest, I expect, is a conceit of the narrative that their essence of power and determination is nonetheless the first and foremost, despite its limits.

                            *of course limit breaks are due to the Great Curse, but this is why the Solars' Great Curse is thematic to them.
