    This is a tree that really, really wants you to be faster than your enemy; if you can’t regularly sustain that, you’re probably in for a bad time. A lot of its powers are more effective if the enemy is Crashed, though, so if you can get that upper hand, you’re going to be piling on a lot of hurt.
    I'm saw you note the Gambit thing a few times. Gambits are Decisive, flat out, but do not reset like Decisive attacks. Which charms are unclear?

    As for the attack use, in many places in the book they require you to notice the use of a word such as 'reflexive' in the main body, or 'use this after an attack', to state charm order or whether it consumes a combat action. It requires close reading, which many people may not immediately realise.

                                                            White Reaper Style

                                                            Essence to Master: 3
                                                            Armor: Any.
                                                            Weapons: Scythes, spears, staves, unarmed attacks.

                                                            Shtick: Kill battle groups dead. Charge up from defeating one enemy to face another; expend that charge for temporary bonuses. Layer defensive buffs based on the “halos” you’ve gained from clobbering battle groups.

                                                            Charms:
                                                            1. Add bonus damage based on Strength to a Withering or Decisive attack.
                                                            2. Your Onslaught penalties turn into bonuses vs. one attack.
                                                            3. Form. Double-10s on Decisive damage. -1 Defense to all battle groups. Move through battle groups. Collect halos that grant +1 Resolve each for crashing or killing notable enemies, or for damaging battle groups.
                                                            4. Limited double-9s (or double-8s, vs. battle groups) on an attack roll. Expend halos for +1 die each.
                                                            5. Extra Withering or Decisive damage. Vs. a battle group, ignore soak from Size or add extra damage for high Initiative. Burn halos for post-soak Withering damage.
                                                            6. Make two Decisive attacks, or two Withering attacks on a battle group. Burn halos for +1 attack each.
                                                            7. Add (halos) to Parry, or burn them for double that bonus.
                                                            8. Add (2 * [halos + 1]) to soak for the scene, or more if you aren’t wearing armor. Battle groups don’t add Size to damage.
                                                            9. Burn halos to heal damage. For the scene, reduce wound penalties by (halos), increase difficulty of rout checks (I bet!), and expend halos for Initiative.

                                                            Revolving Crescent Defense (#2) is pretty great – especially with the Mastery rider that makes it turnlong. The Initiative cost stings a little, but in some fairly reasonable circumstances, that’s about as cheap a full Excellency as you’re ever going to get – woe betide the guy who mega-flurries you.

                                                            White Reaper Form (#3) is just always -1 to the BG’s Defense, right? Like, it has this calculation based on their current Drill, but practically, it’s just always -1 Defense.

                                                            Bleeding Crescent Strike (#4) is at least the second MA Charm to say something like “double-9s (but only to a maximum of Stat dice).” I wonder if that’s a standard thing for non-Solar Exalts.

                                                            How do “automatic successes” on damage interact with soak, anyway?

                                                            That is a White Reaper Lunar just absolutely murdering a bunch of dudes. Every martial art so far has gotten its own illustration, which I appreciate.

                                                            It’s kind of odd that so many White Reaper Charms require burning halos – it seems like that makes it hard to use the style at all while amassing halos. Enemies Like Grass (#6) is probably the exception: burn five halos to make seven Withering attacks on a battle group, and immediately recover all halos. Also, y’know, probably kill the battle group. (This is an expensive power, but it’s also about the only one I’ve seen that allows you to flurry Withering attacks – almost all big Solar multiattacks are Decisive-only.)

                                                            Oof, Flickering Corona Barrier (#7) is potent. 2m for, potentially, a full defensive Excellency, not consuming your halos, that a Solar can activate after the attack is rolled. That’s just nasty – if you can use it at all, of course; absent a handy BG, you’re going to have a much harder time building it up.

                                                            The styles seem to favor these permanent form-enhancers, which I absolutely approve of – it’s cool to have your existing Charms become more awesome over time.

                                                            Standing by my initial impressions, here. When four Dragon-Blooded jump you, this style isn’t going to add much; you’ll only infrequently Crash or kill anyone, and so you’re rarely going to have more than one or two halos to burn – that’s a ho-hum bonus at a not-insignificant cost. Against a battle group, though, you could easily pull a halo every round – or more, with Enemies Like Grass. That’s a big buff – and anyone who doesn’t want Flickering Corona Barrier in a setup like that is crazy. In its niche, this is a pretty horribly lethal style. (Probably still wasn’t Lilith’s best choice against one Sidereal and one god, but apparently that worked out for her.)

                                                            This is probably the most defensive style I’ve seen so far. No general-use mobility buffs or anti-kiting, but we’ve got Charms to help with Onslaught, soak, late-step Defense boosting, healing, and debuffing battle groups. On offense, the style is lighter on solid powers… unless you’re a battle group, of course.


                                                            Ebon Shadow Style

                                                            Essence to Master: 3
                                                            Armor: Unarmored only.
                                                            Weapons: Sais, tiger claws, knives, unarmed attacks.

                                                            Shtick: Kill from the shadows. Make surprise attacks every chance you get, bypassing armor or hitting harder than your enemy expects; your stealth helps mitigate your foe's counterstrike.

                                                            Charms:
                                                            1. Penalize attempts to see you in combat. Fake your own death to escape battle.
                                                            2. Subtract (Stealth), or (2*Stealth) in a surprise attack, from enemy’s armored soak.
                                                            3. Form. Anyone who fails their check when you enter stealth loses 1i. Bonus Initiative when you reset after an unexpected Decisive. +1 Defense.
                                                            4. Enemy rolls a Decisive twice, taking the lower result.
                                                            5. Subtract (Essence + Stealth) from a Decisive aimed at you.
                                                            6. Move in shadows more easily; do extra damage if you use this movement to reach Close range on an enemy.
                                                            7. Extra successes on a Decisive attack roll convert damage dice to successes – without limit, on a surprise attack.
                                                            8. Strangle an enemy with his own shadow, doing extra damage.

                                                            Nothing But Shadows (#1) says it can be used to go to ground by pretending to be dead. I believe that works once… but after your enemy knows the trick, surely this should stop being so effective.

                                                            I assume this is the Ebon Shadow illustration, with the lady choking in the air? No idea what’s happening here. Edit: Ah, it’s the pinnacle Charm in action.

                                                            Ebon Shadow has probably the easiest reflexive form activation: successfully hide. There’s a fun flavor there: you ambush the stylist, he vanishes and reflexively enters the style, and now the shoe is on the other foot.

                                                            So, here’s fun: combine Elusive Flicker Evasion (#4) with an enemy using Thrown’s Shadow Wind Slash. Does he roll four times? If so, does he take the lower of the higher of each pair, or the higher of the lower of each pair?

                                                            Shadow-Body Dissolution (#5) is a weaker, more expensive Adamant Skin Technique – that lets you reflexively enter Stealth, if it works well enough. I’m cool with that trade, for a general-use power.

                                                            I like the flavor that several of these Charms leave no bodies – yours or the enemy’s. That makes for some nice “Can’t prove anything happened” moments. (Although, shouldn’t this help Nothing But Shadows? Faking your death would be a lot more persuasive, and harder to stop, if you were also fading into a shadowy mist.)

                                                            I’m a little bit “eh” on Ebon Shadow overall, but maybe that’s the thematics rather than the actual powers. The style definitely rewards a high investment in Stealth, and it plays well with the stuff Solar Stealth already does rather than just being redundant – but I’m not sure it adds enough oomph that I’d want this for my main offense. Maybe I’m undervaluing it – the defensive component is quite solid, and there are a couple of nasty offensive tricks in here, if nothing with the sheer killing power of Single Point or Tiger.
