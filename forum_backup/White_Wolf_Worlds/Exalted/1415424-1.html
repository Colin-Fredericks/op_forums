Who can say how the ravaging of time works in the flowing seas of unreality? Severely weakened by the ordeal, he washes up on shore in the Age of Sorrows, his divine flame weakened but not extinguished. He sets off across the land, hungry for information and desperate for allies. His greatest journey lies ahead of him still...

            I'm thinking like an intro fiction something like this...


            There are dark places of the world, the deep crevasses that even the gods do not know. They do not know because they were never meant to be. When the Titans first molded the earth and the seas and pressed them together, these were the forgotten places. These were the little folds between places that formed then. It is in one of the deepest, darkest of these folds that three figures now labor under the light of three flickering lanterns.

            "Are you certain this is the door?" said one hunched over the sixteenth, and final lock.

            The tall one standing up replied flatly "Are you kidding? This location was from the third-hand account of somebody who said they read the journal of the architect."

            The first replied without looking up "Okay, so that's not giving me a lot of confidence that I'm not about to unleash a horrible demon."

            The scholar nodded, seeming to be undisturbed. "I am 100% sure that the builders of this place thought it was a demon, and 70% sure it is the one we seek."

            The thief's breath caught in her throat as, unexpectedly soon, the lock hitched and turned, sliding open. For a few seconds that seemed to last an eternity all was still in this place of forgotten dark, even the lanterns grew still, and dim. Then when no great horror burst forth, the third figure stood and reached forward, grasping the enormous jade handles and pulling hard the doors swung open. Even in this there was an eerie stillness, a whisper of nothing more than dust as the great mass split apart to reveal the sole captive of this prison.

            It was a figure sat cross-legged on the floor with their head bowed, cord-thin chains of soulsteel fastened to shackles on their neck, wrists and ankles. Everywhere, on the floor, the doors, and even covering the figure themselves, was a thick layer of dust. Some of this now trickled off the head of the captive as they turned their head away. Eyes that have seen nought but perfect darkness, and ears that have heard nought but perfect silence for centuries now bombarded with light and sound.

            The trio stood there, stunned. The prisoner then looked up at them with thin, watery eyes, and placed a slim hand on the floor in front of them. Shakily, they clambered into a standing position, the chains making a quiet hiss as they slid across the dust on the ground. Each movement creaked bone and cracked joints that have sat still for decades at least. Having not spoken to another soul for so long, dragging thoughts into speech was like walking through molasses. The words came out slow and awkward, but deliberate.

            "How long?"

                    Right. But the unjustly imprisoned guy (Oldboy or The Bride or The Count of Monte Cristo or the Man in the Iron Mask or The Beast from Kung Fu Hustle) is basically just a montage and a shave away from kicking ass in their chosen field. I'm having trouble coming up with someone (besides an Elder Evil/Sauron sort of thing) that demonstrates what you are looking for. Gollum doesn't lose power from his cave living, but from losing the Ring, which is actually what has the Power. Do you have a literary or movie example? In most of the examples I can think of, they either come back with Incredible Motivation as basically a Randian Superpower, or having basically not lost a step, or only minimally so, before they suddenly regain their secret Technique. Very rarely is imprisonment, particularly of a hero, actually emasculating. (Maiming is a little more common). And it's particularly rare to lose a secret Technique, or ancient wisdom. If anything, they usually gain from their experience, in either wisdom or motivation.

                    Like, I'm really not trying to Bah Humbug your stuff. I adore that intro. It's great for that flavor. But even if physically crippled, and unable to perform the Secret Technique (unlikely for the Exalted) that's the sort of Wizened Master who has the knowledge and the power to teach it to the young hero. And generally, that sort of wisdom and mastery is represented in Exalted by higher essence, and the charms it unlocks. I have zero problem with the PCs unearthing an ancient crippled Exalt. That's awesome. I'm just having difficulty seeing how that's supposed to damage his transcendental understanding of Creation. Stymie further development? Sure. And maybe while he was trapped they stole his power with evil sorcery? Fantastic. I just don't think the setting represents it being something that can be lost because you ... were trapped . You don't lose essence when you abandon the strictures of the Immaculate Coils. You don't lose essence when you completely restructure your belief system. You don't lose essence when you alter your world-view for Sorcery or Necromancy. And the older NPCs we see, even ones who are stated to be declining? Don't have low essence scores.

                    Even the meanest of the Exalted is capable of overcoming the ancient and unkillable enemies of the gods. I don't see imprisonment, even in the heart of Malfeas as being enough to forget that. They can be killed. They can choose to surrender to corruption. They can be defeated or crippled. And they can be the victim of greater powers, sorcery and shaping. But I don't think they can be bored or bound to lesser their mastery of their self and Creation. But that's my take on the feel and the setting.

Not to drag too much real world religion into things, but similar to how many versions of Christianity view Jesus as being both fully human AND fully divine. The Shard, the Divine Essence is perfected and sublime. The person, the host is fully mortal and fallible. An Exalt is both. But especially in 3E, where there are no mortals with command of their essence, that part of it is more of the divine and perfected portion, in my view of the setting.

                                                Edit: To be clear, I do think an Exalt could go crazy from isolation, or lose muscle or have skills go rusty from disuse (though I'd totally allow a PC to Uncle Iroh stunt their way past that, and potentially even use imprisonment as training time). I think they're generally more resilient to such things than mortals, but that works for me. But I view an increased permanent essence as more transcendental, and probably immune to such vagaries, because command of essence is Not mortal. I think it takes a potent spiritual attack directly on that to damage it.
