    One of the books -- probably Invisible Fortress, from what TheCountAlucard just said, but I'd have to go check -- had a Solar Craft Charm called World Within a Picture Style that let the user create images people could enter. You could paint a room full of comfortable chairs, then everyone steps into the painting and sits down.

    (IIRC, though, if the picture was destroyed then anyone inside it just fucking died. I thought that was a little ridiculous -- it makes a certain degree of sense, but someone shouldn't be able to kill an Exalt with no resistance by sneaking up and burning a painting.)

    Paintings that people can enter are like the opposite of what the OP asked for, but it was certainly cool (and the same character might learn to do both).

                    Originally posted by johnsonhavoc View Post
                    Ooo that is pretty cool. Does it translate well from 1e to 3e?
                    Nothing about the function of the Charm is edition-specific.

                    So here we go:

                    World Within a Picture Style
                    Cost: 10m, 2 gxp; Mins: Craft 5, Occult 3, Essence 3
                    Type: Simple
                    Keywords: None
                    Duration: Instant
                    Prerequisite Charms: Flawless Handiwork Method

                    A Solar who has truly mastered some form of representational art -- most commonly painting, but tapestry-weaving, carving, and metal-casting are all possible -- can create an image so lifelike that it may as well be real. The physical matrix of the picture need not be "life size" -- a metal brooch engraved with an incredibly realistic miniature scene would still work.

                    Normally, the World Within a Picture cannot exceed one acre in size, although it may appear much larger. (A person who tries to step out of the simulated area either steps out into the real world, or goes in a circle.) The image may contain plants, animals, or even people, but the people are not real -- they react only in simple, stereotyped ways, like automata. Adding realistic sapient beings to the World Within a Picture would require a sorcerous working; trapping an actual sapient being within the still life as a permanent resident might be possible with a spell of the Celestial Circle. All manner of sorcerous workings are possible, if the sorcerer wishes to add strange effects to the environment inside, such as an image which allows visitors to fly or transforms them into different forms.

                    Entering a World Within a Picture costs 1 mote; leaving is free. One person may spend additional motes on the behalf of others to allow them entrance. Damaging the picture causes visible cosmetic damage to the world within; if the picture is actually destroyed, any real people inside are ejected into the real world, each suffering one unsoakable level of lethal damage from having the world destroyed around them (a small price to pay, all things considered).

                    Creating a World Within a Picture is a major project, and takes as long as the Storyteller judges it would normally take to make a painting or tapestry or whatever. Once created, it lasts until destroyed.

                    An Essence 4+ repurchase allows the Solar to spend 10m. 2wxp in place of the usual cost, in exchange for increasing the size limit from one acre to (Essence) square miles.

                    A few notes:
                    - In the original version, if the picture gets damaged anyone inside is trapped until it's repaired, and if the picture is destroyed they're all just dead, consigned to Oblivion. I really didn't like that so I changed it, but figure out what works for you and your group. (Personally, I think that if you want to create a painting-world that acts as a trap and makes it easy for you to imprison or kill anyone who enters, you should need a sorcerous working, or possibly one or more additional Charms.)
                    - The original version cost 20 motes. I figured that a cost in craft XP made sense in 3e. I didn't want to make it require white XP (requiring characters to make actual artifacts first seems unfair -- if major projects are enough to prepare you to make a daiklave, they're enough to prepare you to make a World Within a Picture), and then I hit on the idea of using white XP to expand on the one-acre limit.
                    - Flawless Handiwork Method is the same prerequisite it had in 1e. I suspect if it showed up in a 3e product it would have a much longer prerequisite chain, but I couldn't decide on anything appropriate so whatever. I did consider adding Terrestrial Circle Sorcery to the prerequisites, since it already required Occult 3, but eh. A Solar doesn't need to be a sorcerer to master painting to a supernatural degree.
                    - The original version had a listed duration of "one day or more." I assume that's a mistake, because it would require committed Essence then.

                                        If we're thinking of spells flavored as coming from paintings then the obvious thing for them to do to gain sorcerous motes is, well, painting. That sounds like a slow process done in advance, which reminds me of this shaping ritual from the core book (and WFHW):
                                        The sorcerer has been tutored in the arcane arts at the Heptagram or a similar sorcerous academy. Once per story, she may perform sorcerous research and then roll (Intelligence + Lore), gaining sorcerous motes equal to the successes that last for that story’s duration. Access to a library or a mentor adds one to two bonus dice to this roll.
                                        I would modify it as follows:

                                        The sorcerer's spells are paintings that come to life. Once per story, she may create a new painting and then roll (Intelligence + Craft (Painting)), gaining sorcerous motes equal to the successes that last for that story’s duration. Access to a workshop with quality tools, or other support, adds one to two bonus dice to this roll.


                                        Or you could go with this, modified from one of the What Fire Has Wrought ones:

                                        The sorcerer's spells are paintings that come to life. Once per scene, when she stunts the first shape sorcery roll of a spell by describing the painting that is coming to life, she gains (stunt rating + 2) sorcerous motes toward completing the spell. Enhancing her control spell doesn't count towards this once-a-scene limit.

                                                With the help of everyone, I made this! What do you all think?

                                                Creation Painting Initiation

                                                “Every artist dips his brush in his own soul, and paints his own nature into his pictures.”

                                                The art of the person is the true window into the universe. The sorcerer has tapped into the raw pallet of the world through their painting. The Wyld nature and chaos that lives just beyond creation revealing the magic within and guiding them to inspiring creation with their imagination.

                                                Shaping Rituals
                                                • The sorcerer inspires creation by tapping into the Wyld and giving it form on canvas. Once per story, she may create a new painting and then roll (Intelligence + Craft (Painting)), gaining sorcerous motes equal to the successes that last for that story’s duration. Access to a workshop with quality tools, or other support, adds one to two bonus dice to this roll.
                                                • The sorcerer's spells are paintings that come to life. Once per scene, when she stunts the first shape sorcery roll of a spell by describing the painting that they created that is coming to life, she gains (stunt rating + 2) sorcerous motes toward completing the spell. Enhancing her control spell doesn't count towards this once-a-scene limit.
                                                • The sorcerer may feed on inspired energies of people, shaping the passions of others through spellcraft. Once per scene, when another character forms, strengthens, or weakens an Intimacy with their art, the sorcerer may feed from it, gaining sorcerous motes equal to the intensity of the Intimacy (its new rating if it was strengthened, or the old one if weakened). These motes last for the duration of the story and can be spent towards any spell she casts.
                                                Other Benefits:

                                                Paint to Canvas Method (Merit •): Sorcerors of the Creation Painting Initiation frequently make use of their trade tools such as painting their desired effect onto a canvas, a wall, or even the ground. A sorcerer who makes use of their trade tools while taking Shape Sorcery actions adds +1 die to their pool.

                                                The Painted Lotus (Merit •••): The sorcerer's understanding of the Wyld nature of creation has gifted them the ability to alter the form a spell takes when released in creation. Their imagination alters the spell’s look, color, and size. The sorcerers imagine must still play within the rules of creation cannot work against the theme of a spell. A summoned demon cannot be made to look less of a demon, a water elemental must still be made of water, etc.

                                                Master of Creativity (Merit ••••): The sorcerer is immune to the twisting power of the Wyld, and need never fear mutation, delusion, or addiction from time spent in the chaos that inspires them. However, her Wyld affinity makes her vulnerable to iron, causing her to take aggravated damage from weapons made of it as though she were one of the Raksha.
