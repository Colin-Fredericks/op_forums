    Can abyssals revel in their dark fates? (lots of explatives)

    05-24-2016, 12:57 AM
    So while at work today I randomly dreamed up an abyssal loyalist. Now i know a lot of the schtick with them is bemoaning their dark fates and trying to cope with their mistakes but this character just didn't work out that way. I wound up typing out the story of his exaltation to a friend in character and It went a little something like this.


    "The Sacrament Purified in Rage. That's what they call me now. Name used to be Wan Ping. Just don't use it. bad things happen when you do. Anyway my story starts off pretty normal. I was a merc. a firewand for hire out of the south. one day my outfit put down in some little shit hole out the ass end of An-Teng for a bit. I go to the bar hoping to get shit faced and or laid. Was having a damn good time the wench serving my wine had a great ass. so when I go to get a more 'hands on' appreciation for it some guy gets all pissy. 'show some respect' he whines. 'that's a lady' he goes on to bitch and blah blah blah. so I do the only reasonable thing. I punch him right in his bitch nose. apparently the owner doesn't like that shit so he goes to throw me out. now I'm a reasonable man for the most part but this shit was just disrespectful. and where I come from you don't take that shit just lying down so I did the right thing. I turned my firewand on the liquor cabinet and lit the place up like its mid-calibration night and boarded up the dive behind me. man you should have heard them all screaming and cryin like bitches. it was fuckin hilarious. gives me a bit of a chub just remembering it. anyway while I'm there appreciatin a good blaze the towns people snatch me up and drag me off screaming shit like 'murderer' and 'monster' and other shit like that. they strap me to this fucking stick and set the shit on fire. fucking fire man! gods the irony is fuckin palpable. anyway so while my ass is up on that stick getting extra crispy like things go all funny. like the fuckers all freeze and theres this asshole in some overcompensating looking armor standing there. don't really remember what all he said but basically he told me I could go on fighting and killing till the whole world was dead and all I had to do was sign up with him. fuck yeah I took that deal. next thing I know boom the fire all over me turns black and explodes. burns everyone to bones. man that was beautiful."

    pardon the typos and lack of some grammar. but has anyone ever played a character like this before? a loyalist who absolutly loves their job? how much mileage can you get out of this kind of character?

          Can they? CAN they? CAN THEY!?



          Hi, I'm Foxy. Have we met? I believe all 50 out of 50 Green Sun Princes should be evil as hell, and I'm here to tell you (yes you!) that not only CAN Abyssals revel in their dark fates, the vast majority of them should high-five those fates as they fly screaming into the void. The fates are the ones flying screaming. The Abyssals will save themselves for last. The vast majority of Abyssal Exalted should be thoroughly terrible human beings throwing the power of pure negation and entropy around screaming fuck the world like 2Pac. So if you want to play an Abyssal who actually acts like a goddamn Abyssal, instead of all the Drizzts out there Drizzting up the joint and making it so that the vast majority of Abyssals actually played are tragic self-insert fanfic gothbois. Well then you, monsewer, are a friend of mine!

          To answer your question in all seriousness, your character concept is wonderful and you can get tons of mileage out of it... depending on your GM and group. An effective evil character ain't roleplaying 101, son. You have to actually play the guy honestly without ruing everyone else's funtimes, which is easy to do if you're surrounded by do-gooders. At some point, they won't be able to play their characters honestly without Macho Elbowing you, or dying trying. The storyteller must also want you to blackguard up the joint and have ways for you to do that without triggering a campaign-crushing apocalypse.

          You could save such a character for an evil campaign, but most players think evil campaigns are a chance to play Greg Costikyan's Violence and just vent their spleens and weird everyone out murderaping everything. The right evil campaign can be a great chance to exorcise some of your demons and explore the darker side of humanity, or try to pull off the Kolvoord Starburst of roleplaying feats: the villain protagonist.

          So, short answer: You got a group you trust? Go for it, but respect everyone's enjoyment and you'll have a bonzer time.

          I ran a solo campaign for my friend starring the Fallen Tyrant of the Frozen Throne, our loving homage to Arthas Menethil. That campaign absofuckinglutely ROCKED. He slew his dad and hungry ghosts poured from the wound, devouring the courtiers. That was fun to describe in detail. He had to pull together an Abyssal circle and supplant Walking on Laughter, who rode in a bonestrider that rode on a giant undead "horse" like some horrific giant knight errant. His Day Caste buddy was pretty much just a serial killer. My main Solar campaign will run into the Tyrant very soon. It will rule.

          Evil Abyssals, buddy. *clinks glass* Evil Abyssals.

                  There's room for any kind of character as long as everyone on the table is up for it.

                  Loyalism doesn't mean "I kill and pillage because my dark gods tell me to," just as much as being a Renegade doesn't mean "I will save the living from the dead while mourning my regrets." They're purely labels that hinge on allegiance: are you in your Deathlord's service or not? A subversive Deathknight who works against their Deathlord but proves indispensable to replace is still a Loyalist. A Renegade who kills everyone they meet with a smile serves the Neverborn's purpose, even if it doesn't serve the Deathlords.

                  And then there are paths beyond Loyalist and Renegade. I, for one, would like to see more Abyssals entirely untethered to the Deathlord-Deathknight dynamic who just really care about ghosts.
