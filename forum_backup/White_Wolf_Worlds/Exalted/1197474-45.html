                        ​

                        You don't become the head of a Great House and cultivate the sort of powerbase it has for centuries without being something of an asshole yourself
                        But you also don't often achieve it without managing to cultivate some positive relationships, and that's an element that the new write-up placed emphasis on.

                        Originally posted by Murcushio
                        Anyway. Mnemon hasn't held onto her position by having a secret soft center.
                        I think the mistake here is looking at it as a dichotomy, rather than elements that can complement each other. I mean, it's there in the write-up; to her scions she can offer wisdom and comfort, she's counselled nervous children, lavishes affection on those whom she takes as lovers, and in private is known for generosity, good humour and deep devotion to the national ideology.

                        ​Mind, I would argue that it's a false dichotomy to regard such things as necessarily constituting softness. She doesn't need to be somebody that has a secret soft centre, or any less ruthless, to still be genuinely loving and capable of handing out affection and positive reinforcement. I've known or known of people whose general demeanour was rather harsh even in private, even with people that they liked, and it wasn't an obstacle to being able to engage in positive interpersonal interaction.

                        ​Hmm, note to self; take cues for her sense of humour from Lilith from Frasier.

                        Mnemon is hard as nails and she is absolutely as likely to try murdering her way to the Throne as Ragara is.
                        ​I don't know; from this write-up, I can't see willingness to murder basically everybody, with an allowance to employ some especially underhanded means.

                        ​That being said, I would presume that even if Mnemon's resentment towards V'neef is currently a Minor thing, I would assume that it might be a bit difficult for a scion of her House to convince her to give it up. Not because it matters a lot to her, but because of how she regards her own position in the House; I can see her as being galled by the presumption of a scion who would try influencing her priorities like that, although I'd need to review how that might play out in rules beyond applying that Defining Tie to her Resolve.

                                    Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                                    Semantics. This was published after Exalted; the Abyssals, players and Storytellers knew she was in way over her head dealing with a Deathlord directly and it undeniably shaped how they used her.
                                    Okay? Yes? This is a problem?

                                    Every Storyteller and player who has read the splatbooks and the setting books knows a SHIT TON MORE about the secret, hidden truths of Creation and the true motivations of major setting figures than even very well-informed NPCs. This always shaped how they used those NPCs, as it should have done! That's... not actually a bad thing.

                                    It's a bad plot seed because Storytellers felt bound to it
                                    "Some Storytellers are very bad at their job" isn't a problem the game should design around. "Mnemon has entered into negotiations with a Deathlord over... something" is an extremely vague plot seed that is both easily inconsequential or easily very consequential.

                                    I believe Jenna Moran once said explicitly something along the lines of "Chejop Kejak is meant to be useful as a powerful and effective antagonist, but if you can't figure out a way to do that without breaking your game because you only conceive of things he'll do that will just make your players miserable and the game unfun, don't use him." (I'm paraphrasing.)

                                    and once the Anathema become involved in the Realm civil war it's very easy for it to become all about them.
                                    Anathema are already involved in it in 3E. That's in the cores chapter fiction, in fact, there are Anathema building power bases on the Blessed Isle and totes getting involved.

                                    And what, precisely, is wrong with that? In a Solar game why SHOULDN'T Anathema be involved in the Realm Civil War? One of the most popular kind of Solars are Dynasts, in fact. Heck, even in a Dragon-Blooded game there's both in-setting and out-of-setting justification for Anathema becoming involved, because Solars and Lunars do not stop existing just because the Realm is fighting amongst itself. If you're running a DB game and don't want the Solars and Lunars involved in the Realm meltdown, you can plausibly keep them away, but if you DO want them involved the game should, and it very explicitly does, provide avenues for that.

                                    I also think a secret deal between major NPCs that the players know about because they read a particular source book is inherently shite.
                                    That's literally Chejop Kejak and the Empresses entire relationship; a secret deal between major NPCs that the players know about because they read a particular sourcebook.

                                    In fact that's... that's a TON of Sidereal stuff. And other stuff as well. Sesus Nagezzar's entire "I am secretly a giant super-patriot and am single-handedly stabilizing the threshold" thing is something players only know because they read a particular sourcebook. Secret deals between major NPCs are a big big part of the setting!
