        Somehow I imagine that if an Exalted savant actually came up with a formula for hate, there would be a bunch of people to say "no, that's not hate, it doesn't cover cases X Y Z." Operationalizing amorphous social concepts is a pretty tricky business, and even the metody, who are in a very broad sense "hate elementals", express that concept in a complex and variable way. There's also no particular reason to assume that the set of things which constitute "hate" (itself an amorphous cloud that's impossible to pinpoint in a binary way) occupies a tidy space in terms of energy -- e.g. it could be that if hate has energy, it can have a hugely varied amount of energy without changing substantially as hate.

        Practically speaking, of course, your ability to do these things in the game follows from the desires of the setting developers and GM, because this is miles outside the default play space in the land of totally-made-up technobabble. So there are really no assumptions about it that a player can bring to the table because it's so far afield that the laws of the universe are subservient to narrative need.

                    This thread is strange. When informed that there is a setting where the scientific method is not a useful method of inquiry, it seems uninspired to blankly reject or contradict the premise. It's much more interesting to explore what sort of world that must be, where the most basic system of learning is not reliable. A world without the scientific method is a world without empiricism, and a world without empiricism is a world without laws. Everything is subjective, nothing can be known. Simultaneously, anything can be discovered, nothing is solipsistic. There may exist certain mathematical patterns, but none are strictly enforced. Does the earth concern you? Walk far enough from the Pole of Earth, and it will trouble you no more. What are distance and walking? Illusions made real by the feet. Meditate long and empty yourself, and it will trouble you no more. What are meditation and emptiness? Illusions made real by the mind. Just forget the earth and be forgotten. Simple enough for a charm. Or maybe not. Is Pasiap watching?

                    Look. This is more fundamental than the lack of conservation laws, or quantifiable properties of matter. Those are the scent of the lotus, not the lotus itself. No scientific method means that experience does not inform prediction. Root, stem, and blossom, all of repeatable observation is thrown out. What remains true is: some things are, others are not. What else to call it but Essence?

                    This is a setting where existence is personal, and visceral, and may be invested in the voices and the blades of the wise, to make them more real than the fragile things of lies and iron lesser men hold. What stores can be told in such a place? Different ones than ours, to be sure.

Each artifact requires a "certain amount" of Essence to be committed, and once someone has committed too much of it, they won't be able to use any charms or attune to anything else.

                                        And Dirk Dragon Blooded probably can't attune to as many artifact swords as Joe Solar can, because Joe (as a Solar) likely has a larger Essence pool, which means that, in-setting, you could calculate that Joe has a larger Essence pool than Dirk.

                                        Isator didn't like my "Essence Box" idea, but as I mentioned, people can still measure their Essence through artifact attunement. Imagine a type of artifact ring that, out-of-game, costs 2 motes to attune to. From an in-game perspective, you could measure someone's Essence pool by seeing how many rings they can attune to at once. After they can't attune to anymore (because they've committed all their Essence) then you can get a variable you can calculate with.

                                        Even if people, in-game, didn't talk about motes, they could talk about how many "rings" someone's Essence pool is, or how many "Swords" it is (if they're calculating Essence pool size by how many small Daiklaves they can attune to). Exalted could even use this method to realize that it costs more Essence to attune to one type of artifact than another (Joe the Solar finds he can only attune to 10 jade Daiklaves, but he can attune to 20 Orichalcum Daiklaves, whereas Dirk Dragonblooded can attune to 12 Jade Daiklaves and only 6 Orichalcum ones).

                                        I don't mind changing things to add mysteries in 3rd edition, but this just seems like an incredibly awkward thing to change. Why is it that First Age Exalted weren't capable of doing some basic experiments like the above to figure out how much Essence they're capable of outputting?

                                            I beg pardon for my impending self-indulgence, but while I was peeling clementines, I was thinking about this thread, and a logical extension of my earlier post made Vance's comments about spirituality fall into place for me.

                                            In my earlier post in this thread, I mentioned that trying to systematize what a mote "can do" is inherently doomed to failure, because that's not how motes work; I think we'd all agree that a Charm which allows you to tap your staff in a barren desert and conjure a liter of water should cost much less than a Charm which enables a user massing 100 kilograms to jump 90 trillion meters straight up, despite both these actions requiring about 9E16 joules to do "fairly." Motes have a lot of applications, from the extremely concrete to the extremely abstract, and so the scientific-reductionist approach to understanding them is not very good. You can only apply reductionism up until the point where phenomena are no longer explained by lower-level behavior, after all, and if what you terminate at is fundamentally complicated (in a way that the lowest-level elements of our universe, it bears mentioning, are not), you're SOL.

                                            However, Essence isn't just a fancy name for energy in Creation - as Vance and Eric Minton have been hammering on in this thread, Essence is a spiritual entity that admits spiritual understanding. A reductionist scientist after the model of our own universe's scientific method would have to throw up their hands in trying to figure out what a certain amount of Essence would be capable of doing, or to a priori determine how much Essence would be required to obtain a desired effect, but an enlightened occultist could arrive at the correct answers through their intimate and intuitive understanding - they grok Essence flows.

                                            Now, the scientific method isn't completely borked in Creation - Creation might have a privileged "down," but I see no reason why its gravity-analogue couldn't be measured, or why gifted and curious occultists could not gain a rough understanding of how much Essence Exalted at various points along their spiritual development might have (as I mentioned before, "cast this known, discrete spell" seems a solid unit of standardization for motepool math, and I agree with other posters that there's no way someone didn't think to try to figure this out). But there will be a number of points where empiricism has to give way to mysticism, because Creation simply doesn't run on the same kind of engine as reality.
