    04-04-2023, 08:03 PM
    This primarily related to a character's backstory but how does gods and mortals interaction go in an immaculate heavy area (such as Lookshy or the Realm)?

    Can a god appear before a mortal praying at their temple and give guidance/training/gifts? (Without an immaculate monk getting upset?)

    Can a god testify on a mortal's behalf in a trial etc (They didn't do the crime officer etc) if they chose?

    Would they have to act through a Immaculate Monk intermediary? Would the Monk take it seriously?

    To be a bit more specific, in Lookshy could Tien-yu or Tu-yu show up and guide a mortal soldier/helot/student ? Without the Dragon Blooded getting upset and maybe the Dragon-blooded listening to the god if they spoke on the mortals behalf?

                  The Immaculates doesn't believe human-god interactions are always wrong, but that they are potentially dangerous and shouldn't happen without some form of Immaculate oversight. But the nature of the oversight can be variable. Gods that help the Immaculates with tasks they considers important are given more leeway. At least one god was allowed to make an Exigent, which is a pretty extreme form of god-mortal interaction. So anything less than that is certainly possible for a favored god.

                  Probably in Lookshy Tien-yu and Tu-yu are on pretty friendly terms with the Immaculate Faith so I think they could be allowed to do something like that, though maybe guiding a helot would be seen as disrupting the Perfected Hierarchy and thus potentially problematic (although maybe the Immaculate Faith - Immaculate Order differences means that members of the Immaculate Faith don't believe the helot's status is a religious matter).

                                      My personal take is that the official party line of the Immaculate Faith re: the appropriate level of direct interaction between Gods and Mortals is "none at all." When mediated by a terrestrial, especially an appropriate authority within the proper contexts, then okay.

                                      If a god decides to so much as say, "hi." to a mortal, then they enter into theoretically actionable territory, much like someone going just a bit over the speed limit. There's probably a little leeway that individual Dragon-Blooded are allowed to exercise: Dynasts who have better things to do (whether distracted by their own cares or well-placed bribes) may be incredibly lax, while someone like Peleps Deled may brutally beat a god to nonexistence for even manifesting outside of pre-designated circumstances.

                                      So any god who decides to interact with a mortal just has to ask themselves, "Do I feel lucky?"

                                      Well, do they?

                                                      If I recall the official stance for the whole immaculate issue vis a vis gods interacting with mortals is that they're not supposed to. The whole DBs being the bridge between the divine and mortal and all that so technically they're supposed to get involved if the god is interacting with mortals outside of stuff ordained by clergy or DB authorization.

                                                      In practice that's probably pretty commonly ignored so long as the god isn't doing anything to weaken the power the immaculates have or get themselves extra worship and so on. Especially if its an older tradition. Like no one is going to object if its just a long standing thing for the local harvest god to show up at one of the farms that had struggled the most to go, "Okay, so here's what went wrong with your crops this year. This part here you couldn't have done anything about storm dragons aren't a thing you can prepare for. But here's what you can do to lessen the flooding that caused havoc to this field." Or if the island's forge goddess holds generational contests where the winner is granted the privilege of being an apprentice blacksmith under her. There is probably objections to say Ahlat training his own personal general.
