    How do you play a non-brainwashed Sidereal

    07-10-2015, 11:21 AM



    So according to the book, Sidereal are found when they're still babies and then got raised carefully in Creation with some creepy Sidereals pulling the string behind-the-scene so the babies Sidereal will want to become a Sidereal when they finally exalted. Sound like an evil organization that I do not want to be apart of ! And since I found Heaven just a little bland and unimpressive, so a special snowflake Sidereal who never got found by the Loom is the only way for me to go.

    But I vaguely remembered that ronin often gets murdered if they refuse to work for the Bureau of Fate, is this thing canon ? Since MoEP: Sidereal just mention other Sid blah blah blah about the ronin wasting her talents or some such. If the whole "Murder the Ronin" is true (kinda unlikely, Sidereal aren't that dumb....aren't they), then how many teacup assassin's balls must my Solar daddies crushes before Sidereal Elder Creeplord stops bothering me. If it's false then what kind of reaction should I expect when my character finally meet another Sid who is working for Heaven ?

    I heard all this jargon about the Sidereal being a close-knit group (hence the name Five-score Fellowship, or Ahn-Aru freaking out when she killed another Sid), but why do it seems like it Bronze and Gold Sidereal just murdering each other all the time, I feel like my character will have a giant knife stick in her back the moment she refuses to work for the Bureau.

          Not all Sids are taken to Heaven as babies.

          Heaven being bland and unimpressive is a failure of writing, not a failure of concept. I mean granted 2e had a good share of both and yes it's hard to imagine stories set in Heaven when the Sids are basically portrayed as the Gestapo.

          If your ronin Sidereal has never met another Sidereal then they probably have not ever heard about Sidereals and would not be likely to make broad sweeping generalizations about them. That said if someone came to you and said "hey, you're like me. You seem to have had a hard life out here. Come up to Heaven with me; you'll get a place to live, a good job, and expert training," well I don't know many people who wouldn't jump at the chance to at least learn more even if they were somewhat wary.

          As for them getting murdered I have issues with the presentation of flat NPCs who are comfortable performing casual murder. With Anathema you can justify it as religious indoctrination for DBs or maintaining the status quo for Sids but that doesn't mean there aren't people doing the killing, and killing affects people. It's a shame how much this has been minimized in the books for years.

          Your interpretations here are pretty gonzo and I can never tell if your posts like this are intentionally hyperbolic for humor purposes or if these are your actual impressions of the setting. I hope the former but again, I don't know (it's hard to read the intended tone through text). In any case, if the Bureau worked like you describe then it functionally could not work in the setting or do the job it is supposed to do. If that's how it's written then it obviously needs to change since PC splat organizations should not be inherently set in solely antagonistic positions to the PC members of them.

                                                  This response from Stephen -- I think from around the time Coik describes -- may help cover things on the Ronin front. Spoilered because size:

                                                  Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                                                  I think there's a sort of misunderstanding about what ronin are here, also a misunderstanding about what Sidereals are and why they work for the Bureau of Destiny.

                                                  Most Sidereals work for the Bureau of Destiny because they want to work for the Bureau of Destiny, because they're found when they're babies and carefully raised, not in Heaven, but in Creation communities with lives and childhoods managed by divine and Sidereal minders who work behind the scenes, Truman Show style, to ensure that the child grows up with the sort of upbringing that will forge them into the sort of people who want to work for the Bureau of Destiny. This is not, precisely, evil -- it's a job that needs doing, and it comes with fantastic rewards, and also Sidereals suffer from the Arcane Fate which means that only other Sidereals and high-Essence gods will ever be able to be proper peers. When they finally come of age and realize that everything in their life up until that moment has lead up to their Exaltation and the beginning of their great work, a reaction like "Oh my god you've been manipulating me to be complicit in your evil schemes this whole time, my entire life has been a lie!" is very uncommon. What mostly happens is they get that hero moment where they realize that they're the heroes of destiny and their patrons have been watching over them and ensuring they learn the right lessons, and now they're finally ready to assume the awesome responsibility of protecting the world. It's like something out of a fantasy book or story where the predestined hero realizes he's the chosen one. (That this position comes with a mansion in Heaven and access to the best food and prostitutes doesn't make the decision to embrace their new roles harder).

                                                  Ronin aren't Sidereals who aren't found when they're babies. I mean, that's probably happened! A couple of times! I'm going to say, oh, a dozen times in the history of Creation, and at least half of those were in the wake of the Usurpation. There are probably five-hundred or a thousand-year stretches in Creation's history where there are absolutely no ronin like that is alive.

                                                  Most ronin are Sidereals who have left the Bureau for personal reasons, grown disillusioned with Heavenly politics, abandoned the patronage of their, uh, patrons, and gone into Creation to find their own way.

                                                  To be clear the Bureau of Destiny is not Treadstone or Blackbriar and ronin Sidereals are not Jason Bourne. They're not hunted like fugitives except for the ones who actually do go ronin because they're fugitives. The Fivescore Fellowship is a tightly-knit peer group, because they all work together and they all suffer the Arcane Fate, so they can't have meaningful, lasting relationships outside of each other. If a family member leaves, reactions are mixed -- some relatives will turn their back, some will hope they return, some will respect their decision, some will try to convince them they're wrong, some will maintain covert ties while pretending to be just as alienated as the rest of the family. Many Sidereals who go ronin come back on their own, eventually. Ten years, fifty years, they find themselves, or they accomplish what they set out to do, or they realize they were wrong, or they just get lonely; they establish ties again and report back to work. That this is a thing that happens is taken into account when the Fellowship decides how to react to a new Sidereal going ronin.


                                                  Further exchanges spoiled because moar size:
                                                  Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                                                  This seems to be a thing then that changed from first and second edition in which they were explicitly brought back into the fold or killed.
                                                  Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                                                  Yes, that's because Second Edition was written on the assumption that every social group in the setting was at every other social group's throat.
                                                  Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                                                  Understandable. I guess my point is I was just working with current knowledge. That you can just leave now isn't such a big deal. Though, what happens if they decide to take their ball and go home?
                                                  Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                                                  Sidereals are strongly incentivized not to do this for a number of reasons. They're abandoning duties after having been raised from birth to value duty; they're abandoning luxuries undreampt-of by the majority of mankind to live in dirt and squalor in Creation; they're abandoning the only peer group in existence who can truly know and understand them. The Bureau of Destiny and the Five-Score Fellowship have seen Sidereals go ronin before, and they know the danger signs; if at all possible, Sidereals judged to be in danger of going ronin in the near future are afforded lighter duties, other Sidereals try to figure out what's going wrong and address the problem; if times are tough they're reminded that times are tough and everyone is counting on them and they'll be letting everyone down by leaving. Even the Gold/Bronze divide, to some extent, helps prevent Sidereals from going ronin -- this isn't the intent of the split, but Ayesha Ura can always tell her Gold Faction ronin candidate "If you leave now that just gives more ground to Chejop" as a discouragement tactic.

                                                  Nobody decides to take their ball and go home over a minor issue. Each ronin's circumstances is unique and unusual; there is absolutely nothing you can say about what happens when one leaves that applies to all of them.
                                                  Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                                                  (And it's not always a 100% thing -- there's always those guys who are effectively ronin except they have the one or two other Sidereals who they'll still talk to, and who they maybe do favors for now and again because they know how important it is. You can always do the story about the one cop who retired because he couldn't take the pressure of the job anymore, except his best friend is still on the force and calls him back to consult on the latest serial killer case because it's just horrible and the retired cop is the only guy who was ever good enough to catch 'em when they're this bad.)
