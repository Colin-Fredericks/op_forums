                            It does seem to me that a Lunar who is faced with a tyrant lizard that has been raised and trained and invested with power by a dedicated Solar animal trainer, and opts to fight it by turning into another tyrant lizard and getting into a straight up brawl, might be doing it because they feel as though they have something to prove. I suspect that a fairly advanced Lunar might be able to prove it fairly well, but in most other cases the Lunar might be better off turning into something small enough for the dinosaur to have a hard time seeing or hitting, and then poisoning them, or getting behind them to attack the Solar.

                            Still, that's probably not the most pressing comparison; I can see how the people that have raised concern are looking at things more in terms of how either of them utilise the form of a tyrant lizard to exert themselves against other obstacles.

                            In which case, it may be that at the end of things, the Solar produces the superior tyrant lizard; they're still the mightiest of the Exalted. The Lunar advantage would strike me as being able to place the tyrant lizard more readily among a variety of other options, that Charms to benefit the tyrant lizard form will also be able to benefit a variety of other functions (whereas a Solar who has put a lot into empowering their animal is giving up the opportunity to learn things that directly benefit themselves, and becomes a bit dependent on the animal being around), and the Lunar doesn't need to worry about some of the practical concerns (such as housing, movement and feeding) that really ought to be attached to the issue of a tyrant lizard familiar.

                            Besides all of that, as far as I know, we thus far only have an entry level Lunar Charm for turning into huge animals, which is being compared to the result of a whole suite of Solar Charms being applied at once. That seems like a necessary limit when we're comparing a published book to snippets being furtively glimpsed through a key hole, but it should probably be acknowledged as such.

                            And overall, this is information coming from a playtest, the purpose of which is to figure out whether or not ideas work. Pointing out possible flaws or emergent issues is all well and good, but it should be kept in perspective, and hold short of being acrimonious about it not being its best the first time around (no published work is), or authoritatively declaring it to be terrible and doomed.

                            I have approximate knowledge of many things.
                            Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.
