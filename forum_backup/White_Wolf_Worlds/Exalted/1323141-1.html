    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post


    How does this square with the fact that the term "law" and its equivalents in various other languages were things first applied to concepts of human relations and religious proscriptions?

    Setting aside any specific religious beliefs, is the Law of Moses a mechanism of nature?
    I think it is a matter of connotation. The Law of Moses actually means a set of laws/ teachings. The Law of Diminishment speaks about a law of nature. Formulating laws of nature is what science do. As such, the Law of Diminishment gains a scientific aesthetics and lose a mythical one, IMO.


    Hmm, I'm pretty sure that's what lightning was before... life existed.

    Coulomb was trying to describe a phenomenon that actually exists, not compose an artistic narrative. [/SIZE][/FONT][/COLOR][/LEFT]
    Yes, but it is not what people believed. They thought it was the will of the gods, or a sign, or some primordial power. Until one day, people understood what lightning really was, and then made a set of equations which explains how and why it worked, and it turned from a mythical entity into an natural phenomenon.

    It is all about presentation, in the end, and by giving a name to a natural phenomenon and formulating it into a law of nature the presentation turns from mythical into scientific, because, in essence, that's what science do.

                    Kind of an unstated premise of the OP is that lots of power eventually equates to some form of omnipotence. That since you should be able to surpass powerful beings in some aspects (mostly in order to defeat them or notably impactful changes to the world) that you should be able to also to do everything they can do but better.

                    I think folks get in their head that because the creators were defeated and defeatable, that they have nothing they can do that's off limits to Exalts wihtout downplaying htem. But I Think i'ts just fine to say "Exalts are mighty...they just don't get a blank check." Exalts can't Exalt because well, they just can't. They are powerful beings, but unless you do something that's basically a "Your story and campaign probably resolves around doing this the first time as its culmination" sort of effort, the assumpion by default probably is it's impossible because Exalted power just doesn't expresss itself in a way to allow it.

                    This kind of goes why I don't think we need a Law of Diminishment of late, myself. It could just be the Sun only made around 300 Solars has nothing at all to do with his decisions to do so, or some pain on him in doing so. It might just be that...that's literally the only way he could have made them. "But what if it were different?" is kind of irrelevant. It isn't, and assuming it coudl be has a bit of a begging the question aspect to it.

                                Kind of another thing too with why I don't think Exalted shoudl be making sub-Exalts: The game is about you going out into the world and doing shit. While creating other Exlats is doing something, it in the end has this feel of just sub-delegating. Rather than making people able to do more things for you, I think the idea is you should just do things. What does even making an Exaltation like, do for an Exalt besides act as a "Look what I did!" moment. And while there are plenty of things that are that in actions of hubris and glory, it's not really as interesting to me. You don't really do anything with an Exaltaiton save delegate to others. It's an interesting thing for NPCs like the gods because well, they're not supposed to be major actors in the setting. But it's not so clear for an Exalt, because well....Exalts are the ones supposed to be actors doing things besides making more people meant to do things. It's wierdly just kind of recursive to me.

                                This also happens especially when themes get involved. Like, what's the Exalted ofa Solar Exalted like? Just an even-more-er filtered version of the Sun? The Sun but filtered through Bob? This is a big issue there too. Unless you have in your mind a clear idea of what the Exalt like....is, then there's not much to be gained from it. It won't be a new Solar since that in the end just kind of feels like the whole "power = blank check" and more makes you an Exaltation Bootlegger than anything interesting. If it is something more distilled to you, I tink that while a cahracter has themes, the kind of limited nature of them (and not being able to back-fill like NPCs can) are probably going ot lead to a weaker Exalt theamtically. A big advice the deves have given on Exigents so far is don't just htink of them as a power gimic, think of the god they're based on and its thematics. Gods have a bonus of being able to have weird mish-mash of thematics. And Exalted are reflective of their patrons, so that's good to have. I fail to kind of see what a Solar gives in that regard that's not just....more Solar. If the goal ils just blank "Exaltation!" then there's already an issue since Exaltation manifests in diverse ways based on patron. While a Chosen of an Exalt is likely going to be very much like the Exalt, whcih is very muc hlike th patron, which then isn't actually as interesting besies existing in itself.

                                Note in this context, finding is different than creating. Rakan released the Getimians, he doesn't have really any control on them. His relationship is one of a mentor and a patron it seems like, not some controller/creator. This isn't actually a bad area for PCs, but again, probably is rare enough to not just be something you assume due to "My Circle is powerful enough." Getimians have their own things, idea,s and concepts beyond their relation to Rakan Thulio. He imparts their reason to be in the setting, but none of their powers, themes, and such. They're pretty expliclty not his Chosen. Because their patron, whatever the hell it was, is probably very much designed and written in its own way expansive enough to support an Exalt, that a single powerful-but-pissed-off Chosen of Endings probably doesn't do so well for.

                                                    I am not sure how I feel about my players in particular creating exalts but I have toyed with enemy groups in the past having their own ‘versions’ of exaltation. I haven’t used any of them but I like the idea of ‘human made’ champions.

                                                    The simplest one was an infernal cult that learned a theurgic ritual to summons demons into a person creating a persona/stand like power set. They would be weaker than dragon-blooded but could also move undetected among the peasantry.

                                                    Another that I might expand upon when exigents comes out is based on ancient Egypt. Basically a special ghost poses a willing and worthy person becoming one with them. The ‘exalts’ would be very tied to place as they would need to rest in their tombs to recharge their energies. They were going to be a rival kingdom to Persaud that the players could either convince to be allies or antagonize. I really like the idea I had for them because they are human made exaltations out of human souls for the purpose of protecting humans (or rather their kingdom) and sustained by human effort. They were going to be both weaker in some areas and strong in others than Dragonblooded.

                                                        Originally posted by Mockery View Post
                                                        Blaque has hit a lot of the points I would. I feel like the difference in metaphysical nature between the patron and their Exalted is significant, a tacit admission that the god or whatever is unable to act, either by some sort of restriction, or by a sort of narrative law that this is the place of a hero and not an inhuman power.

                                                        Exalted creating Exalted of their own just feels like hubris to me in that light.

                                                        Oh its meant to be Hubris, and like going out in the Wyld to create their own world it backfiring can work for the story too, or at least having unintended consequences.

                                                        But there are a lot of things you can want that by definition you can't do yourself. You can't birth your own Grandson for instance, well you could in a way but lets not.
                                                        Or even just empowering your family, not something as simple as sorcery something genuine like creating a familial line of Guardians to ensure their prosperity. Or even ensure your culture survives, as many Exalted have discovered their birthcultures don't survive the test of Apocalypse as a sure thing.

                                                        And you could be busy in another endeavor like war or discovery and say want someone to be a hero for a place you don't want to live, like say Malfeas, the Underworld, Rakshastan, Heaven, the Ocean, the Sky, or Underground.

                                                        And there is the idea you want a legacy that extends beyond your lifetime. If half the Solars have been Compromised into not being Solars anymore and the Exigent's aren't reliably something you always want then you could want legacies that will survive you to fill in the missing spaces in the heroic tableau of creation.

                                                        But two facet question. What is wrong with doing this as a capstone or Epilogue in a game, and what is wrong with doing it in the middle of a game. They should be two different discussions in a lot aspects. We mostly seem to have addressed only the latter. And a lot of good points have been made.
