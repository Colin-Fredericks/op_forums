      Huh, never heard of Goldencat's before. *Googles, reads through.* And now I know why.

      It's mechanics are horribly, horribly done.

      First warning bells started going off in the section on building animal with mutations. No biggie, that's what Revlid did for his mutations rewrite, and got used for TAW's right? Well, Revlid never made these mutations:
      Afflictions
      Allure - Adds +1 to Charisma and Appearance or +1 to Manipulation.
      Cunning - Adds +1 to Perception and Intelligence or +1 to Wits.
      Force - Adds +2 to Strength
      Might - Adds +1 to Strength and Stamina or +1 to Dexterity.

      And yes, you can make those count towards your die cap. It... pretty much went downhill from there. Their was the weird decision to penalize the hell out of Lunars for learning MA. It still has the Fury/Gift one-true build issues. The excellencies are weird, and not in a good way. No I(A)M equivalent, in fact no excellencies that go past action length. Their also just plain hard to understand, since it seems like the author left out the phrase 'to successes', so the only way to intuit what the thing does is the sections about converting successes in the damage rolls. The capstone ability turns the 1st and 2nd excellencies into anima effects, which does... what exactly? Note everything is auto combo'd in this rewrite.

      And then their are the charms themselves. It here that the fact this is an edition lift of a 1e project becomes very apparent. Odd mentions of brawl do that. And I hope the +3 'bonus' to Speed is another 1eism (yes, that's applied to your weapon). Beyond that, there are insane die pool inflation weapons (a Accuracy +10 weapon is easy to do), HLs for ammunition, the ability to not use virtue channels when using them for one willpower on the DBT equivalent.... And all this? A cursory reading. I gave up on this rewrite after I saw the Appearance charm that lets you use Appearance in place of Charisma or Manipulation in dice pools. Which is actually worse than it sounds, because one of the anima effect adds dice to die pools that use apperance.

      In summary, it's clumsy written to the point understanding it is difficult, the mechanics are horribad, and I honestly just don't like some of the design choices it made (form lock, MA penalization). The last I might have given a pass for if the charms had been usable. They aren't. Canon Lunars are considerably more playable then these guys.

      (If you want to see the Silver Masks rewrite for your selves, here's the link.)

      Consider my recommendation thoroughly given to the TAW's. They have their problems, but their mechanically sound by this point, and cool powers are fun. That said, a more standard take on Lunars would be nice to have.

                                              Adamant Siaka did two rewrites, rather good ones at that; one near-total revamp of the canon charm-set, the other a complete revamp of the Alchemical charm-set into Lunar-equivalents. IIRC, he removed form-lock, replaced the tell, and created a lot of awesome knacks for hunting people's dreams and turning into things depicted via effigies. Balance might be questionable if you try to min-max that, but if you play it straight you get Lunars who are less "The Animal Exalted" and more "The Shapeshifting Exalted". Also took a lot of the basic combat charms and tweaked them, adding odd side benefits like a small social bonus in social situations based on your presence being backed up by physical force, or the, ahem... Bedroom Applications... Of being strong, fast, flexible, and indefatigable.

                                              My favourite remains the TAW though, for sheer comprehensiveness and flavour.

                                              Aretii: "As one of the players described it, 'After initiation, it was actually a relief seeing who had eyepatches or bandaged hands - you know what they sacrificed, instead of wondering what bits people cut out of their own souls to gain power.'"
