        The difference between withering and decisive is diegetic. The character is always struggling to overpower, find openings and stab, and awareness of their own dominance in a battle relies transparently on both its pressure and the (pun intended) decisive wounds they managed to cause.

        So it's not a character decision, it's a player decision. Do it as much as you feel is narratively appropriate. Right after a crash is a scary moment where the decisive has extra tones of "well, fuck".

                                Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post

                                Interesting. That would certainly change things a lot. The beasts and devils of creation aren’t fighting to win in the system, as in either victory or death, they’re just fighting. A river dragon
                                may just start the fight off with a decisive bite and go for the clinch straight away, then the rest of the battle takes place slamming the dragon around and prying its jaws apart to free the unlucky guy who got bit.

                                That’s not a strategy I would have used before because no river dragon starts combat with enough initiative to take out a character. Usually they won’t get to act with that much either but if they spend some willpower on their stealth roll...
                                I think the idea is really to consider the options at your disposal from a strategic and meta point of view, and then chose the most optimal one. The only barrier should be "Is this absurd from the situation inside the game from the perspective of the character ?". From your example, say your river-dragon (sorry I don't remember all their stats) is fighting the PCs. He got quite low on initiative so he will not finish an enemy, and everyone around him has high initiative, so they can threaten a kill on him.
                                • His first option would be to run away, because strategically you know he is gonna die, but it doesn't matter. If you put him here in the situation of an ambushing, it's clearly to die while fighting the PCs. From his in-game perspective, all he sees is that the preys are defending themselves, not that their "damage level" is red and ready to strike.
                                • His other option is to try a decisive attack against a random close enemy. It's more in line with your strategical objective as a ST, and it's a viable option to me.
                                • The even better option (in my eyes), because it's in line with your strategical objective and more dramatically engaging, is to try to grapple a player character and drag him in the closest river/lake to drown him (my translator suggested "to walnut him" I just can't stop laughing). It's certainly in line with his usual tactics in game (again, didn't read them recently, maybe it's not their thing at all it's just an example), with your strategical objective (put pressure on the PCs), and with the situation at hand (you don't have enough initiative to seriously threaten a player, for example).

                                Once he has started dragging a player into the water, but he suddenly lost 3/4 of his hp because everyone else attacked him, it now starts to make sense for him to try to escape : It's written on the description of most animals that they will try to flee if they lose too much hp, and hp loss in an information the characters have conscience "in game" for the most part. So it's line with his psychology, and you should not make him "continue the fight just to die", because at this point in the fight he has enough information to not do the absurd choice to stay and suicide for sure.

                                Here would be my analysis of the kind of situation you suggested.

                                        Originally posted by Marcob View Post

                                        Chausse mentioned keeping them crashed, and that's what I was refuting.

                                        As for it being smart to crash an opponent first, I agree and I mentioned that myself. Chausse seems to feel that is unthematic or meta gaming.
                                        For the part about crashing someone for more benefit I was being generic : If the other guy is not in crashed and you don't have 100% certainty to kill him, you could keep withering him to make sure he is crashed and maybe lose some special Perilous Charms. If he is already in Crash, you could keep withering him to make sure he will not make an Initiative Shift on you after a decisive attack. I mean, the strategical options are pretty numerous if you want to find good reasons to keep withering someone.

                                        As for the other point, it is obviously "smart" to crash an opponent first, I said already in many cases it has great chances of being the optimal play. But I think one of the ... particularity (I was going to say drawback but I think it's a matter of perspective) of the initiative system is going for the "optimal" or "rational" play can become pretty dull, precisely because optimal plays are not always the funs or dramatic ones. I feel it's unthematic because I was promised exciting reversals and unexpected development thanks to the initiative system, but arrived at a point where someone with 25i decides to keep withering, it rather becomes asthmatic pace and meaningless abstraction.

                                        I know I'm a bit hard on this one, I'm usually a rational player as well so if I see a logical play I will do it, but I feel at this point there is a misunderstanding between what the system is supposed to feel like and how the players use it. I don't blame it on the players making the rational choice, just wish it was a bit different (hence my home-brew proposals).
