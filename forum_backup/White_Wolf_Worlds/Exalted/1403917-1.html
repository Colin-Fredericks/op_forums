            It's not necessarily "deluded" because most DBs do not have the opportunity to witness Solars and Lunars outside of a wyld hunt. The majority of Dragonblooded are locked into aristocratic, virulently hierarchical and classist societies that both isolate them from interacting with other social classes and outsiders and diminish their empathy from them. For the average Dynastic Dragonblooded to give Solars or Lunars the benefit of the doubt requires a level of empathy and goodwill for the Other that they are simply socialized against having.

                            One thing that I think about is the possibility that people in a state of anger or bitterness or grief can start to actively (if not quite intentionally) eliminate their sense of nuance regarding a subject; details that would be contrary to their emotional state become less apparent, while they can indeed start to see elements that reinforce and justify how they feel that aren't truly there.

                            I look at the example of the Iron Flame clan among the Toumon, and the reference to how they're courting the Immaculate Philosophy, because they happen to have never accumulated much in the way of the usual tutelary ancestor ghosts and have been particularly subject to assault by alien gods (while also having an apparent economic base that I would read as putting them somewhere equivalent to a comfortable enough middle class existence to be isolated from other afflicted clans while also being stung by the lack of social or political prestige underlined by the absence of divine favour and any associated lesser contribution to the more prominent martial undertakings).

                            We're notably not told how this is reconciled with the presence of Wake. I can hardly imagine that a Lunar being in their midst has gone unnoticed by the Immaculate missionaries, and even if some form of syncretism was tolerable the point is rendered moot with the pains she has gone to in order to not be regarded as a divinity. I would imagine that for a start, they would quietly sidestep the point that the immortal hero-aunt is an intolerable monster, but eventually they're going to have to bring it up and the Iron Flame are going to have to decide how to feel about that.

                            I picture a scenario in which things come to a head at some kind of major social or political event past the point where more people have realised that a clan has widely adopted the Immaculate Philosophy, and word has been widely spread that this entails labelling Wake Anathema. That members of the Iron Flame might feel ganged up on as everybody else demands, in a mixture of tones of outrage and frustration and sorrow, how they can side with a people that would despise Wake so, after everything she's done for them. And in that moment I can see a loud voice shout back something to the effect of "everything that she's done for you, you mean".

                            And it's not really a thing that they've thought before, but in the heat of the moment and the need to defend and justify oneself and just plain being driven to win the argument, it boils up and starts to gather momentum. And everything that Wake actually has done for them, specifically, starts to fade from their attention, and they rile themselves up more with the idea that everything they ever suffered at the hands of gods shows a failure by Wake to protect them, which can build into a narrative of her complicity, which can ultimately end in a place that conflates her with the devils that did her harm. They fold in every instance of seeing Wake in battle, and feats that were once celebrated are now pointed to as indicators of her malefic nature. People double down as they feel more alienated and wronged, and they emphasize and embellish and conflate the details to feed a narrative that supports everything they're feeling, and it comes to a point where they declare that the Toumon have been deluding themselves to ever think that Wake was safe to be around, and it's only a matter of time before she turns on all of them (it's awfully convenient that she can attribute the loss of her own people to somebody else, isn't it?). Well, we're not going to be deluded any more; we're leaving before it's too late, and we're taking our property and our expertise that can establish us in some other more sensible commune with us. You can have your devil-warrior, and best hope that your fading ancestors can protect you if she ever turns those martial talents upon yourselves.

                            Probably not a situation that entails the entire clan rising in violence against the Lunar specifically, but maybe a few young zealots (egged on in secret by some of the missionaries) make an attempt. Perhaps Wake tries to shield the Iron Flame from reprisals by the rest of the clans; it breaks her heart that one of them is rejecting her like this (and insinuations about her past might push her to the brink), but for the sake of the memory of better times and the friendship she recalls she's willing to allow them depart unmolested and not hold the actions of an eager few that didn't amount to much against them. Perhaps it proves that such restraint lies beyond her power, and the seceding clan is fallen upon by their fellows who are enraged beyond reason at the treachery and ingratitude and dishonour. Any who make it out despite that might find other stories of what Lunars can do that they can seize upon to recall Wake as having used twisted words to affect magnanimity while actually turning everybody else against them.

                            Anyway, apart from indulging in this scenario having occurred to me as I imagined the Iron Flame's conversion playing out in similar terms to impulses for viewing other people that I've had to curb during some extreme periods, I can see this as a way that Dragon Blooded ideas about the nature of the other Exalted could have evolved that have a bit more going on for them than just making something up wholecloth and then committing to it deeply. That a narrative which emphasizes Celestial offences can cross over with many people technically not being well-acquainted with the mechanisms of their Exaltation "people who almost literally acquired demon powers". It wouldn't have been helped by the fact that the period in which these narratives are developing is one in which most of the Solars are removed from sight while the Lunars are drawing deep from their scariest toybox. One might wonder if any Dragon Blooded who are starting to promote the rhetoric of being demon sorcerers are doing so while afflicted with that curse to literally wear down your capacity to feel anything other than terror that a Lunar is hunting you. Over time they just start to conflate the memory of the Solars with what is becoming more of an everyday reality, even if the powers of those Exalted diverged significantly at important points.

                                As for the role of the Sun and Moon in exalting Anathema, i'd imagine that the average orthodox theologian would try to dance around the question or subtly(but never directly) imply that the Sun and Moon are in fact amoral, capricious, or irresponsible. Regardless of the virtues and power of the Anathema, their might is ultimately inauspicious and corruptive and their connection to the Incarnae, legitimate or otherwise, has no bearing on their morality, because the Incarnae are not moral actors in the Immaculate doctrine. Much like how The Ramayana, a Vaishnavite Hindu text(meaning it places emphasis on Vishnu as the greatest of gods) subtly casts Lord Shiva and Brahma in a negative light for empowering the Rakshasa King Ravana. It never goes so far as to condemn them, but it certainly does not assign them any moral superiority over or even moral equivalency with Vishnu.

                                The immaculate order and philosophy are largely a patchwork job to unite pre-Usurpation theology with the political goals of the Bronze Faction and the Realm, there are going to be flaws and weaknesses in the doctrine, but those have been planned around and believers are often socialized to ignore those weaknesses. The Immaculates have several well-constructed, and technically true arguments for hunting Anathema, and they reinforce this by imparting a values system to believers that categorically excludes and dehumanizes Anathema regardless of their behavior. In that values system, Anathema are dangerous because they ignore the Perfected Hierarchy from which the Dragon-Blooded derive authority, making their existence an existential threat against the Dragon-Blooded hegemony. For an ordinary Dragon-Blooded to consider showing mercy or empathy for Anathema requires them to act against what they perceive as their own best interests, which most are not inclined to do.

                                    Sister Ivy frowned as she lowered the scroll her sifu had given her. "So... The Anathema aren't demons," she said, chewing on her lower lip.

                                    "That's right," her sifu said, dutifully taking the scroll and rolling it up. "It is a story we tell the populace at large. Only those who show focus and dedication and piety, like yourself, can know the truth."

                                    "Sifu, why?" Ivy asked. "Why do we tell the faithful and the other Dragon Bloods that the Anathema are demons?"

                                    The Sifu frowned and sighed, rising to his feet. "Walk with me, Ivy."

                                    Out of the archives, and in the sand garden of the temple, the sifu finally spoke again. "When you tell the parable of the farmer, the hen, and the fox, that is not literally about an actual farmer, a hen, and a troublesome fox. Except when it is, I suppose."

                                    "Well, yes," Ivy said. "But that's simplifying something to make it easier to understand. This... Is a lie."

                                    "... Yes. It is," her sifu said. "The exact nature of the Anathema is a secret teaching, and them being demons who stole the power of the Sun and Moon is a lie. But something that isn't a lie is what that power does to someone."

                                    "Sifu?"

                                    "The Sun and Moon are great, Sister," her Sifu said as he approached a pond, producing a bag of seeds and tossing them to the ducks swimming merrily. "Too great for a mortal to even comprehend. Even if through circumstance, a mortal is Exalted by the Sun or Moon, that is far greater power than any mortal can, or should, have."

                                    "So when we teach that the Anathema will go mad-"

                                    "That is NOT a lie," her Sifu said firmly. "I have, in the past, engaged in more than a few Wyld Hunts. I've seen Anathema that do their best, that try to rein in the chaos of their Essence, that swear that they're different. Do you know how many I saw that didn't go mad and leave ruin and upheaval in their wake?"

                                    There was a moment of silence before Ivy spoke. "Is the answer 'None', Sifu?"

                                    "Precisely. The Anathema are human, perhaps, but more. Do you recall your Exaltation, Ivy?" the sifu said, tossing more seeds to the ducks.

                                    "It was... Exhilarating, Sifu. I felt so much power, like I could do anything..."

                                    "From what we know, the passions of the Chosen of the Dragons are great and must be tempered. It is also true with the Anathema. And their ability to do more is even greater. Imagine someone with the impulses of you when you first Exalted... With the power you've heard and seen from the Anathema."

                                    Ivy didn't say anything, simply frowned. Finally, she broke the silence. "I don't doubt that Anathema are dangerous, Sifu, But why do we tell the rest of Creation this story? Can't they know the truth?"

                                    "Some can," her Sifu said. "But for a grieving family, it may be easier to tell them that their child is already dead, and that it's a demon, rather than explain that their child is doomed to madness and to lose themselves."

                                    Ivy frowned, clenching her fists on her knees. "... I don't know how I feel about this, Sifu."

                                    "Neither did I, when I first heard it. In time, I've seen this happen far too often. And when you're on a Hunt... Sometimes happiness is the greater good over technicalities."

                                    Another moment of silence before Ivy spoke up. "I will... Think on this, Sifu."
