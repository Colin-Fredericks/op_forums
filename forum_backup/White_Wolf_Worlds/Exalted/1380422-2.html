    Originally posted by Moss Reynholm View Post

    On this, what do people think, if anything, needs doing about the '5 issue'?

    If the rulebook included something like 'you can only have two attributes and two abilities at 5 at chargen' does anything actually change for most people's games? Maybe a sidebar saying 'this is the default setting for Creation and NPCs assume this. But this is Exalted, feel free to ignore if that suits your game?'

    Is that a better game (genuine question)? Do players need this much hand holding? Leaves more space for characters to grow in their secondary fields outside charms, but (a) charms are generally the most gratifying (I.e Impactful) way to grow and (b) loads of people play 'the world's greatest warrior exalts' stories - do restrictions hamper this too much?
    I think it's fine.

    The NPC rules are clear you don't need max stats.
    The static difficulty rules are clear you don't need max stats.
    The stat descriptions very clearly denote what a stat of 5 means.
    You have to spend BP to get 5s in your Abilities.
    The example character has one 5 (Dexterity).
    The five pregen characters have eight Abilities at 5 between them (one of which not having any 5s).

    Exalted works if you take eleven 5s. It just doesn't work the same way as if you only take one or two 5s. What kind of game you play should be decided in Session 0 -- where you should hold the players hands WAAAY more than this.

                    I think it might be fine to start character creation with a bit fewer points to spend on Abilities, and maybe even Merits and Charms, and let more of that develop through play. It's not like 5s in favored Abilities are hard to train, if you want them.

                    But I agree with John and the Wizard that what's most needed is advice. Some points more detailed than "talk to your group about how many 5s you want to take, and if you're not sure, aim for a low number" that I might give two new players (which anyone is free to dispute if they disagree with):

                    (1) Remember during character creation that Abilities are relatively easy to raise, in terms of XP and training time, and Attributes less so, especially in terms of training time, while many Merits can't be gotten by spending XP at all. If you're not sure what to set an Ability on, you can start it lower and raise it later.

                    (2) It's more important to make sure characters are balanced against each other, and the ST's expectations, in terms of their main combat stats, where they'll often be facing the same threats, than noncombat stats, where they'll usually be making different rolls. That said, it's fine to make a character who plans not to participate in combat, or to participate but focus on self-defense.

                    (3) You can make your starting character concept quite broad. Saying your character is a ninja, a pirate, or a wizard (to pick the first three things that popped into my head) would be a good starting point in a lot of games. Your character in Exalted can easily be a ninja pirate wizard from session 1. You'll have plenty of Ability points to spread around to make your character an expert in all these things, who can reach superhuman levels by spending Essence.

                    (4) Think about things your Circle may all want to do together, such as sneak into somewhere (Stealth) or climb something (Athletics), and make sure you have a point or two at those.

                    (5)The suggestion in the book that you may want to start with mortal characters and Exalt as Solars during play should probably be bolded and underlined.
