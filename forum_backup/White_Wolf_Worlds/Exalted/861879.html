    Regarding the AggieCon Thread and Solars

    04-08-2016, 12:08 PM
    I was prompted to write this by the AggieCon thread. It started as a reply, but it mutated. These are just my thoughts on the matter, I'm not particularly married to them.

    I feel like there is an undercurrent that runs through the discussions on this forum regarding Solars. It's felt, but I rarely see it expressed. Bear with me.

    The Solars' core theme is "Better Than You."

    A Solar is Sherlock Holmes, who makes everyone around him look slow and stupid.

    A Solar is Achilles, a single warrior who turns the tide of an entire battle, outshining everyone else on the field.

    A Solar is Albert Einstein, who decades after his death is being proven right experimentally, though he was operating on pure theory and math when he was alive. Entire teams of scientists and engineers work with super computers and satellites to prove what one person did with chalk and white boards(It can be more complex than that, but go with me for the sake of this example.)

    In RPGs, we play these people in many games not just Exalted.

    In real life, these people can be fucking infuriating.

    ================================================== =======

    How does this related to the AggieCon thread?

    What struck me most about the AggieCon thread was how some people made their points from the perspective of obligation, or rights. By that I mean their language itself inferred these fictional constructs we call Exalts were owed something by the developers and/or players. This is not the first time I've observed this.

    There were several posts in the AggieCon thread that are essentially rants at the idea of Solars "taking everything," or the Dragon-Blooded "having something of their own."

    When I unwind all that, I see two things. First, some people identify with their favorite splats so strongly that they become defensive about the splat's "rights". Second, that defensiveness often comes up when the discussion turns to Solars precisely because their themes of ultimate mastery and superiority touch on that very human sensitivity. Bluntly put, when we invest emotionally in these splats, and our lovingly crafted characters are associated with them, it can be frustrating to see them "outshined" by Solars, in the way we have all at one point or another experienced in real life

    If Sherlock is a Solar, who is Watson? More importantly, who wants to play him?
    ================================================== ======
    Why does this matter?

    Well, I feel this way of discussing things isn't good for the game. Exalted is a make-believe world we use to play a game. As such, the only consideration that merits weight is how it plays as a game. No living being is a member of these splats. As a result, concerns of fairness are irrelevant, as long as the final product is fun for people to play.

    (There are two factors I'm not really addressing here. First, that not all ways of playing the game will be equally as fun, because that's impossible, and setting consistency matters because it's necessary for the kind of fun on offer in Exalted.)

    In fact, in Exalted, unfairness is intended. A young Solar could be a farm boy with a pitchfork and be brutally murdered by two Dragon-Blooded warriors in full Jade kit. A Dynast might be over a century-old and a master of combat, and fall to a Solar who Exalted five minutes earlier. Any of us could produce a plethora of such examples wherein each and every Exalt is the winner or the loser.

    Exalted is unique and fun because it sells us unfairness.

    Talking about what's "fair" to splats is pointless, and leads to sensitivity and/or rage. Instead, the conversation should be oriented around what's unfair in a fun way.

    If a given unfairness, mechanical or narrative, makes for interesting player choices, fun stories, and fits into the giant ball of unfairness that is Exalted, then it is a Good Thing.

      Dude, have you read the Holmes books? Watson is freaking awesome. I would love to play him. In fact, I can think of a few times when my character has played a Watson-esque role, and I loved it.

      For me, my participation in that discussion wasn't motivated by wanting to give the Solars something or keep the Dragon-Blooded down. It was to point out the risks to having balanced and appealing options across the board, both for Dragon-Blooded and the Martial Arts system.

      I don't want to play a Dragon-Blooded graduate of the House of Bells with Archery and feel like a chump because the player across from me decided to make a Wood Dragon stylist. I don't want to play a Dodge/Thrown Dragon-Blooded character and feel shortchanged for not taking Air Dragon. I want there to be some legitimate question whether I should take Steel Devil or Fire Dragon.

      To me, the most elegant part of the Martial Arts system is that, if a Style fits your concept, it's a tempting (but not mandatory) option, regardless of what kind of Exalted you're playing. It should never be The Obviously Superior Choice, and I also think something is lost if it's the Obviously Inferior Choice. Without Mastery, I'm having trouble envisioning a technique where you could make it an appealing but not obviously overpowered choice for both Solars and Dragon-Blooded. The idea that some people were arguing for - that it should be BETTER for the Dragon-Blooded than for the Solars - means that it's obviously either overpowered for the Dragon-Blooded or underpowered for the Solars, and that, to my mind, is deliberately throwing away one of the best aspects of one of the better systems in 3E.

                                      Re: main post, I don't see a lot of relation to that aggiecon thread, which, to me is really about quite reasonable ideas of whether an MA in 3e can and should be able to have an essence that limits and enables certain kinds of practitioners.

                                      (Well, when it did not become about people rehashing the same arguments they made in the other thread about Dragonblooded MA, seemingly with absolutely no updating their priors based on new information that might contradicted their arguments, in ways that if I were a dev, might make me a little chary about sharing information).

                                      But in the spirit of commenting on the OP itself, I feel like that's the return of one of those memes that turned me off of the 2e-era forums a lot. No offence Totentanz, because you're from that time, and I'm having a flashback to you talking about the same meme back then, and I know seem to remember that you've always been big on the idea that one of the special things about Creation is fundamental unfairness or imbalance of power.

                                      Back to the aggiecon reaction thread, you've got a a few people in that thread (or maybe just Sanctaphrax) who were talking about "Let's not talk about what these characters deserve, or what's fair to them. After all, they're just abstract characters, not people, who deserve rights and recognition and "social justice".".

                                      While this is true in principle, I do feel like this does feel like, there and then, it was a bit of indirection of a underlying tendency that is very uncomfortable about talking in a positive sense about what different splats should be able to achieve, what achievable goals should be when playing them, what should be unique to each of them. If they don't deserve anything, then it's easy to sideline any discussion about what should they should positively definitely be able to do.

                                      Rather than thinking about them purely in a negative sense of how overshadowed they should be, how unfair things should be to them, what they should not be able to achieve that the Solars can, what they'll fail to do that the Solars won't fail to do, how and when they'll lose to the Solar Exalted, how their abilities are lesser than Solar ones, how they're fundamentally inept to make the places and people they love any better or safer.

                                      That's a mentality I remember fairly well. I can't remember the poster, but one 2e era conversation I can remember during a conversation was one in which a discussion about the abilities of different splats came up, and it got to the point where I was saying "OK, less powerful, sure but how much less powerful? What should the odds be?" and the guy I was discussing it with responded with, more or less the attitude that so long as it was less than the Solars, very much less and very obviously less, it didn't really matter. And no one seemed to find it remarkable that there was no real concern with finding a level of ability and capability that suited the splat in question, just "It's balanced and well designed so long as they're very clearly lesser than Solars".

                                      Or worse, some sort of meta reasoning from those ideas, about how the Solar Exalted represent the highest and best and most true parts of human nature, and the highest types of human person, and the only types of people who can lead humanity forward and upward, to which other ways of being and other types of people should be and naturally are subordinated. (Which is just such a narrow, narrow, hierarchical perspective on humanity and our ideals for ourselves, as people, for me).

                                      That's all an incredibly negative perspective and not one I really want to spend a lot of time in and not one that a simple one on one power difference between Solars and others automatically means we have to spend a lot of time in.

                                      The conversation around non-Solar Exalts should be fundamentally positive, about what they can do, and what is unique and true to them and how they won't simply be made irrelevant and how they can contribute and achieve their goals. Then we can season that with perhaps a little negativity about how certain things the Solars can do, they cannot. But it can't be the main dish of talking about them is just how much they can't do things and will lose against the Solars and how much they'll fail and how much they can't do anything that is both unique and desirable for humans.

                                      (That was more tl:dr than I wanted it to be).

                                                          What's usually been a sticking point about Solar Excellence, at least in my circle of friends, is that it's such a broad variety of excellence. Solars are the default splat, the corebook splat, the most played splat, and thus the one that has to be by far the most broadly capable. You couldn't launch the corebook with Sidereals or Abyssals as the default option without drastically changing new players views on the intent of setting and system. So Solars need to be just as good at being Solar Bureaucrats as Solar Martial Artists as Solar Wrestlers as Solar Survivalists. You can't get around this. Solars have been written about having no mechanical downsides (And no, the Great Curse does not count. Everyone has an equivalent), and having only in-setting downsides.

                                                          On top of this broad excellence in all fields, Solars have the most immediately Big Shiny Toys. Solar Circle Sorcery. The Mastery tag. Some lines about being the best at Evocations, but we still don't have enough info to qualify what that means. Biggest motepools. Best excellency (Until you actually look at what the Sid TN fuckery Excellency does).

                                                          But that's fine, as long as other splats have tools Solars can never use/take, right? Solars can't shapeshift like a Lunar can. Solars can't access the final circle of Necromancy like an Abyssal. Solars can't...do whatever the hell Infernals are doing. It's probably unwholesome. Solars can't play Cats Cradle with the strings of fate like a Sidereal. Everyone has SOME tool that Solars can't take, by nature can't even approach them in, let alone outshine them in.

                                                          But to most eyes, DB don't. Dragonblooded do not have any kind of narrative protection towards "This is MY tool. This is MY schtick." And to some, the social benefits of being a Dragonblooded are their actual schtick. Being able to flare without fear of a Wyld Hunt. Being able to announce your status as an Exalt and expect respect instead of (just) fear. Being able to freely own vast quantities of wealth and being able to freely display supernatural skill without fear of reprisal from the largest political entity in the world.

                                                          But no mechanical schtick. Anima Flux? That's stopped by anyone with artifact armor. Tossing elemental bolts? A Brawl Solar can catch yours and toss them back harder. Or duplicate it with a hearthstone or an artifact.

                                                          And I think some people look at the Immaculate Styles and are thinking, well, THAT could be the DB schtick, even though making it the DB schtick is worse for the DB splat than it is for Solars.

                                                          And until we get a preview or the final copy of the DB book and we see some actual fun unique mechanics that make playing a DB as fun as it is with the dice as it is socially, people are going to search for a DB schtick, or react to them not having a schtick by trying to beef them up to the Celestial weight-class.
