    Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
    Lunars mechanically worked decently ; the game worked decently ; honestly, it was pretty good. It was just the horrible, horrible fiction that was bad for them. Super influenced by W:tA in all the wrong ways.
    I've brought up this before when you've made this argument. My experience is the complete opposite from yours. Even through much of their fiction is terrible, I've had countless players really like the idea of playing Lunars. They seem really sold on the idea of being companions to the Solars, to being flexible, shapeshifters etc. However the mechanics let them down terribly, not by being broken like Sidereal or Dragonblooded, but by just not letting them do what they were lead to believe they would be good at. They expected to make No Moon sorcerers, changing moon tricksters etc and go on grand adventures with their Solar companions only to find out their utility began and ended with Shapeshifting+Attribute excellencies because their charms just didn't do anything of note.

    Even if you fixed all the fiction and made it awesome, playing a Lunar with the Ex2 mechanics will never be awesome.

            Well, beyond my insatiable need for tabletop gaming, I ran a MUSH for several years and had quite a bit of mechanical debates during that time, and I can tell you that while Lunars had issues they weren't anywhere near the mess of early 2e Sidereal/Dragonblooded charms. Throw a few houserules on there for the Octopus problem and a few others and we were good to go.

            I find most concerns about Lunar 2e mechanical problems to be highly overblown around here, and that's coming from thousands of xp of Real Play experience. Yeah, they have some stupid charms, but pretty much everybody does/did.

            By the end of 2.5 the main issue with them was simply that the promised 'We Fix Lunars' errata never came. They promised it for, I dunno, like... 1.5 years or something? Then they released the ink monkey pregnancy charms article, which... did not go over well. After that, they essentially gave up in lieu of the imminent drop of e3.

            So Lunars never got the Ink Monkeys repolish that Dragonblooded, Sidereals or Raksha (?) did. Infernals and Solars got significantly more love as well, but Lunars are the red-headed stepchild of the Exalted line it seems.

                                                    Originally posted by Ars Fan View Post
                                                    One of my hopes is that we get our new Lunars before too many years pass. I think that we've already heard that Exigents will come after Dragonblooded. I kind of expect that we'll see Siderieals and Abyssals as fat splats and getimians and liminals as smaller books before we see the Lunars. I wouldn't be amazingly surprised (although I would be somewhat surprised) to see Alchemicals, godblooded or Raksha big books first. [/pessimism]
                                                    There's been no ordered schedule given beyond the first few books, but I got the impression splatbook releases will be partially dependent on applicability to games (e.g. Alchemicals are mostly self-contained, so they're coming at/near the end again, and I think I heard something similar for Infernals). This logic makes sense for releasing Dragonblooded after Solars (Terrestrials ruling the world-straddling empire and all) and Exigents after that (a book that helps one create new Exalts has a ton of mileage*). As EX3 Lunars now occupy large territories in the Threshold, they're a very strong candidate for being next in line.

                                                    For random other release babblings I remember hearing, it was implied that Abyssals and Liminals (Liminals get a hardback) would come out in the same Year in Black. As Abyssals and Liminals are meant to thematically compare/contrast with each other just like Sidereals do with Getimians and Lunars do with a yet unnamed new Exalt, this could suggest a given "duo's" members are adjacent to one another on the release schedule. It's also worth noting that the Fair Folk hardback covers more types of fae than just the raksha, some of which will also be playable. The book's aim is to make them all easier to use both for PCs and STs.

                                                    * Especially remembering that the rules therein will be helpful for not just Exigents, but any homebrewed Exalt, and for maybe planting seeds of things canon splats might use. On those latter points, I fully expect people to create and share homebrewed incarnations of the canon splats before their release. Especially if the mechanics are as friendly to work with as the devs have said they're trying to make it. It's not the same, no, but it will help ease the hunger pangs.
