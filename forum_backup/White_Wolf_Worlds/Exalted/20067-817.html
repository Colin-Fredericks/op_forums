        That's actually a good question. The naval combat rules are kinda written with the assumption of ships maneuvering around each other.

        It might actually be easier to not use the naval combat rules in this case, since Momentum doesn't mean much when one side isn't moving. Track Hull damage for the ship, use (Wits + Sail + Maneuverability)/2 as the ship's Evasion, and use normal combat rules for the siege engine crew fighting against the ship's crew. (I'd allow the ship's crew to use Sail instead of War to aim their ballistas or whatever.)

        This could actually be tough for the ship, since they might not have any large targets to aim at with their own ballistae or whatever.

                    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                    Are there any listed/suggested penalties on Awareness penalties for range? Makes sense to me that noticing a ninja at long range should be harder than at short range.

                    There are no suggested penalties in the books that I know of. I use -1 for Medium, -2 for Long, and -3 for Extreme for a perfectly clear, obstruction-free day. Dust, haze, fog, darkness etc. makes it -1 for Short, -2 for Medium, -4 for Long, and -6 for Extreme. Multiple effects (e.g. it's night and foggy) make the penalties -1 at Close, -2 for Short, -4 for Medium, -6 for Long, and -9 for Extreme.

                            Syndic response question for a 2.5 game pulling from both editions.

                            The Zenith in my campaign wants to make it so that priests are in charge of the cleanliness of Whitewall. Because he figures since the whole city is dedicated to the Unconquered Sun, that the city itself roughly counts as a temple to him, not just the temple manse.

                            In Bastions of the North it says that the Inspectors oversee that area: [quote]There is some overlap between the duties of the judges and the inspectors, but whereas judges administer justice when
                            actual crimes have been committed, inspectors guard civil order and administer municipal policies in subtler areas (like making sure streets are kept clean, seeing that citizens maintain their homes in good repair[quote]

                            In CoTD:North, it says this:
                            Inspectors handle the city government’s finances and paperwork. For physical labor, the appropriate office hires local companies. Some of Whitewall’s largest businesses (apart from farming and mining) perform civic services such as street sweeping, garbage collection and lighting the coal-fired lanterns mounted at intersections of the main streets.
                            In 2nd edition it says roughly that the Syndics favor hard work, and some whitewallers become rich. And that the Syndics give blessings to the wealthy who give to charity. There isn't anything in first edition that hints at that though.

                            Now... functionality wise, this is all fluff I think. Cause mechanically I don't think it matters who cleans a temple so long as it is taken care of. The Syndics probably are aware of this. However, it is Whitewall, where each city block was dedicated to the Unconquered Sun. So... it can't hurt to try this, especially if it warrants more prayers.

                            So... Since the inspectors hire out cleaning to custodial companies, my thoughts are that the Syndics want to try the least disruptive thing. So my guess is that they'd propose having the shift managers of the custodial companies come to be trained in the basics of priestly education so they can teach the prayers to their staff and lead the prayers while conducting the cleaning. That or they pass a law that each custodial company have a priest with them. Maybe also change the uniforms so each custodial outfit has the insignia of the Unconquered Sun and Syndics below it.

                            The Zenith has yet to expend any political capital with the Syndics. So there is no reason to give an outright "no".

                            Does any of that sound like an accurate response that the syndics would give?
