                    From a variance of results perspective vs simplicity, always seems better for mortals to have something like 4 dice and then Exalted mostly to have success adders and not dice adders. Otherwise you get high and very binary variance of results at the very low bound (1 dice). At least the way Storyteller does things.

                    Presuming you're mostly throwing Sux=1 things at mortals, you want them to succeed on the whole, and the gains of exceeding threshold successes are not very large, and then for things that are hard for mortals then you've got Sux=2 (hard but still over 50% of the time?) and Sux=3 (about 20% of the time?). Then you can give trained professionals / etc. at +1 sux, "heroes" at +2 sux.

                    Ideally more dice=>smoother function of successes of course. I get that this may not be as fun from a "Big pools o' dice" perspective for players, and means more rolling for STs than 1 die.

                        Simplifying is great. My players like it.

                        It used the "obscurity system" for simple testing (not extended and not resisted). If difficulty = ability - 2 then success is automatic. BoS5, pg 25-26.

                        I created a turn system for 2ed. Turn = 6 tick (speed of a simple charm), same actions act on the same tick (best initiative first), penalty ends on the remaining ticks of turn (punch without penalty on tick 6 and 2nd circle magic without penalty on ticks 5-6 of second turn).

                        I adapted the techniques of Burn Legend. You bought each type of martial art to use martial art techniques and increased your levels. Attacking with a technique outside of your Martial Art counted only as a Stunt in one of 5 basic techniques.
