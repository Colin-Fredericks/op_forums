        Personally, *my* headcanon about the Jade Prison situation is that they only asked for 50 instead of some more equivalent number because they knew the Deathlords would screw it up, or even personally arranged for it somehow (they did help the Deathlords with the design process every step of the way, after all). There are multiple reasons they'd do this which I find far more satisfying than just the Yozis being uncertain their devised method would work (and thus hedging their bets on how many they asked for), even discounting their massive alien intelligences coming up with different reasoning (and also discounting Ebon Dragon dickery).

        The state of Creation is such that the Solars likely wouldn't actually make it stronger for a long time to come (though they of course will eventually) - in fact, where the Abyssal and Infernal Exaltations are largely secret weapons, the Solars are (from the Yozis' perspective) essentially a huge, obvious, attention-drawing third party striking behind enemy lines. The Realm and the Sidereals of Yu-Shan (the two main current threats to any Yozi freedom plots) made it plainly clear they would spare no expense to wipe out the Solar Anathema if they were revived, and the Yozis capitalized on that to burn Creation out fighting itself.

        Now, whether those Solars would dilute Creation's power enough to counterbalance the potential strength of more deathknights and Green Sun Princes is another question, but as long as the cost to effectiveness ratio is fairly even early on, it's good enough, because they have more priorities to track than just defeating the current forces of Creation in order to free themselves. Mainly that if either the Deathlords or the Fair Folk succeed in dissolving Creation, that's still a lose condition for the Yozis - so limiting the relative power of the Underworld and giving the deathknights enough counterparts to keep them all busy plays into the Yozis' hands, too.

        Last but not least, as creatures intrinsically familiar with the concept of betrayal (it put them where they are now, after all), the Yozis actually have good cause to limit their own servants - that way if, say, all the Ebon Dragons' Infernals follow their patron's lead and collectively turn against the Yozis' cause, the new force opposing them is small enough that the Yozis can use their Akuma and demons (who *aren't* particularly able to resist Yozi orders) to take control of the situation and re-capture the Exaltations (where that quickly becomes a dicey proposition numbers-wise if the potentially traitorous servants are tripled). This is without getting into the fact that such rogue GSPs have the capacity to transcend their Yozi masters entirely, since I haven't been able to imagine up any good reasons the Yozis would go through with the project if they figured that part out beforehand.

        For all of these reasons, I hold that the Yozis intentionally screwed their dead cousins by giving the Deathlords slightly faulty information on how to cleanly break the Jade Prison.
