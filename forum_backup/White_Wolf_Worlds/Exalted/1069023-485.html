                                                            On the subject of not quite grokking certain aspects of social influence, when leveraging an intimacy for a persuade action, do we ignore the intimacy being used as the basis for the influence when checking for intimacy modifiers to Resolve? If I'm trying to convince a local warlord to fight on my side against the realm by exploiting his Major intimacy of friendship to me, does it also automatically reduce his Resolve by 2? There's reason to think the answer is 'no', as it strikes me that the intimacy used for persuade is analogous to the bribe used for bargaining or the bad stuff you use to threaten someone, and it would also make the maximum number of intimacies at affecting any one roll in a social situation line up exactly with the character creation-mandated number of intimacies (one intimacy as the 'leverage', one boosting resolve, one lowering resolve, one used in a decision point). That is, admittedly, reading into things on my part because I sort of like the idea that even if the reason said warlord is even entertaining the notion is that he has a fondness for me, it doesn't excuse me from having to do actual work to figure out what other heartstrings I should be pulling with him.

                                                            And on the subject of heartstrings. how explicitly does influence need to call out a topic before it factors into modifying resolve? Taking my example from before, if the warlord's wife was killed a Realm legion, giving him a tie of grief, would I need to explicitly say 'this is an opportunity to take vengeance for your loss'?
