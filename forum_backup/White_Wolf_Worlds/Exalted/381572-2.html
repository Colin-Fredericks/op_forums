        I was actually thinking about this earlier. Here's my thoughts.

        Lytek isn't just a generic god of Exaltation; he interacts with Exaltations in specific ways. In particular, his domain seems very focused on dealing with the eternal reincarnation aspect of Exaltation and the memories associated with it. Therefore, I would probably have an Exigent of Lytek focus primarily on that particular aspect of Exaltations.

        They would have powers to help others recover memories and (temporarily or as a form of training) skills from past lives. They'd have charms to identify past lives and gather information using them, to track Exaltations through the ages and sense or manipulate the connections between them. They could help other Exalted unlock their true potential in various ways, often by using past-life memories as a form of training. Their social charms could focus on evoking or manipulating intimacies from past lives. And so on.

        Possibly this focus on reincarnation could be part of how the Exigent's exaltation works. It should reincarnate itself, probably, but beyond that it might choose hosts whose mortal souls had been Exalted or otherwise exceptional in a past life, and allow them to access some of the skills and power from that past (not, of course, generally in the form of actual Exalted charms -- except maybe a small list of whitelisted stuff -- but things like being able to develop their own Exigent-of-Lytek swordsmanship charms based around a thematic of calling on swordsmanship skill from a past life as an Exalt.)

        They'd probably also have a few charms related to the process of Exaltation or for interacting with the Exalted, mostly informational in nature (because manipulating Exaltations directly is generally not doable) -- charms to imprecisely detect people who are likely to Exalt, charms to sense the presence of the Exalted and so on.

        Of course, they should have some charms to empower mortals -- akin to the Solar charm Power Awarding Prana in some ways, maybe -- since that's central enough to the thematic of Exaltation that you couldn't really make an Exigent of Lytek without it.

                                    Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                    Since the Solar Lore charm that turn a person into an Enlightened Mortal require you to commit a shit load of motes, you just need to slap this on Lytek's Exigent's charm to prevent him from mass producing a magic army.
                                    It might be boring to copy Power-Awarding Prana exactly, of course. (And also, it's not, I think, a charm that sees much use, whereas empowering mortals should be one of the defining things you do.)

                                    One alternative would be to limit the number of mortals they can empower at once, then give them an entire charm tree for interacting with these empowered proteges. You'd also have to answer the question of what, exactly, these mortals get -- you can't give them Chosen of Lytek charms, I think, for a variety of reasons. But limiting the number you can have at once solves the main problem you get (where people can just grow in power limitlessly by empowering unlimited servants) -- then you just have to factor the total power of the empowered disciples it can produce into the charm itself.

                                    Also, one major difference from Power-Awarding Prana should be that you can't revoke your choice of a mortal (since that's how Exaltation works, and this is based on its themes.) You're stuck with the disciples you empower, at least until they die.

                                                        Man, I don't know if I agree with that.

                                                        Ever played SMITE? It's a free MOBA (Massive Online Battle Arena) in which gods from different pantheons are all beating the poop out of each other. My friends always teased me because they had better Kill / Death / Assist ratios -- and the better those are, the more xp and gold you get, allowing you to power up faster and thus kill even more! BUT to me (and the mechanics of the game) - 10, 100, 1000 kills won't actually win you a match. The end-goal are the Objectives (first destroy the defensive Towers -- then you can reach and kill the enemy teams' Titan).

                                                        So I'll play Aphrodite (whose chief abilities involve healing herself & her partner ; stunning enemies with a kiss (and giving your partner a damage bonus, due to jealousy) ; improving you & your partners speed and mana regen ; providing temporary immunity to both of you ; and one negligible damage ability. She's PURE SUPPORT. My K/D/A ratio is terrible. But my partner runs around like a Lunar DBT on Cocaine thanks to me.

                                                        Or I play Hel (whose chief abilities involve AoE heals, damage to extras, a few other buffs) and purchase pure AoE items (favoring empowering friends over myself) and they grudgingly bitch a lot less (because none of them are going to offend the person keeping them alive).

                                                        Or the old Nu Wa kit -- which was predominantly a PVE one. Early game, I'd get curb-stomped and the strangers with the higher kills would disparage me -- but late game, I often did more Siege Damage (against Towers & Titan - the goals of winning) than the entire Enemy Team (5) and the rest of my team (4) combined. While everyone else fights battles, I fight the battleground and reshape the flow of the fight (and with each fallen Tower, my allies are getting boosts to gold and moral too).

                                                        TLDR Version:

                                                        People who enjoy Support roles find it very challenging -- very fun -- and more interesting than being another SUPER KILLBOT PRINCESS.

                                                        Hell, I designed an entire Devil Tiger around the "Defend Other" keyword and "Intimacies" --- an entire kit that would only work to benefit Intimacies (even if they're enemies) or with huge vulnerabilities (so even a hated enemy could bypass defenses or ignore offensive charms in this kit). It was fun -- support roles require a lot more planning and countering than mere "I spend 2 motes, I stab him dead".
