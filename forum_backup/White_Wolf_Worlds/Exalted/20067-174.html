                    Originally posted by Jen View Post
                    Can somebody explain how Ready in Eight Directions for me ? So you active the charm on your turn, but due to system transparency nobody bother to attack you, which make you waste both your turn and your motes...?
                    It is situational, but being able to tell your enemies that they just aren't allowed to attack you for a full round can be very handy, and it conveniently costs the same amount of essence as you regain per round so you're not actually losing any essence while using it. The more consistently powerful use is to combine it with War Lion Stance. It triggers on every attack you attempt to parry, not every attack aimed at you, so whenever your allies get attacked you can reflexively use defend other and counterattack. That way you can either shut down all your enemies for a full round or throw out a whole barrage of high-damage decisive counterattacks.

                                    Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                    Can somebody explain how Ready in Eight Directions for me ? So you active the charm on your turn, but due to system transparency nobody bother to attack you, which make you waste both your turn and your motes...?
                                    You've got the right idea. I haven't tried it out yet, but it's been a fairly situational power in every edition for that reason. It's a very defensive charm, so it doesn't really work well if your goal is to be the killstick -- it's about buying time for allies (or environmental hazards, or poison, or whatever) to turn the tide. You use it when not attacking you is a dramatically inferior option, and the time pressure is on enemies rather than you. I would say the main limiting factor is not that, but the fact that it's only useful if you expect to be attacked at close range multiple times under normal circumstances.

                                    It's used most commonly in tandem with a tanking build, I think. However, the threat of it can also be worthwhile as a method of locking down a large number of opponents, or one with a scary multiple hit charm like Hammer on Iron/Fivefold Fury Onslaught, while allies wither with ranged attacks, charge up sorcery, or retreat to safety.

                                                        Immaterial beings can sense material ones in all the usual ways, but they do indeed have very few ways to affect them. This is because being immaterial is extremely powerful, and it'd be brokenly good if it were easy to influence material beings while in that state. QC powers aren't going to be super-rigorous in that respect, but you should generally assume they can't affect the material world in ways that involve physical contact or for others to sense them unless they say so. Keep in mind, though, that immateriality isn't just a hard "mechanical barrier": it prevents you (or your equipment, or projectiles you throw or create, and so on) from interacting with people physically, but if you have some way of affecting them that doesn't require that, you're good to go.

                                                        Apparition would allow any social influence you can convey by being visible, since social influence is based on people being able to sense you rather than physically interact with you. (I infer that it also allows you to speak, in which case it would let you do all the normal things, but if you rule otherwise it'd be limited to body language.) Similarly, you could socially interact with someone using Spirit-Detecting Glance.

                                                        Chilling Touch: Nah, I would not interpret this as saying that it can attack immaterial beings. "Ethereal" isn't generally used in that way in Exalted, so I assume it just means it makes it spooky and magical. On the other hand, the war-ghost is a ghost, so it could just get out of the armour and punch an immaterial being in the face.

                                                        Honourable Descendent Blessing: This I think is meant to work when immaterial, since it doesn't require any sort of physical contact or projectile -- you just need to see the person. In general, powers that work invisibly without physical interaction, like the Disease Spirit's Contagion-Honing Methodology, can probably be assumed to work when immaterial. Curse of the Dead probably does require Apparition so the target can notice the threaten attempt, but I imagine that's all.

                                                        Notice that in general, the powers that do seem like they'd work when immaterial tend to be subtle blessings, curses or portents, rather than direct intervention or physical harm.
