                    Meh. There's a difference between sexual, sexy and sexualized. The first two are usually ok, the last is a great deal trickier. Taking your expected target audience into account, and the purpose of your art, seems to be key here. What flies in a porn magazine aimed at straight men obviously does not in Teen Vogue. Context matters, fancy that.

                    Anyway, sexiness without objectification is not some hypothetical or abstract concept. Let me assure you us women enjoy looking at and playing sexy characters as much as men (if not more, but that is different question). Sex also sells to us. Really, it does. That said, and coming back to tabletop rpg's, if you want me to flip through your book and be inspired and fired up to make a character, good artwork for that would be a woman who is sexy as well as looks as if she has agency, motivation, personality, dignity and autonomy. You know, just like the male characters are supposed to be. The usual vacant-eyed, slack-jawed floozy, impossibly bent so both her breasts and ass are prominently visible, passively doing nothing, yeah, no, does nothing except making me wonder if I'm even the target audience for this product.

                    Make your artwork to inspire concepts, illustrate your imaginary world and/or highlight what's going on in the text. In games like Vampire and Exalted sexy is really not out of place, but you can titillate without objectifying. Also, assume the person holding the book can be woman, man, or anything in between, with an equally wide range of sexual preferences, and provide a power fantasy for all. That is your context nowadays, so embrace it.
