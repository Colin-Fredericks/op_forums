                    Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
                    Not sure the norm would be to marry between the Great Houses, as that would in my opinion remove to much of the tension between them. Female and male Dynasts are to equal. It had been another thing if the woman was married off, and then counts as a member of the new family.
                    On the other hand, the Empress wants *just* enough tension between Houses, not too much, and cross-House alliances supported by marriage (and cross-House Sworn Brotherhoods, which are the default) are a way of making sure that there are enough connections between Houses to keep tensions from spiralling out of control. (Houses do tend have particular Houses that they tend to marry into as well, reflecting common alliances.) From a gaming and realism perspective, this provides a lot of the Byzantine social complexity that's needed in order to prevent Dynastic politics from being too clear-cut, as it means that characters have personal ties of loyalty and honour beyond those of their Houses, which also reflects how these things operate in RL.

                    From a historical perspective, for instance, most of the Welsh Royal Houses intermarried with each other, with the Irish, and with the Normans, and it really didn't stop them from having extremely tense relationships with each other or going to war.

                                                    I agree with Isator, probably both. Plus the fact that Dragon-blood are the sort who go out and do things, not just sit on their arses collecting rent money, reinforcing this kind of culture.

                                                    Also, what with the way stipends (and the 2nd ed Arsenal background) work, I think probably an awful lot of property belongs to the House, not individuals. I note that the Houses, in 2e at least, had quite discrete areas of the Isle, not the patchwork randomness you got with medieval dynasties. House Nellens mostly live together in a giant sprawling manor (at least in 2e... I think that's kind of weird, honestly, but anyway).
                                                    So these indicate to me that a lot of Dynasts, while they do own stuff themselves, don't own as much as you might think, as the buildings they live in, the businesses they run, etc, belong to the House. So, if a senior male banker in House Ragara dies, the money he's made is split between his Pelleps kids, but he was mostly making money for his House, which stays in-House.
                                                    Essentially, Houses are corporations, and can own things* by themselves.

                                                    *Pre-modern laws are generally a mess of tradition, de-facto effects, and unwritten rules. It's possible that some Houses work like this and some don't. I could especially see the houses run by their founders as actually consisting of property belonging to their founder. So, Mnemon herself actually owns everything in House Mnemon, Ragara owns everything in Ragara, etc. Meaning that upon their deaths, the economics of inheritance will be rather messy.
