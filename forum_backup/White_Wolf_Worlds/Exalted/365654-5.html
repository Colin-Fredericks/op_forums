    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
    I have to say, I really feel like this is at least as much the game's doing as the community's. On the whole, it's been more consistent about presenting Essence as conforming to scientific rationalism than not, it put nearly all the Essence powers in the "science" ability, it emphasized the technological aspects of Essence use (e.g. things like automatic lights that turn on when you spend Essence) and presented devices analogous to modern technology that run on "essence", it often depicted savants as being analogous to modern scientists, and -- subtly but I think most importantly -- it framed "motes" as an in-setting construct and encouraged people to think about their mechanical resources in that light. Compare D&D or Vampire, which typically refer to such things as "points" or "levels" even when they are broadly analogous to setting elements, to emphasize that they're mechanical abstractions. This isn't just line drift -- it reached new heights of ostentation with DotFA, but it's been that way since early 1e.

            Originally posted by AnubisXy
            This doesn't seem like rocket science to and it does feel like the sort of thing that genius Exalted in the First Age, in their quest to understand Exaltation and their own powers might have done.
            I feel like Nobilis had a good way to articulate a point for a similar issue to this, in which it said that the language characters use to articulate their miracles is probably not the same straightforward language the players use. Like, the player of the Power of Extinction might state "I cause all tigers to die out", while the character articulates the process with something more complex and arcane, the language that a human might use to communicate to an Estate, to preserve the idea that what is happening in character is more significant than the interface players use to control it. Among other implications I would think this would have would be the idea that, in character, different people using the same Charms actually aren't expending the same amount of energy, partially for recognisable, if immeasurable factors of contextual factors (like, the Melee 5 person using Hungry Tiger Technique against a mortal might not be using as much energy as the Melee 2 one fighting a Dragon Blood, even if the mechanics use the same amount of motes, and also when things like the ambient temperature of each are different), and partially for more vague, abstract, spiritual qualities (that we describe out of character with things like stunts and favoured Abilities). Mostly though, even if you did have something like an Essence box, I would say that the functions and observations in character would model the results in a manner that would view the thing in a different manner from a discrete Essence pool, and that would not be able to tell everything about the quality of a person that mechanics describe with an Essence pool. It's about the same as how in the World of Darkness, characters would not look upon a person and how certain actions seem to boost their emotional and physical energy and conclude the existence of Virtues and Willpower dots. Or how neither game has the actual probabilities of any given action align with the probabilities of dice pools. These are things that, if we want to imagine those worlds as being anything like our own, should be more complex to the people observing and experiencing them than they are with the game systems to represent them for the sake of entertainment. Personally, I would have it that the way physical and chemical processes work in Creation, and how Essence means that there is an interplay between the symbolic and spiritual and literal and concrete, that a developed scientist will look more like a Medieval or Renaissance Hermetic than a modern empiricist, where all of that theistic and mystical language is more than an interestingly aligned but ultimately unnecessary prop. Or like in Thor 2 where their observations of quantum mechanics are heavily ensconced in the concept of the soul.

                Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

                Generally speaking, it should be possible to know that Spirit A has a bigger pool of Essence than Spirit B, and that Solar A has a bigger Essence Pool than Solar B. It should even be possible, using the correct charms, to calculate how large someone's Essence pool is, how many motes it costs to fuel a given charm, and therefore how often someone can use a charm.

                And let's be clear - you don't even really need the concept of motes to calculate Essence pools.

                I mean, I can easily imagine Sally Twilight in the First Age making an Artifact box, which you can channel Essence into.

                "Oh, look. I just filled 5 of these boxes with my Essence, and I can't fill a 6th one. That must mean I have 5 boxes worth of Essence. Dawn Caste Dave over there was able to fill 7 boxes worth of Essence."

                And, rather than calculating Essence pools by motes (which are an abstraction), they can calculate Essence pools by "boxes" (out of game, each of which house 10 motes of Essence), which are discrete things in the game. So even if Sally doesn't know she has 50 motes, and indeed the word mote doesn't exist, she does know she has 5 boxes of Essence, Dave has 7 boxes of Essence, and she can begin performing calculations.

                And if that doesn't work, imagine them making identical artifacts and seeing how many of these one person can attune to before they can't attune to anymore (as their Essence pool is now empty).

                I can also imagine similar scenarios where Sally Twilight teaches Dave the same charm she knows, and then the two use the charm the same number of times to calculate Essence. If the charm is a 1 mote cost charm (the smallest amount of Essence you can spend) then they can get a rough count of how many motes (or boxes or artifacts attuned) they each possess.

                This doesn't seem like rocket science to and it does feel like the sort of thing that genius Exalted in the First Age, in their quest to understand Exaltation and their own powers might have done. "Even though Sally and Dave are both using the same charm, why can Dave use that charm more than Sally can? We know Charms run off of Essence. So maybe he has more Essence? Let's find out."
                So this is a natural direction to take things, and one I've seen in a lot of discussions of tabletop settings. But I think there's an important question to be asked, first:

                In your opinion, do the game mechanics of the mote pool translate 1:1 into the setting?

                Like, what really snapped this into focus for me was this example earlier in the thread:
                Originally posted by phalamir View Post
                When you can sit there and watch Joe Solar lift 30 tons exactly 7 times an hour
                Because, the thing is, folks who do weight training have a certain number of reps they can do of this or that exercise, and a certain amount of time they have to wait before doing it again. But those aren't actually integers, nor are they actually natural law, or even 100% consistent; they're emergent properties of the way the body is put together, which manifest differently per person, per day, per specific situation. Some folks are better at push-ups than sit-ups, or vice versa. Or better at bursts of activity vs endurance training. Or maybe they can do 100 reps on a bad day but 140 on a good one.

                Even if you were to write a tabletop RPG where feats of strength are an important part of the expected challenges a character would face, this is all, all too fine of detail to include in the system if you want it to be playable. You'd probably just have a bar, that fills based on rest and recuperation, and empties on exertion, and if you get all the way to the bottom you get tired and have to stop.

                And yet, when we talk about mote pools I see a bunch of people assuming that no, it's not a metaphysical version of "getting tired and having to stop", these really are integers, they really are exactly the same from person to person and replenish at set rates and so forth. And I don't really see why.

                I mean, admittedly, yes, even if motes are a game abstraction, you can and will still get people IC trying to measure their relative strength, testing it out, and so forth. But I think they'd bump into the same kinds of subtleties as you find IRL with trying to quantify people's spending and recovering of physical, mental, or emotional energy in their everyday life, even given everything we know about how the human body works. These are emergent traits and don't operate on a level that you will ever find an IC measurement that maps to the ratio between Alice's mote pool and Betty's, because that ratio is a game abstraction that, in order to make the game playable, masks elements that are below the resolution of the system.

                    For that earlier question of "how many joules is Essence worth", I feel as though the most accurate answer would be that characters in Creation could easily devise a unit to fit into an equation to model how much work a body (as in the physics term) is doing, and they would use that to describe the output of everyone from a baby to an Exalt, and would not actually equate that to the idea that there's a pool of Essence inside of you which is being used up in the course of doing that work. I mean, if you want to calculate the energy it would take to use a Melee Charm to cut through an oak door, that makes sense, but characters would not view the difference between the Charm-enhanced attack and a regular one that would fail to cleave the door as the energy value of the mote cost of the Charm, they'd view it as the difference between trying hard enough and not doing so. Being Exalted is why you're capable of mustering up this greater degree of energy, but it probably amounts to about the same as why one could, say, perform better when in a personally meaningful and relatively low stress situation; those thing don't have an empirical value, they're just a context in which you can get the most out of yourself.
