                        Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                        I just feel that it's arbitrary as hell for no reason.
                        And what about the reasons that I described, both in terms of the rules, and the underling significance to world-building and characterisation?

                        Originally posted by Epimetheus
                        A dragon blooded who wants to be their perfected self shouldn't have to deal with celestial workings when nothing of not screams that it's impossible.
                        Ehh, that's kind of the thing with Dragon Blooded though, isn't it? That there being the least powerful means that they have to deal with more things that are not precisely fair than the Celestial Exalted do. The fact that they're a sizable community, and one in which the subject of their reproduction is a significant deal, makes the disposition of queer people more pronounced, and makes it a subject in which the limits of their magic would distinctly be represented.

                        When it comes to fantasy worlds and marginalized people, it's a difficult balance to determine whether to err on a side that says "the idealised and fantastical qualities of the setting mean that many of your real life issues are easily dealt with to the point of non-existence" or "magic can help cope, and society might be a good deal more accommodating, but it can still be a bit of a struggle".

                        Clearly, the queer people who worked on the book went on the side that the most commonly available magic doesn't quite overcome that limitation for the least powerful Exalted. I can see having arguments against it, but I feel as though they should still respect the difficult position that they're in and any underlying artistic integrity.

                        That being said, I'll also note (in the argument against it being arbitrary) that this subject is neither the only nor the first time in which Vance has suggested that some of the things encompassed by Terrestrial workings ought to require a higher level when something about their scope or specificity is increased.

                        For instance, by a basic reading of some of the descriptions of sample Terrestrial workings, it should only be at that level that you could make a cog that would rotate indefinitely, without requiring any external power input. The potential industrial ramifications of this to the setting are quite dramatic. I'll bet that if one asked Vance what kind of sorcery should be required to imbue an object with such a property, he would place it above the Terrestrial level.
