    I think in the past, I've been less critical of Sidereal Charms than I could've been - partly because Jenna Moran is awesome, so Sidereal Charms are pretty awesome and shiny, in her characteristically conceptually novel way. And they do hit the Sidereal inspirations really well.

    Nonetheless:

    1) Agree that they could do with more iconic Charms that focus on how they operate through foresight and subtle influence over the world that can look like good fortune or chance (or even talent), while keeping interesting choices and restrictions, and that their Charms maybe could feel less like sorcery (like Embracing Life Method feels a lot like a magician doing a spell, not so much like using skill over Fate's essence and nature).

    Their default seems supposed to be fairly subtle, not overt sorcerors who can operate in quite a disguised manner. Their brief to be ostentatiously sorcerous bureaucratic angels of Chinese heaven has to be handled with their brief to be covert seers.

    I see that you can view this to be like kung fu and imperial agent styled version of the Traveller in Black - at first they're this innocuous little man and everything's a bit odd, then sooner or later it become obvious they're throwing down world shaping magic to reaffirm order's hold against chaos. I just have this feeling they feel a bit like sorcerors more than having power over Fate sometimes.

    2) I think also that when you look at the Caste summaries of the Sidereals, they are substantially heavy on information there about how the Sidereals helped to build the First Age civilization, rebuild the world in the wake of the Great Contagion, facilitate the Usurpation in unique Caste appropriate ways, etc.

    Rebuilding this civilization is not, exactly, what 3e games are totally focused on. Yet, I feel like their existing set kind of isn't strong on any feeling of how they would go about this.

    Like the Chosen of Battles write up says "The Shieldbearers advised the Dawn Caste generals ... using the Chosen's visions of the future to place troops with an uncanny wisdom". Are the "Battle Plan" Charms, or Battles Charms in general, sufficient to model this well? They seem good at forcing people to do what they want, and at uncanny magical blessings on the battlefield but not very useful at actually advising people.

    Or for Serenity, "During the First Age, the Chosen of Serenity advised the Solar Deliberative on the health and well-being of Creation's millions of mortals... examin(ing) the Loom of Fate for outbreaks of misfortune and spreading dissatisfaction ... wove destinies of joy to bless the efforts to restore harmony".

    How well do the Secrets Charms support the idea of them managing the process of discovery and occlusion of knowledge their Caste summary provides? Which trees of their actually feel appropriate in helping them do this? Etc.

    Their magic (whether Charms or astrology) should surely actually support this stuff, as a primary purpose?

    It feels like each tree is hung up on making a strong, defining statement (Ride is about using people to get to where you're going, etc.), which is awesomely cool, and leaving the audience a little puzzled about how to achieve some goals (solving which puzzle is supposed to be part of their fun), but each tree sometimes seems to be fairly loosely informed by what the Sidereals are actually doing in changing and reshaping the world and to what purpose they do so.

    I think I mostly have some echo of Wizard of Oz's comment here, except I think it's better to focus on their ability to achieve the Caste goals through their power to have foresight, to advise, and to bless and curse. With focusing on them as generic secret masters without really describing how this links to Caste goals, I think there's already a lot of fans who are hostile to them as people who secretly control and dominate others while really achieving nothing, and that's not really something to emphasise further.

            Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
            If only we had some kind of opportunity to rewrite the Sidereal Charmset from first principles, drawing inspiration from everything cool about them and marrying them to new, better mechanics while correcting the errors of the past. Nah, that's pretty unlikely.

            Personally I'm not worried about the mechanical quality of charms you will develop.

            But there are deep patterns in the charm trees (yeah when it comes to sids, patterns are always involved somewhere) they may very well linger in 3rd ed. And it is the narrowing of the trees theme to the associated maiden's purview.

            Most people think the opposite because they see a few charms mechanically out of the core themes of a tree, like a charm that not only helps resist poison but also redirects it to an enemy in resistance.

            The truth is sids charms, contrary to solars, are mainly tied to the maiden's purview and very loosely tied to the ability. And this is where the problem starts :
            25 abilities cover more efficiently a wider area of skills and crafts than the 5 maiden's purviews, and what makes things worse is that charms have a "double allegiance" to both the maiden as a general theme and to the ability as a means of expression of the power, though in a few cases it can be a very loose tie.

            The basic idea is great, both for fluff and as a concept, but one of the consequences are :
            the medicine charm tree, being focused on the ending aspect, has almost no real healing power, and the pinacle charm has to really stretch the concept of ending (finding a soon-to-die spirit and make a deal where he gives healing to avoid his fate) to do the bloody job. Making sids one of the most laughable physicians/surgeons and possibly less qualified than terrestrials and other lesser essence users it it weren't for their TN excellency.

            there is no "ammo" charm for thrown, because it doesn't fit travels. Instead (in 2.5) you use on of travel's sub theme to send a "messenger" represent the weapon while keeping the actual weapon in your hand, while similarly archery could not have a similar effect.

            So to clarify my wish for 3rd ed :

            It would be really nice to add a few charms in each tree that would express the view of the 4 other maidens about that particular ability, even if you have to leave them on a secondary tree not related to the pinacle charm and the associated scripture.

            The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.

                                        Ever since I first read it, I have loved Avoiding the Truth Technique....in theory.

                                        After I learned a bit more about how social combat and stunting works, however, I realized something: it does literally nothing. You have to stunt your truth-that-is-to-be-seen-as-a-lie to justify using the Charm.                                         Avoiding the Truth Technique doesn't even add dice to the roll, not that it would be all that great if it did, because excellencies do that.
