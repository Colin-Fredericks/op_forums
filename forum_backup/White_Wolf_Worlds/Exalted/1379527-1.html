                            In the Chronicle I'm currently in my character has an adolescent son. Early on, he was something to round out my character concept.

                            Once we all exalted as Solars and it was getting more difficult to obscure our identities as Solars my character's son has become far more central to her story (and by extension the Circle's story). As a result of a poor choice to fight a Fair Folk cataphract she was left with a derangement (thanks to my GM applying a mental crippling wound rather than a physical one) concerning her son's safety. When the Circle fled their kingdom's Capital (hours before it was invaded by a Prasadan Wyld Hunt) she brought her son along all the while looking for a place of refuge to secret him while the Circle looked to grow stronger, build alliances and power base, and avoid detection by the invading Immaculates.

                            Having found a safe haven she left her boy in the care of a village protected by a powerful spirit. The Circle then ventured to Rathess to consolidate their power and collect needed resources to liberate their kingdom. Unbeknownst to my character her business partner in the Rathesian venture hired a sorcerer of some power to protect her son (and partly as leverage over her devouring his soul just in case she actually was a demon infested Anathema). Ultimately, the sorcerer sold her son to a pirate band to settle her business partner's debts.

                            Once we concluded our business in Rathess and returned home we discovered these facts and my character was frantic in eking out her son. A little legwork led the Circle to locate and negotiate with the pirate band which had subsumed a number other, smaller bands including the one "protecting" her son. The negotiator was fairly direct and she got her son back but not after he had spent half a year or more in the presence of various unsavoury types and exposed to all manner of bloodthirsty, vicious, violent, and depraved acts.

                            All this leaves her with an emotionally scarred child with likely abandonment and separation issues on her hands who is having to also come to terms with the fact that his mother and her friends are Anathema. Set this against the backdrop of keeping a low profile while reinserting into society in the Capital -- a Capital occupied by a foreign army.

                            So, yes dependants can drive and enrich stories. You don't have to be a murder hobo to have fun. Even my character having a single dependent (though she has employees too -- followers) makes the story more complex for the three murder hobos in her Circle.
