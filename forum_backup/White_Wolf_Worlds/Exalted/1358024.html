    Ecorur Prefecture - White Elephant for Isator Levi

    12-31-2019, 12:45 PM
    White Elephant for Isator Levi who wanted
    "I wish for a plot hook in the style of a prefecture from The Realm about some mundane trade guild like butchers or candle makers that is involved in a scheme fitting to the setting to either make itself extremely rich and influential or leverage already acquired wealth and influence from things like cartel practices to advance a political agenda? I recommend they read a bit about the Cabochien Revolt to have an idea of what I'm getting at."
    Ecorur Prefecture

    On the Western slope of the Imperial Mountain, nestled along the northern border of Lord’s Crossing Dominion, rests Ecorur Prefecture. While official maps paint the Prefecture as round, its inhabitants know it to be shaped like a grasping hand. Four cities stretch up the Mountain, fed by the city of Ecorur beneath. Here, the finest iron is mined from the Mountain, refined into grand steel in Ecorur.

    Ecorur is managed by House Nellens, another of its many and varied investments. The Prefecture has historically been peaceful, requiring minimal oversight by its Dragon Blooded masters. This, combined with its unappealing location and mundane industry, has resulted in the tourism that typically brings young Exalts to be all but absent.

    Ecorur

    The city of Ecorur is located at the base of the Imperial Mountain and is the gateway to the only safe paths up to the mining cities above. As such, it serves as the center of trade, exporting the excellent metal the Prefecture is famous for and importing the food and supplies needed to maintain its people. What few Exalted deign to visit Ecorur Prefecture tend to stop here, satisfied with seeing the finished product and avoiding the dull clamor of the mines.

    The governor of Ecorur, Nellens Oma, is an old mortal Dynast of little ambition. She ensures that the Prefecture pays its taxes on time and causes no trouble, and in turn exerts only a minimal presence in the daily lives of her subjects. She is regarded fondly by her people, who make full use of their relative freedom from interference.

    The Pact

    Ecorur Prefecture’s isolation, resources and benign neglect have created a perfect storm. While Oma is a kind and indulgent governor, the guildmasters of Ecorur know that she has little time remaining and they may not be so fortunate with the next one. They have been searching for a way to increase their own power and prestige for years, and the coming civil war has provided them with the opportunity at last.

    In recent years, Ecorur has exported ever greater supplies of steel and iron with the curious oddity of insisting on being paid in actual jade rather than scrip. Those desiring the Prefecture’s supplies are more than willing to overlook this, focusing on the coming war rather than an unexpected change in a minor Prefecture. So far, none have uncovered the reason for it.

    This reason lies below the Imperial Mountain. The Mountain Folk have made contract with Ecorur Prefecture, offering their help in extracting the metal in return for supplies of pure Jade. The people of Ecorur happily accepted, extracting more iron to sell for more jade to trade for even greater assistance. This contract is illegal, being a deal between mortals and mountain folk without the approval of the Immaculates. However, there is a goal beyond mere wealth to this dangerous operation. The Mountain Folk have recently demanded an incredible amount of Jade, offering an artifact of impossible value: an axe that carries within it an Exigence. With that power, the people of Ecorur could deal with any governor Nellens cared to send. The guildmasters have arranged for a final gambit to secure the needed Jade, manipulating the market to do so. There is great risk of discovery, but they are so close to their goal that none dare stop.

                          There are three major things that stand out to me.

                          I'm not quite sold on the idea of them being able to demand payment in jade. The text seems to treat the issue very casually as a minor curiosity, but the matter of jade exclusivity has always stood out to me, and something like this would be both a flagrant violation of Realm law and a major challenge to the social and economic order. After all, jade is the exclusive province of the Dragon Blooded, and I should certainly feel as though mortals (especially peasants?) making demands on one of those provinces should at the very least entail more complications.

                          I'm not getting the strongest sense of their motives, or the extent of their conspiracy. I get that they're looking to preserve some form of status quo, but I don't really see a clear picture of what the society that has been going on behind this inattentive governor is actually like, so I'm not sure what the status quo they're trying to preserve is. Without that, I feel it ends up reading as though they're taking a lot of risks and flirting with treason and blasphemy for relatively minor concerns of being used to an easygoing administrator; certainly, bringing in an illicit Exigent seems like the thing that would bring even more attention to them. I'm also unsure of their worldview; they feel confident in demanding jade because of recognising the prospect of civil war, but also kind of like they expect the appointment of a new governor to take place as normal?

                          While thinking about what they seem to be doing to engage in market manipulation, my mind went to the point that it reads as though they're giving all of the jade away to the Mountain Folk. That would certainly alter the market, but in a manner that is... kind of completely economically ruinous.

                          Here's my reasoning: the iron manufacturing has been out forward as their primary industry, the main source of revenue for the prefecture (and one with no small amount of overhead, as iron refinement is costly in fuel and other raw materials). It reads as though they're giving all of the proceeds of sales directly away to the Mountain Folk, meaning the value of all that work goes straight out of the local economy. But running the industry still costs money, and now that can only come from out of pocket, and it doesn't exactly sounds like even the local big shots ever had very deep pockets to begin with.

                          Furthermore, the prefecture is established as being dependent on imports for food; I should personally imagine they also need to bring in cloth and fuel, but let's stick to food because it was mentioned. That's going to mean that even more money is pouring out of society for the sake of an absolute necessity, and that money is also coming out of pocket. Food shipments surely need to be made regularly, and is time goes by they're going to have to get smaller as there's just less money to pay for them.

                          Dwindling money supply combined with growing for shortages is a recipe for prices to skyrocket; first of food, and then for all other trades and services because people are spending less on them when more and more of their meagre accounts needs to go towards food (which has the knock-on effect from all the people involved in those businesses spending less). Even if fuel isn't imported, it's going to become harder to come by (especially since the ironworks are likely to want and be in the best position to get most of it), and on a mountain slope I imagine fuel is going to be a regular necessity; I see people that are going to start burning their furniture for warmth.

                          That's before even getting to rents and taxes, payments that are kind of non-negotiable to the point that people are surely going to at some point have to forego food to make them. Even so, anybody whose income is based on collecting rents is also going to go down, and for taxes...

                          It all strikes me as something that is going to draw a lot of attention and being highly suspicious. That people are going to observe this prefecture is more productive and exporting more than ever, and yet if somehow becoming highly impoverished. Maybe the governor is inattentive, maybe even doesn't leave the compound enough to notice all the shops that are becoming shuttered up, but surely somebody on staff takes notice that the budget for the kitchens keeps going up and some people seem to be trying to figure their taxes. I know that being at risk of discovery is stated, but I feel it would be a bit more acute and is intimately tied to severely growing hardship.

                          And don't get me wrong, that kind of thing is an interesting source of compelling conflict, just that I think it would be the kind of thing addressed and elaborated a bit upon in the text. Or if it's not intended that they're depressing their economy, a clarification on the terms of the deal with the Mountain Folk or any other steps taken against it. I think something clearing up the previous point about greater insight into their society would help here, particularly as regards the extent of their conspiracy and what and how people feel ensured in it; something to safeguard against the first Immaculate monk who comes by with deep pockets to buy up all the rice and just give it away to the neediest while enquiring about how exactly things got like this.

                          I'm sorry I'm probing the piece so much, these are just the ways in which I'll process things like these. The last in particular coming from recent exposure to a lot of media concerned with stuff like that playing out in real life.

                              Thanks for the feedback. I'll start by saying I deliberately kept it to a more shallow look at the prefecture, staying with the 4-5 paragraph examples given in the Realm book. So some of the more precise information - like how they get fuel or the exact extent of the conspiracy - were left out. This both keeps it to a more reasonable size and allows for anyone using it to fill in to fit their needs.

                              I didn't go into how they managed to get paid in jade much, because it's my headcanon that demanding actual jade is becoming more common as the Realm destabilizes. I realize this probably isn't canon, it was just an unexamined assumption that got through.

                              The weaknesses you pointed out are absolutely there, and largely on purpose. The Cabochien revolt fell apart very rapidly once things started going against them, and I wanted to recreate that feeling of desperate running-in-front-of-an-avalanche danger. If they can pull it all off, they will have a chance at achieving practical independence. But there are so many things that can go wrong, and ideally this leaves plenty of room for PCs to run interference on the Dynasty or for Dynasts to pick at threads and watch the entire thing unravel in a satisfyingly impressive manner.

                              Overall, I'm sorry if I didn't go into enough detail in the writeup. This is very much outside my usual area of expertise, but it was fun trying to adapt to it. If there are any specific areas you want me to revisit or answer, I can write some more
