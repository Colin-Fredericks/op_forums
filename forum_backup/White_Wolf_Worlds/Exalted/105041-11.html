    Outside of outright transformation, I feel a lunar should remind those who look upon them of animals and mythic monsters with every move they make. Someone mentioned earlier that a lunar jumping on one from above should look like a flock of ravens descending upon you. Things like that: cunning as a wolf, slippery like a snake, climbs like a squirrel, leaps like a cat, roars like a dragon... okay maybe not EVERY move, but it should still be a strong theme with them. Ya know?

    On Solar versus Lunar: XP, BP and other such things equal, a lunar should be strong enough to beat a Solar in an area that the Solar hasn't specialized. What I know of Lunar fluff suggests that Lunars are more versatile, but Solars are stronger in their specific skillsets.

    Sorcerey: I've heard it said Lunars should be the masters of Sorcerey... whiiiich is a little odd imho considering that, as I just finished saying, a specialized Solar is usually gonna be better at whatever they throw themselves into, but it could work.

                                I'm a TAW fan. I get the impression that's a bit of a pariah these days, or that mentioning it when talking about 3e Lunars is a faux pas but here goes. I mentioned a whiiile back, on the last forum I think, that I think 3e Lunars is going to be a lot like TAW Lunars, thematically, they'll just be using different words for it ("scary monster" vs "terrifying witches"). TAW's words and theming seemed to make it favor No and Changing Moons, because that's what you get with witches, and 3e's mention of monsters favors Full moons. TAW delivered on making its Full Moons interesting and the developers promise to deliver on making Changing and No Moons interesting.

                                That was my hitch with 2e Lunars, it favored Full Moons (And T-Rexes). I read one Exalted campaign, back on OotS's forum, and it was all Solars except for one guy, a Lunar. He was a Full Moon (And a T-Rex). I read KoC, and Marena gives me the impression she would have so many less problems if she were a Full Moon (And a T-Rex).

                                So, TAW. It's cardinal sin, perhaps, was being 2e. And having some rabid fans. And also trying not to step on too many toes when it came to rewriting Creation's history to include them. It gave me a reason to play a Lunar and not be a Full Moon (Or a T-Rex).

                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            What did they have, a Charm to instantly designate an area as being Owned by them? I feel that one was a bit of an answer to a problem that wasn't already present (I'd assume the regular rules for asserting Ownership should be enough), and a bit of a speed bump for that.
                                                            There weren't particularly rules for asserting Ownership over anything; the Corebook had a rule of thumb which worked for objects and vehicles, but landscapes exist literally on a different scale.

                                                            As a way to claim ownership over an entire populated village without being the chief or even one of the people living there, Boundary-Marking Meditation was not merely a speed-bump. In less than an hour (or the time that it would take to race around the village in the form of a wolf), you could transform the village into a place your territory-reliant Charms would work, which would help in turning the place into a defensible death-trap for invaders.

                                                            Let's also not forget the -1 external penalty imposes on unwelcome intruders with absolutely no action or mote-commitment on your part. It's not even keyworded. Between that and the fact that native gods "accept the Lunar's primacy" (roleplaying and stunting opportunities when dealing with them!), this isn't simply a Charm which is only good for enabling other Charms.


                                                            I don't think Charms that let you shift your senses around a territory you own are too bad.
                                                            They're better if the Charm works even when you're not in the territory anymore, even if all it tells you is whether or not someone crossed your borders.


                                                            That Charm Sleeping Dragon Awakens... I'm not sure how it would be adjusted, it felt a bit too intricately embedded in the Overdrive concept...
