                                                        09-23-2015, 11:58 PM
                                                        Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
                                                        Are Deathlords obliged to tell prospective Abyssals that they're signing on to destroy the world? The core book is ambiguous.

                                                        If so, what's the reasoning behind the 'you must tell them X' here? It seems odd given that not all the Deathlords are working toward destroying the world. "If you accept, you must serve me loyalty until the end of your days," sounds more like what a Deathlord would demand.
                                                        Can only speak for 2e, but unless my memory fails me -- and Hand-of-Omega's answer makes me think nay,* it does not -- the answer is, "Yes." I think it boils down to just the way the Black Exaltation works. It's not just about agreeing to take the Last Breath, it's about agreeing while knowing that one is signing up to kill the world and -- at least in that moment, a hair's breadth from death -- meaning it. Since we're going off 2e, presumably the Neverborn just set it up this way: it's probably not the way that Deathlords who aren't as big on destroying the world would want it, but it's what they have to work with, so work with it they shall. I think the Scroll of Errata mentions that even Abyssal Exaltations flying freely without a Monstrance convey the gravity of the choice.

                                                        Since I get the sense EX3 is making the Deathlords more central to the creation of the Abyssals, this exact pattern may not hold. We'll see how the particulars shake out.
