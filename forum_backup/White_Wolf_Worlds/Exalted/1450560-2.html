                                    I have a question regarding Gentimians. Are they all the result of destinies of people that never existed? Can there be a Gentimian that is the result of someone who exists but was "cheated" out of their glorious fate? It's just that there's so much potential drama in the second option.
                                    Just imagine an encounter between a Gentimian and the "official" version approved by heaven. Your Gentimian was a king, a general, or scholar with fame and fortune that made the world a better place in their reality, but then they found out that heaven decided "Nah...forget about all that crap. Let's make this guy clean manure till 40 when he dies alone from an infection from cleaning manure"
                                    Just imagine how angry you would be. And what about the "official" version? Would he hate you for showing just how much of a failure he is? Would he become your ally seeking revenge against heaven? Perhaps you inspire him to become something better now that he knows for a fact that the possibility exists and change his fate...
                                    I'm just throwing ideas out there. I thought it would be cool, but it doesn't matter if it's not an option.

                                                    You know, all of this talk about Getimians made me remember this quote by Richard Dawkins (as it was quoted in Greatest Show On Earth by Nightwish):

                                                    "We are going to die, and that makes us the lucky ones
                                                    Most people are never going to die because they are never going to be born
                                                    The potential people who could have been here in my place
                                                    But who will in fact never see the light of day outnumber the sand grains of Sahara
                                                    Certainly those unborn ghosts include greater poets than Keats, scientists greater than Newton
                                                    We know this because the set of possible people allowed by our DNA
                                                    So massively exceeds the set of actual people
                                                    In the teeth of those stupefying odds it is you and I, in our ordinariness, that are here
                                                    We privileged few, who won the lottery of birth against all odds
                                                    How dare we whine at our inevitable return to that prior state
                                                    From which the vast majority have never stirred?"


                                                    I must admit that before connecting those dots I had second thoughts about the Getimians- but now that I think of it in that respect, that not only are they those people who were meant to be heroes yet removed from existence, but that from the endless destinies only a few scattered fates were chosen, and from those fates only those specific unreal people were deemed heroic enough to be born into a Creation which refused to allow them to exist- well, how amazing is that?
