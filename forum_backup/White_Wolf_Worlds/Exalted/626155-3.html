        That's got to be one amazing foetus...
        I think you're actually kind of right, facetiousness aside. The Maidens are goddesses of time and destiny. They're only tangentially bound by linear time. It creates a chicken-and-egg situation because one has to ask if it's their exceptional nature that grants them the Exaltation, or whether their future Exaltation affects their early nature, but the temporal questions can be sidestepped by asserting, simply, that the Sidereals really are hyper-competent before their Exaltation.

        False dichotomy. Also, wrong analogy altogether; personally adopting whatever lineage perpetuating role isn't an act of subtlety, it's a significant investment of time and energy. It has to be worth more than "this person I want to control will trust me more if I have their kid in a year", and the genetics of the Sidereal are unlikely to contribute anything of significance.
        Says you. Neither you nor I, though, have ever been secret agents, so artificially limiting what spies are willing to do to get the job done seems preemptive. I will say that there have been plenty of revealed spies in history who have married and had children, only to use their families as deep cover for their real activities, so it's not bizarre for a Sidereal to do the same, I think.

        At what point does having a baby become necessary to that?
        I was using it as an analogy. I can use another one to make my point more clearly: In Vampire: the Masquerade, a Ventrue Prince could easily command loyalty from the city's vampire population using Dominate regularly. But it's considered a mark of weakness and laziness in that Prince, and it's very likely that relying on the crutch of supernatural powers will blunt that Prince's personal skills when someone immune to their Dominate abilities shows up in their city.

        I imagine that if a Sidereal has the luxury of choosing between mundane methods and supernatural ones to solve their problems, they're likely to go for the mundane solution.

        This is begging the question of what kind of universal quality there is to Sidereal missions that earns this kind of description. It's mostly irrelevant, though; Sidereals are sent into Creation to correct errors in Fate. I can hardly imagine a situation for this where they're not equipped well enough to just go straight for the task rather than "hmm, I've got to weasal my way into an inner circle in order to actually have the resources I need, what's the most efficient method... I know, I'll have somebody's baby". There's a whole bunch of question marks seperating you from that profit.
        Ahn-Aru in the 2e sourcebook had an assignment with an indefinite duration. I don't find it odd to consider that a being with a 5,000 year lifespan might be given assignments that last years and decades. The conception of a child might just be one thread in the elaborate decades-long tapestry a Sidereal weaves against a kingdom.

        You know, I've not even considered the points about how much of an imposition the whole thing is on bodily autonomy.
        Like I said to Lioness, I can't imagine that Yu-Shan would ever order one of the Sidereals to create a child. But I can imagine a Sidereal freely choosing to do so for the sake of a plot.

        Spending Essence does not work that way.
        Hell, I wouldn't want to risk spending motes from my Peripheral Essence pool with the Dragon-Blooded around. Would you?

        I think you're still not quite getting what I'm saying. If a Sidereal needs to work their way into somebody's social circle in order to accomplish something, there are numerous far more effective methods of doing that than personally going through the mundanities, not least of which is being able to steal the role out from somebody who already has it. There may be occasions in which an individual Sidereal opts for an approach like becoming the parent to somebody's heir, but it's not going to be anywhere close to a standard operating procedure like it is with the Bene Gesserit, because there are so many reasons not to do it. That is only if the child itself is the end; using a child as a means to an end of some other form of social closeness? Having somebody's child so that they'll trust you enough to poison them? That is ludicrously circuitous.
        I gave an example to Lioness where the creation of a child would be an easier and more direct solution than other methods. Now, I will admit that I find it unlikely that any of the Sidereals other than the Chosen of Venus or Saturn would use it as a method, but I think you're unnecessarily limiting what the Sidereals would do to achieve a goal.

For example, a Mars-Chosen general could use a Chosen of Mercury to control supply lines and the information that comes in and out of the battlefield. That general could use a Chosen of Venus to break the morale of the enemy's soldiers before a battle. A Chosen of Jupiter could relay information about the numbers, tactics, and armaments of the enemy to the Chosen of Mars. And if all else failed, the Mars-Chosen general could use a Chosen of Saturn to surgically assassinate enemy officers in order to tilt the balance of power in favor of their side.

                                Could the Chosen of Mars be spies and solitary assassins? Sure. But I think that they're the most "team player" of the Sidereal castes.
