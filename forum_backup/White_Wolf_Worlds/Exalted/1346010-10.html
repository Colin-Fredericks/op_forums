    Yeah that is totally implayable I feel you. I think in general in your case you have 2 obvious options :
    • Letting the player use all his combat options, which makes 1 player having basically 4-5 controlled unit for himself. Drawback : Way too long to play, generally too powerful, difficult to balance with matching opposition that doesn't take too many units either.
    • Telling the players : Actually your units are not strong enough so they'll be cinematic/props for most of the fights we are gonna do. Drawback : Why invest so much (I think we are at about 7-8 dots of merits at least in your case) if it's just a mechanical prop for fights ?

    I get that there are numerous considerations that follows (the 8 dots of merits can do more useful things than fight : guard cities, run administrations, etc ...) but that's not what the player wants. I guess the straightforward answer is : you are the hero, others are not. If you want other stuff to follow in battle, they are battle group and that's it, and you command them. And I guess it's kind of fair, but it's not so easy to grasp for everyone.

                                        11-14-2019, 01:10 PM
                                        Originally posted by Magnus K View Post
                                        I have one question though, because I don't understand why there is this seemingly vast, stop gap in development though. What I mean is that as far as I understand, and please correct me if I am wrong, art for various books are only ordered many months after the manuscript is written.
                                        In addition to what Eric already said, that art direction happens concurrently with a bunch of other production, bear in mind that art direction really can't proceed until the manuscript is already in a reasonably firmed-up state. Art direction is not just a matter of saying "it would be cool to have a picture of this character! Hey, [ARTISTNAME], could you make a picture of that, please?" In order to commission an art piece for a book, you've got to know how big it's going to be, which in turn means having at least a rough idea of how the layout is going to look, and that, in turn, means having a basically-completed manuscript. If you started art direction when the book was still getting manuscript bits turned in, you'd end up with situations where a piece of text was longer than expected, or shorter, or just had to be cut entirely, which would change what sort of art would fit in. And you can't tell an artist "sorry, the book ended up not needing that bit after all. So we're not going to pay you." Artists, understandably, don't like that.

                                        So in order to do your art direction, you've got to have the complete manuscript, more-or-less as it's going to print, and have a pretty clear idea of how it's going to be arranged, even if that's not totally finalized. Only then can you start commissioning your art pieces.

                                            While I too would love to have all the books out now, and yes, it's probably hurting some groups, i have two thoughts.

                                            1. Not only do writers like money, they love the little ego boost that comes from people buying their stuff. I write, and I know this to be true. No writer sits on something or doesn't write quickly if they have a choice. Hell, many writers I know, given a chance between watching a movie and writing, choose to write.

                                            BUT...

                                            2. When people talk about the era of lots of splats coming out, I'd also note that due to the speed, lots of well, problematic rules issues arose. Sure we got a lot of books, but often they were like that big fancy car that drops the transmission a day after you buy it. Fast is not always good, and writers only have so much time in the day.

                                            I just expect that it's coming as fast as it can, and until then just kitbash or focus on campaigns that do currently have support--I'm getting ready to launch a DB campaign in the Blessed Isle that probably isn't going to have ANY non-DB exalted show up, and it'll last me the next six months, easy.

                                                    Well, after seeing this forum post, I figured it was time to dust off the old account and chime in. I am one of those that did not make the transition to the latest edition of exalted, mainly in that I played Abyssals and Infernals. I liked the alchemicals and dearly wanted to see them get some actual screen time rather than being a throwaway final splat before the edition wheel turned yet again. I am one of those who waited and am still waiting, for this current edition to take notice of me, before I pay a considerable amount of money to get those hefty books.

                                                    I doubt I am alone. I have no proof of course, but I would imagine that there were a fair few like me who held back, because of all the troubles and who were not content to simply reskin solars to play the game they wanted. After how things like necromancy were always a second class citizen compared to magic, I wanted to see the Abyssals actually given equal, but different standing and an actual share of the exalted storyline. Quite why Dragon blooded got such attention is beyond me, but I wont take that away from their fans in the current edition....I just know that all that material is not for my exalted, where dragon bloods are meant to be the antagonists.

                                                    So, there are certainly some of us who have been left behind, but who are watching and waiting. Checking the forums a few times each year, thinking "hey, maybe they finally got round to abyssal" and finding yet another book about items, the dragon blooded or some other thing that isn't the main splats. I wish the new writers well and hope that I may well see the maiden of the mirthless smile and her merry cohort once again....fighting an uncaring sun and preparing to bring this whole sorry saga of creation to its merciful end.

                                                    I`ll likely see you all next year and who knows what we may find, but till then my Soulsteel Grand Daiklaive rests easy in my hand and goodness knows what is going on in Autochthon.
