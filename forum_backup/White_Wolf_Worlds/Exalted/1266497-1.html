            Edit: Ninja'd

            So a couple of things about abolitionism.

            Britain freed all slaves in Britain in the 1770s, banned the slave trade in 1808, and eventually freed all slaves by the 1830s. Now, previously, it hadn't been the case that everyone thought the slave trade was awesome (except the elites who owned the slaves, of course). But what they thought was that:
            Slavery is bad, but, you know, it's not like really, really bad.
            Slavery is just the way of the world. There's not any alternative.
            Slavery is bad... but I mean, the economy would collapse without it.
            Slavery is bad... but the slaves are all savages anyway, so not much has changed, it might even be good for them.
            Slavery is bad... but what can I do?

            The abolitionists used various tactics to get slavery banned, but essentially what they did was to convince the British people that slavery was something they could actually change, and they should. They appealed to people's moral sensibilities by getting freed slaves to give talks about the conditions, they hinted to the military and merchants that this would be a great way to screw over rivals like the Dutch (by boarding slave ships and freeing the slaves), they argued that the economic costs weren't that high (when slavery was eventually banned across the Empire, it was reckoned at 5% of Britain's GDP for a single year, which isn't that much) and by getting people involved in lobbying parliament they showed people that actually there was something they could do about it. They also played to British people's sense of superiority by encouraging them to think about how morally superior this would show Britain to be compared to other Empires. And finally, of course, they explained how most average men on the street, who didn't own slaves, would not be seriously impacted.
            Of course, they didn't convince everyone. But they didn't need to, just enough people to get it banned.


            There are two points here; first, they made multiple arguments to different people about different things. For some they appealed to their arrogance, others (the Navy) their greed, others their morals, others their religion. They played down the costs and played up the benefits. There was an economic cost in the end, but they managed to convince people to accept it.

            The other point is that they didn't do this by appealing just to the King, or even just to the politicians. It was a mass campaign, in an age when only rich men could vote (in an extremely corrupt system). Why bother convincing people who don't actually make the decision? Because even monarchs think about how their people would react.


            I was reading about British responses to the American Civil War. The Confederacy hoped to ally with Britain against the Union. And many British elites wanted to join the Confederacy (the Prime Minister's family had originally gained their wealth from Caribbean slave plantations). But the common people generally supported the Union, even though the Civil War was causing unemployment in Britain, and so the government didn't believe they could get support to ally with the Confederacy.


            Your PCs are going to want to convince the Prince of course. But even if they convince the Prince it's bad, is he going to want to piss off powerful mercantile interests who might overthrow him? On the other hand, if you can get the people clamouring for the abolition of the slave trade, even if he doesn't care, he may ban it.

            And of course, the final point is that people are sometimes willing to take an economic hit for morality. It's hard to convince them to do so, but it's not impossible. Not everyone is 100% cynical and amoral.

            Edit: I'm sure others can talk about the success of abolitionism in other places, but I don't know so much of the details about those.

            One other example I'll mention which happened in various places is that... they just got invaded, and forced to stop involvement in the slave trade. The Normans invaded Britain and (not instantly, but gradually) stopped the slave trade. In the 19th century, the British sometimes attacked places involved in the slave trade, which made it less appealing. China often justifies its annexation of Tibet (sorry, eternal rule which has always existed) by pointing out they banned slavery. There's some evidence that slavery had already ceased to exist by the 1940s, and there's an argument about whether hereditary household servants were slaves or not, but anyway.

            My point is, at the end of the day, your PCs could always just seize control of the city and ban it. Or murder all the slavers (that's how our Lunar stopped the Slave Trade in Azure. Once she'd murdered and eaten 30 slave traders, the slave trade dried to a trickle).

                        I myself like to have believable realism. Verisimilitude.

                        But... abolishing slavery is better. I mean, not for slave owners, but for the entire population as a whole. That's just fact. There may well be problematic side-effects, and these might be long-term, but then, there's problematic side-effects of having slaves (and I do mean side-effects, on top of the obvious problem of the slaves having miserable lives).

                        Inequality will decrease, which leads to more stable societies, lower crime, etc. As slave raiding reduces, the no-longer-targeted countries will develop better economies. With less work as slave raiders, in the short term mercenaries may turn to banditry and destabilise stuff, but in the long term, there will be less soldiers, which means more people can turn to productive pursuits. Similarly, slave overseers and jailers will no longer be necessary, and can do more productive things. Related to that, violence and oppression will be required less, which will create a less violent culture and reduce crime generally. There will be a reduction in the slave-holding class, weakening the power of elites. Many countries (though not all) have justified slavery via socially divisive ideologies, and without that need, these will decrease, creating better social harmony. And a big benefit of course, is that slaves are more likely to improve their skills and situation, further developing the region.

                        And then of course, there's slave revolts, which obviously kill people, damage property, etc, and stopping slavery means stopping slave revolts. Not having to worry about slave revolts will allow the army to more effectively defend against outside threats instead, as they can be stationed more at borders than near slave plantations.

                        Altogether, when the PCs stop the slave trade in this city, especially if they then go to other countries and do the same, the overall economy of the region will increase, it will become more peaceful, and more stable.
                        (Though obviously you'll have to find something else to do with prisoners instead of selling them. But there's plenty of alternatives. And allowing the sale of prisoners as slaves may be a way to exile/punish dangerous people, but they're still dangerous somewhere else, and the option of slavery creates perverse incentives to arrest people on trumped-up charges. West African economies were devastated partly because some Kings kept finding excuses to arrest people so they could sell them for profit, which of course in the long run actually meant less subjects. But rulers often suffer from short-termism.)

                        Admittedly, some of these may take a while. And it may not be realistic to show an immediate beneficial effect in the place where the slave trade is banned (obviously the place they're getting slaves is going to have a pretty quick beneficial effect) because the benefits are general to the region, rather than specific to that city (which doesn't have slaves itself anyway). But ultimately, banning the slave trade is beneficial for society, and so it's not unrealistic for Kelly to show players that it is beneficial.

The best human policy is that which is connected with a reverential regard to Almighty God, the supreme governor of the earth.                                     Originally posted by Kelly Pedersen View Post

                                    If that was what you were saying, then, well, I disagree. Slavery's a fraught subject, and deserves more careful handling than some others. Also, "most realistic" is, in my experience, something of an illusion in any case. So I would avoid worrying too much about "realistic" consequences, and just remember that history contains plenty of times where things really did work out for the better, overall.

                                    One thing that speaks to how I see Exalted is that it is (generally, and with some exceptions) intended to be a hopeful game, especially in comparison to a great deal of White Wolf's other works, like Vampire (or God help us all, Wraith). Thus, unless your players agree to a different tone for the game, I think giving god-like beings god-like powers and a god-like mandate to go forth and change the world, and then screwing them when they do so for the sake of realism, is bad form at best. I feel like changing things should not always be easy or simple, but it should generally go well when the characters are doing what they're supposed to do, which is, in extremely broad and subjective terms, "make the world a better place."
