            Originally posted by michael allen View Post
            1) Would an evocation meant for a crane stylist that works just like the brawl charm "Iron Battle Focus" be bringing across an ability that crane style shouldn't have?
            Brawl's defensive options are much shoddier than Martial Arts', so any time you give the martial artist access to one of Brawl's unique tricks, there's going to be synergies and combinations that outperform what the brawler can muster up. That doesn't necessarily mean it can never be done, but it's a balance consideration.

            2) Would an evocation like "Meteor Fist Meditation", that allows you to activate a simple charm reflexively if you win join battle, be OK on crane form? I worry that because crane form can already be activated reflexively, but is quite hard to do, that maybe it is supposed to be hard to activate reflexively and making it easier might be busted. Would adding a wp to the activation be enough to balance it or should it not be done at all?
            I'd be hesitant to add new ways to activate forms reflexively, for the reason you mentioned, but as long as they're generally similar in difficulty of achieving them, I don't think things will break too hard.

                        For siege weapons, they have a base damage of +15 or +20 for mundane and artifact respectively, and do not use strength for withering damage, but none of them possess the crossbow or flame tag, which is the source of the usual 'sub 4 in place of strength' clause. So is it intended for a Fire Projector to have the same damage as a Firewand (or any other mundane heavy archery weapon were they to exist), and for artifact seige weapons to have +1 damage over their non-seige artifact counterparts?
                        We didn't give them the Flame or Crossbow tags because the damage boost was just baked into their statline, and we wanted to save wordcount.

                                Originally posted by Calboon View Post
                                So, I have a couple of questions related to power levels.

                                First off as background third edition is the first edition of exalted I have ever played/Dmed and one of the things that does interest/confuse me is the idea that the different types of exalted are not on the same power level/level of competency. From my understanding there is going to be three broad power “Areas”. You will have Solar/abyssal/infernal at the top, a competency gap then Lunars/Sidereals other guys that fit here, another gap and finally dragonblooded etc.

                                The first question then is going from second to third edition what is the benefit of keeping the power gap between the first and second group from a gameplay perspective?

                                *note tradition/didn’t want to change how it has worked for two editions is totally a valid answer I am just curious if there is something else I am missing as a newcomer to the game line.

                                Like as a DM I am just imagining showing off the game in a couple years when all the splats are out and it going as follows:
                                1. You can play as the Solar exalted, legendary Golden heroes returned to a world in crisis. Possessing unparalleled skill and power. Also, the most powerful thing you can play and will most likely wreck everybody’s shit as you see please
                                2. You can play as the Abyssal exalted, powerful death knights and masters of the dead and death. Also, the most powerful thing you can play and will most likely wreck everybody’s shit as you see please
                                3. The infernal exalted, demon tainted exalted with all types of crazy esoteric powers and they can just straight up turn into a final fantasy boss. Also, the most fucking thing you can play and will most likely wreck everybody’s shit as you see please
                                4. OR you can play a fate ninja, werewolf, Frankenstein monster etc. but they are noticeably weaker than the first three options.

                                So, my second question is if the game line was fine with having three equally powerful groups, each with their own very different themes, powers etc, tied together loosely by the idea of “is at its core a solar” why not just make all the playable splats different variety of solar?

                                As in vanilla solar, death knight solar, demon solar, shapeshifting monster solar, fate manipulating solar etc.

                                The idea being from my perspective why is it someone has to be worse at sword fighting/talking/crafting etc just because they chose animal based shape shifting/fate manipulation as the thing that interested them versus the guy who chose all the death or demon stuff?
                                The premise of Exalted is that Creation is a fallen, post-apocalyptic world, diminished by the betrayal and the sealing-away of the Solar Exalted. In their absence, the Sidereals and the Lunars have both played their part in shaping Creation - one by raising up and shaping the Realm, the other by doing their level best to tear the Realm apart - but neither can restore the world to the heights of glory it once knew when the Solar Exalted reigned and worked hand-in-hand with the other Exalted. If the Lunars and Sidereals are just as powerful as Solars, this premise, and the setting built around it, falls apart.

                                With that being said, the power gap between Solars and Lunars/Sidereals in Ex3 is smaller than it was in previous editions. I'm writing the Lunar Charmset at the moment, and one of my goals is that you can play a Lunar in a Solar Circle without feeling like you're lagging behind the other player characters.
