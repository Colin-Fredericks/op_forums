        Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
        I keep wondering if GKS is actually making a disaster happen by virtue of conveniently predicting it (where he disaster otherwise wouldn't happen), or is it taking the concept of introducing a fact to its logical extreme.

        Also if it's meant to be the former, that is totally a Sid Charm, not Solar, and I will fight with knives anyone who disagrees.
        Eh, I'll pick up a butterknife and wave it halfheartedly in your general direction.

        I'd argue it isn't really a Sid charm on the grounds that Sids are more about influencing reality in more sideways and indirect fashions, usually by rewarding behavior they want and punishing behavior they don't want by inflicting curses and blessings on someone by manipulating the threads of Fate.

        GKS is more a brute force method, saying "THIS WILL HAPPEN, DEAL WITH IT" by looking so closely at the fabric of reality that they hit one of the hidden flaw points in Creation and cause a cascade of events that culminates in the utter ruin that they were attempting to investigate. There's an Occult charm to that effect, and an Integrity charm that lets you temporarily fuse with reality at a fundamental level, so I don't think it's too out of their themes to declare a fact as true.

        Plus, Astrology is probably going to be way more versatile, so Sids will still trump Solars in manipulating causality. As they should.

            Originally posted by TDS View Post
            My reading of that charm was the Loremaster saying "The weather will be very bad for a Summer campaign (based on my study of meteorology) so the smartest thing for her to do is gather resources for a winter campaign instead". So of course the rebels take a penalty when they try attacking in the summer, because the Solar has predicted that the weather will be bad for fighting, which is why Mnemon is delaying in the first place.
            If you predict that a series of heavy storms will hit Lathe and so the smuggler business there will suffer a hit, that still causes storms to hit Lathe and mess up other stuff as well, like regular trade or maybe a naval campaign that was supported to be launched. If you predict a plague to hit a City and cause a famine, that may cause the plague to spread even if you didn't specifically include that in your prediction.
            The charm doesn't really say that, though. It says you do some research and then say "Boy, there are some really nasty things that could go wrong if she tries to invade in the summer, so, being a smart tactician, I'm sure she'll decide to consolidate her forces and wait until fall to attack." Then, if she attacks, just like you predicted, all sorts of things can go wrong. It doesn't say that if she doesn't attack, all the elemental attacks and resource shortages happen anyway, just to other people. Maybe they do? But you're not making a prediction about the world in general, you are, to quote the charm text, making "a pronouncement against an ongoing project." That's the key disconnect here, I think. Are you making a "pronouncement against a project," which implies this charm targets the project itself, and doesn't affect outside things, or are you making predictions about the state of the world that leads to certain project choices being advantageous? Eric is saying it's the latter, which seems to be supported by the example, but I think the former is a perfectly reasonable reading of the mechanical part of the text. A bit of rewording of the charm text and the example would probably help avoid this confusion and would be pretty easy to do.

            Honestly, my bigger concern is how vague the whole thing is. What does "vastly more successful and easier to complete than otherwise" mean? Sure, for the NPC army in the example, the storyteller can just hand-wave it pretty easily, but what if I make a prediction about a Manse I'm building? What does that do for me? Can I suddenly complete it months ahead of schedule? Do the magical materials I need just show up on my doorstep? And how big of penalties do I get to my rolls if I then decide to violate my prediction? And why is it only the first War rolls that get penalized in the example?

                                                    Medicine

                                                    Number of Charms: 23
                                                    By Ability requirement: 2/0/4/3/14
                                                    By Essence requirement: 5/6/6/4/2
                                                    Nonstandard dice tricks: Reduce Excellency to 1m/2 dice, free full Excellency

                                                    Themes: Heal any injury, sickness, or poison, probably on the cheap, potentially rapidly, and certainly with as many successes as you’ll ever need.

                                                    Charms:
                                                    1. Grant target bonuses to resist disease.
                                                    2. Cure disease immediately.
                                                    3. Restore health levels immediately.
                                                    4. Convert target’s aggravated damage to lethal.
                                                    5. Perfectly diagnose an illness.
                                                    6. Gain bonuses to treat illness, permanently.
                                                    7. Perform an hour of treatment in seconds.
                                                    8. Remove a magical crippling injury mid-combat.
                                                    9. Negate the target’s wound penalties, though target cannot be active.
                                                    10. Grant temporary -0 health levels.
                                                    11. Cause a poison to disperse after its next interval.
                                                    12. Convert aggravated damage, or cure bashing/lethal, instantly.
                                                    13. As #7, but the patient also recovers on their feet in seconds.
                                                    14. Restore missing limbs, regrow eyes, or treat madness.
                                                    15. Regenerate motes and willpower, and continue to do while making Medicine rolls.
                                                    16. Spend health levels for motes for Medicine Charms.
                                                    17. Remove target’s wound penalties; if target is active, doctor takes -1 penalties.
                                                    18. Medicine Excellency is permanently 1m/2 dice.
                                                    19. Use a Medicine Charm without paying its cost.
                                                    20. 1/scene free full Excellency.
                                                    21. Reroll non-successes in Medicine.
                                                    22. 1/day, double Medicine successes.
                                                    23. 1/story, double-7s on a Medicine roll.
                                                    What is the intended use case of Touch of Blissful Release (#9)? It feels like most typical PC behavior would immediately negate its effects – who is it for?

                                                    Hm. I think I would have assumed that Instant Treatment Methodology (#7) put the target back on their feet immediately… if Body-Sculpting Essence Method (#13) hadn’t included a call-out saying that that’s what it does. Am I missing something here?

                                                    Wholeness-Restoring Meditation is nice. Broadly powerful, wide range of uses – feels very worth a Charm purchase.

                                                    Not a lot to say this time! Medicine is… pretty medicinal. There’s not a lot of dead wood here – though by the last few Charms, it’s mostly just ALL THE MEDICINE SUCCESSES. There does seem to be an assumption that doctors are going to be rolling a lot of Medicine rolls in quick succession, such that “Shoot, I’m out of motes for medicinal purposes!” is a big concern. That’s interesting – it feels like in practice, most of the GMs I’ve had would call it after a roll or two, or make one big roll representing a day of work, so I’m curious how they’re picturing that.

                                                    Everything here is very much personal-scale, in any event; there’s no group treatment, nothing like 2e’s A Hood on Death. (Which was kind of a weird Charm, so that’s a good call.) I’m a little bit sad at that decision – I’d prefer Medicine scale up the way many of the other Abilities do – but I can understand the decision to keep it on an individual level.

                                                    Yes: Wholenes-Restoring Meditation.
                                                    No: “Convalescence,” a recovery period which appears only in the Charm removing it.
                                                    Just Plain Weird: Life-Exchanging Prana (#16). You have to be pretty desperate for motes to spend health levels at 3m a pop – is this an expected phenomenon?
