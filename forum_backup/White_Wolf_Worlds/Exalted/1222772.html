    I hate Melee Specialties (and you should too)

    05-28-2018, 09:40 PM
    Provocative title out of the way, I hate the way Melee Specialties are boring flat bonuses. Lets posit a few statements first:

    1- Only one specialty applies at a time (Melee: Swords and Melee: While Mounted only give 1 die if using a sword while mounted)

    2- Melee: Sword/Axe/Knife/Etc is rules legal.

    3- Players usually stick to a single weapon type.
    3a- players usually stick to a specific weapon, period.
    3b- unlike action games, there's little incentive to switch between two different melee weapons.

    4- Martial Arts, Thrown, and Archery, have basically the same problem.

    So here we are, a perfect recipe for bland specialties. The book says that specialties shouldn't always apply, but then allows players to specialize in a weapon they will have on hand 99% of the time. I made some example Solars/NPCs to help my players, a Crafter, a Martial Arts Hating Crusader, and a Kaiju Hunter. The Crafter has Melee: Weapons I Made, the Crusader has Melee: Opponents in a Form Charm ** and the Kaiju Hunter has Melee: Legendary Size Beasts**. For the crafter, i specifically wanted to buck the "use one weapon only" trend, so that was a nice specialty. Useful, flavorful, but still almost always applies.

    The Kaiju hunter though? Bullshit. Its a "bad" (in this case meaning "mechanically sub optimal") specialty. There's no reason not to take Melee: Great Axes or the like, which will apply all the time. Same with the Crusader who hates Martial Artists. She'd be better off with Melee: Weapontype in every conceivable scenario .

    If we look at noncombat skills, they are more interesting. Medicine: Treating Poison is cool, but it'd be insane to allow Medicine: Using my Healer's Kit. Lore: Realm is a good choice, but not Lore: Referencing A Book.

    It can get bad with Ride and Sail too. Ride:Horses (or Ride:Familiar) is never going to not cover you unless the ST forces you to ride some other mount specifically to negate your specialty. I have never once been in a situation like that.

    So back to combat, what do we do? How do we reward Interesting specialties without punishing them for not being "bland but optimal"? First, let's skip the argument that Bland But Optimal isn't a problem and Players Should Just Do What Fits Their Characters, Man. We are here to rectify a problem. I have a few ideas.

    1) remove Melee: Weapon Type or Melee: My Personal Artifact as valid. This would be a bad fix and personally hurt all of the "ancestral weapon" type players and "ultimate katana master" types.

    2- allow a 2nd specialty. So we have Melee: Flycutter Yari AND Melee: Fae when using the Artifact Spear against a Fae, double dipping. I'm not sure how the math checks out on that.

    3- devise a broad vs deep bonus system. The Flaws merit rightly only gives you a bonus when you suffer for it to prevent MinMaxing cheese. Melee Specialties should do likewise. Melee:Weapon is 1 dice, Melee: Opponent or Condition is 2, and Melee: Niche Backstory Thing is 3.

    Oh, and I don't know where to fit this in, but Disarm Gambits are NOT a solution to the Specialty Problem, because either A- you don't have a back up weapon, or B- your back up is a crappy dagger and not a Glorious Golden Surfboard On A Hilt. Neither scenario would make me think "Oh great, my specialty for this opponent type outweighs the need to get back my actually useful weapon". Oh, and C- Call the Blade negates that strategy entirely.

    What do you guys think?

      Just tell your players you dislike it when they pick a specialty that they can apply almost all the time. "I fight martial artists, so I've studied the weakness in the forms they go into and since its uncommon for a martial artist, who isn't an exalt, to master multiple forms I tend to not have to worry about them swapping out on me." Part of the problem you're dealing with is that the book does say specialties can be rather specific like a lore specialty in haiku, or a broad specialty like melee with swords.

          I mean...I'd just go for 1 in my games.

          I like my specialities to say something about my character. Less Melee "Swords," and more Melee "Close Quarters Fighting" or Melee "Multiple Opponents" or Melee "Shocking Brutality."

          Like...they should be useful a lot of the time, but any specialty that's going to ALWAYS apply is one I probably wouldn't let a PC use in my game. Instead of Ride (Horses) or even Ride (My Godblooded Familiar Horse), I'd go with Ride (Races) or Ride (Mounted Acrobatic Tricks) or Ride (Herding).

          Specialties should tell you about a character's nature and backstory...and they should mechanically encourage a PC to try to use tactics that make sense given that nature and backstory. A Dawn who grew up with the Marukani doing the whole cowboy thing who has a Ride specialty in Herding is going to try and use that specialty, so he'll make use of his experience to try and herd enemies the way he did cattle-might even take the same specialty in War to double down. A former pirate with lots of experience fighting below decks with the Close Quarters Fighting specialty has a motivation to try and drag the fight into close quarters to get that bonus die.

          So yeah, narrow specialties are good for the game. They should be something the PC knows in-character they're good at and tries to arrange to be able to use.

                  I agree that the speciality in a weapon type is far to common a choice for players and does not add interesting flavour to the character like other specialities can. However I think you are exaggerating the circumstances to far. If you are heavily invested in Evocations for a single artifact weapon then sure you will always do your best to be using that weapon. However outside of that weapon tags can play a big role. Also you may not want to pull out your surfboard in every fight due to discretion.

                  Know you're going to fight a crane style martial artist? Bring a flexible weapon to ignore his full defence. Mounted Opponent? Go for Reaching tag. Opponent in heavy armour who has neglected dodge so is terrible at standing up? Go for grappling or smashing to get him on the ground. The situational bonuses from the right tag do outweigh a simple +1 from a speciality. Yes many players may choose to ignore these factors, but that can likely be attributed to a Solar just being so powerful that they break the system.

                  Disarming is still a factor. Comparing a mundane disarm attempt vs a charm based counter seems silly. If we compare disarm charms vs Call The Blade then disarming is definitely still valid. Charms such as Iron Wolves' Grasp, Fallen Weapon Deflection(depending on ST ruling on order of application), and Table Turning reversal can prevent Call The Blade from working. Mountain-Burden Stone also works, or any other custom magic or evocations people can come up with. Also the dagger isn't "crappy" unless compared to evocation investment. Loss of a speciality & +1 attack/+3 dmg on withering attacks is not huge and may be the preferred option compared to the difficulty of retrieving your weapon.
