                        ... I have no time for conversation. Maybe when I'm done.

                        (Partly driven by the concern of people still talking about things past where I have read)

                        Anyway.

                        Now, continuing with chapter two. Always helpful for a group to clarify its aims.

                        Fairly standard, gather and train the young Lunars, help one another out, seek to gather people behind you and break down the threat of the Realm. As usual, I like how it includes a reference to them employing a method based on guile and misdirection, rather than direct (even if stealthy) force. I also like how a lot of our new Lunars from the core are recurring names now; they’ll supplant Leviathan yet!

                        Ah, good, each goal is receiving elaboration. Let’s see how they handle the youths.

                        Good range of mundane and supernatural methods for becoming aware of the appearance of a new Lunar there; I’m particularly imagining something like in the Lion, Witch, Wardrobe movie with the trees, having a relay system of minor spirits to transmit information along very long distances. I’d say it should also be helpful to emphasize that, yes, going to find a new Lunar entails going far out of one’s way, while laying out the compelling motivations to do so.

                        I always thought it was good for the corebook to present the idea that the Pact doesn’t use force, or even obligation, to bring in new recruits when they think the world at large will educate them, and I like how that’s expanded on here with the idea that a young Lunar spending an indeterminate time on their own can even come back with unique and beneficial insight and methods. That being said… a couple of these paragraphs are highly repetitive in a manner distinct from a simple misprint. Leaving that aside, it’s an effectively and plausibly accommodating picture of the Pact’s process, and I think the most interesting thing is the point on how recruitment can often be facilitated by the simple awe of a powerful rescuing Lunar.

                        Now, sidebar concerned with the Sidereals… ah, it’s just comparing tracking methods. Sidereal aid to the Wyld Hunt provides precision, while Lunars can have shapes to give them the edge in speed (flying probably helps particularly; cover the kind of distance that an unladen swallow could).

                        Okay, mutual aid society. Approached in a freeform manner, probably determined by circumstance and the personalities involved, with some of the incentive coming from the prestige entailed by being open to a great number and variety of tasks. The book keeps referring to council, which seems like occasions where a number of qualified Lunars gather to arbitrate a dispute (or perhaps more likely, several that have gathered to be heard at a designated time and order). As for the prospective forms of repayment, I think the notion of bringing desired animals to be hunted stands out as particularly Lunar, and the benefits are obvious. It’s got to suck for the animal involved though; hunted once, trussed up and dragged who knows how far, and then hunted again to the death. Also the idea that debts can be repaid by long-term respect, that sounds like it would be a practical way to maintain decorum without overburdening too many Lunars. I like how the details of the consequences for exploiting or not honouring the system are multifaceted, including circumstances where there are people that can get away with it.

                        Ah finally, detailing these councils. Not just for arbitration, but varied decision-making as required of local circumstances, as well as just an opportunity to get together with Lunars and share what they have in common. I imagine that, given the somewhat loose standards for the rank of shaman-ya, the arrangements of councils can have a unique plasticity. Heh, given the description of expectations for hospitality, I can also imagine that many Lunars are drawn to participate in council just for the sake of being offered access to those luxuries at somebody else’s expense, especially if many Lunars are often otherwise more hard living. I’d been wondering about Leviathan, so here’s a first really major revision to him, a more socially active leader, harnessing the distinct wealth of Luthe to put on a show; I like the image of him leading Lunars in the shape of oceanic predators in grand hunts particularly. Somehow, he’s still overshadowed by Amatha Kinslayer’s councils undertaken in plain sight, attended by Lunars in disguise. If nothing else, that feels like a real horror story scenario for, say, an impromptu visiting Dynast, the sudden or gradual realisation that you’ve walked into the hyena’s den. A bit of storytelling advice should tide people over until a dedicated book, and it presents an interesting picture of how to interact with the process while not yet being a big player in proceedings. I particularly like the emphasis on the idea that it’s not the best suited to having Persuade actions navigating varied Intimacies, and bargaining is the better option.

                        For the interpersonal protocols… I’ve had thoughts in the past about outright prohibitions against violence against other Lunars, but the framing of it here is less objectionable to me, and in the context of conflict resolution via council rulings I can see sufficiently compelling alternatives (still including duels) as to be unconcerned. The image of the process for executing particularly criminal former members of the Pact entailing hunting them down feels like something intended to insult and degrade, giving the organisation a nice bit of grit.

                        Now, warring against the Realm. I’d say it’s good to focus the attention almost entirely on those who partake of the Shogunate’s legacy, rather than Dragon Blooded as a whole, if only to make them feel less indiscriminately hostile. Given the contention of the subject, it’s also useful to detail the various grievances that can be caused by Realm policy, even beyond the boundaries of their more direct power. I can even picture some Lunars embellishing that a little bit for some more distantly removed people, with the aid of their silver tongues.

                        I like how the sidebar frames the matter of trying to assassinate Dragon Blooded on their home turf, both in terms of describing the obstacles and the problems that can be entailed by succeeding, while the focus is on the need to be discriminating rather than a straightforward “it won’t work, don’t do it”.

                        Aaaand this should redress some concerns, a dedicated section to some of the successes of the Silver Pact. Ah, my initial take was that it would be a list, but a description of the standards and overall model makes more sense for this placement. Let’s see… ah yes, we saw some of this before. The reference to being a threat that curtailed the territorial ambitions of the Scarlet Empress and the like. What I like from the first few paragraphs is that sense that for many of the Lunars, it’s better to extend the length and range of conflicts than going for the small decisive victories, which feels like it lends another reason to be conservative with assassinations; why eliminate a major general early on when you can keep doing enough damage to make them chase you, prolonging the wasteful expense of the process over years? It puts me in mind of things I’ve been reading recently about European warfare of the 17th and 18th centuries, and what they could do to national finances. I think the term “war of attrition” too often conjures the image of two forces grinding away at one another until one has run out of bodies, disregarding the idea of one that keeps effectively running away from the other, until somebody can’t pay for their armies anymore (or the process of doing so is ruinous to an economy or political body). It's an interesting finalisation of the long promised picture of the Silver Pact’s grand strategy, even if it ends on the note of how the return of Solars throws some of its viability into doubt.

                        Oh, and there’s even more detailing for the different kinds of approach. First off, I think the sidebar effectively covers the benefits and drawbacks of asymmetrical, decentralized warfare. Yes, the description of attrition takes about the same tack as I described above, and it’s helpful to be specific about what it actually means for the Realm to be infrastructure dependent. Even the point about where Dragon Blooded come from, look back at how What Fire Has Wrought described the processes and resources dedicated to gathering them, educating them, matching them to one another. If they weren’t facing an enemy that could draw a significant mortality rate, would the Realm be so concerned with maximizing Exaltation rates? Perhaps not, and it’s another strain on the system. And what’s especially useful is to describe this all in terms of the Realm more than the Dragon Blooded; Terrestrial Exalted could also function in this manner (many outcaste bandits clearly do), but they can’t exercise empire like that. If they were competing against Lunars directly in that arena, they’d be at a disadvantage, and besides that there’s the question of whether Dragon Blooded not operating as an empire is even something the Lunars would find as objectionable.

                        Heh, I initially misread the line about the resources of tending to wounded soldiers meaning that the Realm is incentivized to merely abandoning them, but I can see now that the point is for Lunars to leave more wounded (who the Realm ends up with some obligation to care for) than dead (who can merely be replaced from the vast population). And then there’s the point about how outright defeat may draw an overwhelming reprisal, or make them stay within their safe boundaries, or shift focus, and it’s preferable to the long term strategy to keep things drawn-out indefinitely, perpetuating the vicious cycle.

                        And finally a sidebar clarifying on a distinction between the relative professionalism of Realm legions and the likely excesses of auxiliaries that they’re dependent on. The point about how Dynasts generally don’t have a need to heavily engage in plunder in their own right… that effectively offsets some of my own prior ideas, as well as providing a bit of background as to why the Realm might employ a system of purchased commissions.

                        Oh, there’s more, let me just range ahead a bit… the other two approaches are less heavy, so I’ll finish the stuff about Silver Pact warfare and then take a break before moving on to the group composition stuff.

                        Ah I see, the rest was focused primarily on the logistical results of Lunar action, and here’s the layout of the various things they can actually do; lend their power to commanding small bodies of troops deep into enemy territory, personal combat prowess, and shapeshifting to attack, infiltrate and foment severe paranoia, as well as their mystical powers to apply more indirect and esoteric pressure. And yeah, under circumstances like this, I can see the benefit of detailing the challenges that opponents can bring against them, right down to the more mundane but effective measures that can be employed by powerful and wealthy mortals.

                        And finally (really this time) a sidebar for more dedicated focus to the occult side of warfare. What interests me here is something to provide some clarity to the recurring point about Lunar mystics being able to draw upon strange secrets, the forgotten workings and lost monsters that their power and placement might find and direct against the Realm.

                        So, next is defensive withdrawal… which feels like a slightly misleading description of what sounds a lot more like fortification. In any case, there are a few implications for this, in my eyes primarily noting that there are instances where the Lunar relationship with dominions is more benevolent (for those who balk at the callousness of spending them on weakening the Realm), and applying their talents towards presiding over zones of prosperity. It puts me in mind of many disproportionately powerful small states, such as island kingdoms, little strategically and economically powerful duchies that are able to heavily fortify themselves, and city states (often located at the tips of peninsulas) that serve as major financial centres.

                        Suddenly, I’ve got a picture of that Lunar I came up with for BrilliantRain’s White Elephant eventually becoming enamoured enough of that little kingdom to take up residence to adopt it as a dominion of this kind.

                        And the last of them is their open warfare, for the Turin Turambar’s among the Silver Pact. Most of it is the fairly basic needs of such a force and the point of how often it fails against the considerable advantages of the Realm, so it seems primarily beneficial as an option that many Lunars might explore in the Time of Tumult. In any case, it’s right and proper to have a sidebar pointing out that conventional and asymmetrical warfare are more of a continuum than a binary, and Lunars dedicated to either approach can utilise some of the other (conversely, one can see a few examples of the Realm employing some irregulars to great effect, such as the strategies of House Tepet).

                        Last of all is the point on what the endgame is, which is about what I expected; a few vaguely defined possibilities, held off on for the sakes of focusing on the present and preserving the unity of the movement (an understandable and perhaps unavoidable point that has nevertheless been the bane of many a revolutionary group). Even within those limits… I have my own biases about what the Pact might be capable of, or what it’s preferable for them to do, so it’s good to be reminded that actual characters would have a greater variety of visions for the future.

                        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                            Now, see if I can’t finish the chapter. Next up are the shahan-yas, the leaders and luminaries of the Pact who exert significant influence on its agendas and priorities (with a useful note on not everybody having universal recognition for their claim on the title).

                                            First up is our old hand Ma-Ha-Suchi. I want to put one point straight up front; I had formed a strong impression from the corebook that Ma-Ha-Suchi was not strictly a member of the Silver Pact in his own right, and thought there was a lot that was beneficial to that image which I became attached to but in this context, with how the preceding chapter has set the Silver Pact and the experience of a lot of its elders up, as well as some of the particulars of his own history, having him in here as basically an honoured retiree works and I’m good with it. With that established, let’s see the particulars.

                                            Let’s see, started off as a widely beloved champion in the later First Age, but finds his old ideals having no place in his current environment. Was the type who viewed the Lunars as the only legitimate inheritors of the Realm with the removal of the Solars and made a lot of his name fighting the Shogunate to that effect. Used the typical Silver Pact playbook with beastfolk armies, seducing Exalted generals, destroying old infrastructure and forging alliances between Lunar elder and mortal nation alike, dreaming of establishing a new Realm. Then Great Contagion happened, he becomes disenchanted with his struggle in the face of seeming powerlessness in the face of the eschaton, and the fact that even after all of that the enemy Dragon Blooded pulled back together and established a whole new Realm of their own. The final nail came from allies centred around his dear friend Raksi ultimately rejecting his vision of a new Realm, and after a severe internal struggle became completely spent and retreated to seclusion in his Nameless Lair.

                                            Ah, some things about his response to the Time of Tumult have been clarified; he’s seeing it as the time of miracles, and with reinvigoration is starting to try rebuilding forces and reclaim his old contacts, in hopes of striking out at the opportune moment to establish significant power over the East and maybe try and get that Lunar Realm project back on track.

                                            What do I think… well, he’s come through with a lot more of his corebook portrayal intact than he did in First Edition. Nah, in all seriousness, it’s fine, I can see some compelling drama in the image of an old passion being stirred back to life (especially if playing a circle that has been within his orbit since enough time before the start of the Tumult to see the difference), and a bit more activity directed towards the future.

                                            Oh there’s more… hmm, basically structuring shahan-yas in a similar manner to describing Great… Houses… anyway, what about the pact relations. Doesn’t tend to meet or attend council personally, and it would take something major to make him host a council. Still has the major falling out with Raksi, to the point of shunning her and avoiding contact with anybody associated, which is generally a fun take on the old point about how they were always major Lunar elders in relatively close proximity and seemingly no connection. But not completely isolated, and I think it’s very effectively humanising to give such a lofty character some specific friends and correspondences, including the artistic subjects of interest therein.

                                            Heh, something is fun in the image of him having a passing interest in a marginalized group that kind of aligns with his goals and sensibilities, while their own leader finds that attention to be threatening. And otherwise he’s still got some of the creepy hermit stuff I was liking in the fact that other visitors will almost never get a direct audience with him. I like the image that I’m getting of visiting the Nameless Lair, and Ma-Ha-Suchi’s presence lingers as a tense pressure (informed by the certainty that he’s somewhere on the premises) while never actually seeing or speaking to him.

                                            Hehehe: “To those in attendance of the Third Age ball, Ma-Ha-Suchi… has more important things to attend to. Therefore, I will be observing in his place”

                                            Ohhh Lord, I’m just remembering that the actual Great Houses were so long that I could only write two at a time (per day? I’ll check back later). How long has all of this been… seven hundred words? Eh, see how long my energy and attention can last.

                                            Oh, the Shadow Fang Vanguard sidebar misled me, there’s still a bunch left. Okay, keep going, keep going. The sidebar is no different than the first time I saw it in preview, so to reiterate I like the idea of a more firmly organised faction that is also a weird outlying cult that a lot of the others give a wide berth, plus the idea of a second generation on top and a First Age elder as advisor.

                                            Despite what I said about a similar skeletal structure to the Great Houses, of course a lot of the substance is very different, and a description of the mentorship styles and standards comes across as an especially distinct and valuable one. So up until recently, he’s only been taking on occasional students recommended from other trusted fellows, who can then expect to learn social engineering, intrigue and warfare through command of beastfolk reavers, from a teacher that is brusque and stoic, but not outright cruel. Huh, he’s kind of like a less murderous Pai Mai… yeah, that boosts this new profile up a fair bit for me. His teaching method is also informed by the desire to have Lunars that can work in the organized manner that would drive a new Realm, and in spite of his own withdrawal he still has friends, supporters and admirers throughout the pact that independently support his positions and ambitions. That idea is especially interesting, I hadn’t considered something like that even back in the core (mind, I had no sense of what his overall worldview was).

                                            And his adherents… is it weird that Kathaka puts me in mind of the protagonist of The Fault in Our Stars, with that thing of being disappointed to find your hero is really a crotchety bastard? I like the juxtaposition between giving her compelling motives as regards her teacher, casting herself in a close familial role and desperate for his praise, and the opening description of her as a bully, suggesting that she’s not actually very pleasant as a person. Likewise the complexity underlying his position as teacher fond of her but not altogether having faith in her, continuously seeking to test if she’s actually in it for the cause rather than just attachment to him, and worried that the changing times will prove she’s not up to it. Yeah, stuff like this, this is a good way to include First Age characters without the whole story warping around or buckling beneath them, I think.

                                            Who else is there… aww, that Ranotis na-Raya is rather adorable, with the retaining a close working relationship with his factor father and seeing Ma-Ha-Suchi in exceptionally Romantic terms and having a playful rapport with him, reciprocated by a teacher that, for all his taciturn stylings, sees a lot of potential that he’s actively cultivating, while also trying to drive a wedge between him and his mortal family lest they cause a conflict of interest or split priorities.

                                            Anja Silverclaws, you’ve got some tough acts to follow, what are you up to this time around… ahh, her focus on Thorns remains, and is harnessed in an interesting new way with her not actually being devoted to the shahan-ya or his dreams, his proximity and short-term plans just seen as useful to her own goal of liberating the city, while he… I’m guessing he lets her in primarily because her drives and actions constitute a strongly motivated asset in making strategic inroads into the East (and might be less exhausting to have around than the other two wanting to get through to him), even if he rates the Mask of Winters as a lower priority than the Realm and the Solars.

                                            What I like with these write-ups, is that it’s creating a picture of a complex social dynamic that is more close-knit and personally driven than the Great Houses, which I think can generate a lot of interest in wanting to play Lunars of a specific school just to associate with them and be witness to and participants of the tangle of their lives. Yeah, overall this is a very good way to do this with them, and also gives me a lot of hope for the Deathlords in the future.

                                            A few moments ago I remembered how it actually came down to only writing on one House at a time, so I’ll save Raksi for a bit later. Hopefully it won’t be too long, so long as there aren’t eleven shahan-yas to read into.

                                                            Okay, one more, and then probably finish tomorrow. What of Raksi?

                                                            Right off the bat, there’s the focus on her as formidable in a courtly sense in addition to being a fearsome monster queen; the idea that she resides in decadent splendour has always been present, but I often felt as though the focus was minimal, even perfunctory, especially in Second Edition, contrasted here where it’s made a strong part of her internal dichotomy. The idea that she cultivates uncertainty about herself already makes her more interesting than I found her in the past. Really, she has nowhere to go but up; for a character that books kept trying to present as scary and impressive, I don’t think I ever really saw a genuine instance where she wasn’t treated like a punchline.

                                                            Moving on… I don’t know why I’m struggling to think of something to say about the paragraph describing how there are some people close enough to know that the revelry and cruelty is disingenuous. What’s easier is the point about how her reign over her worshippers is so prosperous with her applications of sorcery that even her strong opponents in the Silver Pact can’t deny it. People have often wanted such things from a Lunar ruler, I wonder how many might have thought it would wind up with Raksi. I wonder if the use of such a modern sounding term as “Total Control Zone” is meant to evoke a sense that she’s running things in accordance with a more advanced First Age model.

                                                            Now this is interesting, the idea that in the First Age she applied her talents to esoterica that wasn’t really a source of acclaim, but then provided the basis for her providing all kinds of benefits to the cause of the Silver Pact, such as reshaping the Castes and unleashing powerful sorcerous horrors upon the Realm. I also think it’s worthwhile to have this point that the image she projects being disingenuous doesn’t mean that it’s something she’s conflicted over; narratively, it’s okay for her to still come down as a monster.

                                                            Still, I do like the fact that she’s still kind of isolated from the Pact as a whole. She’s a bit more involved than the picture that I had contrived for myself, but I still kind of think that her ultimate motive remains the same; that she wants to have her own slice of the world to herself to revel and engage in her studies, she just pursues that by means of promoting it as the ideal for the whole Pact.

                                                            Then there’s diversifying her method of greeting other Lunars, even when they’re the rivals that she wants to put off balance with atrocities. Sure, the description of her doing such things just to get a social edge is likely to still outrage people, but at least it’s up front about it here so nobody mistakes her meaning. I also like how even when she’s dispensing with the big Queen of Fangs theatrics in the presence of allies, she puts on grand sorcerous displays ala Zorayas. I’ve got this odd sense that, after having been overlooked in the First Age, she has a certain need to be seen. And then there’s her opposite attitude to Ma-Ha-Suchi, going out of her way to mess with him if she can.

                                                            Obviously her mentorship focused on various occult and sorcerous subjects, so the most important part is going into the attitude around it, including how a student might win their way into her good graces and past all of the monster queen shenanigans. Like Ma-Ha-Suchi, I think it’s good to humanise her by drawing attention to the idea that she has some genuine close friends. It’s not said here, but I would find it consistent with the rest that her tutelage can also include developing a sense of self-reliance and a desire for independent authority.

                                                            Huh, Raksi’s write-up seems to be just plain shorter than Ma-Ha-Suchi’s. Either that or she’s a more straightforward character in general, even if she’s more complex than in the past. I’m not complaining, it’s just a curious reversion.

                                                            Anyway, what of her adherents. She’s got a major enforcer who still hasn’t earned his way to being invited past her façade in Dark Eyes, an emissary to carry proclamations to her subjects and get people to try and transform themselves into apefolk. I’m thinking that if Raksi is more politically and administratively active, a lot of her relationships with adherents come from that angle rather than the greater intimacy between Ma-Ha-Suchi and his few students. Hence the interesting element of him having inadvertently become a separate object of veneration to a subset of her subjects, his leveraging that to shape them towards one of his ideals, as well as his own anxiety based on not fully understanding his teacher.

                                                            The story of Weyna Who-Sees-Much makes me think that another useful element of the prohibiton against excessive violence against one another might help a younger Lunar feel secure when calling at a more powerful one in the heart of their domain. Heh, oh dear, it didn’t entirely work out for her, even if the infant child is probably grateful for the chance to not only live, but have an Exalted parent obligated to do the best for her.

                                                            Ahh, here’s something interesting that I think has hardly, if ever, been seen before; a Lunar that was a beastperson before Exalting, as well as being an adherent more from complementary agenda than direct stewardship. It’s an interesting confluence that this character, whose altruism would probably be laudable to a lot of readers, draws real and meaningful inspiration from one of the worst monsters in the setting.

                                                            So, overall, I think Raksi does a good job, both in refining her prior concepts and serving as a distinct contrast in style to her former best friend.
