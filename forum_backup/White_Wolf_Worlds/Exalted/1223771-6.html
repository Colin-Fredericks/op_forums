    Not to necro the thread much further, but Wyldwraith what you may have missed from what I and others in the thread said was that 3E when it has not spoken in a thing is drawing upon 1E roots. In fact, we are not saying throw 1E out except where 3E has said something new on the matter. I think you don’t seem to realize how huge of a gulf there in fact is between 1E and 2E and why we are emphasizing the break. A perfect example of this is an old elemental favorite of mine, the Mercury Ant. Mercury Ants in 1E (Games of Divinity) where communal builders who had some neat tricks about shaping tunnels and would attack only if provoked...2E gave them for some godforsaken reason an aphrodisiac bite that drove people insane overtime. And concocted a really dumb story to make mercury ants hostile to humans, more than jut having someone invade your territory already would mind you. THAT is what people push back against when they say 2E is...problematic. 2E took a perfectly serviceable and cool elemental and added needless sexual torture and emnity when it was not needed to the 1E base. It’s why the Devs reference 1E as their starting point as distinct and different from 2e.

                Apologies then,
                Perhaps I missed the 1E-return thing as an unspoken aside, as a new guy. Thinking on what you said Saipjas, I concur that 2E introduced a lot of unnecessary squick to things 1E had treated, and not sure why that was, honestly. So my sincerest apologies there.

                On the "we've been told by Devs X, Y and Z factoid" though...Honestly, after lurking through some of the explosions about people falling in love with some concept a Dev apparently brought up at one point, only for that concept to be subsequently abandoned (the mini-Loom of the Caul built by Lunars leaps to mind), I've adopted a "It's real when the book gets published" mindset. There's more than enough meat on the bone to tussle over when the meat is actually real, without tussling over the possibly-real/possibly-not marrow of the bone, IMHO.

                Personally, I am hoping for at least *some* homogeneity among the children of Celestial Exalts. Variance would be wonderful, given that Exalted shards are varied by Caste, and that there are two divergences of Solar shards, but I kind of fell in love with the notion of "Golden Children" and by extension Moon-Touched, Star-Blessed etc. I would be perfectly happy if the variances came in such beings mechanical aptitudes, and the similarities were in some more fluff-related element. Like a distinguishing cosmetic characteristic, or a personality influence. I'm glad to hear that God-Bloods and Exalt-children will be receiving different treatment, and hope the other X-Bloods get detailed treatment as well. The penetration of the supernatural into the mortal societies is at the heart of Exalted for me.

                Mechanically, I would *adore* a well-delineated, well-balanced system for dealing with X-Bloods of various purity. I brought up a hypothetical in another thread, concerning the idea of a God-Blood themselves breeding with a God, and a Demon-Blood being ordered by their demonic progenitor to breed with a demon. Such "three-quarters" children, and the inverse more-mortal "one-quarter" children of X-bloods have always interested me greatly. The West is apparently loaded with islanders with various degrees of divine and elemental blood, and being able to chart that would be great.

                Not sure how I feel about the idea of distancing Beastfolk from their Lunar origins. I would prefer a demarcation of some sort, between those aware of their Lunar descent, and those either unaware of it or stem from another source. Fluff material could handle that well enough, if it's truly necessary in the first place. I hope it's well-written and considered if they do distance them from their Lunar roots, however.

                Lastly, I should probably admit a certain bias against some of the changes being made in 3E. I'm a huge "Lunar guy", and this whole "Primal howl in the souls of Lunars. Pushing them to tear down the Realm and destroy the reminders of their failures" thing really feels like more of the Wyld-Taint in Lunar Exaltations thing, or "Lunars are woad-painted barbarians, all in love with Amoth City-Smiter, and seeking to make Creation into one big barbarian anarchy-zone, and those who don't want to be that have to resist the primal howl in their souls just to be civilized." It feels like more, "Must claim a Territory, must do X, Y and Z" that Lunars NEVER seem to be able to escape, and I cannot for the life of me comprehend why incredibly talented writers, who are provably capable of designing amazing Splats, can't get off this Exaltation Homogeneity kick that has hounded Lunars for two editions and looks primed to run into the third. I find the DBs incredibly fascinating, especially Outcastes, but WFHW did not fill me with joy, mechanically. There's a thread someone just created which explains my feelings on the matter with such breath-taking articulation I wanted to applaud when I read it.

                So, personally, I'm experiencing something of a psychological impetus to cling to the old. Don't get me wrong, Heaven knows the mechanics needed an overhaul, and I love some of the new. The very idea of the Caul makes me want to applaud again. I'm just not so sure how much 3E-Love is actually 3E-Love, and how much is 2E-Justified Squick and Mechanic-Hate being expressed as a desperate hope for better in 3E. I also realize the inherent hypocrisy in saying "don't argue over what isn't published yet" and then going on to platform for my own 3E-hopes-and-fears. Sorry about that.
