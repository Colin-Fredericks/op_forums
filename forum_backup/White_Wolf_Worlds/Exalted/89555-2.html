            Originally posted by Morangias View Post
            Tangentially, it's quite fascinating how so many groups have it easier to assemble a ragtag band of murderhobos from bumfuck nowhere into a party than create a group of peers sharing common background in a highly organized, very social culture. I've seen that problem come up with both versions of Vampire, as well as Legend of the Five Rings.
            That's because GMs and players do not regularly sit down during character development and work together to make a cohesive backstory. As much as nearly every RPG book says it is a great idea to do just that, it is very rare.

            Generally speaking, it isn't necessarily a bad thing to be a band of thugs brought together by happenstance. I mean, that's the Italian Job or the Sting right there. Plenty of fertile ground for a game.

            Most systems have their player character mechanics gates to ensure that there's a standard power level for each character. This standardization of power means that it is more permissible for characters to come up with characters in a vacuum, rather than as part of a cooperative experience, because it ensures that one character will not significantly outshine another.

            However with Solar Exalted, it is quite possible to develop a character, right from the get-go, that mechanically far and away surpasses other PCs. In fact, it is incredibly easy and even likely that a Dawn will be created exactly like that because that's the path of least resistance in creating a Dawn. This underscores the importance of the players and ST working together to create a balanced Solar party.

                Most systems have their player character mechanics gates to ensure that there's a standard power level for each character. This standardization of power means that it is more permissible for characters to come up with characters in a vacuum, rather than as part of a cooperative experience, because it ensures that one character will not significantly outshine another.

                However with Solar Exalted, it is quite possible to develop a character, right from the get-go, that mechanically far and away surpasses other PCs.                 This obviously varies a lot group to group. None of the people I play with ever create characters in isolation; group character creation will often take a full session or two before we start actual play. Sometime character concepts will be hashed out for weeks before play starts.

                And there's an inherent implication in this whole thread that combat is a significant portion of play time. I have been in sessions of Exalted where no combat occurred at all.

                I think the bigger problem is really that 2e is so bad of a system that if you play it the way being implied - isolated character creation, frequent combats - you're left in a situation where, unless absolutely everyone has close to perfect system mastery, there are a billion traps to fall into in terms of creating wide power gaps, and those power gaps will matter excessively, because anytime combat happens it's going to take forever to resolve.

                Thing is, 2e's combat is a reason not to play 2e, even before the other issues come into play. So the problems that arise mostly because of that combat system aren't an issue with one splat over another... they're an issue with 2e combat effectively making it so that you don't really have room to do anything else if combat is happening with any frequency. When you solve the resolution time issue, either by using a less obnoxious system or by having relatively few combat situations, almost all of the concerns you were expressing go away, at least, in the hands of a halfway competent ST.

                There is very little accounting for bad STs in any of this.

                            Originally posted by Eolirin View Post
                            And there's an inherent implication in this whole thread that combat is a significant portion of play time. I have been in sessions of Exalted where no combat occurred at all.
                            I think combat, generally, is an inherent part of RPGs as a form. Obviously there can be sessions, stories, campaigns, and whole groups that never roll out combat. However, I think most groups do have combat regularly.

                            The reason I bring up combat is not only is it a significant part of gaming itself, but that Exalted combat, as a system, is bloated and frequently takes way, way too long. This is particularly true of game groups that do not play Exalted exclusively. The system mastery required to run Exalted combat is very high, and I would like it if there was a way to tone those requirements down. That's also why Root's suggestion that any Solar can get extra action charms isn't a solution, but an aggravating element because those charms make combat take way, way too long.

                            I want to be able to run Exalted with a group that traditionally plays D&D. That's just not possible at present.

                            I think the bigger problem is really that 2e is so bad of a system that if you play it the way being implied - isolated character creation, frequent combats - you're left in a situation where, unless absolutely everyone has close to perfect system mastery, there are a billion traps to fall into in terms of creating wide power gaps, and those power gaps will matter excessively, because anytime combat happens it's going to take forever to resolve.
                            Yeah, the problem is not in one system, but the cumulative effect of multiple factors all playing together.

                            ...in the hands of a halfway competent ST.

                            There is very little accounting for bad STs in any of this.
                            One problem is that STing Exalted requires a great deal of system mastery, more than the system mastery required to run most other RPGs. The bar for a competent ST is much higher than a competent D&D DM. This is in part because a new D&D DM can (and probably should) start at level one where there is a limited set of abilities and such with which to contend. In Exalted, the mechanics gates are fewer so it is much easier to create a new, complicated character in Exalted than it is in D&D. I stress that this is a concern of system mastery, as distinguished from general GM mastery.
