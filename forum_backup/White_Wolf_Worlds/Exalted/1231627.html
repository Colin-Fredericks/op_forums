    My Judge's Ears Are Burning... (Ex3)

    07-01-2018, 03:35 PM
    Originally posted by wern212 View Post
    In a similar vein: are there any other ways to decieve a Solar with Judge's Ear Technique?
    ​
    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
    Easiest way is to avoid speaking to them face-to-face.
    ​Vance, as usual, is not wrong, but I'd like to take a moment to expand a bit on the options one has to defeat Judge's Ear Technique. This is far from comprehensive; I might even add to it later, particularly if good suggestions are made by others.

    Even putting aside the possibility of magic forcing Judge's Ear Technique into a roll-off, there are non-magical ways to defeat a Lawgiver with this admittedly-potent Solar Investigation Charm.

    ​Method #1: Withholding the Statement

    ​A Lawgiver with Judge's Ear Technique can activate it to evaluate a given statement to determine falsehood. If there's no statement, the Lawgiver can't determine falsehood. If you have the option to not be there, don't be there. If you're forced into contact with the Lawgiver, refuse to speak. Take the fifth, as it were. If you are compelled to answer questions, write down the answers. If you must open your mouth, talk about anything but what he wants to hear.

    Of course, if he has ways of making you talk, or your silence would only serve to make you more suspicious, you can take advantage of...

    ​Method #2: The Activation Cost

    ​Judge's Ear Technique requires three motes of Essence to use. If he doesn't have three motes, a Lawgiver will have to resort to mundane means to differentiate truth from lies. This one requires creativity and a good poker face, so it's not as easy as Method #1, but it's still worth considering, especially if you've been forced into an audience, but have a way out after. Namely, the trick here is to talk his Judge's Ear off. An Essence 1 Solar only has enough divine resources to activate Judge's Ear Technique fifteen times without taking a break to recharge, and that's assuming they haven't spent any on social rolls, academic matters, or attunement costs, and have no interest in marshalling their divine power for the possibility of combat in the future.

    ​Also remember that just because Judge's Ear flags one of your statements as a lie, doesn't mean the Solar automatically knows what the truth is; telling him that the stolen shipment is buried in the palace courtyard, under the trees that make an "X" shape, only reveals the one place he won't find it.

    ​Of course, if he's just gonna keep you in his custody until he gets his answers, it's best to send...

    Method #3: The Proxy

    ​If the person speaking to the Solar doesn't know that what they're saying isn't true, Judge's Ear has no way of knowing the statement is false. A good tactic is to convince a third party of the truth of your words, and send them to go talk to the Solar for you.

    ​Of course, if none of those are quite the right fit for you, you might be...

    ​Method #4: The Ebon Dragon

    ​The biggest vulnerability of Judge's Ear Technique is that the user has to decide to activate it. If you must tell a lie, tell one that would pain the Solar to know is falsehood; if you must tell a truth, tell one so terrible that the Solar would never accept it.

              The wording on it though is:
              "Her sense of lies is keen enough that if a character
              speaks in half-truths, she’ll know which part of the

              So, something which is technically correct I think still goes. Since there's no actual part of the statement which is false.
              (Actually, the way it says she knows which part of the statement is true seems a little odd, not sure how to read that: it'd make sense for a sentence containing both true and false things, but not really for half-truths. Well, maybe sometimes)

              For example, my Night Caste, Golden Hero (not his real name) met a very annoying Twilight, King Elf-Wise, who indignantly stated "that's a lie" every time my Night Caste lied. He doesn't understand diplomacy at all, or public relations.

              So I told him things like "I was a slave" and "my father was a blacksmith."

              Now saying "I was a slave" is misleading, because he was a middle-class intellectual in a country where everyone was legally a slave to the Scarlet Empress. It's quite misleading. But there's no part of that statement that's actually false.
              Similarly, he didn't mention that his father was a successful businessman with 20 under-smiths under him, who only did the occasional bit of actual smithing himself, mostly he managed others. But "he was a blacksmith" doesn't really have any false parts to it.
              If Elf-Wise misinterprets these statements to think that Golden Hero is from a humble background, that's his own problem.

                                  A word of caution on half-truths such as what Oz suggests, these can come down to Storyteller adjudication on the particulars of the context being used. To take his slave example, if the client state in question were not dejure slaves and were instead only defacto slaves, or they were simply “slaves” in the way Marxian doctrine views the prolitariate prior to revolution, then the ST could reasonably rule those outright falsehoods. As always, where there ar grey areas best check with/know how your ST views these kinds of things to avoid contentious conflicts between player and ST.

                                      Originally posted by Saipjas View Post
                                      A word of caution on half-truths such as what Oz suggests, these can come down to Storyteller adjudication on the particulars of the context being used.
                                      Yeah, I've found that most often, "magical lie detector" abilities in RPG's simply come down to ST interpretation of truths and such. As a player, you might end up having to pause the game and argue for some time with the ST over why what your character said was "technically true" and shouldn't ping the NPC's lie detector. Likewise players might get annoyed when they use their character's magical lie detector on an NPC, are told the NPC isn't lying then discover later that the ST was very carefully treading the truth in order to nullify the player's ability.

                                      You might have some groups where players love arguing over what the definition of "is" is and whether a character's statement is a life, a half-truth or technically true, and whether it will ping someone's lie detector. But you'll have other groups (and I've played in them) with players who find the whole thing aggravating and get annoyed whenever the game comes to a halt so that a player and ST can argue over whether a given statement is true or not.

                                      Most often, just to save player aggravation and time, when someone decides to have a character lie to someone with a magical lie detector, rather than playing it out in character (and then arguing over whether things said by the player or ST are true or not), I prefer to have the character just make a a Manipulation roll to see how good they are at avoiding the whole truth while not tripping the lie detector.
