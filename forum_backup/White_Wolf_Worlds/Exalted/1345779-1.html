                Originally posted by Hark View Post
                That sure as hell is dodgey, and that is what makes it realistic. There is a reason the term manipulative has negative connotations in English, people do try to cold sell you stuff all the time and yeah it does work sometimes.
                I would argue (from both a personal and business perspective) that cold selling generally appeals to what are assumed to be Intimacies. (Note to remember cultural intimacies below.) One that is prevalent today is, 'I want to be comfortable.' So someone 'cold selling' you a car might try a tack that it's more comfortable. Or, in another venue, most people who are applying for jobs are assumed to have an Intimacy, 'I want more money.' (That may be a derivative of, 'I want to be comfortable (and I need money to do that)'), but I think that is more of an indictment of our culture than anything else.

                As a business example, my company is a tech company in a pretty small niche. I get weekly if not daily emails and even phone calls from tech providers who see that I am a tech company in this niche and they think that I can use their services. And I don't. This is a classic example of not having an Intimacy to support your Persuade action. If someone takes the time to understand my personal or professional Intimacies (you know, like Read Intentions), they would quickly realize this and not waste both of our time.

                Basically, I think that, in Exalted terms, cold selling is mainly a straight Persuade action with guessing of Intimacies based on cultural norms, and it has a success rate proportional to how prevalent that Intimacy is.

                On that note, something that would both help gameplay and work with Lunars is to explicitly model Cultural Intimacies. That is, there are Intimacies that can be assumed as part of a Culture. People of that Culture are more or less likely to have this Intimacy. You could also work to erode this Intimacy. I'm not looking at straitjackets, here, but something like this:

                Assume in contemporary American culture that there is an Intimacy of 'Government should not be trusted.' Now, not everyone has this Intimacy. In fact, someone may have the opposite Intimacy. However, they worked to overcome this. (That is, they are counterculture in this regard.) So instead of assuming that, except otherwise stated, someone's rating for an Intimacy is 0 (i.e., it doesn't exist), assume that someone's rating is whatever the culture's rating is. Then, adjust from there.

                Not only does this allow for the definition of culture in game terms, it would then be a system that works with Charms. A few Solar Charms (like Mastery of Small Manners) already dabble in this space. Lunar Social Charms have entire cascades dealing with manipulating Cultural Intimacies, but since they're not defined explicitly, it ends up feeling... weird.

                I dunno. This feels like it needs a bit more work, but this may help (me, at least) with fleshing out the system and working with it.

                    Although I have struggled with the issue of when to spend WP to block new intimacies, especially when STing and it's an NPCs being influenced. I kind of have some internal guidelines built from my experience, but I'd love to see how others handle it. On the one hand letting someone roll into a character's life and upturn it completely with a couple of rolls can be unfun, but so can blocking too many new intimacies and frustrating the players or the ST. I'd love to get people's thoughts on this (might make a new thread if people think it's appropriate)


                    Personally, I look at it this way: Would the instilled Intimacy go against a Defining Intimacy? If so, the character might as well spend themselves empty trying to resist that. Would the instilled Intimacy go against a Major Intimacy? Then they spend WP unless they're at half or less WP and don't feel like it's worth the exhaustion. Would it go against a Minor one? They spend WP if they're at full WP, but otherwise... nah, not worth.

                    If it goes WITH another Intimacy, in a way that causes them to lose Resolve against the Influence, then it becomes increasingly difficult for them to resist with WP: Going against no Intimacy but upholding a Minor or greater Intimacy of theirs means they will not spend WP unless it would change their character concept significantly; the higher the upheld Intimacy is, the less inclined they'd be to resisting EVEN if it changes their concept. Going against and for equal-intensity Intimacies cancels each other out which means they might or might not resist based on context. Going against a higher Intimacy than the one you uphold or vice-versa: basically subtract one with the other, the result applies as if you were going for or against that. For example, going against a Defining but upholding a Minor is equivalent to going against a Major one flatly.

                    Stuff like Psyche effects or other non-natural influence are treated on a case by case basis, but often resisted as soon as possible, which doesn't tend to be very soon.

                    Just a very vague eyeballing of how I think about NPCs resisting or not, in the end just doing the fun thing works.

                                Gotta say I'm loving the idea of cultural intimacies. You could even have the strength of it as how ingrained in their culture they are against how free thinking/iconoclastic they are. So You might have Lookshy culture as an intimacy. At defining level its a character defined by their patriotism, who has dedicated themselves to public service, works hard on living up to the public virtues of the people of Lookshy and probably looks down on those undisciplined rabble of the other Scavenger Land cities. At minor it might be someone who was born and raised in Lookshy but is more cosmopolitan, they've maybe travelled or read widely. They might hold their culture quite fondly while, for instance, also believing that other cities have their own virtues and that the Lookshy virtues aren't the only worthy ones. Appeals to their patriotism would be harder. Someone with a major intimacy might be fully inculcated in their culture but either hasn't questioned it sufficient for doubt, or who has questioned it and come to terms with some of its weaknesses, able to admit the occassional flaw in the people of Lookshy, but generally regarding the Lookshy way as superior. Someone with the intimacy at 0 (i.e. doesn't have it) might be indifferent having been raised outside of the public school system, such as by travellers or criminals. They might well understand the Lookshy virtues and might have them as intimacies seperately, but not see themselves as a patriot.

                                I'd say that almost every character should probably have at least a minor cultural intimacy, unless they have a really good reason (raised in captivity, etc). Its hard, and not always desirable to shed what you learned through your upbringing, and most cultures have genuinely worthy virtues and cultural themes to believe in.

                                Thanks for the feedback all, this has been really helpful. I like the idea of throwing out minor intimacies willy-nilly and also of having a quick check with players every few sessions to see if they think any of their intimacies have changed.

                                    Gribble, I think you misunderstood me. I don't want Lookshy Culture (Respect) as a Tie. That tells me nothing about the Culture.

                                    I want Lookshy Culture to defined as a set of Intimacies:

                                    Military Structure (Trust) - Major
                                    The Scarlet Dynasty (Distrust) - Major
                                    The Scavenger Lands (Protective) - Minor
                                    .
                                    .
                                    .

                                    And then the typical character from Lookshy has those Intimacies. The fact of the matter is that the more Intimacies someone has, the better the Social System of Exalted works. There's a lot of complaints about Exalts just ignoring Intimacies and generating the ones they need. The most likely reason is that just coming up with a lot of Intimacies is time-consuming, sometimes difficult, and (for the players) often counter to what they want.

                                    And it should be noted that PC Intimacies are very often weaknesses. They're handles on a set of people that tend to not want handles. Well... how do you deal with that?

                                    It would also be a source of crunch in the books like The Realm and Across the Eight Directions. You could list some cultural Ties for each area discussed in those books. I realize that it's too late for the Realm, but it may be something to think about in the future...

                                    It also gives a pure niche for Lunar Social Combat. Solars create and manipulate Personal Intimacies. Lunars create and manipulate Cultural Intimacies.

                                    And, again, individuals can diverge from their Culture's Intimacies, but that should take effort. As an example, I've been rereading the Honor Harrington series, and in it the Solarian League's Office of Frontier Security would very clearly have a Cultural Intimacy of 'Corruption? What's that?' However, one OFS director, Oravil Barregos, is well known for the fact that he is not corrupt. This is just an example where someone diverges from the Culture.

                                    I can think of a lot of hooks that this could lead to, with the possible downside of being treated as a straitjacket. Even then, though, I think that are many other systems (and subsystems) in Exalted 3E that have a worse issue with this....

                                    Anyway, just trying to clarify my stance.

                                                Also to answer the main topic (I will mainly repeat what others said so I guess we are now a Battle group of forum posters) :

                                                1) Minor, and to a certain extent major, intimacies can change quite often, I'd say every character see 4-5 changes in intimacies or in inspire per session (like 1 person did a dance stuff, one person tried to threaten everyone because he was angry, there was RP between different characters and one tried to setup a bro bond with another because last time he helped him do one thing, this kind of stuff). Expecting less could become boring I think, but I don't know, some people seems to have different experiences

                                                2) All intimacies are not noted on the character sheet. It has been said intimacies not strong enough might not be noted. I would like to nuance this ; I rather think of intimacies as narrative indicator of what kind of topics makes sense to involve the character in the story, so some extremely important cornerstone of a character might not be noted as a Defining intimacy if they are not narratively relevant. For example, I think lots of characters I have seen could certainly have a Major or Defining intimacy of love to their parents (If I threw their parents in mortal dangers, I'm pretty sure lots of characters I've seen would travel the world to try to save them), but they had no intimacy related to this. To me, it informs the ST and other players that this is not relevant for the story (despite the character personal feelings). It doesn't mean another character should never use these "hidden" intimacies as a leverage, but rather that it should not be considered the go-to solution because concerned players are likely not interested in it. I think I'm influenced seeing intimacies like them with the work of aspirations from the last Chronicles of Darkness games, I really liked these mechanics.

                                                3) Finally, every few scenes, I would propose players to take 15 ins to look at their intimacies and tell me if they felt it was still relevant to their character, and with en intensity that make sense. It also allows to eliminate intimacies that have no more purpose because the story changed.

                                                Overall I think being around 10 intimacies is good (no need to start at 10, but expect lots of people to at least come close to 10 after few sessions of play even if they start at the 4 mandatory). I would never go beyond 15, and I would advise my players to not go there as well. But it's only my advice, I totally get that some people love to have a lot of intimacies. However, I think with too many of them it becomes more and more incompatible with any intent to have a focus on strong narration.

                                                            In the games I've run and played in, I've seen some significant intimacy changes. I've never seen a defining intimacy go down, but the PC I played ended up with a minor tie to his familiar developing into a defining one. He certainly developed a couple of major intimacies, and a host of minor ones. In the game I run, I've definitely managed to develop intimacies up to the major level in PCs mostly through the work of NPCs to persuade them. In most cases they're ones that the character could have spent willpower on avoiding, but which the player thought would be an interesting turn for the character. Ones like that usually remain at the minor level. And other times when I'm ST-ing I might ask something like, "You've spent a lot of time around this NPC. Have you developed an intimacy towards them?" Usually even if it happens immediately before a roll that might leverage it, my players are honest about it. The only ones they're adamant about refusing are ones that I'm obviously going to try to use against them later (Makarios is trying to instill a Principle of "I lust for Makarios' goods").

                                                            And there's always the option to just try to persuade someone based on an intimacy you guess that they have, even without a Read Intentions roll. It's a risky option, especially if you try to boost the roll with charms, since even with a gazillion successes the attempt might be unsuccessful if the intimacy you're banking on isn't there. But if their Guile is high, it might be the only way to glean information about someone's intimacies.
