                        I think it's something that rests in the cross-section of "the Populat still has a lot of agency and creativity because they're humans" and "life in Autochthonia is very strictly regimented, especially with regards to logistics". So food is generally going to be basic because of the priorities for efficiency of the people allocating it, at the same time as some people are going to creatively apply what they can scrounge together to garnish it and what the state will periodically offer up to keep things gratifying and motivating.

                        In this, probably the only difference from Creation is the exact source and distribution of the garnishes. The typical diet of a common worker in the Octet likely only differs from that of the regular farmer of Creation in that at least whether or not you eat is not directly dependent on your own labour (and the individual worker isn't making the decisions that stretch food between available periods and who goes hungry during famine).

                                Corebook p. 173 states that "Any aggravated injury cannot be healed by magic, nor can its healing be sped up by magic—only natural rest and healing will mend it." And yet, even the corebook has multiple Medicine Charms that convert aggravated damage to lethal.

                                Should this simply be treated as a "specific beats general" thing -- that is, Charms that specifically mention aggravated damage do what they say, but things like Body-Mending Meditation (which speeds natural healing but doesn't mention specific damage types) won't work on aggravated damage?

                                Honestly, the main reason I ask is that the sheer number of examples of magic that can do this really makes it hard to take the "magic can't do this" rule seriously at all. Also, a number of Infernal Medicine Charms specify that they heal "non-aggravated damage," leading me to wonder if the writers may have intended things like Immortal Fiend Invigoration (like BMM, not specifying damage types) to work on aggravated damage. But I'm probably biased because I dislike PCs being stuck with long healing times.
