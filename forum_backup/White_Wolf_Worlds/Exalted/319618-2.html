                Originally posted by wulf View Post
                From what I understand, Ex3 will do away with the concept of "Solar-Tiered". So I don't believe there's a worry here with regards to which "level" of power the Exigent Exalted are or could be.
                To a degree. An Exigent that has as much depth of power as a Solar, combined with the breadth of power of a Solar ("perfection" is a broad theme, after all) is probably a little overpowered.

                Holden's Argent Arrow example is really great to illustrate the point. Argent Arrow may have healing Charms (I hesitate to say Medicine Charms, since Argent Arrow could be Attribute- or Essence-based, not Ability-based) that functionally compare to Solar Medicine in terms of power.

                But say Argent Arrow is Ability-Based. Argent Arrow's "mend item" Charms in Craft and "mend holes in knowledge" Charms in Lore and "mend alliances" Charms in Socialize are going to work very differently (in theme and probably also in mechanics) than Solar Craft, Solar Lore, and Solar Socialize. As in, Solar Craft is going to let you build things along with mending them; Argent Arrow may not have "build things" Craft Charms, only "fix them." Also, like thematically, Solar Resistance has generally always been about being harder than the thing hitting you, versus having crazy fast healing; Argent Arrow's Resistance Charms will probably be about quickly purging yourself of poison or disease or regenerating wounds fast, etc. versus making your skin harder than adamant.

                        The answer is that if you have to ask, then you probably wouldn't. But that is more of a statement on you (and what you want out of the game) than it is a statement on Exigents or their design.

                        Not that there's anything wrong with wanting to play The Most Powerful Person Ever. Exalted is totally designed to be a game that lets you do that; I mean, that's one of its core things. But by the same token, it's not like the game is going to try to entice you to play a less-than-most-powerful-ever Exigent if that's what you seem to want. The reward for playing a less powerful type of Exigent is that you get to be less powerful; if you don't want that, don't do it.

                        (Well, obviously the other issue is that people might not let you play an Exigent modeled after Solars in their DB game for obvious reasons.)

                            Originally posted by wulf View Post

I imagine in 3E a similar essence 3-4 Solar would have to consider even a single Dragonblood of comparative essence a threat. The Solar may have an advantage, but I doubt it's going to be large enough that he's gonna handwave the encounter.

                                                    You know, Zelbinnean, I agree almost entirely with everything you posted in this thread, re: the power of Solars, except one thing that's causing me such a nervous tick that I have to break my lurking to mention: the theme of Solars isn't Perfection. It was never Perfection. That was really a flanderization of them in 2nd edition, and it led nowhere good. Their theme is much deeper than that.

                                                    (And absolutely unique as far as tabletop games go, too.)

                                                    As for the Exigent thing, an anedocte!

                                                    In 1st Edition, Terrestrial Martial Arts, the published ones, were extremely powerful for their level. Five-Dragon Blocking Technique was the only persistant defense available to Dragon-Blooded, Five-Dragon Fortitude was a soak-adder with no limit, and I'm pretty sure Five-Dragon Force-Blow was one of the best ways to do horrendous damage cheap at low ess two editions running? Jade Mountain had the hax that was Pasiap Still Stands, too, for anyone without a perfect defense, like say, Ghosts. But the fan-made ones weren't like that. By and large they looked at 'being weaker than Celestial Charms', which means if you looked at all in the wiki, they were... all very poor.

                                                    And in this baseline of poor, I recall at least two that were, explicitly, said to not be trying to be 'overpowered' and just be artful and neat, in their description.

                                                    The baseline was 'suck', and they were going for 'suckier', because, hey, it was pretty, and it fit.

                                                    Always been like that. Making Charms in Exalted is as much art, creative writing, neat ideas, as it is mechanics. There's a reason Infernal themed-sets and Martial Arts were all the rage. So look at how few Celestial Martial Arts were actually overpowered, even in 2e, and there's your answer.

                                                            Originally posted by TalosX View Post
                                                            I imagine in 3E a similar essence 3-4 Solar would have to consider even a single Dragonblood of comparative essence a threat.
                                                            Part of the reason that "tier" is not a useful piece of terminology is because it tends to assume a certain uniformity of application and context.

                                                            I doubt a lone Essence 3 Dragon Blood will have very much luck at all if they act stupidly and just wander up to a circle of Essence 3 Solars and smack one in the face, but if they do something like lay traps and use elaborate tactics and keep their distance somewhat...

                                                            The game equips characters with a diverse range of things to approach challenges with. If an Essence 3 Dragon Blood rushes into town and uses Charm-enhanced frantic warning of approaching monsters to stir everybody into a frenzy, and the Solars come in to find themselves surprised and overwhelmed by a mob of several thousand fanatics (and manage to take two-thirds of them down with them) while the Terrestrial hangs back, shouting encouragement and occasionally lobbing in fireballs, who is ranked higher?

                                                            Let's be clear; they're not "doing away" with tiers, they just don't use the terminology or what it implies as a way of building them game.
