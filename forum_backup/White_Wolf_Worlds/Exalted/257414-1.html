                            Originally posted by Weimann View Post
                            From what I've understood, Exalts will no longer automatically have Essence 2 from the start, so it should be more smooth in that regard at least. I wonder if we'll actually see any Ability 1, Essence 1 Charms beyond Excellencies, though
                            I could see the case against, though I could easily see it happening. I mean, obviously 1 dot in a skill isn't anything world-shaking, but it does indicate that one has developed oneself in that area beyond the assumed default of 0. In principle, that's 5 levels of advancement beyond the baseline you've got to work with when building a Charmset.

                            Hmm. Spoilering, as I abandon all pretense that I'm not on a tangent for a few sentences:
                            I wonder if one of the difficulties of an Attribute-based Charmset is that (at least in 1e and 2e) 2-dots is already the baseline, so you functionally only have 3 levels of development beyond "normal" to build the set. I remember Holden once said a Charmset's structural thesis is harder to make for an Attribute-based one than for an Ability-based one, unless using a very different approach to "cheat" like Alchemicals did. Having fewer levels above the baseline could be one factor.

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                        I don't really care for the idea of the Scarlet Empress having been given a super-Destiny anymore, but when I did I liked to imagine that Chejop despondently looked out over advancing armies of Fair Folk and planted a prayer strip with the question "how do I fix this?", and coming back five days later to find leaves with little factoids about a certain Dragon Blooded officer, as well as a certain trinket that he didn't understand but during his meeting with the Empress proved critical with convincing her that he was on the level.

                                        That's probably only slightly lower down than the Great Curse, but I still think the basic outline of it is both useful and interesting.
                                        Embracing Life Method is great as a plot point generator or as a way to hit the GM and say "Okay, we're stumped, where should we go next?" It's next to useless in an actual investigation, because in the five days you have to wait for it to sprout you'd get the same or better results just by beating feet.

                                        I've never been in the school of thought that says that you should only ever bother to do rolls you can enhance with Charms, hell my last campaign I made two or three times the unenhanced rolls as the other players, but if I'm buying a Charm that's supposed to help with investigation, then, ya'know, I want it to help with investigation.

                                        I love Jenna Moran, don't get me wrong, but I think I'm at the point where I'm willing to sacrifice some of her trademark weirdness so that Sidereals don't wind up with a charmset (a closed charmset, remember) that's half gimmicky bullshit.

                                        And yes, for forty fucking XP to buy all five versions of the Charm instead of something sensible like buying it once and then buying the other four purviews for one XP each, I expect some kind of goddamn concrete benefit out of it.

                                        See, TDO, I'm very very conflicted on 3e Sidereals.

                                        On the one hand, you guys wrote Sidereal Firearms and Sidereal Drive, both of which I absolutely love.
