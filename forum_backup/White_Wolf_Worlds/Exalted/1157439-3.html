                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                        ​And the next one, and the next one, and the next, until there are what? 15, 23, more?
                                        I know I shouldn't, but I've chosen to respond to your direct question.
                                        Short answer; yes.

                                        Longer answer; if you need too. You only need so many charms for a character, or a group there of. Even if you go with say 5 themes per Yozi, making at least 1 per ability (115*25=2875) and keep the duplicate effects (maybe allow the choosing of a Shintai keyword once per purchase and some cosmetic differences), which I freely admit is upping the complexity...you won't need that many charms. You won't want to write them and most people will gloss over the majority anyway (sadly). That including Shintai charms, if those are going to be a thing. So yes, it can be complex.

                                        But in practice, some of those themes just aren't applicable to every ability. I can see the Ebon Dragon create a Melee Charm enhancing a feint gambit (sneaky, treachery, etc) but I can't see a Malfeas charm in Stealth. It is VERY important to remember that they don't have to be. Nor do the Infernals even need to use the caste ability list of the Solars. Infernals get their own design space, with their own designated elements, strengths and weaknesses. Look at the cool weirdness in 3e Lunars and Dragonblooded and see how their general outlines let their themes shine in individual charms. Do we really need SWLIHN to have a Dodge charm? It just isn't relevant to her. Malfeas getting a Performance charm that substitutes Performance for Dodge is much more interesting and flavourful. Meanwhile Cecelyne can have a Performance charm that enhances prayer, possible even two way comunication with spirit in question, with an upgrade at higher Essence that allows the Exalt to hear and respond to prayers directed at them. Different Yozi, different themes, same ability.

                                        The issue is that the themes are represented, not that they are everywhere. Malfeas doen't need that many dancing Charms. If a theme isn't that important or useful to charm design, it doesn't even have to be used. Heck, this is a new edition, the Yozi may have different themes! I'm not against the Infernals having their own themes separate from the Yozi, but I would like the Yozi's themes to echo through the Green Sun Princes both to drive home the connection between the two and because it is simpler to connect the those elements to charm design than generate whole new ones and colour them in after. Yes the charmset i am imaging would be a mishmash of different themes (from different Yozi) spread across all the abilities. I can see how this can create some thematic conflict with infernal characters if we just take Yozi themes apply them individually and let people pick up conflicting themes in said characters.

                                        And it is beautiful.

                                        Yozi might not be charm engine primordials anymore, but they are concepts & themes bound into living worlds who clash and compromise in order to create anything lasting outside of the Wyld or themselves. Someone should struggle to reconcile trying to be both the Ebon Dragon, She Who Lives In Her Name and Isidoros all at the same time
                                        That is part of why I do like some of the ideas being discussed. The concept that Yozi inspired charms are revelatory and transformative is brilliant. It even plays into my ideal infernal charmset. What have you learned? How has it changed you? Likewise I do enjoy reading your posts Isator Levi. I find your perspective to be occasionally enlightening and always interesting.

                                        I'm not sure I can in good faith contribute positively to the discussion, so I'll bow out for now.

                                        Thoughts ripple out, birthing others
