            I have pedantic 3e newb grapple queries if anyone has the time/inclination…

            1. How does grapple interact with onslaught penalties? I’ve searched for definitive answers but come up dry.

            My instinctive interpretation is that the gambit itself inflicts an onslaught penalty, as does each effect (savage, throw etc).

            (My instincts are often wildly inaccurate…)

            As an example, the following scenario has come up while I’ve been doing idle playthroughs when I’m supposed to be doing something productive. It is very possible I’m being an idiot:



            X is fighting Characters Y and Z.

            TURN 1

            X: 11i, Y: 10i, Z: 8i

            X has a -1 onslaught penalty against the next attack.

            Only Z is left to go this turn.

            Z initiates a grapple. He hits (-3i for the gambit), makes the initiative check but loses the control roll. He still gets to initiate a withering savage against X's defence of 0, withering X for 7 initiative.

            TURN 2

            X: 4i, Y: 10i, Z: 13i

            Z gets to go again before X escapes the grapple and goes with a withering slam against X's defence of 0. He withers for 3 initiative.

            Result is:
            X: 1i, prone, Y: 10i, Z: 17i

            Y makes a withering attack against X.

            Should X suffer an onslaught penalty of -4 (-1 from previous attack, -1 from grapple gambit, -1 from withering savage, -1 from withering slam) on top of the penalties for being prone etc?


            2. If you are grappled, do/can you declare the use of defensive charms, e.g. a soak-boosting charm, at the beginning of every grapple effect (this one seems obvious but I’ve been wrong before. I’ve seen a suggestion they need to be declared at the gambit stage as savaging etc is described as an effect/action not an attack on p.201. That seems mad...)?

            3. Urgh, slightly ridiculously – can you grapple someone who is already being grappled by someone else? It makes some sort of dramatic sense. In the above example, Z could have savaged again before Y dog-piles in and establishes his own hold. On his turn, X kicks Z away but remains trapped in Y's vice-like grip etc etc.

            From an actual gameplay perspective it seems like it could create a tedious nightmare of a combat...

            Thanks to anyone who can take the time to shed any light...

                Originally posted by Moss Reynholm View Post
                1. How does grapple interact with onslaught penalties? I’ve searched for definitive answers but come up dry.
                In the book, this is unclear. My own feeling, though, is that the grapple gambit itself should inflict onslaught, but the stuff the grappler does to the grapple-e should not. Grappling is already pretty potent, and includes a Defense penalty. I'm inclined to think that onslaught would be overkill on top of all that.

                Also, your example has one error in it:

                Originally posted by Moss Reynholm
                Z initiates a grapple. He hits (-3i for the gambit), makes the initiative check but loses the control roll.
                The -3i doesn't hit until after the grappler has rolled their initiative to confirm the gambit.

                Originally posted by Moss Reynholm
                2. If you are grappled, do/can you declare the use of defensive charms, e.g. a soak-boosting charm, at the beginning of every grapple effect
                That's definitely how I'd rule it, yes. You get to put up reflexive defensive charms each time the grappler tries to damage you, not at the start of the overall grapple. The latter method would lead to some weirdness anyway - does it only cost the charm's cost once and then applies to the whole grapple? That's too good! Do you have to declare it at the start, but then pay the price on every instance of damage? What if you're out of motes for all the defensive charms you declared? How do you decide which ones get used? It's both more fair and simpler to just say each attempt to damage the grapple-e is a separate attack, and give them the opportunity to declare defensive charm use at that point.

                Originally posted by Moss Reynholm
                3. Urgh, slightly ridiculously – can you grapple someone who is already being grappled by someone else?
                Yeah. As your example suggests, allowing that is useful for the sort of "dogpile" that often comes up.

                Originally posted by Moss Reynholm
                From an actual gameplay perspective it seems like it could create a tedious nightmare of a combat...
                You're not wrong. I'd suggest a house rule to ease some of this, which is that all grapplers share a single "pool" of rounds of control, and that each additional grappler piling on only gets to add a single round, no matter how good their control roll is. So if the first grappler got the target for 4 rounds, and then three of her friends subsequently joined in and all beat the target's control roll, the grapple would have a total of 7 rounds of control, from which stuff that cost rounds of control would be drawn, no matter which grappler initiated them. Also, effects like Drag or Throw would move the whole "ball" in the case of Drag, and Throw would break the grapple for everybody.

                            Originally posted by Moss Reynholm View Post

                            1. Whether this was debate was ever resolved: someone counter-grappling the person grappling them?
                            I don't know of any canonical answer. How I'd handle it is that the victim could try to start their own grapple against their grappler. If they succeed, then both characters would suffer the penalties for being the victim of a grapple (the defense penalties wouldn't stack, though, as those apply to both grappler and grapple-e already). They'd each have their own rounds of control, and if one ran out before the other, it would mean that the person with rounds of control left would have taken control of the grapple. Most grapple actions would work the same. The one exception would be Restrain, where if one side tried to restrain the other in a mutual grapple, they'd have to make an opposed (Strength + Brawl) roll, where they only get to restrain if the other side lost.

                            Originally posted by Moss Reynholm
                            2. What happens when X, who has grappled Y, is successfully grappled by Z?
                            I'd allow it, and I wouldn't say that X's grapple is automatically ended - there should be a benefit to having a good Brawl skill and specializing in grapples, that isn't easily ended by someone pouncing on you and winning because you're penalized from being in a grapple. But the attack to establish the grapple on X would cost X a round of control in their own grapple. And if Z gets enough rounds of control, and uses them to restrain X, that would automatically end X's grapple, since I interpret "maintaining a grapple" as an action, which restrain effectively prevents from happening.
