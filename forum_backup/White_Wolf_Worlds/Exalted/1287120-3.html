    Originally posted by OpMegs View Post
    I admit that one line in the overall thing there made it feel a bit like the DBs are stuck as "inevitably" losing.



    Now, the situation presented here isn't wrong. It's the keystone of how the Lunar strategy works. But the wording basically presents the Empress' overall response as holing up in the Realm's existing territories and not doing much of anything for fear that Lunar bogeymen will jump out of the shadows and wreck shit the minute she makes a risky play. Which is absolutely the attitude that some members of the Realm likely have, given the psychological warfare going on, but attributing the same to the Empress (versus, say "biding her time until she could find a way to effectively engage them" or something more active and befitting the kind of person that founded something like the Realm in the first place) makes it seem less like a contest between equal players rather than the current situation that doesn't seem to give the Realm any scenario in which their being able to substantially threaten the Pact is even an option.

    Now, the Realm is the Realm. We don't WANT them to win under current circumstances, obviously. But it does make it kinda painfully obvious that Raksi and Ma Ha Suchi did more to damage the Pact via in-fighting than the Realm seems likely to ever do even on a good century.
    Prior material has painted the fall of Dragon-Blooded hegemony as somewhat inevitable - all of the wonders they use to maintain their empire will fail, the empire will crumble, and there isn't much, if anything, that can stem the tide, because they're squatting on stuff that Solars built for Solar use and persistent only by Solar maintenance. Short of forcing captured Solars to labor to maintain it, which is dangerous for a number of reasons, they're headed on a downward trajectory.

                                                I think that the books portray very clearly (and well) that all of Creation balances on a razor's edge. The Lunars' asymmetrical warfare policy will win... against the Realm's current fragmented state. The reason the current age is referred to as the Time of Tumult (which I've always thought of post hoc naming) is that there are no longer any 'safe' options... for anyone.

                                                Dragon-Blooded: They are currently a nuclear reactor going into meltdown because the control rod (the Scarlet Empress) has been removed from the equation. The Dynasty realizes that internal (internecine?) squabbling can only serve the Realm's enemies, but they can't agree on the right action to take, and the system the Scarlet Empress set up deliberately has this problem. So there is a Tragedy-of-the-Commons situation in the Dynasty. Every House would prefer to deal with external threats to Realm power first and their power second, because if the Realm doesn't survive, neither does their power. However, doing this risks another House taking over and destroying others' power bases, and so they all squabble over power.

                                                Lunars: The basic question facing the Silver Pact: do they push, or not push? Each has their risks, and the Lunars are too smart to naively believe one way is 'safe'. It could be that pushing will work, because if they do not push then the Realm will have time to reestablish central authority and turn all of their attention back to the status quo ante. But it's possible that pushing will not work, because it will give the Realm the external threat without which they will tear themselves to pieces. So what do the Lunars do?

                                                Sidereals: There are huge upheavals in the Five-Score Fellowship. Their princeps senatus is about to die. Their chief proxy in Creation (the Realm) is dulling themselves fighting each other, which lowers their effectiveness. Their breaking of the Mask basically prevents direct rule, so they must have a proxy, and the best proxy in terms of power after the DB are the Lunars, and that's basically an impossible position without Sauron-level manipulation. To make matters worse, the return of the Solars is causing a resurgence of the Gold Faction, and real dissension in the Fellowship is starting to come back because of this. Speaking of...

                                                Solars: This is the huge wild card. Exalts of unsurpassed power with, effectively, no direct stake in the current power structure and nowhere to fit in said structure. The finely-tuned yet precarious equilibrium of the Empress's reign over Creation has just been shattered. I'm not going to say that the Solars are the sole protagonists in the story of the Time of Tumult, but none of the other Exalts can really afford to overlook them, and their power does lend them to tipping the balance in any given direction.

                                                This is the reality of the Time of Tumult. Decisions are going to be made, for better or worse, and nearly every decision can be defended rationally. The Silver Pact could decide to do their best to exterminate the Solars themselves, no matter how personally painful that might be. After all, a couple more Bulls of the North, and the Dynasty might actually start paying more attention to something more than the mirror palace of the Realm. And so on...

                                                        I think this is a bit unfair to Her Redness. (I almost can't believe I said that.) It is certainly possible that the Scarlet Empress is more focused on maintaining her control than the Realm as a whole for selfish reasons. But consider her history.

                                                        How did she become the Scarlet Empress? Because she was able to deal with the problems that no one else could. In a world literally teetering on annihilation, she who would become the Scarlet Empress--and only her--successfully stabilized Creation. She has prima facie evidence that she knows best. She, unlike so many others, is not a puppet of the Sidereals but their equal. Or, if a puppet, a puppet who knows her value and can treat with them as someone with intrinsic value, and that is very rare for a Terrestrial Exalt to be able to do with elder Celestial Exalts.

                                                        I don't think the Scarlet Empress is scared of any successor gaining control of the nuclear football. I am very much of the opinion that the nuclear football is more or less permanently offline, or that the Scarlet Empress will have arranged that to occur if anything happened to her. But it is very arguable that she is not self-absorbed so much as she is utterly convinced--for the reasons given above--that she is that critical to Creation's survival. Her machinations to make the Realm fall apart if anything happened to her was not a manifestation of megalomania, but an insurance policy that the Dynasty--who did not carry the burdens she did--would not try to overthrow her.

                                                        I do agree with your original premise, though. I don't think any group of Celestial Exalts would survive against a united Dragon-Blooded attack. Maybe if the Solars and Lunars banded together, as the Usurpation implies that the DB Host required the assistance of the Sidereals to overcome their might. But then recall that (presumably similarly to earlier editions) roughly half of the Solars have been corrupted in various ways. But yeah, if I had to bet on any Exalt type vs. any other Exalt type, as they are in the Time of Tumult, I would have to bet on the DBs.
