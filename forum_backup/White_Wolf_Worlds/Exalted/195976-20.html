                Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                As a para-canon ending to the Second Age, the First Edition Core goes with "it slipped inch by inch into barbarism and darkness, until one last catacylsm blotted it out forever. Yet in its sunset, it was a splendid thing, and glorious were the deeds of the Exalted".

                I think that conveys, fairly well, that the Solars were not certain to be Second Age's demise or destroyers, nor, alone, to be its uplifting. Neither of Chejop Kejak's nightmare scenarios have to out.

                (The end, the catacylsm, could come from a number of directions).

                For some reason that's got lost.
                This is important for presenting the setting to the players, because it sets the tone without straitjacketing everybody's games. But the people in the setting have not read the book. The driving conflict that the Solars create in the Second Age is absolutely tied to what they did in the First Age, and that defines how anyone in Creation with even a hearsay notion of the first Age and Usurpation relates to them. It's not literally about recreating the First Age's highs or lows, it's about the fact that the people who were responsible for those things are back, and so those things are now on the table again after a thousand years where everyone assumed they were gone forever. The fact that the game itself does not go out of its way to instruct you to make this the climactic dilemma of the Second Age does not mean that it isn't the defining dilemma of the return of the Solars, so much as it means that there is a lot going on and the return of the Solars might not (though also might) singlehandedly steer the Second Age to its conclusion.

                                                Originally posted by Cenobite451 View Post

                                                You keep repeating this phrase, but it occurs to me that their current status of Jack-of all-Trades, Undisputed-Master-of-Frickin'-EVERYTHING is arguably no less boring, and at least as problematic. I mean, to use an earlier example: why can't Sidereals be better than Solars at learning? Surely there's enough nuance to Solars that they could still be a viable choice of protagonist without hammering so hard on the Better Than You nail? (And if not, that too seems like a problem that needs addressing.)
                                                The question in this case, I think, is, "So what are the Solars?"

                                                Right now, Solars have a very clear core theme: surpassing excellence at human endeavor (or some similar phrasing and concept). They have other, secondary themes (sunlight, for instance), but this is the one that drives their place in the setting, the logic of their Charms, etc. It's the first court of appeal when pulling together an idea for a new Solar power: "Does this express surpassing excellence at a human endeavor?" While we could make them protagonists without a clear narrative theme, it wouldn't exactly be an improvement to do so, right?

                                                So. Suppose we slice off, for the sake of argument, "The Air Aspects are the fast-reading Exalts; Solars don't get fast-reading magic." Suppose we slice off a dozen other areas of human excellence and silo them in the other Exalts: "Solars can't provoke desire, fear, or other raw emotions as well as Lunars. Solars can't teach people as fast or as thoroughly as Sidereals." Whatever.

                                                We still need a core theme for the Solars, and "Surpassing excellence at an unpredictable grab-bag of human endeavors" sure ain't gonna cut it. What do we replace it with?

                                                (There are answers that could be given to this question - others have suggested, for instance, "Leadership." And the Solars could have been made the Leadership Exalted, sure - at the cost of making the default play splat out-of-theme for anyone who doesn't want to play a god-king. What answer do you give that's simple enough to understand, without pigeonholing the default character types? Because that's the advantage of "Surpassing excellence at human endeavor": it's easy to get across, and it enables basically every human-ish larger-than-life heroic archetype. Superman? Odysseus? Achilles? Gilgamesh? King Arthur? King Solomon? Hercules? Sherlock Holmes? Enzio? Sun Tzu? Easy to translate to Solars, and with powers that say, "Yes, you are the embodiment of the swordsman/the trickster/the detective/the king/the scholar/the strongman/the general/the whatever men can be." That's a powerful thing, when trying to get someone interested in the game, and it's a thing arguably worth preserving.)
