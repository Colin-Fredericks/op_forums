                        If it is any consolation, vanilla is by far my favorite ice cream flavor.

                        She can most certainly enfeoff an estate, and in many ways, it would be preferable to having Kasai be a live-in retainer.

                        With live-in retainers, their services are always available, and therefore keeping a solid body of troops for offense or defense is fast and easy. On the other hand, keeping retainers on-hand on your own dime is expensive as hell. Figure fodder for the horses (most Oathsworn/retainers own more than one. Kasai is just poor as hell), food, wine, clothing, linens, etc. Costs rack up fast, especially when the retainers are noble-born, and therefore would complain about being given the same standards-of-living as a common footsoldier.

                        On the other hand, having retainers hold estates and manors removes them from immediate use, since in the case of military action;

                        1) the message has to get out to them
                        2) they have to get ready, which could take some time if they have a retinue of any appreciable size, including peasant Men-at-arms
                        3) they have to make their way (which, again, could take some time) to the central mustering point

                        However, it also essentially eliminates any cost to your wallet, as owning a fief allows the retainer to support themselves off the labor of the commoners, while keeping them happy (it is nice to be recognized for your service, after all), and ensures that the hinterlands of your domain will at least have token protection. Individually, landed retainers have banded together and fought off threats ranging from bandit infestations all the way to minor invasions, since they are individual and capable of moving quickly, compared to an infantry regiment.

                        Generally, the social ranking system in my Hundred Kingdoms goes:
                        **NOBLES**
                        -(Great) Lord; Iysen Shiba, the Lord Yue (Haichi's father)
                        -Lord: Aiken Eisho, Lord Que, Kasai's father's overlord. There are several different "grades" to this rank, depending on amount of land held, reputation, etc. For example: Kasai's father is a Lord, but he is very much the social inferior to his overlord, even though they are both Lords.
                        - Retainers/Oathsworn

                        **COMMONERS**
                        -Peasants
                        -Serfs
                        -Slaves

                        BY right of rulership, Lord Iysen can, quite literally, do whatever the hell she wants with any and all lands and titles within the domain of Xia. She can raise anyone to Lordship, of any rank, and inversely, strip anyone of land and titles. She doesn't really even need a reason, technically...... if it weren't for the fact that her sworn lords have just about as much military might as she does, and that doesn't all disappear if she removes titles and land-rights. She has to play a careful balancing game between her sworn lords, keeping them happy while also ensuring they don't get too powerful, since they might decide to try to become independent Lords in their own right, or (probably more likely), swear oaths of loyalty to other rival Great Lords.

                        ...... you know, thinking about it, if she raised Kasai to Lordship, she would have someone who is basically loved by the commoners, and would be immensely personally loyal to Lord Iysen alone..... hmm. I like the implications!

                        On the other hand, Lord Isyen is likely to view giving Kasai an estate a ....... waste, for a lack of a better term, of his talents and such. Not that she wouldn't, if that is what he (and we) want, but she would prefer something else, something that would keep him ....... a little closer, where he can be of use.

                        As for marrying Mino or Airi ...... if it is any consolation (to them), we have already interacted with both of them to a greater extent than most noble-marriages do. Overwhelmingly most noble marriages are arranged years ahead of time (usually when one of the two are children), and the two partners meet for the first (and usually only, and even then in a heavily formalized fashion and setting) time when they get betrothed, (itself only a year or so before the actual wedding).

                        A significant portion of noble girls get married to someone several years, if not a decade or more, years their senior, and the two partners actually liking each other isn't very high on the list of priorities. "Getting along", yes, actual romantic feelings or even friendliness, no.

                        Nobles don't marry for love...... which is why poetry about courtly and romantic love are so popular amongst the upper classes. Relatedly, Mino was considered to be uncommonly fortunate to have a fiancee that was so close in age, and one that reciprocated her feelings.

                        As for adoption, technically, all it would mean is that we would now be an officially recognized member of the Shiba family, specifically as Lord Iysen's son. We would be her oldest child, but since we were adopted, we would also be the last in line to inherit anything..... unless she decided otherwise.

                        Adoption into noble families isn't even that rare, being a semi-common method of rewarding and ensuring the loyalty of particularly-useful retainers or even commoners (although commoners are likely to be rewarded with retainer-status before getting adopted. It would have to be something BIG for a commoner to get adopted straightaway into a noble family.). Many ancient noble dynasties have entire cadet branches and minor lines made up of the descendants of adopted sons (and daughters). What would be "weird" about Lord Iysen adopting us isn't the actual adoption, but more the fact that it would come out of relative nowhere for the other noble families. Remember; nobody asides from Kasai and Lord Iysen know what actually happened, or what Kasai did for her. Most adoptions come about as a result of heroics on the battlefield, or some other noteworthy event.

                                                            2) In most cases, it would be hilariously presumptuous to ask for either adoption or marriage. Kasai is the lowest of low nobility, while Lord Iysen is about as about as much of high nobility as one could get in the Hundred Kingdoms.

                                                            In this case, however, it wouldn't be. Saving a Lords life is one of the actions one could reasonably expect to be adopted for. It would cause some social friction with Lord Iysen's vassals, but that is more because Kasai is a nobody than anything else.

                                                            3) If we get adopted, it doesn't mean we are going to get money. Sure, as one of Lord Iysen's children, we aren't going to worry about money..... but that is because "Mom" is rich as hell, not because we have silver falling out of our pockets. In almost all cases, if we wanted something, we would have to go ask her...... and with the war coming, she isn't necessarily going to say yes. In fact, unless it is absolutely necessary, she will probably say no. She has troops to levy, mercenaries to raise, and other logistical nightmares to take care of.

                                                            4) If we become a retainer, we will get money..... eventually. The number of harvests varies throughout the Hundred Kingdoms, dependent on local fertility (which, as it is the East, tends to be astronomically high, on par with the most fertile arable lands in reality), weather (which is severe as hell. Torrential downpours in spring, incredibly hot summers, more torrential downpours in fall, and snowstorms out the ass in winter. Most of this weather is due to the Continental climate, as well as the moisture that gets dropped onto the Hundred Kingdoms from the Thunder Plains to the north), as well as availability of arable land (most of the land in the Hundred Kingdoms is either hills, forest, or forested hills, with only the land right by rivers being quasi-flat.). Most farms in the Hundred Kingdoms average about 3 harvests a year, with some reaching 4, and yields tend to be pretty large. However, once we return to health, the growing/harvest season would be over, and we would have another 3-4 months to wait until the next one.

                                                            5) If we became Captain of the Guard, we would receive a steady income, and not have to worry about food or boarding costs, not just for us, but for Saiko, Blue Pines and Xuansu. The paycheck wouldn't be large, but it would be 100% profit.

                                                            6) Plus, we have to consider what kind of role we want Kasai to take in the upcoming war.

                                                            a- If we are adopted into the family, we will almost certainly be playing a command role, and a significant one at that, since Kasai is the only one of Lord Iysen's children that has ever actually been in "real" combat. Sure, Kasai has read his copy of "The Thousand Correct Actions of the Upright Soldier", and has lead the peasants of his father's village in drills, but ........ this would be his first "mass" battle, at least one where he was actually in charge of things. Could we be responsible for the lives of potentially hundreds of men?

                                                            b- If we are accepted as a retainer, we will play a role, but not in command. Instead, we would serve as a cavalryman as part of Lord Iysen's retinue. So, no, the burden of command would not be on our shoulders, but on the other hand, could Kasai kill people he has no enmity for? Up to this point, Kasai has fought and killed people in self-defense, and to uphold his Oaths. Would he be comfortable charging down a peasant who, in all likelihood, doesn't even want to be there?

                                                            c- If we take the position of Captain of the Guard, we would, in effect, get to sit the whole war out, as we would be in command of Teriake Castle during Lord Iysen's absence. That does mean we are in command if, somehow , Teriake Castle falls under siege. Of course, sieges are much less hectic than a pitched battle. Less opportunity to get hurt that way. Almost like a vacation, in fact.

                                                            7) Which segues into my next point: Basically, Kasai isn't going to be capable of doing much of anything until next spring. It is going to take him a couple months to be able to walk again, much less fight, and quite some time after that to be able to run, jump, ride a horse, etc.

                                                            If Kasai was any older, or in less top-physical-shape, this would have been a career-ending injury. All the muscle in his outer thigh, from below his ass to above his knee, got cut clean-through, as well as getting a chipped femur,plus severe blood loss, ON TOP of having an almost-lethal infection develop. Luckily, Lord Iysen keeps a very skilled chirurgeon on staff, and they managed to save his life. Even still, he required several large blood transfusions. In game terms, he was (and still is, and will be for some time) Incapacitated. This injury will plague him for the rest of his life, and if it acts up in combat......

                                                            So, when you choose a reward, keep Kasai's "capabilities" in mind.
