      Unfortunately their use will be extremely limited for running third edition, except perhaps as finding kernels of plot inspiration. Pretty much all of the basic mechanical systems beyond "roll attribute + ability vs a difficulty" have dramatically changed, and the setting has undergone significant changes from 2e as well - things are more left to mystery rather than overexamined as in 2e, the non-Solar Exalted have actual places in the setting other than "inept babies sitting around as the world dies around them because only the Solars can do anything"...

                  Scavenger Sons, as a setting book, is still highly useful in giving a slightly more detailed view of specific chunks of the setting than any of the core books will give.

                  Storyteller Companion does have some setting discussion regarding non-Solar Exalt types and spirits, but the mechanics aren't going to be much use.

                  The 2E core has slightly more word count on most of the setting locations it covers, but it's going to be mostly the same information as the 3E core, just less compressed as each edition has added slightly more to the core setting.

                          Scavenger Sons is a 1e book, actually. So even some of how it presents things in the 2e corebook, its sourcebooks, and how 3e presents some things do differ. Thre is an upcoming book, Across the 8 Directions, which is around twice the size but similar format of an atlas of the setting coming out sometime in the next year or so for 3e. And various lcoations on it get retreaded a bit in different forms in both editions.

                          The 2e corebook is good for 2e, and such, but will generlaly not be as well-known to folks these days. And while it has some of the broad-strokes similarities to the 1e and 3e books, each edition of Exalted is a soft reboot of the setting, and so will often reuslt in some things being broadly similar, and other things in the specifics being notably different. An analogy i tend to use is say, Batman or Spider-Man movies, where some things are broadly the same like the charact,er their aesthetic, their abilities, or their main city, but a lot of details vary between different film versions, animated interpreations, or video games.

                          The Storyteller's Companion books for 1e and 2e ultimatley are kind of "spillover" books, containing things like NPC rules and Charms, some misc. artifacts, and subsystems that couldn't make it into the corebook of that edition. In gneeral the information in them gets supplanted by later books in their editions.
