    "Lunars: The Apocalypse" chapter fiction

    01-31-2020, 06:12 PM
    Dunno if people will be interested in this, but it's not much effort for me to copy-paste.


    Between sessions I write fiction about the setting and world of my game, from the perspective of the NPCs, to flesh things out (and I just enjoy doing it).


    So I thought I could post it up here for people to read. Other people post stuff about their campaigns, and clearly some people like reading it.
    I normally include (a ton of) pictures, but posting pictures on this forum is a nightmare, so I'm just going to post the text I'm afraid.
    Hopefully it's not too difficult to follow (or at least, enjoy); it's reactions of NPCs to events that happened in-game, so there may be allusions and references to things that aren't themselves explained. Some of it will be explained retrospectively in one of the later entries though.




    The game is set in the far, far North-East, north of Iscomay.





    The Lunars:
    NPCs
    King Arcturus, Dragon-totem (Bull-Serpent chimera) Changing Moon. 20 years ago he united the Five Clans to defeat the fae armies of Prince Níðhöggr, and brought in a new era of peace.
    Tabun, his Stag-totem No Moon Shaman

    PCs
    Olaf Keelbreaker, Walrus-totem Full Moon. 12 years ago, his family were murdered by Dragonblood from a mercenary group called the World Eaters (thanks to an Establish Fact roll by Atal's player).
    Krove, the Tiger-Bull, Full Moon and the strongest in all the land (He only joined a few sessions in)
    Amira, spider-totem Changing Moon and the most beautiful in all the land.
    Atal, toad-totem No Moon swamp shaman, hates Tabun.
    Underfoot, feral Gorilla-Totem casteless Lunar raised by a bear-spirit (named Baalu of course).
    Jin, Polecat-totem grifter, merchant, lover... not really so much of a fighter.

      Entry 1
      A military camp in the northern bordermarches.
      Another day of frozen misery. Another day of boredom.
      Nandorun Samatsu sighed.
      Tepeto Taizen raised one perfectly-manicured eyebrow. "Well," said the southerner in his polished accent, "It's your turn."
      Samatsu sneered. "Okay!" He turned over one of his tiles, revealing the symbol for Wood.
      Taizen gave one of his mocking smiles, and responded immediately, revealing Fire.
      "Ah! This game is stupid! These tactics would never work in real life!"
      Taizen gave another mocking half-smile. "Step on a spider, chop up a snake, Nandorun-kun."
      From outside came a cough. "Uh, Taicho-samas?"
      Samatsu stood up with relief, and threw open the tent's fabric door. Any excuse to get away from Taizen's infernal smirking. How he wished he'd been allowed to go with Jadis, rather than stuck here with that lazy rat!
      "Report, soldier!"
      "Nandorun-dono!" The guard lazily snapped a sloppy salute. Such undisciplined louts. "There's a hairy barbarian at the gate. His name is Olaf Keelbreaker?"
      Samatsu frowned. The name wasn't familiar.
      "He says we destroyed his village, and killed his family? He's here for revenge?"
      Taizen gasped. "Nandorun-san!" He grabbed the samurai's shoulder. "That's an Oni!"
      At the word oni, the memories all rushed back into Samatsu's head. The memories of that day.
      The city burns. Samatsu weeps as he holds his dead wife in his hands. At his door, the demon laughs, its witch-mark burning on its brow. "You are nothing, little samurai," it gloats. "I gutted House Nandorun, but I leave you alive. Live in shame, and tell others what happens to those who stand against the Sixty-Six Kuromao, the Black Devil Kings!"
      "An oni!" Samatsu cried.
      "Twelve years ago, a unit of World Eaters destroyed the village of an oni named Olaf-san," nodded Taizen. "We thought he'd died, but he lived. Now he's tracked us down."
      Samatsu grasped the hilt of his katana, his mouth curving into a cruel smile. "And the fool has come knocking at our front gate." He marched out, shouting orders. "Ashigaru, form up! Archers, to the wall, but do not loose until I give the order! Spearmen, arms and armour and then to the gate! Boy, my armour!"
      The boy rushed to clasp Samatsu into his silk-and-scale Dō.
      Taizen frowned, his impassive mask slipping. "Do not let the fires of vengeance consume you, Nandorun-san. This could be a trick."
      Samatsu grinned as the boy lifted on his kabuto and attached the menpō face-plate. "You bring Jadis's pet monsters, Tae-san. I will defend the front. Oni are arrogant in their own power, and this fool will get what's coming to him."

      Ten minutes later, Nandorun Samatsu strode out of the gate onto the snow-covered road, encased in his red armour, the colour of the World Eaters. Behind him crowded spearmen, some in the white-and-blue, others in the red. He felt a coil of fear. What if this was a trick? What if he failed again? But the oni was here. Those who run only die tired.
      A motley group stood before him. A hulking brute of a man led them, his face badly scarred, hefting a massive axe with an ivory haft and silver blade. Behind him was a soldier on a huge horse, his eyes in shadow. At his sides were a wild savage encrusted in filth and an older man with bulging, frog-like eyes. Above, a dark bird flew, its beady eyes taking everything in.
      "I am Olaf Keelbreaker," shouted the scarred brute, showing a mouth half-full of smashed teeth. "Twelve years ago, the World-Eaters attacked my village unprovoked and killed my family! I am here to take my revenge!"
      Samatsu felt his qi surging, red with fiery rage.
      "My city was attacked by demons like you!" shouted Samatsu. "And today, I take my revenge!"
      Fast as a hummingbird, Samatsu's katana flew into his hand as he leaped forward. With an animal howl, the ugly monster charged at him... then flew up into the sky screaming, pulled by Jadis's walkuren.
      For a second, Samatsu felt cheated, but he knew the demon would soon return.
      But, horror of horrors, the oni's two companions began to change. One became a massive gorilla, screaming and beating its chest, while the other transformed into a massive python.
      Samatsu's terrified troops hesitantly engaged the two beasts, while he went after the man on the horse.
      His blade flashed with the speed of a crackling fire, but the horsemen parried with supernatural skill. But the man was off-balance, on the defensive. In the corner of his eye, Samatsu saw a woman shooting a massive silver bow, and he heard Taizen shouting to the men who were taking a beating from the beasts, but he was too consumed with rage to care.
      Samatsu was about to cut the massive horse's head off, when the horseman cackled. He spoke in a dark and cruel voice. "Foolish Samurai. Don't you remember? It was I who burnt your city, but I did not kill your family. Instead, I cursed them, binding them to me as my animal slaves! Bwahahaha!"
      For a second, Samatsu paused in horror. It seemed like a lie, but what if it was true? Death was bad enough, but unending slavery to a demon? But then he smiled beneath his mempo. Today, Voharun smiled upon him. Now, he would free his family.

          A week later, the camp besieging Many Spears' castle, Land of the Five Clans, after Many Spears and Queen Pale Spirit fled, after their affair was revealed.
          Kari poked the fire, watching the cooking fish. She'd never been a good cook, but a shield-maiden didn't have to be.
          "This is bad business, bad business," growled her father. Karl Kingsmilk had once been a great warrior, if not as great as he liked to boast. Now in his 50s, he should have been at home with his grandkids. "What's my milk-brother going to do?"
          Sigurd the Berzerker looked bored. "Well there's a meeting this afternoon, he'll tell us then."
          "I heard that the Lady Amira and Atal the witch have arranged a duel to resolve it," said Black Paladin in his strange, southern accent.
          "A holmgang?" Karl's eyes lit up. He'd rescued Kari's mother from her first husband by killing him in holmgang.
          "It was Arcturus who banned holmgang," said Kari, "He won't want that. It'll be a judicial duel."
          "Who's going to take on Many Spears though?" asked Sigurd.
          "The king asked Krove, that beastman, but he refused. I heard he asked Olaf Keelbreaker too, but Olaf hasn't decided," replied Black Paladin. Kari had thought him some form of black-skinned demon when she'd first seen him, but he was an honourable and thoughtful man.
          "I should do it, not some foreigner." They looked up to find Jarl Galchmei standing above their fire, his face glowering with anger. "It was my brothers who discovered the crime, and my brothers who Many Spears killed escaping."
          Karl looked uneasy. "Look, Galchmei, you yourself told your brothers to leave it. And Many Spears has been one of your best friends for years."
          "Blood comes first," snarled Galchmei.
          Black Paladin frowned. Like Galchmei, he'd also been close to Many Spears.
          "Is a duel a good idea? The goddess watches over Many Spears."
          "Fuck the goddess," snarled Galchmei, never one for reverence.
          "Hey," said Kari, eager to change the conversation. "What's that?" She pointed out north-west. It was snowing, but a set of black shapes were just visible. Mostly man-sized, but some were much, much larger.
          "That must be Jarl Olaf of the Mammoth Clan,” said Black Paladin. "He was supposed to get here soon. And he's supposed to have Jarl Ragna Half-Rat of Clan Relic with him."
          Karl looked worried. "Ragna is Many Spears' second-cousin. She is going to be awkward."
          "No-one's going to get in the way of my vengeance," growled Galchmei. The others looked at each other uneasily.

              A week later, an Iscomayan ship seized by Krove, Atal and Underfoot after the besiegers gave up, as Tabun's ghost appeared and revealed Caer Moroth had been seized while Arcturus was away.
              Mighty Pelt was a sad bear. He was on a wooden iceberg, stuck to a big tree with a big white leaf. Humans and Bad Cat kept coming and shouting at him. He tried eating Bad Cat, but it hit him. One human looked nice, but also smelled like dead bear. Sometimes the humans fought for territory. And there was a storm.
              Baalu said if Mighty Pelt just waited and didn't fight, the humans would bring him fish. Strange. But he was stuck on a strong branch, and couldn't fight.
              The wooden iceberg floated near some land. Mighty Pelt tried to get off the iceberg, but he was still stuck to the tree by a strong branch.
              He slept. He woke up. A human was sneaking around. He got up. The branch had come off the tree! He dived into the water, and paddled across to the land. He still had the heavy branch stuck to him, but he was free. He found some woods, and settled down for a nice sleep.
              He woke up. In front of him was a human-cave-tree. What? How did that get there? He could see inside Baalu and his son, snug and out of the snow, and one of the other humans, the loud one that smelled like a toad.
              He felt something warm and furry resting at his side. A lady-bear? No, it was Bad Cat. Bad Cat had followed him to punch him again! No escape!

                  The castle of Caer Moroth, siezed by the World Eaters while Arcturus was away
                  Einar Hairy-Arms was in a good mood. His pockets were full of gold, his belly was full, and while the weather was miserable he was in a nice warm dwarf-built keep. He leaned back on his cheer, and raised his full tankard of mead. "To Grim the Rich!"
                  His friends grinned and raised their own tankards, giving their own toasts. "To Katsunaga the Southman!", "To the girls of Clan Dragon-Bull!", "To the Guild of Merchants!", "To the ales of Caer Moroth!", "To the White Witch!", "To the blood of the foeman on our blades!"
                  "Hey," said Dag Dogbiter. "There's Akiko. We should invite her over." He waved at the mutant mercenary, while Einar gave a smirk to the others. Dag had been mooning over the mutant since he saw her skewer two guards on one spear.
                  "Dag!" said Akiko as she swaggered over and sat down, surprisingly graceful in her heavy steel armour. She took Dag's offered mead-horn in her seven-fingered hand. "I see today strange thing, you tell. Is big fat animal, like grey dog, lives in sea. What is?"
                  Dag thought for a bit. He wasn't the sharpest axe in the woodshed. "Seal?" The others nodded.
                  "What kind of animals live on your southern beaches then?" asked Magna Axewife.
                  Akiko paused, pain in her eyes. "Once, crab, fishes, too much birds. Now, nothing. The Kuromao killed all."
                  Dag looked awkward. He wanted to flirt, not remind Akiko about her dead family. And they hadn't had been happy when Katsunaga desecrated the Moon-Lord's temple, something they'd rather forget. But Dag was saved by a thrall's interruption. "Uh, Karls? There's something going on outside."
                  Einar put his tankard down and stretched. "Come on then."
                  The group trooped out into the yard. At the gate, they could see a merchant with a massive black horse arguing with the guards, his eyes in shadow despite the light. He had a huge ugly guard with a mutilated face. The guard stepped forward, pulling the cloth of his huge axe; a massive thing with an ivory haft and a silver blade.
                  Einar and his mates stepped forward, hefting their axes, but Akiko hissed. “Is Aku-ma, moon demon!” The guy didn't look like a demon, he just looked butt-ugly.
                  Slice.
                  Einar blinked. The gate guards both slumped to the floor, decapitated in a single stroke. A bloody head rolled out into the yard. The ugly brute stepped through the gate into the yard, hefting his axe like it weighed nothing, followed by the merchant who'd drawn some weird-looking thin sword. Shit. Einar did not want to die today.
                  “Katsunaga!” Einar yelled at the thralls. “Get Captain Katsunaga here now or we're fucked!” They ran.
                  Einar turned back to face the demon. Shit.

                      The southern road
                      Kari was cold and miserable, but like the rest of the Huscarls, she rode stubbornly, one hand above her eyes to keep the snow out of it. Besides her Karl Kingsmilk kept up a constant stream of profane grumbling. Everyone looked haggard and cold, except for the Princess Birthed from Sunken Depths, who was immaculate as always.
                      Somewhere scores of miles ahead of them was the Fyrd. Arcturus would have reached them by now, using his sacred King's magic. They would be marching back toward the capital, but the inexperienced farmers would go slow through the snow and storm, even on the ancient Elderling road. The Huscarls could catch up with them if they rode hard. And while Arcturus shouldn't have difficulty leading 500 men to retake his own capital, the enemy was clearly cunning, possibly allied with Many Spears, and probably had spies amongst them. If things went sideways, the Fyrd would need famous heroes like Jarl Galchmei and Black Paladin to bolster their resolve.
                      As the sun began to sink below the horizon, Jarl Galchmei gestured to a light ahead. It was one of the roadside inns that Arcturus had set up. Part of his ideas to create a modern kingdom of trade and prosperity, modelled on Iscomay to the south. For half the year the inn would be crowded with iron-traders from Clan Hollow bringing their wares east, and merchants and peddlers travelling the opposite direction to bring their wares to Clan Hollow. But in today's brutal weather, it was likely mostly empty. She heard Sigurd the Berzerker cursing the fate that had led them to campaign in winter.
                      The Huscarls clattered into the inn's stables. Kari looked hungrily at the cosy light coming out under the inn's door, but the horses had to be seen to first.
                      Once all the horses were tied up and fed from the waiting hay, the soldiers eagerly poured into the inn, leaving the thralls to brush them down. Kari was near the back, so just heard the end of the conversation.
                      “... staying with her cousin. I'm sorry my Jarl, we didn't expect anyone. But me and my wife will do our best to do what she'd have done."
                      Kari had stayed here briefly half a year ago when she, Eric the White and Ivan Lionfriend had led a mission to hunt down mountain bandits. She didn't recognise the man Galchmei was talking to, or his foreign wife. But the couple bustled around, seating the Huscarls along the crude benches along a big table. It was crowded, but they just about fit. They brought out barrels of mead, and the Huscarls gave a roar of approval, waving their drinking horns.
                      “We'll fetch some salted meat and bread for you, m'lords,” said the man, bowing subserviently. Kari watched the man's foreign wife pour mead; she was quite beautiful in a strange and exotic way, if grubby. She could see some of the Huscarls had also noticed; she hoped they didn't cause trouble.
                      Soon the table was covered in salted meat and hard bread. The hungry carls set to it with gusto. The man looked worried, she could hear him muttering to his wife that they'd depleted their stores. But the faerie enchantress, the Princess Birthed From Sunken Depths, stood up and swayed over. “Do not fear,” she said languidly, “we will repay you for all your trouble. Now, why don't we leave these smelly men to it and go and discuss the details of payment?” The couple nodded and went into a backroom with her.
                      “Why aren't you drinking, Moreth?” asked Kari, noticing the young man's untouched drinking horn.
                      “Just not in the mood,” he grunted.
                      “Ha! You're never in a good mood!” chortled Sigurd the Berzerker, clapping Moreth on the back.
                      “Why should I be?” snapped the bastard. He stood up. “I need to piss.” He marched away. The others raised their eyebrows and returned to their revelry, but Kari watched Moreth. He didn't go outside, he went into the back-room where the faerie and the innkeeper's cousin were. Maybe he was asking them where their outhouse was.
                      Karl Kingsmilk coughed. “Ugh, I think I'm going to be sick.” He stood up, and started swaying towards the door.
                      “You haven't drunk that much!” called Black Paladin as he stood up too. He swayed. “Oh, I think this mead was stronger than I expected.”
                      The innkeeper stepped back into the room. Kari frowned. He was carrying a weird-looking sword. “Hey, what are you doing?” she asked, as the others turned to look.
                      The room was smoky from the fire, and she squinted. The innkeeper looked like she was seeing him through a heat-haze, his form flickering and unclear.
                      He stepped forward, and Black Paladin died, blood splurting from his neck. Two others jumped up, drawing their axes, and with a slash they fell too.
                      “Treachery!” screamed Galchmei, standing and drawing his star-forged blade.
                      The huscarls surged forward, swinging their axes, but the man was hard to see. His sword flickered and three more men fall dead.
                      Kari went to flank him, but a sudden pain in her back threw her down. She coughed, rolling sideways, and found the foreign woman, now in strange steel armour, laying about her with fists and feet. Bones cracked and necks snapped as her fists seemed to hit like stone. She even parried an axe-strike with the back of her hand. How?
                      Within a minute, the inn was a scene of gore and the dead and unconscious. Only a wounded Galchmei just about kept to his feet. Kari tried to rise, but she couldn't feel her legs.
                      The woman stood in front of Galchmei, grinning. She had a few cuts and bruises, but nothing seriously. There was a strange dark glow around her, like the darkest depths of the winter sea. “This is my favourite bit. The bit where you know you've lost, we've won, and there's nothing you can do about it. Now beg, little mortal.”
                      “Fuck you, witch!” snarled Galchmei, blood dripping from a brutal wound on his leg. “Who do you work for? Many Spears? The World Eaters?”
                      The foreign woman's grin grew. “So you know who to haunt? Nah.” She gestured to the innkeeper. She said something in a foreign language.
                      The man nodded, and raised his hand. It burst into fire, yet did not burn him. He threw it right into Galchmei's face, who feel screaming to the floor, the smell of roasting flesh filling the room. Kari screamed.
                      The woman looked around, smiling. “Well, well, a survivor! Good. I thought the fun had finished.” She walked over and stood above Kari. She smelled like blood and... seawater? She pulled out a vicious-looking barbed knife.
                      “You know, I'm going to let you choose. Where shall I start, hands, feet, or genitals?”
                      Kari looked up at her in horror. “I'll talk! What do you want to know.”
                      The woman shook her head. “Oh dear. There seems to be a misunderstanding. I don't have any questions for you. My friend over there,” she said gesturing at the innkeeper, who was cleaning Galchmei's sword, “he's our spymaster, and he's very good. He already knows everything we need. No, I'm just going to torture you because I really enjoy torturing people.”

                          The southern road
                          Arcturus rode at the front of the column. He looked impassive and confident, but Ivan Lionfriend was sure he was just masking his worries for the column. The weather was still awful, with snow blanketing the road, slowing their progress, as well as hampering visibility. Riding hard, Jarl Galchmei and the other Carls should have reached their slow-moving column by now. The snow must have slowed them down.
                          A horse cantered through the snow towards them. A scout, returning. But there was someone riding behind the scout.
                          They stopped in front of Arcturus, the scout sliding off his horse. He turned to help the woman behind him, but she jumped down gracefully.
                          Ivan's heart skipped a beat. The woman before him was the most beautiful he'd ever seen. Her skin was smooth, her hair and clothes immaculate despite the snow and hard ride. Her face was uncannily beautiful, like a goddess. That must be what she was.
                          Her voice chimed out like music. It didn't quite sound human; it sounded better. It reminded Ivan of a babbling brook, a summer's day, the feeling you get when you come into a longhall of happy cheer after a cold night's ride.
                          He realised he hadn't actually been listening to what she was saying, and tried to concentrate.
                          “... 100 or so. Only about ten World Eaters, most are just local mercenaries and vikings wintering in town out of the storm.”
                          Arcturus frowned. “I was told before that the thralls you rescued in the Nightmare Lands said that one officer and ten soldiers had left the fort separately. That must be them. But surely they know this is crazy. I have five hundred soldiers and the support of the people. It'll be brutal, but they can't hold out. I feel something else is going on here.”
                          The goddess nodded. Like everything else she did, it was utterly charming. Ivan could feel his heart beating, his cheeks flushing.
                          “Maybe,” she said, “Thane Jin believes the whole situation with your wife, trying to get you to go to war with Xor, maybe even the Jotunn raids and kidnapping of Volundr's son, it's all a conspiracy from within your court. This seizure of your castle could be to embarrass you, perhaps this Captain Katsunaga and his guards will flee and leave the mercenaries to die, having weakened your position.”
                          Arcturus grimaced. “Perhaps, Amira.” Amira! What a beautiful name! Ivan almost repeated it, but managed to swallow his words. He didn't want to embarrass himself in front of her.
                          “Or,” continued the King, “It's all been leading up to invasion. The situation with Many Spears has left my troops out of position. We need to be ready for an ambush.”
                          “What do you wish me to do, your grace?” asked Amira.
                          “Return to Caer Moroth and carry on watching them,” replied the King. “If they make a move, come tell me.”
                          She curtsied, as elegantly as she'd done everything else. “As you will, my King.” Ivan felt a surge of jealousy for the king. Amira turned walked into the snow, soon vanishing. Ivan let out a breath, realising he'd been holding in his gut. He looked around at the crooked peasants, callow youths and scarred, grey-bearded veterans that surrounded him. He'd never really considered how ugly they were.
                          “Ivan. Ivan!” Ivan snapped his attention back to the King, embarrassed. Arcturus didn't seem surprised; Amira probably always had this effect. “Tell the troops to keep an eye on their left flank. I'm worried about ambushes. This doesn't feel right.”

                              The southern road
                              Alfhild was exhausted. Marching all that way, and now back, through the snow... she'd dreamed of glory, but all she'd done was march, march, march. If this was what being a soldier was all about, she'd rather be a farmer. At least they'd got past the mountains, and back into Ice-Haven valley.
                              She fell in with the other shield-maidens in her group, as they wearily set about making camp. “Shit.” said Sigfrid, looking up. “Clouds are back.” Everyone groaned; they'd had a good few clear days, but now it looked like it would snow again.
                              The clouds came in freakishly quickly, and soon they were huddled in their tent as the blizzard raged outside.
                              There was a muffled noise from outside. Shouting?
                              “What's that?” asked Alfhild. They looked at each other uneasily. No-one really wanted to go out into the snow.
                              But then they heard the voice of Arcturus booming out clearly. “Ambush! Ambush! Form shield-wall!” The women looked at each, horrified, and then grabbed their spears and shields, pulled up their hoods, and ran out into the blizzard. They could just about make out the shapes of other soldiers, and ran into a messy, loose formation.
                              “Fuck!” A javelin landed in Alfhild's shield. She looked to her right... Sigfrid was dead with another javelin through her head.
                              She heard a war-cry from somewhere in the blizzard, and they emerged. Dozens of snarling mutants, heavily armoured and with long spears. Some just had strange-coloured skin and weirdly-shaped faces, but others had tentacles, claws, fangs. One man looked to be made of living ice, another slithered with the tail of a massive snake instead of legs. They wore a mish-mash of colours, some all in red, others in white-and-blue, and some a combination, rusted red armour with white-and-blue cloaks. One dog-headed man carried a flapping banner on a pole tied to his back. It showed a fanged maw, with green and blue shapes between its teeth, though she couldn't make it out clearly.
                              But the weirdest thing wasn't their appearance. It was that none of them had snow on them. Alfhild glanced up and realised; snow was falling on the Fyrd, but in front of the mutants it just... stopped.
                              The mutants formed a spear-wall, and began steadily stepping forward to the beat of a drum. Arcturus's voice boomed out again “Tighten formation!” Alfhild stepped over her friend's corpse, trying not to look, and set her shield next to the shield of some man she didn't know.
                              The mutants marched closer, and their spears stabbed forward. The militia tried to put up their shields to protect their heads, and attempted to hack at the hafts with their axes. But it was difficult to get a good blow in. Alfhild heard screams as men and women were stabbed with spear-points. Within a minute, Alfhild was exhausted, panting.
                              There was a lull in the fighting, as the mutants took a rest. "First line fall back!" shouted Arcturus. "Second line forward!"
                              Alfhild fell back exhausted, as fresh soldiers ran forward to replace her. She made her way to the back of the fight, as she'd been taught.
                              She could hear shouts and screams, and just about see that the spearmen had pulled swords, and come in close. They shouted warcries in some strange, foreign language. "Shogun banzai!" "Ryū banzai!" "Ichiman ryū!"
                              There was a scream to her left, as a fork of lightning hit the militia, throwing soldiers into the air to fall as burning corpses.
                              For a moment, it looked like the line would collapse, but then the Thegn was there, Ivan Lionfriend, backed up by his mighty mountain lion. They tore into the mutant line, which reeled back in shock.
                              But a woman stepped forward, white hair and blue robes. She carried a long curved sword made of some strange blue material.
                              "I am Ivan, Thegn of Clan Dragon-Bull! Giantslayer, Faeslayer, Demonslayer!"
                              "I am Shozei Umi," she said, "Wielder of the Ice Mirror katana, Scion of the Last Clan. And I am your doom, barbarian."
                              Ivan and his lion split apart, flanking the woman. But she leaped sideways, slashing at the lion. She only did a light wound, but ice appeared around the lion, freezing it in place. Then she leaped back, batted Ivan's sword away, and stabbed him in the gut. He fell, blood gushing, and the cheering mutants charged past, back at the shield-wall.
                              The militia fell back under the onslaught, amongst the snow-covered tents. Alfhild couldn't get to the enemy, she was near the back ranks, but she pushed the men in front of her, supporting them as the shield-wall heaved at the enemy somewhere ahead.
                              "Behind us!" As Alfhild turned, she realised she was close to the King. He stood in the centre of the line as they turned and formed a new shield-wall. Out of the blizzard emerged another group of red-cloaked spearmen. They were led by another white-haired woman easily hefting a massive green-bladed spear.
                              "Devil King!" she shouted. "Your reign is over! I, Flashing Spear Lioness, shall bring you down!"
                              Arcturus snarled, and his body rippled and stretched. Within seconds, he had transformed into a massive dragon.
                              Dragon-Arcturus bounded forward and snapped at the woman. She dodged, and slashed her spear at his flank. He roared in pain, and lashed out with his tail, knocking the woman down into the snow. Arcturus trampled her, and then smashed into the enemy. Within a minute, they routed, screaming.
                              The Fyrd cheered, as Arcturus shrunk down. He was surrounded by a silver halo. This must be his fabled royal magic.
                              Arcturus turned wearily, and began to walk back to the line. But there was a call behind him. "Father!"
                              Arcturus looked back. "Moreth?"
                              A young blonde soldier, haggard and with a black eye, was walking through the snow.
                              "Father!" the young man shouted. "Galchmei, Black Paladin, Sigurd the Berzerker, your milk-brother Karl... They're all dead."
                              Cries of horror rose from the men around her. But Alfhild wondered; what was this boy doing here, in the middle of a battlefield?
                              Arcturus ran forward, embracing the boy.
                              But with slow horror, Alfhild saw the knife come out. She stepped forward, but they were too far away, the knife rose and fell, rose and fell in the gap in the King's armour under his arm.
                              With a cry, the King staggered back, but he did not fall. Moreth drew his sword. They said something to each other, but Alfhild was too far to hear.
                              The militia charged forward, but they were tired and the ground was churned with mud, slowing them.
                              The King and Moreth charged each other. The King's spear stabbed into the younger man's side, but Moreth's blade plunged into Arcturus's chest. The silver glow vanished. The King was dead.
                              Around her, the soldiers screamed for vengeance, charging, but lightning cracked, throwing men back, their flesh burning. As Alfhild pulled herself up, she saw the mutants had run forward around the wounded Moreth, and one was treating his wounds.
                              "King Arcturus is dead!" rang out a woman's voice. "Long live his son, King Moreth! Surrender or die as traitors to your king."

                                              Tepeto Goji
                                              Goji was appalled. The capitol was just... gone. He rode towards the camp in shock. Not only were most of the buildings gone, replaced by tents and crude huts, but even the cliff where the castle had stood was gone, just a mound of rubble now.
                                              He rode in, giving the password to the guards. He saw Vermillion Legionnaires around the town, but there seemed less than there should be. There were also local mercenaries, making up the numbers, but it seemed like only two or three hundred soldiers in total. Worrying.
                                              He saw his fellow Yamabushi, Tepeto Itsuko, arm-in-arm with a local hunk, and waved.
                                              Evidently she'd picked up a Northern toyboy. Goji had no great interest in smelly, big-nosed northerners, but Itsuko's appetites, inherited from her sybaritic Synisa father, did generally lead to good local intel, so he couldn't criticise her. He did miss his Eastern catamites though.
                                              "Goji-kun!" she greeted him. "I'm glad you're back! How went your mission?"
                                              The foreigner looked blank, he evidently didn't understand Court Earthtongue, unsurprisingly.
                                              "Sort of well. I delivered Volundr's son, and he's planning to invade Dazul. And I spent a week with three Oni."

                                              They walked through the encampment. Goji saw soldiers overseeing gangs of locals building houses, halls and barracks.
                                              They reached the King's tent, and entered. Tepeto Jadis was there talking to a blonde kid. "Ah, Goji-kun, welcome," she said in Skytongue. "This is the new King, Moreth."
                                              Goji bowed. "Your grace. I'm sorry for the loss of your father, King Moreth."
                                              The young man grinned. "Yeah, it's tragic."
                                              "Your grace," said Jadis, "Why don't you go and inspect the troops? Let yourself be seen."
                                              The smile was wiped off Moreth's face. "Sure," he said sullenly, and stalked out.

                                              Goji frowned. "Where's your husband, Jadis-sama? And Sesusa Nemaja-san?"

                                              "Katsunaga-san took the capitol and killed Tabun as planned. But do you remember Yataro-san's message?"
                                              Goji nodded. "That he was under attack from a coven of Lunars led by a guy called Olaf Keelbreaker."
                                              Jadis nodded. "Olaf killed him and Nandoran-san, then travelled to Caer Moroth and killed my husband. The Guild mercenaries were pretty useless. We arrived later and attacked him. We lost 30 soldiers, and took his eye, but then there was a massive earthquake and he fell into the sea. Then we had to flee the tidal wave. Most of the townsfolk and most of our soldiers died. Grim's been tracking down the mercenaries who fled Olaf before to restore our numbers, but we're undermanned. When Nemaja-san was on patrol, Olaf snuck up behind her and attacked with his moonsilver grimcleaver. He cut off her head and sent back one of the guards with a message saying this isn't over."
