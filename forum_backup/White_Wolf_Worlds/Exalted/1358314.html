    Dahaka, the demon boat (PEACH)

    01-01-2020, 09:00 PM
    Recently I tried to make a second-circle demon with the form of a ship, to be summoned in my exalted game. This was largely inspired by Kimbery's Dawn, a second-circle demon described in the 2nd edition Compass of Celestial Directions book on the West. Rather than viewing the demon as a giant sea monster, I wanted to stat it primarily as a ship that does interesting things when you sail on it (or, secondarily, fight enemies that are sailing on it). The charms are designed to hit as many of the themes of (1) pirates on a pirate ship, (2) a living ship, (3) demons as possible. Their names come from a song, Signapore by Tom Waits, that I felt was appropriate. I would be really happy to hear comments or criticism on game balance, comments or criticisms on the individual charms, and suggestions for tropes or ideas that would be interesting to include.


    Dahaka
    Dahaka appears as a large ship, about the size of a blue-water merchant. His massive sails of black leathery skin are supported by masts formed from still-living trees. Though covered in red-tinted bark, they only have a few branches, which are used to support the sails. His hull consists of smooth black skin which is slick even when out of the water. Below the water, his skin extends out to fins and tentacles, and at his prow he has a mouth filled with pearly white teeth. The interior of Dahaka appears more like a traditional ship, made primarily of violet planks of amaranth wood, interspersed with planks of ivory-like bone, except for his massive, beating, greenish-black heart.

    Dahaka is capable of shouting in a booming voice that is heard easily on other nearby ships. He hopes to eventually be the admiral of a vast fleet that rules all of Creation’s waters, and especially enjoys barking orders at other ships.

    Dahaka functions as a ship with the following statistics:

    Speed: Current +1, empty cargo holds +1, fins +4, sails +4
    maneuverability: +2, hull: -0x1/-1x2/-2x4/-4/incap
    Cargo: 2,000 tons or 125 soldiers and their equipment

    And as a character with the following statistics: (His defensive statistics, such as health levels, apply mainly to his heart. Attacking any other part of him counts as attacking a ship.)

    Essence: 5; Willpower: 7; Join Battle: 6 dice Personal Motes: 100
    Health Levels: -0x5/-1x10/-2x10/-4x5/Incap.

    Sample Intimacies:
    • Defining Principle: “Those who dwell on the sea are rightfully mine to command”
    • Major Principle: Piracy is justified - “Those with wealth must protect it, and those who can take it should”
    • Minor Principle: “I wish to explore the wonders of the ocean”
    Dahaka typically develops a Major tie of “they are like favored pets and I want to protect them” to any crew members who regularly sing to him.

    Actions:
    Naval command: 12 dice; Read Intentions: 4 dice; Resist Poison/Illness: 13 dice, Senses: 10 dice; Social Influence: 5 dice; Appearance 4 (hideous), Resolve 5, Guile 3

    Combat

    Attack (Spit Acid ): 14 dice (Damage 10)
    Combat Movement: N/A Evasion 0, Parry 4
    Soak/Hardness: 20/12

    Merits:

    Submerge: Dahaka can fold down his masts and sails, cover the deck with skin, and submerge underwater. This renders him undetectable to normal observation on the surface, though magical detection methods and careful observation from very close are still effective. Similarly, he is invulnerable to ordinary ship-based weapons, but magical weapons and specialized ones can be effective as appropriate. He does not gain the benefit of sails or empty cargo hold while submerged under water, but can still move using his fins. He is not completely airtight when doing this. Instead, he secretes a transparent, breathable gel that fills the deck. This takes a few minutes, so he cannot normally submerge to escape naval combat. For living beings this is survivable but not particularly comfortable, and bestows a -1 Exhaustion penalty on all actions while submerged and for (6 - Stamina) hours after. This penalty increases by 1 if he is submerged for over an hour, and 1 for each additional two hours, and in this case decreases by 1 every (6 - Stamina) hours until it is completely removed.

    This demon boat’s your home: Intelligent aquatic creatures, including tidemares and certain first-circle demons, can nestle between Dahaka’s tentacles and be carried along when it travels. In return these creatures grant Dahaka various benefits. For tidemares, the primary benefit is that they can attach themselves to portholes on Dahaka’s hull and transport passengers in and out of the ship, including when he is submerged. Some first-circle demons can serve as weapons for ship-to-ship combat.

    In the land of the blind:Dahaka has exceptional low-light vision and can see well enough to navigate even while underwater at night.

    Dangled from a rope of sand: Touching Dahaka's heart allows a crew member to commune telepathically with him. If his heart is ever destroyed, he dies and returns to Malfeas, and in his place on Creation is found an ordinary wooden ship, decaying and rotten to the point where it just barely seaworthy (equivalent to a blue water merchant with one hull level remaining). If Dahaka is sunk, he dies and returns to Malfeas shortly after his heart is submerged, but if he is incapacitated as a ship, his heart can survive for a few days, and activate his non-sailing charms, until he is repaired or else dies and sinks.

    Boarding Action Charms:

    We’re all as mad as hatters here(10m, 1wp; Supplemental; Scene) Dahaka speaks a demonic war-cry that fills his crew with supernatural rage and strength. This Charm enhances either a boarding action, or a defense against boarding. It grants those who start the battle on Dahaka an additional die on all attack and damage rolls.

    Fill your pockets up with earth (12m; Simple; Scene): Dahaka grants his crew of buccaneers unlikely strength to quickly loot treasure from their fallen foes. This grants +2 to Strength for all purposes other than attacks. They also gain 2 bonus dice on actions to search for valuables (not including social influence rolls to interrogate people on the location of valuables). Generally speaking, this allows a ship to be completely looted in one-third the normal time.

    Dance upon a colored wind (5m; Reflexive; Instant): This charm can be used when a member of Dahaka’s crew swings from the riggings or otherwise uses Dahaka’s body to move around in combat. The rigging writhes and shifts, sending them to their destination quicker than their enemies can anticipate. They roll (Dexterity + Athletics), gaining 1 Initiative for each success.

    Every witness turns to steam (5m; Reflexive; Instant) This charm supplements an intimidate action by a member of Dahaka’s crew, when they are standing on Dahaka or when the target is within view of Dahaka’s terrifying visage. It grants double 9s on the roll, and if the intimidation is successful, resisting it costs an additional Willpower.

    Sailing Charms:

    Heave away, boys (10m; Reflexive; Instant): This charm can be used in naval combat. Dahaka screams orders to allied ships. He rolls 12 dice and may distribute the successes among any allied ships participating in the same battle - his voice is loud enough to be heard over these distances. Each success grants one bonus die on naval maneuver rolls. However, this cannot bring any ally’s dice pools higher than Dahaka’s captain’s naval maneuver dice pool for the same round of combat.

    Drink the scent of jade and gold (15m, 2wp; Simple; Permanent): Dahaka can smell another ship, tracking it across the waves. To use this charm, Dahaka must have previously caught the scent of the ship itself, a crew member, or a unique item in its cargo. This enables Dahaka to determine the general direction of the ship as long as it is within a distance of at most 200 miles. If the ship does nothing to prevent tracking, such as sailing along the most direct route between two points, Dahaka can catch up with it automatically, as long as he is fast enough. If the ship attempts to avoid pursuit, Dahaka’s captain must succeed at a (Perception + Sail + Maneuverability) roll against their (Wits + Sail + Manueverability) to track them, with the target taking a -2 penalty if they are unaware of the method used to track. This can be used once per story, but can be reset by finding the targeted ship and defeating it in naval combat.

    Heart Charms:

    His arms are hard and mean(8m, 1wp; Reflexive; Counterattack): This charm can be used to counterattack when Dahaka’s heart is attacked. His heart undulates with surprising force, striking whoever attacks it. This counts as a withering attack that occurs after the attack is rolled but before damage, with 12 dice to hit and 15 damage. If it crashes the opponent, they are knocked prone.

    Away Team Charms:

    Making feet for pirate’s shoes (12m; Simple; Until the targets sleep):Dahaka works a mystical illusion which functions as a disguise for up to 12 members of his crew. This disguise can enact even magical changes of appearance, but is imperfect, and may be detected normally. Dahaka makes a disguise roll with a dice pool of 10 dice to determine the effectiveness of this disguise, ignoring penalties for adopting a different race or gender.

    Through the alley, back from hell(10m, 1wp; Simple; Instant) When crew members on shore are fleeing back to the ship, Dahaka has the supernatural ability to appear at the right time and place to rescue them. This functions by intuition, luck, and sensing danger - it does not allow him to go anywhere he normally couldn’t, but merely to have previously decided to be at the right place at the right time. This does not function if the crew members have fallen asleep, while they were ashore, since their last time on Dahaka.

    Miscellaneous Charms:

    Hurry home (10m, 1wp; Simple; Instant): Dahaka fades away and vanishes on his next turn, drawn instantly to his summoner’s side. This can only be done if he is bound and his summoner is in or near water. In the case that his summoner is swimming in water, Dahaka’s appearance pushes the summoner upward so that she is standing on Dahaka’s deck (or prone, unless the summoner succeeds at a Dex + Athletics check with difficulty 2). Anyone on Dahaka is not transported with him and dropped into the water when Dahaka disappears. If Dahaka is tricked into using this charm while the summoner is aboard, it has no effect.

    Materialize (50m; 1wp; Simple; Instant): A whirlpool draws in driftwood, which gradually assemble themselves into a ship, with parts being constructed in a similar sequence as they would be in a shipyard. It also draws in sea creatures, which form Dahaka’s heart, from which sends out tendrils, from which grow his skin, mouth, fins, masts, and sails.

    Measure the wind(5m; Simple; Instant): Dahaka can discern the nature of his captain, and anyone who touches his heart with bare hands.

          Cool, thanks!

          If you're going to use it, maybe it will be helpful to hear the alternate paths I was thinking about that might be useful in a different game:

          About weapons: My original idea for weapons was it to have built-in weapons that are very powerful but cost something from the gunner to use (like motes, willpower, health levels, that kind of thing). However when I looked at the sailing rules, I noted that the strongest kind of weapons (first age weapons) are equivalent to anti-ship sorcery. Since we have a sorcerer, I didn't write rules for that. Since our sorcerer doesn't know how to summon first-circle demons, I also didn't write rules for the associated first-circle demons, and decided to gesture at a connection between them. If whoever's crewing the ship in your game doesn't have anti-ship sorcery or does have access to first-circle demons, you may want to modify this.

          About the heart: The heart was designed so that, if the ship is an enemy in a game, you can interact with it as a creepy demonic ship, by boarding it, fighting its inhabitants, and destroying its heart, rather than treating it as essentially a giant sea monster or alternately simply an enemy ship. That seemed like a more interesting narrative. Also, it seems more balanced for this demon to be vulnerable to the same attacks as other second-circle demons. Because in my game the players are crewing the ship, I made the heart pretty simple, but if it's the enemy, you could add a few more Charms (or just put cool demons in the room with it to defend it).

          About stealth: A strategy frequently used by our party is to sneak into an enemy base. The submerge mechanic, the note about tidemares, and the away team charms were set up so that, if we're going somewhere on our ship, and decide we need to sneak into somewhere, the giant ship doesn't give us away, and in fact can help a bit. That play pattern is definitely recommended, but if you don’t want to be stealthy you could power up the other piratical stuff a bit.
