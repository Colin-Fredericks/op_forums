    Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
    It's a shame my Chimera treatment on the old forums got erased : it was the post I came out of a 'retirement' from the forums on, and it was fairly well recieved.

    In essence, I felt that Chimerism should focus not on erosion of body or thematic form cohesion but rather the soul - Chimera were willing to sacrifice the 'Self' in the sense of individuality, become more like the Wyld and those that inhabited it. By shedding trappings of identity, they gained mutability. By becoming a force of nature rather than a living creature with consistant dreams, goals and values... they gained adaptability.

    A Chimera abandons her name, abandons her family, abandons her virtues and abandons everything about herself. She becomes pure whim and fancy, but throughout all this she is gifted with an immutable and eternal Exaltation. This allows her a strange paradox. The Chimera, alone of all beings in Creation, can 'melt' completely in the Wyld... and yet remain a cohesive entity, not undone. They can 'die' as the Wyld eats them, yet live on forever.

    It's a strange paradox that many Lunars would find terrifying. That said, this philosophy cannot be said to be -innately- evil... unless you take into account that such a being would probably not ascribe to any form of human (or Exalted) morality at all, and would be capable of actions that would shock even the most cold hearted. As a further irony, the being would be able to undertake these actions with even a sort of quasi-innocence, childlike and incorruptible...

    ... in this way, the state of Chimerism could even have unique benifits. A Chimera would have immense control over Intimacies (or no intimacies at all, alternately), rendering them immune to many conventional methods of control or domination.

    The mechanics of such a transition are simple enough to envision. By abandoning parts of your sheet... your 'dots'... you gain become more of a Chimera. When you abandon you set Attributes, you've begun the path. Abandoning set Abilities is a further erosion of self. But the true challenges come beyond that - abandoning your Intimacies, abandoning your Virtues, abandoning your very name. These steps are so final, so changing, that few Chimera ever complete the journey. Those that do become relatively blase about the fate of Creation or those within it... unless, of course, the mood strikes them to care for a short time before they shift into a new paradigm of ideology and psychology.

    A true Chimera, who has pursued this philosophy to the ultimate extreme, has fed the Wyld everything about her that once defined her - every dot on the sheet, every notation, every specialty, ever merit. All except for the single thing she cannot ever truly dissolve in the Wyld essence - the words 'Lunar Exalted', immutable on the very fabric of her being.
    Apart from some weird devotion to the abstract concept of adaptability, what does a Lunar get out of any of this?

    Also, it might be okay for a kind of one-off antagonistic monster (which really makes it seem better suited as a behemoth then), but you can't really make interesting characters out of it.

                                                    One thing that probably helps the Silver Pact is to view it at least partially in terms of a collective response to conditions imposed upon them, rather than something that all Lunars just happen to stumble upon independently.

                                                    The Dragon Blooded and the Sidereals are the ones who started this, the ones who cast the Solars down and saw the Lunars, the raging, storming Lunars who had been closer to the crazed, tainted Solars than anybody, as a threat to their new order and drove them out and continue to hound them.

                                                    The Silver Pact would probably be at least partially a reactionary movement.

                                                    And the thing about reactionary movements is that since they're all reacting to something, the opposition is probably the same.

                                                    But when you look closer at them, everything else they want tends to be rather different.

                                                    There will probably still be some Lunars who are more actively motivated by the Usurpation, by a desire for revenge for themselves and their Solars and a desire to reclaim their old possessions.

                                                    And such Lunars will be old and powerful, and reactionary movements tend to elevate such figures, so their personal agendas will bleed into a lot of collective rhetoric.

                                                    But still, on a practical level, most of them are being motivated more by the fact that they're living in a world controlled by the enemy. An enemy who, in the course of setting themselves up on their stolen thrones, developed a lot of demonising retroactive justification, that's become so pervasive, and backed up by lifetimes of mutual violence, that it's hard to just turn around and say "no, wait, let's judge each Lunar on their merits and make open friends with the acceptable ones", because it's gone too far for that.

                                                    At least until somebody goes and does it, but history is one long wait for people to do things that are smart and right.

                                                    And all of this makes for a Silver Pact which is likely to be fractious and constantly shifting and filled with various, often contradictory, ideas about what exactly to do next (that won't be hashed out until tomorrow because there's a war to fight today), and more than a bit of resolving internal disputes through various unpleasant means.

                                                    That they have a mutual enemy does not mean that they won't have disagreements with one another, but it also doesn't mean that there are necessarily lots of separate Silver Pacts all doing their own thing, or that there are bunches of Lunars who align with the enemy (who don't want them anyway), although there conflicts amongst themselves probably inadvertently help the enemy (the same as conflicts within the single Scarlet Dynasty and single Fivescore Fellowship probably help the Lunars, without those also being cases where those are Exalted who are generally viewed as a collective, bound by ties extending from, but not limited to, their mutual category).

                                                        I have observed in history that...

                                                        A) Very few revolutions are undertaken for the reasons that the people who undertake them believe,
                                                        B) Very few people participating in the revolution have any idea what will eventually happen after it succeeds,
                                                        C) People who are making a revolution happen are interested in gathering as many allies as possible.
                                                        D) People who are making a revolution happen are not interested in being honest beyond whatever amount of honesty serves the goal.
                                                        E) Environments which are ripe for revolution usually have lots of dissatisfied people, even amongst the elite, who are envious of others.
                                                        F) These people are often manipulated to achieve the aims of the revolution.
                                                        G) Said elites are often instrumental to the actual accomplishment of revolutionary gains, even if they are sometimes (often) discarded after.

                                                        I frankly find the idea that the Lunar Exalted of the time of the Usurpation were 'so entrenched' with the Solars that they had no issue with the government ridiculous. It proposes a single unified Solar powerbase, which is in and of itself silly ; multiple Solar 'camps' would exist, each using rhetoric and ideology to paint competing factions as dangerous and ineffective.

                                                        Obviously, multiple factions of Lunar Exalted would have also existed that had huge slights against the existing power structure ; either because they didn't have a big enough seat at the table, Solar Joe fucked her sister, or just because they don't like the same music that Joe does.

                                                        The idea that the SOLARS are a big happy family come usurpation time is silly. The idea that the Lunars are ALSO such a big happy family is also silly. Finally, the idea that the Sidereals... who needed every advantage they could get... would hesitate to ensare these people in the battle is also ridiculous. They don't give one shit about Team Lunar T-shirts. The goal is political, not splatist. They need to eliminate the state so they can take control ; they will weaken it in any way they possibly can, through devious manipulation and conspiracy, which they are obviously predisposed towards.

                                                        The Sidereals should be taking advantage of a dangerous time of political uncertainty and change that allowed them the singular opportunity to leverage a number of discorporate elements into a Usurpation ; probably much more suddenly than they liked, probably much more dangerously than they liked, and probably not as effectively as they liked.

                                                        The conspiracy should have a LARGE PORTION of Solar / Lunar participants, who are later discarded, but are brought into the whole thing with promises of Everything They Ever Dreamed once this is all over.

                                                        This would mirror ACTUAL revolutions rather than comic book battles between the X-Men and the League of Evil Mutants.
