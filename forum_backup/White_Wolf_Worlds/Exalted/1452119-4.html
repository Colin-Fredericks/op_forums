                    I had really thought that the relative lull in my life would extend through the whole Essence manuscript, but it was not to be. I've only been managing one really attentive read of a Charmset per day, and right now, I've only skimmed Abyssals (they seemed cool and flavorful) and Dragon-Blooded (I had no specific complaints, but I felt like something was missing - I hope to figure that out when I really break everything down). I haven't read Alchemical Charms at all.

                    That's on the to-do list, but today another manuscript dropped, and this one contains my favorite splat in the game! Besides, I've been skipping around the whole time anyway. Surely, nobody will blame for diving into Lunars!

                    (What? You thought something else was my favorite? But whatever for?)

                    Right off the bat, I'm reminded that there's a lot of things here I won't understand until I've seen the animal rules. That's fairly usual for shapeshifting, of course, and I am heartened that "a list of one-off special traits" is the sort of thing which people can curate and balance, unlike the 3E "full statblocks loaded with special exceptions" version, which... well, that'd be a whole other thread.

                    Changing Phases Transformation is the answer to people who wanted the omni-Excellent Lunar. It's not a perfect answer (certainly no 7-die Excellency to every possible pool) but it is the only way to start with a 5-dot Attribute in by-the-book EE, and if you have any advance warning about what you're doing, it can always be the right Attribute. Does your stooped old witch need to topple a statue? Force 5 will do for this scene. Got exposed to a supernatural plague? Time for Fortitude 5 until you fight it off. Versatile.

                    Once again, I am pretty dubious of Deadly Beastman Transformation. Infernals can get a scenelong +2 Soak without reducing their Defense, why take a penalty for a mere +1? On the other hand, Hybrid Body Transformation looks like a crucial combat buff. +2 successes to many of your attacks (and certainly Decisive ones) should be doable with most combat-oriented shapes. I don't know how to read the implication that this could instead boost Defense, however, since it also uses the word "actions".

                    It's kind of funny that I never considered using Mountainous Spirit Expression on a human form until the book told me I had to wait until Essence 3 and buy it again. Honestly, though, forget your true human shape - the real style points are for stealing the face of the Satrap's secret lover, childhood best friend, or sternest old school teacher, then lecturing the whole city on their evil deeds in the form of a kaiju-sized ghost from their past. Keep things surreal.

                    Meanwhile, no love for Tyrant Mouse Dominion. I suppose that a Charm must be good for more than just keeping things surreal.

                    Hungry All-Consuming Cloud seems odd. It's the only Finesse 5 Charm Lunars get, but has an effect scaled off of Fortitude. It has 3E-style verbiage that "only the highest withering damage roll made grants Power," which doesn't seem needed with EE's withering rules. I don't really know what the last sentence means. I am reporting this one as a probable version error.

                    Wow, Laughing Devil Distraction is a brutal debuff. One more piece of evidence on the pile of "Defense looks terrible in Essence."

                    Oddly, Quicksilver Second Face lets you freely take a form you've already assumed this scene, when the 3E version of that build (via Ever-Evolving Defense) winds up trying to pick a new form each action.

                    Shadow Talon Execution lets a character making an attack from stealth gain a bonus to their next War Project action! Ah, versioning errors.

                    Hey, Beast-Soul Awakening is a Charm which seems like it should make a special Project, and actually does just that! Nice.

                    It had not occurred to me that a character could not freely participate in social influence outside of combat time while holding someone in a headlock, until Grasping Pseudopod Method said it could let me sidestep that problem. Sure, in real life, wrestling of any kind tends to leave a body short on both breath and attention, but in action-adventure world, this surprised me - another of my failures to read the authors' default assumptions.

                    Insidious Lunar Transformation has nothing to say about the target's traits while transformed. Surely they're not meant to keep all their best pools, like a Lunar does, but I don't know what really happens. I'm waiting to see Antagonists, but feel like something is missing.

                    Does Adder Fang Method let me poison someone with a Withering attack? If so, does that attack "hit" when it rolled below their Defense, but still got me Overwhelm-based Power?

                    Creation-Spanning Passion (a Charm which I instantly like better now that it acts on existing relationships and drops the bonus to rolls to establish them) mentions Solar Mates. I do a quick search, and see it is the only one to do so. However, I seem to have not registered a few things in earlier manuscripts: the Lunar mode for Integrity-Protecting Principle did this, and the term is used twice (though never explained) in the Storytelling chapter. I really wonder what someone new to Exalted would make of this, especially a non-ST who first sees those words jump out of nowhere mid-Charm.

                    Upon reaching Relentless Lunar Fury, I realize that there have been relatively few combat-oriented Charms, and they've all leaned into very narrow fighting styles and/or aesthetics: the "beastman" Universal Charms, the "duplicates" stuff in Finesse, the grappling ones in Force, and Adder-Fang Method. This is the only native Lunar Charm to seem generally applicable to a wide range of combat characters.

                    The rules are kind of odd, though. It gets activated on Step 7, but doesn't specify it has to be Step 7 of a decisive attack against the Lunar, so you can apparently go berserk on your own first Withering attack. Maybe they just don't want a Lunar to use the pseudo-Knife Biting Attitude the same turn that she puts RLF up, but again, that just encourages people to pop it early in any fight where they might need it. Also, you really want to exceed 10 Power, since unlike the Greater Sign of Mars, going above 10 Power lets you bank bonus damage successes. Eeep.

                    Honestly, I want Shattered Yoke Fury to be the general rule, instead of just letting you shrug off anything that doesn't fit your vision of the character. "Form an Intimacy and take an immediate dramatic action" is a much more gameable concept than "give them a small number of bonus dice."

                    Overall, I think these Charms look fun, and did a good job of selling what Lunars are to new players. People should come away from this list with the idea that Lunars are tricky folklore-ish figures. The combat Charms tend to be oddly narrow (no self-healing?), and there are definitely favorite tricks I'm sad not to see (priority one is bringing back the Many-Pockets Meditation tree, after that I can try more complicated stuff like Mirror Slip Trick), but there's not a lot here which I don't think justifies its presence. The only hole that really makes me scratch my head is how limited their social suite is - Predator-and-Prey Mirror seems to literally be the only way to influence others' intimacies in the whole set. I think that someone with no context beyond this book would see Lunars as loners who bless groups they like and steal shapes from those they dislike, but don't try to sway what those people do beyond being strong or being dead.

                    Writing it out, that's not all wrong, but it is pretty extreme. As with Infernals getting lots of tricks to mess with one person but very little to convince the crowd, I'm not sure how much of this is space limitations and how much is a deliberate statement.

                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post

                        So... can I use my unfaltering dedication to the blade (Close Combat Excellency) to raise my Resolve?
                        Is it applicable to the situation and involve the ability? Since Resolve is calculated using Integrity, Close Combat shouldn't be usable. However, if you have an intimacy relating to your blade, it would increase your Resolve when applicable.

                        What about my understanding of different people and cultures (Embassy Excellency)?
                        Again, Resolve isn't calculated from Embassy. Not applicable.

                        Can I bump my Soak and Hardness with an Excellency?
                        Not in combat, but if you had a Fortitude Excellency, you could increase your Soak outside of combat. Hardness isn't based off of an ability or attribute. Excellencies wouldn't apply.

                        If someone makes a social roll against me as their combat action can I use my Integrity Excellency to boost my Resolve?
                        Not RAW.

                        And, if not, what stops my players starting combat every time they want to negotiate?
                        First, it usually won't benefit them since Excellencies are rarer than in 3e. Second, they'll have a decent chance of triggering Virtues and Intimacies by putting people's lives on the line, which could increase the difficulty instead of making it easier.

                                            My above ribbing aside, I am going to (time permitting) cover the Charms of my old favorite (and, alas, still-namesake) splat.

                                            Absence looks good, though I am sad that a new wave of Sidereal players will try to get out of conversations by shouting that, rather the more-cumbersome but more amusing "Avoidance Kata!" - hey, if there's any splat where I can be a grumpy old geezer, it's this one. It also seems like an odd choice that (1) the Sidereal knows if everyone forgets them, and (2) more people forgetting you makes it harder to hide. Huh.

                                            Invisible Motion fills me with questions. I don't know if it's an old version, or if I was meant to deduce a ton of limitations on Inexorable Advance back in universal modes. At first blush, the Essence 1 Charm seems to do a lot more, but now I presume that you only get the "flash step" effect only happens on a rush, rather than all movement actions? I have no idea whether any of the other Sid/Get Charms which make movement into a short-range teleport are meant to go through walls.

                                            The pet Pattern Spider from Efficient Secretary Technique can no longer tell you who stabbed Sesus Lahor. I can provide a bonus to any non "combat-related" action, which is neat, but make me wonder whether planning a military campaign counts. Also, can it help me when my action is teamwork for an ally? Hopefully not, due to Pattern Spiders infamously hating recursion.

                                            I think Last Vision is new, and I secretly hope it was created explicitly so that we can all solve the murder of Sesus Lahor, now that the spiders won't tell. It does, otherwise, feel like a very odd choice for a Sidereal Charm.

                                            This is mostly errata stuff, but if you spend stunt dice on Of Truths Best Unknown, you technically gain 2 Anima, due to that Charm duplicating the general note at the start of Sidereal Charms. This is actually kind of a tricky problem, since mechanical notes in the lore sentence at the start of any given Charm section are easy to overlook.

                                            Okay, Mending Warped Designs, but why am I rolling Finesse + Craft? What's the difficulty, what happens if I succeed or fail? The implication seems to be "Difficulty 3" (because none is specified) and "the Charm fails" (because this only comes up when opposing powerful effects), but I'm purely speculating here.

                                            Cash and Murder Games keeps the awesome name while getting rid of the creepiest language and effects. That makes me very happy, so I feel a little bad that I'm now wondering if this now requires the Sidereal to be in the same place as both targets at once, and if not, what that means for any other Charms which create new influence actions if they don't give details on range - and Essence almost never does.

                                            Impossible-to-resist effects aren't just for Getimians, since a Sidereal can End Debate.

                                            There's been a lot of discussion about Hard Bargains versus opting out of social influence lately, so it's interesting to me that Icy Hand gives you further benefits if people take the former but not the latter. An odd inversion of Fate-Unwinding Denial and Devil Emperor Edict.

                                            I'm not the biggest fan of things which get extremely literal with the "Divine Bureaucracy" thing, but Secret Sinecure Agreement has some very cool roleplaying possibilities.

                                            With Indecision is Death, I officially have no idea how the writers are expecting me to use the influence system.

                                            Sculpting the Wild Lands is the best special project-making Charm.

                                            Abandoned Words Curse finally makes it official: being incapable of speech is a four-die penalty to influence. I am now convinced that the penalty for Language-Learning Ritual is meant to be dice, since otherwise, using a Charm to magically learn a language is no better for communication than just using pantomime.

                                            Charms which don't count as Charms is not my favorite design space, but than again, if Defense of Shining Joy did count as using a Charm, I have no idea when it would happen, so I assume that was their thinking as well.

                                            Faultless Ceremony sure thinks we're going to keep a lot of stunt dice in the pool. But the 2:1 ratio has me confused - when Sidereal Charms talk about spending stunt dice, I assumed they meant sets of dice, which seems to me like the only quantity of stunt dice that anything lets you spend. But this makes it look like they mean individual dice each time. Huh.

                                            It's used on Step 4, so it looks to me like Shield of Destiny can be enhanced with an Excellency. Worth remembering when we reach Antagonists, but my guess is that nothing short of an outright bossfight is immune. Think about all the poor schmucks spending 2m to raise their defenses, when a Sidereal does the same thing to become totally immune to an attack and potentially hurt an enemy. This may be the strongest combat Charm I've seen so far (especially if we don't include Abyssals, which I only skimmed but seem to have an auto-kill attack).

                                            Man, remember back in 2E, when Unswerving Juggernaut Principle seemed to imply you should track movement right down to the single-digit degrees of a turn? Never let my nitpicking in these threads distract from what an improvement we're looking at. Anyway, this Charm is beyond badass.

                                            See, Shun the Smiling Lady has mechanisms for both the target and other impacted people to resist. That's like 40% of what I'm worried about in the Getimian powers right there.


                                            This seems like a good distillation of Sidereal Charms into a very small space. I have more questions than I did for Infernals or Lunars, but not nearly as many as I had for Getimians - I feel like I could run almost all of these in play without any problems. I do wonder how many of Sids' famously oddly placed Charms have been moved to more "normal" Abilities - I only noticed Transcendent Hatchet of Fate going from Lore/Sagacity to Ranged Combat, but I think there were more. I did find it odd that there are no Essence 5 effects, and only a single Essence 4 one (repurchasing Sculpting the Wild Lands into an unnamed Neighborhood Relocation Scheme) - even without Charm trees existing, the Sidereal ones appear to remain closed.

                                            It always seemed odd to me how the "spy" Exalts had tiny Personal pools which forced them to flare Anima a lot, and for better or worse, Essence very deliberately keeps that contradiction. On the upside, this also shows that the Greater Signs really are meant to be a fairly-regular part of a Sidereal toolbox.

                                            I think that if I came to this charmset without existing prejudices about Sidereals (fat chance), I would see that they're very sneaky, and good at getting indirect or massive-scale effects - that is, I suppose, the strength of the much-vaunted "best Charm set of 1E," courtesy of master of strange and large-scale effects Jenna Moran. I would also notice that many of their strongest effects also pointed at long-term consequences you might not like, which is a very cool and subtle thing to get across in a format like this. However, I would probably also be thinking that combat is a bigger part of their themes than it is for Lunars or Getimians (though not Infernals), since those guys had few or highly-specialized combat tricks, while Sidereals get versatile powers that anyone would want to use, such as Shield of Destiny and Unswerving Juggernaut Principle, and that is probably not the intention.

                                            I try to mostly play support-based characters in RPGs, and just as it was fifteen years earlier, these Sids make a subtle support character seem like the coolest person in the setting. This is my favorite of the Charmsets I've read in-depth.

                                            "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                                                Originally posted by Chejop Kejak View Post

                                                I tend to agree (as I said in the post you yourself are quoting, I think it's clear that the intent is Integrity Excellency helping with Resolve), but it is worth noting that the text doesn't establish any requirements for boosting a static value with an Excellency. As written, JohnDoe is correct that any Excellency can boost any static value.
                                                It follows the same rules as adding dice.

                                                Add that Ability as a dice bonus to an applicable roll involving the chosen Ability. You may add Close Combat dice to Close Combat attacks, or add Integrity dice to Integrity rolls to resist the sway of a Deathlord, but may not use Stealth dice to add to a Close Combat attack or Ranged Combat to add dice to a Navigation roll.
                                                So, you can't use Close Combat for Resolve because Resolve is keyed to Integrity.
