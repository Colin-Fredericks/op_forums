            So I've been brainstorming more shaping rituals for my demonblade sorcerous archetype. Here's one fairly complete:
            • The magic taught by the demon is dark and insidious, twisting the sorcerer to slowly turn his love to hate. By focusing his will, the sorcerer can reduce the level of one of his positive ties by one (a minor tie goes away completely), gaining (Essence x Intimacy level) sorcerous motes instead. When the sorcerer next gains Willpower from resting, he must roll Wits+Integrity (difficulty 5 – Intimacy level) to remember the intimacy again. If succeeds, the intimacy returns to the previous level, else it stays as it is. If the intimacy was a minor one, it is instead replaced with a minor tie of hate towards the same subject. The sorcerer may use the motes at any time until he sleeps, and may only keep motes from one reduced intimacy.

            It kind of scares me how destructive this one is... Not sure I want my character to go down this path. The mechanics on this might not be right, the intention was that it's harder to lose stronger ties, but as soon as they start to go down it becomes a slippery slope to hate. I think what I wrote probably just ends up rewarding only using your Defining intimacies (more motes for them, less risk of losing them)... hmm...

            Another idea I had was to take the ritual from Mara where you get motes from unrequited love, but change it to either hate and/or fear. Maybe those ties are easier to build than unrequited love via Presence, especially with the Dawn anima bonus?

            Thoughts?

            PS. Jürgen, feel free to use mine when it's ready.

                                            So, I've been thinking this week about a sorcerous initiation based on the powers of stone. This is still a work in progress, and I'll post it to the wiki when it's done. I don't like the name yet.

                                            The Flowing Stillness of the Stone

                                            The sorceress has been enlightened by meditating upon the nature of stone. It is widely known that stone is permanent, immutable, and as unchanging as the very laws of creation. Yet, stone flows subtly, moving and changing through the earth. Stone is eroded by the winds and the waves, it is cracked by the roots of trees, and it can be melted by flame, Indeed, the unchanging, permanent nature of stone can be changed even by mortal hands. Such is the nature of creation; that which seems permanent, immutable and fundamental may yet be transformed.

                                            The rituals of this initiation embody the nature of stone. They are slow moving, powerful, and permanent. But their power is tempered by their inflexible nature. The weight of a mountain is enough to crush an entire nation, yet the mountain is helpless before stonemasons and their chisels.

                                            Rituals:

                                            Essence flows through the earth, slowly and imperceptibly, and when the flow of this essence hits a snarl it begins to crystallise, transforming base stone into precious stones and jewels. The sorceress has learned to draw upon the essence contained in the treasures of the earth. At any time, she may consume the essence contained in jewels and semiprecious stones in short range, gaining (Resources value of jewels + Essence) sorcerous motes. The jewels are utterly consumed by this process, turning to chalk, sandstone, glass or even powdery dust. The sorceress may choose which jewels to consume, and if the opportunity presents itself she may even draw power from an enemy's treasures. The sorceress may also draw up to essence sorcerous motes from earth elementals and other spirits of the earth. When casting her control spell, jewels are only reduced to a lesser form, losing 1 dot of their resources value.

                                            This is modelled on the ifrit power, however I've increased the mote gain to reflect the the rather more permanent cost of consuming wealth.

                                            The stone is still and immutable, and the flow of essence through the earth is directed and constrained. The sorceress may harness this power by inscribing a great mandala into the stone. First, she must spend a month studying the flow of essence through the earth around a certain site. She then choses one of her spells, and carves into the earth an occult diagram to direct essence into the shape of the spell. This is an Intelligence + Occult roll, difficulty five. Whenever she is standing at the centre of this diagram, she may gain (essence + extra successes) sorcerous motes to cast that spell. This diagram must usually be cut directly into the stone of the earth, rather than bricks, cobblestones, stone slabs, or other artificial such surfaces. A well constructed diagram may also aid summoning spells, granting (Essence) dice to the binding roll of these spells.

                                            While the power of these diagrams is significant, they are limited. While a sufficiently capable sorceress might construct a diagram sufficient to cast "death of obsidian butterflies" nearly every turn, she is exceptionally vulnerable while she does so. She stands in the middle of a clear stone diagram, with no cover, leaving her vulnerable to hidden snipers or stealthy assassins. Should an enemy approach her, she is limited in her capacity to dodge. Enemies might choose to wait her out, forcing her to come to them, and and savvy enemies might even disrupt the geomancy of the local area until her diagram no longer functions.


                                            Merit: Follow the Flow of the Earth. °°°
                                            The sorceress has gained a deep understanding of the flow of essence in the earth, and can feel the subtle, timeless currents that run through the stone. She recognises a curve in the grain of stone that speaks to a passage beneath it, and the subtle distortions that suggest the presence of a vein of ore. May roll intelligence + occult to find geological features (such as caves or passages leading in her preferred direction), or to prospect for jewels, ores, or magical materials. The storyteller should bear in mind that such treasures remain scarce; while the sorceress might be able to follow the currents of stone to find a deposit of jade on the other side of a small kingdom, she is unlikely to find more nearby.

                                            Merit: Kin to the Bedrock. °°
                                            Through her meditations on the nature of stone, the sorceress has taken the essence of earth into her very self. Creatures of stone, such as earth elementals and spirits associated with the earth, recognise her as their kin and gain a minor positive tie towards her. The nature of this tie depends on the spirit. Least gods and minor earth elementals might feel awe, love, or filial piety, more powerful beings might have a tie of respect, solidarity, or loving jealousy, and ancient creatures such as the gemlords might have a tie of proud parental indulgence, or perhaps more possessive feelings. Even the least forms of stone accept her as their sister and welcome her; she treats damage from falling onto stone surfaces as though she had fallen one less range band, and other sources of environmental damage from stone are similarly reduced.

                                                    Originally posted by Swooper View Post
                                                    First off: Hi, I'm new to this forum, and fairly new to Exalted, starting my first third edition game in early January. I did study 2E pretty extensively, but never got the chance to play it except in a single rather short one-shot.

                                                    I've been spending the past couple of weeks tinkering with my character, a Dawn caste sorcerer-general, including his custom artifact + sorcerous initiation combo which, upon finding this thread, I decided to share with you guys for help with fleshing it out.

                                                    The Demonblade
                                                    The idea is that he has this daiklave that he took from a barbarian warlord (outcaste DB or exigent maybe) he defeated in battle. When he attuned to the blade, a voice started whispering into his mind, offering him power. It claimed to be a demon, imprisoned within the daiklave by a solar in the First Age. In fact, the demon went on, that solar was the previous incarnation of my character's exaltation, and he bound him there so that he might seek out the next incarnation of his essence and teach him sorcery, thereby preserving his most valuable tool between reincarnations. Whether this is actually true is up to my ST to decide, I just assume it will be interesting either way.

                                                    The daiklave itself is a red jade leaf-shaped double-edged daiklave, inlaid with oricalchum Old Realm runes spelling out its name. The blade is patterned like folded (Damascus/wootz) steel, and when held by an attuned wielder, the pattern seems to shift slowly. The hilt is solid oricalchum set with emeralds, and green-dyed leather is wrapped around the grip. It does not exhibit any obvious outward signs of being possessed.

                                                    Shaping Ritual
                                                    The demon thirsts eternally, and drinks eagerly whenever the daiklave draws blood. Indeed, blood staining the blade seems to quickly seep into it and vanish. When the sorcerer makes a decisive attack with the daiklave to an opponent that bleeds, he gains sorcerous motes equal to the health levels inflicted as damage. Withering attacks against battlegroups instead grant one sorcerous mote for every three points of post-soak damage. Sorcerous motes granted by this ritual last until the end of the scene and are technically stored in the daiklave rather than the sorcerer himself. As such, they may only be used if the sorcerer still has the blade within reach (it does not need to be unsheathed). Only up to (Sorcerer’s Essence + Permanent Willpower) sorcerous motes may be stored in the blade. When the sword is holding any sorcerous motes in this way, flames seem to dance inside the emeralds adorning the hilt.

                                                    I didn't create any custom merits yet (being new to the system, homebrewing seems kind of intimidating) but I did snag The Burning Name, mainly because being able to throw fire around at will seems awfully useful and the kind of thing a sorcerer should be able to do and a demon able to teach.

                                                    The things I need help with:
                                                    • The identity and name of the demon, name of the daiklave (note that it was created before the demon was bound to it)
                                                    • Does the shaping ritual I wrote seem balanced?
                                                    • More shaping rituals
                                                    • Associated custom merits
                                                    • Evocations for the daiklave (these should probably go in a separate thread, which I'll gladly set up with some more info if anyone expresses interest in this part)

                                                    I like this, although I wonder if it might end up being overpowered in play; I haven't tested the new combat rules much so I'm not sure how much lethal damage a sorcerer might be expected to deal over the course of combat, but if you're garnering twenty sorcerous motes over a battle I wonder if it might be unbalanced, at least without some kind of drawback or limit (eg: sorcerous motes may only be used for spells that directly deal violence).
