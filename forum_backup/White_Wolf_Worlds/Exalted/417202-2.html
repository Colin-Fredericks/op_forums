    The TAW Project - in many ways it is the zenith of that whole Infernals focused fandom from that time of 2e. And Revlid is actually a good writer! He managed to make a lot of 2e and late 2e ideas exciting (which is hard to do). If 3e were about continuing late 2e's vision...

    For the TAW themselves, my impression of them has always been (and this is with the disclaimer I'm not a head who has read them a lot)...

    They're pretty much what their name says they are :

    - Terrifying - their magic sows terror and provokes fear, they unsettle, they trouble, they predate - they can use this to coerce and control humanity

    - Witches - they feel witches in the Western tradition, which are made distinct from sorcerers and wizards in they invoke magic which is decidedly irrational, bloodied, earthy, from the natural world, rooted in sacrifice and bad luck, unsettling, does not really feel "clean".

    (And I guess they're argent in that they're also somehow shiny?)

    Your mileage with the TAW as a concept will depend on how interesting you find these two main aspects together. Do you want to be terrifying and work through terror? Do you want to wield magic that feels like witches' magic?

    Yes, they clearly come from the parts of the fandom who were overwhelmingly in love with Infernals - particularly with those ideas where you are a person who has the challenge of using morally tainting and inhumane for ends which are moral or humane. It is a dark vision.

    I don't have much interest in any of that stuff, really! (For instance, I'm not interested in people who use terrorist tactics "for good").

    (I also have a slight distaste for the way TAW seems to use comparatively large amounts of complex mythology from their god to yield comparatively small levels of interesting magic, seems uneconomical - many more splats seem to get a lot out of beginning with a comparatively small amount of ideas. This is also true of Infernals).

    Your mileage will vary.

                    "Heaven" in Exalted is more analogous to Mount Olympus, with a lot of asshole gods sitting around, pushing humans into taking courses of actions. It also draws fairly heavily from Asian (Chinese, Tibetan, Japanese and Hindu) mythology about Heaven. It doesn't really draw much on the Abrahamic concept of Heaven, as this place souls go to when they die. You'll note in those earlier religions, souls don't go to heaven when they die - in fact for the most people, people don't go there much at all.

                    If you're looking for undying feasting halls and battlefields or the Elysian Fields you'd be better off looking at the Underworld.

                                Mmyup. If you were to say that office politics and bureaucracy shapes who Sidereals are (which is, I would point out, not quite the same thing as their place in the setting, although the two are connected), I would agree that it plays a part. That's represented in Chinese myth as Heaven, but it's also a thing in itself.

                                See, there's a couple of layers of separation at work here, from my perspective. "Who Sidereals are" is Wise Heroes; figures of experience and knowledge who work behind the scenes and never (seem to) put a foot wrong. For example, take Avoidance Kata. Sidereals don't have Avoidance Kata because they have any particular association with teleportation or retcons, rather they have it because the Wise Hero never gets into a fight they cannot win, unless by losing they advance their cause.

                                This is represented in Exalted with their ties to Destiny and Fate, which is in turn represented by the Loom, the engine of reality, which loops back around to Sidereals having powers like Avoidance Kata. Heaven is not who Sidereals are; it is the method of realising who Sidereals are.
