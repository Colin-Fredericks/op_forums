                    For the range band issue, I'd probably describe extreme range as being "out of effective longbow range", not necessarily out of range altogether. So, once the target reached extreme range, I'd describe them as being behind too many trees, building, sand dunes, etc. for a bow shot to work. In situations where there was a lack of general cover and it would be totally implausible for someone to run far enough in a few turns to be out of range, I'd say that the terrain counted as difficult only for purposes of moving away from the ranged fighter. Thus, if someone was running away from an archer across a wide-open prairie, they'd actually take two rounds of movement to cross each range band, and thus six total to reach extreme range. But that wouldn't apply to the melee character chasing behind them. Of course, this means that someone could be at effectively different range bands with two PCs, despite neither of the PCs moving, but I find that in 3e combat, you've pretty much got to treat range bands as a relative thing between individual characters anyway, so it doesn't bother me much.

                    In battlefields with more cover, but the ranged combatant has access to magic that lets them ignore cover (like the Solar charm Wise Arrow, for example), I'd allow the archer to retroactively apply the difficult terrain thing to their opponent as long as they were willing and able to spend the motes on the cover-handling charm at the same time as they made the attack. So, in the original scenario, the Solar would activate Seven Omens Shot, and take her three rounds of Aim. If the target had been running away the whole time, the Solar could activate Wise Arrow when she made the actual attack, and determine that the target was "actually" only at Medium range (since they would have needed two Move actions to go from Short to Medium, and the single Move since then won't take them out to Long yet).

                                            A alternate world I had.

                                            So things went up into the shitter. Things exploded, the Unconquered Sun was Conquered and died, and the loom of fate was broken, reshaped, and basically everything went tits up.

                                            The Solars died in one great big battle, the conflict slaying many of the terrestrial host, smashing apart the sidereals and Yu Shan, and basically killing the celestials. The celestial exaltations were locked away, and the terrestrial host was smashed apart.

                                            Creation splintered, somewhat similar to time of the Cascading years. history itself rewriting, warping. In some places, this the world was scifi like star wars. In others, the world was a flat disc, carried by 4 elephants, on a turtle floating through space. In others, it was a world that was a sphere, surrounded by the cold void of space. Some were wyld zones where narrative laws existed, where conforming to specific archetypes granted you bonuses.

                                            In this split apart universe, the exalted were long gone, with only the rare dragonblooded surfacing. Either way, it was slow. Terrestrial sorcerers and thaumaturges still existed, and could open up portals to other worlds. Some, began conquest. Great fleets of ships swimming through the great beyond between worlds. Some took technology and added it to tech. Others, suppressed it.

                                            And then, in one of the ships that salvaged old ruins for precious artifacts and magical materials, they found... something. A box. Eager for treasure and long-forgotten riches, they opened it.

                                            And out flew the exaltations, all 1,000 of them. And they began to exalt people....
