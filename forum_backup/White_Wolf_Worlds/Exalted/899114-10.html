                    Whoof. Did I really put this off so long?

                    Welp, I'm Arian Dynas, and before you ask, no, it was an ill-conceived name picked out of a character generation book, not some kind of comment. I've gone by that handle for quite a while now, it was the name of a D&D character I made but never did play, mostly because I was a lonely preteen, and when I did actually get to play D&D as a kid, I was always the DM.

                    I'm a prospective writer who's just graduated from College, I live in Alaska, and no, it's not really as cold as you'd think. I'm an anthropologist and philosopher, or at least that's what my degree says, and simply put; I freaking love Exalted. I've loved RPGs since I was 5 years old, watching my big brothers sitting at a table, sometimes playing AD&D, sometimes Space-Hulk. I actually got to playing about in middle school. I keep a big ol library of RPG books in my apartment; friends have been concerned that an earthquake may well crush me under falling bookshelves.

                    I'm also one half of the dev team for an Exalted character generator and manager, Anathema Reincarnated, if you're interested in that sort of thing.

                    In my case I discovered Exalted through World of Darkness; I'd been reading a Ghenna scenario in which it said one of the possible outcomes would be an "Exalted" like setting. Having a vague frame of reference based on a TV tropes article about "Always Chaotic Evil" demons being justified in setting, I talked it over with the friend I was plotting out this massive Ghenna scenario with, and we figured we'd try Exalted as the result of the players winning the Ghenna scenario. Aaaaaand that was when we first fell down the rabbit hole. I picked up a copy of the 2e manual in a used bookstore and was deeply intrigued; we decided to give it a crack sometime, so I read up, discovered Keychain of Creation, and was pretty well in by this point, but, something was wrong. Basically, Second Edition burned me multiple times on what I saw was a wonderful idea.

                    It didn't help that at the time I had a friend who was *way* into Second Edition and took everything as gospel; including all of the fluff that John Morke would have termed "Shitdark" so for a while, everything that I got excited for in setting, and mechanics wise, it'd get shot down. This culminated in when I read DotFA, and although I was clinging to a love for it, the magic was gone. Maybe it was a sunk-cost fallacy on my part.

                    Then I heard about EX3. I read some stuff; I got excited. I started clearing out some old assumptions 2e had engendered in me, then, I read Richard Danksy's Trilogy of the Second Age. The last two books... weren't that good, but the first one... even with a disappointing ending, the book opened my mind again, so many things that were boxed and labelled, forced into specific unchanging roles in my mind about Exalted suddenly burst out again, just because of the way that Exalted's first Dev wrote about it.I started reading 1e books, and suddenly, the magic was back! There wasn't much of it, but everything was so evocative and powerful again and I felt I could grow to love it again that it didn't matter that there wasn't nearly as much of the passion as before.

                    Then 3e came out, and I loved it; it seemed to be a fulfillment of all of the promises that I'd been given the first time I'd heard the name "Exalted" Something was still missing though... then I read Tales from the Flat Earth, and everything just clicked. Conan, Anime, Western Fantasy, Ghibli Movies, The Bible and bullshit Wuxia Demigods suddenly all crystallized. And now, here I am.

                    I'll be stomping these forums mostly for the fun of it, and to share a passion with you all, so I'll catch you on the flip side.

                    Oh and in case anybody knows me from the Bioware Forums, I have a tendency for Walls-O-Text. I'm told I got rather infamous for it there.

                    Oh and for the people who care about that sort of thing, since I'm an anthropologist, the big reason I love Exalted, especially now, is not just the bullshit Kung-Fu Demigods, but also the deep down verisimilitude of the setting, along with it's kitchen sink tendencies. So being an Anthro, my political leanings tend toward the egalitarian, liberal, and generally treating everyone with compassion. For those of you who care to know that sort of thing, as I said.
