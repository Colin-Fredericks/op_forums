                                                        Huh; I don't think I've ever really wondered before where the idiom connecting blood with heredity came from. That whole line of a parent's blood running in your veins.

                                                        ​I wonder if it has some kind of origin in pre-natal blood exchange via the umbilical cord, and perhaps some kind of ancient misconception that this was literally where your blood came from and that you had the same blood for your entire life, but it wouldn't really account for people extending the idiom to fathers.

                                                        ​Hell, it's even inherent to the name Dragon Blooded.

                                                        Heh, it's curious how a phrase can be so elementary that one takes it for granted.

                                                        Of all the things to make emblematic of the connection between related people, why blood, I wonder?

                                                        ​Maybe it's just an extension of that idea that views blood as something essential and emblematic of life itself, so there was a persistent notion that the blood contained something essential and mystical that was in common between family members.

                                                        ​Or perhaps people just thought that bodily fluids weren't very different from one another.

                                                        ​It's actually a little bit funny, considering how little DNA is actually present in blood compared to everything else, including having the cells where the most noteworthy thing that could be made redundant so that they could fit in even more oxygen was the DNA.

                                                        ​That your blood has to be the most interchangeable thing in you is a big part of why transfusion is so simple compared to transplantation.
