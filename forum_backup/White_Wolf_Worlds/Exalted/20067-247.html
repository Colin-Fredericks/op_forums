                Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                I keep seeing people mention that they don't like 3e's difficulty system because it makes hitting a soldier more difficult than performing surgery, but I'm confused, is there a place where it suggests that Surgery or other Medicine actions should be Difficulty 1 excepting the circumstances which increase or decrease difficulty described in the actual medicine rules themselves?
                P. 187 - "Tasks which run-of-the-mill individuals in Creation would consider challenging (such as picking a lock or removing a patient’s appendix without killing him) are ordinary fare for heroes. Such tasks are appropriate for difficulty 1." The following paragraph supplies the other thing I always talk about in this discussion, where removing said appendix at night in the middle of a storm bumps the difficulty up to 2.

                Not only is it easier than hitting a soldier, it's easier than hitting a farmer with little to no training (the Brigand/Conscript/Militia on p. 496)... and since those malnourished farmers have a 4-dice Medicine pool, they are all more effective surgeons than warriors. No wonder Creation has so few health problems!

                To be fair, these malnourished farmers have very broadly-applicable "little to no training," since they seem to have two dots each in Melee, Archery, and Brawl. They haven't figured out that you get a Parry bonus from a Medium Weapon, though, so they've still got problems. They are also shockingly sneaky, as the only mortals to get a Stealth pool save the Assassin/Spymaster. If you send farmer conscripts to fight the Brides of Ahlat, they actually have a better-than-even chance of successfully sneaking away when they inevitably realize this is a suicide mission.

                        Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post

                        It seems, to me, to be an issue of rules intended to be game elements that need not directly correspond and simulate in-setting truth being read as if they are rules intended to be simulation of in-setting truth - which is what the "for heroes" bit of the p. 187 quote is there to try and help people not do (by which I mean the book says those tasks are appropriate difficulty 1 rolls for heroes, not for farmers, malnourished or otherwise).

                        And that mismatch of writing intent and read intent is further shown by inaccurate statements of "those malnourished farmers have a 4-dice Medicine pool," (they have no medicine-related pool listed) and "these malnourished farmers have very broadly-applicable 'little to no training' since they seem to have two dots each in Melee, Archery, and Brawl" (they have no dots in any abilities, just dice pools of appropriate size to present the intended challenge difficulty for malnourished farmers with enough fight in them not to be represented by the trivial opponent rules).

                            Originally posted by Drake View Post
                            It seems, to me, to be an issue of rules intended to be game elements that need not directly correspond and simulate in-setting truth being read as if they are rules intended to be simulation of in-setting truth -
                            This is, I think, the biggest issue many people have with Exalted.

                            Many people look at the setting and say, "I expect that the setting of Exalted will be reflected by the rules for Exalted. So if the rules say X, then we should also see X in the setting." But that isn't the case at all.

                            At the most, I think, you could say that the rules of Exalted are inspired by the setting, but trying to look at the setting as if the rules were an accurate representation of how things operate in Creation is going to be entirely unsuccessful.

                                Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                                For what it's worth, I don't think people are wrong in being sort of dismayed by that, I'd probably bump it up to Difficulty 2, though I do think other circumstances mentioned in the medicine write up seem to cover other obvious issues for why its so challenging, such as in the case of making an improper diagnosis and lacking the tools to do so (not having any kind of anesthetic when you're performing surgery like that seems to definitely fit the bill for raising the difficulty!)
                                Surgery is something that people train for years to do. In real life, we didn't manage an appendectomy until 1735. In Exalted, a dimwitted klutz with no medical training using bronze age tools will literally always succeed if they care a great deal - even in the worst circumstances, they've got a 40% chance, and a PC would have to be trying to get worse than 78.4%. So, a +1 bump to the difficulty is probably a good start, but it feels marginal to me - I'd most likely go for Difficulty 4, minimum.

                                But, really, surgery isn't the point. It's an extra-goofy example, so it's the one I use, but the reason I mention it is as a symptom of the oddness of the Difficulty system in general. The one where First Age Exalts created N/A Artifacts which could reshape the world in their image... and also locks which scale up to Difficulty 4. Where gritty realism means that the greatest of mortal heroes will almost certainly die from a 100-foot fall... unless they jump into a hay bale, which most combat characters can manage to survive falling a mile. When Difficulty 5 is (1) reserved for tasks which seem to be literally impossible, and (2) not a huge obstacle for high-end mortal dice pools, let alone Exalts, tasks with Difficulties wind up in a weird place. And that goes double when you realize there's nothing "impossible" about a mortal with Parry 5 - you can do that with Dex 3, Melee 3, a Specialty, and a sword! Hence, surgery is easier than hitting the worst combatants in the game - because opposed rolls are just magically harder than any other action.

                                Well, except for picking things up, I guess, but Feats of Strength are a whole separate discussion.

                                        It's an option, but much as people always seem to misconstrue this point, I don't really want to make EX3's rules even more granular - and I especially don't want to randomly keep someone's Ultimate Doctor character from doing cool things just because they didn't take the right specialty. I hate that stuff in Lore and Craft.

                                        I want PCs to do impressive things. To me, making every non-opposed action effortless doesn't actually accomplish this goal, because things like surgery (at night, in a storm, etc.) suddenly stop being impressive. Adding random barriers of entry has the opposite problem, by keeping PCs from doing things in general, impressive or no.

                                        But I think I've ridden this sidetracked train far enough. If you want to hear more of my difficulties with Difficulties, drop me a PM - I suspect most folks here are about as eager to revisit this argument for the umpteenth time as I am, which is to say, I'd rather chew my keyboard off.

                                                What happens to artifacts stored in Elsewhere if the player or NPC dies?

                                                Example: A player (or NPC) has their weapon or armour banished via Hauberk-Summoning Gesture [H-SG] (pg. 377), or Summoning the Loyal Steel [StLS] (pg. 347) then are killed.

                                                Do the artifacts pop out of Elsewhere or are they gone forever?
                                                I can see either making sense with StLS's simple action, indefinite duration, and mote commitment requirement allowing the item to pop out after commitment ends; H-SG items being lost since it does not requirement (though probably should, oversight?) and is both instant and reflexive. I'm leaning towards both 'popping out', but having items lost would certainly add an interesting layer of fear and politics to the mix. (Finding First Age documents on who had to die because they had some reviled or desired artifact stored Elsewhere)
