                        Choice # 4 aka “Bring it all the way to the top” it is!

                        You stop and think for a moment, pondering your choices and the possible outcomes of each. Sure, you could technically bring this info to any of the authority-figures in the city, and something would get done as a result. But ….. what would that do for you? In all likelihood, the info would just get passed on, levels of security would increase, and the plot would progress without any more involvement on your part. You would likely be “safe” from any repercussions, so long as you stay quiet about your part.

                        On the other hand, doing so would completely eliminate any possible rewards or renown for foiling the plot, especially from a lord grateful for a spoiled assassination/coup attempt. On the other-other hand, even if an assassination or coup was foiled as a result of your actions, you could be viewed as an accomplice, or even just as a scapegoat for blame. Those kinds of things tend to end badly, even if you avoided outright execution or assassination. Your character in the region would be shot as long as you lived.

                        You sigh. What the hell have you stumbled into?

                        Glowering, you stare at the wall for quite some time, as the doctor works on Saiko and Blue Pines looks curiously at the various oddities lining the walls and shelves. All three of which start in surprise as you suddenly rise from your chair and make to put on your cloak.

                        “Where are you going, Boss?” asks Blue Pines

                        “I am going to bring this straight to Lord Iysen, as it almost overwhelmingly likely concerns her safety. It seems like the method most suited to actually seeing results, and Lord Iysen is known to be fair. I am not that concerned about repercussions, but if you don’t hear from me in three days, flee the city. Go somewhere, anywhere, and lay low for a while. The money I’ve earned should last you quite some time. With that in mind, I am going to ask you to repeat your story again, so I can be sure of the details.”

                        Perturbed, Blue Pines repeats her tale of following the mysterious Oathsworn from the docks to the castle, the passing-off of another package, and as many details as she can remember. The cut of the cloak the Oathsworn was wearing, how they walked, the odors of the cologne they were wearing (Lavender and some other flower), etc. Once you are certain of the details, you turn to leave without another word, before your followers can see the anxiousness on your face.

                        No need to give them another thing to worry about.

                        Mounting Xuansu, you make your way through the town to Teriake Castle, passing through the market so you can examine your person by the light of the lanterns. Being presentable would make it more likely you got an audience at all, much less being believed or even humored.

                        Overall, your clothing is decent, if commonly-cut and a little dust-stained at the cuffs. You brush this off and run your fingers through your hair, trying to make some semblance of order in the mass of grey, tan, white and black strands. Eventually, you decide on your usual style (in a tail on the back of your head) and run your licked-palm over any flyaway strands in effort to make them lie down. It suddenly occurs to you that you are nervous. Very, very nervous. Asides from the obvious (that you are carrying proof of a plot against Lord Iysen on your person), this would be the first time you meet the equivalent of royalty. Back on your father’s estate, you mainly interacted with your father’s peers, themselves as equally poor and rural as your family, little more than up-jumped and lettered peasants compared to the likes of Lord Iysen.

                        What if she doesn’t want to see you at all? What if she takes offense at your impertinence of assuming you could have an audience? The doubts gnaw a hole in your heart, and they make the trip to the castle-gates seemingly take no time at all.

                        Before you know it, the walls and gates loom over you, illuminated by the lanterns of patrolling guards, who look down on you with interest. After a moment, a small panel in the day-door of the gate slides open, revealing the eyes of one of the guards within.

                        “Can I help you?”

                        Dismounting from Xuansu and drawing yourself up to your full height, you walk over to the port and whisper at the guard.

                        “I have on my person something that would be of immense interest to the Lord Xia. It is of great importance that I present it to her, and to her alone.”

                        The mans eyebrow rises, “Interesting, but I can’t just let you in. I don’t have the key, first of all, and this sounds like it’ll be above my pay grade. I’m sorry, but I’m gonna have to go get my captain. He’ll be able to help you once you talk to him”.

                        You frown, but it won’t accomplish very much to argue. “….very well.”

                        With that, the panel slams shut, and you are left in the dark. You become intimately aware that there is no cover from here to the town, well-within range for a crossbow, and you firmly grasp the handle of your sword to prevent your hands from trembling. Maybe this was a mistake.

                        After a few minutes, the lock on the day-door rattles, and the foot-thick door swings open to reveal a small chamber, illuminated by a paper lantern and filled with armed men. The majority of them are guards, wearing helmets and armed with spears, but in front is the Captain of the Castle Guard.

                        He can’t as well be anything else. Dressed in mail and armed with a sword, the man stands with the figure of one used to giving orders, and having those orders followed without question. He is well-groomed, with a neatly-trimmed goatee and a top-knot. However, by far the most prominent aspect of his character is the aroma lingering about his person, evident even a couple of feet away. His cologne. The man stinks of lavender and roses.

                        …. Shit. This could be awkward.

                        The Captain taps his foot impatiently, and you realize you were just staring at the man. He clears his throat and states,

                        “I am of the understanding that you are in possession of ‘sensitive information” concerning the Lord Xia?”

                        “… Yes. But it is for he..”

                        “ I am the Captain of her Guard, and partly responsible for her safety and person.” He holds out his hand. “ I can, and will, be taking whatever information you have. I will present it to her Lordship when it is expedient. If you please, pass whatever you have over to me. Nobody else will see it, I assure you”. His tone makes it certain that he isn’t asking.

                        “… No. I mean no offense, but it is imperative I show it to her”.

                        The man sneers. “You think a ruffian such as yourself is worthy of speaking to the great Lord Xia in person?!. “ His hand falls down to the sword at his side. “Men, arrest him, at once!. I’ll see what you have on you, and you can spend a ‘pleasant” couple of evenings in the cells, until I figure out what best to do with you!”.

                        The guards surge forwards and you leap back, hand falling to your sword instinctively. The men circle around you, spears at the ready, and your heart jumps into your throat. This was the worst possible outcome, what were you thinking? The captain strides forwards, smirking fully now, and takes hold of your sword-arm, jerking you forwards towards the doorway

                        “What is all this shouting? What is going on?

                        Almost instantly, the guards snap to attention, spears to their sides and bent at the waist in a bow. Even the Captain releases his grip on your wrist and bends his head in respect. From inside the doorway, descending the stairs from the top of the wall-walk, strides a woman, middle-aged and dressed in plain, but finely-cut, robes, and at her heels closely follows a warrior in full armor, hand on sword and partially-drawn.

                        The Lord of Xia and head of the Shiba family Iysen Shiba, and her personal bodyguard. You are either saved or doomed.

                        The Captain clears his throat, clearly surprised at her sudden interference. “My Lord, forgive me. I was not expecting to meet you so late at night.”

                        She raises an eyebrow. “I was not aware that I was prohibited from walking along my own walls in my own home after dinner, Captain. Besides, that does not answer my question.”

                        He stumbles, “Forgive me, Lord. I was just apprehending this scoundrel, who has some nonsensical claim that he had information for you. Please forgive this disturbance.”

                        The other eyebrow raises, “For all you know, Captain Ito, he just might. Either way, manhandling him is not the way to get the information.” She turns to the guards and directs them to stand down.

                        The bodyguard rumbles from within his helm, “Lord, may I suggest we continue this exchange from within the safety of your own walls, as opposed to out here in the open?”

                        She shrugs, “Quite. It would be warmer, anyways.” With that, she turns and walks back through the door. The Captain stands and glares at you with a look that would have set tinder to smoldering, and then follows the Lord. Flanked (more like guided, shoulder-to-shoulder) by two of the guards, you bring up the rear. After Xuansu is brought in, the door is shut and locked with a boom, sealing you inside the castle.
                        You are escorted to a small chamber, within one of the watchtowers along the outer wall of the castle. Inside, a brazier with some coals, a candle and a table and chairs are the sole furnishings. Lord Iysen sits on one side of the table, chin resting on a palm, and the Captain and her bodyguard flank both sides of the door.
                        There is no possibility of escape.


                        Once inside the room and free of your armed escort, you quickly follow protocol, falling to your knees and pressing your forehead to the floor in the most formal of formal bows. There, you wait until she indicates you to rise (with a slightly amused inflection to her command), whereupon you turn to the side and remove your sword from your sash, offering the sheathed blade to the imposing (seriously, the crest on his helmet almost scrapes the ceiling) bodyguard, who takes it without a word.

                        Lord Iysen gestures for you to sit, then continues, “Now, I understand you have something for me?”

                        You hesitate for a moment, “Yes, great Lord. I have on me certain evidence that suggests there is a plot in motion, either against you personally or against Xia”. Contrary to your nervously-beating heart, your voice is strong and without stutter. Good.

                        She raises an eyebrow and sits up in her chair. “ ….. Interesting. If I may ask, could I see this proof, Oathsworn….?”

                        “ Kasai. Kasai Harutane. Son of Mino Harutane, himself of the domain of Tsen, sworn to Que.”

                        She stops for a moment, “Kasai. I know that name.” Frowning, then “Did you beat up a priest yesterday, in the market? My Captain of the Militia sent me up a report concerning that very topic, and I remember the name ‘Kasai” clearly”

                        You wince. “ ….. Yes, Lord, I was the one mentioned in the report. I did not actually assault the priest, however. More of an accostation.”

                        She smirks, resting her chin on both palms. “And, if I may ask, what caused you to accost this priest?

                        “I took offense with how he administered “instruction” to a puppeteer, who was performing in the marketplace. He beat her, badly, breaking several bones and destroying her stand. All for the minor infraction of idolatry. If I did not intervene when I did, the woman could have been killed. As of now, she now serves as one of my armsmen.”

                        She sits back, raising an eyebrow again (this must be a personal affectation), “So, you hold the Oath of Compassion in high regard. Admirable, and rare.”

                        The Captain interjects, “My Lord, I really must protest. We are here to determine if the intelligence this man carries is noteworthy, not to conduct a performance review!”

                        She frowns, “Quite.” Turning back to you, “If I may, Kasai Harutane, might we see this information?”

                        You nod, “Great Lord, this information is not only physical, but also recollective, in the sense that events were witnessed, both by myself and by my retainers.”

                        She tilts her head, “Explain, please.”

                        You take a deep breath.” Several weeks ago, my current armsmen, the one whom I rescued from the turbulent priest, was working down by the riverside docks as a longshoreman. Late at night, at the end of one of her shifts, she took notice of a group of individuals down at an unused section of the wharf. She proceeded down to the group, to admonish their presence, only to hide when she saw what was occurring.”

                        “She told me that she saw several men, dressed for stealth and to prevent their faces from being witnessed, accept a package from another individual. This individual was well-dressed, bearing clothing of a cut not common to dockworkers or wealthy townsfolk. Upon receiving this package, the group headed downriver in a rowboat, with no lantern, and the individual proceeded to make their way back up to this very castle. My armsman tells me that she saw that the person was armed, with the straight sword indicative of an Oathsworn, much like myself. The individual also entered the castle not by the main gate, but by a sally-port on one of the towers, indicating they had access to a door-key.”

                        Lord Iysen purses her lips, and then raises her gaze to her Captain, who shifts slightly under her gaze. “How many keys are there to the castle-gates, Captain Ito?” It wasn’t really a question.

                        “Three, my Lord,” comes the instant reply. One on my person, one on yours, and a third in the vault. My key never leaves my side, Lord. I sleep and bathe with it around my neck!”

                        Her lips deepen into a frown. “We shall have to see if that last key is in the vault, then.” Turning back to you, “Please continue”.

                        “Then, just today, my retainers and I were eating dinner, in a restaurant right at the foot of the mount this castle rests on, when my page spotted someone climbing down the cliff-face. Following them, my page and I came across a group of bandits hurriedly burying the climber in a shallow grave, while making ready to flee the area. After engaging the scoundrels, I found one of the bandits attempting to throw a package in the river. They were unsuccessful, and I picked up the package, as it was obviously important.”

                        “Are the bodies still at the scene?”

                        You shrug. “They should be. This only happened right before dusk, right at sunset. A couple of hours ago, at most.”

                        Lord Iysen stands, “Captain Ito, take two of your men, whoever can be trusted to remain quiet about this, and go down and investigate this site. Where did you say it was?”

                        “Right at the base of the cliff, on the river side, in a small copse of trees, Lord”

                        With that, she nods to Captain Ito, who bows and leaves the room. Lord Iysen remains standing, now pacing the small chamber, hands clasped behind her back.

                        “Now, I understand there was actual physical evidence as well?”

                        “Yes, Lord.”, you say, reaching within your sash to pull out the letter. “But…. If I may, and I do not want to imply anything, nor accuse anyone, but I left out a key detail of one of the stories.”

                        She stops pacing, turns to look at you, “Since the good Captain is no longer present, I am assuming it has to do with him?”

                        “…… Possibly. When my armsman was stalking the well-heeled stranger from the docks, she took careful note of a very particular identifier of the man.”

                        She stares at you levelly. “Go on”

                        At this, you sigh internally, all-but consigning yourself to a possible death
                        .
                        “She told me that the man passed quite close to her, close enough where his cologne was cloying. That said cologne reeked of lavender, and some other flower. But that the smell of lavender was particularly strong.”

                        Lord Iysen sits down, face in hands, slowly massaging her temples.

                        “Are you implying that the Captain of my castle guard is a part of this plot, Kasai Harutane?”

                        You are treading on very shaky ground, here. “Like I stated earlier, Lord, I do not want to accuse anyone, not with the scant proof I have to offer. All I know is that the essence of lavender is expensive, out-of-reach for all but the wealthiest of merchants and nobility. Most of the Oathsworn here for the tournament are as poor as I am, and I am far from being able to afford cologne in general, much less essential essence of anything. And, when I first , ah, met your Captain Ito, the first thing I noticed about him was the scent of lavender and rosewater, itself rather expensive.”

                        Lord Iysen’s bodyguard chimes in, “Captain Ito has been wearing heavy cologne as of late, my Lord, as well as fine clothing and other frivolities. This only started a few scant months ago.”

                        She sighs, nodding her head. “I have noticed as well, Nori.” Standing, “The only remaining option is to inspect my vault, to see if the one other key is there. If it is, then Captain Ito is all-but guilty of treason, or, at least, lending out the key to my fortress. Basically the same thing.”

                        “How so, Lord?”

                        She smiles grimly,” Because I have the only key to the vault, Kasai Harutane. I am the only one with access. “She makes to leave. “ Come; let us get this over with, before the good Captain returns.”

                        With that, the three of you make your way deep into the bowels of the fortress, passing through hallways and down stairwells, until the rock walls of the cliff press tight overhead. Ahead of you, at the end of a long passageway, sits a rather depressingly-nondescript door. Striding forward, Lord Iysen pulls a small bronze key from underneath her robes and inserts it into the keyhole. From the other side of the door comes the rattle and clank of many chains, and it is a few moments before the door is able to be opened.

                        Inside, as Lord Iysen’s bodyguard raises the lantern, your jaw drops as you take in the sheer level of riches on display. Bars of silver, iron and copper, bolts of wool and silk (silk!), charters for damming rights, logging rights, guild-charters. Racks of spearheads, helmets on stands, enough to equip an army (which is probably its purpose). The wealth is boggling, and you stare, mouth open, until Lord Iysen reaches up and closes it with a klik.

                        “Do try not to stare, Kasai. It is making me self-conscious.” She quips, amusedly.

                        You shake your head embarrassedly. Is this how Saiko felt? You feel like such a yokel….

                        Lord Iysen and her bodyguard are already at the far end of the room. Walking up to them, you take note of what they are looking at; a bronze plaque, adorned with hooks. On these hooks hang sets of keys, in iron and bronze. All of the hooks are full, and the keys are heavy with dust.

                        “…. What does this mean, Lord Iysen?”

                        She sighs. “It means I have been betrayed, Kasai Harutane.” Turning to her bodyguard, “Nori?”

                        The man bows, hand on his sword. “Yes, my Lord. Shall I bar the gate, or allow him inside?”

                        She ponders this for a moment.” Allow him inside. If we can capture him, he will tell us what he knows.”

                        “Understood.”

                        With this, your group leaves the vault, locking the door behind you, and head back up to the tower, where Lord Iysen reclines back in her chair, staring up at the ceiling. Nori buttonholes several of the guards, who hide in various places around the tower, ready to rush in at a moment’s call.

                        After a few silently-awkward moments of waiting, Captain Ito returns, striding in through the door and bowing at the waist.

                        “It is as he says, my Lord. The bodies, the grave, all are present.”

                        Lord Isyen remains silent, still staring at the ceiling.

                        “.M..My Lord?”

                        “… Your gate-key, Captain Ito. I wish to see it.”

                        The Captain blanches. “My gate-key, Lord?”

                        She sits up, staring directly at the Captain. “ Yes. The gate-key.”

                        He bites his lip, face pale. “Are… are you accusing me of losing the key!?”

                        “…..Yes. Are you confessing?”

                        The Captain wheels about, glaring at you. “ You! You accuse me of wrong-doing, you peasant!. How dare you! You have no proof! Lord, you must believe me, I did not do anything!”

                        Her eyes are as hard as stone. “Your key, Ito Aori.”

                        His gaze jumps from you, to Lord Iysen, to the massive man now standing behind him, hand on sword. With a curse, he jumps up and makes for the door, only to get clotheslined by the steel bracer of Nori. He drops to the floor, choking, and Nori gives him a solid kick to the ribs to ensure he stays down.

                        Lord Iysen stands. “Strip him, Nori.”

                        Reaching down, Nori yanks off the former captain’s armor and clothing, popping the buttons on his mail and tearing cloth. In a minute, Ito Aori lies naked before you. There is no key around his neck.

                        Exhaling slowly, Lord Iysen rubs at the bridge of her nose. With a quick command, she orders the former captain brought to a cell, and for Nori to personally ransack his former quarters to see if the gate-key can actually be found. Standing, she gestures for you to follow her.

                        “If I am going to be doing anything else tonight, it is going to be with a drink in hand, in some place more comfortable.”

                        And so you head up into the central keep, followed at a respectful distance by two guards. Higher and higher you go, up to the upper levels, until you walk through a door into the Lords personal Solar. Thick carpets cover the floor, wall-hangings and curtains adorn the walls, and there are comfortable tables and chairs throughout. After a servant kindles a fire in the fireplace and brings a lantern and a carafe of hot coffee, the two of you are left alone.

                        Pulling out the letter, the two of you bend over it, and almost instantly Lord Iysen bursts out,

                        “What is this?!”

                        “Forest-tongue, Lord. The glyphic language of the Hill-tribes.”

                        She frowns, glaring at the mass of branching glyphs. “You can read this?”

                        “Yes, Lord. My grandfather taught me to both read and speak it. I must tell, you, however….

                        She waves her hand. “Kasai, I know this is a breach of proper protocol, but you are already inside my private quarters, drinking my coffee and telling me about a conspiracy. You don’t have to be so stiff, at the least.”

                        You stiffen, surprised, then laugh. “If that is what you want, Lord.”

                        She shrugs,” If you say everything so formally, something might get lost in the doublespeak. For now, at least, you may speak plainly.”

                        “….sure.”

                        “So, you were saying this is, what, Forest-tongue?”

                        “Yes. It isn’t really used as a “reading” alphabet, not really. More of a symbolic and ritualistic one. Each character can be read vertically and diagonally, and word-breaks and punctuation is indicated by specific “branches”. You start at the middle-bottom and work upwards, like a tree…..”

                        You work your way through the document, writing out what each “sentence” means, only to be interrupted by Nori, who informs the Lord Iysen that there was no sign of the gate-key in any of the former captain’s quarters, nor the barracks. Lord Iysen acknowledges this with a nod, and Nori takes a seat by the door.

                        You finish translating the Forest-tongue into Riverspeak, and it makes just as much sense as it did before (read: none). Lord Iysen stares at the words for a few minutes, then asks,

                        “Kasai, have you ever heard of a cipher?”

                        “…. A what?” You never heard of that, living out in the hinterland.

                        “A cipher. It is a means to disguise letters as something else, to hide the meaning of a document. I use one all the time for personal correspondence, and I am willing to bet this is in a cipher as well.” She stands and walks over to a desk. Pulling out another key, she unlocks a drawer and takes out a small brass disc. On one “side” of the disk are the 36 characters of Riverspeak, and on the other is a matching disk. Both sides are able to spin, letting each alphabet match up with other letters.

                        “This is called a substitution cipher. It basically replaces a letter of Riverspeak with another. It is probably the most common cipher in existence, and I am almost certain the particular cipher will be on here.”

                        “Why?”

                        “Not many people actually speak Forest-tongue, as you said. Therefore, all the ‘difficulty’ of disguising the contents was done by translating to Forest-tongue. A complicated cipher would be superfluous.”

                        She sits down, and with a pad of paper between you, you start copying down each possible substitution of Riverspeak that is possible using the disk. Even with the disk, each substitution still takes some time to do. The lantern has burned down and the coffee is long-cold before Lord Iysen sits up, startling you from your doze, and blurts out,

                        “I think I’ve got it.”

                        You bend over the paper, and even with your fatigue-blurred eyes, the message is still clearly visible in Lord Iysen’s neat hand.

                        S.
                        The preparations are all in place. The footpads all know their places, the armaments are secured, and I have even got help from several greedy persons inside the castle. It is amazing what a little silver passed around can do! We only wait for day of the wedding, after the tournament, and everything our ancestors have yearned for will come to pass! Be patient, I play my part well.
                        Yours, H.

                        Lord Iysen sits quiet for some time, absorbing the meaning of the letter.

                        “So, Lord, what does this mean?”

                        “I am …. Not sure. It suggests either an assassination, or a coup-attempt, but the letter itself doesn’t really imply either…….”

                        She looks up from the letter, out the windows and across the Red River. The eastern horizon is stained red with the coming of the sun, and birdsong is starting to filter its way up to where you are sitting.

                        “Either way, I have three days to figure out what it does mean.” She frowns, shakes her head. “Wonderful, just what I needed, on top of planning a wedding and officiating at a tournament.”

                        You cough delicately, “I was actually planning on competing in the tournament, Lord…… If you want, I can ask around if anyone has seen anything suspicious…”

                        She shakes her head, “No, that would draw too much attention. I don’t want to spring a trap too early and scare off the conspirators.”

                        She turns and looks out the window again. “Either way, I need rest. The day was already going to be busy, and now it will be even worse.” Walking over to Nori (who is still wide awake. Is the man even human?) and whispering something in his ear, she turns to leave, but not before looking to you,

                        “Kasai Harutane, we thank you for your assistance in this matter. Without your forewarning, we would have no idea about this plot concerning our possible safely. We owe you our thanks, and in return, offer you a place to rest for the night”. With this, she nods her head (about as much of a bow as you are probably ever going to get) and closes the door softly.

                        Groggily following the bodyguard, you are led to a small chamber on the lower levels of the keep. Inside, there is little more than a futon, some blankets, and a rack holding your sword, all of which you quickly take in before falling to the futon, asleep in moments with your clothing still on.

                        It only feels like a few moments have passed before you are woken up by a soft knock on the door. Outside, a serving-girl offers you a bowl of heated water and some fresh clothing, both of which are welcome. After you perform your morning ablutions and put on the fresh clothing, the girl informs you that the Lord Xia is waiting for you up in her personal dining room.
                        Confused (and still quite fatigued, which almost certainly is contributing to the confusion), you allow yourself to be led up the stairs again; back to the room you were in a few scant hours ago. Inside, Lord Iysen sits at a long trestle table, set up for breakfast.

                        Seeing you standing awkwardly, not sure of what to do, she calls out, “Sit. I kept you here all night, I might as well give you something to eat. The tournament can’t start until I get there anyways, so might as well do something useful with the time.”, and you gratefully sink down at one of the corners. A servant offers you a cup of coffee, which you accept happily (you might be developing a taste for the stuff. Not good, as it is expensive, imported from the Southeast.). Only a moment later, the door opens again and a group of people move through, talking far too animatedly for your taste this early in the morning.

                        Lord Iysen embraces each as they come in, and introduces you.

                        “Children, this is Kasai Harutane, an Oathsworn who has been of immense assistance to me of an important matter, yet in development. He will be joining us for breakfast today.”

                        She gestures at the oldest, a girl about your age.
                        “Kasai, this is Mino, my eldest daughter and heir. She is betrothed to Haichi Matsu, son of the Lord Yue.”

                        The middle, a girl of about Saiko’s age.
                        “My middle child, Airi.”

                        And the last, a young boy around 10.
                        “And, last but certainly not least, my son Aketchi, who has just started arms training”.

                        The boy bounces closer, eyes excited, “ So, Mr. Kasai, why is your hair so weird?”

                        The sudden silence is only broken by the sound of Airi dope-slapping her forehead, and Lord Iysen sighs.

                        “It is way too early in the morning for you to be so forward, Aketchi. Please apologize to the man.”

                        You hold up your hand in protest, “In all honesty, Lord, his questions are far more innocent than most.” Turning to the lad, “My hair and eyes look like this, Master Aketchi, because I was born amongst the Hill-tribes, in the mountains to the north. We all have some form of indication of our heritage as Wyld-touched.”

                        Lord Iysen sips her coffee. “You said that you were taught Forest-tongue by your grandfather, yet you are an Oathsworn warrior. Were you raised along the Tribes?

                        Nodding, “Partially. After my mother died, I lived with my father for some time. After a few years, my grandfather told me it was time for me to experience the other half of my heritage, and fostered me at Dun Paunuxet for several years. In addition to Forest-tongue, he also taught me archery and woods-craft.”

                        “Dun Paunuxet? So you are a member of the ….. ah….. Nemasket tribe?”

                        You are surprised. Not too many Lowlanders know about the different tribal groups and Confederations.

                        “Yes. He was the Chief back then.”

                        Eyebrow raised, Lord Isyen sets down her coffee. “… You are telling me that you are the relative of a tribal Chief?”
                        “No. He is no longer the Chief of the Nemasket. He hasn’t been so for many years.”

                        Confused, “What exactly do you mean?”

                        Ruing revealing this much information already, “A Chief can only be removed from station through death, a vote of no-confidence by the tribal council, or through ascension to another post.”
                        “So, he is dead then? I am sorry”.

                        Slowly, anticipating the upcoming reaction and not looking forwards to it. “ No.”

                        Her eyes narrow. “ No?. Out with it, man.”

                        Taking a deep breath, “My grandfather is the Warchief of the Dumoniapoccasset Confederation, Lord.”

                        Lord Iysen sets her hands down on the table, silent for some time, as her children look confusedly at her, then at you.

                        “Mother, what does that mean?

                        She takes a deep breath, “It means we are basically breaking bread with the equivalent of Hill-tribe royalty.”

                        Surprised at the revelation you,
                        1. Drive home your relation to your Grandfather. Damn straight you are important!
                        2. Downplay said relation. Humility is a virtue.
                        3. Describe in detail how Hill-Tribe society works.
                        4. Something else?
                        OOC: (you actually should probably read this)
                        Firstly, I would like to describe some of the funny-looking words I am throwing around.

                        I live in New England, and a couple hundred years ago, the place was populated with Native tribal groups. The one native to where I live was called the Wampanoag, a subset ( the reality is far more complicated than this, but bear with me) of the larger Algonquin cultural group, which in turn spread across most of the Northeast US and eastern Canada.

                        I tend to use examples of Native American culture in tribal groups across my version of Creation, and Wampanoag/Algonquian is especially prevalent in the East and Northeast. I find it adds a bit of flair to my otherwise boring whitebread northern European cultures that I usually play with. For example, in Wampanoag, the suffix “–et” usually indicates a place-name, and you will see it in many town, river and landform names around southern New England (Nemasket, Poccasset, Mattapoisett, Acushnet, etc) My home state of Massachusetts comes from the Massachuset tribe, which means “people of the great hills”, referencing the Blue Hills (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Hills_Reservation) in the Boston area

                        One of the most common aspects of Native American life is use is agricultural techniques and crops. I am a big fan of the Colombian Exchange, and therefore, much of Creation is going to be growing a mix of Old World and New World crops. Most commonly, I am going to have most farmers grow the “Three Sisters”, an interplanting of corn, beans and pumpkins (itself arguably the most effective pre-Industrial form of agriculture, space and efficiency-wise), along with potatoes and sunflowers.

                        Also, most groups are going to have some cultural aspect of Algonquin origin thrown in. Most of the time, this is going to be governmental, with tribal/familial “elders” voting on matters of state, and the chief/king being a diplomat and warleader (much like the modern US President) as opposed to decision-maker. I am doing this in my up-and-coming Byrtenwalda campaign, which meshes well with the real-world Germanic “assembly” system.

                        Secondly, Lord Iysen is seriously misrepresenting the relationship between Kasai and his grandfather here. The Tribes don’t exactly practice inheritance of position like the Lowlanders, and Kasai can’t really get much of anything "concrete" from his grandfather, asides from being the relative of the most powerful Tribal warleader in the northwestern Hundred Kingdoms.

                        Kasai’s grandfather got his position by getting elected by the Chiefs of the 12 tribes of the Dumoniapoccasset Confederation, and can only get removed from the position by dying, failing a vote of confidence, or being challenged for the role and losing (not likely). When he dies (or is removed), Kasai will not “inherit” the position, it isn’t like a Lordship of a domain.

                        On the other hand, Kasai is the much-beloved grandson of the figurative equivalent of Metacomet and Verceingetorix. While Kasai’s grandfather isn’t actually capable of making decisions-of-state on his own (he is merely the “face” of the Confederation, representing it in peace and war. The Elder council of the 12 Tribes makes the actual decisions) , he is still insanely influential, with a large number of warriors in his warband, and his word carries heavy weight. If Kasai could persuade him to act in a certain way, a decent number of Hill-Tribes would follow suit, or at least be amenable.

                        On the other hand, the Dumoniapoccasset Confederation is basically at war with most of the domains in the Hundred Kingdoms. There isn’t official war, but warbands of young, glory-hungry warriors regularly raid trade caravans, outlying farms and suchlike. For more information, look up “Mourning Wars” to get an idea.

                        Make of this what you will.
