                    I do like the idea of cutting out the pause after a successful attack where you check extra successes, do a little math, and then roll damage. However, if we're really blue skying with ideas to improve the flow of combat resolution, my best-case would be for attack and damage to be "values," whilst defense and soak get rolled.

                    After all, how many players know what it means to face an Ox-Dragon Smash attack with 19 dice and 12 damage (overwhelming 1)... made by someone in Black Craw Form, and enhanced with Divine Predator Strike, Reed-Breaking Banda, Finding The Needle's Eye, Foe-Driving Attack, and Unerring Fang Technique? I sure wouldn't without looking it up! And most players' intuition will probably mislead them: if they see an expensive attack with fairly low damage and decide to let soak handle it, assorted post-soak bonuses still give the attack a real shot at 4-5 damage... and on an attack with one debuff for 5+ damage and a different one for crashing the target, that could be a big mistake. Alternately, if they do try to boost their defense, "19 dice" doesn't really cover the +1 autosuccess, re-rolling the first three 1s, and getting an extra dice for each 10. Once you've done the math for that, you should also note that you'll be losing 1 defense to a magical onslaught penalty, another to a generic penalty if you're too close to your friends, and in all likelihood, taking away 1 defense from your medium weapon by "ignoring" it, which penalty-negating charms cannot prevent. And don't forget those re-rolled 1s if you (like many Solars) are relying upon low enemy dice to boost your defense after the roll.

                    Even for a game as complex as Exalted (and, admittedly, a bit of an extreme example*), that's clearly unreasonable. But it doesn't have to be! You could have exactly the same amount of tactical depth by baking all of the penalties and dice tricks into a static attack value, moving the randomness over to rolls by the defender, and only expecting people to remember all of their own charms, rather than every charm ever printed.

                    I don't expect that to happen, since most of what little we know about Essence's mechanics is to emphasize how many things it'll keep from the game's past editions, but if we're dreaming big and they're finally willing to cut into that massive 25-stat array of abilities, then why stop at slightly speeding up the damage roll?


                    * Not, to be clear, the MOST extreme example which could come up in play. This is a combo with a mostly-logical game plan, involving essence 1-2 charms in two attributes, and low-level investment in two reasonably-synergistic MAs.

                            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                            I’m a bit confused about what you’re saying Kejak. Do you mean to remove all offensive charms from the game as well?
                            Yeah, that explanation needs work.

                            I'm not talking about removing offensive charms from the game. I just want them to work more like the charms which boost defense and soak (that is, by raising an unrolled number), and have the dice rolling (and thereby, any dice tricks) be on defensive Charms. As it stands, a given number of dice on an attack or damage roll can mean very different things at different times. Since most people know their own charms better than anyone else's, I feel like the tricksy probability stuff should only expect you to know your own charms.

                            To backtrack a little from my overly-complex example, here are a few one-charm situations I have seen in play:
                            1. Being attacked by a Sidereal with a target number of 4-5, and mistakenly thinking that, say, seven defense was a sure thing against a ten-die decisive attack.
                            2. A player who often uses Hail-Shattering Practice or Rumor of Form takes a nasty hit because they didn't understand that this attack re-rolled 1s.
                            3. Incarnated Fury Attack is unblockable and undodgeable - most serious celestial combatants will have some way to defend against it, but that costs extra motes (and you're already crashed if you take an IFA), so they may be tempted to tough out what looks like "only" a withering attack with a light weapon. Since it gives double 7s on damage, this can net a lot more initiative than a player expected when they saved motes by taking the hit.

                            If those examples said things like "attack value 8" instead of "10 dice, target number 4," there is less information for players to miss, and no need for people to stop the game and calculate unconventional dice probabilities. In fact, many Exalted players tell me that they don't think in terms of probability at all, and instead follow intuition gained from play. If enemy dice mean different things enemy-by-enemy and action-by-action, that intuition seems very misleading to me, but if it's only dependent on your own abilities, that becomes a much better plan

                                        Right.

                                        The way the system works right now, the attacker decides how many resources to put into an attack, then the defender decides how to respond. Usually, this will take one of two forms: either they will make a gamble in which they try to spend as few motes as possible while still preventing the attack, or they will decide they cannot reasonably accomplish that (and can live with the consequences) and spend nothing.

                                        In other words, the system is built around the attacker making their choices blind, then the defender making an informed choice in reply. My concept keeps that dynamic, but tries to make sure the defender actually has that information.

                                            I think we might be having a bit of a clash of playstyles here.

                                            One big one is that I as an ST and the other guy who STs Exalted in my group fairly rarely use complex antagonists with dice tricks in general. It's not that we never use them, but generally dice tricks aren't super heavy in any quick character antagonists, and in the past 5 campaigns there's only been 3 fully statted up NPCs that have been built from the books.

                                            The other thing is barring some odd circumstances we're open about what attacks do what. Like if I'm attacking with a full excellency Deathknight with Savage Shade Style, I'll tell them that a 22 die decisive attack with double 10s on damage is coming their way. Part of that is because I consider it fair, since I know all the charms they're coming at my NPCs with, so nobody gets special treatment in that aspect of the game. The other part is that without that knowledge you could get sucker-punched SO hard. "Yeah this rowdy rough looking dude at the bar is throwing a punch, what do you do? Not spend any essence on defense? Damn shame, he was a Brawl supernal Dawn and he knocked you into that mountain with a 20 mote Heaven Thunder Hammer attack."

                                            It's even worse in social interaction. If somebody is using a Read Intentions on you and you have zero idea about the nature of the read, or even who's doing it, you have no clue what's appropriate to spend on defense.

                                            It can sometimes sort of spoil a mystery about the identity of a person, but not too often.
