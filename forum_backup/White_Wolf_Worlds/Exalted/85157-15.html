                            Oh! I thing I thought of at one point, but never remembered to bring up:

                            So, the Seven are supposed to have comprehensive mastery over time and space, and Zoss is supposed to outdo them even in that regard.

                            It's not yet been clearly established what that might mean, although it would probably at least include comic-book superpower style local time manipulation.

                            But, given the nature of this story and setting, the metaphysics and cosmology, the capacity for it to be something more elaborate and esoteric...

                            I'm wondering if at least one application is the fact that Zoss can till be around in a certain capacity even after he was apparently killed. I don't think it strictly involves time travel (and the predestination paradox headaches that would entail, considering the circumstances), perhaps more that they don't quite need to pass through time linearly. Or at least that their linear time is on a certain different plane to that of those who don't have such power.

                            So it might be that from the perspective of Zoss as he gave Allison her name, his own decapitation might be somewhere in his own future, inevitable but still far off.

                            Although, given the term "complete mastery of space-time", what if a demiurge (wielding a Key of Kings?) is able to stretch out the space between the current moment and the one of their death to an almost indefinite degree? The idea that somewhere in the multiverse, the moment of your death exists, and you'll catch up to it eventually, but it doesn't really impact your capacity to still be around long after and far away from it, at your own discretion.

                            Although power over a Magus Gate might make it easier to make such a death stick. At the very least, I'd say their monopoly on that power is why the Seven came out on top, and why Allison is going to need it to kill them.

                            I'm probably overthinking this a bit, but if nothing else I think some of the ideas are interesting. I think the most straightforward details for the moment are "mastery of space-time means you don't necessarily die when you're killed".
