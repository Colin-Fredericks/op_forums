        What everybody is saying.

        In occult mistery/horror games, but otherwise set in our modern world, it becomes useful to have another worlds, so you can have weird magical adventures you couldn´t have in Earth, with weird races and monsters and strange climates and all the things that are more common in a fantasy world, but if you put them on Earth, you would break the setting, in the sense that "Eh... If there are dragons, and floating crystal palaces, how the hell is the masquerade still going, and the world even slightly similar to our own?". So, these more... visual things are relegated to other worlds. The Umbra/Supernal Realms/Arcadia/The Dreaming of World of Darkness/Chronicle of Darkness, the Kelipahs of Silent Legions, the Otherzones of Unknown Armies (although with that game focus on humanity, they have been largely ignored. Third Edition is the first, I think, in which I have seen focus on an Otherzone that wasn´t a Room of Renunciation).

        In a strange, big, fantastical world like Creation? Some other worlds are useful (Malfeas, Yu-shan, the Underworld, mostly so kinds of spirits can live there, and you can go there nd have spirit adventures!), but I have had in my games shadowlands along a road where you entered into the Deads´ Realm by sleeping in the abandoned inn while travelling South, I have had adventures on Malfeas, uncontrolled demesnes which corrupted the waters and made plants grow in weird forms and transformed the animals who ate them, a forest clearing with a magical eternal tree under which, anything buried, would never rot and eternally preserve (and a player buried there the woman his character loved, because she was a crazy sorcerer obssessed with bringing her back to life. The player knew that was impossible, but wanted to play the story of someone becoming obssessed with an impossible thing and pacting with Deathlords and weird things from the Underworld. He left the game for college, but was an awesome player), a walking castle who "lives" in the middle of a ruined city but sometimes wander across and steals walls and cattle and kidnaps people, and nobody really knows why, a tower that was exactly the same overground and underground, being the underground part a Underworld reflection which could lead you to the Sea of Shadows, but you could only leave the Tower if someone outside opened the door for you (it wasn´t even a very special door, but you had to be invited to the Realm of the Living), underwater cities in which tribes of sharkmen fend off giant zombie fishes, or the underground Goblin Markets of Chiaroscuro. Oh, and a castle in which a Faewith the shapeof a beast used his servants, which took the shape of living objects and uncommon animals (for the region: they were parrots and monkeys in the North) to kidnap youngs from the village, both men and women, turn them into their brides, their special ones, and finally kill them when they finally disappoint him (not a fault of them: it was rigged from the start), to start the cycle a new! And a player is playing a sorcerer and want to summon demon-spiders to produce magical silks, and train refugees and create magical manufacturing villages in the middle of nowhere, Soviet planned cities style, to produce awesome clothes and ropes and sails with strong, magical, beautiful silk (so he can be rich as hell, yes, but also as a way to provide a new life to these refugees)

        And I favour a relatively "normal" version of Creation, in which there aren´t sorcerers in each corner and I´m more inspired by real societies and cultures than by pulp fantasy (although I´m a lawyer, I´m also an amateur historian. Love my damn history!). The Creations used by other groups will probably be stranger than mine.

        So, I don´t really see the need of Other Worlds so you can have real exploration. There is enough weird things in Creation!

        (Although I have never been a fan of the Umbra. I like to focus my games in a region or city, and in the groups and characters living there and the consequences of their actions, and I find that when the players spend half the time exploring far-off realms to no real connections to the place they are playing in... It dilutes the style I like to play, and my players are also not fans of roaming, travelling characters. So... When I put pocket realms, they are localized things, with influence in the place they are playing in. In my current "Occult investigators in a Japanese High School" game there is a pocket realm, they haven´t still accessed but will probably visit soon, that is a corruption and mirror of the ultracompetitive and standarized school system in which they are immersed, with hateful students with no faces but codebars where the eyes should be. In my last Mage game, they had to do a trip to the Umbra, but was trying to save the Sanctum and it was memorable, but they liked to play in the city more)
