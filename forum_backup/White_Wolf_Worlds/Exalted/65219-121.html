                So I have been just doing some preemptive world-building for the Optional Exalted, the Hearteaters, the Umbral Exalted, and the Dream-Souled, so ready to change when Exigents drop and get more info. Basically just figuring out "If going by the assumption that they have always been in the setting, what is a way I can explain their presence without changing the current setting or making them seem like they have just been sitting doing nothing for centuries."

                What I mean by that is well, the Exalted are big things. The very world change to their actions. Just look at the Dragon-Blooded and Lunars, how they have created entire cultures and civilizations around themselves. You don't have to ask what they are doing, you can just see it. This becomes very apparent when you have a lot of the Exalted working together, that's how you get the empire that is the Realm or the alliance which is the Silver Pact. It is because of this whenever you introduce a whole new Exalt type, literal divine heroes who will change society in their wake, you have to ask that assumption, to find exactly where they can fit in the setting without making it overall look different. At the extremes of this, it requires making a whole other realm you can put them in (looking at you Alchemicals...) but well, I'd like to avoid doing that but let's see.

                So the easiest ones I think are the Hearteaters and Dream-Souled, mainly because we have precedents on how it can work. With Hearteaters we know one compelling fact about them, everyone freaking hates them. It's the same case as with Lunars, rather if people knew of them and what they are capable you can see that going to the top of everyone's kill list. So what this means I would think is that keeping hidden would be their top priority, so you won't be seeing Hearteater run empires or the like, or well you wouldn't be aware of it. I can see their being a mutual support organization for Hearteaters like the Getimians but like them, it be more secret and isolated. Well though, this would depend on how stealthy Hearteaters can be on average, so will be looking forward to see.

                With Dream-Souled, it be a case similar to Liminals, in that they have small numbers, not that common, and don't have the same impact as Celestials. They are Terrestrial, rare, and came about relatively recently from what we have found out about them, meaning their probably more like individual cases for most of Creation. Sidereals have a stance on them but for everyone else they would just be they would they considered these strange Wyld-beings with wierd powers.

                And now Umbrals...now they are more difficult. One, they are Celestial, meaning they are carrying around as much firepower as Lunars and Solars and two, they are not hated by the realm or are automatically labelled as Anathema. So that raises a question of where would be the kingdoms and organizations of the Umbrals then? I believe they have low numbers, given how they are expected to be played in mixed circles but still, you only need one Lunar to allow themselves to create their own Dominion. And I can't see the Realm or other core factions being particularly fine with that, just have these powerful figures being largely neutra or just doing nothing for centuries on end. So what to do, honestly the best answer I could come up with was simply, put them in Hell. The way I'm thinking of it is, Creation is already full with splats, Heaven has Sidereals and Getimians to play with, the Underworld Abyssals and Liminals as well. The Wyld is it's own thing, pretty covered with Lunars and now Dream-Souled. So that just left well Malfeas. From what we know so far it seems to fit, the Umbrals are about self-guilt and hate over their actions and failures and their patron has been confirmed to be a traitor. I feel there is much in Malfeas that they would get and play off with. Now they aren't Infernals, the Chosen of Hell. I see them similar to Getimians and Liminals with their respective realms. They aren't supported by Malfeas and its demons, rather it is just an area that they would very much interact and play in, or at least that's gonna be my plan with them. Gives Malfeas some more things to craft stories around, that is my intent. This may change when we learn more about Umbrals and I doubt my vision is gonna be supp on the write up, it is however a compromise I made to give them a world to play and make legends in without infringing on all the current conspiracies and factions dominating Creation proper.

                    In the Demon City, the ownership of clocks or timepieces of any kind is outlawed by the Priesthood of Cecelyne.

                    Savants have discovered that, on occasion, odd hourglass-looking devices in the possession of Demons. These are known as Cecelynian mileglasses. They are filled with sand from the Endless Desert, and with a small investment of will can be attuned to a user. When the user of a mileglass names a location or person or thing and turns the glass over, sand begins to drain from the top reservoir in proportion to the wearers proximity to their target. A few grains fall with every stride, or in a steady stream when traveling on horseback or riding the winds. As the user gets closer more sand will continue to drain, and should they stray from their course sand will flow upwards to let them know they've taken the wrong path. These artifacts are coveted by demons and see extensive use among the ranks of Cecelyne's priesthood. Some have fallen into the hands of savants who have discovered, fantastically, that they work just as well in Creation as they do in the Demon City.

                                                        I see that my previous post has elicited responses. I'm interested in cordially addressing them, but... don't really feel as though this thread is the right place, I don't like when the Simple Question ones take off their own tangents. Perhaps I should start a separate thread to discuss it?

                                                        The Mileglass sounds to me like something that is just useful enough to be attractive and having that proper uncanny demon quality to its magic, while also being, at the very least, highly inconvenient owing to the need to constantly recalibrate yourself as you get your bearings towards your destination. Mercury help you if you want to find something that is very far away from you and continuously on the move. I feel as though such factors would effectively balance it.

                                                        Were I to read that description, I would presume only objects as discrete, concrete examples rather than broad abstractions, and that asking to find something that did not exist would simple result in... nothing. Don't see why figuring it out should be overcomplicated.

                                                        (It's way less powerful then an alethiometer, those things were capable of providing you with complex information. They wouldn't just tell you where to find something, they could tell you that somebody was coming to you or provide you with useful explanations. Lyra divined an event in the distant past with it.)

                                                        Anyway, the reason that I came here. I had been familiar before with the practice behind the practice behind the palimpsest, the durable writing medium that has had its inscriptions cleared away to be recycled, but not the word. Nor did I know that such things can be analysed to discern what used to be written on them, which can lead to the recovery of lost literature.

                                                        Head canon: many of the original manuscripts of the Immaculate Texts are palimpsests. Important writing of the First Age was often placed onto specially made material intended to endure the ages and disasters. The Sidereal Exalted viewed such materials as useful for placing their own words on, for purposes of preservation and lending an air of older authenticity, and lacked the means to conveniently produce them, so repurposing was frequent. Considering that many such samples already contained the kinds of metaphysical and philosophical writings that the Sidereals wished to appropriate, it was often convenient to take those, wipe away the incompatible words and edit in what conveyed the narrative they wished to promote. A Solar who recovered a first edition Immaculate Text would probably be able to examine it to find traces of interesting information that might otherwise have been lost.

                                                        (This is in no way an effective gotcha even for people with more rudimentary understanding of Immaculate history. It's the easiest thing in the world to rationalize that the medium was recycled for practical purposes, and even evidence of earlier iterations of the writing can be attributed to the original author redrafting.)

                                                            In Autochthonia, among the Eight Nations, the obvious gap in the Alchemical Castes, the fact that there is a sixth magical material, but no Adamant Caste to match it, has not gone unnoticed. Savants have long speculated about the potential for such a being, and some experimental attempts have even been made by a few of the Nations to catalyze one (such attempts have always failed, whether due to imperfect understanding of the process, or due to sabotage by actual Adamant Caste Champions under the direction of the Divine Ministers). These speculations and theories have given rise to a couple of cultural practices among people of the Eight Nations.

                                                            The more common of the pair is to refer to the "Empty Caste" or "Absent Caste", to mean an obvious break in a pattern or clear idea that has somehow been overlooked. A shift chief might tell one of her lieutenants "I think you've missed the Empty Caste here - if we don't have any bolts, we can't keep working, no matter how motivated the crew is." Or a military historian might write a treatise where she referred to the one lost battle in a string of Estasian victories as the Absent Caste of the war.

                                                            The other practice, stemming from conspiracy theories that the Adamant Caste do exist, but are somehow being concealed from the general public or the Eight Nations as a whole, is to refer to the "Hidden Caste" to mean the concealed power behind the throne or someone covertly interfering with events. A Wisantian rabble-rouser, for example, could claim that there was a Hidden Caste of conservative Theomachry members influencing Nurad's National Tripartite Council to continue keep a lid on innovation and development.
