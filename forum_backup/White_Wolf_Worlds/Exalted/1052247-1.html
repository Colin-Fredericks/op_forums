                            Originally posted by Totentanz View Post
                            The Wyld Hunt in Ex3 is mentioned as being explicitly a Dragon-Blooded institution, rather than a Realm one.
                            ​That's a detail I quite liked; it gave me a sense that the communal aspect of the Dragon Blooded is something that continues to function without needing every single Dragon Blood to belong to an explicit community. That the legacy of their worldwide rule and the continuing prominence of a particular group means that they form the standard for the term "Exalted" across the world, meaning that any given Exalt has a background that informs them that they're part of something bigger with certain obligations even if they never even meet another Terrestrial.

                                Regarding the matter of how there's a society ruled by Dragon Blooded (who are apparently either something like a disconnected colony of the Realm or another enclave of Shogunate influences) proverbially next door to Ysyr, I think something analogous to my idea about a certain dissonance between their abstract notions of the Exalted and their actual political relations with them would be a lot of situations in pre-modern and early modern Europe involving the relations between kings and certain subjects, or kings and the pope.

                                ​If you're a powerful noble in a European kingdom of the time, you might maintain the narrative that the king is God's special creature in an overall sense, you're just a bit insistent that the king stay over there and not interfere in your administration of your own property, or infringe on your aristocratic rights. You're not fighting to replace the king, you're fighting to make the king sign the great charter of your liberties.

                                I'd say that Ysyr can oppose Prasad in terms of a very emphatic insistence that they can get by on their own and don't require any of the assistance of the Exalted oblige. A "thanks, but no thanks" spelled out with massed ranks of spears. Or occasionally even an assertion that while those Exalted have legitimacy, they're overstepping their bounds a bit.

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                        Regarding the matter of how there's a society ruled by Dragon Blooded (who are apparently either something like a disconnected colony of the Realm or another enclave of Shogunate influences) proverbially next door to Ysyr, I think something analogous to my idea about a certain dissonance between their abstract notions of the Exalted and their actual political relations with them would be a lot of situations in pre-modern and early modern Europe involving the relations between kings and certain subjects, or kings and the pope.

                                        ​If you're a powerful noble in a European kingdom of the time, you might maintain the narrative that the king is God's special creature in an overall sense, you're just a bit insistent that the king stay over there and not interfere in your administration of your own property, or infringe on your aristocratic rights. You're not fighting to replace the king, you're fighting to make the king sign the great charter of your liberties.

                                        I'd say that Ysyr can oppose Prasad in terms of a very emphatic insistence that they can get by on their own and don't require any of the assistance of the Exalted oblige. A "thanks, but no thanks" spelled out with massed ranks of spears. Or occasionally even an assertion that while those Exalted have legitimacy, they're overstepping their bounds a bit.
                                        This makes sense to me. It's a bit like the stories of George Washington toasting to the King's health during the Revolutionary War. He and his officers figured they would fight for a bit, an agreement would be reached, and they could continue as loyal subjects. Similarly, European Kings often made a big deal of being completely loyal to the Church while simultaneously opposing the current Pope's policies.

                                        I don't know if this is strictly necessary, though. I could see the ruling class of Ysyr deliberately cultivating a culture that doesn't inherently respect the Dragon-Blooded over time. Reverence for the Exalted can be replaced with reverence for Sorcerers. To me, this seems dependent on how long Prasad and Ysyr have been fighting. if the Dreaming Sea's post-Contagion/Crusade history is defined by on-going conflict, it's unlikely the deference to the Chosen has endured.

                                        On the other hand, if shifting geopolitical winds cause the powers to sometimes make peace and other times make war, a complex cultural situation could emerge. Those in Ysyr who benefit from peaceful relations favor the Dragon-Blooded, while those who profit from war (or at least don't profit during peace) tend to emphasize autonomy/patriotism/jingoism. It's a bit like the political situation in the United States currently. Those who economically benefit from open migration and globalization tend to support it, and those who have lost out on the economic gains tend to oppose it.

                                        Regardless, I don't see Ysyr discarding distrust/fear of Anathema. They have empirical evidence to support that bias, and competent rulers rarely encourage competing power centers.
