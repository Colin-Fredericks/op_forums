                                        Seems to me like there obviously isn't any deep logic that flows directly from the essence of the Solar splat as to why they should get the highest Sorcery or Evocations. It's just their place in the game.

                                        Solars who used Fate weaving magic as their primary tool Sidereals wouldn't be or feel like Solars. Sidereals who could use Solar Circle Sorcery like Solars would still feel like Sidereals.

                                        I wouldn't say it is annoying that Solars can do more with Evocations by default than other splats as, after all, it is the easiest thing in the world to houserule if you are so inclined and its pretty consistent with their role in the narrative. It is just annoying, perhaps, to see it cast in terms of the Solars getting such an ability as a deep birthright from their very nature (let alone a questionable "humanising" nature to their power), rather than their designated protagonist role in the game. To me, this just seems like some unconvincing flim flam because John Morke feels its important for Solars to feel like they "deserve" everything they have, that they aren't arbitrarily the mightiest of the chosen.

                                        ...

                                        "The return of the champions of old will shake the world" as the core premise of the game..? This is part of the game, however I find calling this a core premise implies, to me, that if you wanted to write up a shard where things were more or less exactly as they were except without any Usurpation or Solar power edge, then that would fail to be Exalted or it wouldn't share the same core premise or it would be rejecting the core premise of canon Exalted. That feels really obviously false to me - 99% of what makes Creation an interesting and unique setting to play in and consider and most of what makes it appealing to most of its fans, has absolutely no dependency on where the Solars sit in their power and narrative role. "Core premise" feels wrong here.
