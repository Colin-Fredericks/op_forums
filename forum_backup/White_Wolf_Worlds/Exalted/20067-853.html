            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post


            Dramatic tension is found
            One of my favourite series of books is The Dark Tower by Stephen King. In it, the protagonist is a crack shot gunslinger who relies on his right hand to live and die. He is maimed in the first book of the series and left unable to shoot. No-one can contest that one of the most compelling moments in Game of Thrones/A Song of Ice and Fire is when the greatest swordsman in the land gets his hand chopped off.

            It's fantastic drama.

            Another, character defining, moment of drama can be found in Poker House where Jennifer Lawrence's character is left with her mother's pimp (whom she has been romaticising up until this point) who decides to teach her a lesson. It wakes her up and makes her realize the truth about her situation and what she has to do to keep her family safe.

            And if "dramatic tension" flies at your table, then play on and have fun.

            But I prefer player agency.

            Make the offer: have drugs in exchange for shirking your duty. But keep it a player choice: if the character has a Flaw to do with drugs then the player probably WANTS to be in this position and will take the 2XP. But don't force it on the player with a lucky dice roll.

            If you're hitting a character's Intimacies and Flaws, appealing to what they care about and what shapes their character, then you can engineer drama without resorting to random chance. Talk to the players about playing a role rather than mechanically optimizing to win a game, but give them agency. Don't swap player agency for a dice roll and call it drama.

            If "it is compelling for the resolution of that dilemma to be in the hands of weighted odds" for you, then have fun. I like this in combat encounters, I'm not advocating for a diceless system.

            But I like the Persaude action because you have to appeal to what the character cares about. Bargain doesn't care. Offering a drug addict drugs against their better judgement IS dramatic... but you could equally offer drugs to literally anyone with Bargain. Or offer the drug addict a pair of warm socks in exchange for shirking. And the only mechanic to combat that is for the player to say "that offer is not sufficient": that mechanic needs to stay in the player's hands.

                                    I’m saying the reverse may also be true. If you have a defining intimacy of blinding hatred for the First and Forsaken Lion, and I tell you we’re going to go unmake him, send him to Lethe, whatever, you could say “now, existence is suffering for the Lion right? So really you can’t leverage that intimacy to go kill the Lion because I understand that if we kill him it will end his suffering, so I actually want him to live as long as possible and that persuade action fails automatically.” Which I think goes against the spirit of the social system. Even if that is an argument you could make, the person making the argument to go kill him beat your resolve, so they Successfully made the case to you that death is the best punishment for his crimes. Likewise if your broke-ass character gets offered a huge sum of money, or your information broker gets offered a bunch of secrets, as long as what’s on offer is reasonably valuable, your character got convinced that it’s worth it.

                                    It’s not 2e mind control style, you still need to justify the bargain, but you have a bit of an obligation not to try and twist things into inapplicability at all times. Just like the Dawn is there to buzzsaw through monsters and save everyone’s ass from being killed, the Eclipse is there to stop everyone from losing their shirt to a mythic level huckster.
