    Paradoxical Sidereal Thoughts

    12-05-2016, 05:24 PM
    I've been one of the stubborn ones when it comes to Sidereals for a long time, and have always argued for them to stay the same. Don't mess with Astrology, don't let them create Charms, and so on. Because most of us don't want to lose the 'magic' of 1e Sidereals.

    So it's ironic that I'm the one suggesting this.

    But since 3e is a time of new beginnings, maybe it's time to get rid of Paradox completely. Pattern Spider Bite was a fun mechanic, but it's primary reason for existing was because Sidereals didn't have a standard Limit flaw, and having two is just too much when one of them acts as a hindrance to Astrology. One of the things about 3e is getting rid of such hindrances and speed bumps that might keep you from doing the 'cool stuff', which is why Excellencies are free now and Solars start with more than 10 Charms. Some of the effects and triggers of Paradox could be retained and folded into the Sidereal Limit flaw without too much trouble I think, and Sidereal players would no longer feel punished for using Astrology. While Censure provides a method of reigning in excesses that's more appropriate from a setting perspective as it is more of a roleplaying instrument rather than a strict mechanical one. Whatever other changes to Astrology that might (or might not) come, I think that's one that should definitely be made.

              I loathe pattern bite. "You dare use your basic power? Take 7 aggravated." is such a fucking terrible mechanic. It's not evocative, it's not interesting, it's just "Tear up your character sheet. That'll teach you to play the game."

              Closed charmsets, though, I like, because I like the idea that "The maidens saw into the future and created every Charm Sidereals would ever really need".
              Players (and I'm including the ST because the ST is a player) should never forget that they aren't the Sidereals. Nothing stops us from declaring a homebrewed charm to have always existed in-universe. ("Why don't the other Sidereals know it", you ask? Maybe they do and none of the PCs have ever seen it in action. Or maybe the Maiden of Secrets kept it hidden all this time. etc. etc. handwave handwave)

              There are ways of making paradox interesting, of course, but the current implimentation isn't it, imo. I wrote up a prototype of what I'd have liked to see somewhere. I'll see if I can find it again.


              Edit: here it is. If you notice the peculiar terminology, it's because it's from a sidereal homebrew thread, but it doesn't really affect the overall idea.


              Originally posted by Fata-Ku View Post
              Tell me about it. In Awakening, a horror game where reality is literally out to punish you and erase you from existence, Paradox still isn't as bad (read: is more fun) as it is in a game about high-octane kung-fu bureaucrats. Come the fuck on!

              I like the vision that it's bureaucratic red tape and that you can lower it by staying late at work, but it's still missing an actual engagement mechanic. Doing generic extra paperwork isn't engaging, nor is trying to weasel into the smallest possible paradox pool (it might be intellectually rewarding the first couple of times, but once you've solved that puzzle it's just rote).

              By "engagement mechanic", I mean something that actually generates story and player action. Examples include:
              • Your Fate Request means that the pattern spiders can't overlook a certain aspect of creation, causing reality glitches that you must fix.
              • Your Fate Request directly competes with an other one, creating a destiny loophole that the PS are too busy to solve. Solve it yourself or get the other party to cancel their request.
              • The Pattern Spiders are tired of your bullshit and have decided to give you the silent treatment. You are now a glitch. Every Scene that you start in Creation, roll a d10. On a 1, you cease to interact with Creation, as though you were an illusion, until you pay a cost equivalent to what gods pay to Materialize. On a 7, you are mistaken for a local celebrity by literally everyone. On a 10, you gain [essence] points of storyteller-decided mutations for the remainder of the session. These points stack, but are purely cosmetic and don't actually exist, they just look like they do.
              • The Pattern Spiders are so tired of your bullshit that they're taking it out on you. Gain the Enemy flaw for the remainder of the story at a level deemed appropriate by teh ST. This may be a scorned lover, an ally who feels betrayed or a Deathlord that decided that you are their next plaything.
              • Seriously, stop pissing off the pattern spiders, they gave your address to some fairfolk/demons/ghosts that were looking for a good time, and more will come until you do X.
              • I warned you about the pattern spiders, bro. I warned you dawg: Instead of giving you the silent treatment, they're giving you an angry rant. Sometimes stairs lead to the opposite of their destination, doors open to the wrong room, even your own pockets are sometimes those of someone else. Whenever you roll for an action, roll a d10. If the resulting face is higher than your amount of successes on that action, the environment changes such that the opposite happens. For unrolled actions, treat this number as 5. This doesn't apply to combat. They're mad, but they're not that mad. Yet.

              Originally posted by Fata-Ku View Post
              I gotta agree with Unsung Hero. Just suffering damage is one of the effects of Pattern Bite that was the least interesting, same for catatonia. It's not really fun to just be taken out of the game.

              Something that creates plot hooks or gets players to act in interesting ways usually works better.

              Some suggestions for madnesses:
              • The characters intimacies are reversed for a [day/week?], and the player must act accordingly.
              • The sidereal gains a fixation they must adhere to, like speaking only in rhymes or needing to mention the subject of their kismet in every sentence (and rolling a die of paradox if they fail to do so).
              • Once per scene for one session, the Sidereal gets either nightmarish or ecstatic, but very vivid and potent, hallucinations relating to their kismet, which the Sidereal is convinced are prophetic (but may not be).
              • For a day/week the Sidereal dissociates from their identity, becoming convinced that they are someone else entirely, often using Resplendent Destinies to this effect. Attempting to reconcile them with their "true" self causes distress and tangles their fate further, either draining one WP or adding a die of Paradox. Repeated attempts may trigger the Sidereal to withdraw further into their new personality as the paradox reactivates. Once the effect passes, they remember both selves as being simultaneously true.

                      To diverge very briefly on the subject of the Sidereal closed Charmset, I think the splat will benefit greatly from both the charm mechanics that were created after the 2e Sidereal book, but by now are well established. Namely Charms that gain new powers when the Exalt reaches a certain Essence level, or when you have the option to re-buy a Charm for expanded powers. The true problem with the closed Charmset is that the simple limits of practicality mean they can only devote so much space in the book to Charms, and so must choose the Charms that are 'best' for them overall, whether or not there are others that would also fit and be very useful for Sidereals (and I for one have also been of the mind that ST's and Players can get together and decide that "this custom Charm has always existed" when it suits). At the very least the previously mentioned mechanics can save on word count so they can get more of those Charms in. And that's all I'll really say about that, before I side-track my own thread completely.

                      I certainly wouldn't mind going back to Paradox as the a mechanical flaw, but obviously with more interesting traits to it than like those mentioned. I never felt really comfortable with the Sidereal Limit mechanic from a storytelling standpoint even more than the mechanical in 2e, even if the purely roleplayed Limit flaw was easy to miss, and sometimes hard to pull off in 1e. And using Paradox as a cost for high-level Charms and large abuses of Astrology would make a lot more sense than Limit ever would. Perhaps even Astrological ones, who knows honestly... I'm more open to change overall this time around, though I think the core of what makes Sidereals what they are should be retained from past editions. And I also just considered that since we have multiple new types of Exalts, including some that were never around to be cursed by the Neverborn, it's logical to propose that a Limit track will not be universal either, though I think we can count on there being something similar for everyone.

                      And Fata-Ku... I love the ideas you have there for Paradox, but won't be able to give a full commentary of them until I'm able to stop chuckling. But I will say it's exactly the kind of weird shit that I love to have happen in games, and I'm definitely going to borrow them for use in the meantime.
