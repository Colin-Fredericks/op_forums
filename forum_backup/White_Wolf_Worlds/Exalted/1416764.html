    Need Help With Designing Manse Defenses

    10-04-2020, 05:17 PM
    So next session I'm going to have the PCs attack a couple of manses: The Burning Breath of Heseish and The House of Black Waters.

    I'm trying to work about appropriate levels of resistance for these targets.

    I figure that there might well be an entire Sworn Kingship guarding the Breath. It's a fire aspected manse which (in my game and previous editions) sits reasonably close to the Imperial City and is held by House Cathak. It's supposed to be impossible for anyone by Fire Aspected Dragon-Blooded to endure it comfortably, so I'm figuring heat-based environmental penalities and hazards. Maybe a Guarda Bird drawn to the Essence of the place (maybe two Guarda Birds, courting each other with a mind toward self immolation). The manse itself is a giant dragon statue filled with super-heated volcanic air, and ringed by a compound of buildings on top of a hill -- so I'm thinking clear lines of sight, not two many "traps", flying enemies, more or less a straight fight in a hostile environment. Easy, done, move on.

    The House of Black Waters is the public home of House Iselsi. It's also where they keep the details of their Vendetta against the Realm. I figure it's got to be pretty well defended. If it's the public face of House Iselsi, I figure there's got to be, what, a ~8 Young Dynast level Dragon-Blooded, ~3 Shikari level, 2 Experienced Dragon-Blooded and a Berit level House Elder (will probably reskin the QCs out of Heirs so it's not quite so generic as that). Plus bound demons. And it's a Water Aspected manse. Folks on Discord have suggested it be filled with traps... but I'm struggling to see how that plays out in play.

    For example, folks are saying use "Saw" as inspiration. But the PCs are attacking the manse. They've not been kidnapped and placed inside the manse. The manse that is the public facing headquarters of House Iselsi. I'm not sure how much of Saw is useable. It's a great aestic, I just don't know how I practically translate it into Exalted. Someone suggested a room where they have to torture someone to pass through to the next room, but the correct torture tools are hidden in room and they have to find them then work out what the correct method of torture is... I'm not even sure how I'd reduce that to a dice roll... or how I'd frame that scene narratively (like, unless they watch a creepy video of a doll on a tricycle explaining to them that they need to torture someone, how do you even convey that this is what they have to do by the set up of the room).

    Poison on papers, doorknobs etc. all seems pretty straightforward. Rolls to lockpick doors. Easy basic dungeon stuff. But it seems kinda... boring to me. "You disturb the poison on the desk, and inhale the dust roll [Stamina+Resistance] against Curea." I don't want stuff which is "roll this dice pool and instantly win": if I'm going to think up puzzles and traps, then I want the players (not the characters) to have to think up how they're going to get past them. Straightforward stuff like "roll against this poison" is great as an entree (roll against poison, now you're poisoned you have to fight this demon). But I need main courses: I'm thinking seven back-to-back challenges - two noncombat, one combat, three noncombat, one boss combat.

    One thing I really like is a Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell style maze, where the manse rearranges itself to stop them making progress through it. Which I can narrate with the best of them... but what I'm stuck for is how, in play, the PCs solve the manse. Like, it can be, roll (Perception+Occult) with a good Stunt from the players and a good description from me... but I'd prefer a real puzzle that they actually have to work out so that it feels more like they're defeating the defenses of a powerful, mystic building rather than rolling dice against a static difficulty. But I'm stuck on how to actually play that out. I'm not against the PCs straight-up powering their way through these puzzles with an Excellency, but I'm asking for help in making that interesting to play through (which might be some unique and novel thing, or it might be really basic Storytelling Advice).

    So... dear Exalted forum:

    Give me your riddles.
    Give me your puzzles.
    Give me your ideas from dungeons past.
    Favourite Zelda levels.
    Take a scene from Saw and set it up as something you could actually play through in Exalted.

    No criticism too harsh, no advice to obvious. I've got five days and 21 hours to think of some plot that doesn't suck. Help a brother out.

    What would be great, is something like DrLoveMonkey's Adventure Ho! for attacking The House of Black Waters. But any cool puzzles or challenges you have given players, or played through as a player, for any game would be neat inspiration. (And whatever I don't use next week can be used further down the line/might help someone else.)

          One neat possibility is that, when your players leap over the garden paths and kick down the front doors of The House of Black Waters, they find a normal, unassuming Imperial residence, with the typical frightened servants and haughty, offended hosts. If the player characters burst in arms blazing then there'll probably be some initial fight, but it's also possible that they leap in and catch a group of five young Iselsi Dynasts and their hangers-on about to leave, and the youngsters run deeper into the manse frightened out of their minds.

          The first puzzle for your players, of course, is finding the 'real' House of Black Waters beneath the banal disguise. Maybe one player gets tired with looking for secret passageways and just blasts through some floorboards only to fall into a swiftly moving underground river that sweeps him into a dark oubliette he's got to bust out of. There could be similar traps and stuff designed to target people who just start ripping through things rather than search for the hidden entrances or what-have-you.

          I'd play up the cliches of Dynastic residences, with a big pagoda palace building hiding a secret fortress of stone and water beneath it. I'd have their be lots of hidden chute-like traps to channel invaders into perilous chambers and deadly traps deep below. Maybe there could be swift water-filled passages that flow through meshes of razor wire, all the better to maim any who get caught in the current.

          Also, since the House of Black Waters is where Iselsi herself is laid to rest there, her family are probably bastards enough to do something with her corpse. A reanimated corpse might be too much, but I could see there being some hyper-potent artifact that uses some of her bones or essence to deadly effect. Maybe a bow wielded by the House Elder that forms arrows out of water pulled from nearby sources, like the aforementioned underground rivers, pretty-looking fountains, her foe's blood, etc.

          Do you know what your players' plans will be?

              Actual riddles are going to be very hard here, and need to be crafted in very particular ways.

              Mainly because a lot of people aren't good at riddles, and even people who are great at them can often just get stuck with a little mental hitch.

              Like "I have cities, but no houses, mountains but no trees, lakes, rivers and oceans, but no fish. What am I? Speak my name and you shall have me." the answer there being a map, which would be very helpful. If nobody gets that after 5 minutes of guessing and they're just stumped, and they NEED the map to progress, then session over, or you just have to give them the answer, and that's no fun either because then they could just give up all the time and have to get it to proceed.

              So any real life riddles should be treated like pass-fail checks in the game. If it's 100% required, you have to let them fail and then continue, or pass, but with a penalty. The only time true failure should be a thing is when they've failed like 6 out of the total 7 challenges, and they're sitting there with no motes, willpower, or health levels left, and decide to press on anyway, even knowing the house elder and 3 exalted bodyguards are waiting in the next room.


              That being said I'll think of some stuff to post tomorrow if I can find the time to write it.

                                  Traps are good. I like traps. I have a DnD folder full of traps.

                                  Puzzles are good. I like puzzles. I have a DnD folder full of puzzles.

                                  I don't have any Exalted folders full of those things because Excellencies and Charms shatter them like glass. And Int 5 Wis 5 Lore 5 Investigation 5 Twilight is literally a once in a millenium super genius. They figure out the puzzle. They solve 4d rubix cubes for fun, before breakfast. I play a this character and I have more than 10 Craft skills at rank 5 and I have Shattering Grasp, Words as Workshop Method, and Vice Miracle Technique. There just isn't a puzzle that he can't solve or disassemble unless it was made 2000 years ago by a different Twilight.

                                  Traps? Athletics and Resistance obliterate these. There's very little a superhuman muscle machine can be challenged by. The Str 5 Stm 5 Ath 5 Zenith can hold up the collapsing ceiling. The Night caste sees the tripwire in the pitch black darkness. Et cetera, et cetera.

                                  That said, if you wanna screw with them, i might have a few Diceless puzzles.

                                  The 5 Potions: pretty straitforward. There are 5 potions on the table, different bottles sizes, colors, etc. They each do something and the only way through a door is to drink the right one. There's no clue or way to discern which it is. Give out 2 boons and 2 banes. Drink up!

                                  Blood Price: you wanna pass, you gotta pay the toll. Its blood. Your blood. Half your health in blood. No cheating. Get to it.

                                  The Missing Piece: there's a mural of a famous scene on the wall, something from Exalted's Shakespeare or Waterloo or whatever. However, a major detail is missing (i.e. Romeo's knife in Juliet). You have to find it in a room full of junk, Last Crusade style. The catch is its a trap and the real key is a piece of the junk.

                                  The Wire Trick: there's a black tripwire in the hallway. About a foot after that, there's another wire camouflaged like the floor. Then 2 feet after that there's an invisble wire that's tied to the real trap. Gotcha.

                                  The upside down room: its about a 100 feet to the ceiling. Theres a door high up, that touches the ceiling. How will you get there? Surprise, gravity is reversed in this room. Hope you have a way to mitigate the fall.

                                  Sharks: just a bunch of sharks.

                                  Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.
