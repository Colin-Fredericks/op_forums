                        Okay so my Creation is more a presentational quirk than anything I'm doing drastically different in terms of world building.

                        While I didn't like last edition's industrialisation of magic, science is nevertheless pretty important to how I present my game world. When I say “science” I mean there's a lot of observation and experimentation with natural (and supernatural) phenomena, rather than modern amenities that the players take for granted being supplied by magic that's wearing the trappings of science.

                        I could go into detail but other people have given their preferences and I don't want to shit on them.

                                            Mortal sorcerers, while not exactly common, are extremely widespread. Most major townships have one lurking at the boundaries, and cities can and do have small cabals.

                                            This is thematically because I like to use sorcery as a metaphor for the equalizing and alienating power of knowledge and education, partly because that means Exalted always have at least one rival who might be on their level.

                                                I go with rarer myself, since I tend to run with some of the game biggest fictional insprations to infomr things. So the for sorcery I have been using myself has been what is hinted at in The Black Comapny. There are minor sorcerers about, and a kingdom might actually have a court sourcerer. Sometimes that sorcerer might not even be one officially on paper (like Smoke in the later books). And concentrations of more than two ro three is notable in how much well, power, that is in one spot. Or if htey're dedicated, do weird shit that builds up like Bomanz in the releasing of the Lady or the necromancer in the recent Port of Shadows intersequel.

                                                Another is to me Tales to the Flat Earth, where there are scholars and sorcerers, but are a notable amount of folks who think they're magic (and not) and folks who have potential fo rmagic (but don't relaize it). Sorcerers there are not common enough to be ordinary, but not rare enough to be unheard of basically. Enough there are the myriad sorcerous initaitons, but that having more than one or two in a polity means a lot.

                                                Or they could be Lisa Tepes from the Castlevania animated series, where she's not relaly a mage at all (she's Medicine and Lore in Exalted contexts) but the uninformed/uneducated won't know the difference if you do enough things that work. And the local medicine woman's soultions aren't shit but are probably running on some half-known occult principles.

                                                I guess for me it is a genre thing. It's also admit it is a bit of a philosophical thing. I think meritocracy i n general is a lie that the powerful create to make themselves feel justified in keeping others down. So I'm not oen to go and put actulal in-setting "rewards" to folks who work really hard, when I think a lot of power and success is down to luck and circumstance as much if not more than inherent ability. There's a lot of folks who could have been good sorcerers who never got a chance, adn a lot of folks who had all the chance who are good at being a quack basically.

                                                    In my creation Eye and Seven Despairs is almost a completely different character, tossing out anything 2e edition said to use the 1e incarnation as a baseline.

                                                    I know he's going full-on gothic mad scientist this edition, and while I found the idea of him playing his deathknights against each other and 'himself' as he did neat, I found the 'posing as his own concubine' stuff a little too squicky for me (and likely my group), and had the thought that perhaps Star of Dirt and Doubt was always the real Eye and Seven Despairs, and used the mummy as a puppet she could possess so that she could do her experiments without being interrupted, and used the deathknights as a way to retire the puppet so she could focus on her work without needing to run the kingdom herself. Now she's basically Liv Octavius from Into the Spiderverse as a gothic mad scientist with a trio of lovers running her kingdom for her while she "recreates their former master's studies".

                                                    I also ditched the 'Games of Divinity are addictive' plot, only the Unconquered Sun is addicted to them, and only because he's using them as a crutch for his depression after the 1st age went so wrong, basically the Thor plot from Endgame. Luna is splitting their attention between disguising themself as various minor spirits both in creation and heaven to aid the lunars without openly contradicting the mandate of heaven, oppose the bureaucracy of heaven without openly condemning it (at the urging of the maidens), while also trying to support the US and keeping the lighthouse lit for Gaia's return. The Maidens are doing exactly as they say they should be, not even the sidereals know why that has led to inaction in heaven for so long, some think it's to place the sidereals in charge of heaven, now with the return of the solars exalted new theories have emerged.
