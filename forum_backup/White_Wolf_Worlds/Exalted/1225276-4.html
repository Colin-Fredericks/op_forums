    I don't really find many of these arguments convincing, I'm afraid.

    Originally posted by Wise Old Guru View Post
    Iron is metaphysically associated with Earth essence, harmful to the Raksha. The more you work it/alloy it/whatever, the less useful it will be against the Raksha.
    The problem here is that the iron that does harm the Raksha (wrought iron and cast iron) is not notably less worked than steel. The process of making wrought iron involves considerable hammering to work out most of the impurities (its name literally means "worked iron", remember), while cast iron involves melting the iron down completely and pouring it into moulds. I'd argue that such a major transformation in the process of creating it is going to be perceived as more "worked" than steel.

    Originally posted by Wise Old Guru
    It's not just about stabby times, it's about enabling Creation to feel more real by giving it superstitions.
    I don't see how making the effects consistent with real-world superstitions is somehow making it impossible for Creationers to have superstitions? Remember, in the real world, the superstitions about iron affecting the supernatural didn't make a distinction between iron and steel. The peasant burying a knife under her door to ward off witches was not being careful to use only a crappy knife, made of wrought iron instead of steel. Rather, I suspect a good knife would probably have been seen as better, not worse.

    Originally posted by Wise Old Guru
    Steel just feels more symbolically mundane-it's less raw, less earthy, more ubiquitous, more useful, less esoteric.
    Almost none of this is actually true compared to the materials that do harm Raksha, however. I don't believe that steel is "less raw" or "less earthy" than cast iron. It's certainly less ubiquitous than either wrought or cast iron (the reason both of those techniques continued once good steel was created was that they were still cheaper and easier to produce than steel for a long time). It's not "less esoteric" at all - steel making was generally considered a trade secret, one frequently respected and held in high demand, with considerable "industrial espionage" that went on.

    The only thing I'd concede is "more useful", but I don't see why that should make it less useful, magically. Narrative tends to work hand-in-glove with symbolism, and the best symbol for a weapon that's very effective against monsters is... a good weapon.

    Originally posted by Wise Old Guru
    A magic weakness-metal should be something inconvenient to use and carry around, otherwise it'd be ubiquitous and feel less magic and interesting!
    I think a magic weakness should be rarer than otherwise, certainly. But I feel steel in Creation fits this niche quite well, since they're at a tech level where it's harder to produce. As I mentioned upthread, I don't think letting steel weapons hurt Raksha really hurts the balance of the game much at all.

Which considering gravity doesn't even exist in pure chaos would be hard.

                                                So at the very least electromagnetism and gravity don't work like they do in real life, and considering there's no atomic bombs in spite of Twilights with superhuman intelligence in the first age I'd maybe wager that there's no strong/weak nuclear force either. Which, if true, means that not a single fundamental force of our universe is duplicated in Exalted. So things definitely don't behave the same way in our world because they are fundamentally the same.


                                                Okay so physics in Creation is not physics in our world, but it's not just pure magic-we-don't-have-to-explain-it either. Everything that's kind of magical has an essence explanation. In fact a lot of stuff that isn't magical has that even, like fireflies which are made by flies going into the Wyld and breeding with the concept of fire*. That little glow butt is a mote of fire essence.

                                                Raksha interact poorly with earth essence. The reason the Wyld doesn't push into Creation is because of the stabilizing Pole of Earth*, the Wyld-destroying hearthstones are earth aspected, in the DB charmset it's the earth charms, even in non-earth abilities, that beat back chaos. So what the Fey are weak to isn't iron exactly, they're weak to the earth essence in it. In fact the only other thing in the books that is so hostile to the Wyld is death essence, which I don't think cold worked iron has, I could be wrong.

                                                Let's build on this. Why does the process of making steel dilute the earth essence? Maybe that's why it needs to be cold worked as well, any more fire than what was used to cast it in the first place is too much, becoming more a thing of fire than of earth by the end. A thing of the forge rather than the mine. So you cast it once right out of the purified ore and then work it with grindstones.

                                                Either way, somewhere in there is your answer. Or at least the easiest explanation that has a basis in setting physics.

                                                    First, thank you for with the topic rather than dismissing it. I appreciate everyone in this thread who's actually put some thought into this.

                                                    Now, as for your post. I absolutely agree that physics has a different basis in Exalted. As you pointed out, gravity works differently, the day night cycle works differently, much of the scientific concepts that underpin our world are not the same as those that underpin creation. I also agree that creation is set up such that most things are simple to understand from our perspective. Things like gravity being the same everywhere in creation and everyone having the same day/night require different applications of fantasy to achieve. Due to this very forced similarity to Earth, I would argue that the majority of forging/metalworking techniques are the same as they were in Earth in the iron age. The reasons that a smith might hammer steel repeatedly might be different but I believe for the smith, it makes no difference whether he's forging something to properly work the crystal structure and balance the carbon content or if he's bashing the earth essence and molding the fire essence through it. I doubt he knows the specifics of it. It's not like they had microscopes in the iron age to look at crystal structures either.

                                                    However, I still disagree with you.

                                                    Primarily, I reject the idea that the weakness of the raksha has anything to do with Earth essence of any sort.

                                                    First, it makes no sense that iron represents earth essence for two reasons: 1. Iron and metals are more the purview of Autochthon than Gaia. There might be a few elementals made of iron or other metals, but I think we can agree that the majority of them are made of earth or stone. However, elementals of metal and iron are common in the halls of Autochthonia. 2. both wrought iron and cast iron count against the weakness but steel does not. Wrought iron is often as worked as steel is, in the forge. Some more some less. An argument could be made that wrought iron includes some amount of the slag (Earth essence?) that would normally be removed for good steel. I would counter that cast iron does not include these impurities and that it requires good metal as well. It's easier to get decent cast iron out of shitty ore as you can run it through the coal more often and draw out the slag. This adds carbon to the cast iron and would make it useless for steel. So if the coal burning represents fire essence then cast iron would have more of it. Cast iron is also melted down and poured into a cast which requires a much higher heat than steel forging.

                                                    Next, let's examine the far stronger representations of Earth essence: White Jade, The elemental Aura of Earth Aspect Dragon Bloods and earth elementals. None of these things trigger the weakness of the Fae. White jade in weapon form is apparently alloyed with steel. However, pure white jade (which is the magical material created from exposure to massive amounts of earth essence) doesn't work either. You hit a fae with a block of white jade and he gets bludgeoning damage, not aggravated. Once again, there's a counter argument where you could say that white jade is created from earth essence but isn't earth essence itself. Okay, but why then does iron count? What amount the DB? Why is there no special benefit from pouring out pure Earth essence. Perhaps they aren't dumping out actual Earth essence but Earth essence through a lens of mortality.
