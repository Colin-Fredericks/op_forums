    Originally posted by Aeshdan View Post
    Question regarding DB reproduction: In the "Bloodline & Potency" section of WFHW, it talks about a "progenetive potential" that is expended with each conception, and takes years to replenish. Is that the Terrestrial's actual fertility, or the chance of producing a new Terrestrial? In other words, is it that a Terrestrial who has had a child is exceedingly unlikely to be able to birth another for some years, or is it that she has the same potential as any mortal to conceive new children but those children will have a minimal chance of Exaltation?
    Elfive is right: it's the chance of your kid Exalting, not your chance of conceiving a kid. (This is discussed a couple of paragraphs down in that section if you keep reading if you wanted more info.)

    Originally posted by Aeshdan View Post
    Also, is it at all common for matriarchs of the Great Houses to cross-marry scions of their House? Each house is large enough that it would be easy to find members who are only distantly related from a genetic perspective, especially with adoptions to bring in completely new bloodlines, and keeping a promising bloodline in the family might be worth the loss of a potential marriage alliance.
    It's not uncommon, but the main point of marriage is to produce alliances. So I wouldn't call it "common".

    You'd be better off finding a strong bloodline from another house and swapping scions. Then you're not "losing" a promising bloodline, you're trading it like a baseball card AND gaining an alliance. Heirs to the Shogunate discusses double marriages a little bit in the Realm Civil War section.

    If, for example, House Mnemon has two scions of excellent breeding and house Cathak has two scions of excellent breeding, then a cross-house marriage (one man and one woman from each house) means you lose nothing and gain an alliance based on two marriages.

    But if you wanted your character to have a same-house spouse or same house parents, it's not unheard of. And uniting disparate parts of a Great House with a marriage alliance can still be useful. And there's love matches. And, maybe, you do want to keep a promsing bloodline (or individual) in the family.

    Cathak Cainan, for example, is married to another Cathak (Cathak Urima).

        I feel as though the particulars of how the Scarlet Dynasty works means that the subject of marriage as a basis of alliance merits some elaboration. After all, under the Empress it wasn't as if they were had the freedom to conduct war and peace among one another, and a Great House is a unit with hundreds of points of contact with all of the other Great Houses operating at various levels of prestige.

        Certainly there would be cases in which the negotiations for a particular marriage underlie a particular formal agreement between Houses over some mutual endeavour, but I think the broader environment comes from two axioms: that in the broadest sense and at the highest level, the Houses could recognise the benefit of them being intertwined as a contiguous unit, and that there's value to acquiring members for the family whose background and upbringing means that they bring skills, perspectives and contacts that might not be found within one's own ranks.

        I think that's particularly evident in something like the description of House Cathak, and their reputation for selecting marriage prospects based on personal merit, that the main thing of value acquired from the transaction is the person themselves. I expect the prevailing world view of the Scarlet Dynasty is that the Houses are strongest when they have their specialised fields while still benefiting from the vitality that comes from cross-pollination. An insular marriage can occur, but I would expect there's often a question of what they're really getting out of this

        That one family gives up a member in almost every marriage might just be regarded as a necessary compromise in this system, that you've got to give in order to get, but I think there's also a matter where the sons who get married off are regarded as representatives of one's House and its values to the family of their wife, with varying degrees of formality. A Mnemon son married to a Sesus wife functions both as a means of advertising the benefits of dealing with House Mnemon in his new home and a means of perhaps making House Sesus a bit more pious than it otherwise would have been, bringing them closer in line with what Mnemon wants for the Realm. That's not something you get from marrying that lad to one of his cousins, who is already a few haircuts short of being a monk.

        It's got to be a fine line to walk for married men in the Dynasty. Honour and prestige comes from bringing your actions and your values in line with the House of your wife such that you are properly serving them and maintain the cohesion of the House, but you've also got to bring some of the character of the House you came from both as an enduring obligation to them and a reinforcement of why they brought in somebody from outside of the family in the first place. You're not enough like your marriage family, and even if you aren't looked on with suspicion or disdain then you just aren't achieving the kinds of things that advance your standing. Act too much like them, then why are you even here? For extra fun, I'd also suggest portraying the latter as the kind of thing where you'll also be disdained for failing to properly respect the mother and matriarch of your birth.

        Of course, that's what it's like when the Empress is on how to maintain that cohesion through a combination of incentives, remonstrations and presenting a figure for them all to kind of want to be mutually combined in a degree of competition to. It's not said outright, but I would infer that in the last five years marriages have focused down a fair bit to be exclusive with Houses you intend to make formal military alliance with, but are also becoming a bit more insular.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            *All good things*
            Agreed. Yes, and...

            An insular marriage can occur, but I would expect there's often a question of what they're really getting out of this.
            Consider a Sesus branch of the family thoroughly married into House Cynis. They're Sesus in name, but have accumulated very close ties and very bad habits. When the time comes to marry off the next scion, you could increase the ties to Cynis, or try to loop in another Great House, or try to tie the family back to Sesus values and strengthen loyalty to the House.

            Then there's love matches. And farming off a pair of unmarriagables (maybe a sorcerer when you don't want to build ties to the Houses who value such... persons) when you don't want to match them to a mortal.

            I think you're right, of course: it can occur but people question what they're really getting out of this. I think that's the exact in-universe reaction. But my concern is less for the verisimilitude of the fictional world and more for enabling the game. I want to stress that I don't disagree with you, just providing a list of pre-prepared excuses PCs can use.

            Of course, that's what it's like when the Empress is on how to maintain that cohesion through a combination of incentives, remonstrations and presenting a figure for them all to kind of want to be mutually combined in a degree of competition to. It's not said outright, but I would infer that in the last five years marriages have focused down a fair bit to be exclusive with Houses you intend to make formal military alliance with, but are also becoming a bit more insular.
            Emphasis added.

            The caveat here is that as of 768, the Dynasty is still being coy with the subject of civil war. The lines of alliance are still blurry. Candidates still expect to take the throne by diplomacy or acclaim. Even the militant houses aren't ready to strike.

            And whilst the house alliances generally fall where the marriages do, you've still got stuff like Mnemon married into Cynis, with Cynis antagonistic to Mnemon ambitions. And even if Mnemon has spent the last five years marrying for alliance, you still have a lot of pre-existing Mnemon-Cynis marriages that make things blurry.

            And consider also the length of time marriage negotiations take. It's not inconceivable that a PC's "engagement" might have begun before the disappearance. Or even might be with an "enemy" House as part of a plan to either win that House over or at least give them pause.

            In other words, Isator Levi is absolutely correct that marriage alliance is more complicated that I was presenting it. But ultimately, it's so complex that you are pretty justified in presenting it however you want and still being true to canon. (But even of you want to go against canon for your game, that's fine too. It's a make-believe dice game. Though I do think it's worthwhile understanding the canon -- it's a thought through and detailed game world that is deeply enjoyable. )
