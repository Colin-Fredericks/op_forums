        Originally posted by Warpwind View Post
        I have become confused with melee/brawl combat and it's relationship with number of hands.XD
        .
        Per the rules, you get -1 for non-primary hand actions and -3 on a flurry, with no ability to flurry 2 attacks. right?
        Correct so far.

        Lets say I'm using a sword and a shield, I also flurry attack and defense. So, I get -3 penalty on attack and -4 penalty on defending with a shield?
        No.

        You get -3 on attacks with your mainhand. Assuming you are right handed, and the sword is in your right hand, then that part is correct.

        "Parry" is not an action. So it does not take the offhand penalty nor the flurry penalty.

        Even if it did take the offhand penalty, your Sword Parry (all other factors being equal) is just as good as your Shield Parry, so you could use that instead.

        Taking a Flurry does reduce your Defense by one, however.

        You can't put a Full Defense in a Flurry without a Shield, but even with a Shield you can't Flurry a Full Defense with an Attack. You need specialized magic to do that.

        There is some good news though. Unlike in older WW games (like Exalted 1E), you don't need to Flurry an Attack and Defense. You apply your Defense against every single attack made against you (unless the attack has some way of bypassing Defense). Each attack made against you in a round reduces your Defense by 1 until your next turn -- this is called an Onslaught Penalty. The Full Defense Combat Action simply raises your Defense by 2 (at the cost of 2 Initiative).

        Can I attack with shield and sword simultaneously? couldn't find the rule for that.
        You may not (absent magic which allows you to do so).

        You may Stunt your attacks as using either or both weapons, however.

        Also what if I have 2 swords? can I even attack with both? and what about MA and brawl attack? am I attacking with only 1 fist and have to take -1 to use the other one?
        You may not make two attacks. You only get one attack. Mechanically, you make this attack (under normal circumstances) with your main-hand. You only have to attack with your offhand if you get handcuffed to a lamppost or something.

        You can (and should) fluff your attacks however you like. Mechanically your Unarmed attack is a punch with your main-hand, but you can describe it as a punch with your left hand. Or a headbutt. Or a rapid-fire flurry of spinning kicks and elbow strikes. Or a good old-fashioned right-jab left-jab one-two punch. But you only roll one attack. If you describe your attack, you get a Stunt bonus of two extra dice. If you describe it in a way that everyone playing thinks is cool, you can get bigger bonuses.

        You can Stunt your attacks as using whatever weapons you have Readied (and remember you never have to Ready an unarmed attack). So if you are wielding a giant two-handed golden sword, you can attack with the sword and stunt it as a kick or a headbutt if you like. Or stunt it as throwing your sword into the air, punching your foe in the face with your left fist and kicking them in the shin with your right foot then catching the sword as it comes back down. You still only make one attack roll, using your choice of the statline of the giant sword or the statline of your unarmed attack (whichever you like, I'd take whichever is better).

        You do get a bonus to Clashes if wielding two of the same weapon though. (Unarmed attacks only count as one weapon, no matter how many fists you have though.)


        The other point is about number of limbs. Let's say I take Extra Limb mutation that gets Flurry down to -1. What If I hold a firewand or sword in each or something like that?
        You still only get one attack.

        You also only have one mainhand. Your extra hands are offhands.

        Also it matters what hands your weapons are in now. If your flame piece is in an offhand then your flame piece attacks are at -1.

        But you could, say, hold a two-handed bow in one hand and a sword in the other so you can still Parry.

        Really not having any luck with ctrl F here all I could find was a claim I can't flurry 2 attacks and a sample character that explicitly attacks twice with 2 swords.
        There are many magical effects (such as the Charm Double Attack Technique) which explicitly allow you to make multiple attacks.

        But unless you have such a magical effect, you only get to attack once.

        This is a big change to previous editions of Exalted.

        Actions in Exalted are typically either Combat Actions (like Flurry or Attack), Movement Actions (like Move) or both (like Rush). You may take no more than one Action of the type "Movement Action" on your turn unless that action has a specific exception (e.g. Rush specifically also allows you to take a Move action as well). You may also only take one Combat Action on your turn, but the Flurry Combat Action lets you combine two Combat Actions into a single Combat Action.

        Each round, you may only take each Action once. Attacking uses your "Attack" action, so you can't attack twice, even with a Flurry.

        Some Actions are Reflexive, meaning they are free to take. The Move Action is an example of this. You can only take the Move Action once (because you can only take any Action once; and because you can only take one Action of the Movement type per round), but using it doesn't use your Combat Action. Some powerful Charms allow you to take the Attack Action Reflexively, meaning you can use your Combat Action for something else -- like a Rush. Some VERY powerful Charms allow you to Attack without using your Attack action for the round, allowing you to Attack multiple times.

        If you're confused, that is because the rules are confusing. Keep asking questions until it makes sense.

            Originally posted by Warpwind View Post
            Lets say I'm using a sword and a shield, I also flurry attack and defense.
            This may be the problem right here. You don't need to flurry attack and defense - defenses aren't a separate action, you just get to do them. You might be thinking of the Full Defense action, but that's a separate thing from defending normally - it represents focusing on defense basically completely. You can't normally flurry it. If you have a shield, you can add Full Defense to a flurry, but still not with an attack (it's intended to allow you to do things like charging forward with your shield up).

            Originally posted by Warpwind
            Can I attack with shield and sword simultaneously? couldn't find the rule for that.
            Also what if I have 2 swords? can I even attack with both?
            These are mostly covered by the section on "Dual Wielding", p. 586 (annoyingly, it's tucked away in the gear section, rather than in combat). The basics of it is, using two weapons of any type (which a shield qualifies as, note) doesn't get you extra attacks. It just means you can use the best stats of any weapon you're carrying for your attack and defense and other stuff where that matters, and if you're wielding two identical weapons, you get a +2 bonus when you make a clash attack.

            Originally posted by Warpwind
            and what about MA and brawl attack? am I attacking with only 1 fist and have to take -1 to use the other one?
            Again, this is covered by dual wielding - unarmed is counted as a single weapon, no matter how many limbs you have. So you can stunt your attack as a ferocious series of left and right hooks, finished up with a devastating high kick to the enemy's sternum, and game mechanically, it's all just a single attack with no penalties.

            Originally posted by Warpwind
            The other point is about number of limbs. Let's say I take Extra Limb mutation that gets Flurry down to -1. What If I hold a firewand or sword in each or something like that?
            You still don't get to make multiple attacks. You never really get to make multiple attacks, aside from charms. But you could wield two paired swords in two hands (getting that clash bonus I mentioned above), and have a firewand in your third hand or prehensile tail or whatever, and when someone was at range, you could fire the firewand at them without having to take a Draw/Ready Weapon action to switch from swords to firewand. The flurry penalty reduction is useful when you're doing stuff besides attacking in combat, or in addition to combat. For example, say you're playing a roguish character, and you're in an ancient Solar tomb, where a fight against the animated statue guardians has just broken out. You're picking a lock to give the party an escape route, and you don't want to stop that. On the other hand, you also don't want to be unarmed. Normally, if you wanted to keep working the lock and simultaneously draw your firewand, you'd have to flurry those actions, taking -3 on the lockpicking (Draw/Ready doesn't generally require a roll, but doing it and the lockpicking is still a flurry, and thus imposes the penalty on the action that does require a roll). With the extra arm, you're basically drawing the firewand in your third arm, leaving both your "normal" arms free to keep picking that lock, imposing only a -1 penalty on the lockpicking roll. Later in the fight, the door is still unpicked, but now someone's getting uncomfortably close and you want to shoot some fire at them. You flurry an attack with your miscellaneous action to pick the lock (you can flurry an attack with a non-attack action, as long as it doesn't specifically say you can't), and so you can keep doing both, taking only -1 on the lockpicking and the attack.

                                                        Extra Limbs can be a pretty baller merit. Using a bow you can fire of long shots at range and still have a solid Parry, which is otherwise basically impossible. You can also flurry things like the Loading of a firedust weapon with an attack, which means instead of firing every other turn, you're firing every turn at only a -1 penalty.

                                                        Also, if you're holding a ranged weapon and a melee weapon you can actually just not give a flying fuck about what range band you're in. At Short? Fire the bow. They close into Close (normally a near death sentence for bow users) don't care, you get the sword now.

                                                        That's not even counting all the miscellaneous actions you can do with free hands, like disarming the First Age door lock while flurrying an attack or whatever. Not bad in the clinch where you're backed into a deadend hallway by overwhelming force.


                                                        Shield are also intentionally an aesthetic choice, as opposed to a realistic one. You are penalized for not having a sword in your off hand. If you're wielding a light weapon in your main hand, your off hand can be a medium shield and give you +1 parry, giving half the advantages of a medium weapon, while all the advantages of a light one, for example. It also does give you some benefits, but they're not supposed to be so good that you're an idiot for not taking a shield. You can take just one one-handed weapon and be done if that's what you want your character to look like, and you won't be gimped.
