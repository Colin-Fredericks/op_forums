        Had a friend who was kind enough to let me get a look at the backer PDF, so now things are easier.

        My reading of the Lunar text is the Old Ways are dead. The Bond shattered, and while that freed the Lunars to an extent, it also hurt them. The Bond was replaced with Rage. The text literally talks about them being driven to tear down the things and people that stole what was once theirs. The "never going back" thing sounds more like Lunars as a group are not interested in restoring the Solar Deliberative., rather than the death of the Bond itself.

        So, I'm thinking the Bond is still a thing(especially given that all the dev statements indicates it's staying), but some Lunars either have no Bond, or the Bond is replaced by another mechanic focused around this vengeance/rage angle. It makes sense, really. When the incredibly powerful magical bond that connected you to someone is destroyed, there is a metaphysical hole in your being. Where you once were compelled to love, protect, and guide, now you feel compelled to lash out at those who severed your Bond.

        I can see shades of the same ideas in the other splat examples. The Liminals are drawn to the unquiet dead and told to destroy them. The Abyssals follow the chivalry of death. These both feel like setting explanations for Limit mechanics.

        So, in a few years, we will have a Lunar book, and there will be a Bond, and I will laugh merrily.

                The concept of the Bond going both ways such as to allow the Lunars to temper the judgement and actions of the Solars in much the same as Luna tempers the Unconquered Sun's light and gives Creation repose from it has a lot of potential, in my opinion. It makes Lunars more than the right hands and devoted sidekicks of the Solars where the Bond is applicable. It makes them partners who can question and better their mate, Enkidu to Gilgamesh.

                And it might play rather nicely to Luna's standards and ideals for her Chosen. Lunars were made in the chains of bondage, but she only chooses those with the ability to free themselves. They were made to be the seconds of the Solars, but they can be more. They have the power to become something in Creation that is not defined by the Solars. They already are, which is why many look on the return of the Solars with anxiety. They've changed. For the better or for worse none can say yet, but the fact is they changed without the Solars, and they don't want to be forced to turn back now.

                On a different topic, I thought the Thousand Streams River was an interesting Lunar ideal, even if its execution left something to be desired. I wouldn't mind seeing it return in some form, especially if it adds to Lunars without cheapening anything else in the setting. The Silver Pact isn't a homogenous entity with a singular mind, even if they irrevocably share the objective of bringing down the Realm by definition; so you'd think that some will ponder what to do next after they have achieved their goal, a train of thought from which I can see the Thousand Streams River developing and becoming a factor in the minds of the elders that not all agree with.

                And we really shouldn't already judge everything about Lunars so early when their write-up in the core likely doesn't have everything they're about. The Sidereal write up certainly doesn't specify any of the threats to Fate that they deal with.

                        It occurs to me that the flipside of Rage is Despair.

                        Consider Kratos, of God of War. A man so depressed from accidentally murdering his wife and child that he throws himself into more murderous anger to have a reason to live. And when he has no one left to murder, his despair returns in full force and he tries to commit suicide.

                        Consider the Garou, of Werewolf: The Apocalypse. Gifted with the curse of Luna's supernatural Rage in order to fight an endless battle against the Wyrm to save Gaia, a werewolf who loses hope falls into Hirano, a deep despair that immunizes them against Rage but leaves them without the motivation to do anything, and puts them on Suicide Watch.


                        But just as we don't expect Raging Lunars to be universally furious to the point of insanity, neither does Despair mean a sad Lunar need to be suicidal.

                        Consider Sadness, of Pixar's Inside Out. Her job was never simply to make her person feel sad, miserable, and hopeless. Her job was about sympathy. When someone is feeling sad, others can see the signs of her misery even when the person doesn't know how to communicate them, and they will come to help. But also, your memories of sadness give you the ability to understand the pain of others. Further, if you couldn't become sad about another person's sadness, what would motivate you to make them happy?


                        The Lunars who focus their attention on the Realm, they're angry. They aim to kill, destroy, and take back what is theirs.

                        The Lunars who don't focus on the Realm could very well be sad. They aim to save, to build, and to create a new place removed from all of the fighting.
