                    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
                    I'm not sure how many times it needs to be said that nobody is suggesting blocking DBs' interaction with physical elements, and the DBs have never been portrayed as lacking that interaction. It is increasingly difficult to take you seriously when you just keep polishing this chestnut over and over again. As far as I can tell our disagreement is entirely about degree, and about how those powers should be handled within the system.

                    I mean, I just do not know where you are getting this. Even the one passage you cited as an example of someone making this argument wasn't, and I've never seen it myself. I can totally believe there's like one or two people in the fandom who object strongly to that Earth charm that raises fortifications, or the Archery charm that grows arrows out of a tree or shrub, or the Melee charm where you summon a sword made out of ice and hitting people with it chills them to the bone, or whatever. However, if those are actually the people you object to then I wish you would take it up with them.
                    Actually, I think we're getting somewhere, even with the problem that we're fundamentally discussing subjective impressions of theme and so on, with all the variation in interpretation that that implies. I think that the fact that you're pulling up three charms, rather than a dozen-plus, is evidence of under-representation, but the important part, the upside of the conversation from where I'm sitting, so to speak, would be your feelings on the idea of, while retaining all existing nonphysical charms and adding new ones as felt appropriate, extending the kinds of effects of those examples to all aspects of the charmset - of pulling stone daggers from your anima and throwing them, or of being able to rearrange tree branches at will in survival, for example.

                    I gather that this is the plan, so, despite all this hooplah and my annoyance at handling of community management in regards to it, I'm actually rather positive about 3E Dragonblooded.

                    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
                    This isn't hugely bad, and I'd probably allow it as an unreliable cosmetic thing, but generally the way I would prefer to run it -- and I think the best compromise between elements -- is that the stuff that comes from anima flux is real but has a very short "half-life" and tends to dissolve into loose essence once you leave the area or tamp down your anima. This gives it a physicality without constraining your anima manifestation to "stuff that won't screw up the setting if every same-Aspect DB can do it on demand."

                    The basic problem here is that one doesn't want people using their anima to accomplish this stuff except as a rare neat trick that isn't presumed commonplace, which fits nicely into the stunting model. Even if DBs can do that stuff, doing it through the anima is often not good for the anima. For instance, the Solar anima tends to bleach things, and I would let a Solar stunt bleaching out bloodstains with their anima to evade the notice of police, but I wuld not make this an on-tap "motes for bleach" effect. You buy a charm for that. If it's useful and reliable to use your anima to whip up a bunch of raw materials, or reseed an orchard, or conjure enough water for your group to survive in the desert, that' s warping what the anima is to the point where I think it's lost its appeal as a thing.
                    Dawns can - reliably - make themselves terrifying; Full Moons can - reliably - make themselves stronger. Do you, therefore, see their existence as weakening the appeal of anima displays as a concept?

                                            Which side do you mean? I tend to view the Primordial side as frequently fielding slavering hordes of 1CDs, but on occasion it's just a Primordial and their higher souls themselves, depending on their temperament. Like, I feel that Malfeas-That-Was would have a crazy horde but also possibly be doing his own thing on the other side of the battlefield, for example.

                                            I tend to believe that at least some of the Primordials would think "kill all humans = stop these stupid Exalted from coming back" and thus most mortals were not part of the front lines of the war effort, being kept spread out so as not to be wiped out all at once, or providing logistical support like cooking and keeping secure locations tidy and running arrows to the Exalted archers or whatever.

                                            On the Exalted side, I presume that the DB host was given time to breed and mature several new generations between their creation and the war outbreak, but even if you have ~100,000 Terrestrials, they're going to serve better as squad leaders and junior officers and special forces, with few being the grunts. I don't really see the Exalted side as truly having grunts, in that manner. The DBs remind me of the heroes of the Three Kingdoms Era, to be quite honest.

                                            As to gear, there's no reason to believe that as King of Craftsmen, Autochthon could not produce Artifacts on a faster scale than even the Solar Exalted, or that the Exalted did not have a long time--decades, centuries--prior to the start of hostilities to build up personal panoplies, whether crafted by themselves, Autochthon, the Mountain Folk, or friendly gods who saw it as a social opportunity to serve the Chosen of the Incarnae. Hell, there are probably Solars who performed some act that impressed a Primordial before the War broke out and were rewarded by some eldritch piece of equipment made by their hands - the great irony of that work to be turned against them is terrific story fodder for anything set in that time period, or for the imprisoned demons to remember today.

                                                            A reflection: I think the "modularized" elemental powers from 2e were, on the whole, a good move given that they weren't going to do 3e's method and have a million charms, but I feel like it exacerbated the issue.in some ways. 1e had this issue where a lot of core charms strongly reflected the character of their Aspect element, and this could get a bit weird, since it meant that (e.g.) you naturally got a tornado shield for being sufficiently good at throwing knives, regardless of your Aspect. 2e turned most of these into modularized powers, but the downside of this method was that because you only got a sentence or two to differentiate each of the five versions, it made the elements feel more cosmetic.

Similarly, it's fine if fire aspects can reliably start fires, because it goes well with what is functionally a giant anime battle aura. But a lot of stuff associated with the elements works much less well in that context, even when (as in the bleaching example) it's sometimes an incidental cosmetic effect of a full anima.
