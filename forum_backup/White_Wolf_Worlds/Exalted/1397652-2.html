                    The Shogunate is meant to essentially refer to the whole world as governed by the Dragon Blooded.

                    Just not governed by the Dragon Blooded well. The original portrayal had consistent difficulty keeping subject peoples in line, local daimyos would frequently hold out and fight amongst themselves, and the central government was subject to inconsistent power and frequent assassinations, coups and total collapse.

                    The Realm is more centralised and effectively balances its factions, while exerting power beyond its borders with a more indirect method. Even within the range of satrapies, it definitely controls much less of the world.

                    Still, the Lunars have not so much carved out much of the world for themselves as they often occupy key positions and engage in harassment and sabotage that ultimately limits how far the Realm's authority can extend. Ma-Ha-Suchi's chunk is small, but it's strategically placed to limit access to the Dreaming Sea, a zone that widely evolves devoid of direct Exalted influence.

                    Still, that's probably more emergent than deliberate, when the Silver Pact emphasizes varied local strategies that keep the Realm off balance. There are plenty of Lunars who don't want to cut off parts of the world, they want to make those parts cripplingly expensive to govern and a source of constant internal tension. Better to make the Dynasts destroy one another over the question of who rules in Chiaroscuro than unite them around preventing you from ruling there.

                        I see it as the Shogunate notionally controlling most of Creation.

                        Post Contagion, the Shogunate is gone. The Deebs are forced back to the Blessed Isle and consolidate there. The Lunars, principally, exist in the Threshold. New Lunars are Exalting in the wake of the apocalypse... and they want to rebuild their shattered homes. If they're young enough, they might not even know what a "Shoguate" or even a "Dragon-Blooded" is. Lunar elders are either squabbling with each other, like Ma-Ha and Raksi, or rebuilding their own homes, like Leviathan, or saying "f-this, I'm an owl now".

                        Some are going to strike directly at the Realm. But they're spread throughout a destroyed, post-Apocalyptic world. The Bronze Faction, itself divided by infighting, have few elements of the Vision of Bronze left to defend. The Realm has the Sword of Creation, greatest weapon in the Exalted aresnal to defend itself.

                        In 2E, Dragon-Blooded and Lunars fought side-by-side against the Fair Folk. Perhaps furative envoys seek to turn "ceasefire" into "peace" during this time.

                                            chiming in -

                                            One of thing I like about the current edition of the setting, is that it positions the various factions as made up of individuals. Individuals with beliefs and goals, passionate emotions, and as prone to irrationality, limitations, and sometimes just plain bad luck as anyone. If there was a single hand guiding the Lunars, that had read the sourcebooks and knew the proper strategy to "win" the game, then I could see a Lunar takeover happening. Unfortunately, they were a scattered collection of individuals who had just suffered a unprecedented calamity and most likely had higher priorities than knocking off yet another DB that proclaimed themselves Queen of the World.

                                            And this (IMHO) feels far more organic and realistic, as it is closer to how people (even powerful ones) actually behave, and how events tend to actually unfold. For the most part, we can only tell when the world has changed in hindsight.

                                                        I'm finding myself inclined not to go too far in the direction of Lunars being dismissive of the danger. That would not gel with what the driving purpose of the Silver Pact was, and the experience of its members. It's also plausible enough that there are Lunars in the correct place to hear the stories coming in of how the Blessed Isle is in a state of recovery, how its surviving daimyos and legions are clustering around the ancient manse, and then the news of images of a newly proclaimed monarch appearing all over it. Very ominous rumblings.

                                                        But such a person is also plausibly alone at the time, and the things coming down the wire that make the nascent Realm sound worth attacking are also the kind of thing that make the prospect of attacking it alone seem unviable. That person needs to range around and try and figure out which Lunars are still alive and who can be rallied to try and do something about it first. The time taken to do that is time in which the Realm consolidates itself.

                                                        But yes, there's also the way that reading between the lines of Fangs at the Gate conveys an impression of Lunars at the start of the Realm Era who were... sluggish. You've got older ones that have become weary of the fight, you've got a whole generation that Exalted during the Great Contagion who aren't really invested in struggles for dominion over Creation in the first place. This does contrast significantly with the kind of verve and energy that necessarily goes into the formation of the Realm.

                                                        Ultimately, it is possible to read the inability of the Silver Pact to capitalise on that vulnerable moment and nip something that will prove very dangerous and challenging in the bud. That the moment was theirs and they lacked the initiative to seize it.

                                                        That's okay. It's okay for key moments in the history of the setting to be driven by some group's failures or mistakes. The Exalted are not perfect, not tireless, not fully rational actors even without the interventions of the Great Curse. History as driven by the interplay of success and failure makes the setting more representative of reality.

                                                        The assertion that one circle of Lunars could have held the entire Blessed Isle against all of the opposing Dragon Blooded residing there is still completely absurd, though.

                                                        And pushing even beyond that... the establishment of the Realm is not an immediate thing to be sure, we're even informed in reasonable detail of the successive stages and the fact that it took decades to get truly up and running, but it does still come across as a thing driven by a distinct level of speed, energy, ambition and good decision making. It recalls to me things like the historical context of Napoleon or Alexander the Great; it wasn't just that those people were excellent tactical and strategic planners, it was the fact that they just kept going at it, that there was a determination there that does seem to be uncommon historically (it was certainly uncommon among their contemporaries).

                                                        There's nothing in the nature of the setting that should mean that, in that particular moment, the personality for that kind of rapid mobility was possessed of a Terrestrial Exalt and lacking among the surviving Lunar Exalted (even if perhaps it was something that they'd had in the past and lost; Napoleon was ultimately defeated by his own aging as much as he was by the greater strategic concerns and growing tactical acumen of his opponents).
