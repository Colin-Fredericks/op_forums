                    “I’m Tavi,” you tell her.

                    “What’s that mean?” White Moth raises an eyebrow.

                    “Mean? It means me.”

                    She groans as if in pain. “Look, um, Tavi. If I’m gonna be teaching you, you need to get that words are important. Words have power! A name is a word you give power over you, and the meaning of your name can affect your destiny or get used against you by your enemies! Do you know why you got named Tavi?”

                    “I guess my Mother thought it was a nice sounding name. Certainly better than the pile of words you threw together!”

                    White Moth frowns. “Princess-“

                    “No, seriously. If people can’t remember your name, or pronounce it, then what’s the point of even having one?”

                    “It’s aspirational!” Moth seems to be on the edge of tears. You decide not to press her any further, and to change the subject. To something actually useful.

                    “Sure. Look…right now, your name doesn’t matter, what matters is that this apartment gets clean. Let’s get started.”

                    “Okay.” Moth sits up. “But I have to leave a little after noon, I have a meditation session scheduled with the Master of Nine Black Chains.”

                    “I think we can get the place cleaned up by then,” you declare.

                    And so you do! The big trash basket Gleaming Smile gave you comes in handy. Most of the clutter in the apartment goes right into the basket: bottles and inkstained rags and crumpled paper wrappers from the fried food stand down the street, which White Moth apparently frequents regularly. Moth keeps wanting to throw away other stuff too, valuable stuff: dirty dishes, dirty but perfectly serviceable clothes, empty glass bottles…just talking to her is a little grating. She actually suggests throwing away a full-sized fur winter coat because it will “be out of fashion by next winter anyway.” She gets grumpy when you force her to actually wash her dishes and her clothes.

                    The two of you agree to a demarcation of space, and Moth gives up half her closet for your clothes. You let her keep the bedroll, you’ll make a nest in the corner once you bring your pile of clothes over from your current nest. Maybe someday you'll even be able to afford a bedroll of your own. After a dozen or so trips from your new apartment down the hall to the waste chute to clear out piles of garbage...the place is starting to actually look nice! You take a cruel pleasure in teaching an increasingly whiny Moth how to scrub a floor, something she has obviously never done in her life.

                    By the time you let White Moth leave for her meditation practice, you have a nice clean wooden floor and a clean chamberpot and some new clothes and a stove and a window of your very own…and most importantly, a door you can put between you and the rest of Nexus. Closing the door after White Moth, you put your hands on your hips and survey your apartment with a sense of pride. You’re a little tired from spending all morning cleaning, and you’re more than a little hungry. It’s early afternoon, so Aunt Dwi is probably meeting with her alchemical contact right about now…what should you do next?
                    *****



                    -Take a nap. Now that you think about it, you’re exhausted! You’ve been sleeping on a hard roof for weeks, and your dreams last night kept you from getting much actual rest. Even before last night hunger has frequently interrupted your sleep. Now you can sleep under a roof, which you’re given to understand is much more comfortable. You deserve a rest after all this work!

                    -Go check up on Aunt Dwi. See how the deal went. Unless you make more money you only have this apartment for a couple of months, you need to get out and start hustling to keep your good fortune going! Knowing what your merchandise is going to sell for is important, you need that information to make other decisions!

                    -Go get your stuff from your nest and bring it to your apartment. Your clothes will keep longer if you get them out of the weather, and your River Dragon bits, which are hopefully a source of further income, are probably safer behind a locked door than just sitting on a roof where the birds might get at them.

                    -Go find something to eat! You have a little money, a rare thing for you! You should get over to the market and load up on cheap durable staples like rice and dried mushrooms! If you can’t find a buyer for your river dragon meat…well, it’s pretty well smoked, so it should keep for a while, and if Dragon tastes anything like lizard it’ll be pretty good in one of your new soup pots, so you should get some bamboo and some spices for stew as well, if you have money left over...a pot of soup can last a good long while, if you keep it simmering.

                    -Go set up a bank account. You’re living with a deadbeat and possibly alcoholic roommate now. It's not clear whether she's at all trustworthy! Who knows who else she owes money to in addition to the landlord? You need a safe place to keep your money where she can’t get at it. A bank account with one of the Guild Banks is a way to do just that.

                    -Go talk to Gleaming Smile. Tell him about your arrangement with White Moth, and try to persuade him to ignore the back rent she owes him and let her stay with you above board-at least until she gets her allowance from her rich mother. If he lets her stick around until her mother gives her a pile of cash, he stands a better chance of getting his money back than if she leaves-and if her allowance never arrives, he knows exactly where to find her to get it back the hard way. Worst-case scenario, he doesn't agree and drags he away. It’s not like Moth has earned a lot of your loyalty, here, and you don’t want to risk losing your new home by angering your landlord if she gets caught on her way in or out over the next couple of months.

                    -Go spend some time with Grinning Eel. Even if the River Dragon thing pans out, that’s just a one-time infusion of money. Nightside is pretty close to a major canal. A stable career like working a canal boat would give you a reliable way to keep a roof over your head and food in your cupboards, and getting that apprenticeship with Grinning Eel started as soon as possible, before he forgets about his deal with you or changes his mind, is the only lead you actually currently have on steady work.

                    -Go do...something else?

                                                Go to the Bank: 1
                                                Visit Dwi First: 4

                                                You decide to head back to the Flaming Peacock to talk to Aunt Dwi. If she’s gonna be paying you, you might as well have all the money when you go to open up an account with one of the Guild banks. You leave your apartment with more than a little reluctance, but locking the door behind you feels really, really good. You have a place that’s yours!

                                                It’s a bit of a hike back to the Flaming Peacock-now that it’s mid afternoon, traffic in the streets is much busier. You save yourself some time by cutting through a few alleys and taking some of the rooftop-ways, but still you find yourself panting a bit by the time you arrive at the brothel. You tell Ox at the front door that you’re here to see Dwi and he nods and tells you you’re expected-but to keep quiet and take the left-side stairs behind the beaded curtain right after you go inside. It’s still early, but there are now paying customers in the building-local merchants springing for a quick bit of fun on their late lunch breaks, mostly, and a few sailors in town for a couple days while their ship gets loaded who have now woken after last night’s debauchery.

                                                As you climb the stairs toward Dwi’s office, the smells of the Flaming Peacock assail you with familiarity and with old memories. Incense and sweat and the cloying scent of opium pipes and sex and wine. It reminds you so much of Mother…You knock politely, and Dwi calls you into her office. Her makeup has been reapplied, and she looks like a gleaming porcelain doll. Also, she’s grinning from ear to ear.

                                                “Did you know, Tavi, that one can grind a River Dragon tooth into a powder that you can mix with some other herbs to make a tea to increase strength and vitality? Apparently it’s very useful if you own some gladiators and you want them to bulk up quickly and get very aggressive…although apparently some folks just prefer to use it as an aphrodisiac.”

                                                “No, Aunt Dwi. I didn’t know that.” You smile. That sounds promising.

                                                “And did you know that you can pickle a River Dragon’s eye in Malfean honey and drink it to gain the power to see invisible spirits for five days and five nights?”

                                                “That actually sounds really neat. It’s too bad we don’t have any Malfean honey. So…your alchemist is going to buy them, then?”

                                                “Yes! Apparently a lot of the other people who helped butcher the thing with you last night had the same idea as you did, so we’re not going to get too much for the teeth…but the eye was a good pick, there’s only one more of those out there so the increased supply won't mess with the demand. I…ahem. I made very sure the apprentice offered us a fair price for it.” Dwi looks as smug as a cat who has just eaten somebody's pet bird.

                                                “I like the sound of that, Aunt Dwi.”

                                                “I’ll bet you do! It’s a shame you didn’t get your hands on its tongue or its liver! Apparently if you can smoke the tongue over a sacred brazier under the right constellations, you can eat it and gain the power to talk to birds. And if you bathe in a concoction that includes the bile from the liver, it makes your skin as hard as River Dragon scales so blades can’t harm you. That one would sell really well.”

                                                “…interesting.” You manage to maintain your composure. Is it possible that the organ you have back at your nest is a liver rather than a spleen? You don’t really know River Dragon anatomy all that well.

                                                “Alchemy is fascinating.” Dwi nods. “Although in my experience, alchemists are more so. I haven’t collected yet, but I’m sure Blue Root will pay up. So here’s your fifty percent. We can go shopping for your clothes once I actually get paid.”

                                                Aunt Dwi hands you a cheque, written in her careful hand upon thick and ornately decorated Guild stock. You look at it. You…can’t actually read, so you’re not sure what it says, but…

                                                Dwi seems to recognize your problem. She smiles sweetly. “Your share is 25 Dinar.”

                                                Your eyes get big. That’s…that’s more than a year of rent!

                                                “My advice, Tavi? Go set up a bank account with the Guild. That way you’ll earn some interest on it. And don’t spend it all in one place!”

                                                “I don’t know that I could spend 25 Dinar all in one place if I wanted to, Aunt Dwi!”

                                                “It seems like a lot of money now, child…but you’d be surprised how fast 25 Dinar can go, when you’re buying food and clothes and real medicine. And especially when you need to buy coal and oil in the winter. Be frugal.”

                                                You nod, stupefied.

                                                “This is a cheque drawn on my account with the Guild. It’s made out to you by name. This way you don’t have to carry all that coin on you. But don’t lose it or get it stolen, child.”

                                                “Of course not! I’ll head over to the Guild district right away and put it in account!”

                                                “Good. I recommend you try the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants, their interest rates are good and they’ve been around a long time so you know they’re not going to invest all your money on a risky caravan route and go belly up. And you might want to put most of it into a sealed account without cheques so you get a better interest rate on it and you don’t have the option to spend it without a penalty. That way you have to save it for when you really need it. Wish I'd set one of those up back when I was your age...”

                                                You nod, and smile, and shake Dwi’s hand, and you head down the stairs feeling lightheaded.

                                                Twenty five Dinar! That’s food for months! That’s enough to buy a small herd of goats, or a few milk cows! That’s enough to buy a bow, or a really finely forged sword! That’s enough for all the weaving supplies you could ask for! For that much money you could buy a cart and a permit and start your own business in one of the street markets selling baskets or carpets or something! Or you could buy a big coop and a bunch of chickens and start a rooftop farm and sell fresh eggs to all the market stalls, and pay Gleaming Smile to let you use the roof! That’s enough money to hire some people to work for you for a few weeks!

                                                Wandering through the crowded street markets, you don’t notice the smell of sweetrolls or frying meats, or the sound of sizzling noodles, all things that would normally fill you with irrepressible longing. You are so distracted by your thoughts of what you could do with all that money, in fact, that you turn into a shortcut alley on the way to the Guild district and run right into the Leopard Gang. You stop just short of physically bumping into their leader, Seven Leopard. He’s holding a middle aged and balding man up against the wall of the alley by the front of his shirt, and holding a curved knife to the man’s gut. The man is sweating profusely and rustling in his purse with one hand, a desperate expression on his face.

                                                “Hey, kid.” Seven Leopard looks lazily over at you, without letting go or easing up on his knife. “How’s it goin’? Don’t see you much over on this side of the market.”

                                                You bow deeply. Seven Leopard likes that sort of thing. He thinks of himself as a criminal with class.

                                                “I am doing well, Seven Leopard. It’s an honor to see you at work.”

                                                Several of Lepoard’s thugs smirk or chuckle. There are six of them. From time to time these guys shake down you or the other urchins who operate in the market-in exchange for being allowed to operate in “their territory,” they take a cut of whatever gains you make from pickpocketing or begging or stealing in the market. They don't usually do more than rough you up a bit-most of them were street kids themselves before they got big, and they tend to go easy during the winter. Rat tells you he's done some lookout work for them, and they treat you okay as long as you're working for them and Seven Leopard is around to keep order.

                                                “Just keepin’ the neighborhood safe.” Seven Leopard smiles. “You see this bastard here? He’s been selling Qat. Not the good stuff, though. Nasty shit. Got a friend of mine real sick. Guy stops eating, starts getting real bad stomach pains. Then he goes all nuts and starts tryin' to paint all the walls he can find, but he doesn’t have any paint so he starts stealing peanut sauce from the market and getting it all over anything he can reach. Nearly bit my nose off when we tried to calm him down. Just doesn’t seem right to do that sorta thing, not without givin’ the neighborhood some sort of compensation, does it?”

                                                “Not at all, Seven Leopard.” You nod.

                                                “Yeah, it’s just not the kind of business we want around here.” Leopard pokes the point of his knife into the sweating merchant’s gut. “Tell me, boy, what do you think we oughta do with garbage like this?”

                                                The Leopard Gang is smirking and sniggering…and apparently waiting for your answer.

                                                What should you say?


                                                -You should kill him. He doesn’t matter much, but it’ll send a warning to the other garbage like him.

                                                -You should take the profits he made from selling his Qat. No need to hurt him, since nobody really got hurt, but he shouldn’t get any benefit out of selling inferior product in your market.

                                                -You should mark his face, so people know not to mess with the Leopard Gang. That’s what that big curved knife is for, right? Leaving impressive scars?

                                                -You should get him to give you the name of his supplier. This guy’s just the middleman-if you really wanna protect the neighborhood, Leopard, you need to track down his source, or you’re just gonna get somebody else selling the same shit here tomorrow.

                                                -You should let the poor bastard go. If we rough up all the merchants selling inferior goods around here…well, soon we won’t have any merchants left. If you have good stuff, you sell it in the rich parts of town. If you scare away all the guys doing business in your neighborhood, Leopard, the money will dry up, and that's no good for anybody.

                                                -Throw him in the canal. What else would we do with garbage?

                                                -Make a public example of him. Take him out in the market and make him apologize to everyone for selling his tainted qat.

                                                -Look, Leopard, you already scared him shitless. Just let the poor bastard go home. He’s just trying to make ends meet, just like the rest of us.

                                                -Don’t say anything, just pull out your knife and stab the bald guy yourself. You know from experience the Leopard Gang is gonna shake you down next, and showing a bit of killer instinct will make them think twice-and hey, impressing them isn't a bad idea. You might need to hire some muscle a little ways down the road...

                                                -Don't say anything, just run. You know they'll shake you down next, but you can probably lose them in the crowded market, and they probably won't be eager to chase a street urchin who's likely to only have a pocketful of food or stolen coin when they have a bigger fish in hand already.

                                                -Something else?
