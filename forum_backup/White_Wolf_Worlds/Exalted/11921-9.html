        A Deathknight known as the Jester On The Wings Of Night has gone rogue. Stealing one of the most potent Artifacts of his master - and, indeed, of all the Underworld - he makes his way to Lookshy, where he simply drops it. The Jester did not rebel out of moral outrage, or even a desire for personal freedom, for he believes in neither. Rather, he is profoundly bored, and wants to see what a resident of Creation would do with the greatest powers of death.

        The one to find it is a young Air Aspect - Amilar Yu-Ming. A child of privilege, Yu-Ming realizes that he now holds the power to slay almost anyone in Creation, so long as he has seen them, and rather than being horrified or running amok as the Jester might have expected, seeks to end the sinful ways of the River Province (and eventually, the world) with tactically considered murder.

                    The Princess Magnificent has gained her freedom from the First and Forsaken Lion, and has begun her own conquest of Creation. A Sidereal sage prophesies that a baby girl, born with within the Princess' domain, and bearing the sign of Saturn upon her brow will be the final doom of the Deathlord. Fearful of the Sidereal's prophetic powers, the Princess orders that all pregnant women be housed within her dungeons, until they give birth and their children can be examined. When the prophesied child is born, the desperate midwife smuggles the infant from the citadel, sending it downriver before being slain. The child ends up in the hands of an aspiring djala sorcerer, who tries to find the child a home among her own kind. Shenanigans ensue as the djala encounters a half-mad Dawn-caste Solar, encounters some Fae, and battles with the Princess's Dusk-caste General and Moonshadow-caste daughter. In the end, the Princess captures the infant and begins a Necromantic ritual to cast the child, body and soul, into Oblivion. The djala's slight-of-hand tricks cause the ritual to back-fire, destroying the Princess forever.

Rescued by a high ranking Guild member, the Exalt is trained to use his powers, before he is let loose on the trail of those who destroyed his home. Turns out the bandit leader was once in the Guild and has his older sister (who is now a Sidereal), and she points the Exalt to a plot for some jerk called "The Onyx Dragon" to get this immensely powerful Infernal Daiklaive that was once his, but was stolen from him by someone named 'Scarlet' of whom the Exalt is a direct descendant (through his mother, who is also an Exalt, and is also inexplicably alive). Only one of his bloodline can release the Sword, so the "Onyx Dragon" cuts hismom's throat above the sword, getting it back. The Exalt beats him up and seals him in another dimension (which is apparently where he was from to begin with), and may or may not have kept the sword.

                            But he totally didn't, because keeping it means that he would have had to kill his sister, who may have been the evil mastermind behind the plot of the sequel (which takes place an undisclosed number of centuries in the future, when the blood of the Dragons has been watered down a TON), and but proved that she was actually just a person with sufficient foresight and willing to make the hard choices for the good of the world in the next sequel.                                         In the First Age, there was a great Twilight sorcerer-king who ruled a mighty nation with the help of a council of twelve other Exalts. After his death, the Council tracked his next incarnation to a young girl. The new Exalt had no experience in statecraft, and so the Council ruled as a regency while she grew up. The Council, however, proved to be corrupt, grievously misruling their subjects in their sorcerer-queen's name. The queen herself came to believe that the solution to her problems lay in Sorcery, and so, while the Council grew decadent, she labored on a sorcerous working of terrible power. After many years, her craft was completed, and she unleashed it on her kingdom.

                                        Tragically, her spell was fundamentally flawed: Her kingdom was torn from the fabric of Creation and plunged into the Labyrinth. The queen could only watch in dismay as the necrotic energies of that place twisted her subjects into horrible monsters and drove them to kill each other. Not even the Council could stand against such horrors, and soon the Twilight was left to wander her former kingdom alone. This strange portion of the Neverborn's dreams proved an apt reflection of the Void, for once the monsters that had been her subjects died, no new creatures came to replace them. All that was left was silence and emptiness, and, with no track of time, the queen did not even notice when she died. She lingered as a ghost, mourning her lost kingdom and regretting her foolish hubris, until she finally succumbed to the whispers of the Neverborn.

                                        In the Time of Tumult, there was a noted Patrician thaumaturge of good Breeding who had recently joined the Wyld Hunt. Though he had never Exalted himself, his distinguished lineage still gave him some degree of influence. His unit was given a village where a Lunar Anathema was predicted to arise and ordered to prevent its birth by any means necessary. The Patrician's commander, knowing the cunning deceptions the Lunars were capable of, ordered the village burned and all its inhabitants put to the sword. The Patrician was a good Immaculate, but he could not bring himself to do it. While the Hunt carried out its massacre, he managed to smuggle a single young girl to safety. It was she, he later learned, who had been destined to become Anathema.

                                        The Patrician's actions brought inconceivable dishonor to his family. He was dragged back to the Blessed Isle in chains and forced to watch his House disown him and his immediate family suffer for his transgression. With a combination of luck and desperation, he managed to escape immediate execution, but he could not escape his own knowledge of what he'd done. Finally, the shame became too much to bear, and he resolved to end it all. Yet as the murky waters filled his lungs, he heard the voice of a woman long dead. She, too, had seen disaster spring from the best of intentions. She, too, knew how cruel the world could be. Together, they would rise up and smash the petty, rotted Creation that had perverted their goals. And so the Patrician became Exalted after all.

                                        In the present day, the world lives in fear of a great Abyssal necromancer. All who dare to oppose him have their souls mutilated by his death-magics, reduced to pitiful mockeries of human beings. Yet there are still places free of the necromancer's touch: Small pockets of reality that, even after an entire Age, survive in the ocean of the Wyld. From one of these pockets, a boy emerges. Guided by a Raksha companion, he crosses the Wyld and enters Creation proper. He wields great magics, and in his soul he bears a special Exaltation: That of the necromancer's Lunar mate! He has the power to undo the Abyssal's magics, and he believes that defeating the necromancer is the key to saving his mother's soul. As the Deathlord exerts increasing pressure on her pawn, the world moves closer to a mighty confrontation...

                                                Sleek Lone Pi is the assistant director for ecology of a small kingdom. She is an active, type-A personality who looks up to the powerful female leaders in the Deliberative. Her boss is a staid Twilight known for both a disregard of civic duties and a keen hand at crafting. Sleek assembles a group of friends to work in the ecology department, including her best friend, a Water DB healer; a hirsute slacker Lunar with a talent for music; an entrepreneurial Night caste; and a morose Abyssal. Together they repel attacks from demonic raccoons and try to beautify the kingdom. However, the kingdom’s finances have not been managed well, and soon the Deliberative sends two Eclipses to oversee the running of the kingdom. The elder Eclipse is friendly and cheerful to fault as he always delivers bad news with a big, shit-eating grin. The younger Eclipse is quiet and has a penchant for the arcane. The elder Eclipse has an on again, off again relationship with the DB, and the younger Eclipse falls in love with Sleek Lone Pi. Eventually, the junior Eclipse is appointed chancellor of the kingdom. Sleek Lone Pi herself tries her hand at the governing council, but is chased off by an angry mob when she engineers the conquest of a nearby kingdom. She returns to her role as assistant director and lives happily ever after with the young Eclipse.
