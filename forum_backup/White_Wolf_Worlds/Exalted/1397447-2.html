                                            06-20-2020, 11:51 AM
                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                            Narratively, I have to assume that the Usurpation runs partially on the dramatic conceit of many of the Solars being arrogant enough to presume themselves totally secure, as has been the downfall of many a tyrant in myth, fiction and more than a couple in real life. The resources are there to make the conclusion (although less extreme than some might describe them as), but they're not being applied. That includes the occasion in which a Solar becomes aware of some plotting and then laughs it off as an absurdity.

                                            The other part is an assumption that the large numbers aren't the part that is important for the planning, but the consolidation when things go down. When word comes down the wire that the honour guard of the Calibration Feast have launched an attack and key positions are being seized all over, it comes down to you to decide which side you're going to pledge yourself to. It is entirely plausible to see this play out in favour of setting consistency as "group loyalty, growing disillusion with the Solars, personal benefit and a healthy dose of fear (both for my fellows if they win and I didn't support and for the Solars who might not give me the chance to pledge loyalty) are enough to make me independently take steps in favour of the Usurpation".
                                            I agree, and going a little further, I would think we can divide the Usurpation into three stages:

                                            (1) Planning and surgical strike. Here numbers need to be low, because the utmost secrecy is required. Here the Sidereal's fate-manipulation powers are probably doing the most work, to counteract the Solar's supernatural investigation / intentions-reading abilities. Because this attack has the advantage of complete surprise, and enough planning to stockpile whatever weapons are suitable for destruction on this scale, numbers are not actually needed. One could imagine that the number of Exalts who are fully in on the conspiracy is less than the number of Solars + Lunars, although one could also imagine that Sidereal magic could conceal a far larger cabal.

                                            (2) The immediate aftermath. Whichever Solars survived the initial assault are on the back foot, but whatever plans the Sidereals and DBs made are quickly going to turn chaotic as well. The Solars are going to be places the usurpers didn't expect them to be, and demonstrate powers the usurpers didn't expect them to have. They are still Solars. Here is where outnumbering the enemy, to deal with these unpredictabilities, will be very helpful for the Bronze Faction side. At this point, it's crucial for them to convince DBs who weren't involved in the initial scheming to take their side over the Solars. I'm imagining a typical scenario involves a Solar who was known not to be at the Calibration Feast for whatever reason having a Sidereal-led assassination team sent against her. She survives the initial assault and kills sum of the assassins, but their backup plan is to rally whichever Dragonblooded are nearby - most likely the Solar's own (hopefully mistreated) subordinates - for a second strike. However the full 100 to 1 numerical advantage is not being used here - more likely 5 to 1, like a modern Wyld Hunt, or 1 Sidereal + 1 DB in on the plotting + 7 of the Solar's personal guard vs. 1 Solar + 3 of the Solar's personal guard.

                                            (3) The days, weeks, years, and decades after. At this point, the surviving Solars will have had time to consolidate bases of power loyal to them - it will be hard to get them alone. They have armies, and time to figure out where enemy armies are amassing against them. Thus they can start making use of powerful magic like Rain of Doom, God-King's Shrike, powerful artifacts the Sidereals did not steal or destroy, and so on. At this point I would imagine almost every combat-capable Dragonblooded in Creation will end up choosing a side (although Creation at this time was even bigger than it is now so maybe not) and a supermajority, though not quite all, will favor the Usurpers.

                                            Here the DB's greater numbers are clearly necessary for victory - one can't defeat the Solars in a fair fight on even footing - but their drastic numerical advantage, which is now many hundreds to one (though less vs. lunars) is probably not. They could have made do with far less. Each Solar is still only one person, and I think for this period the Jade Prison is still accepting new guests, so they don't reincarnate. To defeat a Solar, the Dragonblooded only have to get them outnumbered, depending on the Solar, somewhere between two-to-one and ten-to-one. The Solars can try to stop this by hiding in fortresses and surrounding themselves with armies, but the Usurpers have access to many artifacts and sourcerous workings themselves, and can use them to devastating effect. There is also the possibility of lesser betrayals by previously loyal troops, perhaps set off by nasty instances of the Great Curse. Because this war goes on for decades, and most of the Solars are already dead, we only need a kill rate of something like 2 per year here. A lot can go wrong!

                                            The Sidereals, meanwhile, are reincarnating as normal, and new DBs are constantly growing up - for the first 15-20 years, at exactly the normal rate, and then probably somewhat less for the remainder of the war.

                                                I wrote a long post and accidentally deleted it, so I'll just hit the high points. Apologies if I don't go into detail or phrase things carefully.

                                                1) Essence 6+ First Age Solar Charms are basically the same sort of thing as the Essence 6+ Lunar Charms in Fangs at the Gate.

                                                2) In Third Edition, the First Age didn't mollycoddle its Exalts. Solars died while diving head-first into the same perils as the Exalted of the Age of Sorrows: wars and duels, monsters and natural disasters, exploring ancient cities and alien realms, etc. Veterans of the early days of the First Age would have been rare; most would get themselves killed after centuries, decades, or only years after Exaltation.

                                                3) Third Edition's history speaks of a Second Deliberative, not a Solar Deliberative. Solars were powerful, prestigious, and overrepresented in the Deliberative, but not every other Exalt owed them fealty.

                                                4) The Usurpation happened in the game's history in every edition. Unless you want to run a game set during or immediately after the Usurpation, or you want to run an alternate-history game, a deep dive into exactly what happened at the Calibration Feast is probably not the best use of your time.
