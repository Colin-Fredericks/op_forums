    You never actually use the word “stubborn” when talking about Earth Aspects. Impressive.

    So, the failure modes for the aspects are: dreamer out of touch with reality, stubborn reactionary jerk, spoiled brat, chronic backstabbing syndrome, and free love hippie?

    My essence is a fever and the only cure is more Cowbell.

    The prestige spread puts a pleasant face on empire, but doesn’t avoid mentioning some uncomfortable aspects. Which is probably about right.

    Lookshy’s entry is about as accurate, isn’t it?

    So, how will they know the worthy shogun when she arrives? Is there a Daiklave in a rock somewhere? Also, isn’t the Shogun just the military commander? So, then, isn’t the person with the biggest army and best tactics automatically the rightful Shogun?

    I am not familiar with a meaning of “tempered” that includes “squandered on petty pissing matches.”

    So, is Prasad well covered in this book, or should we be expecting a Stretch Goal for more Prasad in the companion?

    This feels a lot like India to me, which is intentional, I’m sure.

    Visit exotic places! Meet interesting people! Conquer one and kill the other!

    I assume that the Realm couldn’t project enough force to actually bring this place into the full and proper fold. I find myself wondering how Chejop feels about this place.

    This has been your reminder that the Forest Witches are hella weird.

    ...Yeah, being an Outcaste is kinda nice. No one is going to call a Wyld Hunt on you, but you’ve got enough of an edge to handle most people that might come your way. The real problem is when you’re forced to fight outside your weight class and you probably will be.

    Well, you also don’t usually get to recline on a jade pleasure couch in between snorting heroin mixed with gold dust off a beautiful slave’s taut stomach like they do in the Realm, but you can’t have everything.

                    Chapter 2

                    ...I have an Eclipse Caste merchant PC who wishes she was that awesome. Fortunately, she has a Dawn Caste bodyguard who can be that awesome for her.

                    What does failure look like for a house? Tepet?

                    Never have the parallels between the Shogunate and the modern Realm been more clear. I suppose Lookshy might claim that this will “temper” the Realm into something better.

                    I feel like we get a better sense of motion here, with the Realm as a dynamic entity. Just because there are ten-ish houses now, doesn’t mean there always will be and certainly doesn’t mean there always were. Even the fact that Households can rise in importance or fade away is a very useful note.

                    Discussing the privileges and duties of the House Matriarch is useful here. One has to wonder what happens when a Matriarch becomes selfish or indolent. Well, ok, I’m sure they get deposed sooner or later or the House falls apart, but I doubt it happens quickly and the lead up to it should be pretty interesting. Why is the house Matriarch doing this? Can she hide it successfully? Are other family members hiding her weaknesses and, if so, how long can they keep doing it or are they sharpening the knives?

                    Good note on names.

                    Good info on land holdings.

                    I wonder just how badly you have to screw up to have the Matriarch take away your artifact. Yes, it says they mostly don’t do that, but there’s gotta be a point where they at least threaten to take it away.

                    Maybe call it House Heraldry?

                                    Hm! This is pretty interesting. Very evocative and brings forward the “intellectual house” in a whole new way. Bringing out the whole Monster Hunter aspect that they have seems good.

                                    I like it.

                                    .....and of course disagreements over resources are causing problems here too.

                                    I gotta ask, is the DB great curse that fellowships eventually break down and turn on themselves or is that just human nature?

                                    Why do people marry into this house? Because they have a bookish son they can’t otherwise get rid of? Because they want to show support for the Realm in general? Because they have an Anathema problem they need solved? Because they need to know something that Ledaal knows?

                                    Ok, I bet that, just cause they focus on monsters, doesn’t mean they don’t play politics at all. They have to have resources and satrapies and people in positions of authority and everything, so marrying in for all that jazz is also reasonable. Especially if the Empress regarded them as highly dependable and gave them choice places to exploit as a result.

                                    Still, I’m not surprised they’re scrambling for alliances now, just to make sure the Realm doesn’t fall apart.

                                    So, when her mother consorted with Anathema to try to take over the world, she just went and killed her. That’s pretty impressive and kinda sad.

                                    I am now imagining Batman as a Ledaal. Or at least one of those brooding demon hunters in the long cloak and crossbow.

                                    ....ok, I grant that there is a point here. Going after Thorns or retaking the Caul or dealing the deathblow to the Bull and his forces or going after Perfect Soul’s rogue satrapy or any of several hundred other problems... these are all existential threats to the Realm as a whole and they’re all only getting worse. Another issue is that the House doesn’t have our omniscient view of everything, so one important member of the house dismisses half the reports out of Thorns as obvious exaggeration while another one thinks they’re underestimating the problem (and really both are technically correct) so they have different ideas about the needs of the situation, while a third agrees with the second, but thinks that Thorns is a lost cause without a unified Realm and ....yeah, ok. I can see why they’re in a pickle and why the house leaders are pulling in different directions and making the problem worse.

                                    So many irons in the fire.

                                    Ledaal allying with Mnemon would be bad. For anyone who isn’t the Realm.

                                    So, they’ve developed a potion to temporarily boost a DB’s Essence to 5 (or maybe even 6) ...but anyone who takes it will lose 50 to 100 years of life at minimum and they’ll likely be crazy for the duration and impotent afterwards. And this is the best case scenario. Finding test subjects for that must have been ...complicated.

                                    ......I would know more of Perch and it’s Hungry Ghost Sphinxes.

                                    Ledaal and Rowena Ravenclaw had a lot in common it sounds like.

                                    I clearly need to read the Ragara section now.

                                    Nice twist on Ledaal Kebok Coren’s ancestry reveal. I could see the house ignoring that in this time of need ...and then quietly assassinating her afterwards.

                                            ​Right?! This kind of strategic situation, where they're faced with a number of objectives, several contradictory, all critically important, and none with an easy answer about how to fulfil, I think that's genius for the complexity and compelling character motivation it presents. There's a concrete basis here for Realm politics, whether one is playing a Ledaal or having them as supporting or antagonistic characters, or even just in the background of a chronicle; characters having specific goals that they need to commit to and convince others of, even though it can represent a risk or a gamble or a sacrifice.

                                            ​The idea of it as possible background noise particularly interesting to me, because of how it makes the setting feel alive; logically, Ledaal is going to do something whether the PCs are involved or not, so one can generate setting texture or potential plot hooks just from being at the margins of whatever they do. You could play a Realm game focused entirely on the interplay between three other Houses that are barely connected to Ledaal, or some other group adventuring through the Scavenger Lands, and then boom, unilateral Ledaal crusade against Thorns becomes something that is... mentioned, and it alternately serves as an interesting piece of noise or event to generate further drama.
