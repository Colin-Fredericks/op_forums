I love the way combat goes overall (along with most of Ex3 in general), I guess I just find it weird that there's all these mechanics for combat movement, but no good way to go from melee range to a withdraw escape without going into the athletics sub-system. Test of Speed was my next guess, but it feels strange to me to have to do that in order to get use Withdraw (or just ignore Withdraw completely in favor of a Test of Speed escape).
                                            Other options are to use stealth to duck behind a building or lose yourself in a crowd, to move through an area of difficult terrain that requires an ability check than you can make but your opponent can't (imagine one of those scenes where characters acrobatically leap through market stalls while the guards pursuing them crash into them), or diving through an environmental hazard that your opponent is unwilling to follow you through (such as a raging fire). You could also attempt a gambit to create difficult or hazardous terrain (throwing caltrops behind you or spilling a pot of oil and igniting it with a lamp) or one to stun or knock back a pursuing opponent (maybe you push a tree branch out of your way and as you pass it snaps back and strikes your pursuer). Basically, think Assassin's Creed. If you've played any of the recent games, you'll notice it's really difficult to get away from opponents just by outrunning them, you need to outsmart them too.

                                            As others have mentioned, there's also a number of charms in Athletics, Dodge and the combat abilities that allow you to move further, move in ways your opponent can't follow, or knock opponents back.

                                                    Originally posted by prototype00 View Post

                                                    prototype00
                                                    My reading of it is that you can still only make one social influence action per turn. So you could take any combat action and still make a social influence action without a flurry, or you could take a combat action with any other non-social-influence action as a flurry and still make a social social influence action, but you couldn't flurry a combat action with a social influence action and still make another social influence action. I'm basing this reading primarily on the logic that you only have so much time to communicate in the space of one turn, and also the fact that even with a flurry, you usually can't use the same action more than once in a round. The benefit of Poised Lion Attitude is that you're never having to choose between taking combat actions and using social influence.
