    Originally posted by LVTHN View Post
    If I take an Aim action before making a multi-attack via Charm, does the Aim bonus apply to each attack in the multi-attack (against the chosen target), or only the first?
    I'd default to the same rule of thumb that I use for supplemental Charms: If you're rolling a single attack and applying the result to multiple targets a la Arrow Storm Technique, the Aim bonus applies to "all" attacks. If you're directing multiple attacks at the same target, then I'd say only the first gets the Aim bonus. That being said, if you're making an attack from stealth before combat begins in earnest (i.e. you have all the time in the world to aim beforehand), I'd let the Aim bonus apply to all attacks, though at that point you probably don't need the extra help.

                This probably isn't the place for it, but all this talk of a straight-forward daiklave that does nothing but cut things really well demanded a very specific response.

                Straight Sword (Artifact 5)
                Once, there was a Copper Spider who lost a dread gamble with the god of such things, and for his wager gave up every last ounce of his bountiful inspiration. While his skills in the forge remained peerless, and his love for Creation and all its wonders were left untouched, he became listless. Though he admired, he did not desire, and though he reasoned, he could not wonder. After three such days of calamitous idleness, his many friends and advisors sprung into action. One by one, they guided him through introductions to fellow masters of differing arts, hoping that the greatest of performers, painters, sculptors, and playwrights might ignite the potential still within his soul.

                This accomplished very little in itself, for his absent spirit was poor audience to all the arts of expression. As it happened, however, he encountered a beggar on the side of the street on his way to a theater. This was a foul-smelling and ill-kept woman, who his Sun-blessed eyes could not help but notice. Underneath the grime and rotten clothes was a body wiry and strong, old but coiled like a spring with her capacity for violence. Though he was still without inspiration, he was not without curiosity. He asked her, "I can see that you are a gifted soldier. How is it that you have come to live as a beggar, when you could lead an army or have your pick of pupils?" Being quite thoroughly.drunk, her answer of slurred profanities was beyond even celestial record and is lost to time. Still, his Sun-blessed ears heard the truth of it. She had made an enemy of her ignorance and having slain that final foe, lost all desire to pursue a life of violence. Before him was an unmatched master of Cutting.

                In the hollow eyes of this uncaring master, the listless Twilight found a reflection of himself. She seemed so free of want, that he wanted for her. He reached out to take her soul and forge it into a peerless blade that would preserve her skills and her teachings forever, but as he tried, she cut him in half. After relieving herself on the street corner, she then left town. This was her sword:

                Unlike most daiklaves, the Straight Sword is a mere six handspans long and fit for mortal hands that can bear the unexceptional weight of its steel and starmetal construction. All who take the up this sword treat it as a Medium Artifact weapon and benefit from its first evocation, regardless of attunement, but the evocations that follow are exclusive to Solar and Sidereal Exalted who commit the customary five motes. Despite this versatility and its apparent ease of use, no wielder has ever discovered Straight Sword except for those who are not swordsmen: a character with a Melee rating greater than 1 may not take this artifact as a background and never obtains Straight Sword in the course of play.

                Fifth Mastery
                Cost: --; Mins: Essence 1
                Type: Permanent
                Keywords: None

                The finest gift of a sword is the opportunity not to use it. A wielder who takes up the Straight Sword finds it impossible to spend experience points to raise their Melee score or attain Melee specialties. Instead, every time they raise a different ability from zero to the current rating of their Melee score, they are faced with a choice. They may benefit from a free increase for their Melee score, or cast aside the Straight Sword. For example, a wielder with a Melee score of 2 who purchases Craft (Paper Making) from 0 to 1 and from 1 to 2 would be allowed to raise their Melee score to 3 for free. They would not be eligible to raise their Melee score to 4 until they raised another ability from 0 to 3, such as Linguistics or Craft (Architecture). Once their Melee score is raised to 5, the wielder attains a free specialty: "Cutting +1", restrictions upon their experience purchases cease, and they may no longer discard Straight Sword. Destiny conspires against them, should they try.

                Ninth Argument
                Cost: --; Mins: Melee 5, Essence 1
                Type: Permanent
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Permanent
                Prerequisites: Fifth Mastery

                The wielder of the Straight Sword attains an intimacy, "Tie: Cutting Down My Opponent", which may be of any strength but can never be lost. When this intimacy is used in a combat stunt or during the activation of a charm, add its strength (2 for a minor, 3 for a major, and 4 for a defining) to the dicepool as a non-charm dice bonus.

                Third Precept
                Cost: 4 motes, Mins: Essence 2
                Type: Reflexive
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Scene
                Prerequisites: Ninth Argument

                So long as her grip on the Straight Sword remains loose and unstrained, so long as her breathing is steady, and so long as an opponent is before her, the wielder of Straight Sword is unconcerned with the vagaries of circumstance. While wielding Straight Sword and within Short or Close range of an enemy combatant, the Exalt ignores all penalties for fighting in treacherous terrain, be it facing a mounted opponent, fighting while prone, fighting within vast clouds of choking smoke, in acid rain, or the midst of a bonfire. This benefit does not extend to surviving or successfully navigating such circumstances. For example, it does not allow the Exalt to walk on uncustomary surfaces, such as water, heads, or clouds. Complimentary charms in Athletics or Resistance are recommended.

                Sixth Precept
                Cost: 1 intimacy or 2-4 motes, Mins: Essence 2
                Type: Reflexive
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Instant
                Prerequisites: Third Precept

                Victory is possible with only the annihilation of one's opponent, but perfection is attainable only through the annihilation of one's self. This charm is activated when Straight Sword's wielder voluntarily loses an intimacy. It adds that intimacy's strength as non-charm dice to both the attack and the post-soak damage roll of a single Melee action. The intimacy so sacrificed cannot be re-obtained until the passing of an entire Season, and an intimacy forced upon the Exalt by magical instill actions are ineligible for this charm.

                A wielder who has no intimacies except "Tie: Cutting Down My Opponent" may instead spend two to four motes, adding one non-charm success to the attack and post-soak damage rolls of a single Melee attack for each mote. Such a wielder is explicitly a valid target for charms that target those without intimacies, such as Cup Boils Over, and lowers their Resolve by 2 outside of combat.

                Seventh Precept
                Cost: 5 motes, 2 willpower, Mins: Essence 3
                Type: Reflexive
                Keywords: Counterattack, Decisive-only
                Duration: Instant
                Prerequisites: Sixth Precept

                The only truly useful thing in this accursed world is will. When struck by an attack that deals at least one health level of damage, the wielder of Straight Sword may employ the Seventh Precept to make an immediate decisive counter-attack, which must not be a gambit. If it incapacitates his opponent, he may immediately move to another opponent at up to Short range and make another decisive attack, preserving the initiative pool he had for the initial attack for the purposes of determining damage. If he incapacitates this second opponent, this process may repeat for a number of times equal to the number of Health Levels lost during the initial provoking attack. For example, a character who loses ten health levels may counterattack his opponent and make up to nine follow-up attacks, so long as each incapacitates its target.

                This evocation may be used after an attack which incapacitates the Exalt, in which case the Exalt may make an unlimited number of follow-up attack and ignores all wound penalties The Exalt immediately perishes once a follow-up attack fails to incapacitate its target or he runs out of applicable targets.

                Eleventh Precept
                Cost: 10 motes, Mins: Essence 3
                Type: Simple
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Indefinite
                Prerequisites: Seventh Precept

                One truly dedicated to the art of Cutting is a most difficult opponent to ambush, for they live, eat, and shit as someone who has their sword drawn. By accepting this truth and investing ten motes into Straight Sword, the wielder adds his Melee score as dice to all Awareness checks and as successes to Join Battle rolls. Should he win initiative, he gains an additional (Essence) initiative with which to begin combat. Such preoccupation is not without price. He faces a -3 dice penalty to all actions unrelated to Melee, Resistance, or Awareness while outside of combat, and his Guile is reduced by 2.

                Sixteenth Precept
                Cost: 7 motes, 1 willpower, Mins: Essence 4
                Type: Reflexive
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Scene
                Prerequisites: Eleventh Precept

                Even should Straight Sword be stolen from him or shattered in his hands, it remains a terrible tool of destruction for one sufficiently bonded to it. With the use of this charm, the wielder may make Melee attacks, benefit from Melee charms, and in all other ways act as if he was wielding Straight Sword. He faces no circumstantial penalties for lacking unnecessary things such as Straight Sword, arms, or physical embodiment.

                First and Final Precept
                Cost: --, Mins: Essence 5
                Type: Permanent
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Indefinite
                Prerequisites: Sixteenth Precept

                Straight Sword vanishes, if it ever truly existed in the first place. The Exalt gains the benefits of Sixteenth Precept for free, indefinitely, and no longer suffers any of the negative side-effects of holding only a single intimacy mentioned in Sixth Precept. Additionally, he automatically succeeds on all Survival rolls regarding personal well-being of difficulty 3 or lower. The Exalt must still commit the customary five motes in order to benefit from the Artifact bonuses and evocations of the Sword he no longer has.

                Fourfold Closing
                Cost: --, Mins: Essence 5
                Type: Permanent
                Keywords: None
                Duration: Indefinite
                Prerequisites: First and Final Precept

                The Exalt is made aware of who wields Straight Sword, and where they are.

                Special Activation Rules: Instead of being bought with experience points, this evocation is obtained for free by a master of the First and Final Precept, as soon as Straight Sword finds a new wielder.
