        There might be occasions where Lunar magic (which might still cover some psychedelic themes) enables a degree of physical interaction with abstractions in a manner that is useful for, among other things, crafting, but it's probably not useful to look on that as a Lunar exclusive, because it's a thing Solars can do as well and there's a lot that's useful for them being able to do so in playing them and composing the setting.

        If Lunars can do it, it's probably helpful to think of the method and possibly the results as something particularly Lunar, in terms of what makes them different over what makes them necessary.

        I think a useful approach to take might not be so much "how does being a Lunar help you be a crafter" so much as "how does being a crafter help you be a Lunar". Try and think of things Lunars might do and think "how would crafting help with this, and how could it be refined and specified in order to help even more".

        That idea of Lunars in a good position to improvise and put together stuff quickly and on the move might be a good indication, because it feels inclined to look at crafting fulfilling an aspect of the stated Lunar milieu. It's not about needing a Lunar if you want stuff made that way (although if it serves a particularly Lunar methodology, then it might extend to them having some particular tricks that nobody else have, that can serve them in ways that others couldn't achieve).

        I can kind of imagine a modified backstory for Death at the Root coming from this.

                                            I don't really think each splat should have its own distinctive type of crafting, because it seems unduly confining given the breadth of what craft is, and it exacerbates the focus on miraculous wonder-forging as "real" crafts and implies that your merit as a crafter is a product of the crafting charms you know.

                                            I think basically everybody should be able to do artifact creation competently, even if Solars remain on a tier by themselves, and if that requires charms, then everybody should have access to those charms. The one really distinctive crafting motif I'd really like to see represented in Lunars is some kind of jury-rigging tricks that allow them to make rickety or short-lived things very quickly out of whatever's on hand, even in cases where the materials available are grossly inappropriate. I wouldn't want that to be the essence of Lunar crafting, but it seems like they're the group best suited to that trick.

                                            I think "modding" is also a concept space suitable for Lunar Intelligence -- taking an existing creation and tuning it, adding stuff to it, or repurposing it. That seems to broadly conform to Lunar thematics and it's not an ability we've seen before.

                                            On the whole, though, I think we'll need to wait and see what Lunars are like in 3e. We have a broad sense of their fluff, but even that's a bit vague, and given who's in the current writing team I expect their charms to be much more distinctive than we've seen from them to date, so a lot of the speculation about what Lunars' charmset will look like is kind of moot until we've seen what the corebook has to say about them.
