    When their plans were laid, Lans wrote out the details on the back of the shipping record Ash had taken from Dry Gulch office, and gave the sheet to Gaze. She, in turn, would fly ahead to Serge and Bo and give them their instructions.

    Of course, Serge probably couldn't read, and Gaze could not or would not speak common Realm. Ash thought she'd feel mighty stupid if it turned out Bo didn't know how to read either.

    Still, they decided to go ahead and hope for the best. There wasn't time for much else.

    Seres stayed behind while Ash and Lans went on ahead to find the best spot for their ambush. It had taken them the better part of a day to come from their hideaway to Rustbank, and it took nearly as long to reach the canyons. That is to say, they set out in the middle of the day and it was fully night by the time they were close.

    They made camp, and then on the morning of the heist they found their spot. Ash and King scaled a cliffside to survey the area, and reckoned it was as good as they could hope for - the tracks ran through the bottom of the canyon here, and the walls were relatively close in. By mid-afternoon, deep shadows would cover about half of the canyon, and there would be ample space to hide.

    Gaze-of-the-Sun found them not long after, and told them Bo and Serge were on their way. All they needed to do was wait.



    The sound of a train is very distinct. The loud, irregular thumping from the yeddim pulling the front mixes with the rattles of the carriages and the occasional squealing from steel on steel. In the canyons, they could hear the echoes long before the train itself became visible.

    This train had eight sections - a drivers' car up front, where the teamsters guiding the yeddim worked. Behind that was the store-car, where feed, water, and coal for the animals was kept. Then there was one car for freight - boxes of metal ingots, if Lans' information was correct. Then there were two cars for passengers, one car for their baggage, the caboose for guards and extra teamsters, finally the dust car at the back.

    Everyone was in position. Lans and his horse lurked in the shadows beneath a canyon wall, waiting for his chance. Gaze was flying overhead, an oddly placed flock of black birds that would also act as a signal to Seres, should she have a chance to stick her head out a window.

    And the Ranger was ready for her dramatic entrance.



    • From atop the canyon wall, a bright red streak plunged down. King crashed onto the roof of the second passenger car, claws digging deep into the wood.
    • The folks in the first passenger car first heard two loud bangs. Half a moment later, a masked woman in a red poncho crashed through one of the windows, rolled to her feet in the aisle, and immediately butt-stroked a conductor with her flamepiece.
    • A weathered, hungover train-guard stood on the forward-facing stoop of the caboose, drinking strong tea and watching the scenery go by in a blur. She did a double-take when she saw a great, flaming hell-beast suddenly appear from behind the rocks, and choked when it began racing towards her.
    • Something else?


    OOC commentary
    For most trains, the caboose is the last car. Dust trains are different, because of the obvious danger of transporting volatile, flammable substances. Dust cars are usually kept as far from the front as possible, partly to reduce the risk of fire and partly so that if there is an emergency and the car needs to be detached, it won't take too much of the train with it.


    On the frontier of the Wild South, there's only one woman with the grit to take on its most dangerous outlaws and bring them Back Alive, or Maybe Dead.

                    A weathered, hungover train-guard stood on the forward-facing stoop of the caboose, drinking strong tea and watching the scenery go by in a blur. She did a double-take when she saw a great, flaming hell-beast suddenly appear from behind the rocks, and choked when it began racing towards her.

                    It resembled a lizard, but it was bigger than a horse, had a misshapen lump on its back, and was on fire. The guard dropped her tea and fumbled for the short bow she thought she'd brought out onto the stoop with her, only to realize she'd left it inside. She had enough time and presence of mind to open the door to the caboose and shout out a warning before the beast got her.

                    The guard noticed that a noose had fallen down over her shoulders an instant before she was yanked off her feet. She screamed wordlessly as she was pulled away from the door, futilely trying to grab at the door frame, the railings, or anything with her restrained arms. She felt herself begin to tip over the edge of the stoop, and nearly lost her mind with terror.

                    Then a hand reached out and grabbed her by the collar. She heard a grunt as someone pulled her up, but all she could see was a red figure surrounded by flames.

                    The Ranger hauled the hapless guard over King's back, keeping her balanced as best she could while the woman continued screaming and kicking. With that done, she gave King one last pat on the neck before she leaped onto the caboose, trailing fire. King, no longer ablaze, gradually slowed down, then irritably shook himself until the hapless guard rolled off.

                    A burly man with a big iron club came out the door as the Ranger was got to her feet, and made a two-handed sideways swing at her head. She ducked, and the metal splintered the wall of the caboose beside her. She punched him in the sternum. The big man wheezed and stepped back, putting his guard up, and the Ranger took the opening to draw her flamepieces. When the guardsman stepped up and swung again, she intercepted him with a pistol-whip to the wrist. The man swore and pulled back again, but before he could regroup, Lans came in behind him.

                    Lans had ridden for the train shortly after Ash did, but had apparently opted to go for the front of the caboose instead of the back, where the coupling was. He came up alongside the stoop, crouching on his saddle instead sitting in it, and launched himself across. He caught the lip of the overhang above the stoop with his good arm, swung, and landed a kick with both feet on the burly man's back.

                    The guardsman fell toward, arms windmilling. The Ranger caught him, used his momentum to spin him around, and shoved him backwards off the stoop. He tumbled and rolled out of sight.

                    The Ranger let out a breath, then turned to Lans.

                    "So far, so good," the old man shouted. He drew his saber and looked into the open door of the caboose. "Have you seen Seres?"

                    The Ranger shook her head.

                    "She must be in another car." Lans looked toward the front of the train, then back to the Ranger. "I thought there'd be more opposition."

                    The Ranger held up her flamepieces and pointed into the caboose.

                    Lans nodded. "I'll work on the coupling. You make sure there are no surprises."

                                        The Ranger tipped her hat down, adjusted her mask, then turned and walked into the car ahead of them.

                                        The baggage car was, as you'd expect, full of baggage. Boxes and baskets and sacks and cases of all kinds were stacked on shelves, crowding the narrow space. The ranger nearly had to turn herself sideways to fit in the narrow aisle left between all the packed stowage.

                                        As she neared the exit to the car, she heard a thump against the ceiling, followed by a skittery series of taps.. She froze and looked up at the dark wooden roof. A few tense moments passed in relative quiet.

                                        The Ranger frowned and cautiously pushed open the door leading out. No one appeared to be waiting for her, so she walked through slowly, then immediately spun around and pointed her flamepiece up at the roof behind her. Nothing.

                                        She couldn't shake the feeling that something was amiss, but she turned around again and pull open the door into the second passenger car.

                                        Folks were crowded onto rows of wooden seats with barely enough room between them to move their arms. They were murmuring when she came in, but those murmurs became shouts of alarm as soon as she raised her flamepieces for them all to see.

                                        A lanky boy on the edge of adulthood came charging down the aisle at her. He was dressed like a conductor, but today he was also doing guard duty and had armed himself with a knife. He held it high over his shoulder as he ran up, screaming.

                                        The Ranger watched him approach, and waited. When he was in arm's reach, she took a step forward and twisted to one side. His knife and the arm that held it sailed past her, and then her flamepiece clocked him in the head.

                                        The boy's battle cry turned into a strangled squawk as he fell down, bounced off the side of a bench, and crumpled to the floor. The Ranger bent down and hit him once more one the temple, and he was out cold.

                                        She straightened up and glared around the car. The frantic yelling from the passengers subsided into silence. They stared back at her, captivated by something they did not understand, and dared not look away.

                                        All except one. At the far end of the car, a woman in a too-fancy dress stood up and bolted for the door. She jerked on the handle, but it seemed to be stuck. She began cursing a blue streak as she heaved and pulled.
