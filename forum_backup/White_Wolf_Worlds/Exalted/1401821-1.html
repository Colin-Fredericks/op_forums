                    Originally posted by Bastet View Post
                    With third edition rules as-written the difficulty for the actual action plateaus at 5. However while attempting surgery might be difficulty 3 (Intelligence + Medicine) roll, attempting it in pitch darkness might impose a -3 dice penalty to your roll if the Storyteller wanted to acknowledge the adverse circumstances.
                    That's not quite right.

                    5 is a soft-cap. See page 238 for a list of tasks with a Difficulty above 5. Or page 231 (which runs to Difficulty 20).

                    The text on page 185 establishes that Difficulty 5 is appropriate for near impossible feats. If it's technically possible but even Batman would probably fail it, then it's Difficulty 5 (even Batman can't reliably read a letter in pitch darkness by feeling the raised ink with his fingertips, but there is at least something to feel so a Wuxia OTT hero might perform such a feat). 5+ is for things that just can't happen without magic -- like a human lifting a rhinoceros. Or, at least, things which are significantly harder than the hardest feats which can generally be accomplished without magic (i.e. harder than reading a letter in pitch darkness by feeling the ink on the page with your fingertips, or harder than jumping hundreds of feet and landing safely, or harder than running for three days straight without sleep or rest).

                    Further page 188 states "Most of the time, advantageous or disadvantageous environmental factors will simply determine the difficulty of an action, rather than producing penalties or bonuses."

                    Performing a "simple" surgery is listed on page 185 as a Difficulty 1 action for heroic characters. (I'd suggest Difficulty 2 for run-of-the-mill characters.) Performing it in pitch darkness does not impose a penalty, it just raises the Difficulty to 2 (I'd suggest 4 for non-heroic characters).

                    ****

                    For run-of-the-mill, non-heroic characters (but bear in mind that anyone who is standing up to, or fighting beside, the Exalted is probably not "run-of-the-mill"), I'd keep trivial actions as unrolled (no rolling to the your shoes), or Difficulty 1 if failure has a meaningful consequence. Difficulty 1 actions either stay at 1 or get bumped to 2 (an average professional should succeed - a lock smith with Dex 2 Larceny 1 Specialty in Lockpicking, Craft: Locks 2, should be able to pick a lock, but some actions are going to require Willpower or exceptional tools to pull off every time). Difficulty 2 actions become 3 or 4. Difficulty 3 actions become Difficulty 5. Run-of-the-mill characters auto-fail Difficulty 4 and 5 actions -- a merely average equestrian can't break a wild, man-eating horse in the middle of a forest fire.

                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post

                        "As great heroes, Exalted characters are assumed to possess abundant confdence and competence. Tasks which run-of-the-mill individuals in Creation would consider challenging (such as picking a lock or removing a patient’s appendix without killing him) are ordinary fare for heroes. Such tasks are appropriate for difficulty 1."

                        Heroic characters use the heroic difficulty chart. Run-of-the-mill characters don't.

                        A heroic priest can perform surgery at night in a storm 73% of the time. Your average priest cannot.
                        But if the heroic priest gets in a medicine contest with the ordinary priest, they each have an equal chance of success because it's an opposed roll?

                        In something more likely to occur in play, a heroic character sneaking past an ordinary character is a common situation in fiction and roleplaying. The way this is set up in the rules, if the mortal has a typical dice pool, the difficulty is comparable to some truly incredible heroic feats.

                        Hitting battle-ready troops with a decisive attack is also equivalent to plucking a gem from a nest of writhing serpents without being bitten while the temple collapses around you.

                        IMO having a single difficulty scale for everyone and giving heroic characters larger pools relative to non-heroic characters (or not letting non-heroic characters double 10s, or making stunt bonuses bigger) would be a more solid basis for a system.
