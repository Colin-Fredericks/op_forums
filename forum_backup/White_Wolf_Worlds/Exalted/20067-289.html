    Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
    The only time an intimacy lowers someone's Resolve is if you exploit it for a Persuade, right?
    Not just persuade, but any kind of influence that it might reasonably apply to. Someone who's already inclined towards doing something won't need a very big bribe or threat to make them do it anyways.

    If I exploit a Minor Intimacy to persuade, but a Defining Intimacy would theoretically make it easier to persuade him to do the thing, do I only lower it by the Minor intimacy, or is it possible for the defining one to help?
    The target would take the resolve penalty from the Defining intimacy instead of the minor one you exploited, but the ST doesn't necessarily have to tell you what resolve you're rolling against if they don't want to give away that the target has a greater intimacy you could have exploited. You can actually make persuade attempts without knowing a target's intimacies, but if they don't have any strong enough to support the action you're asking them to do, they can treat it as an unacceptable order, and then you'll have to follow the reset rules before you can try to persuade them again.

                        08-26-2016, 12:27 AM
                        Originally posted by prototype00 View Post
                        What if you don't fail but they use a willpower in a decision point to resist? Is that still covered under the re-try rule?
                        Yes. See page 219 of the core book, under Lengthy Debates.

                        Edit: Basically, the requirement for the target to have an appropriate intimacy for persuasion and improving intimacies and the reset rules are the two major things that keep the social influence rules modelling a functional narrative and curbing attempts to abuse it as a means of draining an opponent's willpower. Thousand Courtesesan Ways relaxes the intimacy requirement rules under somewhat narrow circumstances (only persuade attempts used for seduction) but to be able to ignore both would result in many of the same problems 2E had with social combat, and should be avoided at all cost.
