        Okay, keep going through the latter Influence Charms.


        Essence 3 starts with Argent Glyph Enchantment, another Permanent enhancement Charm, this time for Shining Moon-Child Mark. It allows the connotations of the mark to be alternately or thoroughly supplemented with making it impossible to remove (my head immediately went to the diadem example, but its clarified to not work on objects), giving it a term or condition to prematurely end (probably almost entirely useful for when its being used to project an emotional connection that is hurtful to the marked person), more specific information about the intentions or nature of the marked person, and making the power of the mark only recognizable to specific cultures. I’m remembering a thought I had in the past about the prior Charm being able to serve as the Lunar process for designating messengers and go betweens, and I think it’s useful to have a means of elaborating on that.

        Hmm, more social enforcement stuff in Taboo-Enforcing Beast… right, so it’s a persuade action that doesn’t need to target an Intimacy, can be used reflexively and is used to dissuade somebody from acting in a manner contrary to a culture that the Lunar deeply values, but is in in all other respects identical to a normal one. It can be used carefully to force such a Decision Point if used to enforce a society governed by the Solar Mate, and the Protean effects has the interesting inclusion of a socially important animal as well as a position of authority. Not much else to say about this specifically, but thus far I’ve liked the way that this line is being realised throughout Charisma.

        An anima cost for Captivating Siren Voice, eh? Why, that’s a very strange idea of a power to keep people’s attention fixed on a performer, compelling them to press as closely in as possible; want to be careful with that, lest anybody be crushed or trampled. I’m guessing that the Defining Intimacy it can be exploited as goes beyond just the compulsion it creates to approach, although I imagine that there aren’t a huge range of actions that can be leveraged from fascination with a performing Lunar. Heh, it’s got to make for quite a scene when a person compelled to approach or follow the Lunar is actively trying to attack (at least until upgraded by repurchase at Essence 4). Huh, wasn’t expecting the Totemic animal to be a small prey animal… between that and letting it be Appearance… could it be following the logic of something magnetically adorable?

        Pity that it excludes actual kitties, though.

        Oh, I’m now struck with a point from before about how expansive a single Attribute can be, with how Totemic Charms can allow the right Lunar to push investment in one Attribute into even more spaces.

        Culture-Hero Empowerment… at this point I can hardly tell what I have and haven’t seen previewed. Just focus on the idea that it’s new… from the name, and long formed idea about Lunars having a particular culture hero association, my first thought would have been for it referring to the Lunar, but no this is about elevating somebody else to that position. What do they get… ah, still following the customs enforcement subject, empowering somebody around being a custodian of something specific. Others from the society recognise that in them, their Resolve is strengthened against attempts to influence them against their charge, they’re better at ferreting out transgression and get people to seek amends when they do, and the Charm can be combined without cost with a Moon-Child Mark. Having an especially dutiful servant who endures through great struggle can feed back to a Lunar patron to the point of relieving their Limit, and a Solar mate governing the society in question can share those benefits.

        I remember now, a sense that this side of Lunar Charisma, of enforcing societies from within, gives me a certain impression of their sacred guardian beast quality.

        Huh, at this point, Eagle-Among-Sparrows Attitude is surprising in its simplicity. So it’s just… influence made to align with the Lunar’s cares or attachments, and costs some more Willpower to resist unless done so with an Intimacy at least as strong as what is driving the Lunar. I imagine that it ultimately has power disproportionate with the number of words it requires. But then there’s Goddess Crowned by Horns, doing as much as it can to provide an exceptionally difficult and draining to resist influence roll aligned with something Major or Defining once per story; I’m not entirely clear on the reset condition, but going by the assumption of it being used uncommonly this does feel like a particularly good thing to deploy strategically. It goes to my thoughts on Charisma being less diverse but more direct.

        Lion’s Roar Rebuke now comes along to defy my prior assessment with a shouting attack Charm. I don’t think it’s the deadliest decisive attack created by a Charm, but a ranged, unarmed attack to inflict knockback is probably nothing to sneeze at. It’s probably freaky to see in action, at least.

        Rolling back again for another emotion, Scathing Lunar Condemnation gives a non-Charm bonus based on the strength of a hateful Tie to threaten or inspire fear. Thousand Throat Howl follows that with the stark image of a Lunar’s anima erupting from their throat with the scream of their heart’s blood library, and the straightforward and extreme effect of making everybody but allies that hears it flee or seek safety without any reservation, to the point that they actually get a bit of bonus to do so. Also penalizes Resolve (I’m guessing against the original threaten rolls) depending on comparative Willpower (with a better choice by Protean when in an animal shape that targets are scared of, or just plain enormous). I just like the elegance of this, the terrible simplicity.

        The revolutionary stuff gets furthered with Shattered Yoke Fury, going beyond inspiring momentary anger and into helping others form enraged Ties in the moment to apply against people trying to influence them, and then act on their new rage.

        Okay, so, in the run up to the game, some writers talked about how well-suited Lunars were to revolutionary stuff, and I had reservations. I’m really glad to see how these things are ultimately realised; not in the sense of Lunars being Essentially champions of the downtrodden or personifying revolution (at least not specifically, directly), but rather in Lunar elements of powerful, fierce emotion, of transgression, of being anarchic, disruptive or simply external (even playfully tricky) elements, and a bit of encouragement for others, all blending together for powers that can readily be applied to those purposes. I’m very much happy to be sold on this facet of them.

        And with that, it’s into Essence 4. God-Beast Exemplar Spirit: enhances Herd-Strengthening Invocation with a hefty surcharge that allows it to cause those who have a Defining Tie (not a positive one) to the Lunar, causing their attachment to the custom to become a Defining Principle, that… jeez, becomes hard to erode and makes contrary influence unacceptable! And they need to spend a hefty amount of Willpower just to become capable of eroding the Intimacy. See, what I like about this is that it balances the capacity to create such a powerful Intimacy so quickly with the need to cultivate strong feelings about oneself, and, well… more than a few Lunars might decide it’s easier to become hated than loved for the sake of this. The ogre who acts the bastard to make others look good. Yes, picture the Lunar who makes themselves despised, to act contrary to a value they want to reinforce, such that society leans into it just from viewing the hated Lunar as its antithesis. Fascinating.

        Heart-Affirming Tribute allows the Lunar to reward their chosen culture heroes… oh, actually no, it doesn’t specify that, it can work for anybody that has overcome difficulty or danger for the sake of a Major or higher Intimacy, and creates a talisman imbued with the power of an Eclipse-Ok Charm. For most mortals, that’s still going to be fairly weak, although more than they’d have otherwise (but if it can still work for Exalted…). It’s as hard as an Artifact but its power can’t transfer, unless the Lunar wills it so for another valid recipient. Its an indefinite Charm, so the Lunar needs to commit motes to maintain the power, although they have an easier time restoring the Charm if they allow it to lapse; alternatively, it’s not necessary to pay the mote cost if it’s used for one’s culture hero, and at Essence 5 a mere two experience points can make it permanent. Furthermore, 10 gold craft points can be used in place of the original Willpower cost, and 10 white points in place of the XP to make one permanent. For some reason, this one puts me in mind of a Lunar in the role of benevolent fairy godmother; they see some young heiress defy family and tradition to escape the castle and abscond with her dearest love, the prince of the neighbouring kingdom (also in hopes that their eventual marriage will allow them to unite their kingdom and make war on their wicked neighbouring state), and rewards her with a rock or tooth or something to let her walk on mist or turn into a bird or something.

        And now for Essence 5. My expectations are high.

        Argent Anglerfish Lure, a particularly threatening name, and a Psyche Charm to boot. Adds a hefty mote and anima surcharge to Captivating Siren Voice, allowing it to increase the Willpower cost to resist and causing victims to pursue the Lunar heedless of any surrounding dangers, and penalised against defending themselves from such. Is Totemic in the same manner as the prerequisite. Yeesh, this one makes for an especially unnerving alternative to all of the fear Charms. It’s like a follow the white rabbit kind of thing… especially if you literally go into the damn Wyld.

        And finally there’s Divine Monster Judgment, to fully embody the sacred guardian beast (or terrifying taboo beast). Damn thing can last for the length of a story, although it can’t be used sans a Solar mate. Dedicating oneself to a custom of the society that the Lunar is strongly attached to, and that the mate rules, they get greatly enhanced bonuses to Resolve from relevant Ties (including to the Solar), automatically succeeds to read the intentions of transgressors if their Willpower is lower than the Lunar’s Essence, has enhanced persuade or threaten rolls against transgressors, can weaken the Join Battle of transgressors that try to fight and gets the opportunity to make a decisive attack that doesn’t reset to base, aaaaaand gets one opportunity, upon beating a strong transgressor, to make a roll from which they can reap Willpower and shed Limit. I’m not quite sure about the limited use; if it lasts for a story but can only be used once per story, is that in the event that the character drops to commitment? In any case, it’s an impressive and fearsome Charm, something that seems very well suited to stories focused on a society’s internal issues, and has interesting implications for why a Lunar might be motivated to place a Solar (nominally) in charge of something that the Lunar has been invested in.

        I mean jeez, imagine playing a fresh Solar who has to deal with a mate that wants to shanghai them into this. “Just look pretty and let me handle everything.”


        And that’s the Influence Charms. I’d say they’re hefty enough to not require another category besides warfare. They’re a good show, in all, effectively more flavourful than just being good Charisma (although all of the elements of forging connections to the Lunar’s values and unveiling their inner self actually present that, and make it feel meaningfully different from Ability Charms), nicely distinct from Appearance, while still not overturning any major assumptions.


        Warfare won’t take me long to gnaw through.

                    All right, almost finished the second Attribute.


                    Gets right to the point with a name like Pack Leader Attitude; adds bonus dice to a Charisma command action (and with sufficient Initiative, reroll 1s) to allow one to adopt the attitude what is of a leader of a pack. Is Totemic in the manner of the Appearance War Charms, which I’m sure most of these will be. Then there’s Fearless Beast-Warrior Exhortation, doubling 9s on rally actions and adding the group’s Might in additional dice; I guess that represents a kind of appeal to the strength lent by supernatural power. Followed by Vengeful Beast Triumph, to not only reflexively roll to rally a battle group that is routing or has taken damage to Magnitude (pop pop), but sap some Initiative from the enemy that did the damage, that the Lunar actually gets to keep for once. I like the picture for that of an unexpected reversal that gives an enemy pause and bolsters the Lunar’s confidence.

                    Lead the Pride takes things along a different direction starting with adding a non-Charm success to an order action, and then being able to roll non-Charm dice equivalent to 10s on the original roll, before a new tree starts with Unified Pack Tactic, which allows a Lunar to employ some of that positive reinforcement quality to start a fight by transferring some of their Initiative to a lower ranked ally, only needing to actually experience half of the loss if they win the fight order anyway (a little bit of good-natured cheating, I think). This is also the first one that is not Totemic.

                    Hmm, I’ve not often given much thought to Wits, but I’ll be interested in seeing what that might do with Join Battle here (assuming that’s the correct Attribute). Mind, I’ll have to wait until the very end to see it.

                    It’s funny to have to remind myself that some of these Charms are growing out of crossovers between sub-categories, like in Rampage-Unleashing Provocation. With that, the Lunar is able to employ positive or negative reinforcement on an ally who must be strangely willing, but also assert their (unmodified) Resolve against the influence, in order to be whipped into a battle frenzy that steels them against fear and enhances Strength, but makes them incapable of retreat or surrender unless they spend Willpower to end the experience. Relentless Lunar Fury is a Charm that still exists, and I’m curious about how it must function given that an advantage of this Charm is triggering it immediately (unless the benefit of this stacks with that, and it’s simply cutting straight to the heart of matters); other Exalted like Solars can trigger their own battle fury powers from it.

                    Into Essence 2 with Lessons of the Winter Wolf, through which a Lunar can briefly train a battle group to have bonuses against fear and ignore some of the effects of deprivation and fatigue, but only fleetingly, lasting for a single battle or a week if unused. Lunars whose spirit shapes have a Berserker Merit can learn the Charm with Stamina, and its benefits can also be blended into (and made longer lasting within) the following Charm, Wolf-Pack Training Technique. That lets a Lunar spend a month training a group to give it slightly improved Drill, Willpower, Join Battle, attack and Parry. I’d want to put these side by side with the Solar training Charms, but my cursory assessment suggests that Lunars might be better at bestowing a force with fearlessness that provides it with raw power, but not quite as much discipline and specialized battle tactics. Still, those might just be the province of Intelligence, which is fine; as I’ve said, I love the idea of distributing fields of competence around Attributes like this.

                    Now then, Crimson Fang Mantle to cover attacking a foe that has been rendered vulnerable, doubling 9s for command roles to attack an enemy with lower Initiative (with the commanded battle group’s Might counting towards its rank), a nicely flavoured kind of battle tactic. It’s back to Strength Totem Charms again. Then there’s Battle Anthem of the Lunar Exalted, to inspire fury in their allied armies with a fight song that enhances their attack and damage, strengthens the experience of enraged Intimacies that matter to the fight, and bolsters them against rout, although the Lunar maintaining the song can’t do anything else that isn’t reflexive (which isn’t as much of an inhibition as one might think to a Lunar, I believe).

                    Into Essence 3 with Starving Beast Bloodlust, allowing a Lunar to provide further inspiration for a group that has experienced some of their Winter Wolf training, letting them benefit from perfect morale, increased Resolve against fear or influence to stop fighting, and the Lunar will even get bonuses to their commands as the battle group might wear away in a fight.

                    Raiton’s Banquet Assault allows calling for an attack that can be decisive in the battle, letting the successes of their order be added as dice to both attack and damage rolls; it can only be used once per scene, but the reset condition of their allies incapacitating an enemy or causing an enemy army to start breaking down is not too onerous.

                    Hmm, Wild Fury Awakening is the first of these to be Protean, and… ahh, it’s building off of animal summoning Charms. Simply put, call together lots of animals (of a type you can turn into), to arrive in 20 minutes or less; the number you get depends on how big and dangerous what you’re calling is, with the stronger ones in smaller numbers, and a mere recommendation that the environment has an impact on how many are called (probably still within reason). If you call pack hunters, they even get very good Drill. The animals can hence be commanded, though on their own they’ll only attack foes. Oh hey, they technically don’t even need to be called for a fight, although commanding them for something else requires a regular influence. Still, it’s nice to know that a Lunar can call in a few dozen squirrels or whatever, if they should ever need them. Actually being in the shape of the animal they’re calling upon allows them to act as a more powerful commander. And hell, even for a weaker animal, between their Drill and the extra dice provided from a Lunar’s commands, they might still be a bit formidable; might be that flock of pigeons can do a bit more damage to your flying machines than just blocking up your windscreen. Heh, they thought an army of pigeons was tonally dissonant at the end of Hunchback of Notre Dame?

                    Shit, I’m really attached to this now… yeah, screw tyrant lizards, go into battle guiding a flock of pigeons. They won’t be so weak when you’re telling them to go for the jugular, and they can fly and all. You also get to laugh when your enemies initially think it’s silly, until they’re left writhing with their eyes pecked out.

                    Them or bats. “When you’re a bat, you’re a bat all the way, from your first starting stat ‘til the turn you decay.”

                    Finally, there’s Invincible Beast-Warrior Empowerment, since Charisma actually gets an Essence 4 war Charm. My, this is formidable; convert all of the dice bonuses from Size, Might, Drill and command actions directly into successes! To numerous battle groups within a certain range at the same time? And ones commanded directly get hefty bonuses to morale (or extra Might if their morale is perfect). Sure, the Lunar needs to keep paying Initiative each turn to keep it going and it’s only once per scene, but damn, use the Excellency and other Charms to put enough dice into your difficulty 1 command action and let a battle group build directly on those successes, they probably won’t need to last very long. Your pigeon armies will certainly be thankful for the addition.


                    So, the Charisma war Charms are suitably impressive. Not quite as personal as the Influence Charms, but you’re not here for friendship, you’re trying to accomplish an objective. In all seriousness, I both like it being more directly military than Appearance, and still having room for some unique weirdness with the likes of calling animals and making battle music.


                    Aaaaand Charisma isn’t done. I thought it was only two categories, it instead has the much awaited Territory Charms. Heh, I had caught a few references, but I assumed people were just speaking in anticipation.


                    Dare I fit in one more category?


                    Eh, it’s not that long, I might as well polish it off here, and I didn’t need to write too much for Warfare (and I’m pretty sure about half of that was brought upon myself by rambling on about pigeons).


                    So, what are the beginnings of what a Lunar can do with Territory?


                    Hmm, a little nod to the very old notion of giving Lunars the Merged Keyword to allow greater accessibility for a smaller number of Attributes… so Boundary-Marking Meditation can alternately be a Wits Charm. The mote cost is hefty, but it’s Instant and Essence 1, so I can hardly see cause to complain. So a Lunar can claim an area of up to their Essence times ten square miles (for a starting Lunar, that’s still about a mid-sized town) as their territory, so long as they “fully master” it, defined as having travelled most of it, been at least accepted by any resident human communities, and have the consent or submission of any other supernatural beings; I like the story driven elements behind this, although I do want to closely compare it to past forms when I get the chance. Once that control is secure (with mastery, apparently the Charm merely requires staking the claim, which is established immediately), the player designates three qualities fitting the nature of the territory, after which actions supported by those qualities get the benefit of a specialty, and actions opposed by it a minor penalty. The ruling Lunar never experiences the penalty, and can withdraw benefits or penalties to others they perceive as they wish. Should the nature of the area change, the player can reassign qualities, and Lunars can hold a fairly decent, but limited, number of territories simultaneously. The benefits are simple, but still flavourful, and it is after all just the first step.

                    It's followed by the specifically Charisma Charms Moon-and-Earth Blessing, in which a Lunar spends the duration of a night wandering an area (either fairly small or the full size of a claimed territory) placing marks to imbue its landscape with Essence, and increasing the general hospitability of the place; foraging, sheltering, navigation and surviving hazards (even in a barren climate) all benefit from bonus successes, and there are nifty cosmetic features, such as night-blooming flowers, will-o’-the-wisps or odd flocks of owls. While an Instant Charm, it only lasts around a year for normal spots, but endures for the lifespan of a Lunar territory. I think this one lends itself well to what a Lunar might do for guerrillas and communities that flock to their banner, and I like the imagery of it.

                    Going straight to Essence 3 (and with Creation-Spanning Passion as a prerequisite) gives Dream-Shrouded Wilderness, through which an area can be imbued with an influence action that will play itself out in the dreams of sleeping residents; while it only affects a given person the first time they sleep, the effect can last to target new visitors as long as the Lunar keeps the motes committed, and visits occasionally. It can also be imbued with Moonstruck Reverie Trick (the cupid Charm) to also give sleepers a capacity to fall in love per the Lunar’s specifications. There are a few interesting implications and applications to this; strategically claim a territory that people are intended to come to later to widely and covertly influence them, have a distinct means with communicating with subjects or neighbours, make your influence felt without ever needing to make personal appearances, and (more skeevily) micromanage a community’s love life a bit. Really, just add a bit to the mythos of an area, associated with a Lunar or otherwise; don’t go into the Whispering Woods, people have been known to come back out of it changed.

                    King-of-Beasts Sovereignty then goes in a direction informed by Charisma themes of animal communication and social reinforcement, by broadly targeting local animals of a particular species in an area enchanted with the Moon Blessing to create an enduring tie of loyalty to mortal inhabitants (strongest in a Lunar territory), that plays itself out as voluntary action to protect, work for and generally beneficially live alongside humans that treat them appropriately. It can only last a story, but well-treated animals might stick around voluntarily. I get a nice image from this of something of a more Arcadian image for a Lunar homeland; it reminds me of the Smurfs, for some reason. In any case, it’s very pleasant.

                    I’m happy about the callback in Songline Legend Runes, a Charm building off of the one for placing a characteristic mark on people. The Charm is modelled as a permanent enhancement with surcharge, but I wonder if it can be applied retroactively, or if the Lunar would have to relinquish and reclaim the territory. Mind, there’s no reference to it growing with the Lunar’s Essence… for important ones, it’s probably good to relinquish and reclaim it. With this, that can include adding special markers that give it the quality of a Moon Mark, both projecting the emotional context and adding as another source of minor benefits. Mortal inhabitants get to proactively mark themselves in like manner with the Lunar’s consent, and native spirits are fully susceptible to either effect unless they’re fully opposed to the Lunar. Not much else to say about this one, it’s just a nice further characterisation to territories (although the visual element is making them more interesting to me than they have been in the past).

                    Ah, something nice and ominous with Omnipresent Monster’s Lair, a Psyche Charm. While within a territory, a Lunar can enhance Endless Nightmare Hunt to apply its effects to any applicable characters throughout the area (even if they enter after it’s been initiated), without needing to be personally present for them. They’re freed if they depart, and the Lunar has to stay, but considering how transgressive that Charm was, this feels like a very effective way to make a Lunar’s domain a distinctly threatening and unwelcoming place, with minimal effort on the part of the Lunar. Heh, it reminds me of something I said recently, about the idea of a Lunar going to ground in an area that enemies would be cautious or foolish about following them into.

                    Conversely (and at Essence 4), Nature’s Seductive Lure allows for a rather hefty surcharge to the Siren call to designate a feature of the territory (glowing with uncanny light) as the thing that will draw people’s attention. It can only last for a few hours, but otherwise requires no effort on the Lunars part, which might free them up for other activities. I’m picturing rummaging through an army’s camp while they’re all off beguiled by a very pretty rock surrounded by talking trees.

                    Finally, there’s the Essence 5 Charm Living World Embodiment, for the blue-feathered, red-breasted Lunar who wants to play Fisher King. Oh, it’s a Permanent Charm, that’s very nifty. So the Lunar’s territories all get health tracks, and while within one a Lunar gets the option to shunt up to half of any damage applied to themselves (or the worse detriments of disease) to the territory, which will manifest itself as the despoiling of the land and native flora and fauna (maybe the occasional dramatic surge of red in bodies of water), making Survival actions there more difficult, and diminishing the quality of its natural resources for Craft. It can heal over time, more rapidly if it was Moon Blessed, or if the Lunar forgoes some of the healing of their body. On top of that, there is the option to treat such Lunar provided healing as a means of averting things like curses or disease in the area, as well as letting it enhance the Moon Blessing in an otherwise undamaged territory to greatly increase the local fertility, bringing it to unnatural heights. Setting aside the strategic values of this Charm and the ways in which it can enhance the quality of a territory, I just like the setpieces that it can lend itself to; a Wyld Hunt in battle with an old and mighty Lunar in the heart of their dominion, the Lunar enduring terrible damage as the landscape around them withers and cracks, until everything around is finally rendered utterly barren before the Lunar is finally overwhelmed and succumbs.


                    And that’s the end of Charisma. I’d say the Territory Charms did a lot to help bring it up to the level of how impressive Appearance was.


                    I’ll read about shapeshifting stuff when it’s in the Traits chapter preview tomorrow.

                            Anyway, a sufficiently canny observer might realize that the Lunar can lose that effect if they should be forced out of the boundaries of the territory.

                            With Appearance and Charisma side by side, I see some very interesting characterizing trends for them that really set them apart from one another, and from a lot of comparable Ability Charms.

                            Broadly speaking, Appearance is all superficial. It's very heavily focused on raw, primal emotions, on controlling the surface level perception of you and projecting that forcefully outwards. Even when the Charms aren't actively about subterfuge, the underlying Lunar remains obscured beneath them. A lot of it might even be viewed as shaping one's image into a kind of shield or intermediary, letting people perceive and interact with that while holding them at a distance.

                            Charisma has more nuances, and lets you burrow yourself a bit more deeply into places, but is counterbalanced by needing to put more of yourself out there, and thus leaving some more of yourself exposed. So many of the Charms are based on empowering things along the lines of what you care about, but the consequence is that it requires you to care about things, and the right observer will have better avenues for figuring out what those things are. The Charms for interacting with societies in particular stand out there, with the degree to which you need to be invested, need to interact, even if it's making yourself look bad for the sake of cultivating an improvement.

                            I'd say that's why it has these Territory Charms; in simple terms, it's driven a lot by home being where the heart is.

                            (My giving more words to Charisma is largely because it's fresher in my mind)

                            Going from that, I'm guessing that a major line of Manipulation will be about getting ahold of the Intimacies of other people, of drawing power from them, harnessing them to the Lunar's ends, twisting them against the people that hold them. If the heights of Appearance can burn an image of yourself into a person's soul where it torments them, and the heights of Charisma can let you fiercely reprimand people who infringe upon your cares, I would speculate that high level Manipulation Charms can curse a person so that the objects of their own dedication or adoration destroy them.

                                                            Now, let’s catch up by taking in the traits of the Lunars.


                                                            With the Castes, this is going to be an occasion where I do something a bit different and consider a previous text in making my assessment.                                                             Something I like about the imagery used in describing the Full Moon Caste is the sense of complement it gives to the Dawn Caste, and how it also roots them in a different context; that idea that the night can give dark creatures no respite from light. I get why the Second Edition one focused on Wyld imagery, but even considering the stronger focus on the Wyld, in retrospect I find it a bit too rooted in lore minutia to be very evocative. Similar goes for describing them expressing their fury in a variety of combative shapes.

                                                            Given that the onus is on the Lunars for remaking their Castes, I like a section examining the motivation underlying a given Caste’s function. Like here, the idea that the Full Moon Caste was devised with a very deliberate intention to wage war (as well as surviving the assault of enemies). And I think it’s helpful to provide a description of the kinds of people that might wind up in the Caste, not just for the sufficient diversity but the significance of the fact that Caste is chosen by the Exalt (or at least determined after the fact of becoming a Lunar); what’s particularly interesting is the crossover that the non-directly physical might have with other Castes, but who end up here for aspiration towards its combative ideal.

                                                            Overall, I think that this Caste description is more succinct in the past; less fluff, less focus on irrelevant history, more about what they are.
