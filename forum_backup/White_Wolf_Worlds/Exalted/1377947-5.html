                        Originally posted by Gigaton-Falcon-Emu View Post
                        Sort of the thing, why on earth is something like Lyta at all a desirable thing to play? Its sort of feels like I can hear 'crawling in my skiiiin' every time I hear someone start describing their idea for a Celestial Dynast. Exalted has always struck me as a really cool and feel-good game with depth, I don't understand the appeal of an archetype that is basically about making a character defined by 'my life sucks, everyone sucks, yay everything sucking'. It seems more like asking the entire table to suffer with you than anything fun to me.
                        Well, not everyone plays it the same way though. Black Company, Berserk and Elric are inspiration fictions. Numerous pulp heroic fiction exists around characters who aren't that likeable. Looking back at myth and saga, we see Achilles of the Iliad, Outlaws of the Marsh and so forth. And many more. Some people presumably will want to play a game where they play somewhat doomed, messed-up heroes, and presumably those some people will hopefully have scope at their table to all be on the same page with this and manage it.

                        Sure, we can also play it as a more R-rated and adult version of your general JRPG / action-adventure / Saturday Morning Cartoon setup (and this is not in any way a slur, as I have some love for all those things) where all the characters are generally positive and likeable, with larger than life character and style and they generally like each other and get on and have no major personality problems (or at least no more than the relatively trivial disagreements we see in "The Avengers" and the like). That's fine too.

                        You're only really going to get problems if you don't all agree on where you should be going. If someone's playing the doomed, troubled loner in your story about the fellowship and friendship of your circle.

                        That said, even if no one is playing her, Lyta I see her reported as used quite a bit as an antagonist or foil. More so than many of the characters introduced by Caste Books. So there's some value in her from that, too.

                        ....

                        Re; magitek again, I think we just tend to over-analyze this topic a bit. Really it's most summed up that they felt like they probably overshot both the fanbase's desire and what the core setting's feels supports for magitek, and dialing back a bit, for more focus on geomancy, sorcery, personal scale non-mechanical artefacts (magic rings, swords, etc). More "Kingdom of Zeal", less "Phantasy Star" or "FFXII", and certainly less "modern day magic". You can still see some mechanistic stuff.

                                                Originally posted by Kelly Pedersen View Post

                                                I'm not sure about "so much better", actually. For one thing, Dragon's Egg Hatched is a Simple charm with no suggestion of requiring a dramatic action or anything to activate, meaning you can call up your elemental servant in a single turn in combat if you like, rather than the 4-hour ritual Summon Elemental requires. Also, the ST is the ultimate arbiter of what elementals exist in the setting, and is not under an obligation to allow an elemental perfectly customized to your needs, whether in powers or personality. The elemental summoned by Dragon's Egg Hatched, on the other hand, while probably less powerful than any elemental you could summon, is also more customized through the mutations you pick, and probably has fewer "personality flaws", with only a single Major or Defining Intimacy chosen by the ST and based on its element, as opposed to the full suite of Intimacies a more standard summoned elemental would have.
                                                So it's the Zero no Tsukaima / Familiar of Zero Charm that Dragon-Blooded students of the Heptagram have been missing.
