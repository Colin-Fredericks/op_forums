    Questions on the Mouth of Peace

    01-22-2026, 10:59 AM
    Hi, so what are the qualities the order looks for in the "reincarnation" of the mouth of peace? Do they have any political considerations, like trying to avoid dynasts in favor of outcastes or the opposite? If the Mouth is from a DB house does the Order try to curtail the house's influence on her? If the child fails to exalt and is deemed not the Mouth, what happens to the kid then? Kicked out of the order? Is the Mouth trained any different from other DB's who join the faith?

      Page 91 of The Realm gives us extremely loose guidelines, favoring nebulous "spiritual criteria" and passing "specially designed tests," as well as the much more specific and perhaps informative pieces, "with Sidereal guidance" and "likely to Exalt." This is leaving a lot on the table for STs, but it gives us a basic framework to piece things together.

      "Likely to Exalt" is a strong indicator towards the Dynasty; even if the process theoretically isn't supposed to be influenced by political connections, in practice I imagine there'll be a lot of jockeying among the Great Houses to get their daughters shots at those special tests, especially among the Houses with reputations for piety.

      It's also interesting, though, because the age guideline given for settling on a candidate is "before she's 15 years old," which, while on the younger side in general, isn't so young that you couldn't just pick from among the plausible candidates who've already Exalted. Maybe there's supposed to be something of cultural significance to finding the new Mouth before she Exalts, something that sells the selection process as special in a way that validates to the public the claims that they're finding the same soul over and over? In which case we flip back to the Dalai Lama parallel and it becomes way better to pick an outcaste. If they can predict an outcaste's Exaltation with any degree of consistency with enough behind-the-scenes genealogical tracking and Sidereal prophecy/shenanigans, that would make the choice both a lot more House-neutral and lend more miraculous credence to the Mouth of Peace having a detectable special quality.

      (Though on the flip side - a young outcaste who chooses the razor is already someone the Order has direct access to and is training potentially well before a Dynast would enter secondary school, so if there isn't a special reason to find girls before they Exalt, that could still work to an outcaste's advantage in potentially being selected.)

      Speaking of Sidereal oversight, we can probably guess from that that long-term outcomes are gonna be factored more than the House politicking, even if that gets girls in the door.

      I would assume that the Mouth of Peace gets special training and attention relative to everyone else in the Order for her specific role, though maybe after going through the basics with everyone else just to instill a sense of humility and cohesion.

      I suspect that wrongly-selected Mouths wouldn't be formally kicked out - it was the selectors who messed up, not them - but in practice, they probably have their public presence circumscribed and are more likely to develop a spontaneous case of dead, because it's almost unavoidable that a certain number of people will have grown attached to the first person declared the Mouth of Peace and be resistant to the establishment later going "oopsy, here's the real one instead" and that's a mighty recipe for factional infighting and a splinter sect right there.

                      First off - she's not "some pre-teen" for long. They select a Mouth young, prior to 15 and prior to Exaltation, but if she's a didn't-Exalt failure case they're not gonna know that for sure til she's over 20, so that's several years in the role to build connections.

                      But I wasn't even really talking about the monks; the Mouth of Peace is an extremely public-facing role. Her job description is basically "inspire the faithful masses + personify and represent them in the halls of power." She is not a figure whose relevance is limited primarily to the monks she works with day to day.

But honestly even without all that, I think it's just a factor of human nature that a certain number of people will have grown invested in the first thing they heard and suspicious about take-backsies. And on an even wider level than any emotional investment in the first Mouth, the simple fact of the Order having just publicly gotten it wrong is going to create complications for the newly-selected Mouth in establishing her legitimacy to the public.

                      I don't think it would be an easily brushed over moment in the religious history of Immaculacy, and, to bring this back to the monks, a good time for any with a movement in mind or who want to swing a higher position in a smaller group over their current status to throw themselves behind a public splinter.

                      ... Mind you, I don't want to exaggerate this; I don't think assassination is a first or common way to deal with the occasional wrong-Mouth. Not only is assassination kind of frowned on in general, it's also fuel to the fire for any sentiments against a new Mouth. No, I think mostly it's dealt with by making sure the wrong-Mouth stops going outside and talking to people for a while until her supporters mostly get over it and get with the program. But if she tries to maintain her position of public prominence (which, with or without claiming to be the real Mouth after all, would certainly keep such sentiments alive), that's where elevated risk of death starts setting in.
