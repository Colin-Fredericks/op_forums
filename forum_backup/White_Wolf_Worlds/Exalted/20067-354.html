        Environmental hazards are decisive attacks that ignore soak and hardness:
        "An environmental hazard’s damage is the number of dice rolled against characters subject to the hazard, as well as what kind of damage is being inflicted. This damage ignores soak and hardness, and is applied directly to the character’s health track, like a decisive attack." (P. 230)

        Traps are environmental hazards:
        "Traps work the same way as environmental hazards, save that characters are allowed a (Perception + Awareness) roll to spot them before setting them off, and they usually take effect instantly, with no ongoing interval." (P. 231)

        Falling damage is also treated like a decisive attack.
        "Characters facing falling damage suffer a number of levels of automatic damage, as well as a number of dice of damage. All such damage ignores soak and hardness and is applied directly to a character’s Health Track, as though it were a decisive attack." (P. 232)

        None of these apply since Iron Skin Concentration specifies a withering attack for that function. The decisive effect certainly would though.

        As a sidenote, for all its inconsistencies, the way Exalted deals with typical adventuring threats is remarkably simple, clear and consistent.

                Originally posted by Epee102 View Post
                If I have a battlegroup with poisoned weapons, and they hit another battlegroup, what does the poison do? Does it inflict the initiative damage in Magnitude or?
                In a context outside of superhero kung-fu duels, there's relatively little wiggle room for lethal doses of venom. You're going to die, but you probably have enough time to kill the thing that killed you, hence the combat-time rules. The Exalted can survive a couple of cobra bites, but the odds for the rest of us are essentially non-existent without immediate treatment.

                For mortals fighting mortals en masse, I would rule that the damaged battle group takes the penalty (or maybe half the normal penalty) for the full duration and checks for rout two rounds AFTER initial exposure, once the soldiers realize what they're up against. Losses suffered in this fight are deaths, straight up. Unless you have an especially industrious Exalted doctor on hand, I would not even bother mentioning the wounded except to note how they die screaming (for painful animal toxins) or simply stop breathing and fade away with the horror confined to their glassy eyes (curare!)

                My question is: how the heck are you envenoming that many weapons? Snakes are difficult to harvest and dangerous to work with, but maybe you could set up a curare plantation. It's a major investment, and it would make you an unmatched villain to the armies of creation, but you could do it.

                                        Originally posted by Siegemonkeys View Post
                                        So yeah, because of that I'm wondering if dice and auto-successes added by spells count as charm dice or not. Like, what happens if the sorcerer has 4 Intelligence and 5 Occult, does he only get 4 auto-successes and lose the ability to use an Excellency on the roll? What if he had a charm like Ancient Tongue Understand on which would give him more dice/auto-successes, does he not get that benefit on the attack roll for Death Ray due to the dice cap?
                                        "These limits are absolute unless a Charm explicitly contradicts them, and apply to any magic used to enhance a Solar’s dice pools or static values—which normally means Solar Charms, but also applies to sorcery, Evocations, Martial Arts Charms, and helpful Charms used by other sorts of Exalted to aid the Solar." (P. 252)

                                        Regarding your second question: There is a bit of split between RAI and RAW there. It comes down to the question of how literal you want to read the following two statements:

                                        "The magic of the Solar Exalted cannot add more dice to a roll than the relevant (Attribute + Ability) total affected by the Charms." (P. 251)
                                        "Unless specified to be non-Charm successes, treat each success added by a Charm as two dice for the purpose of dice-adding limitations" (P. 251)

                                        Basically, the question is: Is a dice cap also a success cap? Both statement specify dice adding and the value of a die is variable due to the existence of dice tricks whereas the value of a success is fixed. Depending of the dice tricks in play, the value of adding 10 dice can go way beyond five successes. On the other hand, the limits are specifically stated to be absolute.

                                        My inner rules lawyer says that you can add more successes but RAI seems to cap them at five charm successes. In that case, one of the advantages of Death Ray is a free build in excellency.

                                                        I'd enforce a -1 penalty to every action that uses the offhand exclusively. I'd also require Ambidexterity to claim the +2 bonus to clash attacks for wielding matched weapons.

                                                        Apart from that, as long as wielding two weapons is only a question of style rather than mechanics, such as Steel Devil Style or Sword and Board, I'd ignore it.

                                                        From a rules perspective, the penalty only applies if you take the action with your offhand and offhand only. The rules are rather poorly defined here because that penalty is only mentioned once in the entire book, and that is in the ambidexterity merit that negates it. There is an entire paragraph on Dual Wielding with zero mention of a penalty or prerequisites. Frankly, it seems like a fragment of a rule that got removed at some stage of design but someone failed to remove the merit along with it. Given that, I'd also feel comfortable to simply ignore it completely and just write ambidexterity up as one of the many perks of the exalted.

                                                        On the other hand, its a one point merit. Someone who wants his character to dual wield can surely find that one point or three experience points for it.
