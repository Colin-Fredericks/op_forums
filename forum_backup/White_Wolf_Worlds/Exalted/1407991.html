      I think what you have is okay so far, but you’re going to suffer in non-combat areas, and not be a really stellar assassin.

      With five ox bodies, and you really want Water Dragon up to the form for that immaculate ability to ignore terrestrial keywords, so that’s 4 charms there. Then five single point charms at Essence 2 leaves you with one other charm, which you probably want to be Vanishing Wind Body if you want combat stealth. You will be amazingly deadly, but you won’t have more than the excellency for investigation, resistance, larceny, etc.

      Assassins also really need a good join battle to launch an ambush on turn one, and your join battle right now is 1 with no magic to boost it.

      You’re also a criminal investigator with no bureaucracy, a Sense Motive pool of 5, guile of 3, and a base resolve of 1, none of which can be boosted by magic. Not that you necessarily 100% need those to be an investigator, but you won’t be the best sleuth you can be without them.


      The last thing is, if you’re trying to optimize for combat, like truly optimize, then you’re in for a long road that kind of precludes anything else for your character. There’s so much stuff that’s good to have that will make you more deadly in combat that if you don’t make any compromises you won’t really get to comfortably pick anything else until Essence 4 or something.

                          ****

                          Honestly, you should really talk to your ST about your level of combat prowess. Exalted really starts to break down at the optimized end. It works WAY better when you have your dots spread out all over the place.

                          1E expected every Dynast to have 2 dots in Archery, Brawl (or Martial Arts), Melee, Performance, Presence, Ride, Lore and Socialize. Having less than that marked you as some deficient outcast. I find it's helpful to build according to those minimums, then reduce the stats based on what your character is bad at. Archery 0 doesn't just mean you never studied Archery (you will have studied Archery as a child), it means you actively suck at Archery. Then I'd bring up to 2 any stats you studied at secondary school or use for your job (Awareness, Larceny, Beurocracy, Investigation, Stealth). Then take anything you want to be good at to 3 dots. Then spend BP to meet your Charm minimums.

                          Mechanically, this is probably the weakest way you could build a character. The character creation mini-game actively punishes you for building this way. But it's the way that (if everyone in your group follows suit) works best in actual play.

                                      I'm also in firm agreement with JohnDoe. Exalted isn't like DnD where the game totally works well if all the players max their primary stat and take combat optimal choices. Fighting a Beholder and his lair at level 9 absolutely works if your fighter has 20 strength, heavy armor and a magic sword, and thebard has 20 charisma, 16 dex, and Fireball.

                                      For Exalted on the other hand, if everyone in your party optimizes for combat, your Storyteller has to throw out almost every single enemy in the book, and more often than not instead of having kinetic action fight scenes you'll just skip it and move on.

                                      A really optimized combat character can work well sometimes, but usually only in groups where not everyone is like that. Like if you're the Dawn, for example, and you are really combat optimized, but the rest of your circle isn't. When you guys get into a combat scene YOU are basically invincible, and don't have to worry about the enemy attacks, but you actually do have to push yourself to protect your friends.

                                      Which seems like a game about the Dawn and his cheerleader squad, but only if theres no serious non-combat threats. Like if a manse is about to explode and kill not only the entire party but the city above as well, and the only way to stop it is to decipher this ancient language that the manual was written in, now all of a sudden the linguistics guy saves everyone else.

                                      The even better way to do it is by having the Dawn fight off the manse's automatraon guardians WHILE the linguistics guy is deciphering the code, with like an extended roll with a terminus of three, duration one round, something like that, but that's for another thread.


                                      There's a side benefit about building your dynast as John suggests too, and that's if your ST sticks to the difficulty guidelines in the book, you're going to be pretty damn capable at anything that you even have 2-3 dots in. Like dex 5 and ride 2, you're going to be able to ride a horse across a two foot plank spanning two buildings 90% of the time or something.
