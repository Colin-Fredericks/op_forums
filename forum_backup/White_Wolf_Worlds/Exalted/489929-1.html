                            If there are two different kind of Exalts that are related to them, then I'd expect at least one type to be primarily antagonistic. The other, perhaps less so, more co-operative or at least, as you say, just competitive.

                            Currently, the primary Exalted antagonist Exalts for Lunars are probably the Dragonblood. The Lunars and their barbarians in the threshold oppose the Realm satrapies.
                            The issue, is that these are not antagonists who dwell on the same turf as the Lunars. Some Lunars do of course live in or infiltrate the satrapies, but the Dragonblood's power doesn't reach into the wildnerness, deserts and deep forests where the Lunars have their main strongholds and biggest influence.
                            On the other hand, you have the Raksha out in the Wyld, clawing at the Lunars' domains. Again, some Lunars dwell in the Wyld, but it's not really their home turf.
                            But these are border wars, not Exalt types that live alongside the Lunars.

                            So an antagonist (or at least competitor) whose main environment is also the deep threshold would probably work best, without trampling over Lunar themes (I mean, if there was the "animal Exalted" as a stereotypical example, they'd have an obvious link with Lunars, but they'd be trampling over Lunar themes. Although Lunars live primarily in the deep threshold, and have a "barbarian aesthetic" that's not really their theme, you can still have a Lunar who lives in Chiaroscoro. So putting other Exalt types' power centres there puts them in competition with Lunars, but doesn't mean they're taking away thematic weight from the Lunars).

                            STing Bronze Age Exalted

                                                Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                That would be an obvious idea, but it seems to me that it wouldn't add a lot to the game: you'd just be making a more human, more powerful Raksha, essentially.
                                                I don't find that argument convincing. By that logic, Exigents wouldn't add a lot to the game: you'd just be making a more human, more powerful god, essentially. Just as there isn't some generic god, there isn't some generic Fair Folk: there's breadth there for the Chosen of a raksha to develop Charms rooted in the personal mythology her patron has built for herself over her lifetime. There's more than one way to tackle "dreams and the unreal" across two splats. I mean, Sidereals and Getimians are finding different spaces in which to riff on "fate-associated supernatural martial artists," which feels like a much more narrow space to me.

                                                The raksha could also provide a fairly unique feel as patrons. They are the exiled remnants of a proud people brought low, trapped between the home to which they cannot return and a world they love and hate that is antithetical to them by its nature. If one of them elevates a mortal, there's going to be some inherent contention and friction there that could make for a very interesting relationship. It's not exactly a secret that the raksha find Creation-born souls to be delicacies beyond compare; even a Fair Folk who scrupulously avoids it, or certainly would with her own Chosen, is going to carry some low-level tension to their interactions.

                                                Here's one original (ish) take, off the top of my head:
                                                There are times when a Fair Folk finds itself trapped in Creation with little time left to it -- or all too much time. When dying to a wound or the cruel pressure of calcification (or from within the empty doldrums of existence as a reliquary), these princes of chaos will sometimes reach out to the dreams of mortals. Every once in a great while, such a one is lucky: it finds a mortal with vast dreams that resonate with the faerie's Heart, and beckon it in. Sometimes this raksha come with sweet lies or cruel truths, sometimes bearing bargains fair and foul, and sometimes simply surging through. Whatever the course, the raksha claims this mortal's soul as its place of respite from a too-harsh world. And this Dreamer finds a spark lit within her, one that blossoms into a full flower of the Wyld's majesty and possibility.

                                                Despite this power, the Dreamer's course is imperiled, as the injured and hungry Fair Folk can only exert so much control over its appetite. If the Exalt does not take care to nurture her dreams and keep this legend nourished, her guest will either feed on that which is human in her until only her dreams remain -- and then swallow even those -- or briefly loose itself on the world in a terrible, beautiful bedlam. Caught between a sleeping life now impossible to resume and the monster that dwells in her soul, the Dreamer wields Charms that draw on her guest's resonant narrative and the dreams that drew it in. She charts a course to bring those dreams into the real.

                                                This would also echo that, "good person with a monster inside or monster with a good person inside," remark John made about Lunars ages ago, so that's fun, too. ^_^
