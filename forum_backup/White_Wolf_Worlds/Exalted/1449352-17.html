                "Perfect Defenses" in the 2E sense already only exist as a single Charm in 3E. If Seven Shadows Evasion is coming back, we won't know until we hit Solar Charms, late in the week.

                Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                It's true that the counter attack rule seems a bit specific for so few charms. I will ask on the discord if the charms which allows you to Build Power reflexively (like GCG or the abyssal mode of the dodgkng Charm to greate a phantom double) do count as your action for the turn or not
                ...
                People on the Discord seems to think that most Charms that create actions (like GCG or the Abyssal Charm on Mongoose-and-Cobra Escape) counts toward your limit of 2 actions per round, but you don't need to "flurry" them or take any penalty for using on another action on your turn (contrary to some Multi-attacks Charm that specifically say so in their text for example)
                That does seem to be the intention most of the time. However, GCG specifically creates a reflexive Build Power action, where Mongoose-And-Cobra Escape -> Spectral Deception doesn't. If that doesn't mean GCG's Build Power bypasses the action limit (as reflexive actions do, unless they specify otherwise), then that word really needs to be cut.

                I agree that it probably shouldn't bypass the limit. Letting someone take two actions (generating a weapon or armor for each), Build Power twice, then do the same thing the next turn before uncommitting the motes, is clearly abusive. But, as has perhaps become obvious, I don't always agree with the writers about how things "should" work, which is why I'd prefer them to tell me what they mean than rely on us making the same guesses.

                Somewhat related to all of this, my current working hypothesis is that whenever the book talks about an unspecified "action," it means a simple action, and it doesn't mean reflexive unless it says so. If someone finds statements or text which either support or refute that, please do let me know.

                Originally posted by Roadie View Post
                When it comes to Charms and the limitations on actions per turn, I think a lot of the ambiguity here comes from not having clear designation for cost, action type, duration, and so on for each Charm (especially action type). On top of that, it seems like most of the usage of Steps (given that Charms are Step 1 by default and you can only use 1 Charm per Step) is actually just to reinvent the Simple action type in a way that takes more words and that is slower to understand than just saying so.
                Yes, it does look like that. I like Step-based resolution, but it only works when it's clearly defined, and here it isn't - it seems like every action has at least a Step 1, but the book won't tell us that, let alone what other Steps (if any) they've got.

                Here's a puzzle which occurred to me recently: "During a fight scene, only one Charm may be used per step of combat... If a Charm has no listed Step, treat it as a Step 1." Read literally, this appears to mean that you cannot use an Excellency to enhance, say, Evidence-Discerning Method in combat. Outside of it, you can, because conflicting Step 1 Charms only matter "during a fight scene." Was that deliberate?

                "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin
