    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    On Withering/Decisive...

    I think that you can actually simplify the Exalted combat system a lot while still keeping withering/decisive. The idea of it modifying your initiative is quite a radical one, and has a lot of interesting strategy. But if you take out different dice pools, maybe simplify the way damage is done to create a 1-step (rather than 2-step attack), simplify Stealth, take out Clashes, simplify gambits (especially grapple!), simplify the movement rules, take out Onslaught penalties, take out mote regen, simplify battle groups, simplify being crashed, have everything use double 10s, etc, etc...
    I reckon you could end up with a reasonably simple system that still has attacks that change your initiative, and attacks that do damage but are based on your initiative, and has some kind of tactics. Of course, each thing you remove reduces possible tactics, so it's a question of what best to remove.
    I really have the feeling that Exalted 3e didn't use the Initiative system to it's full potential, especially for the ST. I'd really like they have gotten farther to represent what Initiative abstracts than just "martial advantage", as least for the ST, and I think it could go for players as well to a certain extent.

            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
            Hmmmm... what do you mean?
            I'm not a dev so my designing skills suxx. Here's an example of a "ST Charm" I wrote once to try out some ideas.
            Will you stop me, or save the children ?
            Cost : 5init
            Type : Reflexive
            Keyword : But.
            The luck every villain gets when the hero finally close on him. The ST designates something a PC care for deeply or has a positive intimacy toward. The designated object or person find itself in a terrible situation, usually through the confusion of the fight : the close walls start crumbling around it and threaten to smash it, a close magical relic activates and starts contaminating it with Wyld influence or Strange powers, a group of innocent bystanders get thrown in the middle of the confusion need protection else their fate sealed, etc …

            (I've used the shortest and simplest one as an example, you can find the others I did here)

            As you can see, my perspective (which is not everyone's perspective but I'M defending my opinion) is that Initiative can be used to abstract so much more about the flow of combat rather than just "I can strike you dead", at least from the ST perspective. I'm the kind of ST that doesn't care if the PC's win or loses as long as I was able to come up with reversals and unique situations, and I think an Initiative system could be one of the best thing ever to implement it in a "tactical fighting game". I think it's a shame they didn't go this way with the core, and I hope Essence will give some replacement/ideas for that.

                        Everything I've heard about the mechanics has gotten me really pumped for Essence. Except... I don't like the idea of flattening the curve. I think the different roles for the exalt types are a key part of the lore of the world and the fun of the game. Having solars be generally the best but the other celestials shining through with their special thing makes it interesting to me.

                        I am very concerned about the change in power dynamics between the dragon blooded and solars. The return of the solars (and abyssals and infernals) is supposed to be a world changing event. The future of Creation is up for grabs as the powers of oblivion and hell stake their claim on the world meanwhile individual people who stand up against the darkness are being invested with perfection from the sun. But if each solar (or equivalent) is only worth two dragon blooded... that's not important. That's the equivalent of 600 new dragon blooded when the Realm alone has 10,000 and all the entrenched artifacts and power that come with it.

                            Although it's not always that simple, of course, since a character with 100xp isn't twice as powerful as one with 50xp, and having more charms isn't always better than having more powerful charms.

                                I've been thinking about that a lot too and I'm still undecided. On the one hand yes as outlined there's a lot of issues with stuff, I've posted about that before.

                                On the other hand, in my not inconsiderable experience, a lot of new players kind of struggle with the idea.                                 Then again that asymmetric balance and different styles of play can be fun...then again-again so many people are excited for Essence because they want mixed groups, and that's why they won't buy a single book of 3e for the next ten years because to them Exalted is about an Alchemical, an Abyssal, a Sidereal and a DB bombing around Creation.

                                    To add to DrLoveMonkey's point :

                                    Exalted is a game which offers the possibility of playing completely overpowered characters, but which does nothing to educate players/STs about the dangers inherent to playing OP PCs, or to warn them about what specific builds/setups are likely to break their games.

                                    Players are handed dozens of different ways to derail their own campaigns and spoil the fun at their table, and are then expected to have both the system mastery and the personal maturity to thread the fine line carefully on their own.

                                    Don't get me wrong, I love this freedom that 3e offers to be as balanced or as broken as you like... but that is definitely something that can lead to terrible new player experiences. So in that respect, I think that flattening the curve should help limit these sorts of power disparities that can create problems at the table.

This goes the other way with other Exalts, who are as powerful or not as need be. PCs in general are able to beat eachother doens't mean that NPCs work on teh same logic. Dragon-Blooded PCs are exceptional people. Solars are next to them doing thier thing too. That's a conceit the game has currently really that needs to be kept in midn with the flattened curve. The system is trying ot be simple and foster cross-splat play. But the system also assumes NPCs don't use PC rules, and don't have to weaken PCs to a point of crippling to make up for the in-setting numbers advantages or no that the setting has.

                                            Second, I actually tink in 3e there's a general feel I get that had the united Realm of the Scarlet Empress had tried, they probably could fend-off all the Solars and other shit showing up. Not like, completely. The empire can't last forever, there were cracks a long time forming that Lunars and over-extension had shown, and so on. The Realm is a juggernaught of thousands of Dragon-Blooded who have put it sboot on the neck of the world despite hundreds of Celestial Exalted working against htem. It's also one who's leader is gone, and which quickly falls apart as being able to be that when it collapses and drags the rest of the global structure down with it. If the Empress were still there, the Realm very likely probably could carry along for a few more centuries, although dozens of Solar dominions and other things all over the map showed up as the influence shirnks. Think the Eastern Roman Empire and how it kind of slowly just withered away with the encorachment of Arabs, Persians, Armenians, Mongols, and eventually Turks. What the Realm is in now though is something more akin to the 5th Century Western Empire and is straignt-up falling apart and going to change hte world as a result.

                                            That said, the way Solar magic at least in the base system manifests, the edges come in acces to magic the Dragon-Blooded just can't (higher sorcery, Evocations, unique artifact creation, exptertise in some leements of leading armies and statescraft their magic expresses). Solars also ahve Supernal Ability in 3e, which means you get out of the gate pulling bullshit fast, creating wonders unseen in centuries and often not doable by most characters ins etting (Essence 5 is rare afterall but every SOlar has Supernal). Basically, Solars leverage resrouces better, they can get them better, and so on. But they and thier dervitives are still in the end of the day a huge extra side-swipe in a world that was already trying to capitalize on the Empress being gone. It's less that Solars won't matter, it's just that they're not (to me at least) the Number One Crisis in the world (Realm hegemony collapsing is) and happen to be pretty damned good in their nature in exploting it and making it worse.

                                            Kind of how I see it, had the Realm been united, they might have been able to put Solars in a similar situation Lunars have been for a while, while still losing stuff like Thorns or some other satrapies. As it is now, I kind of doubt they can survive with the Blessed Isle as a united polity, let alone a lot of satrapies not gobbled-up by new Solar empires and dominions about.
