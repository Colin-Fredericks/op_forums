                                    Since I don't want to derail the Charms thread into another attempt at hashing out general rules (as well as trying to keep the community's efforts to understand this all in one place for future reference, here's my confusion about Flowing/Still modes for Getimians:

                                    Originally posted by Essence
                                    Getimians may activate either Mode of a Charm based on whether or not they are presently Flowing or Still. If the Getimian has a majority of one type of motes, they may access that Mode. A Charm, when used, is always either Flowing or Still; there is no neutral Mode.
                                    (1) We don't know what happens if the pools are equal, and there is explicitly no "neutral" version - several Charms either cannot be used or have no effect without modes.
                                    (2) We also don't know whether the game checks the pools before or after paying for the Charm. If I have 3 Flowing 2 Still, then spend 2 Flowing on Icon-Obliterating Passion, which version do I get? (This may be why the other two 2-Mote Charms are oddly lacking in Modes.)
                                    (3) Two Charms (Devil-Queen's Court and Throne Smashing Ambition) refer instead to whether the Getimian used Flowing or Still Motes on the Charm. Either that's from an old version of the rules, or the sidebar which starts the section is, or those two Charms are explicit exceptions to the general rule. I do not know which.

                                    My current guess is actually that the lack of neutrals means it's always meant to pick a mode based on which Motes you use. I have no textual support for that, it would just be the cleanest to use in play.

                                                    So, I know the conversation has moved on a bit to specific charms, but I just wanted to bring up Anima, in case I'm missing something or reading something wrong. As written, it seems like every single mote spent contributes to your Anima, compared with 3e where you needed to spend 5 peripheral motes in a single instant to bump your Anima up. This seems...way way faster and harder to avoid building Anima for characters that probably want to keep Anima down, and sets up a pretty strong incentive to not use charms in combat at all, especially if the combat's likely to last more than a few rounds. In base 3e, you have the personal pool to draw from, and you can keep your peripheral spend low, but the choice is always "use less of my cool shit" vs. "use more of my cool shit". The way it's written in ExEss seems to be "use none of my cool shit" vs. "use some of it" and risk the Wyld Hunt. Charms are kinda the beating heart of Exalted, and so changing the incentive from "be strategic vs. go nuts" to "don't use charms vs. use some" doesn't feel great to me. Am I missing something that makes this less a problem than I think it is? Alternatively (and understanding we don't have the full ruleset), does the system break in any big ways if I just....double (or 1.5x or whatever) the anima threshold values?
