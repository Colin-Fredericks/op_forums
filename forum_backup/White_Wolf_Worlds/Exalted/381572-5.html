        That isn't my understanding of how Exigents are supposed to function - "Do whatever you want with them." They're going to have certain rules and mechanics and such, and even if they are primarily a homebrew splat, they'll still be intended to follow those rules and mechanics. But nobody will stop you from doing what you want with them, so there's that.

        That said, if someone in my group did want to play a one-trick pony buffer, I'd work with them to make a godblood with some custom charms, as I think a godblood would tend to fit the idea of having a powerful theme with a limited scope much better than one of the Exalted.

        Personally though, I'd steer clear from the idea of "Chosen of Lytek = only buffing," more for concept reasons than mechanical ones.

        The Exaltation is supposed to be a miracle beyond comprehension, a divine process that uplifts a mortal and confers upon them power beyond god or man, and Lytek is the god of this process. IMO, distilling that down to, "My Exigent can buff people well, and only buff people well," diminishes the concept of the Exaltation itself from this divine miracle into "just" a particularly powerful buff, and I wouldn't really want to give that idea too much play in my gaming group.

        I prefer to present the idea of Exaltation as something absolutely special and miraculous, so I try to avoid things that may lessen it. As such, I wouldn't really like the idea of seeing an Exigent of the god of Exaltation be something of a "lesser" or one-trick pony Exalt.

            I notice a little confusion that I feel I should clear up now before it skews all the great speculation I've been seeing.

            Questions like "I wonder if the Exigents will have an apotheosis path like the Devil-Tigers?" and "How good will the Exigents be at Evocations?" tell me that you are thinking of them as one kind of Exalt. They're not.

            Every Exigent is unique and works differently. You can have an Exigent who has an apotheosis path and you can have an Exigent who is all about Evocations.

            Exigents are there to be the custom Exalts of Whatever You Want.

                I would be very surprised if an Exigent of the God of Cooking were to be solely capable of Craft: Water but couldn't use his charms in any other way. Or if you had a "Cyclops" Exigent who can only shoot energy blasts out of his eyes and but had no capability to do anything else. I wouldn't really like it if Exigence were presented as something akin to super-powers in Marvel or what have you, though that goes back to my earlier point about not liking the idea of Exaltation being diminished too much.

                "Exalts of Whatever you Want" is a great soundbite, but probably not entirely accurate. These guys will still have limitations, guidelines and rules. The gameline won't support Exigents being capable of performing Creation-Slaying Oblivion Kick at Essence 1, even if that's what you really, really want your Exigent to do. I also don't know that Exigents would be written with the idea of "one-trick pony" Exigents who essentially have one charm and one charm only (shooting laserbeams from your eyes or whatever).

                While they are the, "Do whatever you want," Exalted in theory, that doesn't mean that in practice, you can have Exigents do whatever you want without guidelines, rules or restrictions.

                            So base on Holden's quote on a hypothetical chosen of Healing it seem that an Exigent of Lytek ought to have charm that involve non-buffing. I mean, the player that create the Exigent may be only interested in learning buffing charm, but the Exaltation itseft must have broader capabilities than that.

                            The Solar is not 'trying to be' anything. He is a physician-- since he's a Solar renowned for his medical acumen, let's say he's one of Creation's greatest physicians, in terms of skill. Because he's a Solar, that means he's going to be quite possibly the greatest doctor in the world, or certainly will stand among their ranks. That's what Solars are.

                            Then let's say you have Argent Arrow, Chosen of Nala Ku, God of Healing and the Healer's Art. Let's say the Healer is an Ability-based Exalt. What this means is that he's going to be excellent in every aspect of healing within Medicine--as you'd expect! It also means that his Craft Charms are going to focus on mending what's broken, his Socialize Charms will focus on mending broken pacts and alliances, his Presence Charms will focus on restoring lapsed Intimacies or mending broken hearts, his Performance Charms will promote good health or the recollection of lost memories in those who hear them, his Resistance Charms will focus on rapid healing, his combat Charms may suck health out of enemies and give them to himself, or disarm an enemy's weapons the way a surgeon's scalpel might cut away infection, etc.

                            The Solar's competence maxes out at "can probably cure any ill." So will the Healer, possibly, because you don't uh, really get any better than that? This doesn't say anything about his mote costs or prereqs, of course, because we don't know how strong of an Exalt he is.

                                                Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                                                At least the Fabian Cortez concept can actually defend themselves.

                                                I'd consider an Exalt type who couldn't really defend themselves something of a faux pas for a game I was running.
                                                My experience of such characters is that they often put other players in a situation where their character has become a bodyguard out of a sense of social obligation rather than because it's something they enjoy and even if the initial dynamic supports it - that relationship may become strained due to in-character and out of character factors. It also potentially puts the Storyteller in a situation where they feel a similar obligation to have NPCs be reluctant to attack the 'helpless' character because the odds of killing them would be far too high and fudging things

                                                A character who hasn't invested any ability to defend themselves is okay because this weakness isn't baked into how their powers work and they can invest in being less squashy if it becomes an issue or naturally as part of their character development.
                                                I'm never that big a fan of this sort of thing. Sometimes it just falls naturally out of the system and isn't anyone's fault, and 2e was one of those games, but it's kind of the default for RPGs that PCs are specialists who cover each other's weaknesses, so narrowing the kinds of weaknesses a PC is allowed to have too much, regardless of their strengths, seems very constraining.

                                                As a party, you don't want to pick a character who will totally drag the others down, but at the same time it's generally taken for granted that you will help shore up the others' weaknesses out of a sense of social obligation. If I'm the healer and someone gets hurt, I'm not going to resent them for getting hurt or refuse to heal them because I want to save my motes. In my first Exalted game I was playing a shirtless ascetic hobo with a giant beard and I ended up becoming the party face just because I was the best equipped for it and everyone had dumped their social traits. It can impose weird obligations on people, but I like that better than the thing where you build the character you want to play and then the GM says you're not allowed to play them because they're too bad and will hold the group back.
