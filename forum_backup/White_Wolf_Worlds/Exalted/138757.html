    Habits of highly effective Sidereals

    05-15-2014, 11:01 PM
    Since Sidereals have graduated from "sneaky-bastards-who-F*cked-everything-up" to trusted mentors, advisers and friends in 3e, let's talk about using them in the game. The Devs have promised more story opportunities besides teaching Sidereal martial arts, but I'd like to talk about roleplaying interactions. If you have any useful books, websites, or sources of inspiration or philosophy that a Sidereal might teach to teach to Solar (Abyssal, Infernal, Exigent, Dragon Blooded) student.

    Also, if you have any original proverbs, parables or fiction, I'd like to hear that as well.

    We're not looking for "Chicken Soup for the Exalted Soul" or how to be a life coach. We want words of wisdom that will guide heroes on the path to raising up or casting down mighty empires, turning the tide of battle or doing other, nifty heroic things.

    Since I'm starting, here goes:

    The Bhagavad Gita: The god Krishna encourages a warrior king to accept his destiny. Deeply steeped in spiritualism and philosophy, this is an excellent read.
    The Demon's Sermon on the Martial Arts. Required reading for any Dragon-blooded student of swordsmanship. The strong Buddhist bent makes it especially good source material

    And a parable I wrote:

    Once there was a maiden who came to a monastery seeking enlightenment.
    After three days, she was granted admittance.
    Wordlessly, the abbess ushered her to a small hut in the back, and lit the forge within.
    Wordlessly, the abbess took up a hammer and and a leather apron, and began to strike iron ingots, heating them, folding them until they became strong steel.
    After the sun had risen and set five times the maiden cried "I came to learn sutras! I came seeking enlightenment! Not pounding iron into steel!"
    Looking up from her labors, the abbess put down the hammer.

    "Is there a difference?" she asked.

                                          I don't have much philosophy, but I do have some tips from experience for someone playing a Sidereal friend of a solaroid circle.

                                          - Forget what the book says: don't make plans. Elaborate plans never survive contact with the real world, much less contact with the Exalted and even less so contact with the ST. The wise man steers.

                                          - A little bit of paranoia is not only not crazy but entirely justified in Creation, even more so if you're watching over a Solar circle. Consider at least in passing that every single person your circle meets is out to get the lot of you and how you'd deal with them if so. There's a pretty good chance you'll be right about a fifth of the time, minimum.

                                          - Communication saves lives.

                                          - Be polite, be efficient, be thought-provoking. You are trying to teach these people things for the benefit of Creation, and asshole mentors have a real chance of backfire, much more with the kind of princely temperament many Solars have. If you really need to make them pause, momentary confusion and koans work far better.

                                          - Let them know they're useful. Most Solars enjoy being the ones helping you once or twice - in most cases because they feel they can trust you a bit more if you absolutely need them, and in some cases because the Sun managed to land some actual decent people. Trying to distance yourself too much from them usually only leads to distrust, distrust leads to arguments, and arguments lead to Daiklaves which don't lead to the Dark Side because if you come to that point with a Solar then you're honestly screwed anyway.

                                              @ NuSoardGraphite & The Wizard of Oz :

                                              Sidereals are (supposed to be) trusted mentors just like solars are (supposed to be) invincible righteous heroes.

                                              Story-wise they generally lived up to their respective reputation except the interference of the great curse. Solars (more or less temporally) became evil monsters and sidereals became traitors because of that but's that's it.

                                              Now they are trusted advisors, in the shadows, of the dragonbloods. I don't think it's an accurate perception to still describe sidereals in the present time as traitors. They "switched sides" but they are generally fully loyal to their new "allegiance".

                                              Skillset-wise there are a lot of charms that, if you look carefully at, are very effective at counseling and supporting a leader. A few exemples :
                                              - Anima powers in general

                                              - for social interactions : Preservation of resolve, Presence in abscence technique, Force Decision, Cash and Murder Games
                                              - for combat : Life Gets Worse Approach, Trouble-Reduction Strategy, Meditation on War, Throne Shadow Style
                                              - for "adventure" : Sky and Rain Mantra, a lot of sail charms, Yellow Path, Blue Vervain Binding
                                              - general cases : Flawless Ceremony (version 2.5), Perfection in Life (2.5), A lot of lore/awareness charms to see the future or predict outcomes of choices, Terminal sanction (as it helps all the team instead fo allowing only he sidereal to interact/kill the spirit)

                                              The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.

                                                          Don't call them Sidereals . I think it was the Odyssey where the hero uses a thaumaturgical ritual to call up the ghost of an ancient seer to get advice? Maybe that was a Sid wearing a destiny, and he previously arranged for the Solars to get access to the ritual.

                                                          Also, have them usually be scarily accurate but occasionally completely wrong. As a player, too many half-truths would make me paranoid and wonder who this weirdo is, but normal good solid info makes it easy to lead up to an Oops moment. Which makes me think... read some Shadowrun. Specifically, any info about fixers and Mr Johnsons. They have a similar role and similar "will he give me a good deal or will he jump out of a teacup & backstab me?" relationship. The Sidereal is probably as worried about the Solars messing everything up as the Solars worry about getting betrayed by the Sid.

                                                          Knowing the present is much better than guessing the future. Loom, godly contacts, and weird charms can give you reliable info on what the enemy is doing right now. And makes it easier to guess what the Solars will do once they know all this, versus trying to force them to stick to a plan.
