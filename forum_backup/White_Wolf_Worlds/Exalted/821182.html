    Crossed Wings Denial: what does this do?

    02-01-2016, 04:42 PM
    For the record:
    "Cost: 6m, 4i
    Type: Reflexive
    The perfection of the martial artist’s defense is like the beauty of the crane, so absolute in its calm refinement that seemingly nothing could ever disturb it. The martial artist makes a (Wits + Martial Arts) roll that cannot be enhanced by magic, and banks all successes. Until her next turn, she may spend successes to add to her Parry on a one-for-one basis. Alternatively, she may expend a success after successfully blocking an attack to respond with a decisive counterattack."

    Why would I use this? Ever? I have no idea how this Charm even works. Are you spending the successes before the attack roll, making this 6m 4i for maybe +5 total Defense? That might be the worst Excellency in Exalted history - well, except for the Terrestrial version of the same Charm, where you'd be luck to get +3 Defense for that same investment. Do you apply them after the attack, or as a non-Charm bonus, despite nothing in the official wording indicating that? Why are you paying 4i to get a resource which can be cashed in for Counterattacks if you could just take Full Defense for no Motes and fewer Initiative to get a non-Charm Defense bonus and unlimited Counterattacks with your Form Charm?

    I don't know. I am baffled. I've become reasonably proficient in 3E rules, but I need help making some sense of what this is trying to accomplish.

                                              Originally posted by Korhal_IV View Post
                                              [COLOR=#252c2f]Generally, a counterattack lands before the attack that caused it, ne?
                                              Ne.

                                              There is no "generally". There is no general case laid out anywhere. Like multiattacks, the actual rules of every Counterattack Charm are spelled out in the Charm which creates them, and they all work differently. The only place "Counterattack" appears outside of specific Charm descriptions is a Keyword - which, we have established, these Charms lack*. Since "make Withering and Decisive Counterattacks" is not a rules action the way that something like "make Rush and Disengage actions" would be, I could argue that that Charm does nothing, or that it lets you treat diseases, and technically have the same level of rules backing as saying that it lets you attack anyone who swings at you before their attack resolves.

                                              But obviously, that's a silly example. "Counterattack" may not be a rules term, but it is comprehensible English. Let's see if we can make a reasonable theory of authorial intent. Among the Solar combat charms, there are 5 Counterattack Charms**:
                                              • Solar Cross-Counter (3m, 1i, 1wp): After taking Withering damage, make a Decisive attack with damage equal to the damage you just took, rather than your own Initiative.
                                              • Solar Counterattack (3m): After being hit but before damage is rolled, make an otherwise-normal Decisive attack.
                                              • Ready in Eight Directions Stance (5m): Until your next turn, respond to every attack which hits with a Solar Counter Attack - your Initiative does not reset until then.
                                              • Flashing Edge of Dawn (4m 1wp): After being hit but before damage is rolled, make a Withering Attack. Instead of gaining the Initiative the target loses, roll it against them as Decisive damage.
                                              • Over-And-Under Method (7m, 1wp): Make a Clash attack (Fervent Blow) and use Flashing Edge of Dawn against the same attack. Note that this will never let you actually hit the enemy twice: you'd have to lose the Clash to ever trigger the Counterattack.

                                              Note that none of these can "land before the attack that caused it". Note that none of them can increase your Initiative, either. And that none of them will let you hit the same person out of turn more than once.

                                              So I'm glad that your group has found an interpretation of Crane Style that works in your game. However, I don't think that interpretation works across EX3 in general: the rules are vague and often confusing, and their most obvious interpretation - allowing scenelong Initiative-increasing Counterattacks which resolve before the attacks which trigger them, and can possibly happen 6-7 times off of a single trigger - is outrageously broken, letting Dragon-Bloods be vastly superior to native Solar Charms in their area of focus. I hope that someone quicker than I caught this during the errata period, or that the writers noticed it themselves, and it will be fixed.

                                              But until that time, I urge STs to strongly consider banning or seriously reworking the style. I like the core concept of Crane more than almost any other Martial Art, but its current execution is a mistake, and can cause serious problems.


                                              * Do not underestimate how big of a hole in the rules that omission is, either - it means that, for example, if you attack someone and they Clash you, you can counterattack the Clash. But that's a really easy fix compared to how dysfunctional the rest of Crane is.
                                              ** There are three more in other Abilities: Asp Bites Its Tail turns attempts to undercut you back on the person who took them, Effective Counterargument raises someone else's Resolve after an Influence action is rolled, and Rousing Backlash Assault has the same problems with assuming "counterattack" would be defined elsewhere in the book that Crane does.
