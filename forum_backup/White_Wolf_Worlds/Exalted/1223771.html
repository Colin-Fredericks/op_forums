                  Is the syntax meant to be "tell me about YOUR half-caste characters"? Or just a generic comparison of types?

                  If type:

                  Demon-Blood > Ghost-Blood > God-Blood > Fae-Blooded > Half-Caste

                  Demon-Blooded top my list (with Ghost-Blooded a close second). They have the "stuck between two worlds" schtick while simultaneously being the most adept at fighting both lineages. It's a great way to get a "Witcher" or "Devil/Ghost-Hunter" vibe if you want a more subtle game (less hulking Essence 6 demon warlords ripping down buildings - more shapeshifters, parasites and possessive-spirits..... where the X-Blooded are aware of the corruption, but can't necessarily reveal how they know).

                  God-Blooded are rather lackluster - and usually I've seen them as higher-end canon-fodder. They're just "human but awesome" and the ability to sense sanctums is "useful" but hardly iconic.

                  Fae-Blooded are more lackluster - the ability to resist Wyld mutation is pretty niche - and they don't really apotheosize into anything. Like God-Blooded, they're merely "human - but more awesome!" with no strong signature trick (unless you're exposed to the Wyld a lot.... but the majority of the Wyld is on the outskirts of creation, meaning you're the most useful in places where no one cares).

                  Half-Caste are perfectly named. From the perspective of Gods and Exalts, being "Half-Caste" almost suggests your human side is the inferior one. However, the Lunar Exalted are likely the most likely to produce Half-Castes.... many of which would thus be Beastmen.... and thus, from a human perspective, it's the beastial-side which is inferior. So these characters have a strong pull to wrestle with their identity and humanity - but this isn't seen often in play. They often fall into the trope of "human - but more awesome!" (alongside God & Fae blooded) except they also lack a signature "type" ability (like the other 4) and their charm mechanics were bonkers.

                  Also, worth noting "for the record":

                  Just because (group A - who once colonized) finds a term troublesome because of "negative associations" - doesn't mean (group B - who was colonized) find it offensive. The English-language (and culture) tends to obsess on what is said - while other languages (and cultures) focus on how it is said. Nothing about 2E Half-Castes implied insult nor offense.

                  For comparison, we have dozens of half-caste type terms in Spanish - used regularly - and absolutely none of them are viewed as pejoratives today. Luckily, Spanish speakers don't have that hang-up.... but periodically, an English-speaker hears "mulatta" or "sambo" or "negra" and has a heart-attack. Us "natives" care less about what is said (every time an English word is banned, a new one will just be invented anyways) and more about how it is said (which really is all that matters).

                  Worth noting because avoiding half-caste terms can be equally offensive, since it almost implies "instead of half-and-half, let me pretend one of your parents didn't exist". Calling my step-father a "sambo", my brother "un moreno", my cousin "una mulatta", etc is all simply accurate - doing the "American thing" and lumping them into Red/Black/White/Yellow would just confuse the shit out of them.

                  The fact that Half-Castes (in 2E) do fit an "inbetween two worlds" role actually makes the term appropriate. In a game with necrophilia, beastiality, incest, etc - having a term for a group with "complex issues of identity and heritage" is the least offensive thing to have. If anything, kudos to them for using the term correctly and elaborating on the difficulties Half-Castes in Exalted face.

                          So these characters have a strong pull to wrestle with their identity and humanity - but this isn't seen often in play. They often fall into the trope of "human - but more awesome!"
                          I don't think they do actually.

                          God-Bloods, Elemental-Bloods, Fae-Bloods, Demon-Bloods and Ghost-Bloods have a lot to wrestle with.
                          Half-Solars? Not really. Solars are super-humans with sun-themes. So you're half-human, half-super human. So a weaker super human.

                          With the other kinds, an ST can do slightly more interesting things with them. I had some Were-cats, though they ended up not being used much because the PCs weren't interested, they of course were the kids of a Lunar. You could have Dhampyrs as well.
                          But there's not really much of a struggle between the two halves of their nature the way there is for a Demon-Blood.*

                          *This is partly why I consider half-Exalts to be very dull. Half-Solars far more than the other ones though. Although half-Sidereals are fairly dull too.

                          Also, Half-Caste isn't a great name, because they're not half-Twilight or whatever.

                          Lastly, if you find a word neutral, and others find it offensive, is it that much hassle to avoid it? Especially a word used in a fantasy setting that could easily be replaced with some other mystic term? Half-Caste is a boring, uninspiring, and not very accurate term.*

                          *For a boring and uninspiring set of characters, IMO. Though, as I said, more could be done with half-Lunars and half-Abyssals than the game has really bothered with. Still, half-Lunars are less interesting than beastmen anyway.


                          Originally posted by Salagimsim
                          Is the syntax meant to be "tell me about YOUR half-caste characters"? Or just a generic comparison of types?
                          I wondered this too?

                          Demon-Blood > Ghost-Blood > God-Blood > Fae-Blooded > Half-Caste
                          Yeah, I pretty much agree with this. Fae-Blooded are just a bit wacky, and can be intermediaries, but power-wise they're not much use. Most half-Exalts are pretty dull. Whereas Demon-Bloods and Ghost-Bloods have more interesting powers (and Ghost-Bloods are relatively common).
                          God-Blooded are useful for an ST though, as they are often interlinked with a social structure and organisation, and can be given quite a wide variety of powers.

                          In the last game I ran, I wanted to have a social villain who was far weaker than the PCs. His name was Jin the Just, King of the Southern Djala, and he was an god-blood. His powers were a)he could make plants entangle things, and b)he could jump into a tree and jump out of another tree. What this allowed him to do is run away if there was ever a fight. His real strength was his army and political pull (and the fact his mother was a jungle goddess featured a lot as a king in the jungle), I just wanted to give him powers so he didn't die in some random fight, and the PCs couldn't easily just beat him up like they did most kings.
                          In the end, of course, he stood up to the PCs rather than just manipulating them, and the Full Moon gave him a pox-handshake, but in an Essence 5 Solar/Lunar game, I think an Essence 1 god-blood did pretty well to last 2 years as a villain.

                                                      I should probably talk about the Moonborn that's become increasingly prominent in my game of late.

                                                          Since we're sharing half-blooded characters:

                                                          During a 2E game, I had a god-blooded character that exalted as a Solar. My divine parent was a goddess heavily influenced by Athena, the Greek goddess. Our Creation-Athena, named Linnea, was similar to Athena in that she was a maiden goddess. Meaning she really didn't care for men (didn't hate them, just had no use for them). She "lay" with women to have her god-blooded children via a unique charm. Nearly all her children since she came into existence have been daughters. My character was the second son she'd ever had, and it was not a good relationship. When I exalted as a Dawn caste, she felt that my character owed her for my upbringing. She would occasionally show up and make requests/demands of my character, which tended to end with them arguing with each other. The other players in our group always found the interactions amusing, even going so far as to take Linnea's side against their circle mate.

                                                          As for 3E, I have yet to reprise this character, though it has been on my mind lately. As for the nature of my parentage, I'd probably work with the ST to take a unique supernatural Merit or two to signify my heritage.
