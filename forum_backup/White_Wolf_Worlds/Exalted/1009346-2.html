    When it comes to developing the mythos of creating Exalted, I would incline more to the idea that a god who is sacrificed is handling something so beyond them that they're consumed (doing so regardless for the sake of a higher ideal), rather than it being a question of how much effort you put into it.

    Originally posted by Kyeudo
    If you want to be the best, you can't hold anything back.
    This makes me think of how good advice for running a race is to pace yourself early on, so that you'll have a lot of energy at the end to outrun everybody who tired themselves out at the start.

    ​Or how in certain kinds of sport fighting, "not holding anything back" means you injure yourself at least as badly as your opponent.

    ​None of these examples are really significant to a description of mythology, but I am bemused by the general sentiment.

    ​The story of John Henry is folklore verging on propaganda, and not something I find to be an especially useful reference point.

    ​Not to mention all kinds of endeavours, even ones based on physical prowess, in which working to the point of death or disfigurement is not only not useful, but not really possible.

        We also have to remember that Exigents can run the full range of power levels... from the equivalent of the Dragon-Blooded to as powerful as the Solars. A God that would be consumed creating an Exigent as powerful as a Solar may not be entirely consumed if it chose to create an Exigent with equivalent power to a Dragon-Blood. Some Gods may not be able to create an Exigent as powerful as a Solar even if they give their existence to the process... and may have to settle for an Exigent somewhere lower on the power scale.

        It all really depends on what the God creating an Exigent needs... does he need a Champion as powerful as a Solar at the cost of his existence... or would an Exigent of lesser power suffice (giving him the chance to survive the process). Not every God who is given the chance to create an Exigent will create the most powerful one they can... but rather will only create one as powerful as they need.

        ​As for Kyeudo's responses... while all answers are a response to a question, not all responses are answers. His response was so vague that it didn't actually answer anything... but seemed to provide the only answer anyone could expect. Just because someone can think of something or have a need for something, doesn't mean that an Exigent can fill that roll. Yes, Exigents will fill the role of custom Exalts... but that doesn't mean one can create the most powerful and broken splat that can beat anything the setting has with just it's pinky finger... there will be guidelines and limitations to what can be done... and what it will cost. So no... "if you can think it then yes" is not an acceptable answer.

                        Originally posted by AtG View Post
                        I would answer no-ish, mostly because the game's rules just aren't really designed for playing animals, or non-people generally. And the game is about people, heroic people, not heroic animals.
                        More than that, I think there's a question of intent and practicality.

                        ​If one is referring to the san animals of Halta specifically, then in a game focused on Halta specifically, I would say that it can be reasonably serviceable. Perhaps a bit of a challenge, some things may strain a bit at times, but apart from that being a description of Exalted in general, animals are at least playable enough to facilitate Lunars turning into them, and different people have different ideas about how Other the intellects of san animals should be in the first place.

                        ​Conversely, in games where they've gone rather far afield from somewhere like Halta, I would wonder at the possibility of using the idea primarily for the sake of novelty, in which case, well, people can still do whatever they want, I would just question whether or not this is something that will be very fulfilling or last long.

                        ​If a person has a very very intriguing character idea to actually take advantage of the distinction, then I'm sure they can keep it going.

                        ​
                        Originally posted by TheCountAlucard
                        That assumes that a given god can even decide how much of itself it's going to invest
                        I don't think it necessarily does; I think the statement "the god will create the most powerful Exalt that they need" does not necessarily attribute a conscious decision to the process.

                        ​All of this being said, I think the idea behind the mythology that the books will present to us will attribute occasions in which Exigents are at the height of their potential power will be less about the depth of sacrifice of the god involved and more by the grace of the Unconquered Sun. That's not saying that the Sun is making a conscious decision either, rather that it's just a boost that is made available by the whole thing using power created and bestowed by him.

                        ​I think one thing that the internal logic of the setting might contain that could discount the idea of it being based off of the depth of one's sacrifice is that I think that, for most gods, being consumed utterly is a fairly low bar to jump. That is to say that if any salt god or dog of the unbroken earth creates any kind of Exalt, they will be used up in the process.

                                            So, I fully approve of the idea of non-human exigents, at least in the case of things being run as NPCs. I'll likely veto ones trying to be run as PCs in things like the PbP I am running, if only for the reason of 'interacting with people', but in certain campaigns even they might be allowed, especially if it was set in somewhere like Halta, and the rest of the party was cool with it. But I actually had a villain who later turned ally be an animal Exigent.

                                            See, Loquesh, God of Sea Life, tired of his post in Heaven after the Usurpation, and gave up his titles in the celestial halls, to go and live with those who he had tended to and loved since the time of the Primordials. He made a home in the North-ish West, deep in the ocean, where he created a society of sea life both animal, beastmen [saving the children of Lunars who fell during the Usurpation was one of the reasons he left originally], and his own children, intelligent godblooded animals and humans alike. And then the Deathlords appeared, hundreds of years later. Loquesh was no god of Battle, but the Silver Prince ripped out of the underworld, and took over less than a day's swim away from where Loquesh's underwater society lay.

                                            And they were advancing, on dark boats with spirits of death upon them, unknowingly traveling over his head. Tainting his waters. And so Loquesh held a contest, to all creatures of his land, to find His Champion. A contest of skill, wit, strength, and general prowess, where the winner would receive his essence, contained within an Exigence, granted by his former friend, the Unconquered Sun, in this time of need.

                                            Against all odds, a normal, simple Dolphin, who understood the importance with no more intelligence than any of her kind... bested every opponent, Beastman, Godblooded, even great underwater predators. It proved cunning and capable, and was infused with the essence of Loquesh, becoming the Chosen of Sea Creatures. It was only after being bestowed the exigence that it gained human-like intelligence to begin with - though Loquesh could communicate with sea animal life much like Survival Solars could - but this consumed Loquesh. So Iiiiiqiiiii was created, and became leader of this underwater nation - where she began to defend her home against any entry from the Deathlord's, turning the zone into a veritable 'here thar be dragons' on Skullstone's maps. Out of the way enough to be deemed insignificant to fight, years of guerilla warfare lead to the Deathlord abandoning routes leading over her home.

                                            And left her with no idea what to do with herself. And so she became the leader, with no idea how to lead. The Loqueshans, previously saviors of drowning mortals became isolationist, sinking the ships of anyone daring to enter their domain, led by their great leader who possessed the might of every creature of the sea.


                                            And then the party, thrown off course after a battle they nearly died in, against the Silver Prince's fleet... wandered into Her territory.

                                            Long story short, after a few sessions, they all became friends and allies, and worked together to defeat Skull Stone completely. But before that, she nearly killed them.


                                            I probably broke setting 'rules' with making this story and character, but the key fact is that I didn't care. It made for a fun story that my players really enjoyed playing with, and they all really liked Iiiiqiiii [partly because of how I said their name, and voiced them in general.. It was very squeaky...] and I was glad I did it. And if the Exigent book, coming sometime after the next Epoch, tells me this is impossible, I'll still do it again.

                                                ​While I will agree that the Exigents will fill whatever role one needs them to fill there will still be some obvious limitations on the power that can be given. There really is no way that the mechanics of the system will allow for a player to create an Exigent that can defeat any opponent with ease... because that would make the setting more or less pointless... as such a character could never loose... and having such a character as an antagonist means that no one that goes up against it could ever win... not the Solars... not even the Unconquered Sun (and I seriously doubt that the Unconquered Sun would ever allow any God to create an Exigent that could beat him... even if another Incarna were to sacrifice their existence to do so).

                                                ​Exigents are not meant to be an "I WIN!!!" button for the game... but rather a means to allow Exalts that don't fit into the standard Solar, Lunar, Sidereal or Dragon-Blooded molds to exist. The requirements to create the Ultimate Exalt that can not be defeated by anyone or anything is likely so high that it may as well not be considered.

                                                ​Additionally, we don't know what is needed to empower an Exigent. Yes, we are given an example of a God of the Harvest sacrificing its very existence to empower a single Exigent (who's power level we do not know), but we are also given examples of other Exigents... at least two of which likely didn't result in the spirit empowering them sacrificing their existence to do so (unless they've decided that Plentimon and Shalrina no longer exist... which there has been no mention of). The power levels of these Exigent examples have not been described in any significant way... within the setting or outside of it (actual mechanics). Even the one example provided in the Antagonists section suggests that this lone example is somewhere between Dragon-Blooded and Sidereal in power (going by the amount of Essence at her disposal), and her charms seem to reinforce this assessment.

                                                ​Thus, saying that any level of power is possible without the information to back up such a claim is both foolish and very inaccurate. Yes, it might be possible once we are given the rules for creating Exigents... but until then we can't make that assumption. Just because something can be thought of, doesn't mean it will be possible to do. One of the few things that has been stated on the subject is that it may be possible to create an Exigent that is more powerful than a Solar, but in a very narrow field... which is true of most Exalts. Abyssals will be better at Necromancy than Solars ever will be... and their necrotic charms to drain essence from opponents and inflict penalties on them is also likely beyond what Solars can do. The same is true of Lunar Shapeshifting, Sidereal target number reduction or the Dragon-Blooded's connection to the base elements. In this manner, an Exigent could surpass a Solar in a very narrow field... such as Crafting... or Movement... or any single Ability or Attribute. This is because of the God's purview over such an area. To Create an Exigent so powerful that it could backhand both the Unconquered Sun and Malfeas at the same time with ease would require a God who's purview conceivably covers such an ability... a God theoretically more powerful that either the Unconquered Sun or Malfeas... of which there isn't (if there were, it would lead the Incarna and the Primordial War would likely have been won with ease). Even your Celestial God of Victory example would be limited... by what is considered a Victory... the Ultimate General may be victorious in any battle... but what of other areas that aren't related to war and fighting... such as winning the hearts of the people (social combat) or criminal endeavors (where stealth and underhandedness are more often the decider of victory than one's skill with a blade)? Not every General is a likeable person... nor skilled in atypical fighting. He might never be defeated in battle... but is only so when people fight him... go after any other target and

                                                ​Exigents will be able to do many things that other Exalts may or may not be able to do... in ways that other Exalts usually can't... but what and how they do this is still a big unknown. Saying "Yes" to even the wildest and most game-breaking ideas with no knowledge of what will actually be possible is like giving someone a blank check to cash without knowing how much is in your account before hand... they may take only a little... or try to take more than you can actually cover.

                                                ​To put it at its most basic... we really have no idea what the Exigents will be able to do... and how much it will cost the Gods that empower them. To say anything other that "We don't know" is just setting people up for huge disappointment if the power levels of Exigents turns out to be anything less than we imagine them to be (and going by your posts... you imagine them to be the most powerful Exalts ever... or at least have the potential to be, and won't be satisfied if they are anything less than that). I do hope that when we do get to the Exigents... you won't be disappointed.
