    Resplendencies, Effect Points, and Paradox

    06-28-2020, 11:18 PM
    Since we’ve got a while to wait before the official version, and I’ve needed some creative distraction, I’ve been playing around with some ideas for Sidereal rules, and I’m looking for some opinions.
    In previous editions, Astrological effects are mostly wrapped up in Resplendent Destinies. An RD is an identity that the Sidereal can use to interact with the world (getting around the Arcane Fate), and it grants access to special powers. When these powers have resource costs they come in two flavors:
    • Paradox: In 1e, this is what Sidereals had instead of Limit. Store up 10 points, and a bad thing happens: Pattern Bite. This is a list of 10 things you really don’t want to happen, and one is chosen at random. It might be sudden aggravated damage, or the death of a loved one. Techniques exist for bleeding off Paradox, but they’re pretty inconvenient to do in the middle of a story, so in practical terms this creates a rate limit (or at least friction) on your Astrological powers.
    • Effect Points: When you create the RD, your overall successes create a pool of Effect Points that can be spent on powers. When these points run out, the RD ends. Since you can only have one RD of each College at a time, this creates tension; if you really like the powers of a College, you probably don’t want to get attached to that identity.
    My question is this: How critical do you think these are, in their traditional forms, to the Sidereal experience? Does Astrology need the threat of Paradox to keeping it in check? Is it important that Sidereals lose access to the Resplendency powers of Colleges they want to use for identities? I’d like to pick this apart a bit but I’d like to hear if anyone is really attached to these mechanics as-is, and if so, why. If either were meaningfully different in 3e, would you yell "ruined!!" and never play Exalted again on principle?

    (Note, I already have ideas about what I might do differently, but right now I'm more interested in other perspectives on why the traditional versions are good or bad)

      Paradox is mostly there as a WoD-homage that didn't pan out. And I think that Astrology as it was written in 1e and 2e was too much of a Decker-like mess to be worth much of the time or effort.       Right now we know that apparently a lot of the Resplendency powers are probably just going to be roleld into teh main Sidereal Charmset (as poetic as it is, they are probalby not doing one-Charm-per-Scripture-line this time around so we get less four-Charm trees, so might as well use that room). And to be frank, I think that's probably going to be for the best. I think astrological effects need to be simplified greatly to be more usable and a lot of that is binning the tratis and rolls associated with them, and having the exotic stuff be just accessable via Charms. Basically the Charm system exists to let Sidereal be Sidereals, and they don't need to ahve a whole set of Not-Charms to also do things meant to be signatorially Sidereal IMHO.

              They are fine as a concept. Sidereals have special access to the very currents of fate and can use it to make Your Mom jokes, so abusing it hurts.

Maybe both, maybe none, I have no idea.

                                  "reality itself" is a valid term, but I'd be more specific about what in reality is rejecting them. Fate is an integral part of Creation, and precedes the divine rebellion.

                                  After the war the rules changed. Reincarnation, for example, broke. Why wouldn't Fate also break a little?

                                  Sidereals ever since became agents of fate, and are trusted to enforce it to their best judgment. Many understand (mostly because they don't understand jack) that on some level whatever they do pushes fate forward.
                                  BUT, no matter how much their gift is from fate, it was bestowed on people, not machines.

                                  (this works best if you keep the Fate, the Loom and its caretakers inscrutable. Maybe the Maidens can make sense of how a pattern spider really thinks and works, and that itself should be cause for alarm)
                                  This is a good way to play "Fate is a permissive boss but don't abuse it" type of mechanic.


                                  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                                  An alternative view can be more attuned to the sidereal herself:
                                  A Sidereal's gift is born of Fate, but the exalt herself is a human.
                                  When she looks at the Threads that connect what is and what should be she understands what her role in it is, ties herself to it. At this point to avert her gaze or to reject that place she will unavoidably tear against these threads.

                                  That rejection won't always go smoothly, and paradox is the consequence. She may fix it by adopting a role that easily ties itself to fate (something very archetypal not unlike the destinies she wears) and obediently playing the type, or she may tear again and again until it bleeds and scabs.

                                  This one is better for a "conflict of loyalties" type of mechanic, presenting the player with frequent omens that foretell something to be (when she doesn't seek them on her own) and having a few of these occasionally pull in a direction she does not desire.
