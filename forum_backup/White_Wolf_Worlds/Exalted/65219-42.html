    Cathak Cainan is held up as an ideal candidate for Scarlet Emperor, except that he's near the end of his life and wouldn't last long on the throne. Fans have asked why the Bronze Faction doesn't just give him a Peach of Immortality to extend his life. The answer given so far is that it's not so easy to get them out of heaven, but that unsatisfactory, given the immense influence Sidereal elders have (hell, the Salary Background outright *says* that at Sal 5, they regularly dine on the things!). So, this is my solution:

    Instead of PoI being harvested by the bushel from entire orchards, they are rare. Like, incredibly so. There are Divine Orchards, and many of the trees do give forth peaches. Most of these simply taste like the greatest food one will ever have. A few trees are special, bearing fruit that actually has mechanical effects, usually restoring all WP or Essence or HLs, or bestowing significant visions.

    Once a century, however, one of these trees bears a true Peach of Immortality...and only one. The problem is, there is no way to tell where and when it ripens. Some master Gardeners have made a heavenly reputation for developing a knack for sensing the exceptional fruit, although even they can be mistaken.

    Yu-Shan's highest security takes immediate custody of the PoI, hiding it in an undisclosed location (many assume they must be kept in the Jade Pleasure Dome, so as to be readily available to the Incarnae on demand). Aside from the Incarnae and the highest ranked Gods, the peaches are never dispersed, esp not to public affairs such as even the most exclusive party (where the other peaches can readily be found). In the whole of history, Mortals and Exalted have eaten of this fruit but a handful of times, and then under the most extraordinary of situations that have since become legend and myth.

    Currently, Heaven has a store of only a couple of Peaches, as the Incarnae have taken to gorging themselves on the treats as they play the Games, and have almost depleted the store of them, so soon even those will be gone. However, the Chief Gardener's whiskers have been twitching recently, and he has begun scouring the orchards for the new Peach he believes must be ripening even now...

            Here’s something I like to use with demon related or malphean characters

            The Five Element Cycle is not the one of the original foundations of creation but was added on after the fact to provide an easier tool for the gods to use in the absence of the primordials direct intervention. (think of a GUI rather than say, DOS)

            The original form(s) underlying creation are the four fold contradictions.
            Law & Chaos
            [Law became earth]
            When the primordials wrenched creation from the maelstrom of chaos they did so by enacting law upon the formlessness of the Wyld, thus boundaries were created and “I” became “I am” and “I am not”
            The Principles of Form and Function
            Now that creation is, and no longer was, or would be, the next step was to establish the concepts of form and function.
            On one side was Form, here the concepts of matter were arranged, undifferentiated matter became shapes, and those shapes told the matter what It could do. Matter pushed into the form of a snake could quicken and bite, matter in the form of a tree would grow and sprout. Matter assumed the traits of the things it emulated
            At the same time opposite form, Function was established. In this ephemeral region only the ideas mattered a tree was a tree, because a tree it was. In this realm you could smash the forms, but the ideas would remain and exist independent of matter.
            [Form became Wood, Function Became Water]
            The Principles of Motion and Stillness
            Now that thing could be, and now that things could become, now needed to do. To put it simply the next contradiction was to verb, and to not verb. Action and Cessation.
            [Motion Became Fire, Stillness Became Air]
            The Final Contradiction
            The Primordials looked upon creation and saw that it was good, but observed that in the end creation would never be more than they had made it to be, so they gathered together and set creation spinning in the infinite wild forming a great disc with a spindle in the middle and created (or possible discovered) the final contradiction, which remains unchanged. Life and Death.
            Connected to and pulled between the two contradictions of life and death, creation gained the ability to evolve in more than just the mechanical ways originally envisioned by the primordials, new things and concepts trickle in through life, and old concepts and things trickle out through death.


            If True the Well of Udr is part of the physical connection to the concept of “death”, the shadowlands and the underworld are a result of the neverborn’s metaphysical “weight” pulling creation “down” toward death and stretching the disc of creation along an axis it was never meant to be. (imagine a bead on a string and you start to pull down on it, the difference between where it is, and where it should be)
            That would also mean that there should be an equal yet opposite site connected to “life”, which I would argue is hinted at by the groves of yu shan and the peaches of immortality. If this headcannon is true, then carefully following the roots of the trees would lead to a cave or spring with a connection to a “wellspring of life”
            This would also imply that utilizing a bagua incorporating these eight elements one could more accurately manipulate creation than just with a five element cycle manipulation. Of course since this would have to originate with forbidden yozi knowledge (or neverborn) it would be blasphemous at best.
            It doesn’t really change much in the grand scheme of things, but it does help in my mind explain how sorcery can have such far reaching effects that seem inconsistent with the elemental cycles, why the poles positioning are so messed up given the five element cycle, why the elemental pole of earth was so badly damaged when the scarlet empress returned (boundaries and law were smashed to make the escape happen), explain the peaches of immortality, the origin of the underworld, and a few other little pieces here and there (like calibration being the point in creations rotation where all the poles and concepts resync as the core of law adjusts to all the changes and strengthens/weakens boundaries to allow the concepts to stabilize)
            4 Dimensions
            Life <-> Death (Z Axis)
            Motion <-> Stillness (Y Axis)
            Function <-> Form (X Axis)
            Law <-> Chaos (Inside to Outside)

            Ironically a 4 cube Orthagonal Projection produces an octagonal figure that matches in many ways a bagua so that’s a nice little bonus

            You can even map out the points on a circle and split it into two haves (Earth, Water, Death, Air, Wyld, Wood, Life, Fire) If Earth and Wyld are “equally Yin/Yang” you can have the sides pretty fairly split into two sides of a yin / yang cycle (Water Death Air, as the YIN; Wood Life Fire, as the YANG.

            This is really just an amusing bit of flavor to differentiate “malphean” thaumaturgy from “creation” thaumaturgy, i do like that you can take it and use it to generate quests to "prove" or "disprove" the heretical theories. (cause seriously, the positioning of the elemental poles makes no damn sense in the context of the 5 elemental cycle.)

                Autochthon:

                One of Autochthon's major themes is transgression. His sickness transgresses the immortality of Primordials. His inventions, ideas, and creations transgress prior limits (and good sense, and sometimes sanity). His dogma establishes boundaries that define and are defined by transgression. His rebellion against his fellow Titans was a transgression of law. He transgressed the hierarchy by uplifting humanity with the invention of exaltation, thus letting humans transgress weak human nature. He transgressed his own Primordial nature by mutilating his own soul-hierarchy (for Science!)

                His fetich soul, the Core, is a Trickster deva (when it's awake, that is.) Some mythological Tricksters are creators, and so is Autochthon, and his fetich to some extent too. The Core defines metaphysical and social boundaries by transgressing them, and defines dogma by mocking it. It transgresses by daring what others will not dare. It's a creature of contradiction and hypocrisy -- and is there any greater contradiction than a dying immortal? It identifies weakness by mocking it, so that others can transcend their faults -- even mocking its greater self. It improves others and pushes them to transcend their limits (and transgress the limits set on them by others) by teaching through trickery, and uplifting through technology and discovery (some mythological Tricksters are culture heroes, too). Many mythological Tricksters are also imprudent fools, and a short-sighted, short-attention-span, impulsive, self-destructively curious Titan has showed himself to be a fool.

                It's also a pain in the ass when trying to establish an orderly, rigidly controlled, closed society like Autochthonia, so it's probably a good thing for Autochthonians that the Core is asleep.



                Sidereal caste marks:

                They're not European astrological symbols. They’re stylistically similar to Old Realm glyphs and constellation diagrams, but simpler in design.

                * Journeys: A stylized ship.
                * Serenity: A stylized lute.
                * Battles: A boxlike symbol representing strategic arrangements on a battlefield.
                * Endings: A stylized scythe.
                * Secrets: Jupiter has never explained her symbol, although her Chosen believe it represents something specific besides the Maiden herself.

                    Its kinda become a thing that the Realm in my games has become something different than it is usually depicted as.

                    First off, Regent Fokuf is not a bumbling fool, rather this is an affected persona, He is actually a rather calculating bureaucrat and patriot. A mortal, to be sure, but one who is keenly aware his nation is ruled by people so aware and assured of their family's innate superiority that he makes use of this arrogance for his own ends. When the Empress disappeared, he and several other high-ranking bureaucrats decided to elevate V'Neef to the Crown Princess. It should also be noted that House V'Neef is also different, with V'Neef being much younger, not in her majority, but still held as the Head of State, due to her being a living descendant of her Scarlet Augustness, but without the baggage of Mnemon and Ragara. Thus, the Great House of V'Neef was created out of Outcastes and those who were unhappy with their positions in the other clans, making a new Empress for the Imperium to function with. Fokuf secretly hopes that V'Neef being so young and naive will smooth over relationships with her siblings and that the other houses will finally send more funding to the Imperial Military so that more expeditions to the North can be prosecuted. It infuriates him to no end that the Bull of the North still is alive, but one step at a time. No sense in starting fires overseas when your own house is a mess, after all.

                    In a similar vein, I've always held that Ma-Ha-Suchi isn't the insane "Kill everyone and burn Civilization" guy he's depicted in 2e. Oh, there's a guy like that, and he lives in MHS' old pad, but that is one of his many lieutenants. Aware that the Realm and those damnable Sidereals will track his movements, he created the insano imposter as a red herring while he plots and schemes elsewhere. Oh, he really doesn't want to burn down Creation or Civlization; he quite likes Civilization, with its soft beds, nice wines and actual floors, but he is deeply affronted about his banishment from what he still views as the Center of all, and forced to be confined to the Threshold. To that end, his process of press ganging newly exalted Lunars into his cadre has allowed him a unique opportunity of not only creating the force needed to play at assymetrical warfare with both the Realm and also several other, smaller nations, but also allowing him to be largely separate from it all. In fact, he often considers if he should pursue revitalizing Outer Sanctuary...

                    And that leaves us to....

                    After the Usurpation, the Waning Moon assassin and Infiltration expert, Joyous Cobra, decided to not go quietly into the Wyld, but decided to use her infiltration skills to wage war on the nasceant Shogunate. Working out of the Summer Mountains, she assembled various downtrodden tribes, beastmen, wyld mutants, anyone she could get, and made fighting forces out of them, harrying the entire southeast. Calling themselves the Outer Sanctuary because they represented the last sanctuary of peace outside the Shogunate. Existing in one form or another for several centuries, until Peerless Ocelot and Holok took it upon themselves to finally get rid of the meddling Elder. However, this was not the end, as Joyous Cobra's reincanration, a mercenary who dubbed himself the "Massive Manager" reunified the disparate tribes in the area, rallying around a new weapon that would sieze fear into the Shogunate and later, the Realm. This new form of siege strider, able to launch super-heavy explosives many leagues, would be mass-produced and sent abroad, to ensure that the Realm would be hit on multiple fronts by such weapons. Sadly, Massive Manager was caught in a gigantic explosion at the forge where these weapons were being produced, and has been presumed lost. However, those remianing fighters, their warrior cultures, and supposedly even the land itself do not forget, and await the return of their hero to set them free from the tyranny of foreign tyrants.

                                                        Ah. Cheers mate. (EDIT: Or rather, mates.)

                                                        Going through my "Borrowed Headcanon" document (which is otherwise filled with things from this very thread), I found this gem of an attempt to explain the Lunar Bond as it existed in 2E. Pretty brilliant, I reckon; always did mean to re-post it.

                                                        Originally posted by Phigment (RPG.net)
                                                        All the Incarnae could reasonably have predicted that something was going to exist after the Primordial War ended, that their chosen would exist, and Creation would exist, and that the one would have lasting effects on the other. And have planned for the eventuality. You can see a lot of that planning. But they all think differently, and plan for future problems differently.

                                                        The Sun is a straightforward solutions guy. His answer to the problems of an uncertain future is to be so good that he can handle anything. And the anything he expects to handle is direct threats; monsters from the Wyld, leftover behemoths, renegade Primordials, and so forth. Discreet, external, unambiguously Bad Stuff. So, he built his chosen that way, like him, because that's the best way to be. If a Solar can identify a problem, he can work, and prepare, and eventually, probably pretty quickly, stand in front of that problem and shut it down cold. The Sun doesn't anticipate problems, or plan for them, he identifies them and then solves them.

                                                        Meanwhile, the Maidens are cheaters. They're the opposite. They intentionally look ahead in the game, via crazy precognition magic and Samsara, and then, at the right time, just conveniently happen to have a solution already prepared for the problem. When ghosts show up, having never previously existed, well, gosh, it's sure convenient that their chosen have magic that puts the animate dead to rest, isn't it? Gosh, it's like they knew... And so, they skip all the intermediate steps and just insert the solutions to problems. This doesn't always work, because the Maidens still like a surprise now and then, plus the illusion of Free Will, so they just peek ahead a little bit, and stop as soon as they think they understand enough of the problem to solve it, rather than getting a complete walkthrough. This is frustrating for their chosen, because frequently they get to stroll around making it look easy, but sometimes they drop into a situation ready for a zombie apocalypse and realize they're actually dealing with a flash mob of unruly cosplayers.

                                                        Luna, on the other hand, plans for the future all the time, but she doesn't have cheaty future-looking powers. She plans the old-fashioned way, by thinking hard, sometimes with multiple brains at once. And she's powerful, but not infinitely powerful, so she can't always plan on steamrolling any threats. Plus, she's good friends with some who CAN steamroll just about any threat, so why should she bother? It's no fun to be, literally, a pale imitation of the Unconquered Sun. If there's a problem that can be solved with force, she can always pick up the phone and call the sun. If there's a problem that can be foreseen, she doesn't even have to call the Maidens, they'll know about it and deal with it on their own. Luna plans for the problems that Sun can't steamroll, and the Maidens can't cheap-shot. She's there to pop up when some jackass Ishvara thinks he's discovered kryptonite for the Sun and Creation is his for the taking, to deliver a quick kidney punch and send him crying back to Nirupadhika. Luna thinks sideways, to solve problems that weren't anticipated. And, I maintain, her chosen are built from that same philosophy.

                                                        So, then, what does this have to do with giving every Lunar a solar bondmate?

                                                        The answer is that the solar bond does not, in the end, exist for the benefit of the Lunars. It also does not exist for the benefit of the Solars. Luna wasn't trying to help either of those groups; they didn't exist yet when she was forging exaltions.

                                                        Solar Bond exists for the benefit of Creation, which Luna is Fiercely Protective of, after all.

                                                        I think Luna, in all her tricksy, tricksy, glory, looked around at things and tried to figure out what Creation would need the most. She tried to figure out what the greatest threats to Creation would be, and make sure her Chosen were set up to do something about it. And, being the sort of outside-the-box thinker that she is, the being whose first act upon creation was to win a knife fight with infinite potential varieties of herself and eat them, Luna realized that the greatest possible threats to Creation, in the long run, stemmed from the Solar Exalted.

                                                        Solars, in their purviews, are unbeatable. Like their patron, if a Solar sets about doing something, it will be done, unless opposed by someone of equivalent power.

                                                        Therefore, so long as the Solars exist and are reliable, Creation is safe. It's protected. It's secure. That's good.

                                                        It follows, then, that any real threat to Creation is necessarily going to involve either some enemy neutralizing the Solars, or the Solars themselves turning on Creation and becoming a threat. To threaten Creation, you must either take the Solars out of play, or turn the Solars themselves against the world.

                                                        Luna is the only patron who could make this determination. The Sun could conceive of his chosen losing, but there's nothing he can do to protect against that which he hasn't already done. He's made them the best he can. And he can no more imagine them turning against the world en mass than he can contemplate turning traitor himself. It's not how he operates. And the Maidens are totally unreliable. If they look ahead and see that Creation should end that way, they'll sit by and let it happen, and praise the workings of Samsara. Plus, there's no cheat code for beating Solars.

                                                        So, Luna spat on her hands and set to work, and built a solution Luna Style. To protect the world, she needed her chosen to protect the Solars; protect the solars from getting chumped when some unanticipated weakness popped up to take them off the field, and to protect their loyalty; to ensure that most of them always served Creation, and would not abandon it to build their own castles in the Wyld or tear it down with their own hands.

                                                        And, being Luna, she drew on her experience with Gaia and utilized her most powerful and terrible weapon: love. Gaia wished to abandon Creation, and would have, but Luna loved her, and was loved by her in turn, and so anchored her to the world. Luna bound Gaia to the good of Creation with bonds of affection, and not even the other titans could pull those apart.

                                                        So, Luna recreated that, in miniature, with her chosen. Each Lunar loves one Solar, values him, will fight for him. And each Solar, no matter how far he grows from humanity, no matter how old he becomes, has one person who loves him, and will seek him out, sooner or later, and stand with him.

                                                        Capture a Solar alive, and send him to the most distant shores of the Faraway, lost and unable to reincarnate? There is one being who will never stop looking for that solar, never give up, because love does not give up, and that being is equipped with every advantage Luna could give to survive any hardship and trail her prey across any distance. Bury a solar at the bottom of the sea, and the lunar will become a fish and swim to the rescue. Entomb a solar in the underworld, and the lunar will turn into a ghost and hunt him still. And whatever means you used to disable the solar, whatever trick or weakness you employed, will not stop the lunar, because the lunar changes like the moon, and can change weaknesses as easily as changing a face.

                                                        And, if a solar were to turn against Creation, become its enemy, still the Lunar is there. When the neverborn whisper to an Abyssal that the world has nothing good in it, that all is ashes and pain, there's one being who will stand there and call them liars, out of love. One being that can't be scourged by Resonance, can't be driven off by horror. No abyssal can truly say, "I hate all things, for all things hate me." When the Yozi sing their songs of failure and spite and envy, when they say that an Infernal has been given nothing good by Creation, and should give it back evil in turn, there's always a Lunar there to call them liars, and offer one good thing, at least. One being they can't be punished for associating with, for making compromises to, one being who will not flinch at monstrous form and inhuman changes. No infernal can truly say, "I despise all things, for all things despise me."

                                                        Obviously, this plan hasn't been perfect. The Lunars were driven off, for a time, and the solars were neutralized, and then many turned against that which they should protect, just as Luna thought might happen. But that's when you need a plan of last resort the most. And there's still time for this plan to bear fruit, because Luna's chosen are still there, they still love, and they were selected to never, ever, quit.
