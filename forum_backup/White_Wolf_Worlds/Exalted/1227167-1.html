        Originally posted by MoroseMorgan View Post

        I'm guessing going with an Artifact/Evocation model, where instead of having to actually attune to every charm, you attune to one "base" charm and then have evocations off of that? You could still have addons to the base that do need additional attunement, as a knob for balance.
        That's pretty close. Essentially my alchemicals have a caste and a favored material and they can buy evocations for them at favored costs. Caste determines anima powers and which set of three attributes that become your primary attributes for character building. Favored determines the secondary three. Using the 2E set of attribute to material associations each material has three attributes. In the case of overlap (both jade and soulsteel have stamina for instance) you can buy that attribute up to 6 at char gen. Otherwise the attribute cap is 5+ (essence/2 round down).

        Certain charms that are central to every champion and possibly meta-evocations will be available as straight charm purchases. My conceit there is that the alchemical herself is an N/A artifact that has certain evocations built off of it. So far these include the excellencies and ox-body.

        To facilitate swapping and modding of your 'charm' set, I think that at most it would require minor surgery at a difficulty of the artifact rating that you're installing and impossible to fail (failure takes longer, a botch takes longer and does a level of health damage so there's still some incentive to have a professional install them) and new charms can only be constructed at the VATS. Furthermore, I'm thinking to avoid a sunk cost type situation like with evocations, they'll be able to essentially buy a number of favored evocations that can be selected from any installed favored charm and rearranged every time you install/uninstall a charm. So if you decide you want to drop your piston-driven megaton hammer (which is now an artifact smashfist with evocations) and want to install a different favored charm in its place you can reassign the evocations you bought for PDMH in the new charm. I want to avoid having giant pools of xp spent on evocations that are only available when they're installed.

                                                    Originally posted by Sith_Happens View Post

                                                    That sounds a lot more like a feature than a bug.
                                                    It’s a consideration. Really everything here is something to consider, including “giving Alchemicals reconfigurable charms is actually a bad idea and we shouldn’t do it again.”

                                                    Honestly, it might be worth going back and asking “why are we giving Alchemicals reconfiguable charms in the first place?”

                                                    The answers I’ve come up with so far for that are as follows:

                                                    * It serves as an external power check on Alchemicals, so that they cannot use all their charms at once, which helps set their rough position on the power scale without having to give them entirely useless charms.
                                                    * It feels techy and cool, so it helps set the atmosphere and make Alchemicals distinctive from other Exalted.
                                                    * It helps tie Alchemicals into the industrial infrastructure and society of the Eight Nations, making everything more cohesive.

                                                    And here are some fundamental issues with it:

                                                    * On the other hand, it can make characters feel less distinctive, since they don’t necessarily have iconic charms anymore.
                                                    * Or a character who does have a favorite set of charms might just skip using the mechanic entirely, which will either make their character much less effective overall or simply point out that the mechanic is unnecessary.
                                                    * Unless abilities and attributes are equally discounted as charms, it might be difficult to get enough dice together to really make use of the swapping mechanic.
                                                    * Most players are used to creating a team of specialists who each have a particular skill that enables the party as a whole to handle a wide range of tasks. Suddenly being able to have an entire team of diplomancers might cause problems and complaints of “you’re getting into my niche” without explicitly examining the idea.

                                                    For that last one, designing in fairly obvious and somewhat narrow builds that work well in concert might be a good idea. Like a good build for Reading Intentions that can give ammo to the Persuader build, while an Inspirer build helps set the mood in the background to give extra bonuses to the others. Since it’s difficult to do three things at once, it can be useful to have those as three different people, so setting things up like that in the charms might help bring that out.
