                    Two solutions I've used in the past are:

                    1)Specialties cannot be so broad as to apply to every situation, and specialties must be a descriptive phrasing using 2 or more words.

                    2)Specialties are your 5th dot. Skills go to 4 dots and to get access to the 5th, you can only do so while using the specialty. Usually in combination with rule 1. Reason for this is to head off some common complaints and problems from maxing-out assumptions making scaling challenges really dumb if everyone is just maxed out all the time.

                    If I were to go with a streamlined solution, I'd just nix them altogether. You don't lose much if everyone is functionally losing a die they get almost all the time anyhow. Do they really value-add in mental or storytelling consideration or is it just another avenue for optimization? My experience is without houserules, it's almost always the latter.

                    Incentive is not permission or justification.

                        Something to keep in mind is that if you completely nix Specialities, or make them the fifth dot, is that now can mess with Dragon-Blooded a fair bit. Sure, it's just one more die or one more Defense to a Solar, but to a DB, it is one more die, one more Defense, one more Charm die, and potentially one more automatic Charm success or Charm Defense.

                        Now making them slightly more restrictive I can understand (and I kinda like that they must be a descriptive phrase with 2 or more words), but it is important to note that they are no longer "just" one more die.
