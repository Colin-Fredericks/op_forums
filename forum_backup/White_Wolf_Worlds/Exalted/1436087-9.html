    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
    Some general comments on this stuff:
    Exalted meant to present a big world, with Solars near the top. Solars are, by design, like dropping Goku into Berserk, and other Celestials get there by Ess 2 or 3. 3e made more effort to actually depict this, which caught a lot of people by surprise. But the answer for "why have so much space devoted to stuff that Solars clown on" is twofold: first, to make Solars actually feel like god mode, and second, so that the game still functions if you want to play Dragon-Blooded, or God-Blooded, or even mortals. (I agree that mortals could use some more widgets -- I'd have cut at least half the fiddly bits Exalted get and given more options for baseline. But the system works fine with mortals and correctly depicts the world the game is selling you, it's just rather bland.) I think the game needed a sidebar that explains this; it's in the lore, but they needed to step out of narrative voice somewhere and be like "yes, really." I keep seeing people running an Ess 4 Solar circle with multiple combat-optimized characters and complaining that nothing's challenging, but that's intentional! This is the game that people pitch by saying you can suplex Cthulhu; in the past, when the numbers prevented you from actually doing that, people were not happy!
    For me this crosses the line into being actively hostile towards Storytellers.

    CRB: "Oh man Storyteller, have we got some antagonists for you!"

    ST: "Okay, hit me. DnD is leaving you a high bar to measure up to. Liches, dragons, lairs that themselves come alive and attack the players, walking brains that can hijack player's bodies, fire breathing chimeric creatures born of wizard experiments gone wrong."

    CRB: "Pfft, that's nothing. How about a giant of the Wyld with a humanoid body and two terrified deer heads on its shoulders. It's so big it wields polearms in one hand and with its two heads it can attack twice in one turn!"

    ST: "Okay damn, that sounds like an exalted enemy!"

    CRB: "PFFT no, it's so weak that all rolling combat will do is waste 35 minutes of your session. It can barely hit with its regular attack and its double attack it makes at -3 dice. Don't bother, your PCs are just TOO AWESOME."

    ST: "Right...I see down here there's also a cockitrice with poison breath and a stare that locks you in-"

    HDNP: "Yeah sorry, you're gonna want to ignore that, it's too weak to be worth running."

    ST: "But I just read through all its abilities and it seems really cool. It even has this fun one where you can medusa-style trick it into looking at itself and trap it in its own gaze, which is a great way to kill it without having to fight it."

    HDNP: "Sorry, you wasted your time. We just printed that thing to show off how awesome your PCs are without even trying. There is no way your PCs are going to need to use that mirror trick, because the thing is basically harmless. If you like I've got two dozen other entries that are equally cool, but all exist for the sole purpose of showing off how badass your players are, rather than being run in an actual game."

    ST: "Yo, hang on, it says here at that start of the antagonist chapter that enemies with more than 6 dice are supposed to be real serious threats to even specialized charac-"

    CRB: "No, no, no, no, no, look away from that section, we don't talk about that section."

    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
    The game gives you the power to design the kind of character you want, but it also gives you the responsibility to ensure that you actually want what you're making. It's not their ST or the game's responsibility to nerf Solar players enough that fights are challenging, it's their responsibility. Most of the problems I've seen come from people not taking that responsibility seriously enough, and being surprised when they get what they told the game they wanted. If you're having this problem with your group, here's my advice: emphasize that the descriptions of what different ratings mean are correct, and then tell players to come up with an Exalt-scale character concept and then give them the Attributes, Abilities, and Merits that suit that concept, without limitations, making clear that it's fine to have a bunch of 5s if that suits the premise. This is the correct mindset to be in, and helps to fix the thing where players are like "oh, I'll just take Melee 5 because it's so cheap!" If there's a player who still wants Dex 5, Melee 5 on Gandalf, you should make sure they understand what that means, but at that point it's not your responsibility to make armies or big monsters challenging to them, and if they can't enjoy the game because they clown on everything, you should be offering to let them retire that character or giving them a plot reason to retool their stats, not trying to change the entire setting. Customers who order steak get steak, even if they really wanted chicken; adding a houserule that you can't order steak is kind of missing the point.
    That could work, but if under the current system people are still spending a tiny amount of resources to get their stats like that, I can't imagine that telling them there's no limits and if they want their characters can just have 5s in literally everything that's fine will change it. I could really emphasize what the dots mean even in the current system I think. I mean, Star Trek Adventures does it's totally open chargen the way it does for a reason, and it works, I think for that same reason.

    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
    In a system that gives you a wide breadth of options and power levels in character creation, you're stuck either having 80% of builds being newbie traps, or having the upper 20% being unusually strong. You need to have this mindset going into the game, because in 3e, pretty much everything is viable. If you have a player thinking "I want to be a decent fighter, so obviously 2/1/4 physical tertiary isn't viable", they're going to misjudge the power level of what they're actually creating. A game where everyone takes a 5 in their combat ability is not an average game and a party where everyone does that is not an average party; the content isn't aimed at people who do that because those people are playing an unusually strong party. If you want to do that, that's fine, but I have limited sympathy for people who ignore what the game tells them, take huge combat stats because It's What You Do, and then get annoyed that they've priced themselves out of two-thirds of the game's fights. It needs to work that way so that people who want to play the game the intended way, as well as people who just suck at mechanics and do what the book tells them, can, without denying people the ability to be a legendary fighter if they want
    I agree with the premise, but I disagree with the numbers. I've been in several exalted groups, many of which have have zero overlap among players, and several have had brand new players to Exalted, and pretty much all of them have just assumed that the 5 combat ability is just what you're basically supposed to do. Which makes sense, it kind of feels like it's what you're supposed to do.

    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
    I've mostly been framing this issue as a criticism of players who don't engage with the game on its own terms, and I think that's true, but I also think this is one of the fundamental problems with 3e's mechanical depth: if you are a Solar who goes pretty deep on your Supernal, you almost always win automatically at that thing, which is fine, but it also means that 80% of your character's mechanical complexity is concentrated on the thing where you win automatically, which makes that complexity feel ill-used. It's like having a Rube Goldberg machine to press a win button; combining five Charms to get some ludicrous gigantic number is fun the first time you do it, but that's not enough to justify how fiddly the high-end stuff is.
    Yeah that's definitely a bit of an issue. That's particularly an issue with Sail, because it has a thousand abilities that let you boost your successes up into the 20+ range but then don't actually matter.

    The only real application for that kind of thing is to make sure that you do the difficulty 5 tasks with pretty much 100% reliability.
