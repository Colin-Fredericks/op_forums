        She flicked the big lizard's reigns and he began to canter towards the wall, well away from the gates. His path curved, moving parallel to the stone as he came closer. Ash released the reigns, got loose from her stirrups, and pulled her legs up. She crouched atop her saddle, holding out her arms for balance, and eyed the wall moving past. When she felt ready, she whistled a few low notes.

        King's canter became a run as the beast stood up on his hind legs. Ash flung herself up and away as he rose, easily clearing the wall's spiked top. She hit the ground on the other side and rolled, coming to a graceful stop on her feet. Then she stood, dusted herself off, and took stock of her situation.

        She'd landed some way away from any buildings. The ground where she was was flattened and marked with all kinds of trails - perhaps it was a staging area, or a place to hold overstock. . She began walking in the direction of the gates.

        Ash figured the refinery offices would be in a spot that overlooked traffic, and she was right. There was a wide, squat, one-story building just off the main track through the gates. It was the only building in the complex with walls and a roof of stone - this place was not meant to make way for fire, but to resist it for as long as possible. Ash studied the exterior from the shadow of a building across the way.

                            Then, bold as brass, she walked right up to the front door and knocked.

                            She heard footsteps from inside. A moment later, a mousy little man in flat cap opened the door. He looked up at her and squinted, as if he were unaccustomed to the light.

                            "Yes, can I help you?" he said, in a tone that conveyed no desire to help. "I don't believe you're with the Company."

                            Ash crossed her arms and glared. "Stationmaster sent me," she said, brusquely. "We've got word of a shipment comin' from Roca Roja - tools and equipment. The manifest says they're yours."

                            The little man sniffed. "Well, it's about time. We put in that order a month ago. Do they expect us to make quota digging with our hands? Huh!" He shook his head. "When is it arriving?"

                            "Marsday eve. Maybe Jupiter morn. Stationmaster wants to make sure there's no logjam with outbound freight." Ash looked over the man's head into the room behind him. It was dusty, and dim. The shuttered windows barely let in enough light to see by. A lamp on a table covered in small clay blocks stood beside a series of shelves loaded with paper. There were two desks against the far wall - one covered in yet more paper, and the other covered in dust.

                            "Yes? So? You've got our shipping records for this week. I hand-delivered them four days ago!" His lips turned down and he sniffed again.

                            Ash looked down at him again, her arms still crossed. "A yeddim ate them."

                            There was a long silence. The little man's face shifted rapidly from confusion, followed by disbelief, then anger, and finally disbelief again. "Are you-" he started, then stopped when he saw Ash's expression. "...nevermind. Fine. Fine! I don't pretend to understand your operation. Give me a moment - should still have the typeset ready..." He trailed off and started mumbling to himself as he turned around. Ash followed him inside and shut the door behind her.

                            On close inspection, the the little blocks on the table were made of clay, with letters and numbers stamped into them. The little man pulled a small box from a rack, which was full of these blocks in neat little rows, and squinted at it. "I'll need to find the master..." he said to himself, and carried the box over to the table. He shuffled through the stacks of paper on the desk, and pulled out a sheet. "Yes, this one..."

                            Ash watched him as he carried it back to the table and began comparing the sheet in his hand to the blocks in the box. She herself could barely make out the writing on either, the light being what it was.

                            A few moments passed in silence while the little man ran his hands over the blocks, apparently intending to check every single character for correctness.

                            Then there was loud crack, like thunder. Ash and the little man both gave a start, and looked towards the window. "What was-" the little man began, but then the noise came again. It was thunder, and it sounded close.

                            "Strange," the little man said. He put the paper in his hand down on the table and went to open the shutter. "We're out of season for storms..."

                            He was saying what Ash was thinking. And she could only think of one reason why there would be thunder nearby when there wasn't a cloud in the sky.

                            • She forgot about the shipping records and bolted out the door.
                            • She snatched the master sheet off the table and bolted out the door.
                            • She forced herself to stay calm and said "Gods only know the weather. You mind hurryin' this along? It's gonna rain, I don't wanna be caught in it."
                            • Something else?


                            OOC Commentary
                            Creation has printing presses in most civilized places, but the sophistication of these presses varies. Wood-block printing is the most common kind, but presses using wood or clay moveable type can be found in the urban centers of the Realm and Threshold. Printing presses using metal removable type are very rare and very expensive - the technology to mass-manufacture type blocks does not exist, so each one is hand-tooled.

                                                She forced herself to stay calm and said "Gods only know the weather. You mind hurryin' this along? It's gonna rain, I don't wanna be caught in it."

                                                The little man ignored her, and opened the shutter. The light he let in gave Ash a newfound appreciation for just how dark it was in here - how could anyone spend all day reading like this? Shaking her head, Ash went to join the little man at the window.

                                                They looked outside. There were no clouds, but Ash had figured as much. The thunder boomed one more time, and some time passed after that in relative silence.

                                                The little man clicked his tongue, shook his head, and closed the shutter. He returned to his work, scanning the master sheet as though nothing had happened. When he was finally satisfied, he loaded the tray and its blocks into a press, brushed ink onto it from a pot he took from underneath the table, and laid a blank sheet onto the block. One pull of a lever stamped clay and paper together, and then it was done.

                                                Ash took the newly printed document and left without another word.

                                                She fairly ran back to the wall where she'd come in and scrambled over it with her own hands and feet. Her exit was less grateful than her entrance, but at the time she was more concerned with speed than style. She whistled for King, and once the big lizard stopped chasing desert hares they got underway.

                                                As near as she could tell, the noises she'd heard came from the south, in the shantytown. King cantered in that direction while Ash peered into the distance, looking for signs of disturbance.

                                                Before too long, she spotted one - a pale-skinned short woman, sprinting away from the camp almost on the opposite heading from Ash. Seres was being chased by a sizeable group of frontier roughs, brandishing a range of tools and hollering. Seres was easily outpacing them, but they were a tenacious bunch.

                                                Ash adjusted her hold on the reigns.
                                                • She urged King into a gallop, heading to intercept Seres.
                                                • She nudged King to turn and canter on, aiming for the empty space between Seres and her pursuers.
                                                • She turned King to face the mob and slowed to a quick trot - not to lead them, but to come at them from behind.
