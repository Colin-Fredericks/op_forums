    Are Exigents Yoo much of a generic catch-all?

    08-15-2017, 03:13 PM
    Does anyone else feel that Exigents could become too much of a "solution" for every problem? Just in my personal experience and what I see on these forums it feels like they are showing up disproportionately as NPCs and Antagonists perfectly endowed with the godly power to exactly fit the situation as it arises. I think this stems from the way they have been presented, Exigents so far have no coherent theme as a splat the way Solars, DBs, Lunars, Sidereals, or Abyssals do, they just seem to be a toolbox to do anything but are missing those narrative walls which challenge players to work within them or break them.

    I personally think there never should have been Exigents in the first place, to me a much more interesting area to explore would have been beefed up god-blooded and a exploration of familial expectation, inheritance, and even apotheosis, but that seems like a moot thought at this point as the gameline has committed to Exigents as the mortal expression of lesser gods. So instead I wonder what could added or even just highlighted more clearly to make Exigents feel like a splat of their own rather than just a toolbox to make the exalt of expressly the purview you need or for homebrewing Alchemicals and Infernals.

      I think it's easy to white-room the perfect Exigent for such-and-such a problem, but in practice it shouldn't be too much of an issue.

      I mean, say you're talking on a forum about which Exalt type is best at stabbing people, you could be like "Hah, the Exigent of the God of stabbing people would kick your Invincible Sword Princess's ass!" (Which they probably wouldn't, but they might be able to compete)

      But real games have lots of different problems, and most of them are not stabbing-related. What does the Exigent of the God of stabbing people do at a party?

      I'll admit, I frankly find God-Blooded more interesting from a fluff perspective, but I'm not worried about Exigents being a solution for every problem, because any particular Exigent is only going to be good at solving a small number of problems (or, perhaps, they can try and solve any problem, but only in a particular way, which means they're probably not going to be the best at it. If you're the Exigent of the God of revolutions, you'll be awesome at fomenting revolution, and you could certainly try dealing with social problems through revolution, but what if revolution isn't the best way to solve the nation's lack of doctors? So you're not going to be the perfect tool for every problem).

      I am worried about how the devs are going to write a book that helps you do homebrew, because I think that's actually really hard to do well (and, I'll admit, I think I'd find Exigent PCs a lot of hassle as an ST, because there's so much to approve), but I'm not worried about them being the perfect solution for every problem.

      Mostly, Exigents just sound like a lot of work to me. They* could, potentially, be awesome. I think they're also really useful for the ST, if you have a great idea for a really bizarre or unique NPC who wouldn't fit as anything else.
      I just can dread a player coming along with a really unbalanced Exigent idea they've based on the guidelines and hassling me to approve it all. And they might have a good fluff idea, but then we'd have to go and argue over every single homebrew charm... with some players, it'd be great, with others it could be a giant hassle, and it's not like I can tell my players that some of them can play Exigents and some of them can't. That's just mean.

      *I don't mean Exigents as a whole might be awesome, I mean any specific Exigent might be awesome. I think Exigents will be really variable, not something you could judge the coolness of as a whole, but it really gives players the potential to come up with something extremely interesting. Or something awful.

                      I dunno, I see what you mean, but I think that's more about how people on the forums are home-brewing them than anything inherent in their concept.

                      There is definitely a temptation to tailor an exigent to a situation, especially since exigents seem to be often created specifically to address some problem. However what we know of exigents is that they're empowered by individual gods with themes relevant to that god.

                      I'm not entirely sure that it makes sense for an exigent to be tailored to any specific problem. Consider that a god's powers are a reflection of their place and purpose. A god of the field is gonna have field powers, a god of the river is gonna have river powers etc. Now to me, if they're encountering something that they can't deal with on their own and they feel it's worth spending some of themselves to create an exigent, that doesn't seem like it would be directly related to their power set. If the god can solve the problem with a pure application of their powers, what need have they of the exigent?

                      I dunno, I'm gonna reserve final judgement until we actually have the exigents book in hand (hopefully after lunars)

                      As for powered up god bloods, they seem to me to have a very different place in the setting. An exigent may cost a good chunk of the God's power to create, if not kill them outright. Such a sacrifice is very different animal than "I slept with a mortal. It was awesome. Oh hey, super-powerful offspring I can maybe sorta guide/teach that have all my powers."

                                          Kind of hard to get a handle on. What's "too often" or "disproportionately" here? What's an example / cutoff of what you'd call being "perfectly endowed with the godly power to exactly fit the situation as it arises"?

                                          With them, I actually kind of feel more, now, like you won't see a lot of them for a couple reasons

                                          1) Mechanical complexity, obviously. People will want to make characters with similar depth / progression frequency and granularity to existing characters, and Exalted 3 Charms seem kind of complex balanced (in the sense of being built to work well and be balanced in the system when tested, but not prioritising it really clearly be so obvious why that's the case - this is something the devs actually said!). So that means writing and testing a lot of mechanics per player Exigent.

                                          2) Implicit setting restrictions. They're the "Chosen of Whatever You Want", but at the same time there's a strong vibe (and probably actual setting rule!) that you shouldn't be making Exigents who are so broad and strong that the risk overshadowing any of the classic five.

                                          So this is the whole kind of "Exigents should be a kind of one dimensional, one trick weird witch who bites it outside their comfort zone and doesn't have too much effect on the overall world" thing.

                                          For most people, it seems like that would have less appeal compared to just picking up a game that's totally built around playing the "Chosen of Whatever You Want", like Godbound or even Scion, and doesn't labour under those restrictions. (Like, for example, if you want to play this mythic demigod ur-ranger figure, then if you pick up a game like Godbound then you can create a character with words of Fertility, Beasts, Bow, and then not really worry too much after that about if your character is overshadowing any extant character types, or too strong for what level his type is allowed, or anything like that.)

                                          ... But then it's like >two years since I heard about them and I still really don't know too much about their story or how they will work! So.

                                                  What exactly are you worried about in the context of being a 'Generic Catch-all' here? I can't really understand it.

                                                  Are you complaining about it as a GM dealing with players and their choice of PC? Are you worried about people showing up to campaigns set in the West as 'The Exigent of the God of Sailing' and the GM being unable to deal with that? While that may be reasonable I think unless you're just making your Exigent jacked to the gills even an Exigent of the God of Sailing is going to have times in the West where they aren't Sailing.

                                                  Are you complaining about it as a Player and what the GM is using? Like are you worried that GMs are going to show up with 'The Exigent of Fighting on Cliffs while wielding a Spear' as your PC travels up a cliff? That probably isn't exactly accurate for what Exigents should do, and such an Exigent definitely should be on the weaker side for being so specific. It seems that Exigents as a whole are more looking for either 'Unique but maybe not powerful Gods' like Ten Sheaves, or something like 'Powerful and generally applicable', something like the Exigent of Cattle and Southern War. I'd imagine an ST is more likely to set you up against the second one then some kind of generic 'Exigent used specifically as a one off challenge', particularly if they're following the book talking about how Exigence is rare and difficult to use. I don't know why you would make an Exigent as an antagonist if you aren't going to give them spotlight; seems a lot of effort for a one-off enemy, all things considered.

                                                  Or are you complaining about Players abusing Exigence not through their choice of character but rather their solutions to problems? Like are you worried that, upon being faced with a locked door in a campaign, rather then having the Night pick it or whatever they go 'Let's go find the Exigent of Lockpicking to pick the lock for us'. For a worry like this, I'm not sure how valid it is, because a player willingly going 'okay I'm going to give up spotlight to let an NPC succeed rather then risk failing' isn't a player I've ever seen.

I can tell this topic is really worrying you in some respects so I'm interested in understanding what you're worried about here, because it seems like TDO's response didn't make you feel reassured.
