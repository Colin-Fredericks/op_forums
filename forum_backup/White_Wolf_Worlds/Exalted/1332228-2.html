    Stripping the dead and selling their stuff shouldn't be as easy as in a video game, and certainly shouldn't be the basis for the Resources Merit.
    I find the way artefacts and cash work in Exalted quite interesing, vis-a-vis this and how it's different from computer games/DnD.

    Demons, monsters, animals, etc, don't have much saleable stuff. Soldiers have armour and weapons, but without knights in full plate, it's probably not going to get you a vast amount of money.             Because it's non-sustainable?
            JohnDoe244 has already basically covered every point I would make about it. I mildly disagree on some of the specifics, but they're not worth bringing up.

            One thing I often think about with Resources is a line that has been in the game from the beginning; that the stipend of a Dynast is something that should be capable of allowing them to live comfortably for the rest of their lives, but they don't, because they're surrounded by social expectations that would hold such frugality in contempt.

            I think that part of the fun and the challenge of playing supremely wealthy people in a setting such as this is the conspicuous consumption that tends to accumulate around them. Something that, like the car with Dynasts, is not always a matter of people indulging in the extreme, how there's often a component of people taking you widely being dependent on flaunting wealth.

            It makes me think about things like accounts of the pageantry of Medieval tournaments, or of Heian-kyo Japan, where the upper classes live surrounded by art and draped in luxury and swimming in fine, costly food and drink (when marzipan was something rate and difficult to make, it would be a status symbol to serve detailed sculptures of the stuff), exchanging valuable gifts as a matter of course, and surrounded not only by servants, but servants who are themselves dressed in fancy uniforms.

            This in addition to practical expenses. Do you have an army? Do you have infrastructure that you have a wish or a responsibility to maintain and expand?

            These are things that underlie the fact that historically, it's fairly uncommon to have people that are so rich that they're not constantly hovering on the edge of... not having cash reserves, which is different than being destitute, not least because rich people have an easier time getting loans or lines of credit.

            I don't think these things need to be tracked down to the smallest transaction, not least because even the lowest rating of Resources would seem to denote having a system that periodically returns money to you. The question of how you scheme to acquire more money should probably mostly come up under circumstances where a sudden unexpected expense comes up but oh no, you have no liquid assets on hand and you can't really pay for stuff in land (and shouldn't anyway, it's worth more in the long run), so you're forced to engage with something like a friend or a creditor or personally overseeing some transaction, or engaging in a contest or wager to get the money in hand.

            Either an unexpected expense or a scene that opens with your accountant informing you that your current expenses are exceeding your available assets or likely income.

            I think a thing that's often overlooked about land-owning aristocracy is how cash poor they tend to be, all the way up to the early 19th century in at least the case of England. I've got an interesting little book about the Chippendale furniture manufacturers in which a Yorkshire nobleman is quoted as saying something to the effect of "I only get paid once a year, so my retailers should expect no different". Which, yeah, makes sense when the foundation of your wealth is rents from farmers who are only bringing in one or two crops a year. But you're still buying stuff all throughout the year, and it's expensive. Hope somebody taught you how to balance your cheque book.

            Right down to a conception of the practice of needing to prioritise which cheques you can afford to bounce this quarter, so it's more complicated than "whoops, I spent more than I make, guess I'm ruined".

            None of these are strictly necessary, but I feel as though the main point of defining the level of finance and society that you're operating at by way of buying the Merit at character creation is that one finds the premise fun and interesting. If it isn't, then well... worry about acquiring money through some relatively straightforward means only when you need to buy something important.

                            I mean, apparently! You and everybody else who ever felt a need to specifically comment upon the inexplicability of either finding themselves in agreement with me or vice versa. Across two forums.

                            Anyway, something that I think could be interesting to explore with Resources as it pertains to this game is the possibility that it could be holding some of the Exalted back.

                            My observation is that, historically, the primary benefit of being an extremely rich person was that it meant you had a base of resources to accumulate a dedicated labour force that could be focused exclusively on providing services to you. You control all the money and food and you can have craftspeople who can be constantly making fine and delicate clothes and ornaments for every occasion with a constant turnover, you can pour that stuff into throwing lavish parties every other week and support an industry of entertainers, you can even kind of make people into accessories and ornaments by way of ensuring that every room has about five people who'll attend to your every need without you ever needing to look at them or have them moving around in a manner that draws attention.

                            It appears to me that industrialisation changed some of the premises of conspicuous consumption for the super-rich, both in the range of luxury items it allowed them to have access to and changing the dynamics of how many actual people were necessary to engage in any given endeavour.

                            I'm not saying that such games should be inclined towards industrialisation specifically, but I do think that one could explore interesting characterisation in terms of Exalted who apply their power towards becoming quite rich quite fast and then stagnate for a while because their need to involve themselves is greatly lessened when they can just pay large numbers of people to act as their intermediaries.

                            That and the question of getting too comfortable on your laurels in a manner that isn't particularly concerned with where that money is coming from (i.e. the basis of most economies is still going to be agrarian labour), in a manner that might make those systems a good deal more rigid.

                            Although, on the other hand, it's not exactly as though late 18th/early 19th century technical developments were driven by desire to liberate the lower classes.... I mean, the seed drill and the cotton gin weren't invented by people committed to relieving the burdens of the working person, they were about increasing commercial output for the people who owned the commodities. Often in ways that came at the expense of people whose livelihoods depended on providing that labour, in a way that ended up funnelling a lot of them into the factories and cities into work and living conditions that were arguably the most horrific they've ever been in human history.

                            Anyway, my point is that one might engage with the premises of the Resources Merit and system to interact with these questions and create interesting stories out of them. Not necessarily stories about trying to promote Marxism or the like, mind.
