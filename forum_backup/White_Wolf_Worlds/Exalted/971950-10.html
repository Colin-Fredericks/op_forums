                I think there's a bit where for me at least, Lunars should have an air of violence or savagery to me. I think that's the big thing to be drawn from the werewolf motifs they have. It's the theme I feel that most unites the two Werewolf games produced by White Wolf more than anything else at least. And to me, Lunars when you read their 1e corebook write-up have this sort of energy to them (for me at least). Solars ruled by day. Lunars ruled by night. Solars made shining cities where those who bought into the system got utopia. Lunars made pyramid skulls of the people who didn't.

                3e's take on this to me feels to be expanding this. Lunars were the second from the top in th eold order. They helped fuel the Usurpation by their positions as Second and in a very violent, dark and often scary sort of Second. They had to be hunted with the Solars because taking out the Solars took their power base and they were the guys closest to the boss. It's like how in real world coups you have to often purge the loyalists. Because when you want to be in cahrge, the guys who had the most to lose from you being in charge are the most dangerous.

                To me this makes Lunars in 3e insurgents. It's just kind of on a weird, global scale. The throne of the world is to many elder Lunar Exalted theirs. It was stolen from them by backstabbing Sidereals and usurping Terrestrails. They are pushed to the edges of relevance in the center of the world and also labeled enemies by that Center now. The Lunar war on the Dragon-Blooded and Sidereals is a rejection fo the hegemony which chose to eject them from the table preemptively. And they are an Exalt sort who even before that were probably characterized by aspects of darkness, predation, the night and violence.

                To me, 2e Lunars were written a bit trying to run from what 1e Lunars were without asking what was the first principles. And a lot of spin-offs from there have progressively resulted in something that's basically not even what the 1e thing was at all. And it feels like 3e is trying to get back to that. Which is fine by me I guess. I like the idea of an Exalt who focuses on the less savery nature of nature, run on a basic idea that "Hobbes was right, Rousseau was wrong" and to some extent show that your "heroic resistance" in a lot of fantasy settings could easily just be a bunch of savages at the gate who want the throne for themselves.

                Plus, less playing at society-making is nice since well, 2e implied a lot of it was futile (sinc ethe Realm eats them), a lot of it was kind of built on unearned taking of setting elements (because 2e liked not making new things up) and also makes it so the entire Lunar premise is "We don't need you dad!" to Solars and then suddenly having Solars showing up and out-doing htem at everything they were doing ever. Having it be an insurgency that they have done significant damage already, which they can rule in when their enemies can't and which allows for young opportunistic Lunars personal glory and power (since well, let's be honest, most young Lunars are enough normal of people to think that's fine by them) in the process well...power and glory. It's a very cynical, mean and realpolitik bit of cultural heroism I think. But I like pragmatic realism over utopianism anyhow.

                I mean, it's not like the most famous utopia books are all that hot anyhow. (The Republic seems very much like a society Lunars would build. It's also a caste-based information-controlling dicatorship.)

                    Originally posted by Elkovash View Post

                    +1 because I like the idea.

                    In First Age Terms, the Lunar and Solar rule a territory as a pair, common practice. Much of the time the Lunar is "home" tending the Dragonblooded Gens, shepherding and grooming the territory, and tending with great love to whatever children/projects may exist. Over time, the stress of high society and court (visiting lunars in their pissed phase) drives them towards their negative intimacies - going on aggressive diplomatic missions as lord and representative of their nation, undermining or sabotaging rival states, leading armies into the Wlyd and otherwise tearing down and attacking anything they have beef with. Now, they are a contributing factor to the failings of Solar governance, rather than passive standers by - much cooler and better fit to what others would perceive as a fickle nature.

                    In the Second Age, the Elders being far more in the throws of their curse, they are given over to their negative intimacies far more often than their positive. Rarely building anything that they love - only working towards things that they hate. Roxxy, her hate of humanity and the civilization that so ruined her life, is stuck in a constant cycle of debauchery and consumption of her hatreds, making no progress towards her love of sorcery despite all the tools and resources before her. Maha does not build his army because he loves it, but is stuck working towards throwing down the Dragonbloods who he blames for stealing his perfect life, unable to follow his positive intimacies towards romance or love of self to any real degree. This does a great deal to explain why Lunar Elders have done so little constructive work in the last 700 odd years. They can no longer build anything constructive, though they remember how. The terror it fills me with to have your loves wither, to be consumed by your hatred... the true Alpha Predator is the growing hate in every Lunars heart, for it will one day consume the Lunars themselves.

I would not say rulership is intrinsic to their essence - only a mandate handed down to them like a title or reward. This leaves perfection and skill taken to 11. The Solars concept of perfection is important as it is double edged: it allows them to make perfect solutions crafted to each independent problem, it also (should) prevent them from having powers or charms that grant overmuch flexibility as that would not be the perfect solution to *the* problem. Given a hundred years in a world allowed to change or any number (infinite) of other shifting variables, that perfect solution is no longer viable for the much changed problem. I think this would be a far better flaw for the Solars - their perfection is their own downfall. Much akin to the UCS who can not turn his back on his perfect valor/compassion, the UCS who can not hold back the benediction of his blessings to the point where a prayer for light and good weather results in sun scorched crops or compassion so potent that no side can be taken without full knowledge of the infinite repercussions that will result down the stream of time for any particular action... perfection is a difficult maxim to live up to, even if it does allow all doors to be open and all hurtles jumped. Perfection is subjective based on the problem, person and situation - the inflexible Solar constantly forced to build anew or relentlessly maintain the constants that allowed their first great experiment.

                            Solars have been allowed to be far far far too flexible and this has made it impossible to define the Lunar as flexible when compared to the Solar. We say it all the time and point to Attribute vs Ability, but really it's a very thin argument. We point to Solars and preach that their should be no flaw, that they are perfect, but perfection is a flaw. I think if we rounded out Solars better it would give depth to all of the other splats. Right now, with Solars being everything - it just white washes the world into Solars and those guys with power x that are not as good as Solars. For a Storyteller game, that's pretty weak. It gives little credence to why people would rebel against them, well, perfection can be stagnation or the tearing up of huge works/peoples/civilizations to lay down the next perfect solution to a changing world.

                            Given such a focus on the hidebound inflexibility of the Solars perfection - the fickle and enforced flexibility of the Lunars own curse/personality would be much richer. The best solution is the all inclusive one, where all of the Exalts Boons/Flaws and all, are made in such a way as to highlight the others...rather than throw shade upon or wash out.

                                I got what Isator meant. It was a valid point. I don't think he was shit-posting.

                                Anyway.

                                People always seem very intent on finding a role for Lunars, a job for them to do (I must have seen tons of threads about it over the years), and honestly, I think it's a waste of time.

                                The role of Lunars isn't to be Solars number 2s, or adaptable heroes, or something like that.
                                Their role is to be awesome monster-heroes who turn into loads of awesome animals. Their role is to be a character that's more fun to play than a Solar if you like having a whole toolbox of weird tricks to adapt, but not to the extent of a Sidereal. Their role is to get to turn into a giant monster. Their role is to be a savage barbarian out to save the world from an evil Empire.
                                What I mean is, Lunars have powers and themes and so on, but they don't need a role in the world pre-determined for them by some cosmic designer, because their role in the game is to be the bestial antiheroes who lurk at the edge of the world, and that's far more important.

                                Who cares what the Gods designed them for? Sure, maybe the fluff can mention that, maybe not (Luna's mad, maybe she had no real plan for them, or maybe this is just what you naturally get when you make a Luna-based Exalt).

                                For example, the OP said:
                                "Solars-rulers, god kings of creation. They represent perfection.
                                Sidereal- advisers and teachers of the exalted. They guide the world fate in the background
                                Dragon Blooded- Soldiers of creation. They control the elements them self."

                                These are all fine. But Solars don't have to be rulers. That's what they did in the 1st age, and it's what some do now (like the Bull), but most Solars actually aren't kings now. I mean, your average Night PC is an awesome thief-hero, not a ruler.
                                Dragon-blood don't have to be the soldiers of Creation. They were in the first age; now they're the aristocratic imperialists who oppress Creation. Some are soldiers, some are politicians, some are sorcerers, whatever.
                                Sidereals? Well, Sidereals do have an advising and teaching theme, but it's actually not a big one, because most Sidereal games do not revolve around teaching Exalts, they revolve about you, the Sidereal agent of Heaven, saving reality from existential threats.

                                So, why do Lunars need some preset role in the game-world?

                                Look, if Dragonblood weren't the Soldiers of Creation in the Primordial War, it wouldn't be a big deal: they're still elemental-powered super-aristocrats, which is the main thing for them. I'm not saying get rid of the soldiers-of-creation thing; I'm saying it's not that big a theme. Similarly, Solars being rulers is actually not a big theme of them, because most Solars are not rulers. They're reincarnated rulers, sure, but in the 2nd age many of them are wandering heroes, because their primary role is to be player-accessible hero-characters.

                                Lunars have had problems with theme before, often seeming like Silver Solars (with some charms that were just literal reprints of Solar charms). But I don't think the main issue was ever that they didn't have a strong "role" in the setting. I mean, Abyssals had a strong role in the setting (destroy the entire world) and many think that was a bad thing for Abyssals as a splat, and later in the edition writers tried to move away from it. Similarly with Infernals, whose role in the setting was to free the Yozis. Both of these were too restrictive, and drew attention away from the real point of them, which was to be death-themed villains/anti-heroes and Princes of Hell.
                                Their role in the game is much more important than their role in the setting.

                                I'll be frank: I've played a couple of Lunars, and so have several of my friends, they're almost as popular as Solars. And never have I heard anyone say "I really wish their role in the setting's history was more defined." Nor did the people who haven't played them as much ever say "I don't like Lunars because they lack a defined role." The only complaint I've heard about their role in the setting was that they wished they didn't have all that stuff about being No.2 to Solars.
