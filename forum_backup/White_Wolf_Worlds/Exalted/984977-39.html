            Ash spoke up, "I ain't Renaldo," she began. "I don't believe we've met. Call me Swifthand Ash. After the slightest pause, she then thumbed the hammer on her flamepiece. She growled "Who the devil are you, and what's all this?"

            She couldn't see the man, but she could feel the tension all the same. She imagined him gripping a knife tightly, forcing himself not to back up. "I am Goose," he said, then hesitated. When he began speaking again, his tone betrayed growing alarm. "You are not from here."

            "Nope. I ain't," answered Ash. She pointed Judgement in the direction of the voice. "I asked you two questions, Goose. Ya gonna answer that second one?"

            "I am... preparing the offerings," said Goose, almost defensively. Ash heard him shuffle-step to one side, and carefully tracked the sound with her flamepiece.

            "[Elder, give us light]," she said.

            Gaze wordlessly summoned the floating candle-flames she had banished only seconds before. Ash blinked, then let out an involuntarily gasp. Behind her, she could hear Seres cursing.

            Meat. That was her first impression. All around them, piles of meat. As her eyes adjusted, she took in the details that made the scene more understandable, but no less worrying.

            This was a butchery, that much was sure. Bins loaded with skinned red flesh lined two "walls" of a large, irregular-but-vaguely-square subterranean chamber. Bones, some cleaned and some awaiting cleaning, aligned the third wall. The fourth wall was lined with washtubs full of hides, pickling in vinegar. In the middle was was a large, tough, stone-topped table. It was covered in blood and the half-dismembered carcass of what Ash was sure had once been a man. Two bins, one evidently for bones and one for flesh, sat beside it. Blood was all over the floor, although signs of brushing the the smell of yet more vinegar suggested someone had made at least a token effort to clean up.

            "Goose" was a short, heavy-set man with very pale skin, milky-white eyes, a shaved head, and very thick arms. He wore an apron, predictably covered in blood, and held a large meat cleaver in his left hand. He didn't blink at the change of light.

            Almost directly behind him on a table was a wide clay bowl with more flesh in it. When she focused on it, she realized the flesh was all hearts - whole hearts. There were several kinds, too - some she recognized as horse or cow hearts, one very large one she imagined came from a yeddim, and some, she reckoned, were human hearts.

            Goose gestured at the bowl, and Ash realized that he'd moved so she'd be able to see it. "Offerin's?" Ash repeated back to him. "To who?"

            Goose stepped back in front of the bowl. He raised his cleaver in a way that wasn't quite threatening, but wasn't exactly friendly, either. "To the Voice," he said. "I do not know who you all are, but you need to leave. Go. This is not your business."

            "The hell it ain't!" snapped Seres, pushing her way up to the front.

            OOC Commentary:
            In the days before refrigeration, one way to preserve things that would spoil was to keep them in a cool, dry place - like a cellar, or a man-made cave. The problem with any hole in the ground, though, is that they tend to flood. Where topography permits, you can deal with this using an adit - a horizontal shaft at the lowest point in a mine or other subterranean system. But if that's not an option, your only choice is to to dig a sump and pump it out periodically.

            In the world of Creation, the most advanced kind of pump you're likely to find anywhere outside a reclaimed first-age city (like Volivat) is an archimedes' screw with a handcrank or treadmill powering it. Less well-off people make do with buckets and a lot of manual labor.

            I say all of this to explain that it's not really Goose's fault this place is a mess. There's no way to keep a place like this clean without good drainage.

                                            Ash hustled forward, grabbed the man's shoulder, and jabbed the barrel of Judgment into his back. "Now hold up, mister. We ain't done talkin', yet."

                                            Goose came to a sudden stop and nearly dropped his bowl of hearts. "...Qué quiere?" he asked, trembling.

                                            "Answers," replied Ash, squeezing his shoulder a little harder. "To start off, what's this Voice?"

                                            "Eh..." Goose gripped the bowl tighter, struggling with his words. "It is... like a god. We give offerings, it gives counsel." His hands were starting to shake. Ash could see the hearts jiggling in his unsteady grasp. "Please, I must go. We cannot miss an offering."

                                            "Or what?" Ash demanded. "What will happen?"

                                            "Mine collapse. Mudslide. Famine." Goose tried to pull away, but Ash pulled him right back. "Please. I will talk, but I cannot be late."

                                            "He'll lead us into a trap," Seres said. She walked in front of both of them and turned to face them, glaring at Goose the whole time. "Or he'll bolt."

                                            "Someone might come looking for him soon, whatever we do." Lans observed. He'd come into the center of the room and was examining the corpse on the table.

                                            Ash thought, then released Goose's shoulder. "We''ll walk and talk. Walk slow," she commanded. "Run, and I'll blast you."

                                            Goose took a cautious step forward, then reached out a hand to touch the tunnel wall. With that to steady him, he began walking forward with a careful, shuffling sort of gait. Seres continued glaring at him as she moved out of his way.

                                            Ash kept Judgement trained on him, and waved to indicate the others should form up. "How did you find this Voice?" she asked, with less volume but the same amount of forcefulness as before.

                                            "I did not," said Goose, moving slowly but surely down the tunnel. "That was before I came to live in Rustbank. They say the first miners found it, but they were afraid. They sealed the tunnels leading to it, and destroyed their maps. But Red Gulch came, they found the tunnels, opened them again."

                                            Questions that had been swirling around in Ash's head were starting to become clear, and she didn't like the answers. How could a group of people steal bodies and cut them up with nobody raising a fuss? They could if their employer was the biggest company in town, or if enough of the town was in on it anyway.

                                            They came to a split in the tunnel. Without missing a step, Goose let the wall fall away from his hand, turned, walked forward, and grabbed onto the wall of a tunnel leading to their right. "Master Cerny led them. He was the first two speak to the Voice. The first to give sacrifice. And the Voice spoke back to him."

                                            "Cerny? Fil Cerny?" came Lans' voice from the back. He was quiet, as if speaking to himself, but his words echoed through the tunnel just the same.

                                            "What did it say?" Ash asked, only giving Goose half her attention. She was beginning to wonder just where they were in relation to the surface. They were definitely going downwards - the air was getting cooler - but she didn't have a sense of how far they'd gone or even if they were still under Rustbank.

                                            "It told him where to find dust," said Goose. "Firedust. Or gold. And Cerny claimed the lands, and became rich. He grew the town. He built the refinery. He brought the railroads..." he trailed off, and his head turned towards Ash expectantly.

                                            Not sure what Goose was expecting, Ash said nothing.

                                            The man's shoulder's slumped. "This is a good town," he muttered. "We all prosper when business is good. The offerings are a small price to pay for-"

                                            "Shut up," Ash commanded, partly to shut Goose up and partly to hear the echo of her own voice. The tunnel was opening up ahead of them - based on the sound change, it was probably another cavern, much larger than Goose's butchering room.

                                            And now that Goose had quieted, she could hear a noise. The echoes distorted it, but it sounded like a small group of people, all singing the same note.

                                            "They have begun," said Goose, ignoring the letter of Ash's command but preserving its spirit by talking in a whisper. "They are expecting me."

                                            He'd stopped moving forward, taking his hand off the wall to clutch at his bowl. Once again, his head turned towards Ash.
