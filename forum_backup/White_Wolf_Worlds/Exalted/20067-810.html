                                            Two of the most powerful features of Sorcery in Exalted are Sorcerous Workings, and Summoning. As a new Exalted player, I haven't figured out how to use these much in our game.

                                            In part, this is because the most obvious ways to use them feel like they would have bad affects on the overall gameplay experience of the table. In particular, I'm worried about solving problems without help from the other players by just building an appropriate sorcerous working or sending a bunch of elementals / demons to deal with it. I also don't want to build up a bunch of minions and take extra turns in combat.

                                            I could also do stuff like give myself superpowers or have an army of minions doing small tasks, but I don't think that really fits my character concept.

                                            However, I know from reading here that other people have tons of fun with sorcery, and don't seem to have these problems.

                                            Can someone explain to me how I'm thinking of it wrong, or tell me would be a good way to use these features in a typical game?

                                            For the Introducing Facts system, it took me a while, but I eventually figured out that the way / one good way to use it is to start from a problem, think up a way that would be fun for you and the other players to solve it, make up a justification for why it is possible to solve it that way, adjust it to fit your character's lore background or explain why you know it, and then talk to your ST and roll for it.

                                            This is the kind of understanding I would like to have of some of the other systems, but don't yet.

                                                Originally posted by habitableexoplanet View Post
                                                Can someone explain to me how I'm thinking of it wrong, or tell me would be a good way to use these features in a typical game?
                                                I think the best way to approach both summoning and sorcerous workings in-game is as a set of tools with very clear benefits, but also very obvious drawbacks and penalties.

                                                Summoning is pretty easy to demonstrate - demons and elementals all have personalities and behavior quirks, and the process of binding them to your will doesn't eliminate those. If you bind a blood ape, you've got a big, beefy combatant who's unbreakably loyal to you, sure. But its still a hulking creature that terrifies mortals who see it, loves the smell and taste of blood, particularly cat blood, and really likes being material. If you summon one of those up as a bodyguard, you're going to constantly run into trouble with it eating people's pets and refusing to stay dematerialized when you don't want it visible. And you'll develop a really strong reputation as someone who summons demons - people will be developing all kinds of negative Intimacies towards you, and spreading information to people like the Wyld Hunt.

                                                Sorcerous workings also have their drawbacks. Remember, every working should have some kind of weirdness and narrative hooks. Finesse 5 isn't a working without any narrative complications or drawbacks, it's a working where the player gets to choose those complications. And those complications should always be notable. Sure, your shed blood might transform into giant scorpions who attack whoever shed it. What happens when you cut yourself shaving?

                                                Some more specific advice for dealing with summoning and workings, to help integrate them into the game:
                                                • For summons, call up multiple copies of the same creature, if you want to take them into combat, rather than a mixed bag. If you have 5 blood apes, they can be run as a battlegroup, which greatly simplifies combat for you, your party, and your ST. Calling up 5 different combat-capable demons just makes for a big headache and people to start dreading your entry into a fight.
                                                • Outside of combat, though, it's fine to have multiple different creatures called up for multiple purposes. Just be prepared to trust them and let them do things on their own, off-camera, where the ST can resolve their actions without rolling.
                                                • As a corollary to the above, accept that your summoned creatures will get up to some things you'd rather they didn't. They're not mindless automatons who perfectly obey your wishes, they have personalities. Don't view such events as failures on your part as a player, or on the ST's part - they're not trying to screw you, they're trying to provide interesting things for you to deal with. Lean into that, and get ready to have some fun dealing with weird demon or elemental problems.
                                                • For workings, be ready to volunteer to solve local or regional problems with workings. Well in a village you care about running dry? Don't worry! Binding a water elemental to it will freshen it right up! Fields going fallow because there's not enough healthy people to tend them? That bunch of hobgoblins aren't doing anything since the Circle killed the raksha they were bound to, give them a new profession! And so on.
                                                • Again, lean into the complications from workings. Treat them as fun sources of drama. Try to think of interesting ways of accomplishing your goals that don't just boil down to "and then it works exactly the way I want". Finesse 3 workings are a good compromise here, since you can make suggestions and negotiate with your ST about what the complications will be, and still get a decrease in the difficulty.
                                                • You can give other people superpowers with workings as well as yourself. If you're worried about overshadowing the party, just try to give everybody something cool.

                                                Interested in chatting with other Exalted fans online? Join us on the Exalted Discord server!

                                                            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet View Post

                                                            In part, this is because the most obvious ways to use them feel like they would have bad affects on the overall gameplay experience of the table. In particular, I'm worried about solving problems without help from the other players by just building an appropriate sorcerous working or sending a bunch of elementals / demons to deal with it. I also don't want to build up a bunch of minions and take extra turns in combat.
                                                            With the spirits that can be bound by sorcery, I think it's good to recognise that the ones that can most easily and frequently be acquired are also unlikely to outdo what a player character of similar focus can achieve, and otherwise they're doing strange, obscure things that are probably not comparable to most playable Exalted powers.

                                                            Like, summoning a blood ape doesn't make your fighting player redundant, they're somewhere between somebody to send as extra muscle to make the fight go more smoothly and with a reduction of cost or risk, or something to do when you need people killed in two places at once.

                                                            Or you need something guarded by somebody better (and more expendable) than a mortal and don't want any players to be saddled with it.

                                                            If you've got a bunch of minions, well... I assume you mean several individual spirits of different types that aren't really appropriate to roll together as a battle group, in which case it does become a matter of finding reasons to not take too many of them around at once. I propose going with the idea that the sorcerer doesn't want more than might get out of hand during a confusing and dangerous situation where some not kept on a short leash may range out and do unwanted things.

                                                            As you rise up the levels of demons, they do start to get capabilities that might equal or exceed what players can do, but they tend to be fairly specialized in their application in addition to being more wilful personalities with their own agendas. I would also maintain that information about what Second or Third Circle Demons to summon should be a resource of scarcity; that you need to have engaged with and accomplished things in play to acquire knowledge of whom exactly you wish to summon.

                                                            Workings are more straightforward. They take time and XP. Not so much that you never want to use them, but enough to maybe reserve them for things that are really important to you or that can't be accomplished by the other talents of the group. And there are desirable things that don't really fall under workings, such as in terms of interacting with other personalities.

                                                            (Workings also benefit from the assistance of characters with skills complementary to what the working is intended to do.)
