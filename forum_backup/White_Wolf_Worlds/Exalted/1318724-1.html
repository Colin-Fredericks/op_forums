        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
        On the subject of Magical Materials: Actually, my initial thought was making them neutral toward all of existing materials, as a manifestation of Nox's inability to commit to a choice- from one side, they keep all options open.
        On the PC-creation level, making them neutral with everything effectively makes them worse than everybody else (except Liminals) with artifacts and Evocations. Sure, from a worldbuilding perspective they're better off than Sidereals and Getimians, since starmetal's rare and those two are dissonant with everything else. But when doing character creation, you just specify that the artifact you bought with your dots is of the material you're resonant with.

        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
        On the other, they can't really excel at anything. Perhaps they could, temporary, commit themselves to a certain MM, but they would have to give up something in exchange, becoming dissonant with another material.
        Being able to choose a temporarily-resonant material while only paying with one dissonant material, on the other hand, makes them second only to the Solars, and not particularly far. Right now, nobody but Solars can start resonant with the Artifact 4 they bought in chargen, and then be assured that when they loot an Artifact 5 off a foe's corpse, they'll be resonant with it.

        I might suggest instead that they be neutral with most magical materials, and then resonant with "panchaloha". In real life, that's a Sanskrit term for term for a five-metal alloy used for making jewelry and temple statues. In Exalted for this purpose, it would designate a specially-made alloy of the 5MMs (maybe with adamant replacing soulsteel?). This would hold the Nocturnals approximately even with Lunars and Abyssals in affinity for magical materials (resonant with artifacts bought at character creation, neutral with most of what you could acquire by looting foes). It would make them worldbuilding-wise a rival of the Sidereals and Getimians in trying to acquire starmetal for making artifacts. And it would reflect the story of inability-to-commit in that all five wouuld have to be present for Nocturnals to be resonant.

                                Originally posted by FallenEco View Post
                                As for cutting down the keywords for charms, I can get behind that, though I would suggest the removed keywords aare noted under possible themes for Nocturnal charm types. I like Lunars, but until the 3e splatbook, they were a thematic mess with poor mechanical support. The TAW Fan project was arguably the best version of them because of how tight the charms concepts were to the themes.
                                Bringing a simular mentality (say, 2-4 thematic statements per attribute) might be useful and the discarded keywords could easily serve as inspiration for that purpose.
                                Yeah, I am too in favor of minimizing keywords. Maintaining a few, solid themes is much better than diluting the concept with too many of them.

                                Can we find a way to get rid of the Curse of Subservience though? I fail to see a way any good can come of it.
                                I mean, we could remove the Curse of Subservience form Nocturnals- but then they won't really be Nocturnals anymore.

                                I mean, most of the Nocturnals' presentation is based around their themes as Heralds and Harlequins- mostly the later. Their history, concepts and attitude toward the world is centered around their Curse and its influence upon them, making them into servants, arlecchinos, harlequins. They are tricksters and jesters, but not like the Joker or Loki, but like the djinn, monkey paws and other wish granters- they would grant your wishes, but be careful what you wish for. It also worked with Nox's nature as a bound god, and them being chosen from those bound by fate.

                                Now, I'm not saying we keep things as the same- Nox may still be bound, he may be not. We have already considered them showing up during the First Age instead of the Time of Tumult. Adamant resonance was removed both because the original writer said they were not satisfied with the concept and, mostly, because the Hearteaters have that niche. Nox's role as the "forbidden Incarna" is being considered due to the Umbrals having a patron described similarly (even though it may be one of the optional origin stories). And of course, we need to deal with the Getimians, for which we try to work to tone down some of the tension between Nocturnals and Sidereals to make sure they work as companion splats, instead of foils. But the Curse is iconic- it is the reason for their title, and a significant factor for the Nocturnal experience, putting the ultimate conflict of "freedom vs servitude" in the very heart of the splat.

                                So yeah, I personally like the Curse- I'm not saying we should keep it as is, far from it. It should be revised and changed especially as it causes a bad tension between the Nocturnals and the Celestials, not to mention it reinforce that Dragon Blooded are not "true Exalts" (and of course, with new Exalts in the game, how can you define Celestial and Terrestrial anymore?). My suggestion for tying it into their intimacies (and I'm not saying "if you have an intimacy you must serve the person it leans toward to", only that the mechanics would revolve around the Nocturnal being compelled to follow those they feel attached to) makes it that the true person the Nocturnals are bound to is themselves- you are a slave to your own passions, something which they share with their patron and his attempts to side with and satisfy everyone.

                                Now, I know that some people don't like giving so much agency over their character to another character- we can even see proofs for that discussion in the pdf, were a standard "Great Curse" was presented as an alternative. But the thing is that I really, truly, love Nocturnals- with all of their themes as servants, fate twisters and wish makers, miracles of the night and chosen of possibilities. I don't want to "fix" them, because I don't think they need to be fixed- only to be revised and adapted to fit 3e's setting and themes (which is easier said than done). As it is a fan project, anyone who doesn't like the Curse could easily make their own variants of it once the splat will be written- but I'm going to keep its concept in this project, and do my best to adapt it in the most satisfying way and make sure to both stay true to the Nocturnals origin and story and make it as compelling to fun to play as I possibly can.

                                So in short- I think we should at least try to adapt the original themes of the Curse to 3e before getting rid of it.


                                EDIT- I hope this don't come of as offensive. I usually try to not be passionate about things online, but I have a really warm place in my heart for the Nocturnals. I don't try top say that you don't love the Nocturnals, or that there is only one right way to play them, but I do wish that before people would just rule out some aspect of Nocturnals, that first we'll try to adapt and revise it so it could be judged properly. If that won't work, well, we do have Epitome's compromise.

                                                    So let's start from the history section, shall we?

                                                    What I've had in mind for the Nocturnals is to have around 5-6 different origin stories, all dating back to the early First Age, either during or shortly after the Divine Revolution. That is a big difference compared to the other Exalts, as we know exactly from where did they came from and why- but I think that as the god of the night, sky and possibilities, it makes sense for Nox to try and not define his or his Chosen's nature. Perhaps none of the stories are true, perhaps all of them are- it is Nox's nature to provide contradicting options and fulfill them all in the same time. Each of the stories may reflect a different side of Nox, for the Midnight King could never be defined through a single story.

                                                    The main reason, IMO, to move the entrance of the Nocturnals from the Time of Tumult to the First Age is due to the fact that there are already enough Exalts which show up in modern times- the return of the Solars, the awakening of Exigence, the inversion of the Abyssals, the creation of Infernals, the release of the Getimians, the corruption of Gloam's Dragon Blooded.. heck, even the invasion of the Alchemicals may count. There are enough Exalts plaguing Creation as modern dangers, the Nocturnals may serve better as coming into public existence much earlier.

                                                    Anyway, the origin of the Nocrtunals is only a part of the story- for we need to think what the Nocturnals have actually done through the whole time they existed. What were their relations with the other Exalted Host during the First Age? Did they participated in the Niobraran War? Who did they supported in the Usurpation? Heck, which side they even took in the Divine Revolution, if any? Are the Nocturnals going to constantly choose the two sides in every conflict? Because if so, it would have been only a matter of time before the Solars would grow mad and unleash their wrath upon Night's Chosen. Perhaps during the Niobraran, the Solars were the ones to kill and banish the Nocturnals from the world, seemingly with the help of the Sidereals- even though that perhaps the Sidereals actually planned their eventual survival in one way or another?

                                                    Would the Nocturnals, then, return after the Usuraption? Or perhaps after the Great Contagion and the Balorian Crusades, when the "Gates of Night" in which their Exaltations were held prisoners were broken, giving Creation a new possibility to rise above its ruined state. What have they done in those times? As we mentioned earlier, they are unlikely to have produced realms and nations around themselves- well, perhaps not all of them. Maybe they were trapped, in fact, in the mysterious Firmament, were they have started a strange culture which revolves around the strange land? Where they hunted by the Realm as Anathema? Have they sided with the Dragon Blooded, or worked against the Bronze Faction?

                                                    What have they done in the years of the Realm's rule? How do they react to the events after the Empress has vanished? Are they worried of the return of the Solars, or do they see in it as a chance for a change? Are there Nocturnal Elders still around, few beings which have either roamed Creation or watch from the Firmament for centuries, while living under countless false identities and persona?

                                                    And most important than all- what is Nox? Is he indeed a lost brother to the Maidens, as he claim he is? Or perhaps he is just an impostor, liar and a thief who took power and title which were not his own? Does Nox even exist, or perhaps he is a future yet to come.. or a past long erased? What kind of game does Nox play, if he plays a game at all? Is he still weaved into the Loom of Fate, from which he whispers strange possibilities to the path of fate? or is he an Indigo Jester, which brings change wherever he goes for the sake of chaos alone?

                                                    And other thoughts like that :P

                                                            So here are some concepts I have for the origin section of the Nocturnals- I just wish to stress that unlike certain mysteries in the setting, those are not going to be "the truth is unknown in the setting, here are some options for you to choose the truth", but "the truth is unknown in the setting, here are some popular theories the Nocturnals themselves tell". Also, in the spirit of 3e, I've decided to try and focus those origins around the Chosen themselves, as 3e seems to put the Exalted on the backstage and the gods in the background. I would also like to emphasis that each of those origins should be no more than a paragraph or two- the Primordial War is a much more distant event, and it isn't much discussed in the books as such.

                                                            Anyway, that's what I got-

                                                            1) Nox the Betrayer- Nox was an Incrana, the God of Night's Sky. While his siblings shared with him the secrets of Exaltations, he was secretly planning to sell them to the enemies of the gods and use his Chosen as soldiers against the armies of the gods. However, as his Chosen walked the earth, they learned of the harsh rule of the ancients and the terrors they brought upon Creation. Seeing the error in their patron's ways, the armies of the Nocturnals were split into two- one side following the will of Nox, and the other rebelling against it. The war was dreadful, but in the end, those who were on the side of the gods were victorious, and Nox was captured. While the punishment for such treachery would have been death, those Nocturnals which supported the armies of heaven pledged for mercy for both their siblings and patron, and as such Nox's life were spared- but his mind and body were broken, and weaved into the the Firmament beyond the Gates of Night, were it would suffer in eternal exile.

                                                            2) Nox the Betrayed- Nox was an Incrana, the God of Night's Sky. Following the plot of his siblings, he too made Chosen of his own to supplement the armies of the gods. Yet Nox was a lonesome god, never a true part of the Incrana and too close to the ancients. Fearing a change of heart and with the prophecies of the Maidens giving contradictory results, they decide to act in advance. As such, on the eve of the Divine Revolution, Nox was lost, tempted, defeated, hidden and ended, while his Chosen were sent to the war under his name. Yet the Nocturnals were suspicious, and while they sided with the armies of heaven at first, they have wondered why their god didn't came to support them in the war. Secretly, Night's Chosen have searched a possibility beyond that decided by fate, and managed to discovered their broken deity beyond the Gates of Night. Furious, many of the Nocturnals have withdrew their support and turned to serve the enemies, while others, bound by their loyalty to the fellow Exalts, staid on the side of the gods- a choice which was, apparently, the correct one.

                                                            3) Nox the Loyal- Nox was an Incrana, the God of Night's Sky. Seeing the endless possibilities which the future had to offer, he was never much of a decisive individual- and when he learned of the plans of his siblings, his heart was torn. He loved his siblings, especially the Maidens which he saw as actual sisters, and as such wanted the best for them. However, he also loved the ancients, which he saw as his parents, as they were the ones who made everything possible. Looking at the endless possibilities, Nox has decided to support both sides as best he could, knowing that it would cost him dearly. He supported both sides before the war, helping them as much as he could to build their forces with revealing the plot. When he granted the miracle of Exaltation to his Chosen, he divided them into two- one half, he guided to support the gods. The other half, he sent to serve the ancients. On the eve of the war, he waited for the very last moment before his heart broke, and he ran to warn the ancients in advance- yet his sisters knew what was in his heart, and they knew they had too much too lose if the warning would come on time. With heavy heart, the Maidens defeated Nox and imprisoned him beyond the Gates of Night, while his Chosen went to war against each other, their heart torn like that of their patron.

                                                            4) Nox the Mastermind- Nox was an Incarna, the God of Night's Sky. Seen as inferior by the ancients, Nox has always resented and envied their rule upon the world, as they bound him to their service. Seen as inferior by the Sun and Luna, Nox has always resented and envied the light they cast upon the world, as they force him as their servant. Among the other Incarna, only the Maidens were somewhat close to him, and even they were never trusted by the lonely god. Hearing the plans of the coming Divine Rebellion, Nox has gleefully agreed to support the gods in their plot- yet with his heart filled with resentment and envy, he made plans of his own, hoping to turn his enemies against one another and become the new king of both Day and Night. As he made his Chosen, he secretly divided them into two armies- one to support the gods, and one to support the ancients. On the night of the Divine Revolution, Nox has quickly went to warn the ancients, as he knew that it would turn the war even bloodier- but the Maidens knew their brother's dark heart, and turned his passions against him. As his Chosen clashed against one another in the battlefield, Nox's mind and body were broken, and he was thrown beyond the Gates of Night- yet, dreadfully, he was laughing through the whole process, for his plan worked. There, on the desolated planes of the Firmament, neither the ancients nor the Incrana were to rule over him. There, finally, he was free. There, finally, his true plan could begin.

                                                            5) Nox the Jester- Nox was an Incarna, the God of Night's Sky. Unlike the rest of his siblings, Nox never had higher inspirations in his life. Serving as a jester in the courts of the ancients, his only goal was chaos, pure and simple. When the other Incrana informed him of their plans to rebel against the ancients, Nox agreed, believing that the war would change the status que and produce many possibilities in the future to explore. Wishing the war to be as chaotic as possible, he has secretly divided his Chosen with secret commands- one part to serve the gods as the heralds of the future to come, and one part to serve the ancients as harlequins of change and possibility. As the war grew closer, Nox noticed that the possibilities for winning tilted more and more toward the side of the gods, making him bored with the plans for the future- until a spark of inspiration burned in his mind. In the night before the war, Nox has cloaked his figure and secretly went to warn the ancients of the coming rebellion, believing it would even the odds and make the result less predictable- yet the Incrana knew their sibling's nature, and took precautions. The Maiden foresaw his steps, tracked him down and subdued him. While his Chosen fought one another, the Sun judged Nox to be broken and exiled beyond the Gates of Night- and as he heard the verdict, Nox was thrilled and laughed, for of all the possibilities the future held, that was the only one he never considered.

                                                            6) Nox the False- Nox was not an Incarna, and definitely not the God of Night's Sky. Nox came from beyond the Gates of Night, a strange entity of unknown origin, a failed divinity banished long, long ago. For ages, Nox was trapped in the Firmament which he called home, able to only watch Creation, but never to act. However, as the war against the ancients raged, the enemies of the gods grew desperate. They broke the seal upon the Gates of Night, and summoned the Midnight King to supply them in the war. As he watched carefully through the rise of the Exalts and the course of the war, Nox knew a lot about the process of Exaltation, and as such was able to produce Chosen of his own- even though the act has diminished him, breaking his mind and body while his Essence got tangled to that of his home. However, as Nox has granted soldiers to the armies of the ancients, he has done the same to the armies of the gods- which has suddenly discovered new Exalts of unknown origin walking among them, rising their spears next to them and fighting the ancients together with them. To both sides, Nox has started to whisper lies and falsehoods- that he was a kin to the Incarna, a grand God of the Night, either fighting alongside them or against them. And when the war has ended and the gods claimed victory, they discovered the broken Gates of Night which they quickly sealed- yet it was too late. Nox may have been bound to his throne- but the Nocturnals were free, and with them, the possibility for a different future.
