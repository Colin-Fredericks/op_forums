                    Also, who wouldn't be a bit off after being rescued from the Wyld? Trauma exists.

                    Anyway, the point I'm making here is split off from a point Jenna made many, many years ago, during discussion surrounding the release of the original Exalted: The Fair Folk. If you take a source of true randomness, then you can get any signal you want out of it provided you apply the right filter. But does that mean the signal came from the randomness, or the filter?

                    ("Yes.")

                    The Wyld is a font of infinite creative potential, and the bit about noise, signal, and filter is what infinite creative potential means. Things like Wyld Shaping and Wyld Cauldron and questing against the Wyld turn that potential to the will of the shaper, pulling specific things out of it that exist to various degrees of realness until they interact with Creation sufficiently that their realness becomes lasting. Observing things in the Wyld heightens their temporary realness, and it's never clear to the observer whether the increased detail and reality legible in what they're observing is a function of their noticing more detail, or creating/projecting more detail via their observation.

                    So if you rescue someone or something "lost to the Wyld," do they / does it come from the noise or the filter? Is it the original person or thing, or is it someone or something new created out of your observation, desire, and preconceptions? If you use your Exalted super-Investigation to develop an exceptionally clear idea of what was lost, such that you feel confident you could discern the real thing from a Wyld simulacrum, doesn't that just mean you'll be even better at creating a more accurate Wyld simulacrum? Is "Wyld simulacrum" even a coherent concept at this point?

                    If whatever you pull out of the Wyld is a bit off, does that mean they're fake, or are they just exhibiting the natural effects of having had experiences in the Wyld? If you're looking for a fantastic, legendary magical vase sculpted by a famous First Age sculptor that was lost in such-and-such Wyld incursion, and what you get out of the Wyld is a bit warped, is that because you made it wrong or was it warped by spending centuries in the Wyld? If you're too committed to finding the one that isn't warped, are you likely to reject the "real thing" and settle for something you create out of your conceptions instead? Or is refusing to settle for a warped copy and commitment to finding the original... an act of will on your part that can repair the vase to its original state, assuming that being lost to the Wyld means it becomes of the Wyld and becomes subject to the process of Wyld shaping?

                    If you pull a person out of the Wyld and they're a bit off, are they a simulacrum or are they exhibiting trauma? If you pull a person out of the Wyld and they act normal for a while but then hulk out and go all The Thing on you, does that mean you pulled a monster out of the Wyld instead or were they the original, warped by the Wyld into a monster?

                    You can't know the answers to these questions, unless you, like, declare them out of character as one of the people running the game. But isn't even that kind of analogous to what I'm talking about?
