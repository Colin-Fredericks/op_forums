            Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
            Judging by people's reactions to Qrow and Raven's secret, apparently Semblances don't ever involve shapeshifting.

            So, magic seems capable of doing things which are impossible with Semblances.
            Which is great if you're Yang and Weiss and are legitimately freaked out about people turning into birds.

            We are not they. It's hard to know what we're supposed to regard as weird and freaky in a universe where the monkey-man hybrid can create glowing golden clones of himself to go along with the weird shadow copies of his not-girlfriend, who is on a team with someone whose family made their fortune selling rocks with crazy elemental powers, and all of this is perfectly normal. We need more context than that.

            Like... Ruby's weird silver eye thing caught everyone off-guard as something obviously weird and special because we really weren't prepared for Ruby to start vomiting streams of silver death light out of all her head orifices that froze a Grimm the size of a small town into place and super fucked up the lady who'd just murdered Pyrrha Nikos. That was, clearly, something exceptional not only to everyone in Vale, but also to us, the viewers. Similarly, the Four Maidens were presented in a way that obviously made us instantly recognize these were special, unique beings, touched by power and destiny in a way that a regular person with a Semblance is not. Ozpin possessing a fourteen year old was also something clearly not, you know... regular. The show presented all that well.

            Qrow and Raven... were sort of a conceptual failure in this regard, because literally for like a year people just thought turning into a birb was Qrow's Semblance, and it was like "okay, he's a bird. Cool Semblance bro." (In some cases, "ha ha, lame Semblance, bro.")

            They need to present this stuff a little better. Volume 5 has been a very, very talky volume with a lot of exposition dumps that aren't really doing it for me. It isn't like they haven't done that in the past, but... I dunno. Remember when Yang and Blake became Level 5 friends and that unlocked Yang's tragic backstory vis-a-vis the wagon trip with Ruby that went sideways? That was accompanied by cool-ass visual storytelling in kind of a uniquely animated style that was rad as hell.

            It wasn't just Yang talking her exposition into Blake's face for five minutes, which is what we're getting a lot of this year. These exposition dumps are very rarely even conversations, which are at least interesting; Qrow and Raven at the Higanbana bar was an exposition dump, but it was also verbal fencing between two siblings with a messed up relationship with different conflicts and give-and-take happening on, and under, the surface.

            That's way more interesting than Ozpin revealing what he's capable of in dribs and drabs.

            I also feel like the show is either making the kids stupid, or hiding things that they know and are conversant with from us, the viewers, which is also annoying. Like... NONE of the kids have asked "So wait, the Grimm have a Queen? How does that fucking work? Is she a Grimm herself or something different? Why the hell aren't we all just dead if the Grimm have actual purpose and direction behind them? What's her specific agenda? Why would regular humans work for the things that want to kill all humans? DO they want to kill humans?" to Qrow or Oz? At all?

            Because that seems like something someone with half a brain would ask!

            "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."
