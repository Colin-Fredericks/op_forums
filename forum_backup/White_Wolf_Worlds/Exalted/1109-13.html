                                    Personally, my model for the First Age accommodates the potential and actuality for Lunars to be dissatisfied with any relative Solar government, and to act on such, within the boundaries one would expect of their relationship with any given Solar.

                                    My model for the First Age has it that, at various periods, there have been revolutions against Solars (often sanctioned or aided on some level by other Solars, especially in those cases where the latter is Chosen within troublesome circumstances prompted by the former). These include occasions where Dragon Blooded families (or other Exalted) rebel against ruling Solars for one reason or another; the response this brings from other Solars, including the Deliberative, varies with the circumstances.

                                    Maybe these occasions were becoming less common as the Age drew to a close, and the Solars became more uniform in their excesses, but the concept was not unknown.

                                    The Usurpation was not those things. The Usurpation was a changing of the order of the world on a mythic scale. The Usurpation was ending the Age of the Solars to usher in the Age of the Dragon Blooded, to change the definition of "mightiest of the Exalted".

                                    This isn't smaller nations sometimes beating the United States, this is all the smaller nations collectively ganging up to literally wipe the United States from the map.

                                    It's a revolution, but it's also the Twilight of the Gods.

                                    Maybe the Dragon Blooded were acting on certain personal or political issues, but they wouldn't have done so to such a degree and with such a coordination if there hadn't been Sidereals whispering encouragements and instructions in their ears, and the Sidereals were motivated by a vision saying "if the Solars remain, the world is doomed".

                                    The Lunars did not have so much to gain from such a dramatic reversal.

                                        Originally posted by John Mørke View Post

                                        Setting aside the T-1000, and the archetypal genius of Reed Richards, I despise pretty much everything about "stretchy guy" characters.
                                        You don't like Jake from Adventure time? Come on, Dimaggio!

                                        Man, now you've got me thinking about where to draw the line around this archetype, and what defines it as a thing. The comic archetype seems a bit distinct from things like the T-1000 or Odo from Deep Space Nine, because they have a similar power, but rather than presenting that power in a serious way they use it as a sort of...physical punning? I think the reason those characters tend to be kind of jokey is that the power tends to be used as a way to use exaggeration and blur the lines between people and objects for comedic energy, which puts them in the same concept space as a lot of classic American cartooning (animated or otherwise). I think the reason you see them so much in comics is that the style of humour they use is leveraging the "pseudo-realism" of the medium, where a lot of its energy comes from representing reality in a distorted or metaphorical way. In that context I think the stretchiness is not so much about shapeshifting as it is a justification for a kind of image-play. I think in theory that's a rich concept space for raksha, because it has that aspect of relying on the real while subverting it. In practice I can't see a good way to make it work, though, both because it's a nonvisual medium and because it's hard to throw Plastic Man into a fantasy epic without leaving everybody irritated.

                                        But then, aside from those guys, you've got a bunch of other characters who mostly play the gimmick straight -- the T-1000 is the granddaddy here and has many derivatives, but at the lower-power/higher-realism end I would include, say, Dhalsim from Street Fighter. I think Exalted has room for published material that explores this space, but I would tend to give it to different people depending.

                                        T-1000-style "amorphous blob that can redistribute its mass at will" shapeshifting feels very modern: there's little precedent for it before the modern era, and it's one of those superpowers where people take an idea with internal, physical consistency and explore what it would be like if it existed, which is not something you saw a lot of before science fiction. I lump it in with telekinesis, teleportation, and so forth, for that reason. I wouldn't say that nothing in Exalted should work that way, but it feels very far from the earthy sword-and-sandal animism the game is built around, so I think if I were going to add it, I'd give it to one of the "weird otherworldly" types, like Abyssals or Infernals. I don't like it for Lunars because to me Lunars feel very worldly and rooted in the "ancient myth" motif, and it has dissonance with that.

                                        On the other hand, I do like Lunars to have some stuff at the low end of this scale, like that Dhalsim-stretch range enhancer Charm whose name I can't remember. I definitely think there's room for appropriately specialized Lunars to be weirdly fluid: bending like they don't have bones, snapping upright from a prone position like a bent switch, weaving back and forth with an animation-style stretch/smear that makes it hard to hit their weak points, surviving killing blows by just not having their organs where they're supposed to be, etc. I think a lot of Lunar players would be unhappy being forced into that aesthetic, but it seems like a legitimate place to allow people to explore if they want to, particularly in Dexterity.

                                                        In the end, efforts to justify the lazy writing of the 1e Usurpation just kinda fall flat for me because... as we all know... it was just badly written, before much of this was really defined or thought through.
