    Lunar bond-mating came from an event, whether that event was the creation of Exaltation itself (as it used to be) or some Working (which at least gets rid of the Incarna-fiat). If you're going to say that the contemporary incarnation of your character's Exaltation having been affected by that event robs the player of agency, you may as well say that the character growing up in a world with 5 poles robs the player of agency. You can decide the details of whatever effect it has on the modern character (within bounds set by the storyteller), or ignore it as having no special relevance, but unless you change the setting it's not really debatable that it happened.

                The Epic of Gilgamesh was brought up before, and while it admittedly made a good point about the manner in which Gilgamesh first responded to seeing Enkidu, the thing I take from that story is that Enkidu is the one on which his whole story of getting to that position has the most profound impact. The only thing that changes about Gilgamesh is that having a buddy who's up to his level makes him less of a jerk (indulge me in setting aside the whole thing of Enkidu's loss affecting Gilgamesh so deeply that he's driven to find a way to... escape his own mortality, so reverting back into being a jerk a bit). What strikes me is how, in becoming lined up for his role, the entire nature of Enkidu's being ends up changing; he goes from basically being an animal in the shape of a man (who's particularly good for digging out traps) to somebody with language, civility, a sense for living in dwellings, and engaging in human sex (possibly of the kind that affords feminine dominance, depending on exactly what was going on in ancient Sumerian civilizations). When I first heard about how the story includes a moment when Enkidu returns to the wild, but the animals all recoil from him now, signifying what he's given up and can never get back, I actually found that pretty sad, and it sticks with me a lot.

                When I think about the parallels that has to the origin story here, it makes me think that the Lunars were the ones who were most profoundly transformed, and I feel as though putting the majority of the Bond, for good and for ill, on their end of the equation emphasizes that.

                I wouldn't characterise it as letting Solars off the hook. There are still Solars who would have Gilgamesh's experience of becoming enamoured on first sight, either from the weight of memory and shared experience over lifetimes, or some intersection between the Solar's own sensibilities and how the Lunar presents themselves.

                But my thoughts also go to the Lunar who doesn't immediately form their side of the connection, and has it shaped by the manner in which they're connected to a Lunar whom they can't get shot of if the Lunar doesn't want to.

                Looking at the Gilgamesh background and some of the imagery it informs, I'm thinking of a whole thing where the Solar quality is a lot more inviolate when compared to the Lunar (the transition to Abyssal and Infernal being its own huge thing). It leads me into a narrative of... not any particular Solar character, rather the Solar ethos, having not quite known what it was getting into in making that connection with Lunars.

                So part of the value I feel in the Solar side not as intrinsically having the feeling is that it's the Essence thinking that it can get away with not caring about the connection and the old oaths, and the Lunar side laughing at the conceit of it.

                Yesss… there are a few reasons I like using the word "marriage", but forget about that for a moment, think of the originating event and ceremony more on the whole as a union, an alliance. Something in which the Solar side pledged to do or give something. Something from which you got some Bonds that, reincarnated over thousands of years and dozens of lifetimes, never managed to be romantic, or friendly, or even necessarily a rivalry. More business. More the thing that the Lunar felt was keeping the Solar on their toes.

                I think there's value to the narrative of a Solar that doesn't form an Intimacy and feels aloof about the whole thing, and being connected to a Lunar who feels impelled to disabuse them of that notion.

                I will grant that it feels like situations where the Bond persistently and distinctively entailed a very intimate connection should only be a minority of those aloof scenarios. Not absent completely, I've already elaborated on some ways in which I value it.

                A basic rule of thumb that comes to my mind from having written all this (it once again being a case where my thoughts got away with me upon starting what was intended to be a simpler post) is that if one envisions the background as having been one in which the immediate prior incarnation, and a distinct number of the overall, is something in which the connections developed beyond Minor (keeping in mind that a Minor Tie can still entail an important life-long relationship), then reacquaintance kindles a feeling strong enough to form a basic Tie for the Solar.
                If it didn't, then the living Solar's experience of the old memories is more distant and clinical, and nothing comes out of it.

                All still coming from the idea that it wasn't the deep transformation for ancient Solars that it was for Lunars.

                And oh Lord I am looking back at all of that and feeling as though the strongest argument against making something about that mandatory for Solars is that it's a lot of baggage to process. I am thinking of it particularly from the perspective of Solar games, which as presented in the corebook do not heavily concern themselves with the baggage of old relationships to Lunars.

                I find that the baggage of the Lunars' old relationships to Solars is easier to write into their part of the setting, because it's part of the foundation in which their persons and culture has existed for the past 1000+ years. But like, imagine introducing somebody to the Solar Exalted, and saying that in addition to the whole general thing of being righteous heroes returned to save a world from a time of tumult in the face of a vast hostile empire, they've got to bear the intricacies of ancient relationships to Lunars.

                Or, if you don't introduce that in the core, it's a thing that gets introduced to them after the fact in the book of an entirely different Exalted group?

                I think some of these arguments need to allow a lot of concessions to the basic gameplay function of the Solars as the core Exalted.


                I'm gonna stop right here, because otherwise I'm liable to ramble on about every other possible angle on this thing.

                                                            Looking at a lot of the legacy of political marriages, I feel as though the most important thing wasn't necessarily the status of the heir. Sure, marriages were legitimised by consummation and producing children, but that was kind of the case with just about any marriage. A lot of times, heirs to multiple kingdoms were actually rather undesirable, because they could complicate already fraught lines of succession, and the idea of unifying multiple polities in one person was often unwanted within (you don't want to suddenly find yourself ruled by a foreigner) and without (you don't suddenly want half the continent to be unified) I think that, a lot of the time, the important thing was that it made the monarch of the other polity count as your sibling or parent; the original meaning of "in-law" was that, in the eyes of the law, your relationship is equivalent to a blood tie. So it would often play off of cultural attitudes towards kin loyalty.

                                                            Still, that's the side coming at it from a largely patriarchal angle; I think that an often overlooked element is the union of power with your actual spouse. Eleanor of Aquitaine was the most eligible bachelorette of 12th century Europe because she carried her own authority over a sizable and wealthy duchy into her marriages. The political quality of Isabella of Castile marrying Ferdinand of Aragon was them both being supreme in their respective territories, and able to command them mutually in alliance, to fulfil their shared spiritual and territorial goals of driving Muslims out of Iberia (although the two also seem to have been genuinely into each other, and Isabella's marriage choice was actually made against a lot of powerful domestic sentiments, for a number of political reasons).

                                                            You can also get things that go even more symbolic. Something I've found intriguing since I first learned of it was surviving correspondence between the rulers of civilizations of the Levant from around three to four thousand years ago (like the Egyptians and Hittites) in which they would address each other with family epithets even in cases where they weren't tied together by actual marriages, which I think might say something about what those societies thought about family relationships and the common status of their rulers.

                                                            Hell, while I wouldn't find it reasonable to expect it of everybody, I would be inclined to look at the description of the binding of Solar and Lunar Exalted as a marriage as being an insight into some very different and intriguing conceptions of things such as gender roles, family relationships and the institution of marriage, not least because you're talking about humans at the beginning of their civilization; an institution of marriage might still be a fairly new thing to them.

                                                            Alternatively, I'm thinking of marriage being a term ascribed to it later in the First Age, in which the narrative and people's conceptualisations are inclined to see it in terms of contemporary civilization, much as a lot of historical myth and fiction has tended to picture people living a very long time ago as basically looking and dressing and organising identically to themselves. I'm picturing this being a case even for a lot of Exalted who actually lived back then, mostly because I'm inclined to picture a two thousand year old Exalt halfway through the First Age less like a cave person enduring amidst a heavily developed society, and more like middle-aged to elderly Jack Donaghy, who by this point has been fabulously wealthy for more than half of his life and left his impoverished childhood far behind him, and his ignorance of things like basic grocery shopping is a symptom of either having grown extremely out of touch or an affectation.
