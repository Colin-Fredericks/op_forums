                                    Tangent: Is there a better term for (people from the whole region)? Is "British" really the demonym for the entire United Kingdom? "For us", we can variously use Latin > Hispanic / Iberian > Spanish > Provincial identities (including Latin America). But for the Isles, I feel like conflating "everyone" with British is what makes part of this so confusing.
                                    British covers everyone from Great Britain (as in, the specific large island where England, Wales and Scotland are). "The UK" is short for the "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland", as Northern Ireland is on a separate piece of land.
                                    So people from the UK are mostly British, but some are Irish (or... Ulster Scots, Ulstermen, etc. Who live in Northern Ireland but are a seperate ethnic group to the Irish). And I guess there are other, much smaller islands around the UK, with their own ethnic groups who you could argue are British or not British (like the Manx), but their populations are small enough that you probably don't need to remember that.
                                    Basically, if you say British+Irish, that will cover everything you'll feasibly need, unless people are feeling awkward.

                                                I'm prepared to write off as a hilarious miscommunication if you are.

                                                The quick summary of Cornwall (and to a large degree Devon next door) all comes back to tin and the sea. Tin mining in Cornwall and Devon was so essential to the state that tin miners were granted the right to form statuary parliaments who had the power to ratify or not any laws passed by the government upcountry and miners were immune to prosecution for the majority of crimes apart from by their own parliaments. Cornwall and Devon repeatedly revolted not at the behest of the local aristocracy but to resist attempts by London to back out of these deals. The dark side of this is Cornish miners once they had moved abroad were notorious scabs, since they had run their rights without the aid of others, they often saw no need to unionise.

                                                Those who weren't miners were fishermen, pirates, smugglers and wreckers. To this day, any ship that goes down in the west country will have locals descend from far and wide to grab what they can as salvage and ships still slip in at strange hours to dump cheap booze and drugs.

                                                These days the tin and fish are gone and most job prospects are part time holiday work or pissing off to another part of the UK with a better economy.

                                                But the most important part of Cornish identity is feuding with Devon and not trusting anyone from upcountry be they from London, Wales, Scotland, the moon or wherever.

                                                Does that help?
