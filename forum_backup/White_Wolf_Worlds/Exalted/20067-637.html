        Originally posted by The Fool of Creation View Post
        Is it ever possible for an Exalted in 3e to share their Exaltation with another person temporarily? Or to even permanently transfer their Exaltation to another individual if they would otherwise be worthy? For example, could a Dragon-Blooded use a Charm to temporarily share their Exaltation with a mortal relative for a scene or, for a higher level Charm, permanently transfer their Exaltation to a mortal relative, effectively becoming a mortal of their apparent age?
        The general answer to your question is probably "yes", because Exigents are all funky, and I don't think I'd want to rule out an Exigent who could share power temporarily, or pass it on permanently. However, I wouldn't allow either ability for Dragon-Blooded. Permanently passing on Exaltation goes against the themes of Terrestrial Exaltation, I feel, which include being Exalted from a young age, by an "accident of birth". If they can pass it on deliberately, that changes their whole dynamic. As for transferring power temporarily, I suppose I could see that for DBs on a kind of limited basis - only to a relative, probably only one charm at a time, and the DB loses access to it while transferred. But definitely no situation where there are now two people with the same charm.

                        As a (very) general rule in Exalted, the cat doesn't go back in the bag. You cant Unkill someone, you can't Undo past events, and you can't Unexalt.

                        Heck, in 3e you cant even truly Undo most sorceries. You have to distort them or start a whole Working to wrench reality into a shape that doesn't have them anymore.

                        Now what there IS is Exigent exaltation. That's the main thing circa 3e that would be recognisable as imbuing your power in another, but it's notable that a) Doing that doesn't make the God who does it powerless, it just drains them significantly and b) it requires the component of an Exaltation shard, or the framework of one.
                        I like these setting considerations and generally roll with them BUT

                        Like, its just 'rules', brah. If it looks and sounds cool in your campaign, as many others have said: Do that biz.

                                                            Originally posted by Accelerator View Post
                                                            When people say overdrive in Internet discussions, what do they mean?
                                                            It's a Charm keyword originating from 2e's Ink Monkeys material. Charms with the Overdrive keyword have a temporary pool of Peripheral Essence which can only be filled by conditions specified in the Charm's text. Motes in the temporary pool can only be used to activate Charms with a duration of one action or less. If a Charm is entirely funded from an Overdrive pool, it doesn't count as a Charm activation.
