        Okay, Evocations, i.e. “So we heard you like Bleach.” I really like the idea of Evocations, but we’ve gone literally the entire book with no idea what they actually are; let’s fix that.

        So artifacts have Evocations; what Evocations varies with the weapon, its history, and the wielder. That seems complicated, but okay. Stronger artifacts have more or better Evocations – sure – with a minimum rating of Art 3 to get any at all.

        Solars apparently do equally well with all magical materials, now, while other Exalts specialize. Interesting; solves the “But all I kill is Dragon-Blooded, so all I have is jade stuff,” problem.

        We get a brief description of the five magical materials and the sorts of Evocations that resonate with each, which is nice but will probably be pretty tough to homebrew until the artifact book drops.

        Then we get the actual artifacts themselves. Which, wonder of wonders, are described as having a fixed number of hearthstone sockets! So how many do other artifacts have, then?

        Beloved Adorei, Orichalcum Daiklave
        1. “Automatic:” +1 Accuracy, +1 success on Withering damage.
        2. A successful Decisive doubles as a Read Intentions roll.
        3. +1 non-Charm success on targets you’ve hit with #1, with a rolled 10. -1 dice to any other weapon.
        4. Roll bonus Decisive damage based on Intimacies revealed with #1.
        5. If you know Intimacies related to your target, you may destroy an undestroyable thing (the deathlord’s unbreakable curse or lock, etc.).
        6. Heal; gain bonus Initiative; preserve your own Resolve and learn the Intimacies of those who strike you. At the end of the scene, Adorei dies.


        Beloved Adorei offers, basically, +1 Accuracy just for attuning to her, which is one of the very few tools to cap-break in the game. That’s a potent direction to go in, as 2e amply demonstrated. It’s a fun artifact, in any event; I like that the sword has a personality (and, apparently, talks?), and it seems like you could build some fun plots around a player’s relationship with Adorei and, potentially, attempt to revive her.

        Brilliant Sentinel, Orichalcum Heavy Plate
        1. Automatic: Enemies take -1 to Join Battle rolls (or -1 success if Creatures of Darkness); store anima levels in artifact.
        2. Dump 3 levels of anima to blind enemies.
        3. Prevent blinded enemies from attacking anyone but you.

        Moonlit Huntress, Moonsilver Powerbow
        1. Automatic: Gain one of various bonuses, depending on your Initiative.
        2. Spend 1wp for +15i; gain all bonuses from #1 for the next three turns.

        Shining Ice Mirror, Blue Jade Reaper Daiklave
        1. Automatic: for 2m commitment, +2 Defense on any turn when you Aim.
        2. When you crash an enemy, he takes -2 dice and -1 Defense until he recovers.
        3. +1 nonCharm bonus to Resolve vs. attempts to cow you; temporarily reduce all passion Intimacies by one step. Learn this for free given a proper trigger.
        4. Strike at enemy at range, creating movement penalties as well as damage.
        5. Inflict further penalties on anyone suffering from #2 or #4.


        Man, these advanced artifact illustrations are just entirely underwhelming. Shining Ice Mirror in particular has some ugly compression lines through it. This feels like a spot for more of that eye-popping super-detailed art; instead we get… thin, simple line-drawings in a different color palette.

        Shining Ice Mirror is stalked by the ghost of its old Solar owner. That’s awesome. And… goodness, any turn when you Aim also gives a Full Defense. That’s a +2 non-Charm bonus to your Defense, and I’m immediately trying to figure out how to stack this with Righteous Devil – ice sword in one hand, firewand in the other? Nah, I don’t think that works – but there’s got to be something. Maybe something in Solar Archery that applies to Melee Aiming…?

        This is definitely my favorite artifact so far – the sword has a clear personality of wanting you to be a cold, scheming killer, and that comes through even more so than for Adorei. This does exactly what the Hearthstones didn’t: it instantly fires my imagination and gets me wanting to run chargen, or at least drop a juicy plothook in my players’ paths.

        Spring Razor, green jade daiklave
        1. No automatic powers.
        2. Poison a target as per Arrow Frog Venom on a successful Decisive.
        3. On a successful hit, any poison the target suffers from intensifies and increases in duration.
        4. Targets always suffer at least one round of poison from #2.
        5. After three rounds of charging, improve the poison inflicted by #2.
        6. The target grows flowers that leach poison from him… and then inflict twice the damage leached.

        *whistles* Spring Razor does only one thing, but it does it really well. This is a vicious little blade – being poisoned is not quite as bad as it sometimes has been in the past, but those penalties will still kill you pretty dead.

        Freedom’s Cadence, starmetal chain shirt
        1. Automatic: for 3m, doesn’t count as armor for martial arts.
        2. Bonus social dice vs. an enemy you Crashed; reversed if he then Crashes you.
        3. +1 soak when you Evade an attack.
        4. For each Rush you succeed at, +1 success on future Rushes until you fail.
        5. +1 soak permanently.
        6. +1i to all allies at Short range each round as long as you flurry a dance.

        Freedom’s Cadence is basically a “Yes, please, this,” for any martial artist concept that would normally ban armor. Beautiful, but feels awfully close to a must-have, even if you never plan to dip into the Evocations – Dreaming Pearl or Ebon Shadow, say, really want something like this.

                        Orichalcum Hunting Hawk, orichalcum powerbow
                        1. Automatic: Ignore visibility penalties.
                        2. Jump one additional range band with Monkey Leap Technique.
                        3. Hide in trees using Survival and Archery. Free Evocation once you fight a difficult monster.
                        4. Hover while shooting enemies below you.
                        5. Reroll attack dice based on how high up you are.
                        6. Your anima becomes a hawk and fights alongside you.
                        Hunting Hawk wants a very specific playstyle – not just an archer, but a flying archer. I love the idea of having the bow come to life and support you in combat, but I wonder whether the shtick of “always take off at the start of combat” would get a little old.

                        Dauntless, orichalcum lamellar
                        1. Automatic: +4m commitment. Bonus strength for feats of strength; reduce or absorb Decisive damage, especially from battle groups.
                        2. Temporarily reduce soak, but +1 success on close-combat Withering attacks for the scene.
                        3. On hitting with a close-range attack, negate two points of Onslaught penalty to yourself.
                        4. 1/day, ignore all penalties from wounds or disease vs. a Crashed opponent.
                        5. Give up soak to negate damage to you.
                        Dauntless does this interesting thing where the armor becomes less and less valuable over time as it absorbs damage, but you can regenerate that soak by landing attacks. It feels strong: free damage negation + scenelong nonCharm bonus successes adds up.

                        *snerks* Black Wind looks ridiculous. “It’s a big boomerang! With a little gem thingy. And some compression issues.”

                        Black Wind, soulsteel skycutter
                        1. Automatic: on dealing Decisive damage, the blood crystallizes into a “blood blade” granting +1 Withering damage.
                        2. Spawn two blood blades instead of one. Free Evocation after killing someone important in line with a Principle.
                        3. Bonuses to Awareness and Investigation rolls in pursuit of a killer. Free Evocation after hunting down and killing a criminal.
                        4. Spend blood blades for bonus Decisive damage. Free Evocation for killing someone important in line with a Principle and a Tie.
                        5. Spend blood blades to make multiple Withering attacks. Free Evocation after gaining 5 blood blades.
                        6. Kill a known murderer from miles away, regardless of his protections. Kill 100 other murderers to recharge this power. Free Evocation after investigating and killing a serial killer.
                        Okay, Black Wind is hardcore. I take back every bad thing I said about it, for fear that it is listening. Note that this is an entirely free Charm tree, as long as you’re playing the right kind of character. (I know a guy who would absolutely eat this stuff up – it’s a really, well, evocative design.)

                        Volcano Cutter, red jade grand daiklave
                        1. Automatic: free access to #2; rarely, gain 1i on a Withering attack.
                        2. Gather motes every time you attack; spend these motes (and these motes only) on this Charm. Make an unblockable attack out to medium range; target band becomes an eruption point, which gathers power over time and explodes if people re-enter it.
                        3. Consume an eruption point for bonus damage on a Decisive.
                        4. Channel an eruption point to leap two range bands and make a Decisive, carrying the eruption point with you.
                        5. Move all eruption points to attack enemies; create a new point.
                        6. All eruption points explode; anyone within close range or between two of them takes 25-40L damage automatically. 1/season.
                        I absolutely love Volcano Cutter’s flavor. Dude got so sick of tricksy “Cut it in half, the halves grow back” monsters that he built an enormous volcano-forge to create the Daiklave of Everything Burns. That’s a Solar solution right there. Volcano Cutter – it’s the only way to be sure. The final effect here is just insane; anything without a perfect defense is going to die, and even those with, say, AST or HGD are going to have a heck of a time mitigating 25L. (Again, a little odd that they’d do just fine if it was infinite damage!)

                        And that’s it. That’s the panoply chapter.

                        There are only a few pages left. We see again the story of Strawmaiden Janest, Chosen of a single field god. The editing here is atrocious, but the story is everything it ought to be: haunting, stirring, and ending with an awesome heck yeah moment that’s only furthered by the accompanying illustration: Janest standing alone against the horde of the Fae. This is one of the best two or three pieces in the whole book, and it’s the perfect note to end on.

                        We close with a character sheet. It’s imperfect – it features the Attribute “Mamipulation,” and it requires your entire complement of health levels to be redrawn every time you gain or lose any levels. But it does recognize the need to mark, say, Withering and Decisive pools; XP and Solar XP totals; derived stats; and different kinds of soak – and no previous official sheet has. And it has room for Charms – Charms with a hilariously undersized blank for “Effect,” but room nonetheless.

                        And then come pages and pages of lovely backers.

                        And we’re done. Page 657 – still uselessly centered in the middle, edged in lovely cherry blossoms, heavy with the names of the Lords of Creation. There’s a metaphor in there somewhere – but I’ll leave that for someone else.

                        The end.

                                                    I built a damage calculator for withering attacks. At higher Defense ranges dex still wins out in damage, but for mundane weapons (assuming I've got my calculations right), your 5 Dex, 5 strength, 5 and specialty attack combat brute vs. soak 10 is getting marginally more damage out of a heavy mundane weapon through defense 5, a brief peak of medium weapons being best at defense 6/7, and then light starts winning out. Shifting to artifact weapons decreases these thresholds by 1 (to 4 and 6), increasing soak to 15 pushes them back again. A full excellency seems to push artifact heavy into pure superiority through Defense 10 on anything less than, like, 18 soak. Overwhelming favors light weapons, increasing dice pools tends to make the averages closer together, and heavy weapons really like double xs on attack rolls, though the only effects that grant that in 3e available to solars as-is only do so for light weapons or grapple attack rolls very condititonally.
