                Originally posted by CycloneJoker View Post
                If anybody can do it, it's Vance. Though I do know people who like it precisely because of its straightforward no-frills nature, that said.
                Martial Arts Styles are all about frills. That's why they are called styles. They are very particular ways of doing things which forsake most options and tactics in order to specialize in using a very narrow range of tactics.

                When you don't want frills, you learn your own native Charms. That's the difference between Solar Melee and Single Point Shining Into The Void Style and every other Style that uses a Melee weapon.

                Five-Dragon Style tried to do everything, so it was nothing. Second Edition's ghastly "Lunar Hero Style" committed a similar sin.

                I tried to give Five-Dragon Style character by making it a shield-fighting martial art. It could be used while you were holding anything else in your other hand, but it focused on tactics involving the shield, whether as a tool of defense, of attack, or of creating an opening for your other hand, even if that hand was an empty fist, a la Captain America.

                You'd almost never get enough Exalted or divine practitioners together to form a proper phalanx, but the Style would allow a hero to work together with a mortal Battlegroup in a shield formation, and with a sword or shield in hand, you could feel like Leonidas leading his 300 Spartans.

                To give the Style even more character, I tried to justify its name as "Five-Dragon Style" by alluding to dinosaurs who relied on hard plates and numbers for defense: Triceratops (and its general family), Stegosaurus, Anklyosaurus, Pachycephalosaurus... but I sort of failed to find a fifth dinosaur which didn't belong to those families, and people weren't terribly thrilled about this angle to begin with.

                Though without that effort, the "Shield" Style wouldn't have any connection to Five-Dragon Style, which would simply die for lack of an actual identity.

                                Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                Lunar Limit isn't something we've gotten around to discussing fully. I can say that I don't want to tie the Lunar's rage entirely to Limit, something that many players perceive as a drawback or ignore altogether. Rage something they drawn on with their Charms, one of the sources of their power. They're predators, but they aren't driven by hunger—rather, it's their rage that sustains their will to hunt.
                                But what is rage?


                                If a Lunar is a predator, and a predator is driven by hunger, consider what hunger is. It is an uncomfortable, distressing state of being. Hunger is pain.

                                Fear is also pain; like hunger, fear is an uncomfortable, distressing state of being.

                                Rage is a response to pain; a way to work past pain in order to remove the source of it. The predator hunts for prey to eat, and kills to assuage its hunger. A Lunar hunts to retrieve something precious and vital that was stolen from him, and kills to destroy something he fears will threaten him or that which he holds dear.


                                If Lunar magic swirls around rage, they will gain power from injury, hunger, thirst, sleep deprivation, fear, hatred, and love. Even doubt; the fear of uncertainty, of being wrong, of being betrayed.

                                They may care so much about everything that their Curse instead drives them to abandon something they care about simply to have a moment to breathe.
