                                                            If you've got enough Initiative, even bashing damage will kill someone. (Incidentally, in 2e there was an option for hitting someone with the flat of your blade, but I don't see one in 3e.)

                                                            Knockout Blow is a thing (if you crash someone with it they go unconscious), if you're a Solar Brawl specialist (it's a Brawl 5/Essence 3 Charm).

                                                            "Actions have consequences" is an important theme in Exalted, but choosing to leave your enemies alive is a choice that can potentially hurt you as much as it can potentially help you, quite possibly more so.

                                                            A highly skilled Exalt doesn't need any Charm to read in the dark by feeling the ink (corebook p. 185). It strikes me as absurd to suggest you should need a specialized Essence 3 Charm to say "I'd like to avoid the vital organs when I stab him." I mean, how often do highly-skilled warriors in popular fiction accidentally kill someone they wanted to spare, barring some intervening factor they didn't know about?

                                                            And besides, having a really high Initiative is supposed to mean that the momentum of the fight is massively in your favor, not that you've charged your weapon with a massive amount of unavoidable damage. If anything, shouldn't having a high Initiative make you more able to control the damage dealt? (To use an extreme example, surely a PC with 100 Initiative shouldn't be forced to break every bone in his enemy's body twice over if he'd prefer not to hit them that hard.)

                                                            If you don't want it to be too convenient to not kill people, then make it less convenient. To reference my previous suggestion of a gambit (which may not be the best way to do this, but I haven't thought of a better alternative), you could always make the gambit difficulty higher (7? even more?), so that you need significantly more Initiative to be confident of ending the fight nonlethally than you would to just gut them.

                                                            (I was going to suggest that you could just allow players to roll fewer damage dice than they're entitled to, but 3e doesn't have "dying" health levels and "incapacitation by lethal damage is usually fatal," so it's really easy to overdo it if a couple extra dice turn up successes.)
