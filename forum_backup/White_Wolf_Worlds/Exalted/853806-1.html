                        It seems like the sort of thing some Lunars would do for some Solars, but since Lunars seem to stake out their own personal cults and territory more often than not, it might be difficult to combine those into an actual faction.

                                                Well, that's the same for the Cult of the Illuminated. I mean, the Cult works fine for a background, but PCs don't really join, seriously.

                                                I did have one PC who went to a camp in the Scavenger Lands for 3 months, because of the sweet training they offered (100xp of Xp debt!). So, they gave him a light touch in terms of propaganda and brainwashing. And it worked, in that he considered himself a member, tried to live a somewhat decent life, and propagated a version of the religion in his state, but he didn't do what Sidereals told him or anything. Because they thought he'd rebel.


                                                I actually think the Sun King manipulators could be an interesting way to do a Solar/Lunar game with starting characters. If you want some kind of base and mentor for the Solars (and Lunars), maybe have a Lunar elder in his barbarian lair. She can support the PCs, give them things to do, teach them kung-fu and sorcery and how the Realm is evil and Lunars are awesome... and then maybe, as time goes by, the PCs realise she doesn't care about them, she's just another manipulator.
                                                I mean, generally I'm happy to have PCs just do things themselves (that's the nice thing about Exalted), but if you do want them to start with some kind of structure, it's an interesting way to do it. And less institutional than the Cult of the Illuminated.

                                                        I actually miswrote that. I should have said PCs don't seriously join.

                                                        But anyway. It's partly because Players hate being controlled.

                                                        But there's also the fact that joining an organisation rather than making your own isn't a very Solar thing.

                                                        I think the Cult is best at attracting PCs when it's less "join our religion" and more "oh great Solar, we've been primed to worship and obey you!" But then... is that really all that interesting? Compared to making your own cult? Not sure it is.

                                                        Which gets to the fact that I think it works much better as a background thing than in-game.

                                                        Saying this, I have used the Cult a fair bit in my game, but possibly not in the way the writers intended. I mean, none of the PCs have gone to train at Kether Rock, because, honestly, what PC wants to go spend 3 years of downtime training?

                                                        Now, go and train with Sidereal kung-fu masters in a month-long training montage, that's different, but joining what's essentially a school for Solars with a 3-4 year curriculum? No, I don't think many PCs (or Solars!) are keen on that. Solars want to change the world now, not in 20 years.

                                                        So... possibly, what I'm saying is that as much as I like Kether Rock, the camps are the least useful parts of the Cult once the game has started.

                                                        But, hey, there's lots of things that are mainly there for background, maybe that's okay. And it can work well as an organised group of antagonists (for example, barbarian tribes are being united by the 6 fanatical Solars of Kether Rock, you must try and stop them).

                                                        I actually have 5 Solar ex-members of Kether Rock as an antagonist group in my game, who realised the Sidereals were manipulating them and decided to make their own religion. I have always wanted to have a Solar circle of god-kings as antagonists.
