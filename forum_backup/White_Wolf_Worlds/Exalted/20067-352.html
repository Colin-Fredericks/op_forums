                            Originally posted by nonamemaddoxx View Post
                            How does perfect shadow stillness work? Charm text below for ease of reference.
                            Let's say I roll 7 dice, resulting in roll 1 below. I then re-roll the 5 dice that are not 10, resulting in roll 2 below.

                            Roll 1: 1, 2, 4, 7, 9, 10, 10
                            Roll 2: 1, 5, 6, 8, 10

                            Do these results mean that I have 9 sucesses (6 from the first roll, 3 from the second), meaning that the non-10 sucesses from the roll are kept?
                            Or do I have 7 sucesses, only keeping the 4 successes from the original 10s rolled, disregarding the sucesses from roll 1's 9 and 7?
                            You preserve the 10's from the first roll, and use the results of the second for the rest. Your final result is: 1,5,6,8,10,10,10.

                            ​Okay, this doesn't guarantee better results, but it almost doubles the expected count of 10 and slightly decreases the expected count of each of the other faces. On average, a die results in 5.95 rather than 5.5. The expected number of successes is 0.65 per die (for TN 7, 10s double rolls) rather than 0.5.
                            Code:
                            ​p(10)= 19/100, p(1)=9/100, p(2)=9/100...etc.
