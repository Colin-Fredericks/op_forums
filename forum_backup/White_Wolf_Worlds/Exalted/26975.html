    Bronze Age Solar? - Real Life Edition

    12-10-2013, 12:06 AM
    From the Life imitating Art Ahead of Its Time Department...
    Archaeologists in the Burnt City have discovered what appears to be an ancient prosthetic eye. What makes this discovery exceptionally awesome is the striking description of how the owner and her false eye would have appeared while she was still alive and blinking:
    [The eye] has a hemispherical form and a diameter of just over 2.5 cm (1 inch). It consists of very light material, probably bitumen paste. The surface of the artificial eye is covered with a thin layer of gold, engraved with a central circle (representing the iris) and gold lines patterned like sun rays. The female remains found with the artificial eye was 1.82 m tall (6 feet), much taller than ordinary women of her time. On both sides of the eye are drilled tiny holes, through which a golden thread could hold the eyeball in place. Since microscopic research has shown that the eye socket showed clear imprints of the golden thread, the eyeball must have been worn during her lifetime. The woman’s skeleton has been dated to between 2900 and 2800 BCE.
    So she was an extraordinarily tall woman walking around wearing an engraved golden eye patterned with rays like a tiny sun. What an awesome sight that must have been.
    So they didn't stop at just having a prosthetic eye around 2800 BCE. No, it's a golden eye with rays of a sun design upon it. I think the artwork offered up within said thread made it immediately "Solar" to me, but that's just where my mind has been lately.

    Even without the idea of "hey this is practically something out of Exalted!" aspect, I found this to be really cool. Have there been any other archeological finds people have noted to be rather Creation-like?

                                                  All the information I've found for modern day puts average female height at about 5'2" and the average in various countries around the world goes from 4'8" to 5'7". One source.

                                                  Have not found a solid study really delving into historical height prior to the last few hundred years. Did not find anything to suggest a woman's average height was ever 6 foot / 1.82 cm, but understood that lack of data is not necessarily proof.


                                                  I took note of the following paragraph on Wikipedia for context on how wildly height may vary within a specific region in a mere handful of generations:
                                                  In the late nineteenth century, the Netherlands was a land renowned for its short population, but today its population is among the world's tallest with young men averaging 1.84 m (6 ft 0 in) tall. The tallest average in Europe is in the Dinaric Alps, with young men averaging 1.86 m (6 ft 1 in), and young women averaging 1.71 m (5 ft 7 in).
                                                  This also led me to find the following article looking at how the Dutch may have become so tall inside of 150 years: http://suite101.com/a/why-are-the-dutch-so-tall-a55753

                                                  This particular article makes mention to the following:
                                                  In Northern Europe, human height reached a maximum around 800 A.D., but then dropped to a minimum in the 17th century before climbing back up again.
                                                  Perhaps 800 AD can give a lead for further investigation.
