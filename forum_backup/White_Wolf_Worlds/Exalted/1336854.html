    Which has been your favourite area of 3e Creation? How different did the games feel?

    09-19-2019, 09:42 AM
    My survey of where people had played got:
    10 games in the North
    5 games in the North-East
    1 or 2 in the East proper
    12 in the Scavenger Lands
    11 in the South-East, 9 of them around the Dreaming Sea.
    6 in the South
    7 or 8 in the South-West
    8 in the West
    And a few in other places; 1 in Autocthon, 1 in Gloam, 1 on the Blessed Isle.

    But about half of people are actually on there two or three times, as they'd played different games in different areas. So I was wondering which you liked most, and how different areas affected the nature of the game.

      Favourite was probably the Blessed Isle, although that was only about two sessions of a six session mortal game. A group of mortals, including an unexalted Dynast, were sent to the North on a mission by Nellens Poramo, except one of his political enemies thought it was some kind of bid for more power so they sent an exalted assassin after them, and taking advantage of the rise in banditry to the North of the Isle they...anyway I liked it. Then the game went to the super far north and became a desolate horror game.

      I think the worst one was the West game, just because lots of us were really hyped for the sailing and naval combat but that really fell short for us. We never really got that sense of adventure I was looking for but I can’t really articulate why that may be. I will say that having a boat that gave us easy access to many separated island cultures was cool though.

      The far far north east game was fun in that it was so far from any Dragonblooded power that we could afford to be a lot more cavalier with our essence and artifacts. It also had a very dynamic range of seasons like Canada and the northern US, so we could have blistering summers and frigid winters, which was pretty cool. It also gave us a chance to develop cultures relatively unaffected by the shadow of the Scarlett Empire.

      Exact opposite of that, the south game took place IN a satrapy, so the PCs had to be pretty careful most of the time. I liked the aesthetics I got to pull from there, the Alin from Rise of Legends mostly. A city on a broken floating mesa, Raksha sultans. Also easy access to firedust leads to some pretty tense and exciting moments. Also water, one of the few things Solars can’t just smash through. When the city’s water screws got destroyed that was a huge problem.

      The east one near Vaneha was just kind of standard I guess. It wasn’t even bland it was just okay is just about every respect.

          When I first played Exalted what, 12 years ago maybe, our Dragonblood game was in the West, then our Solar game moved to the West, then we played another Dragonblood game in the West... anyway, we got tired of the West and never went back, so I've not played 3rd ed in the West. Obviously as the book hasn't been released,

          I will say that having a boat that gave us easy access to many separated island cultures was cool though.
          So as an ST, I've run two (non-Exalted) games where the PCs had their own ships, and on the one hand it's a great opportunity for a variety of really different and interesting cultures. On the other hand, it can be really difficult because the PCs have so much choice about where to go. And while it may be a long time for them to get there in-game, out-of-game it's 5 minutes so if they suddenly decide to go somewhere you didn't expect, you've got to come up with it quickly. Even if they decide at the end of session to go somewhere different, it's a lot more difficult than when they're in a very definite sandbox.
          And it also makes it much more difficult to come up with interesting NPCs for them to interact with if they keep leaving them for new places. You can do NPCs on the ship, but that tends to mean they're allies/servants of the PCs, whereas if you want recurring enemies, or rival frenemy monarchs

          One thing I found is that it's not so bad if they're in a particular distinct area with, say, 5 or 6 big islands/planets/whatever, and then various smaller islands/planets-of-hats they can visit if they like.
          When running Rogue Trader, I had them in one particular area of planets where two rival powers were fighting over protectorate planets, and that worked well (the game didn't go well for other reasons, but that thing went well I think; though in the very first session, about half an hour in, before they got there, they suddenly went somewhere I hadn't expected and I suddenly had to pull a whole plot out of my ass based on a throwaway line about pirates). The players had choice about where to go, but generally choosing between 3 or 4 planets, so I could plan them all. And they'd go back later to the same planets sometimes.
          Whereas when running Star Wars they could choose between thousands of planets, and I found that really awkward to plot for, and most important make engaging NPCs for (ironically, that game's gone way better than Rogue Trader because the PCs are much better, but I did find making cultures for them to visit and engaging NPCs much harder than Rogue Trader).

          Saying that, if I go back to island-hopping in Exalted, I did once play a game (this was in 2nd ed, but it would be the same in 3rd ed), where we travelled along between different, interesting, islands-of-the-week. But the ST was able to plan them well because we were in the far north-west and were going a specific route, east to west. We couldn't suddenly decide to turn south or north and go somewhere else, and so the ST always knew where we were going to go.

          The far far north east game was fun in that it was so far from any Dragonblooded power that we could afford to be a lot more cavalier with our essence and artifacts... It also gave us a chance to develop cultures relatively unaffected by the shadow of the Scarlett Empire.
          I've found the same with my own game, which is north of Iscomay (it's on the peninsula right at the top-right of Mela's Fangs, on the map of Creation).

          The east one near Vaneha was just kind of standard I guess. It wasn’t even bland it was just okay is just about every respect.
          I noticed Vaneha seems the second-most popular location, after Nexus. I assume due to the Japanese aesthetics.

                                                  Having rules for Dragon-Blooded PCs can in some ways limits their utility as NPCs if you don't escape a certain mindset.

                                                  One of the most impressive things the DB QC could do was that their excellency also allowed them to reroll (essence) dice because it meant a Solar's dice tricks were likely to be necessary against a Terrestrial Exalted elder on the top of their game capable of matching or slightly exceeding their dice cap. Splitting it up gave more things for Dragon-Blooded PCs to work on but made it harder to tell when someone like Mnemon could dial things up to 11 and if she even should if WFHW didn't provide a canonical method in this instance.

                                                      As a storyteller I’m definitely opposed to using PC generation rules for NPC antagonists. Especially if multiples of them are showing up at once, keeping all their dicepools and charms in your head at one time is really hard and not usually worth it. It often deflates characters from a numbers standpoint, but inflated their versatility, so it comes out about even but with way more work.

                                                      Like the Abyssal QC has a charm that’s kind of Hungry Tiger Technique except better. It’s helpful for making them dangerous but keeping them in check as well. You don’t have some perfectly constructed abyssal Dusk caste killing your whole circle.

                                                      I actually think we could do with a few more Solar and Abyssal QCs in the antagonist sections.
