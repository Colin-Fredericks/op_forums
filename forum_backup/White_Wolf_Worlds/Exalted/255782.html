    Lunar Exalted Advice

    09-13-2014, 12:03 AM
    Now that my story teller is wrapping up a Solar focused game, I've managed to get into a game where I have the opportunity to play a Lunar in a longer running game. So keeping mind 2.5 changes to minimum damage and perfect defense costs, I'd first like to know if there are any scene long defense charms, aside from Armor Forming Technique and Hide Toughening Essence, or cost effective ones that will actually hold up on a celestial level.

    The second thing I'm curious about is the visibility of the tattoos. I've seen some sources imply constant visibility and others that imply that they appear with flaring. Just how obvious are they by default? And are there any common house rules or homebrews, aside from TAW, that comment on this?

      Tattoos are obvious on true forms but not on false forms (page 130 of the Lunar manual).

          Originally posted by Grifnir View Post
          The second thing I'm curious about is the visibility of the tattoos. I've seen some sources imply constant visibility and others that imply that they appear with flaring. Just how obvious are they by default?
          By default, moonsilver tattoos are visible when you are in any true shapes (so natural human, spirit shape, and DBT if you have it). For all assumed shapes, they are invisible.

          There is also a knack or charm that hides them in true shapes (I can never remember which because there is knack that hides the Tell and a charm that hides the tattoos - or vice versa, but I have never been able to get them to gel in my head).

          The problem with flare is that when you hit Coat-Me-in-Radium level, you get shunted into only using true shapes, which means you are automatically showing tattoos.

          If you want to wander around sans tattoos, go whack a human that looks really close to your appearance, and then go do the same to another member of your spirit shape species. As long as you are willing to pay the increased mote cost and avoid flare when possible, you could avoid tattoos 90% of the time.

              There's an Ink Monkeys charm that gives you I think a +3DV from a shield made of bone. I might be misremembering, because that seems quite high, but I think that's what it did. You can also end it to provide a free perfect defence. I forget the name though, I'm afraid.

                  You probably want to look at scene-long Martial Arts Charms for soak, or Form-types that provide soak. Snake comes to mind, as does Plague of Hats' Tiger Style Rewrite. Tiger will even let you stack those Charms with normal armor since it's armor-compatible. There may be some others...maybe White Reaper? In any case, MAs will have much more reasonable mins/prereqs that actually provide other benefits (MA 5 is good for your to-hit and parry DV after all) versus Stamina 5 which really does not do a whole lot for you, especially compared to Dex 5. Lunar prereqs and mins are too high even if you use the suggested 9/7/5 Attribute spread from Domino's alternate Lunar chargen.

                      Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                      There's an Ink Monkeys charm that gives you I think a +3DV from a shield made of bone. I might be misremembering, because that seems quite high, but I think that's what it did. You can also end it to provide a free perfect defence. I forget the name though, I'm afraid.
                      Limb-Shielding Growth. If the group is using 2.5, however, they'll have to stick in a houserule about how its shield function works, because shields count as weapons now.

                      Also, don't forget that every single charm with the Fury tag can last for a good long part of the scene, and that includes a lot of the Stamina defensive charms.
                      Lunars are actually very strong in terms of long-term defence, because they can get their DBT and gift charms going alongside their RLF and fury charms. A big cost at the beginning of the scene, but after that you don't need to spend a lot of motes.
                      Most Gift Charms are incredibly underwhelming in what they do. Armor-Forming Technique and Limb-Shielding Growth are at the top of the heap for actually being useful. At the opposite end, you have Fertile Breath Inversion and Moonsilver Stomach Concentration, which do things that should be default anyway (breathing in what your spirit shape can breathe, eating what your spirit shape can eat). Fury is an almost-durable series of effects that slows the game down with book-keeping and action-counting.
