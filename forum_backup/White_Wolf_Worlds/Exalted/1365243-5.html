                                                    Originally posted by mark View Post
                                                    On a related note, reincarnation itself IS a resurrection of sorts. It's even possible for a hun to remember some things from the life right before this one. In theory you could store someone's memories, trace his reincarnation and give them back to him. Depending on how much "mojo" you have, you may be able to recreate them just like that with whatever metaphysical explanation you can come with for your spell/artifact/etc
                                                    Remembering past lives perfectly is the province of Artifact N/As, like Domnica's Mantle. Certain characteristics may carry across successive reincarnations of the hun, but memories are largely wiped by the mechanism of Lethe and the Well of Souls, which are currently the only way to successfully reintegrate the two halves of the human soul. Other methods have significant drawbacks, up to someone countermagicing your sorcerous android and "killing" it, or a Liminal destroying the Frankenstein you've made because that's how they roll. There's just no free lunch.

                                                    You say that the out of game reason is obvious, but it's worth definitively re-stating: Exalted is a game with consequences to your actions. You can't undo what has been done, you can only create something anew. For myself, I find stories with consequences to be frankly more interesting than stories that aren't; instead of making the story about the success of resurrection, it's more interesting to wonder: what did the character do that they want to undo so very badly, and why? Why do they fear death so much that they seek immortality, and what ruin do they leave in their wake?

                                                        It occurs to me to flip the question around. Why should resurrection be possible? Why should one have to justify that death is a permanent thing?

                                                        I get that in many games and other media, once characters reach a certain level of power, death is treated more like a status effect than a narrative event. And Exalted are very powerful, sure, and can do many miraculous things. But does that mean the universe isn't allowed to say that some things are beyond even miracles, because that's just how things are? That death is an ending, not a pause, and that true resurrection is as patently nonsensical as reaching the top of a mountain and then climbing some more?
