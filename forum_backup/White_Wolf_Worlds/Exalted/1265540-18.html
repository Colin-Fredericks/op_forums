                        (Like how 19th and 20th century imperialism, or "neocolonialism", to my mind, isn't actually very useful for describing the Realm compared to earlier empires across the world - Spanish and British in Americas and within Europe, Russia in Siberia, Ottomans, Rome, Persia, Chinese, etc. - that weren't experiencing the kind of very unusual world historical technological revolutions that happened in the 19th-20th century and which the Realm sees nothing much like.                             For what it's worth, I would rather see Lunars have "that certain edge" to them because they're engaged in a war of resistance, and resistance against empire is always an ugly, brutal struggle, and the idea of a "clean war" is a self-serving myth promulgated after the fact by the winners, than because Lunars are essentially and inalienably monstrous. Once again, Werewolf: the Forsaken is a useful point of reference here, because the Uratha are essentially and inalienably monstrous. Yes, their hunting serves a vital ecological purpose. Yes, they can try to minimize collateral damage. The fact remains that they're obligate predators for whom ritualistic murder is something like orgasmic release paired with a religious experience, and who feel no more instinctual aversion to killing humans than wolves do to eating rabbits. The question in Forsaken isn't whether the PCs are secretly misunderstood good guys. They're monsters. They lie, steal, and kill as naturally as they breathe. The question is whether the World of Darkness could function without them to keep all of its other monsters in check.

                            I don't feel that this laser focus on inherent monstrosity is needed for the Lunars. They're fighting a war. War has all the baked-in moral conflict you could ever need.

                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                    I think a far more pertinent argument would be for their scary Lore Charms, since those actually exemplify the Solar power, and even then those are more in the vein of being a prophet of doom.
                                    Along with nearly all of the Occult Charms, particularly the ones dealing with spirits. One even manifests every allied spirit if the roll has exactly three 6s, which seems right on the nose with a steely gauntlet. Or the Brawl Charm that pulls out someone's heart, presumably to sacrifice to the Unconquered Sun. Also, any Charms that conjure up magical objects or mounts. Sail also seems like it has a lot of weirdness in it, especially when it allows you to speak in strange maritime tongues or claim ships mystically as your own. Craft also whips up Artifacts in little time at all, which is probably freaky to see when someone hammers on steel ingots with closed fists, exhales onto them, and then produces a blade of unparalleled quality.

                                    I feel as though this is another case of somebody taking the wrong direction with reading somebody else's metaphor; as though you're reading it as Lunars having powers because people in character find them to be scary, rather than using some templates consistent across scary people in real life mythologies and using them as the guideline for designing Lunars.
                                    I was making a metatextual comment there.

                                    Your argument seems to be kind of going on a weird course; it's like, you ask for a concise definition of witchy stuff, but then when one is offered it doesn't count because a Solar could also do it, but only if they use sorcery?
                                    But we established above that Solars do have disturbing powers without sorcery, so allow me to amend that there are certain facets of sorcery that I don't find to be compelling Charm territory. The setting itself tends to isolate conjuring of large-scale objects and structures like shelter and ships in sorcery, for example, and to others, it adds a ritual flair, like in how Spoke the Wooden Face requires preparatory carving of a tree. I'm fairly certain that Dragon-Blooded or Exigents of arbor-gods could do something like that, but they probably wouldn't require the carving part.

                                    So, when Lunars, for some reason, get wide-scale, long-lasting tracts of territory that can turn humans into beastfolk, I question why it's not a spell or even just a common example of a working. It seems too large-scale for a Charm.

                                    So, what, as long as sorcery exists as a thing that is available to any Exalted, in parallel to their own Charms that actually say something about them, then nobody gets to have their own Charms described in certain terms applied for various kinds of magic user because... it's not technically correct? Do you object to describing Sidereals as kung fu ninja bureaucrats because none of those things are limited to them either?
                                    Sidereals have a culture of bureaucracy (every one of them retrieved by the Bureau of Destiny is, well, working for a governmental organ titled the Bureau of Destiny), wield martial arts that no one else can without their tutelage, and the ninja part at least emphasizes stealth and misdirection, which is readily displayed in their Charms and aided substantially in their Arcane Fate, as they broke the cosmos to make it so that no one would remember them clearly. In contrast, Lunars shapeshift into animals more easily than anyone else, have tended to be very visceral, and their culture is one of insurgents who trade favors, occasionally decided by kicking someone's ass in a dispute, and work to take down their mutual enemies when not doing their own thing. Their powers centered on alteration of their own bodies without preamble, ritual, or even thought, and along the lines of fierce, predatory actions. "Witch" seems to lack the raw, visceral feel that the splat has claimed; it can be polished up and put in classrooms, described as a variation of a wizard, and made to operate in a very Solar fashion with great ease - and when the witches go "awry", they can become Abyssals or Infernals.

                                    (Also, kung fu ninja bureaucrat more of a fan appellation, though admittedly one somewhat less flippant and jokey than "teacup ninja" or "fate Jedi". It still gives no hint as to their mastery and manipulation of destiny, for example, so as a summation, it doesn't really cover their essence.)

                                    Like, you ask for a concise definition, but then reject it because it's not specific enough to the Exalted in question, even if other Exalted getting in on it isn't really something they do with their Charms?
                                    But the other Exalted do get in on it with their Charms. Abyssals and Infernals were the go-to splats for that in previous editions; it's not even relegated just to Exalted, since Fair Folk felt that way, too, and Dragon Kings may or may not be there, but certainly were when the Dark Paths were in their book. Lunars joining the club doesn't mean that they take on the term as a definition of their essence, even if we stop calling Infernals warlocks. Abyssals even serve beings known as witch-kings of the Underworld.

                                    I suppose the tl;dr is that the witch business seems like an ill-fitting, 11th-hour change of nomenclature.
