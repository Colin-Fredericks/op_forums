    JLA seems to be taking off.
    First Batman becomes Bat-God, and now the rest are following. Superman has become the God of Strength, Batman the God of Knowledge, Flash the God of Death, having merged with the Black Racer, and Shazam the God of Gods. Lex Luthor, left to languish on Apokalips, has become the God of Apokalips.
    I'm not a fan of Johns' writing and his run on Justice League hasn't changed that. Like a lot of Johns' work, there's a lot of big and flashy events but they don't have much of an impact or depth. If you aren't a fan of everything Johns is a fan of, they're pretty dull. He has a tendency to write around the League to get to the characters he really wants to write about. There's an entire issue where the only Leaguer is Cyborg who only appears for a few pages, standing in the darkness waiting for someone else to tell him a story.
    I think Johns is capable of being a good writer, but he hasn't really been hitting it in years and years.Lately it seems like he's performing a poor rendition of Grant Morrison's Greatest Hits. So many of the beats in his Justice League run feel like they've already been done. Grant Morrison teased the idea of the Justice League becoming New Gods in Final Crisis then seemed to pull back, realizing that it was a bit too on the nose. The prevalence of the Crime Society of America earlier in his JL story seemed like a pale shadow of how Morrison revived the characters and walked that thin line of goofiness and relevance. And now Johns has set up the idea of Batman facing Joe Chill, which Morrison already pulled off superbly in his Batman run.

    He's done it before. His whole "War of the Light" story in Green Lantern took a clever little Alan Moore story about the fall of the Lanterns and turned it into a Michael Bay style adventure about Rainbow Bright heroes. Modern day Johns just feels like a kid smashing together action figures and riffing off the ideas of stories that have already been told by better writers.

                        Latveria is generally depicted as a country fairly well off but with bouts of extreme tyranny depending upon the whims of its ruler. There's generally two sides of the country shown. One where everyone is horribly oppressed all the time, and another where people celebrate the rulership of Doom openly when visitors come, then return to their mundane lives beyond that. It's a country where the 1% is one person, and the 99% is everyone else.



                        Everyone who is well off there is well off directly as a result of Doom making them that way. And every citizen is a potential subject in one of his experiments. The only other option is death, and since he is a master of the dark arts, even that may not be an escape.

                        There was a story arc where he confronted the Wakandan Panther God, who sought to determine whether his motives were pure. And while it is true Doom has good intentions for everyone, and best intentions for himself, his specifications for a utopia are close-mindedly specific. There is no crime because people who consider crime are killed by Doom. He considers himself a good ruler, when compared to every tyrant and despot in history. Everyone will be united, under Doom, and there will be no exception.

                        As I said before, Doomworld, arguably, shows the flaws in his beliefs, or at least the bad side of it. Given the most power in the universe he uses it to torment his old enemies, and bumps people like Apocalypse and Mister Sinister to his dukes, ruling smaller 'nations' in his stead however they desire, providing they follow his commandments.

                                                        Two thoughts occur to me. First, while I can see where you're coming from, I'm going to have to respectfully disagree with your interpretation of the inversion event. If it was a major/minor-minor/major thing, then we'd see other signs of it - major traits would become much less prevalent. One of Tony's defining characteristics is his belief in the future and the ability of people to create it through technology. However, his actions in SIM can be seen as a continuation of that, albeit in a somewhat twisted form. They’re really more remiscient of a Tony who has simply had his ‘limitations’ removed if you will, which seems to line up with how we saw it affect the other inverted characters; traditionally heroic types spoke a lot about being free of their previous limitations, while usually villainous types focussed more on how they now find their previous actions repulsive and terrible. Modelling it more as a simple ‘morality switch’, as silly as that notion is (and part of why I try to ignore the AXIS as much as possible) fits better. Even fits with the original intent of the spell; if you accept Xavier as purely or nearly-purely good (which seems to be how they spoke about him; I don’t know X-men well enough to definitively comment) and Red Skull as purely or nearly-purely evil (again, consistent with how they spoke of him during the event which is my main benchmark), then flipping the good/bad switch fits, albeit not perfectly.

                                                        If it was a major/minor thing, then I would more expect Tony to lose his interest in technology and the future – sure, we see a bit of that with him drinking and partying, but he’s still interested in studying Teen Abomination, constructed a better suit and even built on Extremis 3.0. Twisted, to be sure, but reasonably consistent with his interest in technology and the future. Just more self-interested than usual. And control has often been a part of his character; look at what it took him to end up entrusting Pepper to run his company, and she’s shown herself to be trustworthy and capable many, many times over. Even with the Most Wanted storyline, he did as much as he possibly could himself, relying on even those he trusted most as little as possible. I vaguely remember some side comment when he was building Resilient and the swarm and such that there were some questions about privacy or something. Plus there's the whole Illuminati storyline, which I'm not terribly familiar with so I shan't comment further beyond saying it sounds more like he's gone from subtle control to overt.

                                                        The second point is that I try to ignore the ‘external event twisting his mind’ aspect of the story as much as possible, as hypocritical as that is. It just strikes me as kind of silly; morality is way too complex to be subject to a simple switch like that, so I try to ignore the origin of his new state and just view it as a result of some kind of trauma and him going overboard to compensate – again, consistent with past portrayal.
