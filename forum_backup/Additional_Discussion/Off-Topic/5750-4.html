                                                    When I was at a patient's house the other day, some old movie was on; I looked up the title but have since forgotten it. Not really the point. The scene I saw (and to judge from Wikipedia's synopsis, this scene was not representative of the movie as a whole, which it bills as a comedy) was Jimmy Stewart working as a salesperson in a boutique owned by the Wizard of Oz. Oz was having marital troubles--his wife was spending was too much money, and Oz didn't have the guts to cut her off--that were distracting him at work, which meant he wasn't complimenting Jimmy Stewart every time Jimmy managed to not trip over his own dick on the way to the toilet, which Jimmy took as a sign that the boss had it out for him. Jimmy confronted Oz about it, but Oz didn't want to discuss his marital problems with an employee and was stressed out to boot, so they got into an argument and Jimmy Stewart had to decide whether he'd stay or leave.

                                                    I was fascinated. It was a scene--and as far as I knew at the time, a movie--about misunderstandings and miscommunications brought on by dueling senses of pride, and what's more, it was...how can I put this? It made me realize I can't remember the last time I watched a movie about things that could actually happen in the real world. I want to see more of that. God-aliens beating up elves is fun and all, but I feel like I imagine an old-time sailor feels when he gets to shore after a month and can finally have something to eat other than pickled meat. I feel starved for something I didn't realize I was missing.
