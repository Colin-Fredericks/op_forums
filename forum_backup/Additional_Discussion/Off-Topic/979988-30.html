                For the people talking about Safe Spaces, I'd point out that the intended purpose of a safe space is for a group that has suffered trauma or has otherwise been marginalized. A place for women who have been raped by men is a safe space because it gives them a place to talk about their trauma where they are not confronted by reminders of their abuser. Shelters for abused women came to exist because there was no real place for those women to go - an ordinary homeless shelter was not typically equipped to address their kinds of problems, the kind that come from assisting a traumatized mother and her children (as many of the women were often mothers). Safe spaces for minorities exist within these groups, too, like a meeting for transgender women of color to evoke the marginalized bit earlier. In most cases a transgender person of color is going to have a very different experience than a white transgender person will, they need a place where they can work through things on a support group level without having to deal with white transgender people 'speaking for them', in a sense.

                Most conservatives aren't going to face traumatic abuse for their conservative beliefs, if you're that mythical unicorn who grew up in an abusive liberal household that burned all of your bibles because the bible is evil - then certainly maybe a safe space is for you, but I don't think anyone in this thread has been outright called a monster, except Luis, and he's gone for good now. People can claim that beliefs you hold are 'monstrous', but that's really not the same thing as saying a gay person is fundamentally evil, or that marriage only applies if it's from my religion and its views. People criticizing your beliefs as being wrong or regressive is not a personal attack on you, it's an attack on your argument. I could be wrong, but I haven't seen a lot of 'If you believe in a god, then you're scum' in this thread, if there was such talk, the moderators would act on it, they have in other threads.

                So conservatives are not victims, and you certainly couldn't say they are marginalized, because broad conservative policies are being enacted every day in America - yes, they are being challenged, but that's not the same thing as being marginalized, because to challenge something you have to in some way acknowledge it.

                                                    When I was a practicing Christian, I believed that legal customs concerning marriage were at best, human interpretations of a divine institution. That is to say that man can say whatever we like about marriage, but we can't change it from God's design. Mainstream culture could deviate from it and forgot about the real thing over time, but a true, sacred marriage would always be possible. So making gay marriage legal would be as threatening to marriage as making assault against God legal would cause danger to Him. It would mean that the state was endorsing sin (fornication, albeit the same as what was before), but since when haven't temporal authorities endorsed sin (particularly greed and bloodshed) and is it really the place of the state to prohibit sin in cases where the only victims are the perpetrators themselves? It may lead to an increased acceptance of such behaviour, but so far as I was concerned, Christians needed to stop adopting non-Christian standards morality just because that's what others were doing. After all, getting marginalised isn't and never has been evidence that one is wrong.

                                                    To Wonderandawe, speaking as an ex-conservative on that particular matter, I very much believed in "hate the sin, love the sinner". That still seems logical to me now, although I have different ideas about morality these days and so I don't object to the practices either. If merely objecting to what I considered fornication in that case is homophobic, then I was homophobic, although the idea that was it wrong would have seemed as well founded to me at the time as I presume you find calling homosexuality wrong based on interpretations of the scripture of one religion.

                                                    I don't how much more I can explain that. Those were just the musings of one teenager. As a gay bastard, I had a personal interest in the topic. I can't speak for all doctrine based objection to gay marriage and I certainly can't speak for its secular critics.
