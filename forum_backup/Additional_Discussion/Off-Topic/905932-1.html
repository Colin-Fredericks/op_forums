                                                        X-Men: Apocalypse (48% on RT)
                                                        Elysium (68% on RT)
                                                        Cinderella (Live Action 2015, 83% on RT)
                                                        Deadpool (83% on RT, of a good reason why RT scores aren't the be all here, but I'll get to that below)
                                                        X-Men: First Class (87% on RT)
                                                        X-Men: Days of Future Past (91% on RT)
                                                        The Martian (92% on RT)

                                                        So he's all over the place when it comes to direct adaptations. Original comedies? Both in the gutter (bad sign if he's going to have a stronger hand on any future Deadpool stuff). While not a "franchise" the drop from Elysium to Chappie is pretty important because that means he did nothing to help a promising director do better after District 9; and instead indulged him as things progressively got worse with each film. His job with both of those movies was to be a producer of Blomkamp, and he didn't do much of a job doing that.

                                                        So RT alone isn't a bad general picture, but it isn't a complete one. Let's ignore the Apocalypse isn't getting a great critical reception. In general his involvement with the X-Franchise is generally the highlight of his career as a producer along with Cinderella and the Martian.

                                                        What do all of those have in common? People that didn't need Kinberg as a strong editorial voice. Cinderella was directed by Kenneth Branagh, and The Martian was directed by Ridley Scott. These are both huge names in the industry that know exactly what they're doing. The X-Men specific films have the continual hand of Bryan Singer on them (Apocalypse the is only X-Men movie to get a poor critical response with Singer involved). Deadpool was Ryan Reynolds and Tim Miller.

                                                        Simply put, this is not Fox's answer to Kevin Feige. He might be a fine producer working with people with strong visions to manage budgets and that side of things for them, but his writing credits alone show that he doesn't have the chops to be the guy managing a major film franchise and guide it to major success. What on Earth implies he's (a) the right guy for this, and (b) the guy to pin our hopes on for an X-Force movie with Cable and X-23?
