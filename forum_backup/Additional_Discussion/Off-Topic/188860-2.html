                                                Most? Or just most you've got experience with?

                                                I belong to a group the promotes planting and maintaining native wild fields near agricultural sites. It's entirely local. We're after both supporting local wildlife and decreasing the local impact of fertilizers, pesticides (and it turns out colony collapse disorder with bees). We're not after any sort of federal change. Because we don't need the government to do anything. Though grants are nice.

                                                Most of us are also part of larger groups that you're more used to thinking of as being too naive. But it isn't that we're naive. It's that there are local problems and their are larger problems. We need local activism and federal activism.

                                                The bees are actually a good example of this. On one hand there's a huge amount of simple local things we can do about bees. And we really need them. But at the same time there is a national crisis here. If bee colonies continue to drop the impact on the agricultural industry is immense. We'll need to start paying people to pollinate crops, vastly increasing the price of food just to maintain current production levels.

                                                Local activism can help, a lot, but this is a problem that doesn't end at any line drawn on a map. The few counties that have managed to start repairing the damage done to bee populations can't magically replace all the colonies we've lost. They can't fix the problems that caused the collapse in the first place. That's why we also need better regulations on monocultures crops field distributions (monocultures are problematic, but the bigger problem is that we have giant fields of them that aren't broken up by other crops), and chemical applications over biological ones.

                                                The thing about politics is that nothing can wait.
                                                That might be how politicians approach their issues of choice... but there are plenty of things that can wait. I'm pretty sure naming something else after someone can wait even if that gets done without much fuss.

                                                Tax code reform should get done... but it can wait. We'll collect taxes even if we're doing it in a suboptimal way.

                                                I think it is incredibly dangerous to just see it as, "well in politics nothing can wait, so everything is equal," when in reality this simply isn't the case. Even if the country isn't going to get destroyed if infrastructure issues aren't addressed or climate change doesn't get acted on overnight, it doesn't mean we aren't digging ourselves so deep by not fixing it that we aren't heading to the point where "fixing" stops being the point and "coping with the complete loss of something and trying to manage what to do" is the new issue.

                                                So obviously it can wait another 5 to 10 years while we sort it out, barring a cascade effect that, if it's going to happen we have probably already caused and can't fix anyway. (This is not to say we should not fix the problem, merely that a problem of such catastrophic size won't get fixed in less than... a lifetime, perhaps.)
                                                The problem is we are, going by the best science on the subject, on that point where if we don't start doing things now, not in 10 years, then we're going to get into cascade effects that we can't stop. We're already seeing the opening signs of those cascades getting set off. Politics has spent decades not doing enough when doing it would have been easier and we wouldn't be stuck in a situation of picking how bad do we want it.

                                                This sort of attitude is one of the reasons why climate change isn't moving. If you don't want to sound like denialist and just claim it doesn't matter, you can throw up your hands and say, "well it's too late, so screw it." The longer we wait, the worse fixing it gets, until fixing it isn't an option any more and the slow death of larger nations starts setting in.

                                                "Stabilize the economy!" Boy howdy if we knew how to do that, it'd be a miracle.
                                                Even if not completely universally effective, there's plenty of prominent economic thinkers both outside of politics and inside of it that have solid proposals to do this. For example, it isn't going to fix everything, but eliminating the student debt bloating would certainly help stabilize the economy. I don't know any economic principle that says this problem is not a problem, or that fixing it will make it worse. Everything I've read on it says pretty universally that

                                                The inability to prioritize issues by need and immediacy is a problem. And it doesn't have to be one. Waiting inherently means there are major issues that are going to get worse - possibly to the point of not being able to be fixed - so telling people that they have to suck it up and wait ~20 years before starting to fix it will even happen is basically just acknowledging that our system is going to drive itself off a cliff because we can't decided to apply brakes fast enough for anything that isn't war.
