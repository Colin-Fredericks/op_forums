                            Originally posted by Morty View Post
                            That's a fair way of looking at it, but I don't think the spear needs to be any particular shape for this to work. I guess I don't take the D&D weapon list very seriously.
                            To be fair, WotC doesn't seem to take it that seriously either. Not if Monks are not just allowed, but suggested, to reflavor weapons as ones more befitting their archetype ("nunchucks can be clubs" is the specific example used). I consider the weapons list to be suggestions for where to start when figuring out how a given weapon works.

                            Because personally, I can think of many ways the list is limited, providing the bare bones for the full breadth of combat tools available. How do you do an Arming Sword? It's a Longsword, except you can't two-hand it. Is it really impossible for sickles to be finesse weapons? Absolutely not. Shouldn't a person be able to produce damage of multiple different types for some weapons, and not just one? Not only should this be true, it should be common practice. (Half-swording is a real historical technique for getting more out of a longsword, and most Halberds also have bits allowing them to act as long war-picks or pikes).

                            At least for your own health, use the weapons list as a starting point, not the comprehensive list of options. I certainly get conniptions thinking about its limitations. On the other hand, I also remind myself that Simplicity was itself a design goal in 5e, and one that has its own value.

                                I certainly appreciate 5e’s move towards simplicity with weapons compared to previous editions. I don’t think longsword, bastard sword, and katana all need to be separate weapons, for example, and while club does seem a bit of an odd choice to stand in for nunchaku, I do think flails fit that bill nicely. On the other hand, 5e’s weapon table has some pretty glaring issues.

                                In my WIP equipment overhaul I give weapons up to two damage types, and add that you can use a weapon to do damage of a type it doesn’t normally deal, but it counts as an improvised weapon (dealing 1d4 if one-handed, or 1d6 if two-handed). On paper it goes a long way, the only trouble is that there are only 8 monsters in 5e that care about bludgeoning, piercing, or slashing, and one of those only cares if the character doing the piercing damage has a Good alignment.

Then let the players describe them however they feel like.

                                    There's always been contention regarding weapons that are versatile and finesse. Not sure if it'd disrupt anything.
