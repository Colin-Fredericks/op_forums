                                    In the British media, if you're interested, I think Thomas Frank has been pretty good on the Presidential race, and worth reading for his article yesterday - https://www.theguardian.com/commenti...inton-liberals

                                    Though I think it's easy to overstate the change in support. The whole flipping of some of Democrat voting workers whose regions used to have good, skilled unionised manufacturing work and now don't that has helped Trump, but I think it's less exaggerated than in Frank's article.

                                    The biggest thing that to me seems to have worked in his favour I think that wasn't is that many Republican voters in the US seem likely to basically not care either way much about his gaffes and likely lack of competence, if it meant keeping a Clinton and a Democrat out of control of the Presidency and out of control of the judicial appointments. I think a lot of people thought much of the traditional Republican mainstream would #NeverTrump, but maybe #NeverAgainClinton seems like a bigger factor. Or at least, the #NeverTrumpers among Republicans weren't as significant as the voters on the other side that dropped Clinton.

                                    Apparently turnout for both candidates were down from expectations and previous elections, Clinton and Trump were both unpopular candidates, and neither have rebuilt trust with a general segment of the population (which continues to decline). Trump might've built a little with blue collar workers, but he lost some of most others. It seems like the turnout drop was worse for Clinton, and that's a big factor in why she ended up being beaten.

                                    Seems like a yuge (sorry) social desirability bias in the polling when we look at actual results yesterday vs predictions. If anyone actually cares to try and make get accurate polls, then they have to be able to account for this, or ideally, get rid of whatever is stopping people openly stating their opinion so people would actually state their true preference.

                                    Re: demographics raised by Omegaphallic, exit polling does seems to suggest that Trump has done relatively better among all non-White ethnic groups than Romney did in 2012. So it seems like the "Trump Effect" of his racist "gaffes" and foolish statements depleting minority Republicans and independents seems of slightly less effect than the "Obama Effect" was of shoring up his support and turnout among non-White ethnic groups. Possibly this is because Trump only targeted "illegals" and Muslims and that just didn't effect his support base among Republican supporting minority ethnic groups too much.

                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                        In general, I feel that most people in Europe and America have (at least for the last half century or so) been fine with reasonable immigration and immigrants who are willing to assimilate and adapt into the larger national identity. But there are issues in Europe and America with immigrants who refuse to assimilate into the national character, going beyond rejecting the national cultural traditions and beliefs, but even extending to the point of refusing to learn the local language. And there's no doubt that these immigrants are placing major stress on the social fabric of various nations in different ways.
                                        I think that's part of an issue, but most migrants do try to integrate quite heavily.

                                        I think it's to me to a great degree that, however much effort people make towards integration quite a lot of cultural change and new culture is inevitable. Also, to some degree the integrating new groups, however much they try to really sincerely get along with the majority, are still going to always think of themselves as special and different (and usually better). They'll be very sensitive to underrepresentation and overrepresentation in different areas of life and to their perception by the majority. There's also often cultural resistance among Western born second generation folk, who didn't choose to take up a life where they'd be in a minority, to reconnect with the parent culture and assert its greatness and equality with the dominant culture.

                                        I wouldn't expect anything more or less from any group of people really. People aren't going to give up their culture and they are going to have pride in their background.

                                        This all introduces stresses into daily life and the public life that weren't there before and creates the backlash. Concern over representation and perception of different subgroups in society seems to have taken over politics and media to an omnipresent, 24 hour degree that people in many Western countries seem to be beginning to find quite an oppressive experience. Large swathes of people eventually just simply will not see why they should bother with having a welcoming attitude to new people when there will inevitably be constant frictions that degrade their experience of life, and create potential flashpoints for of political destablisation and political violence, no matter how well people try to integrate and now matter how welcoming the majority is. The standard mainstream media line that whatever happens, it is a duty to welcome new people who want to make a better life for themselves eventually wears thin, especially on top of economic decline.

                                        I don't think it's something that we can or should can blame on scapegoated recalcitrant people who don't try to integrate; just something that happens with large migration waves even when people have the best will in the world to integrate (which many migrants do). It's not really about effort, it's just an inevitable thing.
