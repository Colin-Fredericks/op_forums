    The existence of the Multiverse and the meaning of choice.

    02-01-2015, 04:23 PM
    There is a pressing question that I have had for some time. Imagine for one moment that there are an infinite number of realities out there, a multiverse, each one created by the different choices we have made and the ones we haven't. My question is, does the existence of other realities formed by our alternative choices make the very act of choice meaningless? I mean, what is the point of making any choice if out there in the infinity there is a universe in which we made the opposite choice? What meaning has any of our choices with infinite universe? I'm curious to know your opinions.

                          These ideas come from the many-worlds interpretation of quantum physics, I imagine. Aren't the kind of "events" they talk about there meant to mean ideal quantum events, that is, the collapse or non-collapse (depending on which universe you are in) of a single wave function? So it's not actually like someone making a post on a forum or not, it's about stuff like a certain atom of radioactive material decaying or not at a certain point. Maybe I'm wrong (tell me if I am), but in that light, I feel like it's not a comparison I have to worry about.

                          Even if we were talking about the bigger kinds of events, I still don't have much issue with the scenario laid out in the first post. We don't seem to be able to communicate past universe borders, so if someone made a post on a forum in another universe, that doesn't in any way affect mine. If I feel like a post on a forum is appropriate in this universe, then I will post one, and it will in no way be in vain just because someone might have done it in another universe.

                          In fact, many people do the same things over and over again in this universe alone, and that doesn't make them irrelevant. Don't worry about originality, it's overrated.

                              It is kind of the same thing as the question "What if time isn't linear...", "If god already knows what you are going to do...", or any other question about choice. If you believe in free will then the answer is your choice isn't meaningless. If there is free will it doesn't matter if your choice has happened, is known, or someone in another universe is going to make a different choice. It was your choice to make and you will, did, or could make it. Ultimately your question boils down to, like with the others, is there free will or not?

                              As to spefifically your question I would have to wonder if every choice we make has to have a different universe for it. The question I would have to ask is why? Even going for only the really important choices in a persons life, I would have to ask why a different universe? That seems excessively "Humans are the center of the Universe". Maybe it is that way. I would think that at least it would be on a more global scale. Though even that seems like a small difference. Personally I would think it would be on a universal scale. Cosmic events that would shape difference universes. There might still be billions or more of you out there.

                              And if that is the case then your choice matters even more because you are making it here in this Universe. Even if in another Universe someyou makes a similar choice when he wakes up tomorrow to walk his dogapus while the sun rises in the west, in that way, it was a different choice.

                              Side thought: I'm sure like some others I've always wondered if maybe our creativity isn't creative, but mayhaps a glimpse into these other Universes?
