            I don't want to detract from Br's question, because it's a good one, but three things:

            1) I like a certain chaos sometimes. This wasn't chaos. This was picking through the scrapyard to find two things that fit together in such away that made 3rd/3.5 seem sane. If 3rd Edition was arcane in it's minutiae and ADnD 2 arcane in it's presentation, then earlier editions were a confusing mesh of arcane academia speak and bits of lopsided lucidity arranged awkwardly by the wizards apprentice after. It was a poor grimoire, the early editions.
            2) Look, I know I've read them. Maybe you should read them before assuming the chaos helps.

            3) Revenge of the TV Head put forth an idea that kind of sums up all of my experience with DnD in general, from the awkward few games with my oldest brother to the fumblings of 3rd and the tediousness of 4th-I had fun with DnD in spite of the game, not because of the game.

            With this in mind, you gotta understand that the best games of DnD I had were with 4th Edition. Not because the game, by itself, was good, but because it was the closest DnD ever got to shutting up and getting out of the way with its' mechanics. Limited, narrow mechanics it might have been, but it also didn't take forever to just understand how to resolve stuff-it was about as close to roll and move on as DnD got with a majority of its' rules.

            4th Edition wasn't a bad game(if we're using DnD in general as a standard, move away from that, and the entire game regardless of edition clearly shows signs of entropy), it was a different game, with some radically new design direction that, while used in RPG's before(seriously, there are games that use a lot of the conceits from 4th Edition that actually work and are likable), hadn't been quite displayed that way before and didn't have to contend with a DnD legacy. WHile the translation isn't clean, it's rather like how Mummy and Promethean are rather different games for World of Darkness-within the context, neither of those games are really bad(in fact I love them both and in fact that in terms of idea, Promethean might be perfect, although the mechanics don't quite match up.), but they don't do as well because the ideas at work are, well, very different from the rest.

            4th Edition has the same problem, it just showcases more because of how much it stood out both mechanically and 'fluffily' compared to its' elder brothers. Does it have problems? yes, I am the first to say that. But I think it has less problems than people think it does, because so much of it was the radical change of idea from all the earlier editions. And to be fair, so much of what was done was done in response to a large number of problems DnD has always had and which, now that we have 4th Edition looking all problematic on the side, are almost actively ignored by the fans of the game now. There were shitstorms aplenty for both 3rd and 3.5 editions, and yet now so many people treat those games like the Holy Grail of DnD because it's not 4th.

            So, to recap point three: Yes, I think 4th Edition was a flawed idea and execution. No, I don't think it's the worst DnD has ever put out, just very, very different, and as such will work better with less traditional groups of DnD. No, I don't think 4th is worse that Chainmail, DnD, or ADnD. Maybe, it might be worse than ADnD 2, and Yes, despite all of that, I had not one, but two really awesome games with 4th Edition, where as I can only claim mediocrity with all the others.

            And Yes-ish, I don't think DnD on the whole is actually a good game anymore, regardless of edition. It's certainly not the worst, it may not even really be bad, but it's definitely not good.

            Simply put, my experience with the game does not line up with what everyone else is saying.

                                The sum point for me is that I think that 4th isn't nessecarily better or worse than 3/.5, it was just a different experience....backed up by a weird sort of love/hate relationship with DnD on the whole. When I get right down to it, I like DnD from ADnD 2 to 4th(3rd to 4th better than ADnD 2, but I still like the outlier well enough), but when I honestly appraise DnD, I find a game that I can't bring myself to call good. It's not bad-well, actually, it might be, but it's a tolerable and semi-endearing bad if it is. But I know I can't call it good, not in my opinion. 4th Edition tried to fix a lot of things and take the game in a new direction for a new era, and in some ways that I like, it worked....but it still tripped over itself in some big ways. At the end of the day, I've had good experiences with both systems, and yet objectively looking at both I still kind of go "How the hell did I do that?".

                                It's a game I think everyone in RPG's should play, with 3rd(Well, fuck am I kidding, 3.5, it's harder to get the range of support for 3rd) or 4th Edition being chosen more for differing play style based on the group(people who don't mind the tediousness of one for the greater flexibility, and one where the system might be limiting but also moves along faster so you can get back to other things faster...or back to the same thing if it's cool wit y'all)...but I don't think I would ever actively seek to play a game of DnD myself again.

                                Which seems bizarre, because I'm still planning on buying DnD Next/5th Edition/THe Next Generation/FuckADoodleDoo.
