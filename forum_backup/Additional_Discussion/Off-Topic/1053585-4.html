                                                            Not everyone from other countries has a strong grasp on US history and American slang. When he first got over here, he didn't know the history behind the n-word and didn't realize it referred to black people specifically. He thought it was a synonym for criminal and that it equally applied to everybody regardless of race, because his only context with ever having heard the word was in rap music or a few action movies, where people casually used the word and nobody cared.

                                                            There were lots of slang words he had trouble dealing with. Like the time he tells me, "I almost punched this black kid today. He came up and called me a dog! But then it seems everyone calls each other dogs. Why do people do that? Back in my country, if you call someone a dog, you would get your ass kicked so hard."

                                                            "Well yeah, it's okay to call someone a dog. Nobody gets mad."

                                                            "So I can just call a person a dog? I can just say, 'Hey, dog,' and he would not care?"

                                                            "Well no, if you call someone a dog you should change the stress of the word to dawg and do it in a friendly way. "

                                                            "So just call someone a dog in a friendly way?" He started laughing so hard I thought he was going to fall over.


                                                            Or with the word 'nigga.' He was using that word that and I had to tell him it was short for the n-word and he couldn't say it.

                                                            "But everyone says it at school! The black people say it to white people. The white people say to black people. The Mexicans say it to everyone! How can it be offensive?"

                                                            I explained the history and how it derived from the n-word and that it wasn't something that anyone should say, even if it seemed like everyone said it.

                                                            So then he went to school the next day and asked some of the black kids there and they said that maybe a few people would find it offensive, but whatever, he (and everyone else) could say it at school and nobody would care. Basically they told him that while the full n-word was offensive, this one was not, that black people had "taken this word back" so that's why it was okay if people, even white people used it. While the other version of the word was offensive, this was just a friendly greeting.

                                                            But then they followed it up by telling him not everyone saw it the same way and if he said it to the wrong person in the wrong place, he might get his ass kicked, so he should only say it at school.

                                                            "So it's okay to say this word in one place, and everyone says it, but if I were to say it somewhere else I could get my ass kicked? I'm really glad I figured that it out before something like that happened." He just shook his head.

                                                            So yeah, lots of confusion over what's fine to say, what's not fine to say, where it's okay to say and who it's okay to say to, and how something can be offensive if you stress the word one way or not the other way. Plus he soon realized that not everyone was even on the same page when it came to these things, which often made it even more confusing.
