      Old school MEGS system. It was used to run the DC Hero rpg made by Mayfair but the rule system was bought for a new game called Blood of Heroes. It is the absolute best to simulate the comic books in my opinion.
      Marvel's FASERIP system was poorly done in that way.
      In the MEGS system, an option was to use your martial arts ability to sub in the stat for taking a hit or rolling with a hit or hitting someone or even to damage them. This means that someone like Batman could knock out a supervillain that was just slightly more than human.

      There is also the Champions system which does a fine job of detailing out characters. I like it since I enjoy point based systems. Most point based character creation games gives you more freedom in making your character.

          Depends on my goal. If it's a light/fast/quick game I use ICONS. It's a simple system that has the advantage that you (the GM) don't roll dice, only the players do. uses your standard d6's and is fun.

          If I'm running something a bit longer I'll use Mutants and Masterminds (preferably 3e) The powers are quirky to configure but they have a number of pre-made packs that you can just slot in. it's a d20 based game.

              I really liked Mutants & Masterminds, though it's been a while and I haven't played the most recent edition. I just enjoyed that it had a lot of options to be able to customize your character and their powers. Or those of NPCs and antagonists.

              I know one of the complaints people have had about it is the disparity in power levels, where you can have the equivalent of Superman and Nightwing on the same team. And of course, it's easy to make a really powerful character, for instance Super Speed and its related powers can give you an overwhelmingly powerful character. But I always thought of those aspects of the game as a positive thing, with the game just being accurate to the source material. But those things could be tempered by a GM watching over character creation.

              A complaint I do share is that the combat system can get pretty complicated, and if you're not willing to estimate distances and areas you might make it even more complicated.



              I understand Champions is also popular, but I've never played it.

                  I had a lot of fun with Marvel/Cortex Heroic. It's very much in the mode of episodic action, and not that great if you're looking for mechanics to rule social or investigative arcs a lot. But it does that episodic action very well; the versatility of the power traits, stunts and complications adds up to feel very much like you're playing through a classic four-color fight splash, and it does a lot of the balance work for you, to the point where there's not really any need for point-buy design because it's difficult to end up too far off-balance just by eyeballing a character sheet. It works in that comic book mode where the answer to "who would win if the Punisher fought Superman?" is very much "whose book is the fight happening in?" rather than Superman's advantages just stacking up so high that the Punisher has no fighting chance, without feeling like Superman is just an arbitrary coat of paint on a generic figure.

                  It's not good for every genre of superhero story, but it does the core iconic action that tends to show up in cartoon adaptations quite well. Marvel Heroic is out of print, but you could find a used copy on Amazon or get the Cortex Plus Hacker's Guide, which has the full ruleset, palette-swapped for dungeon fantasy. (It also has the ruleset from Smallville, which adds more focus on emotional drama and ensemble casts.)

                      The spiritual descendant of DC Heroes' MEGS system is Mutants & Masterminds; and the spiritual descendant of Marvel's "FASERIP" system is Icons. Ironically, both were written by the same guy (Steve Kenson); but they're drastically different from each other in terms of design philosophy.

                      Also, Cortex Plus has two superhero-based flavors: Marvel Heroic, and Smallville.

                      These four (M&M, Icons, Marvel Heroic, and Smallville) are my top four picks, with each having its own approach to how to do superheroes, complete with its own strengths and weaknesses. When I have more time (assuming someone else doesn't beat me to it), I'll try to provide summaries of each.

                      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                          (Just jumping in....)
                                                          MEGS (particularly when the later BoH additional rules are added) is pretty much the best Supers RPG I've ever seen -- the stats carry weight, the dice carry weight, and the Hero Points carry weight, with each intertwined with the others yet having their own specific, unique value and influence relative to the game. Also, the rules can be tailor-adjusted for setting (from slapstick to ultra-"real", and everything in-between) via an adjustment chart located in both 3rd Ed. and BoH (the system-adjustments are *easy-peasy*, and the chart/rules-tweaks are literally a quarter-page or less in size). . It can be as challenging or as "heroically-simple" as you want it to be, and I *ADORE* the specific distinctions they make between Mental and Social/Mystical attributes, and what, exactly, those attributes mean/entail (and how the Social/Mystical attributes can have significant meaning/use even to non-mystical types). It is by far my #1 recommended supers-game.

                                                          Unfortunately, as indicated further up, it isn't supported any more: original material for its DC Heroes incarnation hasn't been produced since around '93, while for BoH it's been a decade. Still, if you can download a copy of DC 3rd or -- better yet -- BoH, it's totally worth it. Of course, the stats for many of the DC supers will be a couple realities old -- Wonder Woman, for example, never got stat improvements past her George Perez era (late 80's,which is when they might have been relatively accurate, but which carried through to '93 and later, leaving many aspects "off", to say the least), which stat'd her as being much less strong than Superman, Capt. Marvel, and kryptonians in general, even though for 2 decades now (and for much of the Golden and Silver Ages) she's been clearly written as being in that league, strength-wise -- and the stat-adjustments from the 2-games-later DC D20-ish (note the "-ish") system, while accurate in some ways, doesn't always reflect what was happening in the comics (Batman's intelligence of 8 is at least 3 points too low, which is very significant in that and the older system; it should be an 11 or 12, which it was across several incarnations of DCH/and the 2000 game, comparatively, which is more accurate to how he is normally portrayed). BoH and DCH are... obtainable... online, via various means. Let your conscience and funds be your guide.... (What I *can* link/indicate, however, is Writeups.org -- they've been doing stat-conversions/support for the MEGs systems since the 90's, not only for DC characters, but for converted Marvel and non-supers, as well; they usually don't re-post any copywritten material -- and several companies have had no problem with the guy(s) who run it, as they keep things very above-board -- but they've updated and/or made stats for later/alternate versions of literally thousands of characters... you should check them out, if interested. All free, of course, and they accept submissions for your own character conversions that you make, as long as you follow the format they've laid out....)

                                                          On the flip, Marvel Super Heroes (which I've played quite a lot of over the 3 decades due to two of my more prominent GMs -- including one in particular -- loving the system) is perhaps not that great, and also hasn't been at all supported since the early 90's. *However*... you can download for free from a particular website devoted to the old system -- I won't try to link it, due to forum/company policies, but as the site has been run for many, many years and has not been pursued by any company to cease and desist (unlike with many other sites, even those that carry out-of-print/defunct-company materials/info) it seems safe to recommend them, if only tangentially/vaguely; I have a massive array of all the old material from them, and they also have some out-of-Marvel supers/characters converted to the old MSH/FASERIP system. It may not be the best, but it has its devoted followers, and unlike with MEGs/some others, it's easily obtainable. (If you take a couple minutes to hunt around on the 'net, you should be able to track down the site....).

                                                          Champions is good if you like your rules extra-crunchy. Strengths: the GM/players can simulate a wide range of potential supers at a comparatively easy "cost", game-mechanics-wise, compared to, say, even DCH/BoH (wherein if a sorcerer/mage wanted to take the Sorcery power, he'd have to dump *all* of his initial characters points into it... and he'd still be several *hundred* points short to get a "0" rating in it, unless the GM allowed for a much larger starting pool of points, which is scaled/available in the system); the system *requires* weakness/limitations/flaws as an intrinsic part of the character build -- this keeps a nice balance of character "roundness" -- that many pwrgmrz like to ignore -- in the picture. Weaknesses: the crunch. It's not for the faint of heart, nor for anyone scared of math (especially scaling-applied-fractions/multiples). While the system is highly flexible in many regards, the crunch has scared off many who were not as "deep-in-the-geek" as some others. While I personally have no problem with quarry-level aspects, and appreciate the relative flexibility, even I can get mentally drained after making just a handful of characters, if using the system rules rather than just free-pointing it (following concept vs. points-counting). Like the others systems, though, it has its devoted followers, and is worth mentioning simply because it *is* a good supers game... just crunchy as all get-out....


                                                          Those would be my recommendations, for various reasons/rationales (preference, relative availability, general views of others, etc.).
