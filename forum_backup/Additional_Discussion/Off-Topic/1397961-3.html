                                                We don't need to just flat out halt beef production (even if we do it slowly so people don't notice as much) or make it economically unsustainable (too many people like their meat to ever get everyone to go vegetarian). One of the biggest problems with this specific example is that industrialized meat is really cheap, while more environmentally sustainable meat is fairly expensive.

                                                It seems highly unlikely that a greater market for both more sustainable cattle ranching via better government based economic policy and/or flooding the market with vegetarian alternatives (we already have plenty) is really going to change that. A big reason is that the US esp. has a strong cultural issue based on the idea that having at least one beef based meal a day is normal (even if not exactly common based on area and so on); which means lower income individuals that are sucked into that norm are going to buy cheap beef even if they know lots about the processing side of things. Putting in the time and energy to push the norm towards beef being something you eat once or twice a week at most is probably a much better goal. If beef is perceived more as a luxury item than a staple part of peoples' diets, more expensive sustainable beef is an easier sell.

                                                So I don't feel any educational pressure on food production is really going to matter (most educational efforts don't really sink in if they're not perceived to be connected to daily life), where educational pressure towards more sustainable eating habits would be more effective.

                                                Of course, society is way more complex than that and we need to address things like poverty and food deserts if we're going to pressures fast food companies away from high carbon footprint options, and move beef into a "special occasions only" meal option

                                                            I had a relative say that the power outages were caused because wind turbines can't function in the cold and they all shut down. I pointed out that the wind turbines didn't shut down and there are lots of places that get a lot colder than Texas, like North Dakota, Alaska and Antarctica, and their wind turbines work just fine all year round. But I just got an eyeroll in response.

                                                            If anyone is interested I have a friend who lives in East Texas and works in the energy business and he was telling me about the situation because this is sort of what he does. When Texas split off to create its own energy grid one of the reasons was the claim that the regulations required by the Federal government were too onerous and expensive (other issues were that the federal government would be able to take power from Texas and reroute it elsewhere, and there were other issues besides). One of those Federal regulations makes it mandatory for energy pipes (carrying natural gas or the cooling pipes in nuclear reactors and others) to be properly insulated against the cold. Texas however, in its infinite wisdom, felt that that would be a waste of money because it would never get cold enough here to freeze the pipes. So they didn't require the energy companies in ERCOT to insulate those pipes and let them skip out on other regulations. Texas then went on to brag about how their energy grid was cheaper per mile than the energy grid in the US and how Federal regulations are onerous and unnecessary and expensive and it's better to let companies take a free hand to decide what is or is not necessary...

                                                            Well, here we are now, with the pipes frozen over and people's lives in danger because of that.

                                                            And this isn't even the worst part. A lot of those pipes burst and are permanently damaged, so even when the cold snap ends it will still take weeks to get those pipes replaced. Moreover even the pipes that don't need to be repaired will need to be replaced with properly insulated pipes because even people who adamantly refuse to believe in global climate change are willing to admit that this wasn't a once-in-a-millennium type of situation. And replacing all of the pipes across Texas' energy grid is going to be a massive and very expensive undertaking.

                                                            It's like the old saying, "Penny wise and Pound foolish." In an attempt to save a little bit of money they skipped on basic protections like insulating pipes against cold and now they're going to have spend massive amounts of money repairing and replacing all of those pipes.
