                                                        The 3e system is a complete overhaul, where certain gamebreaking mechanical fallacies were pulled out of the system, such as Perfect turtling, 2/7 filter, and most of all lethality. For those that don't know what those are, spamming prefect defenses, a paranoia combo of two perfect defenses and your combat load which if used became who ran out of motes first (and is incredibly boring if it happens) and lethality was either you hit and do no damage or you hit something and your character is splattered across the lap while you are sitting getting your murder hobo on in Nexus.

                                                        The new systems for combat give a much more interesting tactical feel to combat and put up a good bit of ablative armor to having your character splattered before you know whats going on, charms stopped doing things, and started enhancing your characters. Example is 2e monkey leap produces a jump, 3e monkey leap allows when you leap to make great distances...because alot of 2e was how your charms solved things not how your character did it, its a subtle but important paradigm shift. The influence system is no longer arguing about going to dinner and essentially mind F'ing people until you get your way, to 3es influence system which relies on playing on peoples desires, beliefs and even a little old fashion intimidation which makes it interesting and being able to work in a organic way to influence was put in.

                                                        Why did it take so long...between my full time job/ full time school, and i can chew through books like a boss, took me until a few days ago to read through the entire thing, there's tons of charms to turn trees into deep and useful options for any character. The dodge tree in 2e was about 5-7 deep, 3e is about 20-25+ish, craft went from about 7 to 56 (not counting repurchases). Essence starts at 1, sorcery is attainable at one (essence rating is no longer a tax to make your character as it was in previous eds), at e1 you can get yourself about a good 8-10 charms out of any ability, and with supernal ability (treats a single caste as e5) you can delve into what makes your characters ultimate specialty is and slid past the cap to get some nice tricks early on. Theres beautiful antagonists, vast and interesting locales (on top of favorites), a very organic and interesting change to the sorcery system, martial arts and craft....i honestly could gush for days.

                                                        As for people freaking out, yeah it might seem ill timed but having worked for many different companies ive learned getting ahead of a problem far outweighs trying to fix something after someone has sat d own and said how its going to be. Morke, Holden, Vance, Grushcow, Minton, have delivered me the game I've wanted Exalted to be for years...i'll be damned if i trust anyone else not to fuck it up for me. >.>

                                                            Originally posted by Siven View Post

                                                            Looking at Storypath, I would have very much preferred that as a base.
                                                            We don't really know how things would've gone had they chosen Storypath as their base system, but Exalted is unlike any other RPG on the market. It not only needs to function in ways any "normal" RPG does, it also has the most precise mechanical combat resolution of any RPG ever. And that's not a "nice to have", it's a necessity. Exalted completely breaks down if the combat mechanics aren't a glorious symphony of perfectly balanced interlocking subsystems and effects. The way I see it, vast majority of criticism that was aimed at the previous two editions simply boil down to mechanics not functioning in such a way in various circumstances.

                                                            It took a lot of work from some dedicated and smart people who have had A LOT of experience with the system already in place to make it the well oiled machine we see in Ex3. Resetting to a new base system would, I fear, necessitate at least a couple more years (probably more like a decade) of failed mechanics to get to the point where some smart people have an idea how to actually make it work.
