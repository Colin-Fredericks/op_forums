            The superhero genre can basically keep putting out movies weekly forever and not suffer. It just can't handle blockbusters.

            Like, the DC animated direct to home/video/streaming stuff? There's a truly massive library of it, makes consistent use of the DC's huge roster of characters and alternative Earths, and its worst entries are still generally better than 95% of the live action stuff. There's kid friendly stuff, there's more mature stuff, they're some great adaptations of the classic/keystone story arcs that don't ruin them in the process (even if there's a notoriously bad addition into a certain Batman story... it doesn't change the actual key story). DC fans tend to love the animated stuff, and consume it in droves, and thus it keeps getting made. It's been going non-stop for 30 years with a constant stream of content. It's so beloved by fans that the comics change to be more like it (the more obvious example being Harley Quinn being an original character to the 90s animated Batman TV show, and now she's one of their most popular characters and she's in everything).

            The appetite is there, the C-suite just needs to listen to the people that know how to feed it.

                    Originally posted by Master Aquatosic View Post

                    I think we ideally can love the movies' quality and protest the working conditions under which they were made without any paradox. There is no universal law that says these movies can't be just as good without breaking animators in the process. You just have to give them more time to work in, a more predictable schedule and encourage rest.

                    I think the big thing in the way of that is the Hollywood release calendar and the short-term profits and growth motives undergirding it. Also, our veneration of "uncompromising mad genius" artists.

                    if nothing is done about this though...i dunno what my response should be.
                    THe problem, particularly in the immediate context of Spiderverse being in theaters only (for anyone not willing to participate in piracy), is that glowing about how good the movie is creates an incentive for people to go see it, unethical management of the artists who made it be damned-and if you haven't noticed, there's a lot of chatter about how damn good Spiderverse. People don't want to miss out on seeing a movie that is this well hyped or be left out of the ongoing conversation about that excellent movie, and time and tide has shown that people will absolutely go a see movie like this regardless of the new information of the team's overwork.

                    Because of this, it's going to be hard to convince the people at the top that they should even try to address the issue, because it's making money and bringing acclaim regardless, which can read as it not mattering. If the audience was actually that upset about the way it was made, surely they'd stop talking about it, surely they wouldn't keep buying tickets. After all, hasn't it been deomsntrated so many times before that if people object to something, they'll draw a picket line around it? No, all this success says that surely it's not actually that big of a deal, and surely the audience would much rather have Beyond The Spiderverse made in at least similar time then have it take more time or get more manpower to be made more ethically.

                    it sucks, but like it or not, creating discussion around Spiderverse of any sort that isn't "The way this movie was made is some god damned bullshit and needs to be fixed" is basically giving a greenlight to do it again to the people who the power to actually give a shit about the production. Every person you get excited to watch it, enough to buy a ticket, is another push on the scale towards doing it again-and with how good it is, it's pretty hard to not excite people who haven't seen it.

                    So, no, I don't think, at this moment, you can love the movie's quality if you also want to protest the working conditions. You can't divorce the quality from the production, because the production is what made the current quality, and more people than not are only going to care about that quality than it's production.

                    Of course, the real tragedy is that it doesn't really matter at this point-Spiderverse has done so well that that message has basically already been sent, and there's no way it can actually be stopped now. Unless something is actually, drastically, done, the producers have no reason to really change course, and the audience is no longer at the point where they can take that drastic action.

                    Nevertheless, all of the above still applies-you can't talk about the quality without risking adding to the piles of reasons to do the same for Beyond.
