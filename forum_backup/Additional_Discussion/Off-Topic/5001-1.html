        If I wanted to discuss teenagers being awful to each other, I'd pretty much just file it all under "bullying." Men tend to bully by calling people names, forcing them to do embarrassing things and, of course, beating the shit out of them. Women tend to be a bit subtler, using emotional manipulation, social sabotage and ostracism to get their point across. A good rumor can do as much damage, or more so, than a good beating, and nothing hurts quite as bad as the feeling of being all alone.

        Thing is, you can see most of that when they're still kids. Adolescence introduces the fun of sexuality into the mix, not to mention drugs, alcohol and the emergence of psychological disorders (many of which only begin to appear around this time). Teenagers love to experiment, and love to pressure others into experimenting with them, which is why you tend to see a lot more drugs and alcohol at parties than anywhere else.

        With sex comes issues of homosexuality and gender-queerness, sexual humiliation (calling people sluts, spreading photos of them semi-clothed or nude, etc.), sexual intimidation (using social pressures to convince someone to have sex with you) and flat-out rape (of both the forcable and date-rape varieties). Thing is, a lot of teenagers don't even realize when they do these things that it's wrong- they haven't really evolved a proper moral compass yet, and so their ability to distinguish good and evil is mostly a matter of what feels good and what doesn't. Usually, they only realize they've done something wrong when the principal calls them into his office, or their parents yell at them, or- in extreme cases- when the police come knocking.

        Teenagers are in a weird place- they're adult enough to be given some adult responsibilities, but too childlike to be given the rights of an adult (when, of course, they'd like it to be the other way around). They struggle to deal with their hormones and emotions, try to develop their identities, and generally just transition from childhood to adulthood, and in the meantime, society starts placing tons of pressures on them. This cauldron of pressure and emotion naturally leads to problems, and what happens to them as adults tends to be a result of how they handle this transitional period. This doesn't excuse the shitty things they do, but it does put a lens on them- they're literally adult children, and ought to be considered as such.

                    Teenage girls are crazy, I can say that much. Middle school was the worst though - by highschool, usually the worst I got was that -look- when I tried to join a conversation. Whenever I tried to say something, everyone would just snap their heads around and give me this death glare in dead silence. It took awhile to learn how to talk in a group again, after a couple years of that.

                    But in late middleschool, it really ran the gamut. I've had everything from the nicknames ("Walrus, ort-ort-ort" and "Dracula" namely, because after my braces were taken off my canines looked kinda fangy until the day they were ground down) to the passive-aggression ("Really? You got me THIS for Christmas? Ugh.") to the good old "grab her by the hair as you pass in the hall and slam her face into a locker door" because why the hell not. (This last one was later followed up by the girl making a super-overwrought, sobbing apology in front of everyone to make herself look like such a wonderful person)

                    The worst though was when I went to a friends' house with a group of girls (other friends) for a sleepover. The mood was rather silly and one girl got all OMG LET'S PLAY HIDE AND SEEK. Aren't we old for that?/okay, whatever. So we all go off and hide...

                    Half an hour later, I'm wondering what the hell, so I wander back to the living room to find they've all been sitting there watching a movie without me.

                    School was a fun time. I'm a grown-ass adult now, but I still flinch around teenagers because I half expect it to start all over again.

                        Hmm, what’s the general ‘tone’ you’re going for? I can recommend a bunch of high-school centric entertainment, but it’s mostly realistic/less dark than I think you want? Although maybe I’m misreading into the “suicide and gun violence” stuff.

                        Either way, the thing I’ve always liked about good high school stories is the combination of that age range being primarily dominated by personalities that treat everything as the most important/devastating/enjoyful/whatever experience that has ever occurred anywhere, the inherent confusion hormones/lack of meaningful experience wreck, and a social environment that elevates/devastates participants seemingly on a whim.

                        Letting ‘big moments’ (suicides, gun violence, grievous instances of bullying, etc.) be the major drama movers* is easier to grok/plan out, but also squashes a lot of the fun nuance. This isn’t to say that those things are bad or shouldn’t be represented, but they’re usually events so large that the range of acceptable emotional reactions is almost predetermined. Whereas things like intra-clique friendship (especially if you choose something besides stereotype to divide cliques into) or romance (where declarations of love emerge within weeks, everyone’s unsure of how to navigate sex, and your romantic activities raise or lower your reputation based on criteria picked to be as contradictory/minefieldy as possible) are dynamic and can be influenced greatly by the players, rather than just having them react.

                        *Note: Not saying this is what you’re doing, this is just my thoughts from watching a lot of this stuff.

                        Plus, all of that is just the drama of regular high school, i.e. one that presumes your teenagers DON’T have super powers and have to deal with supernatural phenomenon. Which, now that I’m thinking of it, means you should probably go and watch/re-watch the first three seasons of Buffy the Vampire Slayer so that you can just raid the episodes for ideas on how to represent high school anxiety through the prism of monsters and powers.

But I was a really BIG nerd and had an intensity in my eyes that years later I would learn most people interpreted as "potential serial killer." I was asocial so this rarely became an issue for me, except for my RPG playing friends and the people from my church youth group. That last part would cause a lot of tension, however.

                                    How so? There was a girl I was very into. Importantly, I never pursued her; she was miles out of my league and I knew it. But I wanted to be closer to her, and she claimed she wanted the same from me. Yet she never interacted with me. At all. I would ignore this until it got to be too much for me, and then I'd ask to talk to her, she'd agree and show up with a female friend and I'd basically ask why she didn't want to talk to me.

                                    Her solution was, shall we say, less than intelligent. I doubt she asked or instructed her lover to do anything about this, but she clearly complained about me to him, and his solution was literally felonious. He threatened to kill me. On eight occasions. He was smart enough to do so without witnesses, but to say that he wasn't thinking everything through when he did this would be an understatement. He probably thought, "I'm a karate black belt; I could take him." End of thinking.

                                    I didn't report him as a favor to the girl. But there is no chance in hell I'd ever forgive him for doing what he did, and knowing his intellect, he probably is proud of what he did back then. We only had two conversations where he did not threaten to kill me. In one, he merely threatened to shatter my hands. In the other, he explained he was a karate black belt, broke a board and explained that when he says he would shatter my hands, he literally means he would shatter my hands.

                                                I had a twin going into high school, and had a lot of fun.
                                                Only now do I look back and realize that all my friends were only friends with me because my brother was awesome. At least initially.
                                                By junior year (17), they were my friends in earnest. We played Magic the Gathering, talked about anime, got reasonably good grades to stellar grades. Most of us had glasses, but as the years progressed the glasses got replaced by contacts. Everyone's braces were off by the end of middle school, so the girls in my nerd-ish group were open to kissing but not much else. If we were called nerds, or dweeps, or whatever, it honestly didn't affect me much. Mostly because, to put it in Exalted terms, 'they were just extras to me'. Didn't know their names. Didn't care.

                                                What I got razed on the most was being on the co-ed swim-team. It was the nerdiest sports team in the whole school, and either the guys' bodies were perfect and pampered to the point of shaving their legs and being called 'gay', or the usual hairy teenager format (that'd be me). No other sports team at school considered us a valid 'sport'. But I got to get some exercise afterschool, earned a few medals, made some friends, and got to look at women (and yes, one or two men) in school swimsuits.                                                         By the 14-18 year range, the worst of it had passed for me. I had a good class, and I went to a school where "popular" and "doing well" were not exclusive properties; in fact, they often went hand in hand. Add to this that the science program (not really the right word for it, but a focus on math, physics and other natural sciences) I went to had a way of attracting slightly nerdy folks. We could sit and program our TI calculators during break. I was actually pretty good at it for a while there.

                                                        The worst for me was in middle school, around 9-12. I was kind of slow, overweight, timid and just not used to talking to people, making me too easy to push around to not do it. I never got into any physical fights, but many were the breaks when I sat in a corner of the schoolyard just imagining things. Some people did see my weakness and pounced on it, but I honestly don't remember much in particular of what happened. Certainly not the kind of physical stuff you can see in American high school drama. More like snide remarks and making jokes at my expense. It wasn't fun, but I lived.

                                                        Funnily enough, one of my biggest bullies became my best friend in high school. I later went to his wedding. Funny how things turn out.
