    The Otherkin Thread

    09-16-2015, 07:10 AM
    I realized this forum was neglecting a possibly large group of our fellow gamers.

    Otherkin, whether they identify as vampire, lycanthrope, fae, whatever...it deserves attention and a platform for discussion. It means a LOT to the people who live this.

    I'll start with my own really skimpy testimonial (I'll totally answer questions if you're curious. Just don't want to ramble).

    I'm kind of a mutt, as far as Otherkin identity goes. That fits me, though. I've been able to confidently identify as vampyre even before I was able to deal with my bi/pansexuality. I've felt a lycanthropic attraction to the moon for as long as I can remember, and I often feel myself being more beast, animal, monster, than human. I often look at my own hands, expecting to see claws.
    This isn't delusion. I know I, a human, don't have claws. I just also FEEL that I ought to. Like they belong on my digits, and that I'm incomplete without them. In my heart, I have wings.

    I'll clarify for those who question the vampyre part.
    I feel out of steam a LOT. I've always been able to supplement that by getting some emotional response from someone, or just being close. I realized this mostly wasn't emotional need on my part early on. I just...needed someone. Something. Even an animal to interact with. And I attracted predatory animals like they thought I was going to steal their lunch. I've fed off the tension of staring down a lynx just outside Nogales Arizona in the desert. I could feel it. Eventually it walked away. But I felt it. I'd taken its fight away. Eaten it. It was in me. I was alive.

    As for sanguine feeding, I've done plenty. Feel free to ask if curious. It was nice.

    That seems an adequate introduction.

The closest I've come is (and this is going to sound like I'm pathologising - not my intent, it's just the words I'm used to!), the brain has a kind of 'map' for how the body looks. It's a pretty basic part of our kinesthetic sense. For like 99% of people, this has a pretty close approximation to their actual body, and it all works fairly well.

                  For a few people, however, the map is... different, dramatically so. In particularly unfortunate cases, this leads to thinks like body dysmorphic disorder, or even some eating disorders. The theory goes, for that particular sub-"type" of transgender/sexual who want to transition because their body is 'wrong' or whatever, this map is for the 'other' sexed body; breasts, penis, broad shape, whatever. Hence why people among this subtype who do transition tend to feel so much better after, because they're not constantly hitting that confusion where their brain-map and their body are different.

                  It's not out of possibility that Otherkin can be thought of as kind of similar, although I suspect to a much lesser degree? Their brain is just wired a little differently, leading to things like Az feeling like they have 'claws'.

                  So, they haven't 'lost touch with reality' any more than a person who feels wrong unless they're a particular level of fit, or a homosexual person is attracted to people of the same sex.

                                              This is why we can't have nice things. *points to a baseball and a broken window, glass bedazzling the floor like a denim jacket fresh from 1986*

                                              Explanations and shit, eh? Good thing I'm so fucking eloquent. I got this one y'all.
                                              Lemme preface this by saying I know two things extremely well, at an almost doctoral level: Jack and Shit but what I've experienced or read places. Don't be too impressed. Got that almost-degree in Tijuana for a buck.

                                              Alright, so let's cut the crap. There seems to be some confusion between beliefs, feelings and the concept of body image here. Let's take that knot apart first. As I've said, I only speak from personal experience and a random assortment of Google searches over the years.

                                              Body image, and especially one's internal/imaginary image of their outer self. I'm not talking about how you'd like to have a certain feature because it's sexually attractive, popularly connected with a certain aesthetic or what have you. I'm talking about that image, which often grows a bit right along with you, of how you *should* look. Not because you want it, but because it's just how you look without significant injury or a clown mask or something. Only instead it's like having a phantom limb without the interesting story about how you lost it. And sometimes that phantom limb that twinges every now and again is your entire being.
                                              Desire for change comes after in my story. It began with me noticing that I felt incomplete. I'll start with the claws. I grew up with animals. Mostly cats. Nothing much to add beyond "hung out with cats sometimes", but it's possibly relevant background information, so whatever. I would look at them clawing at furniture and feel like that was a natural thing for me to do as well. I was literate by age three, so I was already understanding certain basic concepts: the stove is hot, don't wander off unattended (didn't really respect that one..) and, it almost went without saying, you're not a fucking cat, Joey.
                                              That did NOT stop me from nuzzling things that were soft and pushing over heavy furniture because I couldn't seem to get my non-existent claws into the fabric.
                                              In elementary school, I was often picked on for being the chubby quiet one who read a lot, so I got into fights. Little fighting background: my father is insane. Not clinically, he just decided to raise a cage fighter without consulting literally anything beyond the movie Bloodsport. His favorite bonding activity was exercise that could make you shit your pants and then punching an Everlast heavy-bag until my mom eventually found where we were and dinner happened, immediately followed by a martial arts movie and a little exercise.
                                              This is a derail of a derail now, but the fact that I'm still fat after that is fucking aggravating. Just saying. My point here is, I knew how to throw a punch growing up. Despite that, all those fights were mostly me clawing, snarling and biting. That makes sense to me because, when I let myself just relax and picture myself being, I can swear that my teeth are sharper, that my arms are a little longer and that there are dark, almost sooty claws about an inch or two long at the ends of my digits. Toes too. This goes doubly for the wings that I feel I have, at least inside. They're a soft amalgam of avian and chiropteran characteristics, articulated a lot like an oddly distended hand, but lightly feathered in black, like a farm crow.

                                              Now that I've said that, I suppose I should address mental illness. I'll start by saying I am diagnosed with some. I refuse to lie about that to bolster this explanation. I have bipolar disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder and generalized anxiety disorder. I'm also in the process of being examined for suspected high-functioning autism. In order; a chemical imbalance impacting my mood and decision making, a trauma based disorder that causes major depressive episodes along with easily triggered scares and survival type behavior that's accompanied by being nervous and kinda scared in general. And I just might have trouble with interpersonal skills for a reason beyond being a dork. None of that seems to fit the body image stuff, unless there's an "I totally have wings" mood I'm unaware of that isn't caused by Red Bull.

                                              So...

                                              Let's move to feelings and beliefs! I'm torn about religion. It often irks, mostly chafes, and I sometimes just go with it anyway. I often don't. When I am feeling religious, I get rather eclectic. A little Shinto-esque ritual animism and ancestor worship, a little Catholicism, a touch of random ideas that are helping me emotionally and spiritually. It varies sometimes. Again, most of this doesn't really inform how I SEE myself, BUT, it might give background to the vampiric part of me, the predator. Granted, so could the beat em ups, but we covered that. Anyway, I do believe in "life energy" and the spirit or soul. I do see my soul as my inner self, so I expect what I see in me to be what the spirit world sees. What God sees, even.
                                              I also Feel as though that soul, the self in myself, is hungry for crap I can't buy at the supermarket. It's partly why I'm chunky, too. I'm literally never not hungry. I can always eat. I've been told I was "eating my feelings" before, but I've checked and I'm pretty sure even MOMENTARY satisfaction occurs sometimes when people do that. Not me. I like certain food, but when I'm hungry I'm like a human tapeworm. I can just go until my body starts rejecting, and it's never made me feel satisfactorily full. I've been checked for neurological disorders that might cause bouts of hunger like that. Nothing so far.
                                              I've also eaten a tiny handful of live bugs. Many times, which is why I wasn't bug-specific. Worms, moths, certain spiders. I've eaten many a crawling thing. And every time I've felt so much better. Like I just had a relaxing meal with a light massage after. I've tried it with dead bugs, too. Nothing but a slightly over FDA approval-level mouthful of insect fragments. I found out later that I could get that same refueling feeling from certain interactions with living things, mostly predatory animals and people in certain moods, but also certain plants even.
                                              It wasn't until I was a teen that I discovered Real Vampire culture, and sanguine feeding (for now, I'll just say my friends were willing to invest time in my ideas regardless of how weird they sounded) which was just before I bought my first copy of VtM.

                                              ​ I kinda discovered tabletop by accident. I wanted to know more about Real vamps. Were they like me? I got on message boards, chat rooms. Anything to understand what the hell was so different about me. I felt it and not understanding sucked.
                                              It was on an old site called Pathway to Darkness and its forum that finally gave me some answers. About vampirism AND my more bestial Otherkin traits.
                                              I found people that had simple explanations for why I was so goddamn hungry all the time, and so tired even though I hated sleeping at night and even why I got upset sometimes when I didn't see the moon. (Also, thanks for telling me about VtM, Anna aka MorgueAnna, wherever you are. Totally didn't eat my social life. ) I can't regurgitate everything I've learned in one post, but I hope this is a decent start. I'll go on. Need clarification or a deeper explanation, I'll try to provide one. I'm still learning about myself, too, as I suppose all people are to one degree or another, so this definitely isn't the last word.

                                              Just asking, hoping against hope. Maybe if we try to behave, we can actually attempt understanding.
