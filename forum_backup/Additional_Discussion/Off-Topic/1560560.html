    Have some fun Roleplayed moments you want to share?

    06-11-2026, 09:05 PM
    Good evening all!

    I usually come online with questions, but this time I felt like I needed to share a fun collection of scenes from a chapter we had in our previous games. I rarely get to play as a player in VTR, so I guess I’m just excited to share my story lol.

    Before I can share the scenes, I need to share a few details first.

    Homebrew system: We play with VTR 2nd edition, but we use some homebrew rules. The biggest one being the hunger system I adapted from V5. This means that we can have Messy Criticals and Bestial Failures. This also means that Disciplines feel a lot more dangerous to use, which we actually enjoy. Indeed, VTR Disciplines are very strong, but if you have to risk hunger to use them, each time feels like a bargain with the Beast in exchange for incredible power.

    Most clan curses have been slightly adjusted as well, but nothing worth mentioning for my story.

    Background: My character, Yuka, is a Neonate Nosferatu with a few decades of experience. She came over from Tokyo to Vancouver to find her sire after she felt him suffer Final Death across the ocean thanks to Blood Sympathy. While she has a complicated relationship with her sire, after he was forced to abandon her due to the failed Carthian revolt he took part in, she still traversed the ocean by boat to investigate his death in Vancouver.

    Nosferatu Curse: For most Nosferatu, Yuka actually got off pretty easily as far as the curse goes. She isn’t bad-looking. Actually, she’s pretty cute. However, probably because of her abandonment issues, her curse expresses itself by amplifying her actions to make it look like she’s peering too much, stalking even. When she wants to meet, it always feels too close to home. When she’s in a room, it always feels like she positions herself to block the only exit. When you type on your phone, it looks like she’s trying to read what you text, and you can swear she asked you who you are talking to. She blames it on her lack of English mastery, on cultural differences, but man, she always says the craziest stuff with perfect linguistic accuracy and then apologizes with the thickest accent.

    The Setup: Yuka is new in town. Luckily, being a Nosferatu has its own advantages; first, the Nosferatu have actually managed to claim a territory for themselves, and they take care of their own. However, Kindred being Kindred, nothing is truly free, even among clanmates, so Yuka decided against hunting on Nosferatu territory for fear of owing too much to the clan. While she’s not a fledgling anymore, she’s the new girl in town, with a dead sire, and she has absolutely no influence. She needed to find a hunting ground and a herd, otherwise she would fall to frenzy pretty quickly.

    Alright, lets begin! Disclaimer, it’s fairly lengthy.

    First Scene – The Manic Pixie Dream Girl Friend.
    While Yuka can be scary, and she can defend herself against humans, she did not want to start hunting violently. It attracts too much attention, and she’s not an inherently violent person, as long as the Beast doesn’t push her to.

    Having a bit of a nerdy side and being able to navigate social situations despite being a Nosferatu, she decided that she would prey on the local geek scene. Her mindset was: geeks had a lot of young guys starved for attention, and if she could find the right ones, her curse might not even register as a bad thing for them.

    Her first attempt at finding a guy actually went pretty well. She spotted a guy, Gabriel, in a manga café reading some really shady stuff and decided to one-up him by picking something trashier, which definitely grabbed his attention. The first interaction went pretty well. Gabriel had the perfect psychological profile for her. She asked him if he could show her some cool places in town, since she had just transferred in. He agreed, amazed that he had made a good impression on a woman for once, and brought her for a walk in the park, where she fed on him.

    When he woke up, Yuka apologized for being too forward. She explained that he must have fainted, which she found pretty cute. Gabriel fully bought it. He was enraptured by this new MPDG that had just shown up in town. Sure, she was weird. Sure, she was clingy, maybe even a bit creepy, but she gave him what he needed: attention. Finally, he could stop feeling like a loser. Beggars can’t be choosers, right?

    OOC: Thanks to Gabriel, I purchased her first dot in Herd, yay, no more immediate starvation!

    Eventually, she sought him out again. This time, she asked if he had any friends he could introduce her to. Since she was new in town, she wanted to meet like-minded people. Gabriel reluctantly agreed. He was worried that his friends would be a poor fit, but at the same time Yuka showed interest in playing D&D, so why not?


    Second Scene – Flirting with Disaster.
    Yuka is finally invited to this new D&D thing. She heard about it back in Tokyo, but never actually played. While it wasn’t necessarily something she was truly looking forward to, she might even have fun for a night. She could roleplay not being a vampire for once.

    Once she arrived at the DM’s flat, she met Gabriel’s friends.

    OOC: The Storyteller asks for a Presence + Socialize roll. Yuka has a pool of 5 (Presence 3 + Socialize 1 + 2 from Striking Looks - 1 for being a Nosferatu at Humanity 5). This isn’t bad; it leaves her around an 80% chance of success. This ain’t bad, but she can’t mess this up. Besides, she’s sitting at 3 Hunger; things could go poorly. If she fails, it’s over; she gets a dramatic failure on her first impression. So I decide to spend a Willpower as the social anxiety of being a Nosferatu interacting with mortals kicks in. Thankfully, she succeeded!

    Tony, the Dungeon Master, and his other friends, Max, Dominic, and Tom. Max and Dominic are fairly awkward. Dominic talks way too much about this World of Warcraft game, while Max asks her way too many personal questions in the first fifteen minutes of conversation. Gabriel gets a bit peeved by Max and how he’s treating his new girlfriend, but Tony calms everything down by bringing the focus back to actually finishing Yuka’s character sheet.

    For a moment, Yuka feels a bit more comfortable. These mortals are so awkward that she doesn’t feel the need to fear her own curse too much.

    The D&D game eventually starts, and they play for about an hour until Tony calls for a coffee break. He asks if anyone wants a coffee from Tim Hortons. Yuka politely declines; she’s already hungry enough, she doesn’t want to risk the Blush of Life, and besides, D&D runs for so long that she might have to leave the table to regurgitate in the middle of the night.

    After half an hour, Tony isn’t back. A run to Tim Hortons at midnight should have taken 15 minutes tops. Tom thinks that he must have met his girlfriend along the way. This alerts Yuka a bit; it could be a problem later. She doesn’t want any jealous girlfriend poking her nose into her stuff. Probably nothing bad, right?

    Eventually, an hour later, Tony comes back. He smells a bit like weed and looks a bit woozy. Yuka clocks that something doesn’t feel right. His heart rate has dropped and there’s a very slight smell of blood on his neck, only perceivable by kindreds.

    Tony has been fed upon!

    OOC: The Storyteller asks for a Wits + Empathy roll. This is something she’s good at. She has a good chance to succeed, with a pool of 4 (Wits 3 + Empathy 3 – 2 for Tony’s Composure). Since it seems like another Kindred is involved, I decide to spend another point of Willpower. Man, socializing with nerds is draining! Still sitting at 3 Hunger, the expected happens: I roll a 1 on one of my Hunger dice and have no successes; Bestial Failure.

    Yuka’s curse finally kicks in. She asks Tony what he was doing with his girlfriend and suddenly looks very interested in him. Tony looks spooked and quickly finds an excuse to call the night off. She realizes that her curse must have made her say something she didn’t mean to say. She tries to play it off as a linguistic mistake, but it doesn’t really fly. The night ends on an awkward note.

    The next night, Gabriel lets her know that Tony spoke to his girlfriend and seemed like he wasn’t super keen on accepting her again.

    OOC: The Storyteller tells me that Yuka will need to deal with Tony to raise Herd from 1 to 2.


    Third Scene – Nosferatus & Dragons
    Devastated by this interaction with the D&D gang, she comes back to Vesper, her Ordo Dracul-aligned Daeva one and only coterie mate. Feeling vulnerable, hungry, and desperate, Yuka, who had normally kept her affairs relatively secret so far, opens up.

    She takes the rejection particularly hard. It shouldn’t be that difficult; she should be the perfect fit for their group. She’s cute, she’s nerdy, and they are starved for feminine attention. Why didn’t it work?! It must be because of Tony’s girlfriend; she’s behind this. Kindred or not, she needs to be accepted, or she’s back to square one. Besides, she underestimated how tough it would be to integrate into a foreign city, and sure, having only Kindred interactions is tough.

    Yuka decides to ask Vesper for help. She’ll even offer a boon, or help her in exchange, whatever she wamt. Vesper agrees to help, and while she has next to zero knowledge of geek culture, she feels like it will actually be an asset.

    Her plan: she needs to be invited and screw up so badly that anything Yuka will do will pale in comparison. If Yuka sides with them, it should get her back into their good graces.

    So the plan is set in motion. Yuka calls Gabriel to apologize and even tells him she found someone at university who would be interested in trying D&D. She knows nothing about it, but she’s someone new she met and she looked like a nice person. Gabriel is a bit worried but agrees to meet her.

    Vesper ends up making a good impression. Gabriel explains the game to her and helps her make her character sheet for the upcoming game the next Saturday. So both Yuka and Vesper get invited to the next game.


    Fourth Scene – Fighting against the pretty privilege.
    It's game night. Vesper shows up dressed as sexily as she can to distract the other players, and Yuka make extra care to appear mundane as to not draw too much attention on herself.

    Vesper, also good at reading people, clocks something about Tony that Yuka didn’t. Contrary to Yuka’s assessment, the Dungeon Master is actually a fairly successful and social guy. He has a geeky side, but he’s not as socially awkward as the rest of his friends, he’s actually pretty charismatic all things considered.

    Over the course of the night, she starts getting flirty with some of the players to distract from the game. She constantly argues with Tony’s rulings, gets fighty with Max, constantly forgets which dice to pick, engage in PVP, etc. Meanwhile, Yuka simply tries her best to belong and look like she’s getting annoyed at Vesper’s antics, while trying to bring back focus to the game.

    OOC: Since Vesper also has Striking Looks 2, as an ex-model, the Storyteller decides that it actually applies as a penalty to any attempt she makes to make an annoyance of herself, which was harsh, but funny and fair. Vesper manages to get 4 successes to help Yuka on her Presence + Socialize roll. Thankfully, she succeeds.

    Victory! Even though Tony is a very competent DM, some player sins are just too much to handle so he ends up calling the night off early. A few minutes later, he says he has to leave; his girlfriend has just arrived.

    Yuka excuses herself quickly, says that she’s late for the bus, and dips out using the Cloak of Night power to disappear from sight. She steps outside the flat with the intention to spy on Tony and his girlfriend. She steps outside. Thankfully, her Hunger doesn’t increase, but it’s strong enough that it could backfire.


    Fifth Scene – Going Too Far.
    As Yuka steps outside, she notices that Tony’s girlfriend has arrived. She just came by on a bike with two other local organized crime members that Vesper actually recognizes as being part of one of the gangs she has influence with.

    Yuka clocks the girlfriend as an instant danger to her whole scheme. She’s of similar age, also of Asian origin, cute, dressed in geek/alt fashion like Yuka does. She’s everything Yuka would be if not for her damned clan curse. This girl is bad news.

    The girlfriend looks happy to see him, but Kindred recognize Kindred, and Yuka knows what she truly is. She’s not here for him. She can feel her Beast claiming dominion over her herd. Most importantly, she’s competition.

    At this point, Yuka is hungry and getting angry; She’s Hangry.

    This girl must go.

    She’s bad news, but unfortunately for her, Yuka was embraced among the clan of Bad News.

    Yuka walks behind Tony’s girlfriend, obfuscated by her power, and for a few moments thinks about how she wants to handle this. Decades of life as a haunt have taught her that fear is her greatest ally. For once, she’ll be the monster others see in her. Yuka uses her clan discipline to invoke The Grand Delusion and whispers to this new girl that she knows she broke the Masquerade and that she must leave Tony right now or there would be grave consequences.

    How Yuka imagined things would go: The girlfriend is scared off. She immediately breaks up with Tony. Tony, having no girlfriend to manipulate him into rejecting her, allows Yuka to permanently secure her place at the table and be accepted. Herd secured, friendship acquired, the curse as been beaten, and now she has a weekly game of D&D to boot. Perfect plan.

    How things actually went: Tony’s girlfriend immediately told him to get on her bike and that they needed to leave immediately. She fled with her two biker thug friends.

    At this point, Yuka realizes that she went too far by instantly going with the nuclear option.

    She starts to panic.

    What if Tony’s girlfriend ends up killing him?!

    Tony didn’t have to die for this. He’s a cool dude. He’s even a great DM, what terrible sin against the TTRPG community. Can she ever make amend?

    Yuka quickly meets up with Vesper, who just had stepped outside, asks her if she knows the girlfriend in question or how they could reach her quickly. Fortunately, Vesper knows how to get in touch with the biker gang and manages to get the Kindred’s phone number.

    Yuka tries to call her.

    She doesn’t answer.

    Vesper texts her.

    She doesn’t reply.

    Both continue to harass her over the phone until she picks up.
    Yuka finally gets her on the phone.

    The girl’s voice is trembling with fear.

    Yuka tells her to stop whatever she’s doing immediately and that they need to meet ASAP.

    She lets her know that the whole thing was a hoax and not to worry too much. The girlfriend invites her to one of her biker gang's hangouts so they can talk. That way, if anything goes south, she’ll have backup.


    Sixth Scene – Haunted by regrets.


    Yuka, Vesper, and the girlfriend finally meet.

    Once they meet her, Yuka asks if Tony is still safe.

    The girlfriend looks rightfully spooked. Instead of answering, she replies with a barrage of anxiety filled questions.

    She asks how she got caught. She has no memory of breaking the Masquerade. She promises that Tony isn’t at fault. She swears she never did anything wrong to piss off the Nosferatu and that she purposefully stayed away from any politics. She asks Yuka to spare Tony’s life. She already booked him a hotel in the next town and would make sure he could leave as fast as possible.

    Yuka finally realizes that this poor lick is, in fact, a fledgling.
    Not only that, with the way she carries herself, she truly cares for Tony. Something about her care for him is genuinely human.
    Yuka had just started a turf war over one dude, with this one fledgling that probably didn’t even know Yuka was Kindred before meeting her.

    Feeling incredible guilt, Yuka explains to her that she went way too far too quickly and should have never let it reach this stage before talking. She feels like Hunger and desperation pushed her too far.

    She explains how she tried to get accepted into their group and how her curse was making things very difficult with Tony.

    The girlfriend seems surprised that anyone would start beef over the four other geeks, as she had absolutely no interest in that bunch of losers. She doesn’t even play this need stuff they call D&D and she has no plans to. She met Tony during her mortal life, and she still care for him. Still fearing for her boyfriend’s life, she even proposes to tell Tony to leave his group, leave town, and everything.

    Yuka stops her and reassures her that nothing of the sort is necessary. She actually wants Tony to stay in his group. He’s seems like a pillar in his group of friend and he’s a pretty cool guy. All she needs to do is tell him that she and Yuka know each other and to let her into the group.

    Tony’s girlfriend agrees if that’s all it takes. She’s finds the whole situation very intense and it only reinforces her belief ; Kindred sucks… in more ways that one.

    Yuka feels like she’s acted exactly like how her elders acted back home; no humanity, only the beast and politics. Feeling ashamed of herself and her actions, she offers the girl a minor boon as a form of apology, nothing too fancy. It might buy peace with those kindred, but most importantly, it will buy her enough peace of mind to go through the rest of her night.

    With the dust having settled and boons being offered, Yuka left back with Vesper.

    OOC: Situation fixed!(right?) Yuka finally got her second dot of Herd, and it only cost her a boon from a very spooked Mekhet fledgling and a future promise of help to her coterie mate (which she was going to help anyway).


    Man, this was way longer to write than I expected, but honestly, it’s been a while since I’ve had this much fun.
    I always loved Requiem Nosferatu, but the curse actually made me feel like my character had crippling social anxiety, so much so that I felt it.

    Funny thing is, She’s actually pretty good at this stuff, but even then, the crippling fear of failing a roll and having it turn into a dramatic failure is so nerve-racking yet exciting!

    I never spent so much Willpower dealing with mortals for something as mundane as fitting into a D&D campaign.

    I guess we geeks can actually be pretty hard to interact with sometimes, lol.

    Do any of you have any fun scenes from CoD, or any other game for that matter, that you’d like to share?
