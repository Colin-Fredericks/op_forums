            And what goes deeper there, is that the manager didn't actually care about "essential religious holiday" vs. "vacation," but simply who got on the schedule first. The next year one of those employees didn't get to go to Rosh Hashanah services because the worker paraphrased above got their request for a vacation in first.

            It's an annoying thing Jews in the US have to constantly juggle when working in Christian majority businesses; esp. ones that give major Christian holidays off for the company. The Christians don't have to worry about dividing up their vacation days to hit major holidays, and don't bother to recognize that their Jewish coworkers don't get that benefit and give them first choice at vacation days to make sure they can express their faith as a personal choice that means less actual vacation time. I've had years where I didn't have a vacation at all, just going to services because that's how it worked out timing wise that year.

            So yeah, things can be bigoted without directly bringing up the topic of that form of bigotry.
