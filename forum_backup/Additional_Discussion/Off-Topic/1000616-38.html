                                                            Why the hell USA insists on calling nazi concetration camps that were placed in Poland "Polish Concentration Camps" and "Polish Death Camps? This is an issue for like forever, our embassy in US alarms that just tihs year they had to intervene 30ty times due to this term.                                                             Unfortunately it's an old school term that was popular back during the 50's and 60's in America (and other Western nations). Specifically the term was pushed by West German intelligence agents as a propaganda trick in order to distance West Germany from the Holocaust. Since Poland was part of the USSR at the time, and since the Nazi death camps were in Poland, it became the perfect scapegoat. If most of the camps had been located in Estonia, they would have been calling them, "Estonian Death Camps."

                                                            Now, it's safe to say that most everybody in the US (besides those who deny the Holocaust ever happened) know that the Nazis were German and the death camps weren't run by Poles. I don't think there's a whole lot of doubt about that. But unfortunately many of America's current leaders grew up hearing the term during their formative years in the Cold War, they're still using it today, and many of them honestly believe that's the proper term since that's what they heard growing up and have been saying their whole lives. That West German propaganda was pretty effective, huh?

                                                            For what it's worth though, you almost never hear the term from Gen X'ers or Millennials. Most history books and programs where they learned about World War 2 stick to the term, "Nazi Death Camp" or "Nazi Concentration Camp" so that's what most younger people use.
