            I've talked about this before in other venues - the idea of "tough love" is something which never fails to set me off.                 It was a boring episode and not that great, but I'm here cause of Cielle, not the episode(PS, yes, I'm still a pony fan).

                The episode garbles it's message, but it's not exactly tough love as you describe it. There are situations where you need to motivate people to do what's good for them-provided you actually understand the entire scenario. A small example is that inevitably every parent is going to have to say no to something in regards to their kids. It's not that harshness wins out-the episode points that out with Sea(Seed?) Breeze. It's that kindness is NOT a weakness, that it DOES NOT make you a pleaser and a push-over. It's ultimately about firmness in action when acting out of understanding and concern. You'll notice that in the episode, what really set Fluttershy into motion was the realization that the Breezies couldn't live in Equestria without being completely dependent on someone like her-and I'm of the opinion that a life of dependency is a not much of a life at all, feel free to disagree. She showed kindness by doing what she could to motivate them through a rough transition rather than just letting them avoid it-and if there's any redeeming of the toy bait, it's that she did so by making the journey with them at the risk of losing her own home, which is a good show of firm love.

                I understand why the episode rubs you the wrong way, but I think more of it comes from the convoluted way they did it rather than the actual message. I dunno, feel free to disagree with me.

                                    Henry Wong from Digimon Tamers. I'll get back to this in a second, but start googling or remembering.

                                    Here's the thing: as I said, DHX knows that Applejack is one of the two most grown up characters in their roster, quite possibly the most adult period. This is a good thing to know, it's critical to handling a character like Applejack. The concerns she deals with aren't necessarily the sort of things other should deal with respectively, and in fact much of it comes back around the issues of control, wisdom, and motivation-Applejack knows what to do, but not necessarily for the right reasons, in the right way, or in the right amount. THe fact that DHX gets this is, again, good.

                                    What is not good is how much of a caricature they make her since Apple Family Reunion. Which, admittedly, isn't that long of a time, but it drives me nuts.

                                    Simply put, Applejack doesn't feel like a person in these episodes, nor an archetype-she feels like a stereotype, and one of such extremity that if you actively can relate to putting up with er her overbearance, god fucking god almighty do you need to get out of that house-I have met people like that, and they are not good for you and most importantly will not change without a major provocation. The simple fact is that while Applejack is overprotective of her sister, it's never been as bad as it was here today, after god knows what else she lets her sister get up to. THe Everfree forest? Sure. Some of the things going on in Ponyville? Sure. But NOT THE FUCKING FARM AND HOUSE. YOU LET APPLEBLOOM RUN AROUND DANGEROUS TOOLS ALL THE TIME THERE BECAUSE SHE FUCKING UNDERSTANDS THEM AND YOU KNOW SHE KNOWS THAT.

                                    It's not like it's out of left field, but it is way too fucking much compared to what we've seen.

                                    So, Henry Wong. Remember him? Good. Because these characters are just about identical in so many ways,it's not even funny. Henry is a pacifist after having hurt some kids in an effort to be the model of a strong older brother, and when he gets Terriermon from a near death battle after that point in his life, we have a scenario much like this one-Henry is Applejack, overly concerned for the safety of what he regards his younger brother, and Terriermon as Applebloom, an individual more capable of himself than the 'older' one believes, but maybe not as much as they think.

                                    There are differences in the shows style, namely the time they took on this issue, but there's a classic example of how this episode could have gone. Namely, without the overbearing one being portrayed as a god-damned idiot, but as an intelligent person who has their reasons to worry.

                                    MLP Can do that. MLP has done that. So why they fail to here just-ergh, it drives me nuts. Applejack's episodes always kind of get the short stick in the end, and alongside some other episodes since Season 3, I really don't think they care anymore to try. Yes, this was an Applejack issue, but that wasn't Applejack having the issue. That was farce in her stead having the issue.

                                    Applejack deserves the same respect as Henry Wong, and god don't I sound pretentious.

                                                My thoughts on "Somepony to Watch Over Me" can be summarized by one sentence: "Curse of the Mother Hen: The Episode." All negative implications therein.

                                                ... okay, okay, I do want to go into more detail. This episode is not my favorite. It bothers me that Applejack - "the loyalest and most dependable of ponies" - goes flipping psychotic over this. As someone else already pointed out, she's the most mature of the main cast, and it feels bloody out of character for her to be acting the way she does in this episode. I feel like it maybe would have been more appropriate for Rarity and Sweetie Belle to have been the focus.

                                                Oh well. At least there was a nice reference to The Princess Bride.

                                                Now, as for "Maud Pie." My initial impression of Maud is that she reminds me a bit of Raven from Teen Titans. Although, I admit, the "sedimental" pun was pretty good. And the "igneous" one.

                                                I do like how they acknowledge that Pinkie was maybe trying a bit too hard on this episode, And how it seems to have a message about how people need to take their time when it comes to bonding with others, and take things at their own pace. Also that not everyone shows their enthusiasm the same way others do.
