                                Originally posted by RomulusGloriosus View Post
                                I'm a pretty big fan of the Sabbat, admittedly, but I will say in few words that the Sabbat have had a lot of people writing for them over the past 25 years, and there are a lot of different visions for who and what the Sabbat actually are. My vision of the Sabbat has always been religious extremists who seek to draw themselves closer to their God by embracing their sheer inhumanity. That means using disciplines a lot, reveling in the hunt, showing no mercy in feeding on humanity, and generally treating humans like food and food alone.

                                They're the worst parts of the Lance and the Crone combined. Likewise, they're also sort of the BEST parts of the Lance and the Crone combined. They're spooky!

                                I think my vision of the Sabbat is generally what most of the writers are going for, but how they interpret that vision varies. Shock humor and cruelty for cruelty's sake might seem Sabbat-ish because the more evil and cruel you are, the more "inhuman" you are, but I couldn't disagree more. The Sabbat aren't supposed to -hate- humanity, they're supposed to -reject- humanity. Playing football with a live human is equivalent to playing football with a guinea pig. Why would you do that, other than to be a really terrible person? That's not really my Sabbat.
                                I've always liked the Sabbat quite a bit as well.

                                When dealing with the Sabbat, I tend to imagine that many Sabbat members are fairly young, and most of them probably don't really have the firm grounding or meta-theological understanding to truly internalize what the Sabbat stands for and what it's trying to do. So often times the younger members do really fucked up stuff that older Sabbat find utterly repulsive and foolish. They're out to have fun reveling in being monsters, because they think being a monster is what the Sabbat is all about. Meanwhile older Sabbat tend to see those young monsters as little more than disposable pawns, which tends to create a sort of "unnatural selection" process by which the more monstrous often end up perishing and those who are more stable end up surviving and moving up into positions of power.

                                As a whole, the Sabbat is generally okay with this process and even encourages it to some degree. The Sabbat embraces freedom in many ways and for the most part is fine with allowing those who wish to be monsters act like monsters. After all, everyone knows what happens to monsters in the stories - eventually the monster ends up getting killed. But until that happens, monsters can have their uses and even afterwards they can help to serve as an object lesson to those with more foresight and a sharper intellect.
