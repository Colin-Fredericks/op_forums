            On another note, I was reading through Xanathar's Guide, looking at the Downtime rules. Specifically as pertains to scribing spell scrolls. Aside from the other requirements - supply cost, material components, and needing Arcana proficiency - this part caught my attention:

            Originally posted by Xanathar's Guide to Everything, p. 133
            Moreover, the character must have the spell prepared, or it must be among the character's known spells, in order to scribe a scroll of that spell.
            When it says "known spells", does that refer to only those spells the character gets from a class feature (referring to the number of spells known on the class charts)? Or does it refer to any spell that the character can cast, regardless of where they got it?

            For example, Tieflings get a free Cantrip (default Thaumaturgy) and two spells (default Hellish Rebuke and Darkness) they can cast once per long rest (except in some variants, where certain spells come back on a short rest). If we had a default (Asmodeus) Tiefling who had proficiency in Arcana, but no other spells aside from the above, could she make spell scrolls of them? What about a character who got spells from a class, like how Barbarians can cast a select few spells? What about feats, like Magic Initiate, Ritual Caster, or Spell Sniper?

            One could argue that Cantrips would be okay regardless, as the passage specifies "prepared", and according to the magic section of the PHB Cantrips are considered as always being prepared. But would spells like Hellish Rebuke and Darkness also apply for the Tiefling? Is it enough to be able to cast the spell AT ALL? If not, why?


            Moving on, I have further thoughts on a related matter. Xanathar's Guide gives rough guidelines for expanding the usefulness of tool proficiencies, by allowing them to combine with skill proficiencies where appropriate. It suggests, at base, allowing a character to have advantage on certain skill checks if they also have a relevant understanding of certain tools - a character being better at History checks if they involve beer in some way, with Brewer's Supplies proficiencies, for instance. But the book also talks about DMs maybe letting the characters receive greater bonuses, or be able to do more things with tools/skills, due to the synergy.

            It got me thinking. If scribing spell scrolls involves, well, scribe work, the should the creation of spell scrolls be enhanced by a character having proficiency in Calligrapher's Tools? Making spell scrolls doesn't really benefit from Advantage, since it doesn't require a skill check, just that the character have Arcana proficiency. But if a character has both Arcana and Calligrapher's Tools proficiency, shouldn't those add to one another, when it comes to fixing magic into a scroll?

            My suggestion is that a character with experience working as a scribe should be able to create a spell scroll faster. Maybe adjusting the times taken to scribe them. Even if it's just by a day (or a few hours, in the case of Cantrips and 1st level spells), it would be a boon to whichever player decided to take (or learn) both proficiencies. Alternatively, one could lower the price of materials to scribe the scroll, since the character is experienced enough to avoid mistakes that would require them to start over (and thereby squander raw materials on failed attempts before they got it right).

            Any thoughts?

            Magestuck: The Ascension

                I think that any spells acquired as a result of classes, races or feats, where you know a specific spell and can cast it a specific number of times per day should be treated as a "known spell" for the purpose of creating magical scrolls or other magical objects.

                Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                My suggestion is that a character with experience working as a scribe should be able to create a spell scroll faster. Maybe adjusting the times taken to scribe them.                 Honestly I would be a bit weary of allowing proficiencies to overly improve the creation of magical items. Shaving off a few hours or days of work is okay - in my experience most groups don't tend to cleave too closely to item creation times. "Yeah, you have enough time to make X," is common. Saying, "Sorry but you need 7 days and 4 hours to make X, but you're only going to have 7 days before the campaign moves on. Tough luck," is much less common. So knocking a bit off the time it takes to craft something is the sort of thing that wouldn't make a huge difference, but would still be pretty cool. I definitely would not start knocking down the gold cost it takes to manufacture items. I have found that allowing PC's to craft magical items on the cheap is a good way to find your campaign imploding.

                    06-24-2018, 08:10 PM
                    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                    When it says "known spells", does that refer to only those spells the character gets from a class feature (referring to the number of spells known on the class charts)? Or does it refer to any spell that the character can cast, regardless of where they got it?
                    I believe the reason it’s worded that way is because different classes get their spells in different ways. Sorcerers, bards, and warlocks all have a certain number of “known spells”, which they can cast from freely using spell slots. Druids and clerics prepare a limited number of spells they can cast each day from their class spell lists. And wizards prepare a set of spells from their spellbook, which itself contains a set of spells from their class spell list. I believe the intent is not to disallow characters with inherent spellcasting from a race or feat from scribing those spells into Scrolls, but instead to clarify that while sorcerers can scribe a scroll of any spell they know, a cleric can’t scribe a scroll of any spell on their list; they have to prepare it.

                    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                    For example, Tieflings get a free Cantrip (default Thaumaturgy) and two spells (default Hellish Rebuke and Darkness) they can cast once per long rest (except in some variants, where certain spells come back on a short rest). If we had a default (Asmodeus) Tiefling who had proficiency in Arcana, but no other spells aside from the above, could she make spell scrolls of them? What about a character who got spells from a class, like how Barbarians can cast a select few spells? What about feats, like Magic Initiate, Ritual Caster, or Spell Sniper?
                    Check out the wording on the Tiefling’s Infernal Legacy ability:

                    You know the Thaumaturgy cantrip. When you reach 3rd level, you can cast the Hellish Rebuke spell as a 2nd-level spell once with this trait and regain the ability to do so when you finish a Long Rest. When you reach 5th level, you can cast the Darkness spell once with this trait and regain the ability to do so when you finish a Long Rest. Charisma is your Spellcasting ability for these Spells.
                    Now check out the wording on the Wood Elf Magic feat from XGTE:

                    You learn one druid cantrip of your choice. You also learn the longstrider and pass without trace spells, each of which you can cast once without expending a spell slot. You regain the ability to cast these two spells in this way when you finish a long rest. Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for all three spells.
                    Based on these distinctions (which are notably consistent with other racial spell casting traits and feats), a 3rd level Tiefling “knows” Thaumaturgy, but doesn’t technically “know” Hellish Rebuke. She can just cast it with the Infernal Legacy trait. Conversely, a wood elf with the wood elf magic feat does in fact “learn” pass without trace, and rather than casting it “with wood elf magic,” they can cast it “without using a spell slot.” It’s also worth noting that the svirfneblin magic feat uses the “can cast with this trait” wording rather than the “learn and can cast without using a spell slot” wording that every other feat that gives you access to spells uses. So it is possible that the intent is for inherent spellcasting from race not to count as “known,” while spells acquired through (most) feats do. That said, I still don’t think the intent is for characters to not be able to scribe spells they got from their race into Scrolls.
