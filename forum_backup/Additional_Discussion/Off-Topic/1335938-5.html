                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                Primarily? To establish that your phrasing of active discrimination was not your sum total of what's wrong with bigotry. Since it's not, there's not much more as that's clear. Mostly from here, it's that if you acknowledge that racism, even just in thought, is bad, then racism is hard to defend in text just because it's become normalized.



                                Fantasy races largely stem from early modern fantasy writers wanting to have them as allegory; since they're not there to represent their origins in myth for no other narrative reason. Tolkien wanted you to identify elves, dwarves, hobbits, and even orcs as humans; even if orcs are corrupted by evil forces they started as elves and all. The whole point was to use language that (at the time) to make it easier to see them as identifiable instead of alien.



                                Race is a modern concept, not just the general nature of humans to look for patterns and name them. And... what do you think people were coming up with new ways to divide humans into different categories were up to?



                                I'd rather see it be specialized to the setting to reflect how they're being used in that setting. Species works for me if we need a more general one for D&D style fantasy, but type isn't bad. Creatures might work but seems a bit mixed on the "impersonal" issue.



                                I mean, that's what D&D encouraged by pulled them out of the context of the writers that came up with them. This goes back to the whole, "is racism bad even if you don't do anything racist" thing. Pulling racist (or other -ist) tropes from fantasy and making a game out of them that doesn't include the original context doesn't mean you've eliminated the association. D&D races might not be actively racist, but they're built on racist tropes that aren't completely gone, and they're still called races. So in the end, it's both.

                                Though really, the whole idea of "sapient" is such hair splitting it's kinda ridiculous. We call ourselves Homo sapiens for a reason. Sapience is part of what we consider special about or species, and making fictional sapient species is imbuing them with a quality of "humanness," just like race is something we only use to talk about other humans and calling them races is asking for them to be looked as as having human qualities. Sapient is just a word for, "we can identify with them because they're smart like we are," in this context.
                                Why yes, something may or may not have racist associations.

                                So?

                                Was the writer intending it to be racist. Were a writer say, make evil goblins or evil orcs, is he saying in his mind 'mwahahahhaha, I made them crude, savage, and stone tech users! That's coding for primitive savages which are primarily dark skinned! Racism quota, complete!'? Or is he just thinking 'Now, I need to make a race that's symbolises brutality and savagery. Usage of stone tools, strong physiques, as well as more unsophisticated language would bring in the message.'

                                Sure, the savage hordes of monsters usually come from the mongols, and other swarthy men who come riding in horses to set cities on fire, but by this point, they've pretty much become their own thing.

                                Tl; dr. It may have started racist, it may have racist associations, but it isn't racist to use the trope and fantasy archetype in and of itself.
