                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                            My gripe with “almost a classless system” is that I don’t see any benefit in it still having classes at that point. I think Pathfinder would be a better game if it just ditched Classes entirely and let you “build your own class”, as it were. It basically already does so, between the zillions of classes, archetypes, feats, multiclassing, and whatever else.                             So here's what I like about the PF class system.

                            I find that class systems are wonderful for helping new players and even experienced players create new characters. It assists with narrowing down the character creation process. "So, you want to play a character who fights with two swords? Do you want to be a straight up warrior? Do you maybe want a little magic and a combat pet? Do you want to be sneaky and assassinate people?" After that, it's a matter of the player choosing between three different classes/chassis - the Fighter, the Ranger or the Slayer.

                            I've found that often in RPGs without classes and with lots of choices and rules, new players (and even experienced players) can often feel fairly overwhelmed and it can really drag out the character creation process as a player feels they need to to read and understand every single rule for every single ability in the game so they can determine whether they want it or not. That can be really intimidating for a lot of people. Meanwhile with classes, a player only needs to know the rules that involve their class. They don't need to know about anything else. They'll say, "Okay, so I want to make a character who can do X," and the GM or other players can point them to the class that does X.

                            At the same time though, having lots of options within each class gives more experienced players lots of freedom in how they can build their character.

                            I think it's also worth considering what the point of classes in Pathfinder are. They're supposed to be more like general directions or guide posts rather than an extremely limiting and confining train track. So more experienced players who want to do X might be able to find a way to do that with a totally different class in a way that might seem a bit too intimidating to first time players to the game or to roleplaying as a hobby.

                            As a result, I find that Pathfinder works pretty nicely both for brand new groups, and more experienced groups, and groups of mixed players. Meanwhile with more restrictive games like 5th ed D&D (which again I enjoy), I find it's great for introducing new players to the hobby but the more experienced players in my group much prefer Pathfinder because it allows for a lot more player creativity when it comes to making characters.

                                                        Again, there are benefits to having classless systems. I've played a lot. But I think it's really worth considering the benefits of a class system especially for ease-of-access of new players. Pathfinder is very much one of the primary introductions to roleplaying that a lot of people have these days - I know several groups where nobody had roleplayed before, and then someone grabbed the book and started running games for people. Having a reasonable number of fairly defined classes classes gives everyone a good starting place, since most people are familiar with the concept of classes from various MMOs and other video games. At the same time, if these classes have a lot of options built into them, they give more advanced players something to enjoy.

                                                        D&D 5E went with a very basic, very newbie friendly game, at the expense (I think) of lacking very much for more experienced players who enjoy a fair amount of system depth. I know at least my group (which had several new players) played D&D5E for a little bit, but everyone got fairly bored with it quickly. Pathfinder is also very easy to get into, with a simple, class-chassis that's easy for new players to understand, but then has a lot more options and alternative classes for more experienced players to use.

                                                        Also, I'm not a huge fan of the, "wait until 3rd level to really start playing your character" that 5th edition did, since it makes the first several levels incredibly boring for more experienced players. My group ended up just saying, "Screw it, we're starting at third level," even when dealing with new players, because it just wasn't any fun. At the end of the day, I think having a fair number of classes with a lot of choices for players to make in class is better than having just a few classes with an insane amount of choices, or a limited number of classes with few choices.
