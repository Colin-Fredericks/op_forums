                                                        I have been trying to write about this for a while now, but every time, it comes out crap. Whatever gift I ever had for telling stories, it seems to be gone, or at least run down to rust and ruin. Ugh. Fuck it, this is my last shot at it, and I'm hitting that Post Reply button no matter how bad this comes out.

                                                        I have come to a point in my personal development where I have accepted that my identity is part of the problem which needs solving. I cannot keep telling myself how bad I am, because when the statement is phrased that way--"I am bad"--badness becomes my identity, and everything flowing from that is poisoned. For one thing, it minimizes and denies any part of me that is not bad. For another, it mistakes a behavior or a moment in time for a sum total of a person. For a third and most dangerous thing, it writes me off as someone inherently bad who cannot be saved and who is therefore exempt from having to try hard because it would be impossible for a bad person not to be bad.

                                                        I am therefore watching my self-talk, and doing my best to avoid saying things like "I am bad" in favor of more accurate statements like "I have done a bad thing" or "I feel bad at this time." I don't know if it's helping, but I'm working on it even though it's hard. It's exhausting. Being on your guard constantly against your own mind means paying attention to every single thought you have and meticulously examining it through a perspective that is not your own and does not come easily to you so you have to construct it from scratch almost every time. I fail at it a lot, and I expect to fail at it a lot more before I get it right. I am doing my best to forgive myself for those failures, though, because they are a necessary part of the learning process and because I need to convince myself that failing is not the same thing as being a failure unless I choose for it to be.

                                                        It is time for me to step up the game, though, and I mention that at this point because the talk of failure is my next big hurdle, and maybe one of the most fundamental hurdles to my personality and self-image. I live in mortal terror of failure, because I cannot forgive myself for them. I beat myself up endlessly for every failure small and large; for instance, I still want to respond to the year-old failure of having reached across a dinner table for a pot of mayo rather than asking the person I reached over by inflicting physical pain upon myself. Bad table manners equals a desire to grab an X-Acto knife and start a-cutting, which is pretty fucked up when you think about it (it is also pretty fucked up that I need to think about it to notice that's pretty fucked up, but never mind, we're getting really meta now).

                                                        Whatever. I bring that up because my response to this fear of failure is to not set goals for myself. I cannot fail if I have no defined criteria for success. This cowardly approach has immunized me from the worst of my self-hatred, and it has also done serious damage to my physical health (why not have another box of pizza? It's not like I have a goal of losing weight) as well as my mental health, and now I am struggling with learning how to have objective goals. This is hard for me not just because of my fear of failure but because I am just not an objective thinker, instead couching all my thoughts in comparison and subjective impressions. I can't measure height or weight because I don't know the numbers and can only judge things by comparison (taller than me; heavier than my sister). Same with distance, temperature, or pretty much anything a person can measure by attaching a number to it. I need to learn these things because if I want to learn the value of success, then I have to set objective and measurable goals for myself so I can actually tell if I achieve them without having to rely on a panel of judges in my head whose criteria is unknown and at least one of whom is in the pocket of a special interest that wants me to commit suicide.

                                                        I am, in short, having to not only rewire the way I think, but also learn simple models most people learned as children, and that second thing I worry is beyond me. I actually like the fuzzy edges of my vision; they lend my voice an emotional component that I think really defines me in a mostly good way. I am afraid that objectivity will diminish me.

                                                        I hope I can find a balance.
