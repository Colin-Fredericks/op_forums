                Originally posted by Shadowflame View Post
                It sounds like the "Edge of Tomorrow" is going to be an awesome movie. Which confounds my expectation that it'll be Groundhog Day with power armor and aliens.

                I saw it.

                I also expected it to be Groundhog Day with power armor and aliens, and that is exactly what it was. It was awesome!

                Time looping in movies was around long before Groundhog Day, but that movie was so great it came to represent all the films that used it. It is a great film, and it deserves the pedestal it sits on. So when the trailers showed us the basic premise of Edge of Tomorrow (EoT) I, like many others, compared the two. However, the end result does not suffer from being similar to Groundhog Day. Note: I say similar because we do get an explanation for the time looping in EoT that helps to drive the plot, while in Groundhog Day Phil never really understands why he is trapped in the same day until the great conclusion of the film.

                On its own, EoT has a great story, excellent characters, interesting special effects (nothing original, deriving a lot of the effects from other films, but all well used), and all in all, it is a great piece of work. It will never get away from being called 'Groundhog Day in Power Armor with Aliens' but in this case that is not a bad thing as both are excellent films.

                Rotten Tomato is giving EoT a 89% critic rating and a 92% fan rating, and imho, that is pretty accurate. EoT is a great film and I look forward to watching it again (which I will probably be doing this evening).

                                                Sadly, this has been an on-going trend in sci-fi for decades now - getting more and more away from the foundations, which was exploration of the human condition and how we would react to non-human situations (planets, life-forms, etc). Original and Next Gen Star Trek did this well. Stargate (more the show than the movie, but even the movie had it's moments) did this well for quite some time. X-Files and Quantum Leap barely had any FX at all (no ships, few massive make-up effects, mostly done with trick photography and dim-lighting).

                                                Early on (print-only and radio shows early), sci-fi was only differentiated from drama (or, sometimes, comedy) by the unlikely/impossible/far into the future situations the characters found themselves in. People stuck on a distant planet facing their fondest desires as an alien trap were still people reacting like people. It was a new way to explore how people really are.


                                                Now, as long as they have some nice looking CGI shit, it's sci-fi. For a lot of people watching these movies, you could skip the people altogether and just show shit blowing up for 2 hours.


                                                EDIT - Seriously, if they can make a "Sharknado 2", then it's clearly not about the story or the performances.
