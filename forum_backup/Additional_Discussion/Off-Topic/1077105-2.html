                        Originally posted by Piff View Post

                        Yeah, that encapsulates why I'm not understanding your point here - did you read the twitter thread? The complaints aren't only coming from one developer and the authors underneath them that are supposed to be wrangled. They are coming from multiple game lines. So this implication just doesn't make sense, or maybe it's just going over my head completely. If so and I've misunderstood, please explain!
                        Ugh, Twitter.

                        So, couple of things about that thread that I think are pertinent (and if I'm wrong about any of this, perhaps RichT will be good enough to correct me).

                        Exalted 3rd started when WW and the associated games were still owned by CCP. Things (contractually) were different then, and continued to change all the way through. I do not know what the particulars of Holden's contract with CCP/WW/OPP were, so I can't speak to that.

                        Reading over that thread, I'm seeing a lot of claims from David that, at the very least, need further context. Jeez, where to start.

                        It was the case as recently as Promethean 2nd Edition (which, while it was just published, was actually contracted in 2014) that OPP provided a comp copy to authors (hell, back in the day, we got three, but that was when White Wolf was doing actual print runs rather than POD, which changes the metric somewhat). Nowadays, our contracts give us a free digital copy and the option to buy a POD at cost. I usually buy the books I work on because I like having a physical copy, and I've never had any trouble getting codes to do so. When Holden and David talk about having trouble chasing down their physical copies, I have to wonder where the problem is happening (like, I'm not being sarcastic - my experience has been that it's really easy, so I'm not sure where the disconnect is). With that said, I'm firmly of the opinion that OPP should provide freelancers with at least one physical comp. I've said as much to Rich.

                        David mentions something about contracts being changed after the fact, being paid less than what he was contracted for, and contracts moving from a flat rate (prescribed word count x word rate) to some other system based on...words actually published? I have no idea what he's talking about. Every contract I've ever taken on for White Wolf, when they were independent, owned by CCP, and owned by OPP, has been paid. There have occasionally been delays in payment, but almost universally, those delays were because a developer didn't file a pay schedule on time or properly (note that I did say "almost"; there have been delays on the administrative end, too. They have not, however, been anything like a consistent problem).

                        Oh, and? When people are working for me as a dev, they know (or should know) that I'll go to bat for them if they don't get paid, if they don't get comps, or if something's not right...but only if they tell me.

                        For reference, I started freelancing for OPP in 1997, and I've lost count of the number of contracts I've fulfilled for them. So, again, if there's a problem, it's not universal, so you have to ask where the problem is and why.

                        What else (again, man, fuck Twitter; it's hard to parse). Ok, Wood talks a little about word rates. It is true that up until a few years go (around the time of the CCP merger I think, but I couldn't put a date on it so don't ask me to), White Wolf paid 6 cents a word as their highest regular rate (though you'd occasionally find higher ones for individual projects under weird circumstances; I got 10 cents a word once because I turned in 4000 words overnight, last minute). I was making 6 cents a word for a while. Then, WW lowered that ceiling to 5 cents, which affected me and maybe a half-dozen other people (at most).

                        And I'll be honest, that stung. It felt like my contributions weren't valued. I'd literally never missed a deadline, I'd picked up I don't know how many contracts after other authors dropped out, and I'd consistently been able to bring my books as a dev in on time and (though this is subjective, so take it for what it's worth), well. I strongly considered saying "yeah, screw you guys", chasing other work, and denouncing OPP.

                        I didn't, for a number of reasons. I liked the work I was doing. I knew the people and the IP involved, and getting into something else would have been a learning curve. I was (if my timing's right) in grad school or just out, coping with being a new dad and a new SLP, and putting in the time to learn new games and new IP was too steep for me. Plus, I was there in 2003 when the decision to end the CWoD happened, and I knew what shaky ground everything was on. I decided to give it a go.

                        Anyway, sorry to make that all personal, but all of this to say: It's not unheard of for OPP to change procedures or numbers. They have never, to my knowledge, changed a contract once it's been signed. Usually, it's a "going forward, here's the deal" thing. So, again, if what David's saying is true, none of the recent books I've signed contracts for reflect it.

                        OK, this has gotten too freaking long already and I'm not gonna pick through that whole thread. Tell you what: You want to know about something, link the tweet, hit me on FB, email me, PM me, or just ask me some specific. Unless it's confidentiality that's not mine to breach, I'll tell you, if I know.

                        Sunshine is the best disinfectant, and I don't have anything to lose, here.
