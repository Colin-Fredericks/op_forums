    StoryTeller: How do you consider Attributes under one dot?

    05-31-2014, 10:41 AM
    I'm putting this under Off-Topic as it is a general question not limited to a particular game or version.

    How do you consider Attributes under one dot, if at all?

    Now, I have never got around to actually play a game with the ST system. So how things actually plays out in reality, instead of a mental exercise and all that... But...

    As most (all?) of the ST games put 2 as human average and 5 as the peak of human performance, I personally regard one as far from hopeless. As I see it, Strength of one is your average office worker. Intelligence of one is quite an airhead/bubble-head. But they are far from "hopeless". Getting below one is just a matter of getting into some form of "impaired" area, which would make the character hard to play in most games; but still able to attempt a skill using that attribute. A person with shakes and involuntary twitches would be in the below one range for Dexterity. But someone paralyzed from the neck down wouldn't even have a negative number.

    And as I feel to it id needed to be clarified after my involvement in the other thread...
    Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
    Just to clarify. I'm not looking for what the rulebook says. I want to hear your own opinion on the topic. To broaden the topic a bit, I'm also curious on what an attribute value of one represent to you. If you like how the rules work, please explain with your own words rather than point to the rules.

    If you state one way is right, and everything else is wrong, without adding the qualifier "for me", I might start to argue even if your way of right happens to correspond with my own preference.

    But I am not here to argue, I'm here to to share thoughts and read about others thoughts. Others thoughts, especially when they don't match my own, give me a possibility to put my own thoughts in perspective and analyze them. It also gives me a heads up on what areas I need clarify, or have clarified, when I play with a new group so people don't have different expectations. Discussing back and forth gives me an option to put my thoughts into words. Then, once in a while, someone mentions something that never have occurred to me before, that rare gem that forces me to reevaluate my whole thought on a topic.

    That is the purpose of this thread.

      I've never played a game of Exalted or World of Darkness where the possibility of somebody's Attribute dropping to zero was even a consideration. There are circumstances that can inflict this, but in my experience, they're not at all common.

      When the systems address the matter, the most common judgment seems to be that having no dots in an Attribute is crippling to the degree of invalidating any meaningful actions attached to the Attribute, and often with the Physical Attributes, it's outright fatal. But these rulings are usually attached to specific supernatural powers capable of inflicting the condition, rather than categorical statements on the consequences of having no dots in an Attribute.

                  It depends on the attribute, but in general it means that the character is incapable of using that attribute in any meaningful way and is, thus, crippled. I treat Str and Dex of 0 as the character being physically unable to move. They may be able to speak, or move their eyes, if I'm feeling generous. Sta 0 usually indicates a comatose state at best, or outright death at worst. Cha or Man of 0 means that the character is unable to communicate in any meaningful way. Something is wrong inside their head rendering them unable to articulate speach or sign language. This could manifest as some kind of "locked in syndrome," or in less severe cases as a powerful aphasia. Appearance 0 is a special case- the character still has all their faculties, they are just so hiddeously ugly that it is questionable whether they can pass for human anymore. Perception 0 represents complete loss of the senses. The character is still conscious, but cannot experience their surroundings in any way. Kind of a variation on locked in syndrome. Int or Wits 0 represents a character that cannot think- essentially, a braindead vegetable. Perhaps their autonomic functions keep them alive, or perhaps they have to be on life support.

                                      Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
                                      Over here in Sweden, we had a conscript military up to just recently, and they kept the statistics. The physical capacity of the conscripts have been in decline since the fifties, to really taking a dive the last couple of decades. So unless the two is a value relative to the current culture, the average person won't qualify for the average value of two.
                                      I think this is the primary point of divergence I'd have with your perspective on how to use the dot scales. Context rules all; if you're playing a game, say, with a special United Nations task force in a war-torn area, and you've got your average Swede in the mix, and you want to go by these statistics, then sure, that might be a good reason to give that character a one dot score, the below-average/deficient rating in an attribute.

                                      But if you're playing in a small town in Sweden, or wherever it might be, sticking to this representation is a concession to trying to simulate the world relatively strictly, and that's not one of the various Storytellering Systems' strengths. (Well, an Exalted mortals game might benefit from that some; again, context is key.) The games start you with a set dot spread not really because everybody in the world conforms to that level of aptitude, but because the developers felt that created about enough room to create interesting characters with differing fields of specialty at about a level appropriate to engage with the game at the base level. The kind of thinking that would give most Swedes, say, a Strength of 1 would homogenize your characters without really saying much interesting about the game at hand, unless your Vampire game is about declining health statistics.

                                      Or in other words, two isn't a value relative to the current culture, it's a value relative to the context of the game, whether that's a sleepy New England town or jet-setting across Europe. If your mummy frankensteins aren't visiting Moscow then it doesn't really matter to accurately model whether they would be able to arm-wrestle your average Muscovite. Five dots is a simple spread that's versatile without requiring much thinking. Apply the scale to your game and not your game to the scale.

                                      (There are kludges, like some games which assign weight levels to what a particular Strength rating can lift. Consider this a concession to needing some measure just to keep play going; it'd take a lot of work to overhaul White Wolf games to match this level of detail in all respects, and less work just to accept that things like weight lifting values are flattened out and handwaved a little for the sake of the game.)

                                              Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
                                              Over here in Sweden, we had a conscript military up to just recently, and they kept the statistics. The physical capacity of the conscripts have been in decline since the fifties, to really taking a dive the last couple of decades. So unless the two is a value relative to the current culture, the average person won't qualify for the average value of two.
                                              Keep in mind that the scale of 1-5 necessarily means that each dot represents a relatively broad spectrum of competence. It is entirely possible that the least average (current) and the highest one (during the 50s) both fall within 2 dots of strength.

                                              I mean, it is known that IQ tests consistently have to be adjusted to average at 100 because people consistently keep getting better at it. Does it mean that common people have higher mental stats now? I posit that to not be the case because the dots operate on a broad enough scale to cover a considerable range of competencies.

                                              One might also consider that 2 dots is called average not because it is statistically so, but because that word made an adequate label to put between "poor" and "good". It covers everything from somewhat under average to somewhat above average.

                                              As an aside, If you consider that the label "average" means a rather broad average spectrum instead of a narrow statistical average, it might explain those military statistics even if their data doesn't completely fall within 2 dots: More people likely had physical primary in 50s than now, so there are a few 3s or 4s in in more quantity than 1s throwing off the statistics as compared to in-game dots XD

                                              The guy who can carry 30kg box with a decent posture and the guy who does it curving his back to help support the box with his body both have Strength 2 because the system is not granular enough to cover such small differences. Likewise, at least the 10 best placed or so (possibly more) Olympic athletes in the 100m dash likely have the exact same dots in their sheets regarding their ability to run. Again, the system is not granular enough to account for the milliseconds of difference.

                                              So, going back to the actual subject of the thread, given these broad values assigned for each dot, I don't think there is anything to be represented by negative dots. Zero dots cover all the things from dead to very incapacitated in various manners to needing a lot of care from someone to juuuust not capable enough for the exciting life of a WoD character. Then, for various states of poor competence but still capable, one dot (plus merits and flaws, possibly) deals with it and so on as the dots increase.

                                              I mean, keeping with Strength since it is the easiest to measure. If one dot is up to 40lbs, 2 dots is up to 100lbs, 3 dots is up to 250 lbs and so on. I don't really see it as justifiable to have a zero value of, like, 15 lbs, then -1 at 7 lbs, -2 at 3 lbs, -3 at...do you see how it becomes kinda pointless penny-counting. Especially since it mucks up the experience values and probably a host of other system math for very little gain in rather fringe verisimilitude.
