                                I believe the love of older D&D being the TTRPG predecessor and equivalent to the Souls games is one of those "quirks" I mentioned. Some people love how much certain editions of D&D are meat grinders that will chew up and spit out disposable PCs as you master the game. Some people hate it because it disincentivizes getting attached to your characters.

                                The debates of whether D&D should be "easier" so people that don't want that high-death game play loop can get into the game mirror the debates about the same in From Software's game designs. D&D 5e absolutely ditched the idea of it (to the chagrin of people that like it) but it has proven the most accessible and popular edition of D&D yet.... so... the OSR movement has made dozens of retroclones.

                                            As I was learning to take the reigns as DM from the more experienced DM who wanted a break, I was faced with an insistence on an aspect of "XP for monsters killed". If a creature retreats from combat for any reason, the characters get XP for defeating it, even if it was simply because the creature had reason not to kill the PCs. As long as they believe they faced it and forced it to run, they will expect to gain its XP value. I thought about this. They couldn't gain XP from the same creature twice. Some creatures have level-drain abilities.

                                            It was a long time ago, so the details are a bit vague, but I had a situation where they didn't stand to gain anything by attacking it and risked being level-drained. That allowed some roleplay between them and the creature that would not have been possible had I not thought through to find a way to subvert the antiquated XP system. We started adding milestone XP sometime later.
