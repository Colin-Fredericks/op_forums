    The Martial Arts Thread

    10-04-2015, 05:05 PM
    Gamers and geeks can often carry the image of the classic "wimpy kid". Sometimes, that's totally accurate. Sometimes, it's completely wrong. People are diverse in their backgrounds and interests, and sometimes they learn to fight for one reason or another.

    As for the geek angle on this, it's generations deep. The first tabletop rpg games were based on miniature war simulation games on graph paper, which says something in itself, but even Dungeons and Dragons had warriors, knights, etc. Trained combatants. Fighting with a melee weapon is just as much a part of the martial arts as unarmed combat, but as role playing games evolved we got brawler archetypes; everything from Final Fantasy monks and black belts, World of Warcraft Kung Fu Pandareans to White Wolf's own short-lived version of Street Fighter (if someone has a copy, let me know. I would gladly take it off your hands, and at a handsome price if needed. PLEASE BRING THIS PROPERTY BACK).

    From Bruce Lee in the 70s to the huge selection of fighting video games, Sentai style shows like the Power Rangers and anime, our culture has soaked up little bits of the martial arts. Pop culture, sure, but counterculture and hobbyists tend to bend pop until it's not. Think about a thing you think of as niche. It was probably popular somewhere at some point. A friend of mine has been running an rpg game based on Dragon Ball Z for over ten years, which he designed himself all the way from the dice mechanics to the computer algorithms he could use to automatically update and adjust for all status changes in the game by simply entering the dice results. It's...crazy, and awesome. But it's also a geeky labor of love.

    I've met gamers in fighting gyms, dojos and rougher places. There are trained soldiers, Brazilian Jiu-jutsu practitioners and at least one boxer on these forums that I know of. That's not counting myself, the greatest sword fighter in the world.

    So, let's talk about Martial Arts.
    Got in a fight? Have a certain interest? What do YOU know, personally? What have you read? Have some interesting information? Perhaps you have a hypothetical situation, a question you'd like answered or something you'd like examined/to discuss in a fashion you haven't seen elsewhere?

    This is the place to do that.

              On a silly semi related note does yoga count as a martial art?
              You don't need to be coordinated to start, that's the point : you'll get better at it if you train ^^. As for what type would suit you, it really depends on whether you'd rather like to learn how to kick and punch people (in which case, try a hard style like karate or taekwondo), learn to control your body (soft styles like wing chun and t'ai chi ch'uan focus on perfect mastery of your movements, often by training very slowly until you master the moves enough to do them without thinking), or something else (krav maga is perfect if you want to learn self-defense, ju-jitsu is good for both self-defense and control, capoeira is extremely fun to practise and it's almost a dance despite being somewhat effective.)

              And no, I don't think yoga is considered to be a martial art (though if I recall correctly, some stances at least are inspired, if not directly taken from, indian martial disciplines). It can be a good basis for training though (you'll find some stances familiar, learn to keep your balance and control your body and breating... all that is useful and more.)

                                                      I started with Judo which was pretty much obligatory where I came from. Got until the Blue Belt before I quit due to an injury and fought at a few tournaments. Never got first place, but the occasional third and second which wasn't half bad considering how many people participated.

                                                      The one I did the longest was Muay Thai which I did 'til Khan 7. I would have kept going on, but sadly my Trainer decided to leave and I couldn't for the life of me click with the new one. Its one thing to teach people Martial Arts at a competetive level but a whole other one to solely focus on that. I never liked the idea of fighting exclusively to win, as pretentious as this may sound, so I quit. I actually won a full-contact tournament in Muay-Thai once but that sounds far more impressive than it actually was due to the fact that I was in a weight class that only had four contestants.

                                                      Currently I'm doing Monkey Kung-Fu and Ninjutsu, but not too seriously. Sadly, I don't have the time to do so. I also dabbled in Capoeira, Iado and Taekwondo, but not on any "real" level.

                                                      Concerning fights I always try to avoid them, either by trying to de-escalate the situation or, if nothing else, running. One time I actually failed at both and had to defend myself physically which I'm still glad I managed. My Muay-Thai teacher always said that we should consider everyone we fight to be potentially stronger than us. Does he carry a knife? Or maybe even a gun? Will he call for backup or use dirty tricks? And if nothing else helps hit first and hit hard so he can't use any of those advantages. I'm still grateful to him for that.
