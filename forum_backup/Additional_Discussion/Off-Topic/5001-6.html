        I come from Australia.

        I have learned one thing from my experiences, and that is appearances matter, because I was a fat nerd in primary school and the start of high school. My friends and I were bullied pretty harshly during those formative years. In a digital art class (basically, learning how to use Photoshop and other programs for artistic and editing purposes), there were a group of three guys who bullied a friend of mine relentlessly. I have no idea why these guys were in the class, because they didn't really participate in any of the lessons or finish their assignments.

        I recall the time my friend finally told one of the guys: "Fuck off!" The bully then stood up, grabbed his chair and belted my friend with the chair. My friend was badly hurt, went to hospital, and the guy was threatened with expulsion (although, he ended up only getting suspended and a bunch of detentions).

        After the fact, one of the other guys started 'bullying' me. Bullying seems like a non-word to me, really. Really, these guys were just awful people. I don't even remember one of their names. Anyway, the guy used to sit behind me and kick me in the back whenever he felt like it. The third guy was really nondescript and was more someone who was just around and laughed at this crap.

        After I finished that class, I never really saw these guys again, except it turned out the third guy was a mutual friend of one of mine. Weird. Never spoke to him, though, so whatever.

        Another time a guy was making fun of the way I spoke (I have asperger's, plus at the time a speech impediment), and I retaliated by telling the guy he was fat (I was still podgy, so in hindsight it's deliciously hypocritical)... not really bullying, so much as two guys butting heads... he hit me with his backpack, and he ended up getting litter duty. After the fact, we actually got along very well and I have to say he ended up being one of the nicest guys I knew in high school. Weird. But funny.

        Anyway, during mid-high school, I hit puberty proper... slimmed down, got taller, lost my speech impediment (learned to pretend to understand people), and things changed pretty quickly for me. Why? I was now good-looking, fortunate enough to have sisters who told me what was popular to wear, and started building up a lot of friends. Teachers liked me. I was a good kid and never got in trouble.

        I was and am very nerdy, but I didn't really look it in high school. (e.g. a sister's friend came over, and I greeted her politely, then went to another room. I overheard her telling my sister, "I thought you said your brother was nerdy?" And my sister saying, "He is! I swear he is!" -- I joke with my sister about this still and she says she was really weird in high school and doesn't know what she was thinking lol).

        Anyway, so I was mildly popular... but this is another thing that has stuck with me forever and I regret it a lot...

        I got asked out by a really mousy and nerdy girl; she was in my SOSE class and we never spoke before; she drew me a really nice picture to ask me out and in hindsight it was an incredibly nice gesture. I was sitting with friends and my friends took it from my hands. And they laughed. Made fun of it. And ripped it into pieces and it was clearly a horrible thing to do while she watched. I didn't rip it, but I did nothing to fix the situation. And I regret being a sheep and an utter coward for not doing anything helpful. She was very badly bullied. Eventually, the girl committed suicide about a year later. It's pretty much stayed with me forever and is a clear indicator that being a dick to people is really not okay and there can be awful ramifications.

            Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
            There are a couple of problems with the 'just kick out the bad ones' idea.

            1) How 'bad' a student's actions are perceived depends on their race. There have been several studies that show when white students and black students engage in the same behavior, the black students are more likely to be labeled a problem.

            2) Bad students are more likely to be from bad backgrounds. Taking a group of people with antisocial tendencies who are more likely to be suffering from abuse or poverty or other troubled home life and removing their ability to get basic math and writing skills is going to increase social stratification and the number of people who become criminals.

            3) Bullying sometimes happens with the tact approval of teachers and other authority figures. Transgender students, for example, recieve all types of abuse that's ignore or condoned by teachers/principles; if said teacher or principle had the ability to remove troublemakers, it's very likely that the bullies would not be the ones kicked out.
            I have to agree with everything hippokrene said. It's sad, but true.

            Over here in Germany, it's weird, regarding race. 90% of the time it doesn't matter at all, except for people annoyingly trying to be correct about it. But when it does crop up, it's obvious people either preferentially treat those of their race, or in some instances, do the exact opposite, just to not be racist. Add to that, that if you're even just a bit non-white, you just have to imply racism is part of the situation for every adult to freak out and try to make things right for you. Of course that's also racist, but it's not what people think of when you use that term. I think I haven't had any contact with what people traditionally call racism during my school time. I don't think I've even met someone who's been treated racist, ever. That changed after certain insane people with planes, but that I completely blame on the media and while it's affected my chances on the job-market, other than that I usually just have to patiently tell people they're being stupid and explain why I'm not a Muslim even though I'm half-arabic.

            If I had wanted to, I could easily have abused my "race" in school. I had to clear up multiple misunderstandings that led people to thinking something might have happened because of racism. When I did, any interest in my situation went away, which is the actual tragedy. My school was actually pretty quick to kick people out because of alleged racism, which certainly was unfounded in every case I know of and also didn't do those people any favors in becoming productive parts of society.

            We're lucky enough that there have been enough studies about this kind of thing, that we can be relatively certain that it just makes a bad situation worse if you kick people out who cause trouble. Because as hippokrene also pointed out, they usually have something bad going on at home, as well. And it easily hits the wrong people. I more or less had teachers gang up on me to make sure I'd fail one year, which in hindsight worked out very well for me, but it certainly wasn't intended to help me. If they could have, they'd have thrown me out of school right away. And that's simply because I questioned them and saw no reason to treat them with the degree of respect they expected from us students.

            Almost a decade after that crap happened to me, I got into a rather prestigious institution because I passed a "gifted" test. There I got to see how schools are supposed to be run. Every teacher there cared, save two, one who also wasn't good at her job and she was replaced within a month after we reported we weren't impressed with her as a teacher. The other was still an amazing teacher, so it was easy to accept that we had a single mostly uncaring educator.
            Anyone who got in trouble always could go to the educators or the headmaster for help, even regarding things not even related to the academy. There were a very few attempts at bullying. Those people that same day were sent to the headmaster and he talked to them, calmly explaining that opposed to regular schools, there would be consequences, including detailed reports to the people paying for their education. It was weird how quickly those people abandoned their previous behavior. And it took them a couple of weeks to find a new place in our class, but except for a single guy who was expelled because he never attended class, we all got along fine. Not necessarily great, but it was civilized.
            Three things were different in that place. Teachers cared and the academy had insane amounts of money it kept investing in projects to further improve the academy and we actually learned useful things there. Now, I need to stress that while the money certainly helped get the good teachers, other than that it was just a bonus. It wasn't required to improve the situation we're talking about.

            Now imagine I'd still be the same, basically nice, ages ago meek and shy guy I was and got kicked out but with no chance to get into such an institution? I'd have turned to crime or accepted drudge work. There's no question about it for me. And giving people the impression that their only choices are crime, being a failure or suicide is a really bad thing to do.

            Don't get me wrong, there are people who deserve to be expelled, but it's almost certainly a very small amount of people. The sub-culture of school usually simply doesn't teach these people that their behavior is even wrong. Punishing them for making life hell for other students, without using it to communicate that message wouldn't achieve good things.

                        I'm pretty sure (with nothing but anecdotal evidence, mind) that they too are succumbing to stress and pressures that come from outside of school (or maybe even others inside school), like from living with alcoholic, violent or overbearing parents (essentially being bullied by their parents), coping with the loss or the drug addiction of a family member etc; and that helping them deal with those stresses and pressures is more important than "whatever, get out". Essentially, I'm suggesting that they don't know any better. They literally haven't learned how to deal with stress and how what they do impacts others. Ideally, parents should be teaching this, but parents are fallible human beings as well and tend to pass on their failures and biases as much as anything else.
