            Last night I had a dream that my wife had essentially given up on our relationship. When I went to talk to her about it, she groaned and told me to go cry somewhere else. I went downstairs to have a shower on the kitchen table, but some dumbass had dumped a shipping container of pasta salad down the drain and overloaded the septic system, causing pasta salad to come spraying out the showerhead. After I woke up and told her about this, there was much aftercare.

            Thursday night's dream involved us buying a tiny house with a shrubbery next to a natural infinity pool on top of a steep alpine mountain. So steep that there was no road up the mountain, only stairs. The house was a tiny gingerbread contraption that was so small, the bedroom took up the entire second floor. The house was not all it was cracked up to be, unfortunately. The infinity pool was only six inches deep, the front lawn was crawling with snakes and venemous toads, and the backyard was infested with lizards. And the shrubbery? It had a rock panther in it. Rock Panthers are great cats that only live in my dreams. Roughly the size of a tiger, they are covered in tan leaf-shaped scales but are otherwise entirely feline. They tend to hide in thick underbrush, which is the only reason you have any chance of surviving an encounter with one. They are otherwise too quick and powerful, but tend to have difficulty dislodging themselves from their bivouacs.

                    These are the monster's rules: First, it manifests as a sense of unease. If you run away immediately upon experiencing it, then you are safe; it cannot pursue you. If you ignore it even for an instant, though, it can hunt you for the rest of your life, always coming at you from behind, but it cannot take you until you lay eyes on it. It can whisper to you, follow you wherever you go, and lay its cold, dry, dead fingers on you, but it can't take you until you look over your shoulder or see it in a mirror. If you do not meet the criteria, then it is not real to you.

                    I don't know what happened to the little boy. I just know he was dead before the monster had finished pulling him by the ankles into the shadows under the bush in the playground. I saw his sister's death, though...sort of. She spotted her brother's body, whose head was the lone part sticking out of the bushes, its cheek on the gravel and its eyes wide and dead, and her heart seized with fear. But rather than running away, she ran toward him, wanting to help the brother she loved, and that doomed her. She was running one moment, and then the next she was limp and falling, her face replaced by a ruined concavity of steaming red meat where a giant, invisible mouth simply bit it off.

                    Her parents couldn't even scream in their shock. Not until the bodies got back up again. The little girl obviously had no expression to be seen as it turned like a drunken marionette to face its family, but nevertheless they were sure her lips were split in a wide, hateful, insane grin that matched that on their son's face. As he crawled out from the shadows, the meat had been flensed from his bones from the shoulders down. The meat of his neck sagged and flapped loosely, since it was only anchored from above now.

                    I wish I could say my outrage was caused by the deaths of two innocent children, but it wasn't. It was offense that this abomination should have the temerity to exist without my permission, to manifest itself in front of me in defiance of my will. I would kill it for offending me, so I hurled myself at the children who I now knew were not just dead but were part of it forever and ever, a fact which only made me angrier. One hand seized the girl by the crown of the head and I whipped her body over my shoulder, shattering the vertebrae in her neck like a farmer who doesn't want to get blood on his ax killing a chicken; the boy I felt my claws wanting to extend for and I fought the impulse down. I would kill him in my human form, with my fists. My rage was intoxicating but I wouldn't let it take my humanity; I was already too close to the edge. I could hear myself roaring and I sounded like a gorilla, not a man. I grasped the bones and rent them, pulling them apart until no force could reanimate them.

                    Behind me, the parents were screaming, because the abomination didn't need the childrens' bodies to attack them. The father already felt its fingers on his neck, and I charged at them, ready to grasp the thing and beat it to death, but even as I did I knew I was helpless. I was insane with rage at the point, and my heart was so full of hate I had no capacity to feel the fear I needed to feel in order for it to become real to me; and if it wasn't real to me, I could not put my fists on it.

                    I woke up before the dream showed me what happened to the parents, but I assume it was nothing good.
