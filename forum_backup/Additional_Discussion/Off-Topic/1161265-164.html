            I kind of considered MtG for a bit, too. The UA articles for the Heroism Paladin and the Eloquence Bard were extremely Greco-Roman to me, which in turn could be inferred as meaning Theros (we're getting a new set on that plane soon). On another point, I think the Soulknife Rogue is (almost) an exact match for the planeswalker Kaya. Just replace "goes intangible" with "goes invisible", and you'd have a dead ringer (pun appreciated). Remember that in DnD 5e, incorporeal undead have no resistance or immunity to Psychic damage.

            However, I'm unsure that these small bits constitute a commitment to another MtG sourcebook specifically. Least of all one for Theros in specific. The idea of a guide to the planes of MtG seems more promising, but I'm skeptical of the idea that WotC would create a book detailing MtG's multiverse before they covered DnD's. More likely, the sourcebook will be a book about the different planes of DnD, with MtG planes thrown in as their own section. A kind of comprehensive sourcebook that is part Planescape and part Plane Shift (that is, the articles adapting MtG to DnD 5e, that were the forebears for Guilemaster's Guide to Ravnica).

                                                            12-23-2019, 01:00 AM
                                                            The family is all together for the holidays, so we get to play more DnD.

                                                            We also had more time to properly flesh out my players' characters. Establish their Personality Traits, Ideals, Bonds, and Flaws. Figure out their backstories. And establish long-term goals. My sister's character wants to find her horse, which was seized as part of the war effort in my setting. She wants to get it back. She's generally interested in becoming a mounted horse archer (who is also magic, since she's decided she wants to move into Arcane Archer). For her character's previous horse, I have a kernel of an idea of how to handle it (it currently being in the possession of an anti-paladin knight), but for now she needs a substitute mount. Fortunately, I'd established in a previous session that there was a horse running around the area, its owners dead and it driven off by dire wolves.

                                                            After reminding her of this fact during a car ride, the group decided that tonight's session would revolve around tracking the horse down and claiming it as their own.

                                                            For context, they'd just spent much of the day fighting underground, in the buried ruins of the village (destroyed by mudslide). In the previous session, they killed a boss ghoul, which let them reach level 2. They'd been adventuring all day, so I wanted a good challenge before they took a long rest. The encounter I went with was a trio of skeleton warriors, riding skeletal warhorses, that had dug their way from the ground and chased the living horse. When I set up the map of the battlefield, I assumed they would hide behind the buried roof of a house and stay together.

                                                            Instead, when the undead horsemen emerged from the mist, the party had each hidden in a different spot, well away from one another. Guess I once again learn the price of making assumptions as a DM.

                                                            The party killed the riders handily enough, getting a surprise round on them. The undead warhorses, however, were far tankier, and gave the party a run for their money. Luckily, my sister's archer character had managed to Animal Handling the living horse under control, allowing her to ride it, giving her mobility on par with the undead equines. It was still a brutal fight, with both the archer and the Rogue (my brother) getting downed, and the other Fighter (a tank controlled by my brother-in-law) dropping to 2 HP. The DMPC - Oswalt the Halfling Bard - needed to use the last of his spell slots getting both downed characters up. It took all the party had to prevail.

                                                            In short, an incredible fight. I'm glad I didn't throw a fourth set of skeleton rider and steed into the encounter.

                                                            Hopefully, tomorrow will bring more action, as the party delves back into the underground. Not sure what the party will do next, though I've given them a few options. At least they have a horse now (not that it will do much good in the tunnels).
