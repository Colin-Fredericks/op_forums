                            I'm having a hard time finding my voice, so I apologize if I don't establish my point coherently, I am doing my best.

                            You don't necessarily need to be religious to celebrate them, but marriages and their associated ceremonies are highly ritualized. I think it represents one of those messy areas where things start to overlap, and I have friends who argue that the government shouldn't get involved at all. That there shouldn't be tax stipulations, laws regarding income, etc. I don't necessarily agree with all of that, I'm not the best informed. I think those stipulations exist for a reason. Likewise I think of marriage is something that is important, even as an atheist/agnostic/what have you. My issue with the government not supporting gay marriage isn't just because it disadvantaged me. It was because that interpretation of marriage favored one religion, and it wasn't mine. Before I considered myself an atheist, I identified as pagan, a very eclectic pagan, but I belonged to a group, attended meetings, etc. I don't believe it is necessary for the government to strip away marriage in favor of civil unions, I'm not sure that would benefit society in the long run. Especially now, families and couples often need help to make things work - that's not to say single people don't suffer obstacles, 'cause we do, but I'd advocate for helping them in other ways, rather than stripping the provisions for marriages we have now.

                            Gay marriage is a step forward, I think, because it means the government is not reinforcing one religion's concept of marriage, and I'm not the sort of atheist that wants everyone to just stop being religious tomorrow. I'd rather everyone get along and try to treat each other with respect.
