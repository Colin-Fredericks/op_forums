            On the issue of price, I just bought a traditionally printed Shadowrun 5th edition core book. It isn't cheaper.

            In Onyx Path's case, assuming they continue developing RPGs but "revert" to a more traditional publishing pipeline, that doesn't necessarily have to mean a decrease in quality. I feel that increased quality of everything we're seeing from OPP over the past couple of years is not specifically a result of digital publishing. It's a result of not having a treadmill and a certain number of books needing to be published in a certain time frame. Also, having awesome people working on awesome projects in an awesome manner, but that's a fixed variable that wouldn't be changing either way. Now, certainly, digital publishing is one of the reasons why the treadmill wasn't necessary anymore, but I can imagine a scenario where traditional publishing can be set up in such a way so as to respect the no-treadmill paradigm.

            And having White Wolf books back in FLGSs can only be a good thing.

                                                        Originally posted by Dawngreeter View Post
                                                        On the issue of price, I just bought a traditionally printed Shadowrun 5th edition core book. It isn't cheaper.
                                                        It's less about the end cost and more about where the money goes.

                                                        If there's two books, one that is sold in your Friendly Local Gaming Store and one that is purchased via Drive-Thru's partner programme, both sold for $150 the money is chopped up very differently.

                                                        for the book store, $75 of that goes to the bookstore, the remaining $75 goes to shipping, manufacturing, loss due to returns*, replacement costs, the cost of the print run and then whatever is left goes to the developer company.

                                                        for the online version, the developer company gets slightly more than they do with a PDF sale copy, drive-thru takes a percentage cut, and the bulk of the money (and the cost of replacement if needed) is handled by the print on demand company.

                                                        This is why few booksellers really want to get into the selling PoD generated books in the store, that $150 book is suddenly likely to be a just-sub-$300 book.


                                                        *-In the US at least there's some odd quirks in the bookselling business, including the fact that the bookseller has the option to return ANY unsold books back to the seller and get credit for the full price against ANY other product offered by that bookseller. So if your nWoD2 Beast books are not selling as well as you'd like, you can return them and get credit for Vampire 4e, if those were both traditionally sold. This means that a publisher can't afford a badly selling book... or rather that if they have a badly selling book they'll either have to eat the loss or store it in the warehouse for the decade plus it'll take to move it.

                                                            I use print on demand over at my press, and the cost has dropped dramatically for B&W paperbacks. All the books I produce are now in the retail chain and the price is equivalent to that of university presses and indie presses (whereas a mass market paperback might cost £6.99, an indie press might do a trade paperback for £9.99, which is usually okay because it's a larger format).

                                                            DriveThruRPG uses Lightning Source, which I find to be extremely costly for anything that isn't B&W or standard paper sizes. Lightning Source does have retail distribution too, however, as it's owned by Ingram. In theory, they could turn up in bookshops, but most of the chain stores use sales algorithms so they only order in highly popular titles (and these channels aren't really suited for RPGs).

                                                            Really, DriveThruRPG should explore better print on demand companies. The quality of the books would probably be better too (I hate the lack of full bleed, for example). But this is a pet peeve of mine. I find myself really resenting the higher price and lower print quality, so I stick to KS limited editions and PDFs instead.

                                                            The new WW might not be tied to this arrangement, though, and might even diversify distribution channels by making copies available through multiple print on demand outfits around the world.

                                                            Small run digital is another model that might work (print 100-500 at a time, with almost litho quality on every book), although the POD company I use is pretty much at the same standard now.
