    Originally posted by Lysander View Post
    I've been learning about the Karhshav Scale of civilization and how each type would be a game changer for us.
    I got over it. In the end it's not as simple and very unlikely.

    We're currently in the era that put Kardashev Scale to its heavier test and it's loosing. We are ever more clearly learning that civilizational development needs efficiency, which means using less energy rather than more.

    That's not an abstract observation. While it covers dozens of popular ideas in ecology, politics and economy, the root cause of the problem is entropy. Increasing energy consumption also increases the speed the system accrues entropy.

    A civilization constantly going forward in the scale is a civilization running against collapse from excess entropy in its various forms. The problem is that entropy tends to grow faster than your capacity to run.

    Dyson Spheres, for example: you cover a star with mirrors to collect energy. Awesome, but... What about the natural systems you depend upon that used that energy before? Can you substitute them more efficiently than they were? What you do if you collapse them just to find out you miscalculated? You locked the star out of other energy generation and distribution systems, how long can your single one handle everything alone? And what do if entropy builds up faster than you can build the next Sphere?

    Kardashev assumed development would be continually finding answers to collapse in two forms, finding new resources in time and using this energy to produce everything we need. But it didn't just ignored the internal complexity and interconnection of local systems, it fundamentally ignored the Law of Entropy, and that is one of the most important we know across all sciences.

                    I have to say that Two Face is a hard character to use well even in the comics.

                    There are many pieces that don't fit intuitively. Harvey is a competent lawyer, want to do good, but also has failings and isn't immune to corruption despite trying to resist. He is not a member of Gotham's social elite, but has some experience navigating it, which both makes him fit in some high society contexts but also face resistance and some clash. The signs of Two Face exist within him, and probably a first sign of Dissociation should, too.

                    He seemed well, and Bruce couldn't notice the signs at the time, but Dent was in a bad mental path way before the accident.

                    After that Two Face still isn't simply a murder like the Joker. He's a gang boss, a criminal with focus on practical, basic profit and controlling territory. His flair for the dramatic is lower than most Batman rogues. He has a problem with decisions and an obsession with the coin, but that's not a constant focus. The duality motif is a minor affectation.

                    We get then to his relationship with Wayne. His best friend and the person that encouraged the most his staying in the right path, but also his contact surface with wealth and decadence.

                    It is a lot of pathos to unpack, but also many independent parts easy to work with and forget the others, and very, very few authors ever managed to work half of them in a story. Which is bad because isolated they're not really that interesting, the point is how the contradictions interact, or the Two Face motif falls flat, which is what happens 95% of the time.

                    Of course, he's also a bag of easy tropes to use so this flatness doesn't stop a good story, but is still a hard character, one I would usually advise against its use for a movie.

                            That's not it. The problem isn't accumulated complexity, but how hard it is to show the concept in a movie.

                            Joker is a mass murderer that finds it funny and has the Laughing Toxin. You can mix and match addons to this core and still get a good Joker.

                            Catwoman is a jewelry thief, the Penguin is a rich mobster that likes umbrellas, both have an animal affectation that's just that. Poison Ivy is a crazy terrorist with plant powers trying to avenge the vegetation and wearing big leafs.

                            In any depiction, my questions are:
                            1. Is the character recognizable in appearance, behavior and speech?
                            2. Does the concept feel complete, or I feel the lack of something right away?
                            3. The result feels on brand or generic?

                            For most of them you need little to get all those. The Joker needs a color scheme, laugh a lot, kill with glee, clown-themed paraphernalia and stay in-character. Catwoman needs cat motifs, a femme-fatale or bon-vivant demeanor and try to get whatever strikes her fancy.

                            Penguin is already a slightly hard one. He's a mobster, but also an engineer with umbrella guns and penguin-themed gadgets. Stopping at one of those and not exploring the other is easy, and then he will feel unrecognizable or incomplete, sometimes a generic gangster with motifs tackled on.

                            So that's my point with Two-Face. His core identity is contradiction. He need contradiction for the coin gimmick to work. Contradiction is hard to deliver in a limited time. And his basic story beats are many: Bruce's friend > lawyer > high society > a layer of conflict for each > turn into gangster, that's 7 when most others have 1 to 3.

                            And to be honest I don't say any of that with the intention of saying I find him a specially well built or interesting character. I think the major problem with Two-Face as character design is the overkill, too many independent moving parts to make the character tick in the basic configuration. There's a reason why most authors stick to the dual motif plus coin and call it a day. But the result is frequently generic, which works when the story is good despite the shallow Two-Face.
