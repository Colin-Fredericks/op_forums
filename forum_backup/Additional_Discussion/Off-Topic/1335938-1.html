                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                        ...but was supposed to be "this is not an allegory for any real world culture, stop accusing me of being racist." Not that I'm saying anybody here was, but there are obviously those out there who would try to superimpose various ideologies unto the setting and mistake it for some kind of personal attack.
                        But this is just a cop out.

                        The second you say something like, "goblins are an evil race," you've made them an allegory for real world cultural issues. Maybe you avoid racism, or classism, or whatever, but "race" is a fictional concept humans made up as an excuse to be shitty to each other. When you call something in your fantasy world a "race" you're saying, "you should consider these analogues to humans," and then people are going to be primed to look at the context you put in your setting. You can't just say, "Goblins are goblins, and they're a race," and then say, "don't read anything into this." Calling them a race is asking them to read into it. And either you acknowledge that and make sure your message is clear, or you don't.

                        Just blaming people that are trying to "superimpose" is abdicating your responsibilities in crafting your setting. If you write a setting, you're responsible for what you put in it. If you put in stuff that's reads as extremely _____-ist, that's your fault, not some fictional group of people out to get offended.

                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                        As a result, they preferred not to wear shoes since, not because of anything racist, but simply because of how hairy their feet were, they found stuffing their feet into shoes uncomfortable, and due to the thick soles of their feet, shoes largely unnecessary. Some, of course, liked to wear shoes for various reasons, but those reasons tended to be fairly personal.
                        While not in the novels themselves, Tolkien actually wrote notes on the subject. He never intended the Hobbits to be read as "anti-shoes," but rather that the Shire was such a nice place shoes were irrelevant for them given the terrain of their homeland. Hobbits that travel outside the Shire almost always end up wearing shoes when doing so, because the rest of the world isn't all soft grassy hills and farmland.

                        But since hobbits are isolationists by culture, Hobbits wearing shoes in the Shire are basically being anti-social because they're signalling that they don't consider the Shire "home" by wearing something that's for "outside."

                        Of course, with Tolkien, it's generally better to look at classism before racism for the underlying assumptions being made. The hobbits were Tolkien's idealized bucolic agrarian class, having their folksy charm, generous hospitality, and balanced lifestyles being destroyed by industrialization to turn them into middle class factory workers. They were written as not wearing shoes for the same reason Huck Finn doesn't wear shoes: it's a metaphor for the freedom from higher class social constraints.
