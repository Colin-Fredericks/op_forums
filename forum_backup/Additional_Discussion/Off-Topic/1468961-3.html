    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
    I was looking at the original boxed set this weekend, and it occurred to me that Arak was a very weird choice for a domain. Mainly because it was just sort of there doing absolutely nothing. The original book gave little to no information about it, no one lives there (anymore, not since the creation of Keening destroyed its surface), and nothing really happens there, baring anyone spending the night getting taken away by the mysterious underground "elves". I get the feeling that there might've been this idea of having an adventure module set in Arak that was eventually going to detail the domain and explain the inhabitants, which I presume were originally intended to be something similar to the Drow. (I'm not sure if it would've been the Shadow Fey we eventually got.) But in a way, I kind of feel like this was a missed opportunity to give Ravenloft a dwarven domain, perhaps based on Tolkien's works (either the Hobbit idea of a dwarf king consumed by greed or the LotR's mines full of ghosts and tormented by a demonic monster they dug up). Perhaps with the realm of the the shadow fey and Gwydion the Sorcerer-Fiend being below it as part of Ravenloft's answer to the Underdark. (Maybe alongside Bleutspur and the God-Brain.)
    That would be interesting, as they already had an Elvish domain. But in the end they set up for the Shadow Fey, which I think worked as the seed for the whole idea of the Shadowfell.

    Before continuing I must say, the maps on this book are really something. Loved them even when I hated the domain, that much I must confess. I wish we had something like this in previous editions.

    LAMORDIA

    I seriously don’t like what they did here. It could work as a new domain entirely, but even then I’m not sure. This is too Grimdark and stripped the stories it purposes to tell from their needed context. It may work for body horror for sure, but definitely not for the Gothic tale of the Created.

    Lamordia was not a land about the maladies of science and industry as it is now. Quite the contrary, it was about the goodness in science and industry. Why? Because that backdrop makes the contrast needed for the tales of wicked science to work. When everyone is required by necessity to be unethical you don’t have horror about science, you have horror about the system.

    And new Lamordia is horror about the system in spades, an extremely on the nose criticism on capitalism with a strong take on the tales of the Robber Barons of 19th century England, Nazi German and a lot of Atomic Fear. It really doesn’t gel here, despite being good fodder for a Domain for certain. As is now, it robs all the drama from the tales of hidden blasphemies by mad doctors and instead seems like a Black Dog supplement about Pentex.

    For the Darklord, as above so below. They changed almost everything in Mordenheim’s background, mostly for the worse. But lets tackle the elephant in the room first and foremost: Mordenheim as a lesbian woman isn’t in the least a problem. The story works perfectly well with this, even the original tale would, as Victor and Elise never had biological children anyways. If the lack of children was due to infertility, lack of sex, or them being just a homosexual couple is immaterial, as it was never deemed a source of drama on itself anyway.

    But changing Elise to a petty criminal when making them lesbians? That’s some messed up coding. All the drama from the original story was stripped for this shitty arrangement. It is a love story between criminals that end badly because of the creation of a McGuffing, not because of obsession, loneliness or alienation. And now Elise’s exile is just because there’s no honor among thieves. Wait, wasn’t Viktra so obsessed for her that she managed to create the McGuffing in the first place? Whatever, now she just wants the Thing, Elise be damned.

    On the Von Aubrecker front, things got butchered again. Replacing the Baron for his son Rudolph-Brain-in-a-Jar was a bad but expected development. Nothing terrible about that that aren’t already covered by the first three paragraphs above. But turning Gerta from actual power behind her father to a mere dominated butler? Why?

    The more I look at NuLamordia, the more I despise this write-up. Looking at the whole picture, what seems as a diversity-friendly domain at least in the gender/sex area is actually a lot of disguised prejudice. Lesbians with no redeeming quality, women are there to serve, modern society is endangered but the mutants running amok due to its own modern failings. Gods, how I hate this write-up.

    Well, it does have a few good things about it. The adventure ideas are good enough and are really diverse. The advice on running a game for characters experimented upon by Viktra are good, although the lack of mechanics to help the process really harms the intended goal (just use another stat block isn’t a good mechanic for PCs). And the Serving Dr. Mordenheim table is short but does its job.

    My final note, so: they wasted more than four pages with a messed up domain that doesn’t work for its intended purpose and an offensive excuse for a villain that doesn’t justify anything about the place, then used just about one and a half page for the really good stuff, that suffered greatly for getting so little.

    MORDENT

    Simply put, while Barovia is the most iconic Ravenloft domain, Mordent is its vanilla and always has been. It is the easiest domain to make a classic horror tale, it is already very similar to the English countryside that is kind of a staple of the classic Gothic, it has a social structure that is rigid, but not overwhelmingly so and is easy to understand and use. It is the home of the Van Richten’s Herbalist Shop, and of many scholar societies studying the demiplane as a whole. In a sense, it is the heart of the setting.

    On the other hand two big problems plague Mordent as a setting, derived from its origins. First a counterpoint to being vanilla, it lacks a stronger flavor. It is hard to point a unifying theme to it outside “there be ghosts”. Is hard to hear a tale and say “that could only happen in Mordent”, unless it relied too much on very specific characters, of course. The other problem is that Godefroy has never been very interesting as a Darklord.

    Both problems have a common origin in the nature of the original module Ravenloft 2: The House on Gryphon Hill. Basically speaking, Ravenloft 2 was built around a single event that would come to a resolution at the end one way or another, so almost nothing on it can meaningfully endure in the setting, and the story’s villains are better used elsewhere (Strahd and Azalin).

    That means Godefroy was designed for the Domain and not the opposite, and was a simple design. A Darklord that is a ghost, so the haunting on Gryphon Hill remains iconic. And that’s it! Godefroy’s evil isn’t spectacular, or far reaching, you get by his story that even the Mist seem to only care about Mordent to take the Apparatus and the Alchemist, and Godefroy was made Darklord just because it should have one. For many years he remained the single most isolated Darklord with such a large Domain, and even after they made him a kind of ghost mob boss he felt without a purpose.

    My criticism on old Godefroy is important to frame what changed about Mordent here. This is by far the most intact domain in relation to previous material, to begin with, and I liked the changes. Mordent retains most of its features and themes, but they’re more sharp, lets say. A local curse to make everyone into a ghost also helps to make it feel special without changing much in the overall nature of the domain, although it may be a bit too far if taken to a broader setting, but that’s minor. Rudolph Van Richten still being alive is another matter, for another moment.

    Lord Godefroy, though, had just a bit more of tweaks that made for a far greater role in the whole domain, and finally gave him what it takes to be the Darklord for real, instead of just a glorified haunt. Linking him to the story of the Apparatus puts him in the center of the events, and now his actions go far enough to add some thousand deaths under his belt. His personality is better defined and yet the same, and now he actually does have a plan, a greater purpose to direct his actions as the ghostly mob boss he still is.

    Closing the write-up, the adventure ideas and advice on making ghost-story adventures aren’t brilliant, but are passable and close well what is otherwise an almost flawless write-up, and we part ways with an art of how the Ghost Mob looks like that is almost as good as Strahd’s is bad. Up to now, this is my favorite domain art, close to Vladeska’s battle and some points above Saidra’s Masquerade. Others are beautiful, but those are the best at capturing the feel and themes of the domain and its Darklord.

                                Yeah, changing the Darklord from the Created to the Creator really shifts a lot in the themes of the Domain, which probably also represents the shift in the Domain as a whole- they probably wanted to have a "evil science domain" (likely because of the "agenda reasons" mentioned), and it is hard to do so when the Created is the Darklord because they are the "victim", which is a very superficial way to treat it, IMO. Frankenstein's monster may have had sympathetic sides, but he still was the monster who tormented his "father" for abandoning him, killing those Frankenstein cared about yet did the monster nothing wrong- which is a dynamic that original Lamordia preserved. The Darklord is the monster, and Mordenheim has no control over the world he basically Created as it is not his story- it is Adam's story, and he is trapped there because it serves Adam's obsession and keeps the show going on. By focusing on Mordenheim as the Darklord, the dynamics is shifted- the obsession of the Creator becomes the driving force of the Domain, and the Created is on the run instead of being an ominous threat in the shadows of the domain, slowly circling around the maker. Which, in a way, feels more like Promethean 2e than the original Mary Shelly's story. Now, it is not that a Domain like that can't work- but it misses the story that Lamordia tried to tell, and I am not sure that the reason to do so was good enough.

                                    MarkK thank you. This means a lot to me.

                                    AnubisXy agreed. I'll elaborate more on that below.

                                    No One of Consequence the domain you're thinking is probably Paridon, except they focus more on economics and urban violence and less on science itself. It is also home to the Divinity of Mankind, one of the most curious religions in the setting, and the land of the Dread Dopplegangers.                                     Mordent made sense, but that doesn't change that Godefroy could be improved. And to have the Apparatus as something active, kind of, instead of just an often repeated backdrop no one knows what to do with, was a good addition. Specially if you consider the size of that damn thing, it makes perfect sense for it to still be in Gryphon Manor.

                                    LostLight thank you again for the link. It is far shallower, but again highlights how little the authors understood what they were working with.

                                    Frankenstein the book is one of the best and most iconic Gothic tales, and the foundation of Gothic Science. The relationship between Creator and Created is extremely complex, and Lamordia kept that and expanded.

                                    It is an error, though, to think that Lamordia was only a Frankenstein pastiche, because a lot of key elements are distinct and explored upon. Dr. Frankenstein is an obsessed but ultimately kind man, whose moral failing is excusing himself of responsibility under stress, but not doing harm on purpose. He had no direct family and at first no personal drive strong enough to face inconvenience. Under the weight of his sins Frankenstein succumbs with guilty and self-loath, which only drives him further down as he uses his suffering to excuse himself again from action. When he finally goes past this point, it is already too late.

                                    But Mordenheim is his polar opposite. He have a direct family in the form of Elise and no love for anyone beyond that bond. He sins not because he avoid responsibility, but because he doesn't fucking care, not even enough to say so. Where Frank finds respite on company Mord despises it as undesirable distraction. Where the Creature achieves almost complete power in their relationship, Adam is devoid of power over Mord, with no one to successfully vent his frustration on.

                                    There's an interesting discussion on the matter in Adam's entry in the Mistipedia. Lamordia does reflect Mordenheim. It does, because Adam's main failing is his jealousy. He envies everything Mordenheim have and is, so his domain mocks him being everything he wants, yet putting his Creator in the position he wishes. Adam is the one avoiding responsibility, as he blames Mordenheim for every facet of his predicament, including his own evil, instead of seeking any remedy by himself (he does have the intelligence to do so).

                                                            I gave a look at some thing about Domains of Delight, but it seems frankly lacking, which is a shame. They're not so much a counterpart to Ravenloft, but more just closed kingdoms reigned by specific Archfey. If I get the book I may give a more thorough opinion.

                                                            RICHEMULOT

                                                            Simple and short: the Domain of Richemulot was sacrificed for cheap criticism hastily done. There’s nothing worth to see here.

                                                            While in other Domains I could only question their decisions, this one is also objectively badly written. They not only threw aside the old stories and themes of the place, with its horror about the notion of a glass ceiling and corruption beneath a wholesome facade and false meritocracy, to just make a reference to the pandemic. They did it badly.

                                                            The text is inconsistent, the references are obvious and thrown around carelessly, there are no clear goals for anyone and the rat theme is played so ridiculously it is comic.

                                                            I mean it. Jacqueline is using rat slippers.

                                                            Actually, this whole mess is so thoroughly ridiculous I’m not even angry. The overall description is basic and worthless, three slightly different flavors of quarantine cities and a giant wererat warren, they don’t decide if Jacqueline wants or not to rule, cares or not for the place, or whatever. The adventure ideas are basic or just stupid, including asking the party to be Ace Ventura. The Plague is simple and only scares because they go basically the route of “no matter what a PC does to prevent it, some random shit happens and they get infected” again and again.

                                                            Not even the rules for the stages of the epidemic, that should be the heart of the write-up, are salvageable. The four stages are just Nothing Happens and three random encounter tables whose encounters are really random. The worse the plague gets… the weirder the tables are. You start with strangers coughing up on you and end up with common rats playing Macbeth in an alley. For real.

                                                            TEPEST

                                                            Here we have something new for this book: Tepest is changed, but not retconned! Well, at least not much.

                                                            Most of the Domain’s shtick changed. Out with the Inquisition and the “fairy” hunts, now Viktal is the living trope of the “too perfect to be good” village. But instead of things having always been like that, this is actually result of events on old Tepest: the Hag Sisters finally came to blows over something and the youngest came on top. The other two are trapped in the cauldron and Lorinda is de facto the sole Darklord. She went more mad than usual over the idea of having children and started her own independent shenanigans, destroying the other villages in her learning curve of how to become a figure of cult without breaking the toys.

                                                            The land outside Viktal don’t have the Inquisition, but still have the old dangers of Tepest, including the fae. Also it has hermits and small bands living outside Lorinda’s direct control. While the biggest changes do not retcon old material, the biggest actual retcon came to avoid change, making Arak an underground kingdom instead of a distinct Domain. Which works, since none of the main rulers of Arak is actually a Darklord anyway. There is a mountain called Gwydion’s Claw, but it is outside the fairies’ reach and they’re worried about that, and only one old human character stays around, now as an undead, so it does imply quite some time has passed.

                                                            Overall new Tepest works very well. It has a variety of dangers that still exist on their own, unrelated plots to be explored, people to interact. The Inquisition was interesting, but it actually felt one-note unless you explored the fine distinctions between its members, which were meaningless for any group with so much as an Elf or even a Sorcerer. Viktal is presented only in the context of its main plot, but it is actually otherwise quite normal most of the time, so it does serve any purpose a settlement can, only with a little weird colors (and, indeed, with a calm that lets you focus on other things most of the time without making the village an unnecessary hellhole).

                                                            Lorinda is all over the place, but not in a too intrusive way. She appears in Viktal at least for the season festivals every three months, is sought off by people for varying reasons, and wanders the place minding her businesses with her Laoirses. Now she even has a Wandering Hut of her own, but fortunately isn’t flying on mortars. There are several ways the characters can meet her. The fairies are still most as they were before, they’re their own thing and are dangerous, but not mindless, and are in conflict with Lorinda. Well done, useful, the tables help and the section on the Tithe can mostly be used just to add spice to the village. I loved it.

                                                            Wish they had dispensed this treatment to the other domains, Tepest has never been among my favorites.

                                                            VALACHAN

                                                            Welcome to Amazonian Jumanji.

                                                            Old Valachan was about conflicting natures. A people that embraces both civilization and survival in the wild, tormented by tyrants that are both predators and tax collectors. The villages aren’t big, but are definitely urban in the midst of the dense forest. Werepanthers and vampires roam the night, calculating their depredations to not come into conflict nor becoming out of prey.

                                                            It was a weird place. But a weird place that worked. It was interesting, had variety of things to do and interact with, the dangers of the forest, the plight of its denizens and the social structure and traditions, good and bad. The Darklord was also weird, maybe the weirdest of them all. Von Kharkov had a most strange and convoluted story, and it was even theorized that he was done Darklord less because of his evil (that wasn’t that big for a Darklord, at all) and more because the Dark Powers were curious and amused by his convoluted nature.

                                                            Chakuna keeps the strange conditions for being a Darklord despite a lesser evil, yet she is extremely simpler a Darklord and made Valachan an extremely simpler Domain. It is far more reminiscent of the Wildlands, without King Crocodile. The villages exist almost just as a little support for jungle exploration, the forest isn’t temperate anymore, but a rain forest, not even the names of the communities persist except as Easter eggs in the map.

                                                            Even despite keeping Kharkov as a character, most of the Domain’s background seems either changed or so distant in the past as to be unrecognizable. Kharkov had his heart ritually ripped and placed in a maze, where Chakuna found it and her heart is there now, they’re not vampires. He was hunting Werepanthers instead of employing them. Apparently things got much more savage and hunt-like way before his deposition. And the land itself is the greater evil, so much so that Chakuna actually chose to be Darklord to keep Valachan from just killing everyone.

                                                            Not the worse it could be, but I don’t like it. The fact that the inhabitants aren’t necessarily hostile even if they belong to a normally hostile race is interesting, but not enough to change that the entire shtick of the place is that everything else is out to kill you for reasons and Chakuna is a problem just because she hosts the Battle Royale/Hunger Games (I’m one of those people who think HG is just too derivative, but it is better known). It may work for a few sessions, but definitely lacks horror potential. You can increase its usage through the map, at least. Using it as a starting point and figuring out by yourself things for the PCs to do out there actually works better for longer games than the actual write-up.

Unless people specifically asks for deeper comments based on what I know of their originals.
