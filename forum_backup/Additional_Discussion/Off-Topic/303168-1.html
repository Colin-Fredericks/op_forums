                                                My caveat is that this story is immensely stupid.

                                                Little background first:

                                                The chronicle was core nWoD, first edition. We were playing psychic spies, employed by an underfunded section of the British Secret Service, circa 1960. Our main antagonist was Blue Star, an equivalent Soviet agency. They were significantly better funded, and their psychics were abnormally powerful. This is one of the best chronicles I've ever been in. The Storyteller masterfully crafted the setting and the missions, and there was genuine drama every time a character thought he might get caught. Character death was a looming threat. We had to make hard moral choices; we had to engage with the system. The whole thing exemplified everything I love about the Storytelling System and the World of Darkness.

                                                Except.

                                                This guy. This player. He was the Daeva from above. He got into this game by inviting himself into it, and none of us having the heart to tell him no. We thought he'd be better this time.

                                                The Cat Story

                                                We were in Paris, trying to track down a potential asset (i.e., another psychic). Without going into too much detail, we set up an operation to extract the psychic, but we knew that Blue Star was onto us. The player, who we will call "Bob", was our sniper. He had a psychic power, but it's not germane to this. While the rest of the team went to capture this psychic, Bob's character set up a sniper's nest, just in case any Soviets showed up. To make a long story short, they sort of did, right as we captured the psychic. One of our agents ended up being mind controlled by a powerful Soviet psychic. His player managed to knock himself out before Blue Star could use that to their advantage, and ended up passed out in our getaway van, along with my character and the captured psychic. The Soviets didn't know exactly where we were, or where our escape van was, but they were closing in fast. No enemy agents have shown up physically.

                                                Anyway, Bob decided to make his way down, because shit is hitting the fan and we have to get the hell out of dodge. His character knows that we have two agents in the van, and the acquired psychic.

                                                He gets down to the street, and he sees a cat. One of our antagonists, who we met in the previous session, had the power to possess animals. The cat immediately attacked him, so we're fairly certain that's what's happening. The cat is more or less grappling him. Bob tries to fight off the cat, but it won't get off him. He manages to stagger near our van, where all our everything is located.

                                                Bob: "I'm going to get in the van."

                                                One of the other players (we'll call him "Ben") suggests that letting the cat in the van would be a bad idea, for a variety of reasons, most pertinent of all being that the Soviet possessing the cat would see everything, would know where we were, and very likely be able to track us and kill us.

                                                So, Ben suggests that maybe Bob should just throw the cat against the van to stun it or kill it.

                                                Bob says he won't kill the cat.

                                                Ben says, okay, fine, but don't put it in the van. We all kind of nodded at this point.

                                                Bob says he won't kill the cat.

                                                Ben says it's not about killing the cat. Bob doesn't need to kill the cat. It's about the characters protecting themselves. "Your character would know this would be a huge mistake."

                                                Bob says he won't kill the cat.

                                                Ben says, okay, fine, but don't put it in the damn van.

                                                Bob says he won't kill the cat.

                                                If that's confusing to you, imagine how we felt.

                                                What proceeded was the most acrimonious dispute I've ever seen at a gaming table. He couldn't let go of the mere suggestion that killing the cat was an option, long, long, long after anyone was seriously suggesting it. Once it was clear, we understood that this was triggering to him. I completely understand why the death of an animal could be upsetting, even for pretend. I myself am deeply affected by it when it happens in TV and movies. But at no point was anyone pushing for him to kill the cat. It was blindingly evident that he didn't want to do that. We really just wanted him to not put it in the van. That's all. Even the Storyteller pointed out that putting the cat in the van was a very bad choice. But every time we tried to end the discussion, to let it go, Bob would bring it up again, expressly to continue the argument. He insisted on couching it in terms of his character, rather than himself, and tried to argue for the logic of letting it be in the van.

                                                Ben later admitted that he contributed to the length of the argument, and got way too angry about it, but at the time he was apoplectic that Bob, who'd invited himself into the game, was now going to result in everyone's death, and ruin what had been a genuinely awesome chronicle.

                                                Bob: "You're all a bunch of dicks."

                                                Ben: "You don't need to kill the cat! I'm not saying you have to do that! But you aren't recognizing why this is a problem because you won't listen to me!"

                                                Bob: "I won't kill a cat over this!" <-- Direct quote

                                                Me (who up to this point was too mortified to say anything): "It's an imaginary fucking cat!"

                                                At this point, the Storyteller didn't really grasp the situation. He just thought it was annoying, and wanted to move on. To end the discussion, he ruled that the cat would simply die after it was finished being possessed. This wasn't malice or pettiness, this was just the ST wanting to get the hell on with it. He didn't realise this was a player thing, not a character thing.

                                                Bob: "You're a moron."

                                                This ST is one of my closest friends. He's one of the nicest, kindest men I know. I've never heard anyone say a bad thing about him. And I've never seen him angry.

                                                Except for that moment. I don't know how he had the composure to do this, but rather than telling Bob to get the hell out of the house, he ruled that the possession on the cat ended, and that it was no longer attacking him, and that it was running away. Bob finally stopped bringing it up. Somehow, we finished the session.

                                                This was the single worst experience I've ever had in an RPG.
