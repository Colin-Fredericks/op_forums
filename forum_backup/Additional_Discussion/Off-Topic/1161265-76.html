                                    Originally posted by Maitrecorbo View Post
                                    Exactly. I also keep wondering that maybe i do more damage the fights would get done faster and the rest of the team would'nt need healing (or wouldnt need as much).
                                    From my experience on the DM’s side of the screen, Past 3rd level, 5e patties really don’t need dedicated healers. HP is high enough, and you’ve got plenty of Hit Dice to heal up between long rests, between that and a few healing potions, you have more than enough resources to get through the adventuring day, as long as the DM follows the encounter guidelines. But players hate being below about 25% of their maximum HP. They’d rather the Cleric spend a spell slot to get HP now than use it to deal damage and use a hit die or two of their own later. And so hit dice tend to end up mostly unused, unless the DM uses alternate natural healing rules.

                                    Of course, if you face more encounters per day than the game expects, or the encounters are harder than the game expects, the more a healer becomes necessary, and it’s possible ToA is especially brutal. I haven’t read oi, but given it’s supposed to be an homage to Tomb of Horrors, I wouldn’t be surprised if the difficulty is above the expected curve.

                                    Side note, a lot of people complain that the recommended 4-6 encounters per day isn’t how D&D is generally played, but they don’t take into account the expected difficulty of the encounters. 5e’s CR system is actually not terrible, and you can get the pacing the game expects with a smaller number of harder encounters (or a larger number of easier ones)

                                            But in combats, i heal, then heal and finish by healing some more. I mean, i know its useful and i've prevented at least one total party wipe. But i feel like i want to try something else. I've started healing less and using more control spell and summoning but i feel like i'm getting ready for a character change.
                                            I've found that healing in combat is general suboptimal. It's a reactive action rather than a proactive one. For example, you wait until someone gets hit, then cast a heal spell. Maybe by the time the enemy dies, your friend got hit 4 times and you need two spells to bring them back up. Instead, if you had cast Hold Person, the enemy wouldn't have been able to attack at all, and so would have dealt 0 damage. One proactive spell or two reactive spells. Some enemies hit really hard. I'd much rather the Cleric was holding or otherwise slowing down an Ogre than waiting to heal me after it hit. Our group finds that casters who focus on crowd and battlefield control spells are usually far more effective than those who focus on just dealing straight damage or healing damage.
