      I've yet to see AI capable of producing something I recognize as art.

      I've seen some decent copy-algorithm generated images, but nothing that justifies the enormous costs involved (even if hidden from "free users" who aren't the actual customers and are just being used to normalize tech that shouldn't be normalized yet), the lack of compensation for humans that had their work used without permission to train those algorithms, the continued devaluation of human creativity so corporations can pump out slop until that's the only thing on the market at a reasonable price, and so on.

      If you want custom art you don't have the skills to make yourself, find an artist and pay them; art is good, we need to pay artists so they can keep making art, and you get something truly artistic and unique.

      If you want a pretty image for something, there are literally millions of free-to-use license stock images on the Internet that as a normal person trying to find something for their personal use to get you whatever you're after. The time it takes to learn how to find and search those databases isn't any harder or longer than becoming a good "prompt writer."

      The only advantage to the algorithms is speed, but why rush if you want art?

                  The problem for these sorts of learning models, is that they can't be creative. They can only approximate creativity by mixing up stuff humans made and humans feed into the algorithm.

                  Since the people feeding the machines don't want to pay the humans making the art (seriously, there was a recent court hearing where some of the big companies in this space argued that they should be completely exempt from having to obey copyright laws), the people making the art are just going to make it harder and harder to access digitally. Once there's no new art to feed to the computers, we know what happens: the quality drops immensely. The quality of the outputs directly depends on the quality of the inputs (as in human art, not prompts), and once the inputs are just past outputs, the systems quickly iterate themselves into nonsense*.

                  Learning models will eventually lose without human artists. The question is whether society makes sure there's skilled human artists around once the bubble bursts.

                  * edit: this is the fundamental case that what we have computers doing isn't art. Human arts do learn by copying older art as well, but that's to study and refine techniques and mastery over things like materials. Human artists will get to a point where at worst their skills plateau, but they aren't going to degrade if they can't see other artists' work to copy it for ideas. They have their own creative ideas to express. The algorithms can't do that. The current systems in question aren't mastering skills in order to express original ideas. They're just remixing existing expressions, and when you take those expressions away, there's nothing to remix, and there's no original ideas.

                          Well, fortunately in the US at least, the courts have ruled that algorithmically generated content doesn't get copyright protections. So you can claim a picture it "yours" all you want, but regardless of if you're a person playing with a fun digital toy, or a major corporation, the second you post the content anywhere it's instantly either the original IP owner's property or public domain (not entirely clear but it doesn't really matter for the future of trying to turn these things into an industry).

                          While there's going to be a lot of litigation left around how much human hands need to be involved for something to qualify for IP protections (aka something corporations can actually profit from), it is a big brake in the creative industries on trying to automate everything. They're going to need actual human artists - not just prompt writers - if they actually want to own their outputs to sell them.
