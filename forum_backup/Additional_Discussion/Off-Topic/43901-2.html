                                    Sunday 23

                                    My dad came into a lot of money for reasons I’ve explained lately, so he decided to take himself, me, my sister, my sister’s secret girlfriend, my sister’s eight-year-old niece, my sister’s nine-month-old baby, and a girl he works with that he’d been trying to set me up with for a couple of years despite me not being interested all to Hawaii, to the island of Kauai. The plane didn’t actually leave until Monday, but since the flight was from St. Louis, which is two hours away from where we live, and schedule to leave at like six in the morning, we all decided to get a hotel room (or, well, two hotel rooms, because duh) in St. Louis Sunday afternoon and stay the night.

                                    Dad and my elder niece left early, to pick up the girl he works with from the city she lives in, about an hour round trip out of the way. I left with the others around two in the afternoon. We spent the two-hour drive bitching about my sister’s ex-mother-in-law, which was kind of fun but even at the time was a little unsettling. I mean, two solid hours of three adults who apparently have nothing to talk about other than how much we all hate some bitch who, admittedly, is entirely deserving of hate (the primary topic is how a week before we left, she made her drug addict boyfriend get on the phone to my sister and told him what to say to her like that old Shakespeare play, stuff like how my sister belongs in a mental institution for being gay), but still.

                                    The only pause in the bitching is when both cars met up in a parking lot maybe a hundred miles outside of St. Louis, which I only mention because Dad’s original plan had been to pull over onto the shoulder of an exit ramp and just watch for us to pass him, which I hadn’t been able to talk him out of but someone apparently did, so good for them. We made it the rest of the way to St. Louis, checked into a Holiday Inn, and went out to Joe’s Crab Shack for lunch because we felt like getting buckets of crab for a meal, and that is pretty much everything you need to know about Joe’s Crab Shack if you’ve never been there: their claim to fame is that each individual meal is a crab, a couple of potatoes, and a cob of corn served to you in a big, metal stew pot. It only seems like a lot of food until you start eating it and realize how much of it is shell. I washed my hands three times and my fingernails still smelled like crab juice.

                                    I was still pretty hungry when we were done, and no one had anything else to do other than sit around our hotel rooms, so as evening fell, I decided to leave the hotel and take a walk to the nearest gas station, which had a Burger King attached to it. It was about a half a mile away, and all the intervening space was hotels and hotel parking lots, because the area made its living off of people shuttling to and from the airport. It was also completely deserted. I did not see another human being at any point on the trip to the gas station or back; it was just me with my hands deep in my pockets for warmth, walking across asphalt set aside for cars that just weren’t there to fill the spots, my way lit by a constant orange glow from the street lamps. I don’t know what was going on with me, but something about the walk made me think of my time in Texas when my brother-in-law was getting treatment for his cancer, and I had to keep reminding myself that I was not in Texas again. I felt a little delirious, honestly, and it was a feeling that would recur later in the trip.

                                    I’d gone to the Burger King for a burger, something with a single patty because I was looking for a snack more than a meal, but when I got there I smelled the Cinnabon and decided to go for that instead. It was awful. It was the approximate temperature of the surface of the sun, entirely devoid of flavor, and waxy in consistency like a Charleston Chew. Also, my fingers still smelled like crab, but that was my fault. Still, it was calories and helped fill my gut, so I didn’t complain much.

                                    If all this sounds dull and plodding to you, well, it felt that way to me, too. The last few trips I’ve taken had a sense of adventure about them, but this one had none of that. I felt no excitement about it, no actual desire to go where I was going. I was doing it because I had no especially compelling reason not to. The trip was paid for and I wasn’t losing too much money by skipping out on work, so why not.

                                    When I got back to the room, my dad was playing with his iPad, and I couldn’t think of anything to do or talk about, so I opened my laptop and started dicking around on the internet. At some point, my niece came over because the sleeping arrangements were she’d sleep with us and all the others would share a room. She spent the entire time whining about anything she could whine about: the TV wasn’t playing cartoons she wanted to watch; someone needed to close the curtains because it was too bright; then someone needed to turn on the bathroom light because it was too dark; then it was too hot so someone needed to turn on the air conditioner; then it was too cold and the air conditioner was too loud; then she was tired and needed to go to sleep; then she was bored and wanted to get up and play with Dad’s iPad; when Dad wouldn’t let her get up and play, she started crying. I don’t know, maybe I should have had some kind of sympathy for her, but I was growling with irritation at every complaint because all of them were things she could have done for herself. Like, it wasn’t enough that the bathroom light had to be on; she needed someone else to be the one to do it, too. Whether family or foe, whether child or adult, I absolutely hate being manipulated like that, and my resentment of my own, prepubescent niece was reaching levels so black with toxicity I actually feel ashamed to describe them to you.

                                    This continued on well after the lights were off and we were all trying to sleep so we could get up at four in the morning, but she did eventually fall asleep. I did too, about fifty times. The most solid block of sleep I got was at the 3:00 hour; prior to that, I woke up every twenty minutes, according to the clock on the stand between our beds. I don’t know what kept waking me up, though I do know I kept thinking I was hearing sleet hitting the window before waking up a little more and realizing that was just the sound of Dad grinding his teeth.

                                    We were off to a great start.

                                        A few months ago, a high school buddy of mine came to town. He is the kind of guy who does not keep in touch, so we haven't spoken for several years, maybe a whole decade. When he got back, I discovered that he pretty much hadn't changed. He was older and balder, but his personality was still the same, bitter and reflexively cynical and deliberately pessimistic; he still refused to eat any food other than hamburgers and chicken nuggets; he still simpered and apologized for every little thing like the popular kids were going to beat him up. I missed him, but I quickly discovered that I just didn't have anything to say to him any more. He hated everything and explicitly told me he was looking for more things to hate (that little gem came up during a conversation about Man of Steel). When he left, I didn't try to keep in touch with him. We had shared a lot in our childhood days, but today he and I are just too different to be friends. I haven't given him a second thought.

                                        Until today. Today I cannot help realizing that I am in the same place I put my old friend in, and cannot help wondering if this is karma.

                                                        In a thread I recently created, I asked if you would live your life again with the memories you currently possess, so you could undo your mistakes (I also said I'd write this post days ago, but I quickly discovered I had enough material for at least three of these masturbatory essays, and it took me a while to figure out how to pare it down). It was a question I took extremely seriously. One of my first realizations was that I couldn't do such a thing for the sake of correcting my own failures, because I'd still have the memories of those failures to live with; so the only point would be to do better for other people. For instance, I'd stop my mom from standing on the rim of the pool to vacuum it, because that's how she fell and splintered her vertebrae.

                                                        That led me to a second realization, though: I would have to use a very light touch in changing my family's life, in showing my mom and dad how much I love and appreciate them, because my sister has a bad case of the Tommy Smothers (if you're too young to get that reference, she has a bad case of "Mama always liked you best") and it would be cruel of me to buy my own happiness at the expense of hers by widening that gap. And that, of course, led to the third realization that if I had time in this hypothetical scenario, I would have to deeply memorize the dates and circumstances of any atrocity in the past I could stand a reasonable chance of stopping because, let's face it, we'd all of us be happier if 9/11 had never happened and America hadn't gone in the direction it did.

                                                        And of course that led me to a fourth realization that I might have to actually let myself suffer and get really fucked up because my being fucked up was kind of a crucial step in making all the friends I've made in this community, and losing the people I love here is a price I'm not willing to pay for the benefits of staying away from Nintendo as a kid and taking school more seriously so I could get a scholarship.

                                                        So I made a plan: I would live my life again, on a personal scale only making changes to try to bestow better habits in myself about physical activity and seriousness of my academic career, and to avoid a certain predator; on a social scale, to be more grateful for the people who were in my life because I know how easily I can lose them; and on a global scale, to do my best to help people.

                                                        This plan led to a final pair of realizations: the first that I was putting way, way more thought into this hypothetical than into my actual life, and that maybe I wouldn't have so many goddamn regrets if I'd been more mindful; and the second that it was probably pretty fucking telling I'd rather think about using magic to make my life better than actually, you know, do the work now to make my life better. Why exactly do I need to go back in time to start treating my body right? Why do I need a wizard to de-age the world to be able to tell my friends what they mean to me?

                                                        It is a humbling discovery, and one that calls for action. I'm soon going to log off and hide out in the back room at the office to do some jumping jacks in privacy, but before I do that, I want to tell everyone reading this that I love you. I don't show that often enough, and I apologize for that not because I'm ashamed of myself (though I am in fact ashamed of myself) but because you are special people who deserve better. I will be around more often, and I will be more attentive to your needs. We may not get along, or even be friends, but I still love you, and thank you for making me feel the way I do.
