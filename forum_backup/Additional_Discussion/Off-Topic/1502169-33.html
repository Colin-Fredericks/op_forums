                                            I do get very frustrated when people conflate nostalgia with things like artistic integrity.

                                            For example, watching superhero movie fatigue hit pop culture pretty hard is not shocking to me, because that's exactly what happened to myself and a lot of my friends that grew up with superhero comics. Superhero comics were never some bastion of creative vision and artistic integrity. It was a world of creator egos fighting over credit whenever something go popular, while editors saturated the markets with nauseating repetition as they kept trying to recapture the spark of whatever the most recent popular (esp. popular beyond normal readers) trend was.

                                            The MCU isn't ruining things... is faithfully adapting the comics in their entirety: including the parts where most people get sick and tired of crossovers, multiverses, resets, spinoffs, and ever expansive lore that makes it harder and harder to know what's going on if you don't start at the beginning and play catch up. But so few people lamenting the downwards trends in superhero films bother thinking about how the source material was (and still is) a huge part of why it all turned out that way.
