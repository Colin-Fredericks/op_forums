                                        note the numbers I mention. A small crew is taking half a decade to build the game at a minimum burn of 4-5 million a year.

                                        So a few million while good for an indy game, will get you a small dev team with a limited scope on the end product. Which is good if it's a product that you just want to get out there, because it's been repeatedly rejected as a big name game or doesn't have enough pull to do it right (Shadowrun, Wasteland 2). If it's a major product with some potential, you generally want to invest big in it and aim it for a broader market.

                                        And MMO games? The problem there is that you're looking at a lot of running costs and a HUGE need for advertising. In fact when it comes to game budgets, that's one of the big lines between the major houses and the indies... the advertising budget.
