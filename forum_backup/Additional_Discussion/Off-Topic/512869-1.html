                                            Originally posted by brjedi26 View Post

                                            Please elaborate.
                                            The pillory/stock is made out of what looks like 2 pieces of 1x6 or 1x8, crude formed and lacking any padding. This is bad because being only 1" thick it puts a lot of pressure on a relatively small cross section. The lack of padding, or at least rounding of hole, tends to make this even more of a pressure point problem. This is in addition to the fact that it makes it decidedly easier to shake and twist, which is trouble and can lead to unintentionally pinched flesh. Additionally, it's held on by a corner bracket and some cheap and simple wing nuts. They do not secure well against vibration (a critical factor) and the corner bracket means you've basically got a dangerously sharp edge on the end posts of the bed. Setting aside the danger presented by a bit of rough and tumble, you'd keep having problems with it stabbing/cutting against feet in the night when you sleep.

                                            Moving on to the frame construction, they have a total of 2 bolts per support holding the canopy support to the frame itself. That means that as the bolts start to slip with age there is nothing but those bolts preventing it from coming loose with vibration/time... which on something that presumably will see activity is less than ideal. The failure of the bed standing a good chance of crushing anyone in the cage is source of concern. There's also the fact that they didn't bother to paint the bolts over when they were done, but that's a minor gripe at best.

                                            The canopy is all one welded piece (I suspect it's a frame that got re-purposed) and while that means it's solid enough, it also likely means it would be used for attachment points... and there are not any. again relatively minor.

                                            The cage itself is primarily composed of what looks like standardized bar units, which is good and I have no particular problem with, except that the cage is either too large or the door to it is too small for emergencies. In the even of an emergency (such as someone passing out or a fire) you need to be able to open the cage and quickly extract them no matter where they are. The door is at most just enough for a small person to squeeze through, making reaching in to extract someone difficult at best.

                                            Final minor gripe is the size of the bed including canopy for the room. It's too tall to readily fit.


                                            Since I don't want to be purely negative...

                                            If I were to do something like this, I'd probably start with an open-top solid cage, get the gas-lift hardware from a storage bed and use it along with a frame and latch to secure the top. So a top-loading cage, easy to get someone out of if the need arises. This has the double advantage that some quick latching wood paneling around the outside would instantly turn it from "bdsm cage" to a a slightly unusually high storage bed. I would do the pillory as a separate unit, that rises on two steel legs made to double latch on each side to the corner support beams of the cage. Rubber layer glued to the connecting surfaces to negate minor vibration and avoid metallic rattling. That way it's solidly anchored enough and yet is fairly easy to remove as needed. padding on the anchor points, 2-3" oak if I could manage it. I probably wouldn't bother with the canopy, but if I was going to do it, I'd probably have it be on it's own legs each with a double-lock over the corner legs of the main cage for security as needed (same set up as with the pillory). I would make the legs angle in slightly so that the canopy was actually a bit smaller than the bed, and make the legs themselves of the collapsible variety out of the same square steel tubing used for the cage. The reason for that is then the canopy can collapse it's legs and be small enough to store in the cage when not in use.

                                            End result? More flexibility, ability to be slightly subtle and ability to only have the features there when you want them, plus the ability to store everything away neatly under the bed when not in use, and overall less risk of problems.... IMO, YMMV, etc.
