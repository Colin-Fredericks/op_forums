                I was socially isolated and thus, as many here have also reported, picked on, since nobody cared. I was isolated because of various reasons.
                It started with me having grown up in a very different country, though I went to a german ambassadorial school it left me unaware of a lot of the social norms. It certainly didn't help at all, that I tried to change that by learning about the country's history. I pretty naively expected knowledge to help, but it merely set me apart even more. I got teased and hit for knowing things they didn't. I was even more of an outsider for having to say I belonged to another religion than was the norm, which I didn't even believe in, which just made it way, way worse. Since I wasn't attending their religious meetings and classes, I was lesser than them.
                Young adults will seemingly always believe the majority belief in class to be correct and as with everything else, punish deviance from the norm.

                During my entire school-life I spent every possible minute in the school library, though after a while I had to bring my own books since I had read everything but the most dry of literature after two years. But it was my only safe place, since I really desperately wanted to avoid home.

                I didn't really resist being hit or kicked, unless it went over a certain point. The instances in which I was actually kicked or hit with force were very few in comparison to the regular, stupid but playful ones.
                One of those instances was a girl that was into me but couldn't say it because of how it would make her look. About three years later she tried to explain to me, in tears. That was weird for someone who'd never been that close to anyone nor could quite grasp why someone would pretend to have been interested in me. Let alone fathom that she was actually sincere. I'm glad to say I decided I'd rather risk being made fun of for being so naive than risk actually hurting someone else emotionally, so I (awkwardly) managed to tell her that I didn't hold her behavior against her. At that point in time she was the only person in my life who smiled when she saw me. I didn't get it at the time, but I wasn't about to question the only bright spot in my life.

                That all only stopped when a group that kept hassling me for years got a chair thrown at them and suddenly were scared shitless of me. I wasn't even close to hitting one of them, but I was never bothered with hits and kicks again. Nor with a friendly smile.

                But I did become the psycho who obviously must be taking lots of drugs guy, which led to merry visits from teachers, police and concerned parents telling me how awful anyone who takes drugs is and thus I am. My classmates weren't bothered enough to not hassle me, but enough to not "playfully" assault me anymore or include me in games, which I had been allowed to participate in, even though I never got played fair, I still preferred it to total isolation.
                It didn't help that a supposed friend, who was the only one who listened to me at all, let me explain the rather complex strategy game I worked out with about 300 pieces of paper during class before joyfully mixing them up and throwing a handful out the window. I had one of my three rage-kinda-blackouts, where things got blurry but I still did remember what happened through a haze. I threw tables aside to get at him and the five boys present as well as one of the stronger girls had to grab onto me, and I still proceeded at a turtle's inevitable but slow pace towards the guy I wanted to really hurt.
                As you can imagine, it just added to my psycho reputation.

                When that idea finally died off, I was just the weird kid and my classmates went over to stealing my shoes and playing keep-away every break. The saddest part about this is that since this was the first time I was included in any kind of game again, I preferred it to not being interacted with, while hating being treated that way at the same time. Which of course, in addition to everything else, made it easy to justify for them, because they could argue (kinda rightly) that I wanted them to play this game with them and that it was totally harmless.
                That's something I'd highly recommend to use. People often end up in abusive relationships because it seems better than total isolation. It's hard to understand why the kid you may be trying to save from being bullied might get mad at you. But if you understand that they can't imagine themselves in a better relationship with people and don't want to lose that last bit of interaction they have outside of their home (which may suck even more) it can be a deliciously warped human thought process everyone gets.

                And that's when I failed class thanks to a teacher who hated me and didn't like being told the books and internet (whatever crazy thing that is) said something different than her, let alone questioning the validity of democracy as the holy grail of humanity.

                That was when I got into a class of nicer people, who gave me a chance. Being so used to being the outcast, I somewhat blew it by being a standoffish ass to a couple of them but was lucky enough to meet four other weird people, who liked fantasy, M:TG and sci-fi. And even rpgs. So I assembled my first gaming group and life kept getting better for quite a while.

                Everything after that really isn't worth telling, though it was beautiful to live through.
                I was being treated normally, with the normal amount of hassling but also with nice things and awkward interactions with girls.

                                                    I find the median high-school experience in America is directly correlated to what the "feel" of the school is. In my high-school experience, teachers were sympathetic but distant, which was symbolic of how the adults in the region I lived in treated their kid's in the time of my youth (generally speaking). The main concern when trouble popped up, for the teachers, was quietly and quickly resolving the situation and putting it behind them, regardless of whether it was satisfactory to the parties involved or not. The few times the police were brought to the school were basically to wag their fingers at the school to tell them to "keep it down". People were rarely expelled and punishments weren't very punitive.

                                                    I was bullied because of apathy on the part of the people in charge, and the lesson I learned from it was very much a basic "Act or be Acted Upon". At the time, the line was "Everyone is free to be who they are..." but always underlined with "...as long as it's socially acceptable and doesn't inconvenience someone". The bullying I received largely stopped when I started throwing punches and nothing was done to stop me, just as nothing was done to stop the people initially throwing punches at me. That's the lesson I learned, and as a result, as an adult, I react very negatively to snark, and "cute" sarcasm and prickishness. Sometimes that makes social situations a little rough, but it did teach me the value of self-agency. Mixed bag result!

                                                    Now, having come to the other side of the experience as a teacher; the feel of a highschool (and schools in general) have very little to do with the students themselves and more with the pervasive attitude and feeling of "safeness" and "connection" the school environment itself entails. This goes for any school of adolescents, in America or in my experiences teaching abroad. So, Zid, my advice to you in portraying teenagers is consider the trickle-down effect of the environment in which they are being taught, then construct your median from there, and then create a few outliers and extreme-reactions to said environment. That will lend an authentic feel to the NPC's, I believe.

                                                    EDIT: Also, play Rockstar's "Bully" for a great pulpy take on lighthearted high-school hijinks which you might be able to transfer into a game.
