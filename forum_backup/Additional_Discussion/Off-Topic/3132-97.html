            I've grown in some pretty rough environments, socially speaking. In El Paso, Texas, kids having insult fights(and actual fights, for that matter) went all out. Nothing was sacred, we tore each other up with some of the foulest things you can call a person. I joined internet communities pretty early on in my life, and the young internet was definitely a sort of Wild West as far as social niceties go. I freely admit to being a god damn troll in my early teenage years. I delivered shit and took it in kind.

            In order to get along in that environment, you have to be willing to ignore things. Words are just words. The one who got riled up lost. The one who got angry, who got hurt, they became the toys of a vicious and cruel mob. You had to learn to not care, maybe even to revel in the things you got called. And I got really good with that. I went a long time being able to drinks in the lasers of scorn and laugh. I dunno if it works like this for everyone, but I forgot what it was like to feel anything when people said something about me. So, when I saw someone complain about what we were dishing, I fucking responded with variants of "If you can't fucking handle it, get the fuck out." It just seemed like people should know what they were getting into, they should be able to step up to the plate. People who didn't, well, they were fucking pathetic and impossible and dumbasses.

            That started changing when a Sonic site I went to basically crashed, and the people migrated to another one called Sonic-Shaded. Now, for the record, me and the old guard of the site that crashed, we fucking hated that site(it was essentially a watered down rip off of our own, made by people who didn't like ours), so we went in to tear it apart. That was....a mixed response. We held a lot of social power, sure. We were the known figures, and we were intelligent and quick and vicious. But we didn't have mod powers, so, you know, they could kick us. We got away with a lot more than we should have, but no one backed down...but they weren't fighting our way. I remember one member in particular, she was called Sapphire. It was quickly apparent, she was super sensitive to everything, and she cried and would get very upset with us. She just would not be able to toughen up to us. THe very model of weakness, you know. Every day, we'd go in, and we'd make her cry(not deliberately, not like a goal, but we certainly felt no loss for her doing so). But despite that, every day, she'd come back, because she believed the site's makers promise that they would make the place a sanctuary.

            Anyways, part of why we could keep staying is that whenever we were about to be kicked, we lightened up and started talking reasonably and rationally and would calmly explain why and what we were doing, and we even lent credence to the the ideas were jerks before springing off on whatever point we let bug us that day. We were good at it, but it required us to take some steps back. It required us to make concessions. And with that sort of stuff going on, and the repetition of people being truly bothered but refusing to leave, twinges of empathy started coming back in.

            THis went on for years, and we did a lot of fucking damage....but despite that, we actually moderately ended up friends and stopped trying to break the community down. We still didn't like the site, still thought it was stupid, but finally just said "What will be, will be", effectively. We even worked with them to start some other RP sites.

            On my mission, one of the big things that happened was that I saw how that willingness to fight by just taking anything and dish out worse had fucked out my social life in reality, even if I was nowhere near as aggressive in my high school as I was in my elementary school or on the internet. I had lived in a world where my own numbness was all that mattered, and now I was seeing how that pushed everyone aside. I loosened up a metric shit ton in those two years. I learned to take offense, to get hurt again, and in the process, people became people again. They were like me-they mattered.

            So, since then, I've made a point to not respond to things that bother me by just toughening up. I don't want to be that unfeeling again. I don't want to push people out by saying "Oh, people are just so sensitive-they need to learn to take things." In fact, that sort of mentality feels a little rape-y to me now, which makes me hella uncomfortable. I want to be a kinder, more compassionate, and more understanding person. I fail a lot, but I'm getting better.

            I don't want to say there isn't value in being able to tough out commentary isn't helpful-we live a community where arguments often help the lifeblood along,and I'm a writer, for god's sake. But I think it's more important when to let the shield up and to let it down. You keep it up too long, and you forget what pain feels like, and you forget to stop and think about what other people feel as a response. Keep it down all the time, and you just become a wreck. There's a balance here.

            But then again, this may not apply as much here. I'm more distant to the Off-Topic community than I used to be. Still, food for thought.

                                                On the radical minority thought, that's generally how humans spin things. the question is what is accepted or rejected.

                                                Let's take the Tea Party movement as an example. When it started, it was a grass roots movement protesting against taxes and government control. Except it had some fringe elements in it. And the fringe elements got the bulk of the attention. The media and attention focused on it, and the majority of the movement just kind of grumbled but stuck with it. Thus it became a decidedly fringe attracting movement. So more fringe elements speaking up for it. And then the organization, even if a large majority of it didn't hold the beliefs of those fringe representatives, are now presented as representing that fringe view. You either declare 'I am not one of *those*!' and get painted as someone who not only flipflopped but is actively opposed to the goals of the original group, or you keep going because the group is doing *some* of what you believe in, and get painted as one of the fringe.

                                                This COULD of course have been prevented, but only if very early on people stood up an said "No, those fringe elements are NOT what we are about!" Except in order to do that, you'd need someone with central authority. Otherwise all you have are two voices shouting at each other. And which one is the media going to focus on, the one that is very different and attention getting from the fringe (aka one that draws a crowd/sales) or the more sensible one? Exactly.


                                                Now replace the one occurrence of "Tea Party" with "Feminism" "Men's Rights" or any other decentralized "Us vs Them" group and it reads exactly the same.

                                                    It doesn't help that feminism, even if not by design, manages to benefit from this lunatic fringe, even if they disagree with it, which can seem disingenuous. Before I get accused of attacking people (not my intent), let me explain.

                                                    Ok, so we have this movement called 'feminism'. It's done a lot of good in the past, and arguably there is still good to be done now. However, while (let's say) 99% of people associated with this movement are good, honest, compassionate people, there is an unfortunate 1% of terribleness who say and do terrible things in the name of this movement. An author portrayed a female character as less than saintly? These 1% flip their lid, call of him to be castrated, create a bunch of lies online (detecting lies online is notoriously difficult outside of very specific fields), call him a rapist, and maybe dox him. A guy asks a somewhat insensitive question? He's treated awfully, insulted, and chased away without being given an answer. Fun stuff.

                                                    Unless the good-99% of the movement specifically decries this - and they're busy people with lives, and often they're just not aware this has happened, so that's not a reasonable expectation - the target and anyone watching who isn't totally familiar sees organised misandry and psychos doing terrible things in the name of feminism. And since 'dragging down men' kind of looks like 'addressing gender inequality' if you squint, turn your head, and give yourself a fair-sized concussion, at least a few will assume that that characterises the movement. God knows nobody is speaking out against it from within feminism, so this must be the movement (yes, this logic is flawed all over the place, but when you feel you're being attacked your logic circuits aren't always firing perfectly). So people stop asking, or writing flawed female characters, and the anger from "feminism" goes away.

                                                    The next day/week/month, they turn on the TV and see feminists (probably the good-99%) saying about how there is gender inequality in society, and how men are 'privileged'. They remember their contact with the lunatic 1%, and think "pah, maybe those feminists aren't themselves doxxing or whatever, but they're certainly benefitting from those that do, and they certainly don't seem to be doing much to stop it. That basically puts them allied."

                                                    So we end up in a situation where totally reasonable factors, combined with the unfortunate 1% lunatic fringe that there is in most movements, leads to the perception of a motte-and-bailey situation, where statements like "there is gender inequality" are thrown around when 'feminism' wants to seem innocuous, and "GAH ALL MEN ARE RAPISTS AND EVIL!!!" is the "true" face of feminism where they seek to dominate men (again, just to be clear: not saying this is reality. Saying this is one potential resultant perception of a group). Essentially, the perception that I'm trying poorly to describe is that feminism cheats: they pretend to be reasonable, but if you disagree then they let loose the mad bitches, and thus even if most feminists don't do the nasty, they don't do much to stop it either, while (seemingly) coming down mega-hard on comparatively innocuous slip-ups.

                                                    Combine that with teh Internets and teh Tumblrz and suchforth, and, well, it's really, really hard to ignore the lunatic 1%, and by dint of the reasonableness of most people, easy to miss the good-99%.

                                                    Which is unfortunate, really, because that ends up helping nobody.

                                                    tl;dr - The lunatic fringe, while not representative of most feminism, does offer it some perceived benefits, and if not specifically shouted down (which is not a reasonable expectation, due to subjectivity and the people having more pressing and productive things to do) it essentially has attack dogs without the responsibility for any damage they do. Subjectivities of perception does not help this situation.
