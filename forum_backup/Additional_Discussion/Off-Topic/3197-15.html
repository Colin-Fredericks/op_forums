                                                            So, before going into Rainbow Rocks, I'm gonna quickly get out my thoughts on the last movie for contrast.

                                                            Equestria Girls was a big, kinda dumb film that was a ton of fun that actually had some good cleverness underneath, although that doesn't stop it from being kinda dumb. I like Sunset Shimmer the way most people like Trixie, finding that some of her sillier or bad decisions to actually be the product of her working with bad circumstances with intelligent reasoning , playing the sure game out of the lot. I thought the secondary theme of the magic of friendship being everywhere and the possibility of Equestria's idealized world being a possibility was clever addressed in world that bridged the audience's reality with the world of the show that capitalized on an iconic time and place that not only served as microcosm for the grand theme but also as the root of the problem. The dull and boring high school tropes are still superficially derivitative, but have life cast into them as it become a game between two arcane chessmasters for the soul of a people, which in a way comments on the power of the show. It's still dumb in a lot of ways(those photos), but with constant challenges and a great sense of fun(admit it, you rocked out to Helping Twilight Win the Crown), the first movie is watchable despite its' flaws.

                                                            So, Rainbow Rocks. Where to begin.

                                                            Well, with the protagonist...Sunset Shimmer.

                                                            Don't get me wrong, Sunset should be the protagonist, and the film knows this. It introduces her conflict quickly and solidly in the first few scenes as we find her still dealing with the fallout of the last movie. She wonders how she can ever move beyond what she's done and earn acceptance from the people she hurt, and as such deals with self doubt, not feeling it's her place to solve things, answer questions, or ask to be involved with things she really wants to, despite being more than capable. This was really good stuff, and the movie could have had a great arc on this. It was all set to!

                                                            And then Twilight showed up.

                                                            The movie knows Sunset is the central protagonist, but it doesn't know how to handle an audience that is used to Twilight as the hero. As such, it tries to lay it out as a story of two concurrent problems, Sunset dealing with her baggage and Twilight finding herself not up to the task of creating a counterspell to defeat the sirens(more on them later). The problem is that Twilight's problem isn't active enough to invest in(more on this later), and it takes time away from Sunset's struggle, making the exploration and resolution weaker. Her entire arc doesn't create an impact because not enough happens.

                                                            Speaking of things not happening, the main problem the Mane Seven deal with is both brilliant and boring. I mean that about the brilliance, by the way. The Dazzlings' plan goes off flawlessly, and it should: the opening argument in Battle is persuasive, and when pushed with magic it's no surprise things get ugly quick. When the Mane Six try to blast them with the magic of friendship and it fails(speaking of, I'm glad the sirens didn't even worry, knowing that without some sort of item it would never go off), it barely takes a twist to turn it and make the riled up school turn on them. From there, the Dazzlings don't have to do jack-the school's antagonism would fuel the pre-existing clash of personalities in the group and from there they'd fall apart on their own. With Twilight locking up in her own inability and Sunset not confident enough to step up, all that's left to do is eat up and keep the engine going.

                                                            But as smart as that is, it's boring. You have one large chunk of the same things happening again and again without much variation. The arguments between the Mane Six never go beyond each one problem for each pairing, never adding new small problems to increase a sense of dynamics or real mounting conflict within the group. They also don't try other things, like trying to steal the obviously important necklaces by following the sirens, or some other approaches to beating or subverting the baddies. They just keep stalling while Twilight flops with the spell and the Dazzlings grip the school more and more.

                                                            Say what you will about the first movie and it constantly solving problems shortly after they're brought up, at least things happened that fueled a sense of conflict and activity.

                                                            The Dazzlings themselves are, in theory, really good antagonists. They act as a foil for Sunset as mighty creatures that have fallen as a result of their cruelty and are now seeking a return to grace, with Sunset seeking a acceptance and a new way of life while the Dazzlings seek to restore what they had in the past. They also serve as a foil for the Mane Six and picture of what's to come if the heroes continue like they are, with each third sharing traits with theMane Six sans Twilight and portraying a group that sticks together for the same goal but more or less despise each other, a dead ringer for where the Rainbooms were headed under Rainbow's lead. They(well, okay, Adagio) are really intelligent as discussed, with a plan that let's them achieve what Discord had to cheat at-absolute disharmony between the main characters. They also sound really good.

                                                            But since all they have to do is sit back and relax, they never engage or feel like a threat, and we're stuck with their mostly bad traits dominating our minds- they feel annoying and lackluster. This sort of thing can work-the witches of Hocus Pocus and the Dazzlings are basically alike, but the witches all do something and they all contribute. The Dazzlings don't really do anything, so it's just empty.

                                                            I'll clarify and wrap up my thoughts later. For now, I'm tired of tapping on my phone and I got most of it out.
