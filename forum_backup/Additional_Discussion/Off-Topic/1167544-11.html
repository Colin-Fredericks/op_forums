                        I finally got out the barf bucket and roll of paper towels, and opened up my window in case I needed a "NOPE" emergency exit, and forced myself to watch it for the first time tonight, already familiar with the arguments for and against, and basically the plot of the entire movie.

                        My takeaway was it was just...dumb. Not good, not bad, just...dumb. It was just so milquetoast and middling of a movie I can't even find space for strong feelings on it. In some ways it was better than people give it credit for, others way, way worse. The one thing I can definitively say, is the people who defend it because it "subverts expectations", or attack critics because they didn't understand Star Wars or the film, are weapons-grade nutters. Basically the beginning and end of any real criticism I can care enough about to cobble together, is the plot and action of the movie contradict its core themes and commentary on the most fundamental levels.

                        And, I say that as someone completely on board with the movie on the conceptual and thematic level. My favorite piece of Star Wars fiction to this day is KotOR2, precisely because it intelligently deconstructs and critiques the nature of good and evil, right and wrong, within the context of the Star Wars universe, and the role of the Jedi and Sith in relation to those concepts of good and evil. Honestly, if I could express one strong emotion about the movie, it's disappointment Johnson couldn't bring that deconstruction and the themes he clearly felt important home, by writing a screenplay consistent with its own internal logic.

                        To use a baseball metaphor...in the OT, Lucas swung for the bleachers and hit two home runs and an infield pop fly triple. The PT was two foul balls and a single. Abrams bunted with TFA, but in TLJ Johnson swung for the fucking parking lot but spun out on the plate and hit the umpire in the face.

                        There is a single shot in the movie I can point to as pretty much the capstone of how tone-deaf, contradictory, and self-defeating the film is. "We don't fight to destroy what we hate, we fight to save what we love!"...while the siege laser is actually firing in the background, and it's the focal point of the shot. When, you know, the entire point of Finn's story arc in TLJ was that after a moment of weakness, Finn experienced the callousness and cruelty that led him to defect from the First Order in the first place from a different perspective, experienced betrayal, found his reasons to fight again, and in the end was about to sacrifice himself...to save the people he loved.

                                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                                        I agree, KOTOR2 did a good job of portraying why the Jedi philosophy is flawed. By seeing the world in terms of black and white, they lost touch with the people they were supposed to be protecting, and that lead to the people losing faith in them, which enabled the Sith to get a foothold in Republic territory.
                                        In my mind it's not even that, it's the question of how does one handle morally grey actors in a universe where good and evil are truly objective and externally enforced by a phenomenon intrinsic and necessary to the established universe. It even hints at the question of whether or not Force-sensitive individuals have, or are capable of, free will. Relative to the role of the Force in TLJ, Johnson seems to be taking a "yay Potentium!" view (even though he, or probably most people outside Lucasfilm Animation*, probably has no idea what that is), but in the context of the Star Wars universe, it just doesn't work. His own film disproves his thesis.

                                        Luke sabers up against Ben and realizes what he's doing, but it's too late. Any Star Wars fan is going to read that as Luke being tempted by the Dark Side, again, which was the catalyst for Ben to embrace the Dark Side. Ben is pushed into increasingly heinous acts by Snooki, each one he believes to be the one that will allow him to cross the threshold and become consumed by the Dark Side, but he's conflicted as his acts were the consequence of Hobson's choices forced upon him by Snooki.

                                        Along comes Rey, and she whoops his ass in a scene every fan who knows their SW lore walked out of the theater saying "she was calling on the Dark Side to beat him". Hell, the novelization made it clear. Even three years ago when the dipshits on the internet were like "buh how Rey swang litesabur guder", I was there to say "she was calling on the Dark Side, dipshit. Pay attention to the movie." Even then, the point was being laid out Kylo is weak because calling on the Dark Side doesn't come naturally to him, whereas Rey is a natural at it.

                                        That's why of all the theories about Rey's parentage, I preferred "she's either a kid or a Rule 63 clone of Palpatine".

                                        Enter TLJ, and Snooki's up to his usual bullshit with Kylo, while Luke goes from "the Jedi Order is shit and I won't teach you" to "you're a Dark-sider, fuck off". Rey's tempted to go to Kylo and try to redeem him, but it was dead obvious from the beginning she was being tempted by the Dark side. She goes to Kylo, Kylo whacks Snooki -- this being the point he embraces the Dark Side, having made the deliberate choice of his own volition to betray and murder his own master -- and one terribly choreographed fight scene later we're expected to buy Rey has turned her back on the Dark Side because she thinks Kylo is a dick.

                                        The only actually clear, consistent message any of this presents, is "Dark Jedi can do good things, too". Well, no shit, Sherlock. The entire point is the road to Hell is paved with good intentions. Plagueis, if I remember right, wanted for no one to suffer the pain and loss of loved ones' deaths. Maul wanted to preserve and honor his culture. Count Dookie wanted to purge the Jedi Order and Republic of corruption and stagnancy and return them to their former grandeur. Anakin wanted to save Padme and their children. Ben wanted to live up to the crushing weight of his family's legacy. As far as the canon characters, Palpatine was the only Dark-sider with purely malicious intent from beginning to end.

                                        The kill-shot on Johnson's one consistent thesis, even separating the "road to hell" aspect, is there's absolutely nothing innovative or subversive there. Even constraining our frame of reference to the cinematic part of the universe, we've already seen a character who calls on the Dark Side to do right, but does not become consumed by it: Mace Windu. And, expanding that frame of reference to the CG series', Ventress (at least, after the Nightsister genocide) is our case example for a character who is unambiguously, unrepentantly dark, but (sometimes?) turns her power to achieve benevolent ends.

                                        What's the logical progression from this one should expect in Episode IX, based on two entire movies of characterization of Rey and Kylo, and be in any way whatsoever internally consistent? Kylo sits around being emo while the First Order consolidates rule over the galaxy, and decides maybe being an evil despot isn't the best way to pass his time just for Rey to show up, kill him and seize power, and declare herself the next Sith lord, that's where. You know, so those kids at the end of Episode VIII can grow up to fight Darth Rey or what the fuck ever money teat Disney plans to brutalize next.

                                        [Let's be honest with ourselves for a minute. Lucasfilm Animation is obviously the Star Wars nerd containment zone within the company as a whole.]
