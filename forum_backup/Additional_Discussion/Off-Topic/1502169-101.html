        Yesterday was a sad day with the death of Hikaru Kurosaki.

        While the Metal Hero pseudo-franchise started in Japan a few years earlier with Gavan and the Uchuu Keiji Trilogy, it reached Brazil with the fourth production, Kyojuu Tokusou Juspion (MegaBeast Investigator Juspion), known here as "O Fantástico Jaspion" (it's a cognate, I don't think it requires translation).

        In Brazil this was one of the biggest television phenomena in history, by comparison it would be like He-Man and Transformers combined. It was the start of our relationship with Japanese media, which was a phenomenon in Brazil more than 10 years before reaching a similar status in the US (and at the time by dubs that actually tried to stay true to the original instead of heavily editing to blend into local standards).

        Kurosaki was the main actor. His death weighs heavily here, more than in Japan proper. Age 64, relatively young, the cause wasn't publicly disclosed.

            Originally posted by Mr Gone View Post
            If there are no physical discs, what the point of a console then? Makes zero sense to me. Might as well have just have a gaming PC with Steam at that point.
            Unfortunately, the driving factor of console sales for ~15 years has been exclusives rather than hardware. While not just the PS3 specifically, the PS3 came out at the tipping point where price, convenience , and playing in person with friends became less important to sales than exclusive video games that you can only get by being in the right console ecosystem. Console prices have steadily been getting closer to PCs, PCs are ubiquitous enough that it's not hard to get a decent gaming PC and set it up as fast as you can setup a console, and multiplayer has largely moved online.

            By most analyses somewhere around 80% of sales of games that are released in both physical and digital formats are purchased digitally only. So that would be a higher number of digital only sales if digital only games were included (like most indies have to be these days).

            Consoles are going to get more expensive, so cutting out parts lets them be cheaper and most customers already by digital only. Do the numbers add up? Well Sony thinks so. So there's logic there even if it's "short term gains for long term chaos," thinking.

            This of course makes me worry a bit about physical media as whole... because I'm not sure what the down stream effects on movies and such will be. Hopefully there are places that still plan on making physical blu-ray (and 4k) discs...
            The only silver lining is that Blu-Ray is licensed by the Blu-Ray Association; which is a consortium of a ton of major industry companies from manufactures to software to film makers.

            One of the things people don't think about when we compared the Blu-Ray vs HD-DVD situation as a "war" is that it included a "peace treaty." Blu-Ray won, but to make sure a small number of companies couldn't hold the new standard hostage against everyone else the BRA was formed so every company could use Blu-Ray tech, software, and branding on fair footing.

            So, Sony can't blow up Blu-Ray media on their own. The question is what everyone else in the industry decides to do. Is Sony the leader, or going rogue?
