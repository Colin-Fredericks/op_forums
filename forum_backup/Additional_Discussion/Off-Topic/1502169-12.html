    Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
    I think the whole "Batman is a fascist"-discourse started with the idea of Bruce Wayne as a billionaire beating up villains and their henchmen and the economic inequality in that relationship in addition to the idea that the solution is said billionaire beating the shit out of them. "Batman is a fascist" is like the dumbest version of that because... it's just a character saying it. And nobody in that universe knows Batman is a billionaire anyway. But taking that bit of deconstruction and worrying about how rich Bruce Wayne is compared to the sorts of people he regularly beats up seems like a common trope to me for writing Batman these days. Of course, you can't just take that discourse and simply have a character say "Batman is bad!" or "Batman is a fascist!". There has to be some sort of storytelling foundation for that.

    I liked the version in Sean Fletcher's White Knight comic where it turns out other rich people were exploiting the destructive results of Batman's exploits to gentrify neighborhoods, for example. Or that Mattson Tomlin limited series "The Imposter" where the existence of Batman is exploited by another person to do some extrajudicial killings. These versions aren't part of the main DC continuity, so there's room to explore some themes.​
    Well, yeah, no one knows he's a billionaire, but "IdSPtmm" Snyder DC Batman's clearly rolling around with military grade tech that he uses to bust up locales and directly assault peoples without authorization, and for a pretty long time has no problem with indirect murder. He's got a whole secret police thing going on that's easy to see as action for action sake, implies excessive violence as an appropriate response against criminal behavior as a treason against society, with that criminal society often being portrayed in the DC Snyderverse as ethnic minorities. His presence is more solidly supported by the middle class in those films, his entire presence creates the nominal notion that criminals are both too strong and too weak (conventional policing can't do anything about them, but Batman is an effective agent with his military grade equipment), there no compasion shown for the criminal populace, and is in fact strongly suggested that it never even crosses Batman's mind, he clearly values violence as an expression of strength and was at one time obsessed with getting a guy to bleed, and wrapping all of that in his signature icon makes it pretty easy to understand Batman as a death cultist. That may not be all of the points from Ur-Fascism, but that's a lot of them.

    ....


    Now that said, a lot of that can just be applied to superheroes in general (and is one of the many points Alan Moore made with Watchmen) that we kind of to shrug off and accept the metaphorical value of it in opposition to it (as I sarcastically put it, sometimes you need to have Jesus club the devil to death with a cross and then say that peace has won the day*), and I don't think that the film creators are interested in that being a deeply held take on Batman, considering that they apparently trust the Flash movie so little to stand on it's own that they had to lean into the fact they're using the Keaton Batman to prop it up.

    Again, for me, the line feels like a admittedly really bad attempt to signal that the DC Cinematic Universe is trying to cut off it's associations with Zak "I dedicated Sucker Punch to my mom" Snyder and his established themes and ideas in the early movies, which is kind of neccesary. Zak "IdSPtmm" Snyder is a fascist (and an idiot who clearly didn't understand Watchmen), and a lot of those early movies has him being very fascist with the characters, world, and overall ideology, and because of that, the franchise has cultivated one kind of audience and has alienated another, where the goal for most of these companies is to chew on as many people as possible. THe line is clumsy, but it's a calculated risk to start working towards getting more of an audience to be loyal to the brand and actually start seeing all the movies for the sake of seeing it-fun for fascists, antifascists, and everyone else in between makes more money.

    None of that actually saves how awkward that is in execution, but I can at least appreciate the logic behind it.

    *Why I say that with my current spicy meatball take on religion is admittedly a mystery to myself, but whatever, the point is that there's a certain level of allegory that most audiences engage with and must be acknowledged when critically thinking about these kinds of stories.

                                    On the status of the Gotham City police: the problem is that they're always whatever the writer/plot says, leading to an incredibly inconsistent portrayal even by comic book standards.

                                    The example I usually use to demonstrate this: Why is the Joker alive?

                                    OK, Batman has his thing about not killing people, and the Joker is considered too insane to stand trial (or get the death penalty).

                                    But consider:

                                    Police that aren't even guilty of corruption in a technical sense have gunned strangers down on the excuse of mistaking a cellphone for a gun. The Joker is infamous for having lots of hidden lethal implements on his person at all times. No jumpy cop has ever shot Joker because the Joker was just going to fix his tie but the cop thought he was about to get sprayed with acid?

                                    The Joker is generally hated by the more traditional organized criminals of the city because he screws with them and their money too. No cop on the take has figure out how to off the Joker on behalf of who ever's paying them? Again, the Joker is a criminal that is a walking lethal threat. The self-defense argument is just too easy to make even if you gunned him down in front of hundreds of witnesses after he's handcuffed.

                                    What, exactly, is stopping such a corrupt police force from killing the Joker?

                                    The simple answer is that they're corrupt in an exceedingly unrealistic way. It's not just hypocrisy, but unexplainable beyond that they're fictional characters that serve authorial intent.
