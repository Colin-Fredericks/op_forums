            Originally posted by Mr Gone View Post
            The rumor I'm hearing is because they're pissed about the upcoming movie or some such.
            Not exactly. Marvel sold the movie rights to many of its properties when it was in bankruptcy, and the production studios used Marvel's weak position to leverage certain considerations. I don't know the exact benefits the studios granted themselves, but they include a piece of the profits of any product (I think non-comic products, so figurines, action figures, posters, shit like that) Marvel makes featuring the licensed characters, because the studios claim part of the characters' fame is based on the exposure granted by the movies. Marvel and Disney want to stop paying those fees and to get the movie rights back, so they are paring down appearances and even canceling titles whose characters are licensed to other movie studios in an attempt to try to reduce the studios' profit until it would no longer be worth it for them to keep paying to renew the licenses.

            I don't know why they aren't doing this to Spider-Man, except to guess that Marvel cannot get rid of their single most beloved and iconic character.

                        Batman: City of Crime might be one of the best Batman stories I have ever read, though it is dark. It features impressive writing and narration, and is excellent at making Gotham City feel like a character of its own, terrible though that character may be. No one sees her like this until it’s too late. Until they have been stabbed, beaten, or raped. Until their hopes and dreams have been ground to dust, their spirits broken, and their thoughts turn black. Until they are hopelessly trapped in the snarled recesses of their own minds—Just one more layer of cankered rot that feeds this great Metropolis known as Gotham city. City of fear. City of crime.

                                                    The Registration Act did make sense.
                                                    Nitro blowing up a few city blocks and killing hundreds of people was a wake up call to the public that there were costumed vigilantes that had zero accountability when it came to innocent death and collateral damage. The public (as it was written) seemed at times blasé or indifferent, or perhaps they saw the damaged done as a price to pay. After Nitro's little act the issue was politicized and the Act was made into law. Had Cap's refusal of leading enforcement of the Act been made public, no one would have seen IM as the good guy. After Cap's assassination, Stark said the Act wasn't worth it.

                                                    Mankind was once an endangered species. It will likely be so again. And mankind will only have itself to blame.
