                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                    I haven't had the chance to play Warlock, but I was under the impression they get to cast an unlimited number of spells. Or that might have just been for their Eldritch Blast.
                    Eldritch Blast is a Cantrip. All cantrips can be cast an unlimited number of times, and all spellcasters get cantrips. As for the rest of their spell slots, they only get 2 for most of the game, though it does increase to 3 in later levels. They recover on a short rest instead of a long rest, which can make up for the extremely limited number of them, but again, that’s highly dependent on the DM. Overall, far from broken.

                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                    As for them being weak, the fiend pact warlocks get Hurl Through Hell, which deals 100 psychic damage to any victim it targets.
                    First of all, it doesn’t deal 100 damage, it deals 10d10 damage. That can theoretically result in 100 damage, but there’s a very, very low probability of that actually happening. The average damage is only 55. And they can do that to one creature. Once per day. At 14th level. For comparison, a 14th level evocation wizard can cast Delayed Blast Fireball using an 8th level spell slot once per day to do 13d6 fire damage plus an extra 1d6 per turn they delay it to a maximum of 10, and use Overchannel to get the maximum roll result, for 78 fire damage (plus 6 for each turn they delayed up to 138) to every creature in a 20 foot radius sphere.

                    Hurl Through Hell looks flashy, but it’s actually pretty bad for a 14th level feature.

                                                Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post


                                                Caster Supremacy is a really tricky idea. How exactly do people define it? Often times people bring different definitions or sentiments to these kind of debates and it can be really tricky to see where and how people might disagree. So when people say they believe caster supremacy is a core part of the game, what exactly do they mean?

                                                Do these people mean that they believe Casters should be more versatile and capable of doing many things martial characters can't? If that's what they mean I'd probably agree. I don't think martial characters should be teleporting across continents, summoning up super-demons, raising castles with a word, creating demi-planes, bringing forth eclipses to temporarily hide the sun, or otherwise manipulating the world like that (at least not without the use of magical items).

                                                And even if a martial character can do one of those things, they probably shouldn't be able to do all of them. I'm okay with martial characters being capable of using magic on a small, limited, fairly personal scale (such as what was presented in the Tome of Nine Swords or Dreamscarred Press' Path of War) but in general martial characters should not have the type of versatility that caster characters should have.

                                                Or by &quot;Caster Supremacy&quot; do they mean Casters should be better than martials at all levels and all areas of the game? I'd definitely disagree with that, if that's what they meant.
                                                If a caster is supposed to be able to do more than a non-caster... why not just drop the non-casters all together? Assuming the balance that almost no edition has never had, the Fighter is good at combat, and the Wizard is good at everything else - and in most editions, the Wizard is better in combat, too!

                                                I’d sooner see a game with everyone as casters than ever hear someone argue that some class should just be better than another.

                                                    At the end of the day, D&D is a team game, so if one PC has more options than another, that's not necessarily a problem as long as everyone is having fun.                                                         I'm with AnubisXy on this. As I mentioned in the Shadowrun thread, I never get the complaint about caster supremacy (at least the kind of supremacy described as having more tools and, essentially, reality-bending powers) in either game, because well, magic. Magic is just more versatile, than a sword and any martial training. Wizards, played smartly, will be very powerful and will have access to tools mundane classes don't. However, they have their downsides and have finite resources and are lacking in mundane skills.

                                                        The two way I've seen this countered is either the 4e way, as in everyone could learn magic and "dedicated magic user" just means combat caster (and everyone working along the same lines), or games, where indeed, everyone is a magic-user. Earthdawn, for example, or a Mage-only WoD/CofD game.

                                                        Or, just make magic extremely limited and dangerous, like in CoC.

                                                        In every other instance, yeah, magic will be extremely powerfull in the hands of a high-level character and a skillful player. It's just the nature of the thing, really. Also, getting heady of all that power and forgetting, that they aren't invincible and will run out of spell slots, or mana, or accummulate too much paradox, or biting more drain that they could swallow and consequently, getting beaten to pulp by a fighter, whisked away to a prison-realm, or just starting to bleed from every orifice is a dynamic I like. I don't need total mechanical balance, I'm okay with meta-balance.
