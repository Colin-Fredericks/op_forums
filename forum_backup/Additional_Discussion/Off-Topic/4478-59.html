    No. But not far off.
    The Golden Age of Islam centered on Baghdad and began in the 8th-9th century and began declining with the Crusades (11th century really) but got really nailed from the east by Mongols when they sacked Baghdad. During that time however the Kaliphs were united, and independent reasoning and science were kept separated from religious doctrine. (The Mongols were a terror)

    The 13th century saw the rise of the Ottoman Empire and it became a powerhouse after the 15th century.
    Suleiman the Magnificent oversaw the Golden Age of the Empire, but after his passing clerical factions began making more noise, and religious conservatism (as opposed to religious progressivism) began stifling thought, but the reforms brought about by Suleiman would last several more centuries.

    Mankind was once an endangered species. It will likely be so again. And mankind will only have itself to blame.

                                                    I've been taking care of stroke patients for the past week.

                                                    The actual work isn't very strenuous. There aren't a lot of people on the ward at any given time, and by nature of having had a stroke, they're pretty disconnected from the world around them - so most spend the day sleeping. When you see someone lucid, though...it's frightening seeing the deficits people have in the aftermath. Troubles with naming objects, moving their mouths to speak, comprehending what's said to them. You don't want to think about something like that eventually happening to you or the people you care about.

                                                    In an ugly, morbid way it's also comforting though - because they don't seem to be totally aware of their condition. Which is a mercy, I guess.
