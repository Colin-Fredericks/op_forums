    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    I don't think that's a Numenor specific thing so much as it is being a descendant of kings thing.
    I'm not really sure these are separable for the LotR. Aragorn was almost 90 when he became king (so he was already long lived before joining the Fellowship and getting rewarded for all that). The only reason he had the chance to do so many awesome things to prove his worth beyond his ancestry was because of his Numenorean blood (on top of some of the deeds also requiring that to work regardless of his age).

    A lot of proving himself worthy to the nobles of Gondor was proving himself a worth successor of the Numenorean legacy. Numenor might be the past, but it's cultural impact is still vital to the image of the kingdoms of the Westrons. Just like ancient Athens and the Roman Republic are long gone, we still cling to their legacies as part of the cultural mythology that creates the social attachment to western democracy. And while appreciating our history and legacy is important and can be very educational, it's also easily twisted to ugly means.

    That does raise some interesting questions about the absence of much overt religion in Tolkien's work as a whole (even in actual Numenor, the most religion they ever seemed to have was a single annual thanksgiving ritual conducted by the king towards God), but that's getting far too much more off track.
    I'm not sure it's that interesting: Tolkien wanted Middle-Earth to appeal to a wide audience and didn't want to make a religion that was too specific, and as an academic he was well aware that religion worked a lot differently in the past. "Going to church every Sunday," is a fairly recent invention in the history of religion, compared to very personal and private spirituality combined with seasonal major ruler-class lead community rituals.

    I think the rest of what you're saying does get back towards the valid direction of the problematic implications of Tolkien's depiction of race, I just don't think it carries the specifics of the specific subset of white people having a textual anointed right of authority over all other people.
    Again though, audience experience colors interpretation. I hear, on a fairly regular basis, about how white Christian men should be the only default citizens of the US and everyone else should only have a say in the rules if we prove ourselves loyal enough to earn a seat at their table. Their reasoning sounds a lot like everyone explaining why Middle Earth needed Aragorn to become king instead of just getting rid of monarchs and everyone just becoming like the hobbits instead.

                        As someone that doesn't think Death of the Author is an "ideal" to strive for, and is only useful as a partial examination of any given work, I have quite a large list of media creators I refuse to separate from their art.

                        Woody Allen is a specific example mostly because Manhattan left me stunned at how much people were willing to overlook the horrifically disturbing plot being treated as a character-study comedy even before people starting believing Mia Farrow more about how badly Allen acted in his private life. People were shocked that a guy that wrote a story where a character that's basically him - a 42 year old - is dating a 17 year old, and everyone that knows treats it like a midlife crisis where instead of buying a fancy car he's dating a girl that he can't leave NY with because he'd end up breaking the law in those states (and federal trafficking laws)... and then he cheated on his long-time partner (with whom he had shared custody of some of her children) with her daughter, with an even wider age gap than his fictional counterpart... and he still had people that defend him and people that actively want to work with him because the "art" is worth it? Nah. There's more than a enough good art to engage with that doesn't involve lessening yourself to indulge at best an extremely creepy man.

                        There is this very weird phenomenon where artists can basically get away with being horrible people by making art about themselves; the desire to separate the art of the artist always seems to come up as a primary justification for why either nobody paid any attention or worse nobody believed victims despite said artists telling on themselves. Woody Allen's career was an eye opener to me about how bad that was back in the 90s, and in 2024 I don't see all that much having changed even with the impact of MeToo leading to more and more influential people in the entertainment industry being revealed for their atrocious behavior.

                                            Ever wonder how the world would fare if suddenly all music and art were to suddenly be packed up and redacted from public consumption? All the images people sift through online, all the music from recording artists both mainstream and amateur, all the sculpture, the graphic design that goes into ads, the fashions, the movies, all suddenly just halted? I only wonder because it doesn’t really seem like the world actually gives a damn about any of that. Rather it takes these things for granted to the point it can’t even properly gauge their value. People mostly just pirate music and images online, and even if they don’t, AI programs certainly do now. I wonder how long it’ll be before all these things just dissolve away into bland, rehashed, AI-driven garbage.


                                            The only time you hear about any kind of fine art pieces in media are when they’re being wrecked, or when they’re found as part of some rich bastard’s stash, or a criminal’s attempt to launder money. If you’re a musician and you aren’t topping charts then you’re just making noise as far as anyone else around you seems to care.

                                                While a lot of artists are going to suffer, I think in the end this current period of devaluing artists to maximize profits is definitely going to result of a morass of "AI" slop that will eventually have people desperate for human made art; esp, independent art that doesn't go through corporate hands.

                                                Part of the problem is that gauging the value of art is impossible. Art doesn't have intrinsic economic value (sorta by definition). Art tends to flourish when people have enough free time that creative people can earn a living and have plenty of time and energy to make art as well (removing the need for value or wide-scale public affirmation to factor into why an artists decides to make something), or society values art enough that "base line" art that is bland but nice enough to purchase is still assigned enough aesthetic value to price at a sustainable point.

                                                As society is currently in the process of thoroughly trashing the ability of artists to make a living be society decided art is valuable, our hope for the future of art is more in independent and folk art traditions.
