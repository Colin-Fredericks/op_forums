            Originally posted by Shock View Post

            1.Supreme court affirmed that the second amendment was about firearms and thus easy access of firearms from secondary markets.

            2.Stigmatization of mental health except when used for political scapegoating.

            3. Massive demand for drugs throughout society. Less of an issue now but that's more of the low level "This is not news" shootouts that happen in the ghettos. That has been dropping except in certain places.

            4. Mass media spectacle when someplace where middle to upper class people get shot, leads to infamy which in turn, creates more copycats.
            I would also venture a couple other points...

            I would strongly suspect that the massive demand for drugs in the poorer areas is as a form of escapism. (the potholes/safety net bit you mention).

            To make it clear for people not in the US, in most places in the US, you can work 12 hour days 6 days a week at minimum or near minimum wage and still require assistance in the form of food banks for the poor in order to make basic ends meet. And this is assuming no expensive tobacco or drinking habits, with 2 people living in a single bedroom apartment.

            About 1 in 7 people of the US population live in a situation where at least once in the past year they couldn't afford to feed themselves/family/children. And those 40+ million people are almost inevitably going to be the poorest of the nation.

            Now amazingly in the poorer areas it's very *easy* to get sucked up into doing something criminal. It looks like it's one of the few ways out into a life that doesn't suck, even with the risks. So you get a lot of desperate people, crime looks good, and then... well then you get a lot of crime. Which invariably results in people either dying or going to jail.

            Which then feeds into the US prison system, which is a whole rant on it's own, but suffice to say after a relatively short time people are back out, and discover that they have even WORSE prospects than before, less support, can't get a regular job (because yay, criminal record), so things are even worse for them.



            Now on the gun thing. And I fully accept that what I'm about to say based on my opinion may not be popular but it's an opinion. America largely enshrines the second amendment, and is on the verge of a sort of passive worship of guns. Which in itself isn't bad. The bad part is that with that obsession, it ALSO flatly ignores responsible ownership or safety. Yes there are safety courses available, but they're in no way required. The threshold for being a gun owner is largely having a pulse and the ability to pay. As you can tell from my name, I'm not from the US (live here at the moment though) and even I, coming from a socialist country lacking the gun obsession, have had more pubic school required* gun safety training than most americans.

            There's no way to take guns away from americans. Cultural issues aside, legal issues aside, the US is one of the weapons manufacturers to the world, and a famous one at that. It would be like trying to ban wine in France, sausage in Germany or sheep jokes about Wales in England, you can't ban a product where it's manufactured and promoted successfully, it will get out. Nobody except people that are operating on ideals considers it viable.


            So yeah, you end up in this situation where the US has deliberately or otherwise, engineered the society so that a lot of it is poor, desperate, and all of it has ready access to weapons.


            *-Strictly speaking it wasn't mandatory, it was a required part of an optional class, the alternative being Arts&Crafts in ... 8th grade IIRC

                                        The US has always loved an economic underclass, and why shouldn't it? It allows the wealthy more power. Way back in the day it was slavery.                                         Point of amused interest. In the early 1940's it was predicted with all due seriousness (and no major objections) that by 2020 the US would have universal health care, and that even the lowest of jobs would earn enough that with a 30 hour work week a single breadwinner would be able to provide comfortably for a family.

                                        Now unfortunately it's gotten that the underclass is both very large, with the options to advance up into a more middle class have shrunk. So more of the population is poor, and with shrinking options for getting out through work. At the same time education costs have skyrocketed, so that's other advancement gateway drastically shrunk.

                                        Which all might sound good and just on the surface, but... the simple fact is that the US GDP has been growing for the last 40 years. The GDP per capita (effectively how much money or value per person is generated per year) is also going up significantly over the last 40 years. The adjusted for inflation income per capita has been DECREASING for the last 40 years. So the COUNTRY is making more money, the people are making less money.

                                        Adjusted for inflation, the US minimum wage is now Lower than when the minimum wage was first introduced.


                                        Ultimately, it's still the same america. It's just going through some rough times, that honestly I mostly put on the culture that came out of the late 70's and 80's, with a business focus over a social focus.
