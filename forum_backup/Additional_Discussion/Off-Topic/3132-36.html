                        There's something about lines in my life.

                        I dunno, maybe it's just because the logic is really simple, the elegant justice of the act on a very basic level, but the entire protocol of standing in line is more true to me than death and taxes. Your place in this little system is determined by your arrival- you go to the end, work your way forward by virtue of others resolving their affair. You can be generous and offer your position as their need calls for more immediate resolution, you can be a little sleazy and hold places for people(Before you, but after the person in front of you), but you can't cut place unless the officials of the line give you permission. It doesn't matter if you're in line to deal with a major crisis or if you don't even know if this is the place that can help you, you play the game. It can be frustrating, sure, and it's not really fair in a higher sense, but it's simple and utilitarian-it's virtues are readily apparent. I've stood in lines for ages, giving up places to people with more solid concerns than mine, only to get to the front and be told I need to go to a different line in a different building to deal with my problem, and I walk away generally feeling very one with the world, or at least the idea that shit happens and there's not much you can do about it sometimes except accept it.

                        Maybe it's because of that that most fights I had as a youth had to do with line cutters.

                        So, the story.

                        After my first two classes for today I made a beeline(with a slight deviation because bees get confused by stuff, and sadly it wasn't a breathtaking woman but simple stupidity) for the Financial Aid Offices-I had a VA concern that I had been stupidly forgetting to deal with all month that I needed in before July, which is fast approaching. I made it there and waited in line for a bit, got to the front no problem, and I just had to wait for the guy in front of me.

                        In comes this guy with twine wrapped headphones, and he goes to the end of the line(of two)...initially. I'm about to compliment him on his wrappings(to his credit, it looked really nice), when I noticed that he was sidling forward, until he was standing about level with me, a look in his eyes saying he was going to jump for it.

                        Well, that's not unusual. Lots of people try and sneak forward and get ahead. It's an easy temptation to give in to, and usually remedied by the embarrassment of being called out on it. I, very proudly might I add, have moved beyond the point of elbowing such people in the face, and instead just went "Hey. You need to wait your turn at the back of the line".

                        "I don't need to stand in line."

                        Uh huh. No, here you are shifting around uncomfortably with a question in your eyes; you aren't staff, which means you're on the line with the rest of us bait. "Uh, yeah, you do."

                        "I just need a question answered."

                        Like what happened to my hand I thought wryly(and that thought is completely unrelated to anything). "Doesn't matter, you need to go to the end of the line. We'll be quick."

                        "I'm not even sure I need to be here, so I don't need to wait in line"

                        I tried applying that logic to some other scenarios and ended up having a brief philosophical yelling match with myself about the relation of the grand point of life and what lines had to do with that, all while my mouth went on autopilot, pointing out the obvious. "Well then, you can figure that out at the end of the line, and if it's not answered by the time they get to you, they'll let you know."

                        "Dude, calm down, no need to get angry, I just don't need to stand in line."

                        I juxtaposed this scene with that one time I literally started peeling the skin off of a kid's neck and came to the reasonable conclusion. "I'm not even angry, I'm just telling you you need to wait at the end of the line."

                        I hadn't experienced anything like this since the freshman year of high school, and even that didn't really erupt into anything, as an aside. I was dumbfounded that someone in college could not figure this out.

                        Anyways, at this point, one of the secretaries stepped in with a smile and asked him what his concern was. I shut up and back off-he has been granted early access by the angels of the holy line system, I don't argue. He fumbles around a little bit with his words.

                        "Forget it, I don't want to ask while this guy is being all angry here"

                        Well now I'm angry. You come in here, fucking cut to the front, and when given the opportunity to get dealt with, you leave because you think I'm angry, and thus waste all of our time? What. The. Fuck.

                        He walked his weasely ass out, and as the secretary finished with the person she was working with, I indulged. I poked my head out of the office and gave a very hearty-and loud-"GO FUCK YOURSELF" at his backside. There was some alarm and notices of disapproval, but it was over in a second, and then we went about our business. Me and the guy behind me were done in about fifteen seconds.

                        I hate people like that. I haven't encountered anything like that in ages. I really don't see how that can happen these days in this locale-particularly with that attitude. I would have appreciated more if he was just an asshole cutting because he thought he was big enough to keep people from arguing, but no, this guy thought his confusion gave him benefits.

                        I just- I hate people like that.
