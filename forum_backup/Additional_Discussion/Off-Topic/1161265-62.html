                                    13th age is very much not my game, but its “skill” system is one of the best I’ve seen.

                                    As with 3.X, you get a certain number of points to assign based on your class, and gain more as you level up. But instead of a list of skills you assign these points to, you have backgrounds, which can be pretty much anything. When I was fiddling around with the system, I made a Rogue with I think +3 or +4 each in Tinker, Tailor, Soldier, Sailor, Rich Man, Poor Man, and Beggar Man. There was no list I chose these backgrounds from, I just thought it would be cool to reference the poem, so I decided those would be my character’s backgrounds.

                                    When you make a check, if something you learned from one of your backgrounds would be helpful, you add your bonus from that background. The game gives players a lot of narrative power with this system, allowing you to tell a story about how your background as a (whatever) prepared you to be good at (whatever). The example it gives is a character needing to make a Constitution check to survive overnight in the cold could add his bonus from his Assassin background by explaining how training to be an assassin required a great deal of waiting perfectly still in one location, which often meant needing to withstand harsh weather conditions.

                                    I have mixed feelings about the narrative control aspect - it’s great for some players and groups, not so great for others. And I do feel like interrupting the current action to tell a story about the obscure thing that happened when you were a baker totally helps you on this Strength check to swim could get a bit intrusive on the pacing of the story at hand, or lead to arguments at the table. But despite those reservations I think it’s a fantastic skill system. I sometimes adapt it for 5th Edition. I just rip out the Skill list and say you can apply your Proficiency Bonus to any check your Background would be applicable to. I kind of like it because people lean all sorts of things in their lives; too many to put in a list.
