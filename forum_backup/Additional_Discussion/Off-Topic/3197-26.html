                                            S5E12 - Amending Fences

                                            Anyone who has read my semi-regular posts on the different episodes will notice that I often cut the show a lot of slack. If there is something I like in an episode, I often let that colour my opinion of it and the verdict comes out mostly positive. Only rarely do I hate on any single episode (but looking at you, Putting Your Hoof Down). Sometimes I wish I did that more often, though, because it means that when something truly awesome comes around, my praises will ring just a bit more hollow. And Amending Fences really did it for me, let me tell you.

                                            My first feeling when watching the end credits roll, even before thinking about how great this episode had been, was "this is almost the exact opposite of Party Pooped". In Party Pooped, the plot was started by visitors from a place we've never heard of before and never got any real information about, coming in and making demands in a (fittingly) cartoonish way that didn't even make them one-dimensional as much as one-functional; they really only existed to perform the action of disapproving with subsequent wrecking of stuff and make empty threats with no credibility (did anyone really think a war threat would ever actually be realised in MLP?). It then prompted a much better journey-section which was funny, but as all Pinkie Pies antics it will never be referenced again. Even the party archives (is that what we're calling them?) will likely not show up again and if they do, they'll only reinforce a characteristic we have already accepted in Pinkie Pie. The whole episode was a parenthesis. It might as well have never happened in the greater scope of the integrity of the show.

                                            Amending Fences, on the contrary, provides us with a look at, a reaction to and a development of a part of Twilight's life that we have known must be there but which has never really been mentioned before now. The fact that the plot of the episode comes from the character, and not from outside of her, is probably the thing that carries the episode hardest. This is natural, a conflict that has grown out of the character's situation organically, and it feels really important as a result. Yaks, not so much.

                                            On a more detailed level, what did I like about this episode? Well, as I said, it showed the Princess of Friendship dealing with the consequences of her choices earlier in life. It also puts into sharp focus her own development, what has changed in her and what hasn't. Moondancer becomes a dark reflection of Twilight, an image of a path not taken, but despite that they're more similar than different. That in turn becomes a potent message of how your path is not laid out for you. Moondancer and Twilight are very alike, but they ended up in very different places. It challenges the stereotype of the asocial bookworm by showing a bookworm who has lots of friends, without losing her bookishness or drastically changing her personality. Twilight isn't Pinkie Pie, who is super social and has lots of friends. She's not Applejack, who has a big family and have to stay friends with all of them. She's an introvert, bookish nerd, and yet she is the Princess of Friendship.

                                            The callback to episode one was of course great as well, and I really loved that they included Minuette as a speaking role. We also got new info that Lyra and Twilight used to be friends. It ties back into the previous point that the Princess of Friendship would forget her friends' names, and I think it was a nice touch which was fortunately not pushed further than it could handle. Overall, the three old friends were very likeable, down to earth and believable, and for a good while, I thought the moral would be "get over yourself, Twilight, you're not the center of the universe". But when it turned out that Twilight might actually have influenced Moondancer by her not attending her party, it kind of made sense as well. Now, I should say, this here is probably the weakest point in the episode. It's not 100% clear why Twilight in particular was so important to Moondancer, especially when she clearly was better friends with the other three. It could be argued that she would likely have kept hanging out with the other ones at least, even if the grew resentful of Twilight. But as they say, reality has the edge over fiction in that it doesn't have to convince you it's real. I can imagine someone nervously trying her hand at something for the first time, and then breaking down when it fell through; the minds of people are irrational and emotional things. It's also probable that Moondancer felt like Twilight was the most like her, and would have liked the get closer to her more than the others, and when it failed she felt rejected and despaired and grew bitter. I digress, though. The whole dynamic between the characters was splendid.

                                            And that's really all there was! No need for a contrived plot about pleasing dumb yaks. Just characters interacting, creating intrigue as they clash. This episode was top notch. Best of the season? So far, absolutely. Best of the show? Can't say, but it's certainly up there in the running.

                                                    I disagree with Weimann and here's why.

                                                    I think that this is an okay episode. I think that it has implications which the writers did not totally think through and never intended. I think that with minor adjustments, this could be a great episode; one of the best in the series.

                                                    Twilight had several friends back in Canterlot. Most of them moved on with their lives and were totally okay with her leaving, even if they thought that the circumstances were a little strange. One of them did not. She was completely and totally broken by Twilight moving away. I think that the startling implication of this is that you are completely responsible for the mental health and well-being of your friends, and since their lives are so totally and completely in your control, you must be ever mindful of what you do. If your friend breaks down, it's your fault and you should have done better. This personally rubs me the wrong way because I have been Moondance. I had a friend on whom I was totally dependant. They disappointed me and I was broken into small, scared little pieces. I have, however, been able to recover and put myself back together. I am the better for it. Moondance had the first part of this happen: she was totally dependant on Twilight and when Twilight disappointed her she was broken. She, however, is lacking the second part. The only way that she was able to recover herself is when Twilight came and put her back together. This is not going to make her a stronger pony. If anything she's going to be worse off for it than she was before. Now she knows that if she ever is completely broken by Twilight again, she doesn't have to worry about it because Twilight will come and make things better eventually.

                                                    In addition to the detrimental effect that this will have on Moondance's well-being, this also puts too much of a burden on Twilight. She's now completely responsible for Moondance's well-being. If Moondance is upset, it is Twilight's duty to put her back together. This puts far too much of a burden on her; nopony can be that responsible for the well-being of another. It is exhausting and eventually we all fail, regardless of intentions. We have someone for whom we are totally responsible: ourselves. That's enough responsibility for anypony. Having to be responsible for somepony else is an unbearable burden. (And we could get into being responsible for children, but I don't really think that's necessary to get into.)

                                                    I think that the biggest implication that the writers didn't think fully through is that this episode really says extroversion is good, and introversion is bad. While it is eventually made clear that Moondance is not a healthy introvert, not everypony who just doesn't feel like hanging out is unhealthy. Sometimes for introverts, there can just be too much social interaction. That happened for me this weekend. Since it was a long weekend, we played an extra session of Mouse Guard on Thursday night. Then we played our normal session on Friday. Saturday we had a BBQ for Independence Day. After that, the people hung around and played games. For hours. And hours. It was too much social interaction for me for one weekend. I had to leave. It actually was a little overwhelming and caused me some anxiety later in the evening. I think that, if one of Twilight's old friends was an introvert, but not someone who was unhealthy like Moondance, that the episode would have been increased in quality at least threefold.
                                                    It's not necessarily a bad episode, not like the Yak one. But it treated a complex situation with perhaps too much simplicity. I know, theoretically it's a TV show directed at prepubescent girls and children are not capable of understanding the same depth of complexity that adults are sometimes, but 1) I think that sometimes we give children too little credit and they understand things better than we give them credit for and, 2) if you think that you have to dumb down the show this much and dilute a complex situation like this to this degree, maybe you shouldn't have tried to address it in the first place. Friendship is magic (bitches) and everybody needs friends. And you should try to be a good friend. Good messages. But the representation here is too simplistic for what they were trying to address.
