            This actually begs a question that I always wonder whenever I see people posting about these sorts of issues on a hobby forum. If people feel comfortable saying, does tabletop roleplaying aid you in dealing with whatever you suffer from? Do you use it as a tool to explore your issues, or as something to help escape from them? Is your enjoyment and experience of roleplaying disconnected from your issues?

            I'm certain there's some very in depth examples of using tabletop roleplay to treat issues, but I'm curious about individual experiences. I've noticed that as I've become more depressed and retreated from people around me, I've spent a greater amount of time working on preparing game material for sessions that I'm frankly not going to be running until weeks in the future.

            I've also noticed that while I have a tendency to play characters of both genders with an even distribution, I really want to play female characters more than I do now, but I feel immensely self-conscious about it, as I've roleplayed a lot in other mediums and been very not happy with the way men have portrayed female characters, and I desperately don't want to be labeled as one of them, those people who play those characters for the sake of titillation.

            I'll also admit, as terrifying as it is to admit it (I haven't really written about this to anyone anywhere) there's a tiny voice in the back of my head suggesting that my reasoning for playing these characters has to do with dissatisfaction with my own gender. The thought terrifies me, because I'm utterly unprepared about how to deal with it. I'm fortunate that I have family that's really cool about issues of identity, it's entirely my own thoughts that constrain me from dealing with the issue in a broader context. I am... okay, being male, if that makes sense. But I think if I had a choice, I would be female. I look at myself in the mirror and while I am... okay with what I see, I like to imagine what I'd be like if I were different. I feel like I would be happier. Is that wrong? I don't feel wrong in my body, so much as I feel like I'd be more right in a different one.

            ... Which I guess describes feeling wrong? Anyway, I sit there thinking about that, thinking about how I play my characters. I'm not going to do anything that makes anybody uncomfortable, but should it make others uncomfortable that there is an element of exploration in my playing characters of differing gender? For added complication, I play tabletop with my girlfriend, who I love very much. I haven't discussed these feelings with her at all, because I see no sense in rocking the boat, and doubt I'll ever have the opportunity to act on them to my satisfaction.

            I also have to admit sexuality is also a component of that. In the past I've treated myself as firmly heterosexual. I've only had sex with women, not men. The thought of me actually having sex with men makes me distinctly uncomfortable, but the thought of men having sex in general? It's a thing I think about in a pleasant context. So I don't know what this means for me. It almost feels useless, like I might as well identify as heterosexual because nothing will ever come of it. That's a thought that upsets me, but I don't know how to meaningfully talk about it with anyone.

            For added weirdness (as in the "what the hell is wrong with you, Leet?" kind) my twin brother openly identifies as bisexual, I love him to absolute death and it makes me happy that he's figured that out and is comfortable identifying as that. I don't think I'd be considered bi and I feel super uncomfortable identifying myself as anything other than hetero to family and friends. There's absolutely no reason to feel that way, given their track record with my brother and in general. In fact, I'm sure they've seen things about the likelihood of twins sharing or having similar sexual orientations. But I feel like I can't say anything, because, get this, I don't want to steal what I feel is 'my brother's thunder.' How screwed up is that? I really don't like myself for thinking that way.

            I apologize for the oversharing. It's on my mind. As is my original question!

            EDIT: After posting this, I'm starting to get an intangible feeling of dread. Some friends of mine frequent particular forums on these boards and I absolutely dread the thought of any of them reading this. But I don't want to delete it either, because it feels pretty tremendous for me to have finally said some of this stuff out loud somewhere.

            But god. The terror. White hot fear.

            If they see it, it's my fault for putting it in public. I just hope they don't.

                        Your experiences sound very similar to mine, and the feelings I got when playing an opposite-sex character in tabletop RPGs and video games were a big part of what led me to the idea that I might be transgender. I'm not going to tell you that the same is true for you. Only you can decide what, if anything, this means for you. But I will tell you something others told me when I started exploring and asking questions - there is no wrong way to be who you are.

                        Maybe you'll decide, as I did, to seek out a therapist and talk through whether you might be transgender (and if so whether you should seek a gender transition through hormones and/or surgeries.) Maybe you'll decide that you want to experiment with crossdressing (either behind closed doors or in public), or that you'd like to adopt some stereotypically feminine habits like wearing your hair longer, incorporating jewelry into your wardrobe, or putting on makeup in the mornings. Maybe you just like playing female characters and want to do so without fearing judgment.

                        I also want to say that whatever you decide this means for you, and whatever you decide to do, none of this has to have anything to do with your sexuality. Being more comfortable as a female doesn't mean that you have to want sex with men. There have been plenty of people with feelings much like yours, and while many of them do enjoy sex with men (or taking on a receptive role as part of sex, e.g. pegging), there are also many who don't. You can be a trans girl, or dress in girls' clothes, and still be sexually attracted exclusively to women. Lesbianism isn't restricted to girls who've been designated female since birth.
