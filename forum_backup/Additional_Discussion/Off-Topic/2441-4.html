            I really like Hajime. I like how she's kind of flighty and quirky, but also insightful and well-meaning, how she's not in awe of characters who she should be subordinate to or are kind of godlike, but in a wholly good-natured and friendly way rather than some kind of directionless defiance, and how she keeps questioning the premise as it's presented to her rather than just blindly following along with it, and trying to reach out to the things she's on the opposite side of.

            Within the first three episodes, I was really won over by the way that she was pleasantly insistent on being told exactly why they need to fight these aliens, and a few episodes later when she asks exactly why Gatchaman need to be kept secret, just because I'd usually feel a sense of dissatisfaction of how many similar characters either wouldn't bring it up or accept the first answer they're given.

            Like, the social media angle was the thing that shook me of my first episode notion that the show had nothing to distinguish itself, but Hajime's a big part of why I'm actually invested.

                One thing I really liked about Gatchaman was Rui.

                Minor spoilers ahead (though nothing plot related.

                Normally anime has a pretty poor track record with transgendered or transvestite characters. Usually they're only there for comic relief, and have a five o'clock shadow and a feather boa. I liked how with Rui, the fact that she was a guy who wore girls clothing wasn't a thing. Nobody really mentioned it, thought about it or cared. It's not often you see that in anime, and that, plus Hajime's attitude, really won me over to the show.
