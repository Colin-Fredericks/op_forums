                                    So, after putting the numbers into a spread sheet, what I’m finding is that, no matter the target’s AC or the attacker’s Strength, there is almost no difference in expected damage between using a single 1d8 weapon and dual wielding a 1d8 and a 1d6 Nimble. With only one attack, your damage output is (obviously) identical. Wether it’s better to make the second attack with the d8 or the d6 Nimble varies a bit depending on the target’s AC, but generally speaking it’s usually better to attack with the d8 a second time, with the d6 Nimble pulling ahead when the target's AC is in the mid range, though either way it makes a very, very small difference. The third attack is almost always better to make with the d6 Nimble weapon, but usually doesn’t improve your expected damage by more than 1. Using only one 1d6 Nimble weapon is always weaker than using a single 1d8 weapon, but again, usually not by more than 1 damage no matter the target’s AC or number of attacks you make, and sometimes by less than 1. A two-handed 2d6 weapon is MUCH better than any of the above at all ACs and all numbers of attacks, but that’s no surprise. The difference gets smaller the higher the target’s AC, which is also not surprising.

                                    Of course, I did all of this assuming nonmagical weapons and no additional sources of damage like Feats or Sneak Attack.

                                    What all this tells me is that a 2-handed weapon is the clear best choice if you want to do as much damage as possible per hit. If you have a lot extra damage that triggers on a hit, then it can be worth using a Nimble weapon instead, especially against enemies with high AC. If the added defensive value of a shield is worth the significant tradeoff in terms of damage vs. using a two-hander, then losing it will absolutely not be worth the minor increase in damage you’d get by trading out that shield for a nimble weapon. It’s probably safe to assume that there will be Feats that make dual-wielding a viable option for those who choose to specialize in it, but for everyone else, dual-wielding will just be the backup option for if their shield breaks, or if they don’t have Proficiency with shields or 2-handers.

                                    Which, honestly, seems reasonably consistent with real-life fighting styles, I’ll admit. Big two handed weapons hit the hardest, shields offer the best defense on the battlefield, and unless you train specifically with more advanced paired-weapon styles, a small weapon in the off hand mostly serves as a stand-in for a shield if for whatever reason you can’t use one. It’s not as defensively useful, but it’s often better than nothing and can make you just a bit more deadly if you’re quick enough. I still maintain that I would rather dual-wielding be a viable option without needing to invest Feats in it, but I’m not opposed to this way of doing it, if dual-wielding Feats are strong enough that a specialized dual-wielder is on par with an equally-specialized Shield user or two-handed weapon user.
