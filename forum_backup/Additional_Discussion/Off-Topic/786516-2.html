                Well, I'm also transitioning (MtF, 6 months real life, FFS in a month, SRS will be next summer), so I might as well chip in with my experiences.

                My story is actually rather similar to Baroness Nerak's, but there are always differences: I suffered a lot more from denial and gender dysphoria. So, here goes:

                As a child I personally didn't really notice anything different. I was a rather dreamy, introverted kid who was always reading, however. What I do remember was being very conscious of my body. It always needed to be covered, no way my mom could get me in shorts or something like that.

                Anyway, this of course got worse during puberty, and like Baroness Nerak, I realized I wanted to be a girl around 17, 18. This hit me very, very hard. It was actually so traumatic, and the desire seemed so impossible to achieve that I suffered a serious breakdown. My mind, in order to protect myself, proceeded to lock my memories of this revelation in a vault, built a castle around it and threw the keys away. This happened in my first year of university, so went largely unobserved by friends and family, who had no clue what was happening to me, except that I was pretty depressed.

                So, life went on in denial, which brings me to the subject of gender dysphoria. I suffered greatly from it, probably because of my denial, but I still find that feeling really hard to put into words. But let's give it another shot: it is an overwhelming sense that something, everything is wrong. Nothing you do can ever alleviate it. And I tried. I tried just about everything. It poisons every aspect of your life until it leaves you hollowed out, left with nothing but quiet despair and the certainty that nothing can ever be right. In the end I just stopped feeling, let's call it emotionally undead. I know, it sounds grim and dramatic. It was. Gender dysphoria is vile, loathsome stuff. I don't wish it on my worst enemy.

                In retrospect, I am very lucky to have survived it. I have my friends to thank for that. But roleplay was equally important. I always played girls, of course. I have to strongly agree with Baroness Nerak here: being a girl for an evening a couple of times per week kept me sane (and alive). But also, I have the feeling that perhaps I could have broken through my denial sooner if I didn't have that outlet. Dunno.

                Odd observation: I felt the dysphoria more acutely when having sex with men than with women. At least with women, one of us was 'right', and I could, in my mind, pretend to be her. How you pull of remaining in denial when you pretend to be the woman you're having sex with, or that all your sexual fantasies involve you being a woman, still amazes me. Anyway, with men the feeling of wrongness, because I wanted to do stuff with them that was plain impossible, because, well, wrong body , was much stronger. As you can imagine, over the years, sex became progressively more difficult.

                For me, it was also at the age of 39 that the dam broke. Now, 41, I am approaching the end of the transition. I fully identify as, and live as a woman and the gender dysphoria is breathing it's last breaths. All is well, and getting better. The hormone treatment has kicked off what I can only describe as a second puberty, which is a rather wild and at times tiring ride, but yay, welcome back emotions! Very, very, extremely impatient for the final surgery though.

                                                        Good questions

                                                        Honest answer: I don't feel a huge draw to transgender characters compared to lesbian characters, trans or cis. I do identify well with them, but in terms of favorite characters, that tends to fall toward lesbians/bisexual women (again, trans or cis, just not as defining). Probably because in terms of words to define myself, "lesbian" "woman" and "Queer" and even "Goth" are way ahead of, say, "Transgender' "White" or "North Carolinian." It's not that I DON'T identify that way, but give me a queer woman and I have a good projection character.

                                                        Depends on the group. Broadly? No. Specifically? Some LGBT groups are AWESOME and go way out of their way to make sure we feel at home. Some are terrible and wish we did not exist, or even see us as a setback to selling Queer folks as normal. And within that, not all trans are treated the same. I am very feminine, honestly moreso than many of my cis friends or hell even my wife. And feminine trans women get more acceptance than butch ones, or gender nonconforming ones (say, a woman with a beard or goatee) so even in a space that welcomes me (who fully embraces the feminine) may be unfriendly to Charlequin. Then you have stuff like tensions over Drag Queens (which leads nearly to knock down drag out fights in some circles) and it gets complicated. Now, most LGBT organizations give lip service to looking out for Trans folks, and some do a better job than others. The HRC for example has a spotty record. But I have also dealt with incredibly accepting LGBT folks. I want us to get along. But then, I have not been burned by cisgendered queer folks. Some trans women who have now have very, very strong opinions on it.

                                                        My experiences with doctors have been mostly positive. My personal physician does not discuss it much but takes it into account and has given me no problems over it. My Cardiologist is...well, I told him, he looked at me, and went on as if I had not said anything. No issues...but not really what I would call acceptance either. I needed a letter to get on HRT, and was lucky a Cis friend asked a queer listserve at her work about a good place to go, and they recommended a wonderful place that was very affirming. I got my letter in one two hours visit. My insurance covers my medicine. For an American, I have it pretty good, though money is very tight and I have to scrimp and save even for laser. I have not even started looking into surgery of any sort.

                                                            I stalked the good Baroness here from elsewhere; had to, since I haven't been around these forums since they were black and yellow. I hope no one minds me throwing my hat in the ring, but I'm likely to be bored for the rest of the week and I'm hungry for even virtual interaction...and I enjoy answering questions.

                                                            Originally posted by hippokrene View Post
                                                            Hmm...

                                                            Do you have any favorite transgender characters from TV, movies, books, comics? Do you feel there's been improvement in the visibility and representation of trans characters?
                                                            Not many, sadly. There wasn't much representation of trans characters to start with, and much of what there was, was downright offensive or just based on such bizarre misunderstandings of what trans experiences are like that I can't empathize with them. The representations that were well done often cast us as victims (thinking of Wanda from Gaiman's "A Game of You" here), which isn't exactly how I'd want to be represented.

                                                            I can still think of a few I like, despite all that. I think my favorite was Brigitte Lin's character in Swordsman II. She was a villain, sure, but a sympathetic one. Also, one of the two romantic interests for Jet Li's character, which I thought was pretty ballsy. I understand that interpreting the character as a trans woman rather than a male eunuch marks a departure of the movie from the book it was based on, but it made the character much more personally interesting to me.

                                                            Originally posted by hippokrene
                                                            Do you think the LGBTAQI community is doing enough to support transgender individuals?
                                                            It's all over the map, like Baroness Nerak said. What I am starting to see is a worrying trend of LGBT advocacy groups deciding that now that marriage equality is in, it's time to throw in the towel because, hey, job finished, right? I guess I worry, sometimes, that trans folks will be left to fight on alone.

                                                            Originally posted by hippokrene
                                                            What were your experiences, if any, dealing with nurses, doctors, insurance companies? Do they still require a psychiatrist approval before any hormone therapy or surgery?
                                                            I lie to them as much as I can and interact with them as little as I can.

                                                            That probably seems odd to say, for someone who works in the medical field, but at the same time working in a hospital has left me kind of jaded. Since there's almost no data on medical care for trans patients, I feel like it comes down to a choice between which way I want my medical care to err. I worry that if medical practitioners know I'm trans, they commit the common mistake of cis people in assuming that I'm "biologically male," and thus judge things like my blood pressure and body fat etc. by cis male standards. Thing is, most of those differences are hormonally governed, and I've been on HRT for quite some time, so they should be judging me by cis female standards. And if they don't, they could give me seriously bad advice, but I don't trust them to do the proper thing on their own recognizance.

                                                            And then, of course, there's the simple respect factor. Receptionists and nurses...regardless of how feminine I was presenting myself (quite feminine, at the time) and the fact that I had my name legally changed, before I had my gender changed on documents, I got people in hospitals calling me "Mr. [Lastname] in front of the whole waiting room, which is downright dangerous. I had to fight a big procedural brawl with the hospital over that. I won, in the end; got the whole branch to change their policy on trans patients. Even saw it show up as a question on the training documents when I renewed my certification the next year. But having to have that fight still left a bad taste in my mouth, and so I don't like to interact with hospitals as a patient more than the minimum necessary to keep on living.

                                                            Also, tip on insurance companies for any trans folks here: most insurance companies are jerks about reimbursing trans-related care, but you can usually con them into paying for HRT by insisting your doctor diagnose the treatment as "endocrine disorder not otherwise specified" instead of anything explicitly trans-related (like "gender dysphoria" or "transgenderism" or whatever). The informed consent clinic I went to to start HRT did it that way as a matter of course, and I've insisted subsequent doctors do it, and that's worked out well for me.

                                                            Speaking of informed consent, that's kind of the answer to the second part of your question. Lots of doctors still require psychiatric approval for hormones, but there's an increasing number of informed consent clinics that basically just require you to affirm that you understand the risks and likely effects and wish to proceed of your own free will. That's how I did it, and I highly recommend it. Psychiatric professionals often have weird ideas about what qualifies you as trans, and some even require RLE ("Real Life Experience," which means you live as a woman/man for at least a year or two before they'll medically treat you. Hazing for trans people, basically. Imagine if, to get a promotion at your job, you had to act like you already had the promotion while everyone screams at you "Fuck you Becky! You're not actually my boss!")

                                                            Surgery is a little more closely guarded. Facial surgery usually is informed consent, but genital surgery usually needs at least a full year of both RLE and HRT (which may be concurrent). Also, you usually need two letters from mental health professionals, at least one of which has to have a PhD. Fortunately, unlike HRT, genital surgery usually isn't needed to function in society as your actual gender, so it's easier to wait for. Still a tremendous relief to those who want it when they get it.

                                                            Incidentally, I went through that experience recently (SRS, that is). I don't mind answering questions about it for purposes of this thread, if anyone is of a mind to ask.
