    DARKON

    (Back after a long delay caused by real life.)

    So the most notable thing about Darkon is its size. It's the biggest domain in the entire setting (not counting the seas), taking up almost the entire northern portion of the map all by it self. Right off that bat, I'll note that I've always found that just a little odd. It doesn't exactly feel like Darkon needs to be this big, and that that space could've been used for additional Domains. I'm also not entirely sure why the Mists/Dark Powers/whatever would decided Azalin warranted a domain of this size in comparison to the other Dark Lords. Unless maybe they were interested/amused by the fact that he dragged Mordent into Ravenloft?
    (Part of me keeps coming back to the idea that, as a result of his research into the demiplane has somehow allowed him a unique power to bring certain minor Dark Lords - or potential Dark Lords or something - under a degree of his control, having them swear fealty to him in exchange for various boons. Like a weird version of feudalism or something. So under that sort of set up, the Boglands, the Jagged Coast, and so forth would have their own lesser lords, each of whom answers to Azalin, and have their own backstories and curses appropriate to their region. The ability to actually leave their own area and travel around all of Darkon, at least as long as Azalin allows its, might be one of the boons from this arrangement. Anyway, the idea just sounds potentially interesting to me.)

    Darkon isn't described in a great amount of detail, save that it's fairly populous by Ravenloft standards, including the Core's biggest city. Also, it's one of the few places to find the various non-human races of AD&D in any great number. This, combined with the fact that everyone in Darkon seems to know and accept that Azalin is a powerful wizard (even if they don't know he's undead), has always left me to presume that this domain is meant to be a Dark Fantasy setting. I confess that I've never really read enough modern fantasy genre to be any sort of expert on Dark Fantasy and its assorted tropes. But Darkon feels like the sort of place some of the more "typical" AD&D monsters wouldn't feel too out of place while still feeling like Ravenloft. (Dragons would probably be too much, but some of the lesser non-intelligent dragon-like creatures might be OK.)
    There are no knights in shining armor in this dark fantasy domain. Instead we get the local baron's bully boys, and above them, Azalin's own personal secret police force, the Kargat. They clearly draw inspiration from similar groups in historical Russia (especially under Ivan the Terrible) and elsewhere (East Germany, probably). I rather wish that it wasn't explicitly spelled out that much of the Kargat's leadership were vampires and the middle management werewolves. Pathfinder did an interesting sourcebook, Agents of Evil, about playing PCs who are part of evil organizations, and it would be interesting to use the Kargat as the basis for a long running Dark Fantasy/Horror campaign in Darkon. If so, it would be better to not have the monsters running around so thick in the ranks.
    That said, if I were using Pathfinder to run Ravenloft, Darkon would certainly be a great place for things like Dhampyrs and the various Sorcerer bloodlines related to the undead and dark magic. Darkon also strikes me as the sort of place where certain aspects of Necromancy magic might end up being part of everyday life, or at least not something the locals are going to get too worked up about. The entire Domain has this interesting kind of fatalism about death and even the undead that is probably the Domain reflecting the fact that its Lord is a lich. Both the idea that the land was once that of the dead before the living came (and will someday be reclaimed by the dead) as well as the fact that the dead have risen up to defend Darkon from invasion is an interesting bit of culture to explore IMO.
    As Darkon has a number of towns/cities and whatnot populated largely by non-humans, I always felt that those races/ancestries should have a sort of uniqueness that marks them as part of Ravenloft Dark Fantasy. I mentioned earlier ideas about Dwarves who are intimately tied to ghosts and other chains of the past. Ancestor worship would probably be pretty common among Dwarves in a place like Darkon. The Elves are maybe more than a bit alien, maybe with psychic powers or dream magic common among them. The Gnomes are a bit creepy, maybe like something out of the Addams Family, with an excessive interest in alchemical potions (and poisons) and engineered death traps. (And in Pathfinder, probably mostly Bleachlings as well.) Halflings are probably extremely skittish and constantly on the lookout for threats.

    As for Azilin, I'm not sure what the think of a lich who likes to run about in just a thong. More seriously, he's an interesting Dark Lord, completely amoral and only really concerned about himself. And while a genius who has pursued mystical knowledge his entire existence, he's now cursed to go no further. No matter how much political power he may have in Darkon, he is in an absolute and total personal hell.
    Azilin's method of sealing the border is ... odd. As a purely defensive measure against outside invasion, a wall of undead twenty ranks deep fits fine. As a way of keeping people in, it doesn't feel much like Gothic Horror, and I'm pretty sure the very first Ravenloft game to use it probably involved the PCs trying to jump or fly over it. Given Azilin has his own secret police force, it seems that if he didn't want someone to leave Darkon, the Kargat would be able to find them almost instantly and arrest them (or just hound them away from the border).

    (Hopefully, it won't take me anywhere near as long to do Dementlieu.)

        The thick wall of undead gets a tad more Gothic by the fact it is still under his control, a fine control no other Darrk Lord has over their closing borders. You can have things like a zombie coming out of the horde to point a finger in the direction Azalin wants the characters to go, or other crude forms of eerie communication, and even have them realize specific tasks or apply weird tactics, like instead of a normal attack they try to grab a character trying to pass through them and just deliver the character back to the Domain, or let someone pass while blocking others as they don't need to treat everyone equally.

        An interesting option (to not just say that flying don't work just 'cause) is to let members of the Kargath to teleport instantly to the mass of undead to deal with more resourceful characters. That way the mass becomes also a teleporting system for the Kargath under direct control by Azalin.

            DEMENTLIEU (Realm of Terror pg 68; Domains of Dread pg 31)

            I always liked this realm. For all intents and purposes, it's a stand in for pre-Revolutionary France, with its faux-French name, its class divides, its influence on Core culture and fashion (at least in the "civilized" Northwest), and the focus on classic mesmerism. This is very much the Domain of very subtle plots and conspiracies (both personal and political), which should inevitably lead PCs to question what is real and what is illusion, and if what they think they see is what they really saw. It lends itself incredibly well to a sort of general and consistent sense of paranoia and mistrust. This plays very well for a much more subtle and cerebral sort of Gothic Horror game. I very much approve.

            Dementlieu has a really great Dark Lord as well. Dominic d'Honaire. An incredibly skilled manipulator and mesmerist, he has been able to bend others to his will since childhood. Combined with him being that classic archetype, the Evil Child, it made him a little terror who grew into a perfect human monster. Other people are just puppets and playthings to him, all the more so since becoming Dark Lord, and he sits like a fat malignant spider at the center of a dark web of influence and plots. On top of this, his curse is perfectly fitting. If he desires a woman (or anyone else, I presume), she will only find him more and more repulsive. In a domain of illusion and mind control, it's the one thing he can't disguise. It's really a thing of beauty.
            Dominic's method of sealing the border works perfectly fine, though if I wanted to make it a little more subtle, I'd likely have it just making people get lost, take wrong turns, and the like instead of a more blatant mirror illusion. It's mostly just a matter of taste.

            I sometimes wonder how much of an influence the 1920 film The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari had on this domain. While that film is German and not French, it feels like that sort of surreal weirdness would fit well here.

            Although it never really gets talked about in the original editions (and only briefly touched on in the Arthaus/S&S editions), Dementlieu is IMO an interesting place to use psychic abilities and powers in D&D. (While Dominic is unlikely to put up with a true potential rival for very long, I think there's a certain degree of hubris that comes with being a Dark Lord which would make him overlook such individuals for at least a bit longer than he should.) The fact that the Domain is a reflection of its Lord feels like Dementlieu should have more than a few sensitive types or possessors of extraordinary "animal magnetism", if not outright psychics.
            This is one of those areas where I think 1st ed Pathfinder would work well with Ravenloft. Besides its Rogues, Inquisitors, and Swashbucklers, many of the Occult Adventures classes would also work really well here. I mean, the Mesmerist is an obvious one, but I think the Occultist would fit in as well. Also certain Psychic Disciplines, such as Superiority, Rapport, Lore, and Dream feel like they'd fit, as would Haunted, Sorrow, Faith, and Pain.

                    DORVINIA (Realm of Terror pg 68)

                    What can one say about Dorvinia? Well, not much to be honest. It's another one of those Domains whose inclusion I find questionable. The Darklord, Ivan Dilisnya, is the "evil twin in all but blood" of his cousin, Ivana Boritsi, but I honestly find him the less interesting of the two. Where Ivana is a cunning schemer whose evil derives from a nicely Gothic mother vs daughter dynamic, Ivan is sort of a man child in the cookie cutter spoiled rich sociopath model. This isn't necessarily a bad thing, and I think you can do certain stories with it, but the original Ravenloft doesn't really go out of its way to try and do so.
                    Like a lot of the original boxed set's Domains, Dorvinia gets a very bare bones description. Three towns surrounding a small mountain. Nothing is really said about the people or culture, save that they are mostly a sullen lot, owing to the fact that the only law in the Domain is what you can buy. And that's about it, really.
                    Ivan gets an actual character write up in the back of the book (something Ivana does not). In some ways, the Domain fits his temperament. The poor and powerless exist at the cruel whims of those who can actually afford the muscle to back up their domains. (Weirdly, this kind of reminds me of some of the K-Drama's I've seen which focus on that country's issues of corruption among the rich and politically connected.) The single weakest part of the Domain and it's Lord is that his method of sealing the borders is identical to his cousin's. and that honestly kind of feels like they just couldn't be bothered to come up with anything.
                    All in all, I can very much see why this domain was slated for elimination with the Convergence, being merged with Borca. I'm not sure making the cousins double Darklords was such a great idea. Ivana is clearly the more powerful of the two, and I once again think that having some sort of "sub-Darklord" tier of evil individuals who have a great deal of power and local influence but are still subservient to the true Lord would be helpful in this instance.

                    As for Dorvinia, one of the things the political/legal set up reminds me of is Sicily and other parts of Italy under the influence of the Mafia (at least in certain aspects of popular perception, especially back before WW2 and in the 19th century). There is no real law or order save that imposed by local strong men and bandit chieftains who may or may not ultimately answer to some central authority. I sometimes wonder if this might've made a more interesting set up to explore as a means of Gothic horror. In such a setting, Ivan might be more akin to a Renaissance era equivalent to Michael Corleone or Wilson Fisk.
