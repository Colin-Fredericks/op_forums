                                            And yeah, I'm starting to really look forward to Rebels. Except that...

                                            Hm, how to put this: Most people agree that the Prequels were a misfire. So, while they are still canon, it's interesting seeing the approach to the new material. Basically, I see them trying to recapture the feel of the original trilogy. Now, this is smart; but is it *right*?

                                            Part of me sees this as a cynical "Cue Cullen" attempt to manipulate the fans; but another part simply sees it as giving the fans what they (apparently) want.

                                            I guess my uneasiness comes down to my post above, about how I would do the new ones; I don't think rehashing the exact same thing is the right way to go, here. Just as the prequels had a different feel to the originals (however well they did or didn't pull it off), I think the new ones should have a different, tho related feel as well.

                                            But I'm sure Disney is flooding Abrams with memos aimed at "doing just what the original films did." This is strongly implied by the fact that the original script only had the original heroes as cameos, and they fired that guy, and replaced it with a new script that is apparently full of the old heroes. I think the original treatment would have been better...

                                            Of course, "Cue Cullens" aren't always bad! Getting John Williams on the project is the FIRST thing I would have done, too! And Rebels, which takes place basically in the same era as the originals, has every reason to have the same mood and tone, so I have no problem with it seeming like them. Also, it looks like it's going for a "Firefly" vibe!

                                            Anyway, does any of the above make sense? Sometimes it's hard for me to tell...

                                                    One video I saw that was criticizing the newer movies claimed that "Star Wars is a Western". Well, kinda...but it's also a WWII dueling aces movie, and a samurai movie, etc...

                                                    Which makes me wonder: SW is essentially a combo of all the things that George Lucas loved, mixed together into a coherent whole. It's his genuine *passion* for these things that rings true, and that audiences responded to. BUT--now that SW is in different hands, should they stay true to those influences, and risk the new movies being shallow copies? Or follow their own passions, and risk them being unrecognizable as SW?

                                                    When I ask myself, for instance, what are MY influences, things that I'm passionate about, I answer Superhero Comics, Kaiju movies, Anime/Manga, Shakespeare, RPGs, the Victorian Era, Film Noir, Wuxia, just to start. If I make my SW movies reflecting the above in some way, would you enjoy them? Rather, would you recognize them as "true Star Wars"?

                                                    Along similar lines, why should the technology in the new movies be the same as the old? Surely there would have been some advances? Would it throw you too much if the new movies had teleporters, personal forcefields/cloaking devices, antigrav units enabling anyone to make great leaps, powered armor, shapeshifting starfighters, talking, shipboard AIs? Where should that line be drawn?
