    So, apparently Spider-Woman is getting a new uniform. According the editor for the Spider titles, he was already planning it before the Milo Manara debacle, but I'm not entirely sure I buy it.

    Anyway, from that same link:

    "As a woman myself who loves games and comics, I love to see thatfunctional, cool stylish look. It's like a jacket I would want to wear daily," says Tatiana Nahai, product manager for Gameloft.

    On another place I read it being called a more practical new costume.

    My sentiment is similar to when the same sort of stuff was said of the new Batgirl costume: Leather jackets are not more practical than spandex for agility-focused superheroes. Nobody is gonna see gymnasts wearing leather jackets while competing on the Olympics. I was actually especially annoyed at trading lenses on a cowl for easily-fall-from-your-face glasses, but in the link I posted it shows that they turn into a mask at the tap of a button, so I don't mind it anymore.

    In Batgirl case it was both better (because it was an emergency costume due to her losing her costumes in a fire or something. Though by now she should probably have being able to replace it), and worse (for me, personally), because someone I know kept arguing that the jacket was more protective when her previous costume was quite freaking armored and made of advanced materials.

    I recognize that Spider-Woman's new costume capacity to turn from street clothes to super-hero uniform quickly is functional and practical, but not more practical than wearing something that doesn't hinder your movements when the Rhino is trying to gore you.

    (Also, I should probably mention I'm biased against it, as I dislike it and fear it might become a trend because I really don't like superheroes in street clothes, especially more "urban" stuff, to the point I refuse to read anything with the jeans and T-shirt version of Superboy in it and nearly couldn't watch Young Justice for the same reason)

            As I've said elsewhere, my criteria for what makes a good superhero costume is that it should be giving the reader visual clues as to the hero's identity and powers. For instance, despite it being dated, I quite liked Hawkeye's costume:



            It's littered with instances of the letter H, suggesting "Hawk;" the otherwise superfluous teardrop eye flairs draw attention to the eyes, suggesting eye. The flared boots and loincloth make one think of medieval characters like Robin Hood, and the bracers on his wrist are clearly an archer's bracers, both suggesting he probably uses a bow. I like it.

            I never liked Spider-Woman's costume because I don't think it gives any particular indication as to her identity or power. The yellow hourglass might evoke a garden spider's patterning, I guess...sort of...but what there indicates her power to manipulate people by excreting pheromones? What suggests her energy blasts? What suggests her ability to glide or to fly? Nothing. It's just a bodysuit, and the only suggestion as to her identity is that her mask's eyes look like Spider-Man's. If you know Spider-Man's costume, you might guess this person is Spider-Woman. The costume has style without any substance, and that's why I'm glad to see someone change it...

            ...into another costume with exactly the same problems and failures as the first. Crap.

            Nothing about this new outfit gives me anything about Spider-Woman. It's the same lack of substance with a new, updated flash. That's it. I kinda like the lines on the jacket, but that's a very superficial compliment. If I didn't already know this was Spider-Woman, I wouldn't know it by looking at her, or even guess it.

            My original comparison to Hawkeye was deliberately chosen because I think Spider-Woman's costume reminds me of Hawkeye's current look, which of course is based on the movie version. The movie version is not, by my standards, good...but I do wonder sometimes if my standards are outdated. Looking like an archer might be of secondary importance to looking like a character from one of the biggest money-making movies ever; being familiar with Jeremy Renner is probably much more likely than being able to piece together the visual clues of the purple spandex. Still, though, while I can use that argument for Hawkeye, Spider-Woman doesn't even have that defense.

            In the end, her new look is disposable to me. It's slightly neat, but mostly meh.

                                        I've always found it difficult to get into comics. I often want to, as the graphic storytelling medium is something I have a great deal of curiosity about, but I find it really hard to get invested into any of the more mainstream super-hero titles, just due to the insane continuity snarl (it's a guy, who is significant if you've read this totally unrelated title, but if you haven't they won't help by explaining anything! And Hero X is angsty about something, which again only makes sense if you've read this other title [and who stopped numbering comics sequentially, instead going with this volume shit!? Makes it impossible for newcomers to get any idea about what's going on]! Oh, and what you thought you knew and were relying on to give a foundation to this idea is wrong because they changed it last reboot, which they had to do because the story got too ludicrously convoluted, which worked for like three issues because they ended up bringing all the old stuff back - I really didn't expect that to go so long. My apologies). So I've limited myself to more-or-less self-contained stories, like Sandman (refers to other stuff, but you don't need to know it), or Hellblazer, or stories that I know the background to fairly well, like the Buffy/Angel comic continuations, mostly Angel. Or the occasional established hero/villain, but a standalone volume, like The Killing Joke.

                                        Sometimes I look into getting into a particular heros stories, but that doesn't seem feasible. I remember about a year ago I looked into reading The Hulk, but that seemed just waaay too intimidating to work out what I was supposed to read, in what order, and what "counted" and what didn't and when. On the one had, it's sad, because there's this whole group of mythology I'm missing out on. On the other, I find it really hard to convince myself that it's worth the - quite significant - effort.

                                        I'm sure when you work that stuff out, the payoff is great. Just way too high a learning curve for me.

                                                        some suggestions from some favourites of mine:

                                                        - Astro City: Both the older stories and the current ones. Even the worst of it (dark ages) is reasonably decent, and pretty much all the rest goes from good to "among the best comics of all time" quality. Not exactly traditional superhero comics, because it is a superhero comic but it has its own style of storytelling that is atypical for the genre, but I can't recommend it enough. Currently published by DC, but has no relation at all with the DC universe, being completely self-contained. Besides, covers and a good deal of the character design are done by the phenomenal Alex Ross

                                                        - Superman: Peace on Earth
                                                        - Batman: War on Crime
                                                        - Wonder Woman: The Spirit of Truth
                                                        - Shazam: The Power of Hope
                                                        These four are a collection of very well-written stories that really get the essence of each of these characters. You can read any of those that interests you in any order, they are not connected. They don't really require much knowledge about continuity and stuff. Perhaps you will not be familiar enough with the basics of Captain Marvel's mythos, but a short read on wikipedia can fix that (section 1.1.1, section 1.1.2, and section 2 only up to the second paragraph after the powers table, are sufficient. About 2 pages or so of text). I imagine you are probably familiar enough with Batman and Superman to read theirs with no problem, and from what I recall Wonder Woman's doesn't really require knowing nearly anything about her either.

                                                        Despite being independent stories, I put them together because the same creative team did all of them. Oh, and all of them are entirely illustrated by the Alex Ross who is, as I already said, phenomenal.

                                                        - Death Vigil: urban fantasy comic featuring dead people being recruited to the vigil, an ancient organization that fights against eldritch abominations. Written and illustrated by the great Stjepan Sejic (you can see many examples of his excellent owrk and his humor in the various folders to the left of the gallery). His artwork is fantastic, and his characterization and humor are top notch. Since you like Buffy/Angel I suspect you'd enjoy this one. It is quite a recent publication, so you can get on board before there is much on which to catch on. Only 5 issues so far. And, since he is a cool dude, he has made a .pdf of the first issue available for free!

                                                        - Invincible: this one is more "normal" superhero stuff, except that, being creator owned stuff from Image, it is not shackled by continuity snafus (and, at less than 120 issues, there simply isn't SO much continuity) and the forever-return-to-status-quo syndrome that afflicts DC and Marvel. Instead, the author is not afraid to pretty much blow up the status quo very often. Never a boring issue on this one. The tagline of "The ONLY Superhero comic you will ever need" and the former one of "The best superhero comic book in the universe" are exaggerated, of course, but in my opinion not as much as one might think, because it is a really good comic book. Just a reads up, though, the fights can get very violent and gory. That actually usually detracts from my enjoyment, but I keep reading because the book is just too good to pass up.
