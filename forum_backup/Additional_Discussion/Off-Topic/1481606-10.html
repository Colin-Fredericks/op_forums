                I saw Alex Garland's new movie Civil War and... it wasn't what I expected. It's a movie focused on photo-journalism in a warzone. The movie isn't really that interested in the worldbuilding, though, which felt a bit odd. Overall, I liked this one more than Alex Garland's last one Men but I don't know, there's just something about the movie that kept me from really getting on its wavelength.

                        It's really small and it isn't a spoiler because it's just one line that gets offhandedly mentioned... but at one point you get the exposition that the US-President in this movie's setting is a third-time-President (which would be illegal under current US-laws) that has disbanded the FBI and has used drones to bomb protestors within the US. It's just one line so that's really all you get. But there's other bizarre stuff as well like how it seems the faction that is going to take over are the so-called "Western Forces" which is this alliance between the state of Texas and the state of California.

                        And I think I finally figured out what has been nagging me: The conceit of the movie is that you have these photo-journalists in New York and they hear the rumor that the Western Forces are pushing towards Washington DC and plan to encircle the city. It seems like the President's days are numbered. And so they decide to go on a road trip, to get to DC before the Western Forces do their thing, to interview the President before it's all over. And so what the movie is really like is that you follow these journalists on this road trip with these episodic adventures. But the whole reason that there's never much of an interaction between the journalists and the world around them is that they're photo-journalists. They're just there to take pictures of the whole thing. But the whole conceit of the movie is these guys aiming to have an interview with the President. There are moments where I thought "Okay, you've taken the picture, now interview the guy. Let's hear what he thinks about the world he lives in." and that never happens. It's really this mindset I've mentioned before that they're just there to take pictures and thinking is someone else's job.

                        I'm also just wondering... If this had been a movie about photo-journalists in a warzone of the past, like the Vietnam War or Operation Desert Storm in the 90s or the Iraq/Afghanistan wars of the War on Terror in the early 00s... Would this level of disinterest in world-building work? I feel like since it's about journalists you'd want to have them hit on some sort of truth about the conflict, and have them gain some sort of insight through their investigations (aka interviews, chasing rumors, taking pictures etc.).

                                I saw the Fallout TV show and it's really good. Not only do you have a great cast (not just Walton Goggins) but the series actually nails the vibe and the look of the games. And it's chockful of references to the games.
