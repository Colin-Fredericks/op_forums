    Ethical use of AI in art, literature and games

    10-20-2026, 12:46 AM
    I know the stance of most people here on the subject. I know the stance of Onyx Path. But we have to talk about it sooner or later.

    What's happening in many places through the internet is unacceptable and valid to boycott, but the new AI age isn't going anywhere. Even if the bubble bursts it'll just become expensive and under control of the worst offenders.

    My goal here is discuss what we can propose as valid ways to use the tech, how we can fight its harm without banning it, what rules we want to see in the future to accommodate the inevitable.

    So I'll start with a few opening questions, and invite answers. Don't take them as simple, the best answers can be unintuitive:

    1. To whom should belong the rights over the output of an AI?

    2. Should the user that collects this output be identified to respond for its use?

    3. Is a piece generated for personal use bound to the same degree of demand as a piece with public or commercial goals?

    4. What role must be protected for the artist? Not general, but specific.

    5. How AI training should be governed? Should it be subject to audit?

    6. Can AI output be used as a placeholder if the user assumes the duty to substitute it for human work when feasible?

    7. Do AI has use alongside Creative Commons and other fair use frameworks?

    Absolute frames that reduce the question to a version of "AI is bad on principle" are unlikely to produce effect if the premise itself is rejected. I think here at least the safest approach is to assume it will.

    Some applied scenarios we can actually watch for here and in spaces we as RPG players know:

    - A player has a character concept and want a visual representation. They have no money or skill, only the idea.

    - A ST benefits of visual props in their group, but the amount and level of personalization desired is way beyond other available resources.

    - A gamer wants to put ideas on paper, but has problems turning them into words effectively.

    - An author has most of the structure of the final work ready, but lacks the money to hire an artist and/or writer.

    - An artist wants to learn, and will use the AI output as reference material.

    - A company wants to increase output speed, so they can catch up with fan expectations.

    Can those people use AI?

    Can they share the output with friends?

    Can they share with a forum?

    Can they release it as free content?

    Can they use it for commercial purposes?

    Can they sell it?

    How long does the answer hold? What condition changes it?

    What if they're disabled? Poor? From vulnerable areas? All those affect the ability of someone to acquire artistic proficiency.

    Does any of the above change based on the type of AI?

    There's AI everyone can use for free,
    There's AI that runs on personal computers, no server farms,
    There's AI trained by the artist themselves,
    There's AI trained under license agreements,
    There's AI that does only part of the job over an image a human has to produce or work upon,
    There's AI that watermark the output.

          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

          What if they're disabled? Poor? From vulnerable areas? All those affect the ability of someone to acquire artistic proficiency.
          I personally find this question to be treading the edge of "oh, you're poor, so that gives you an excuse to steal."

          Countless generations of 'starving artists' have struggled with poverty before them. Art isn't about securing a financial outcome, it's about creative expression. If you can't make good art, it's not because you lack the wealth, it's because you lack the talent. And you'll never be able to refine that talent, if you rely on a soulless machine to do the work for you.

          AI lacks the capacity for creative imagination. It can't come up with new ideas on its own. All it can do is repurpose whatever it was exposed to during its training. It is tracing over somebody else's work, guided by some key word prompts, and then people are taking the result and claiming that they made it themselves.

              That's way more complex a discussion than it seems, but anyway deviates from the proposal. That's exactly engaging in a premise the world is already rejecting, making AI bad on principle.

              It's also weird when you talk about art being just about expression, not money. Because the great discussion floating around is not artists in a vacuum, it's artist's employment. Were it just a matter of defining true art the whole discussion would be way more pointless.

              People who use AI aren't stealing, anyway, so that's a bad comparison. Those who trained it are stealing. Or not, that's not universal. There are artists who train their own AI with their work and artists that license their work specifically for AI and get a cut on each image generation.

              We definitely need human artists. But where? There's a lot of places where AI is dislodging artists from their jobs or their work. But there's also a lot of places where the previous option was nothing.

              Do you commission a professional artist to make every character image you ever wanted for your games? I can guarantee that I don't, never did and probably never will be able to. I pay artists for really important stuff, which happened a couple times, and would pay again if I needed that kind of service.

              Also, my point about poverty was on getting something done, not becoming an "artist". A person who can commission a drawing of a beloved character can pay it. Who can't just get nothing? Not even low quality?

                  Some of my opinions:

                  1. Every company that profits from commercializing image generation models should be required to contribute new human made material. If they commission or employ as a regular is secondary.

                  2. AI should have built-in audit of their training. Whatever was used, it spits an authenticated list. Companies have to be transparent about what they feed the AI and pay the fair price.

                  3. AI should always inherit the licensing status of the material used in training. If an image is copyrighted to someone, every output of that AI is also copyrighted to that person. No more discussion or discretionary contracts saying who owns the output, it belongs to the artists whose work was used.

                  4. Every output should get an invisible watermark or better tracking system. If anyone uses it in an illegal way or against the licensing, they can be identified. Bypassing is possible, but then it becomes effective falsification.

                  5. Every platform should at least be required to demand and exhibit the identification of AI stuff. If someone doesn't want it, there should be filters to take care of it. If there's no licensing rules yet, then commercial use of the output should be just forbidden.

                  6. Crack down on companies who use AI as an excuse to change job titles and cut payments for artists while demanding the same work. That's currently a serious issue.

Placeholders generally do not need to have the visual or literary quality of finished assets, and when they imitate it through AI, the temptation is strong to simply leave them as is rather than complete the asset. "Placeholder assets" have been used as excuses for AI generated final assets repeatedly already.

                              I’m no AI expert, so take any of my answers with a very large grain of salt.

                              1. To whom should belong the rights over the output of an AI?
                              I think this is too situational to answer in brief, so I’ll abstain. I’d be tempted to answer no-one but I’m sure there are scenarios I would say the contrary. Especially if the AI was used as a simple tool rather than the whole creation process.

                              2. Should the user that collects this output be identified to respond for its use?
                              I’m not sure of what this entails.

                              3. Is a piece generated for personal use bound to the same degree of demand as a piece with public or commercial goals?
                              In our current socio-economical context, I don’t think it is. AI has an unfair advantage in cost to output ratio. Using for personal use has next to no impact, while commercial and public uses actively push out some people of their means of production.

                              4. What role must be protected for the artist? Not general, but specific.
                              As boring as it might sound, every role in commercial and public spaces. I’ll extend that beyond artist. Most of us live in a capitalistic society, with different degrees of social safety net. Losing your job is often dangerous, life-changing and incredibly stressful.

                              I don’t think programming, tech support and numerous other jobs should be replaced by AI either. Ai is actively pushing people off their means of survival.

                              And as far as I know, it’s also extremely polluting; it drinks water like there’s no tomorrow.

                              5. How AI training should be governed? Should it be subject to audit?
                              Absolutely, I do not know if the latest AI hacking controversy is simply corporate propaganda to boost sales, or actively dangerous, but the fact that we’re not sure is telling enough : If AI has even half the capacity that tech companies say they do, it needs to be supervised at all costs.

                              6. Can AI output be used as a placeholder if the user assumes the duty to substitute it for human work when feasible?
                              Yes, as long as it’s not profitable. If you use AI for placeholder arts in a game you’re beta-testing, to find out if it’s a worthwhile investment, sure. As soon as it hits the market, all traces of AI should have been cleansed.

                              7. Do AI has use alongside Creative Commons and other fair use frameworks?

                              Maybe, I’m not sure sure what it would entail.

                              Personally, I use AI liberally in a few manners :
                              • Image generation : I used to have to spend hours looking on Pinterest and other image aggregator websites to find art for characters and NPCs. Now I can vaguely describe what I what to ChatGPT, and it will generate something usable. Usually, it will take a few tries, but it’s way faster. Those websites are flooded with AI anyway, so it’s not like I’ll be able to fully avoid it anyway. I might as well use it on my own terms.
                              • Memes : In our home games we’ve been making meme images of our characters relating to the previous sessions. The image is AI, but memes were already low effort images. The funny bit is what you write and the context in which it arose.
                              • Word Generators : Sometimes I just need names, synonyms, etc. It’s pretty much what LLMs excel at.
                              • Idea Bouncer : I have limited time to prepare my chronicles and I put a lot of effort and sometimes I’m just out of creative juice. I’ll ask for random ideas. 90% of the time it’s dog***, but sometimes it comes up with a key word that makes me think of something loosely related.
                              I also plan on making my own board game based on my current chronicles, as a fun exercise in rule building. I will use Ai for some of the card pictures, and assemble what it can do on my end. Basically, I use AI to save time and money, since I’m working and I’m not rich, that’s pretty much it.

                              What you’ll never see me do, however, is trying to commercialize an AI assisted product I made. The market is already flooded with cheap stuff, it doesn’t need me adding anymore to it.
                              As for sharing AI stuff, I see it as all things that can be spammed with low effort. If one shares a meme they made with AI to their Facebook page, if they share a character backstory and include their ai-generated character image, then it’s all good. If they start flooding the space with AI stuff, it’s not.

                              Something that will grate me is how my father sometimes uses it to make long elaborate message texts for a family gathering ; If it takes more effort to read the text than it took you to make it, I’m not reading it.
