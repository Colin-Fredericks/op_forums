            On the subject of elves- in my game, in fact, the starting point is that the elves civilization is already gone. Oh, sure, the "barbaric" wood elves hide in their remote tribes at the end of the forest, and the dark elves worship their demons under the cover of cursed trees, but the standard, magical, high elf civilization was destroyed around a thousand years before the game started, by the now collapse orc khanate. Only very few elves which have seen that lost kingdom in their own eyes have survived since then, and as such most of the elves are known to live in the shadow of their lost kingdom. Some of the elves have since intermingled with the human civilizations, increasing the number of half elves dramatically, while others have joined the setting's nomadic culture (which includes humans, tieflings and lightfoot halflings), keeping their distance from the rest of the world. They still tell stories about their lost land, and some have dreams of reestablishing it or making a new kingdom of their own, but the most try to keep with their life in exile. Kinda taking to whole "living in the shadow of lost glory" to the extreme, mostly inspired by Dragonlance's 5th Age state of the elves.

            One of the future missions my players were meant to accomplish was finding a gate to the last memory of the lost kingdom, find the lost Crown of Dimiriel and find a new king to redeem the elvish race and rebuilt their nation. Things changed when, somehow, my players managed to almost ruin the world at 4th level by mixing three macguffins together, and the end result was that instead of entering the memory-scape, they summoned the whole freaking kingdom from the past, shattering time in the process, and bringing into life the remembered kingdom haunted by undead and other, dreadful things. And that's why you can't have nice things :P

                                                    In the end there's really no such thing as "the D&D setting", even though 5E seems to push one a bit. The game has always occupied a weird grey area between setting-specific and setting-agnostic.                                                             So, I'm back from a week-long vacation with the family. And finally got my siblings (biological and in-laws) together for another round of DnD. Two rounds, actually, since we played two days in a row this time.

                                                            I had them run through an adventure with a larger scope that my previous one-shots. For the record, the previous ones were 1) Matt Colville's "Delian Tomb", and 2) surviving a night in a cabin in the woods during a blizzard.

                                                            With this adventure, I got much more ambitious. Too ambitious, though thankfully the family seems down with continuing when we meet for Christmas. Further thankfully, I managed to finally get their characters to level 2, and by throwing a challenging boss at them (a buffed Ghoul with some Legendary Actions and a couple swarms of beetles).

                                                            The idea for the adventure was an exploration of the ruins of a village that had been destroyed by a mudslide, with many buildings buried beneath the ground. A perfect place for all manner of outlaws, monsters, and interred horrors to hide in. Oh, and not one, but TWO cults of elemental evil. I think of the place a kind of dungeon unto itself, with its own dungeon ecology. Every threat has its own niche and keeps to itself, except when it doesn't. The party haven't really discovered any intelligent foes yet (except the ghoul, who obviously didn't want to talk for very long), so I haven't gotten an opportunity to let them play politics between factions.

                                                            Another idea I'm considering is having them encounter a dragon, specifically a wyrmling or young dragon. One of the ones that has a burrow speed, so it can find them underground and poked its head out of a wall, offering to trade treasure for magic items or something. Not sure what, but I think it's fun.

                                                            Also, these sessions were where I introduced the new party healer DMPC (I have only three players, none of them spellcasters). The last one - a Lawful Good Cleric - was abused verbally and physically, and eventually left when the party tank killed an innocent old woman for next to no reason. So we needed a new one, or the party would probably die.

                                                            It's a halfling Bard named Oswalt Bedfellow (the family calls him "Shortstack"), that they saved from being crucified (with rope, not nails, thankfully) and left as food for some blood hawks, by the local elemental evil air cultists. The party have had a lot more fun, especially since 1) Oswalt actually talks (the previous DMPC was deliberately non-talkative so as not to upstage the PCs), and 2) because I had a talk with the players about the social contract and not killing/abusing every NPC they encounter just because they can. I'm also having a lot of fun, and it's just useful, from a DM perspective, to have a character in the party who can explain things diagetically (rather than me just expositing as a DM).
