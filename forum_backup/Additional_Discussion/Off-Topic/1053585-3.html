    It was actually bad enough that I'm responding to this thread even though it's probably going to end up being a tire fire.

    It goes either way.

    Besides, you realize that treating members of any given group on account of the worst, most extreme members of the group (especially with groups like race, which aren't chose-based), even when said members are shunned even by the community is... Kinda wrong?

    Look, I get historical grievances and such, but it leads nowhere but to more grievances on both sides and from extremism will born more extremism. Are you really want to live in perpetual hatred and war? Because that's what you'd get.
    I'm familiar, yes, with how race-apathetic white people will cheerfully fall in lockstep with actively racist white people. I mean, Dr Martin Luther King talked about it once:

    I have almost reached the regrettable conclusion that the Negro's great stumbling block in his stride toward freedom is not the White Citizen's Counciler or the Ku Klux Klanner, but the white moderate, who is more devoted to "order" than to justice; who prefers a negative peace which is the absence of tension to a positive peace which is the presence of justice; who constantly says: "I agree with you in the goal you seek, but I cannot agree with your methods of direct action"; who paternalistically believes he can set the timetable for another man's freedom; who lives by a mythical concept of time and who constantly advises the Negro to wait for a "more convenient season." Shallow understanding from people of good will is more frustrating than absolute misunderstanding from people of ill will. Lukewarm acceptance is much more bewildering than outright rejection.
    The idea that we should just bury the hatchet is.. misguided. You're not from the States, as I understand, so you may not be familiar with how that's failed to work- Every time something happened in history that upset the white power structure, the underlying cause of it was never addressed; so it just continued.

    Slavery was ended (at least some of it, hold on); then white people in the south immediately set out to put black Americans back in the place white society had allotted for them. Laws crushed them into the dust and denied them the freedom and power to change or impact the class structure.

    After trials and tribulations, they get the right to vote, but then the racism just gets more rarefied. Gerrymandering that claims to not be racist, gutting of the voting rights act, the careful manipulation of school funds, job discrimination, police violence, feeding people into a prison-industry as cheap labor...

    Racism never dies because we never address it. We ignore it and push it away and pretend it's finally gone; because of that fail to notice how it impacts the country. It just gets more subtle, more rarefied.

    Slavery may be over, the civil rights era may be over, but the shadows those things cast continue to haunt the US. History never dies because it feeds the future. We are the product of the culture we live in, we are the children of the generations that came before us.

    We are the emergent property of the actions and events of the past.

    That's why ignoring them; and, refusing to admit there's a problem won't just make it go away.

            I saw the rest of the show. I'd have to agree that the jump to call it racist against white people was misguided.

            While the show itself doesn't really bring to light any new issues, it shows the issues people, not just black people, have with trying to deal with racism, and depicts it in an interesting and often amusing light. Especially the 'being drowned out by other voices,' which is a problem that's cropped up a lot lately. Through Gabe, each episode focusing on the point of view of one of the characters and him being the white one, we see his own problems in being 'a white face in a mostly black part of an overwhelmingly white place,' and dealing with jokes about his race while 'dodging the landmines of identity politics.' Good acting, all around, except with the oral sex. Very interesting and deep characters, when showcased, and I'm hoping we see more in season 2, particularly of Rashid, Joelle and Al. It's easy to dismiss characters as shallow but once you see them from different views it's clear there's a lot more to them, which was likely the point.

            Probably the most real aspect of the show, beyond the college-age angst, is that there's a dozen different people with different, often diametrically opposed, ways of dealing with their situation and no one has an easy answer that would work for everyone.

            Likely the best 'dramedy' I've ever seen, though it's not a genre I've seen much of.
