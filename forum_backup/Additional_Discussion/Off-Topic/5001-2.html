                                    I had more trouble in middle school than high school too. It was crowded and run by a city that tended to concentrate the elementary and middle school students that they considered troublemakers into a few schools to keep the rest of the schools tidy to showcase how good they were to anyone looking at the school system. They could not pull that shuffle around trick with the high schools since there were so few of them but since the smallest one in the city had over 2000 students the "troublemakers" kind of got lost in the crowds anyway.

                                    Besides the usual shoves and trips, book flips, hair pulls and the occasional locking of someone in a locker (in the junior high I went to the lockers in the old section of the school were the backless bolt to the wall type and one girl who was shoved into and locked in one was small enough to pull her legs up, plant them against the wall and push hard enough to rip the whole line of lockers off the wall) there were other nasty things some students would do to each other.

                                    I often got chased around by a gang of morons who never seemed to catch on to my simple trick of running to string them out and then suddenly turning on the one in the lead and using his own momentum to knock him flat and then taking off again to repeat it on the next. After a long while they finally stopped doing that, especially when they found out that if they did manage to catch me I would kind of snap and go rabid weasel on them (I had some issues back then….) and I think it actually scared them. Sometimes I spent more time in the office on detention than in class though I never started anything.

                                    There was a trick that I was never caught by because I would watch for it though others would walk into it from inattention. It was also kind of rare because it was dependent on the halls being full of standing people which usually only happened in the mornings when waiting for the section doors to be opened. It was called the “pinball slot” locally though it probably has other names and is easy to spot if you know the bullies by sight (and I knew every one of them in the school). The name came from them lining up in two queues with a space between them and when a victim walked into the empty space one of them would shove the person sidewise and the one across would shove back like a pinball between two lines of those kicker things on a pinball table. If the line was very long the victim would be quite disoriented and possibly injured by the time they were ejected out the other end of the column.

                                    Bombs in the bathrooms were another middle school favorite, they would either set them out of sight to give people ringing ears and headaches when they went off or else they would make compact waterproof ones and flush them to (or at least try to) make all the toilets geyser.

                                    By high school most of that nonsense was gone and the stuff that was left was more either malicious pranks or social attacks to humiliate rather than physically damage the target. Sure, there were still a few dim mooks that tried to intimidate people but they were relatively rare.
