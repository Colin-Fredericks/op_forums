            Originally posted by PMárk View Post

            Honestly, I'm a bit tired about the whole "nuanced monstrous races" thing as some kind of benchmark of approval when we're taking a setting into consideration.

            There are some settings, where they aren't different from any other race. There are settings, when they are predisposed to evil. Both are fine, you don't have to redeem orcs, or gnolls in every setting.

            You don't have to redeem drow either. On Goalrion, there's no equivalent of Eliistree.

            It's a matter of world-building, and it depends on the kind of evil race. For races that aren't self-destructive and whose people live decently, I can understand that there's no need to "redeem" them. However, when you have drow-like races, who harm themselves just like they harm everyone else, then IMO nuance is absolutely needed. When you have an intellgent race, and then they stagnate for millennia, without any development/change whatsoever, languishing in a situation that is legitimately self-destructive, that leads to suffering, self-denial, waste of potential and so on, but no one ever seeks a different path, or thinks that *maybe*, just *maybe* there's an alternative, then that lacks credibility. It feels cheap, forced, and quite boring (since it lacks one of the main driving forces that all human-like creatures have, and that leads to growth, change, story hooks, and so on: improving one's own condition).

            I know that there's the whole argument of "who cares? There are dragons and fireballs here", but the thing is that while compelling stories can be had in a world that has different rules than ours, there's one kind of logic that needs to still hold true, and that is the one governing people's actions.

One of the beautiful things about Eilistraee is how she wants the drow to open their eyes, to understand and embrace a different path rather than forcing one on them.                                                             They've never come to the point where concepts like joy or affection are considered weakness and taboo, while hatred (or at very least lack of unity) between family members is the norm (and while the concept of being friends or loving each other are basically forgotten) That's just far-fetched.

Yes, it is possibly harsh and not full of familial joy and happiness, but honestly, an awful lot of people are living like that in the real world too. They have to deal with the whim of those who are above them (and things like being hunted for sport by nobles), and with the utter scarcity of resources of the Underdark. Commoners have all the reasons in the world to want to escape, and they make up the bulk of the drow population. I mean, when canon sources tell you that the Lolthite society would collapse under the weight of its own nonsense if it wasn't for Lolth, you know that the situation is *bad*. Also, the lack of trust, joy, affection takes a very heavy toll on people. Such a situation would severely harm anyone, it's not to be taken lightly.

                                                            That said, I don't understand your comparison with people who actually live in similar situations. Those people don't just passively accept it. Don't you think that they'd wish for something different? That many try to achieve something different? That's my point. That gives nuance to the race: Instead of bending and being like "whatever, man" they try to retake their lives (and the ways that different individuals do it lead to very different kind of groups, and possibly even alignents).

                                                            Even, if, say, orcs, aren't that bad between each-other, they could be absoultely bad toward non-orcs.
