            On and off, for years now, I have been mulling something in my mind. It just struck me again this morning and I feel like talking about it to demonstrably rational and inclusive bunch. So I'm just gonna jump into this thread and get started.

            First off, I'm a married 34 year old white straight cisgendered male - the only thing that makes me slightly less boring is the fact that I'm from Serbia and recently moved to Canada. I make no pretense at knowing the hardships and difficulties of the LGBT community and this isn't an attempt to make myself sound as if I do. Still, inclusiveness and acceptance of the minorities is very important to me (which is a recipe for getting an ulcer very early in life if you're living in Serbia... or at the very least, getting beaten and insulted; but then, that's why I left).

            So, that said, I want to express my frustration with the "born this way" argument. I realize the LGBT community has gained a lot out of it and it helped overcome some rather idiotic issues in the society, such as religious nuts convincing people that you can and should change yourself if you happen to be any of the letters in LGBTQIA+. I don't mind that at all. But it still makes me cringe when I hear at, and here's why.

            First off, it puts you on the defensive. It literally sounds as if someone is saying you're a freak, and your reply is - I can't help it. So then a person should be allowed to be less-than-normal for reasons of inability to change. I do not like that at all. If you squint a little, it almost sounds as if you're agreeing with the opponents of equality in a weird way. If you were born a certain way and can't change it, does that mean we should revisit the "not-normal; should change" argument once genetic research has advanced enough to allow that to change? Basically - I do not think a person should apologize for being a certain way, whatever that way may be. You need not and SHOULD not provide genetic analysis in order to be allowed to like what you like and love what you love. We should strive to be a free society, our arguments should be that people are allowed to do whatever they like as long as they're not hurting anyone.

            Secondly, gender identity and sexual preference are literally the only aspects of modern society where genetics are used as an argument for anything. If you don't like kale, you do not say that you were born a certain way so that your digestive systems can't process kale normally. You say that you don't like kale, and that's enough. You do not say you were born unable to play basketball, you say you're not good at it and don't like it. All that could change at some point. Or not. You are allowed to not be pinned down as a non-basketball-playing kale-hater. Even if you stay that way for your entire life. It is perfectly reasonable and it should be normal for a person to say that they prefer sex with certain genders. Maybe that changes. Maybe it doesn't. It's none of anyone's business and they should stay the fuck out of it.

            Lastly, I firmly believe that people have long passed the point of being defined by their biological makeup. We are slowly but surely transcending what we originally were. Culture is more defining to us than biology and if anyone thinks there is such a thing as "natural human behavior", they are wrong as wrong can be. There is a lot of cultural baggage, some of it still by necessity of procreation - so heterosexual cisgendered behavior is a lot more popular than the alternatives. I am the prime example of that - men do not attract me. And while I did wear makeup for a while, it wasn't for reasons of being transgender (but maybe it could've gone in that direction? who knows!). But just as I like denim jeans and sneakers, these are all cultural artifacts. And I feel they should be recognised as such. In a different society with a different cultural makeup, I am convinced it would be proven that everyone is, essentially, bisexual and gender fluid. These things will show up more and more as we advance. We should prepare in advance by not letting arguments about genetics drag us down.

            People should be free to choose what they are and not be required to provide justification for it.

                Ideally arguing one was born this way or not should not matter. And, frankly, given the shit LGBT+ persons have to deal with I can't understand why a lot of people would choose that. Some sure, but a lot? No. And I anticipate that down the line it won't be a thing.

                But LGBT+ persons have had to defend ourselves from a lot. No one is arguing people who don't like Kale should be given the death penalty or not allowed to be married...and people argued both in public in the US this year, and both positions were backed by candidates running for president, explicitly or implicitly. That fact it is not a choice when everything comes down is very significant. And I think it has helped with our public acceptance. Homosexuals have often been painted as, well, often men who go out looking for men because they want kicks, or are evil, or bored, or something. And people have to be told that that is not how it works...and telling people that is working. Even bisexuals don't choose who they fall in love with; a bisexual woman who falls in love with a woman and pursues her is following her heart, not choosing to be gay, just like a bisexual woman who falls in love with a man and pursues him is following her heart, not choosing to be straight.

                I agree people should be free to choose, and not have to provide justification, but we are not there yet. Trust me, we are not there yet, and not exclusively because of religious nuts.
