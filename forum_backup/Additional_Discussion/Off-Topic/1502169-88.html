                        I'll possibly get a lot of bad rep here for what I have to say, but...

                        Really, I'm starting to hate the "no AI art" stance overall.

                        That's not that I don't see the problems associated with "AI art". I do. But I think most of the criticism is severely misplaced, and this misplacement hurts common people on both sides of this debacle without really addressing the very real problems behind it.

                        What I see is that virtually every problem associated with AI art is actually just a new iteration of older problems that have been harming artists for a very long time, and blaming AI shifts attention from the real causes, real solutions, and maybe more important, real problems that were already there and will stay unsolved. AI doesn't steal art, companies do, and have been doing for more than three centuries. AI doesn't fire people from their jobs to hire at worse, more exploitative conditions, companies do and most artists work under a very oppressive system. AI doesn't flood social media and the internet, users do, and platforms don't curate because they don't want to. AI doesn't refuses regulations, companies do, and politicians abide.

                        AI is a tool. It doesn't do art, but this doesn't change that people can do art with it. A camera doesn't do art either, the image being a precise, soulless record done through light and physics, yet a photographer or filmmaker is an artist by planning and executing how the camera will be used, and the results curated. Doing slop with AI is certainly easy and fast, but this kind of uninspired and unskilled mass production of media is hardly a new phenomenon. There is such a thing as good art with AI, but it takes time, work and skill like any other art, because you has to plan and curate, you have to learn and iterate complex prompts, and you have to know how composition works to produce anything worthy.

                        Seriously, those problems have been pointed out by people in the industry at least for several decades in their current form. Companies force most artists to relinquish rights on their creations and pay pennies for their valuable work. We need serious legal reforms that stop the exploitation system, not to blame a technology that, seriously, won't go away like that. AI even has the best advantage to solve common problems that people blame on it, but existed before: it can infuse metadata on the output that identifies it was AI made, and it can index its own training database for easy audit. More than any other technology around, AI can make it easier for everyone to know where something came from and what was used as "inspiration".

                        Lets demand AI companies be required to audit their training datasets and remove unlicensed material. Lets reform copyright laws to guarantee creators their rights against companies that control the market and can simply gate-keep you from work if you don't sell the IP. Lets demand AI companies to implement a simple standard that lets any program to identify quickly if something was made by AI. Lets demand platforms to curate or forbid AI content (there's a lot of difference between AI for content creation and AI for personal use). Lets demand real solutions to real problems by real, deliberate offenders that will otherwise just find other ways to exploit us as they always did.

                            I think there's some strong points that come from trying to remind people that all computer programs are just tools and how they're used is what really matters.\

                            That said, "just being a tool," doesn't mean the potential for good in a given technology can ever overcome the risks of the bad. For example, I've read lots of essays trying to convince people otherwise, but I cannot hold any position on nuclear weapons besides, "nobody should have these, everyone that already has them should be pressured to the maximum possible extent to disarm from that particular technology, and everyone trying to make more should face maximal pressure to stop." Mutual assured destruction is a demonstrably failed geopolitical stance, and to this days vast sums of human resources are wasted maintaining arsenals to make sure we can end civilizations (if not humanity completely) if the wrong people get into a hot war. Nukes are also just tools, and while I think in the hindsight of history their first use is growingly seen as far less justified than it was presented that is debatable at least. In the now? There's no good reason for any country to be making more of them, and doing anything with their olds ones besides destroying them as safely as our current technology allows.

                            While I don't think generative algorithms are as potentially destructive as nukes are, I think a much stronger case has to be made for their good than is currently being made. Passing laws and those laws being followed on an global level is not exactly something humanity has a good track record on.

                            Another problem is money. Multiple major companies in this sector have said that the regulations that you're proposing would simply cause them to pull their products because it would render them impossible. The people that make the biggest models have, even if that's not their spin on it, admitted there is no non-exploitative form of these models that can operate at the scale necessary for commercial use. They have made it clear our options are "No AI art," or, "human made art's primary role in society is to be stolen for model consumption." That is the stance of the companies responsible for the most popular models.

                            From that perspective, the public has no real power unless we rally around "No AI Art." Either that is a bluff by these companies, and they do have ways to monetize their product in an ethical fashion if faced with losing a vast sum of money already invested in these things happening, or they don't and morally we have no choice because ethical use doesn't really exist at scale. This gets back to my nukes analogy. Either, at least at commercial scale, these programs are in the same moral category as nukes, and thus we need to be opposed to them for the sake of stopping massive damage to human flourishing, or we need to be opposed to them as if they're nukes until the people in charge of these companies start rolling out the normal TNT version that has clear ethical use cases that can be adequately covered by regulations.

                            If "AI Art" (also the AI label is a gigantic problem of its own) is just a tool like any other, it also needs to compete with existing tools like any other on a fair ground. Nobody on the planet is making "good" "art" with these tools on a fair playing field with other creative endeavors in the same style of media. They are all being subsidized by companies out to prove their product has positive use cases. How many "AI artists" would there be if you had to pay $100 US per prompt you run? How much would they have to charge for a final product? Can that really be competitive to more traditional methods? At some point the free prompts for quality tools era has to end. Data center capacity costs resources, research and model improvements cost resources, complying with regulations for ethical use are going to cost resources. Right now, all of that is coming from rich people investing in other rich people. But they didn't get that rich giving their money away. They want returns on their investments. That system doesn't work in an ethical non-exploitative economic model.

                            I assure you that exceedingly few people that hold a "No AI art," don't also agree that we need to find a way to fix late stage capitalism. There's too many problems in the world to try to treat any of them in isolation, but that's part of the fuel for unnuanced rallying cries. Like all protest slogans isn't generally necessary to take a far stronger stance than the more nuanced positions people would actually accept. I'm sure you'd find most "No AI Art" advocates are more receptive if you treated the slogan as just being short for, "Pause AI art until we fix late stage capitalism and then we'll been in a place to give AI a fair shake to see it the technology is a net positive."

                            --------------

                            That all said, I do want to highlight one thing I think that really hurts your position: "There is such a thing as good art with AI, but it takes time, work and skill like any other art, because you has to plan and curate, you have to learn and iterate complex prompts, and you have to know how composition works to produce anything worthy."

                            You can't learn composition from LLM/etcs. This is a fundamental weakness in the argument for the current level of technology actually being able to produce "good" outputs. You essentially have to study composition in an essentially traditional fashion so you can filter outputs. This means that all "good" outputs are just a traditionally trained artistic eye spotting when the model lucked into something worth refining.

                            It's a largely self-defeating position. "Learn how to do artistic photography to the point where you could be a professional photographer so you can understand photographic composition well enough, but then stop working in photography and move to working in prompts as a whole secondary skillset to develop so the computer can generate the images and you have to spend lots of time refining outputs until you finally have something good." That really isn't that great of an argument over just.... being a digital photographer. Art is an extremely weak area to argue for these models because if you assume all the ethical issues get solved, you're fundamentally left with adding steps to do what people are already doing.

                                                Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                                                The question I have to ask for AI is if the benefits outweigh the drawbacks? Regardless this technology is going to happen and we better be smart on how we handle it.
                                                The problem with this framing (as far as real discussion/understanding goes) is that it greatly misrepresent the role of the decision-makers and social systems involved, and how they can guarantee or impede anything about anything.

                                                "No AI Art" slogans and demands work because they threaten the bottom line of the people who are misusing the tech, and that's a real fight worth of fighting. But "benefits and drawbacks" do not exist in a vacuum and aren't solely dependent on the technology in question.

                                                Implementation is also a very complex thing. As Heavy Arms said, technologies can and do fail at social relevance. Virtual Reality as we know it, motion sensors and all, exists since 1968 (with primitive versions as old as 1956), yet it's just a fad that comes and goes with ambitious pretensions never delivered to this day. Commercially available biometric sensors exist since 1974, yet only in the past two decades did it gained real trust for development outside edge cases and spy movies. And flying cars suck.

                                                AI as we speak of today, that is, specifically Generative Neural Networks (particularly GAN-type ones), has a lot of intricacies and complex problems that are still far from a given on what they'll do to their use. LLMs in particular are intense resource consumers (as are Crypto Farms that no one gives a serious thought about, but should), and even media generators can get pretty hungry at scale. Their need for training material is also very far from a settled matter, nowhere is it written that AI companies will get away with stealing IP material forever, especially given that on top of heavy public distrust being levied, that also creates a lot of issues with large companies that are also heavily IP dependent, not to say how slop is making platforms rich right now, but is also compounding problems that may become an intense headache for them in the near future.

                                                My position is that we should talk seriously about the tech and how we can use it ethically and positively to the degree it's possible to begin with, but a lot of things about it are far from settled, and questioning if it should be implemented to begin with is definitely still on the table.

                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                I think there is a lot of justified emotion around it that's hard to push aside for a healthy conversation.
                                                You are right on this, and that's why in my last post I tried to re-frame my position as less one of disagreement and more one of personal frustration. I'm doing research on the area and liking this work a lot, yet I can't talk to my usual circles about it and that's emotionally draining.

                                                But this whole situation has a lot more to it. For example, until now the numbers don't match the overall perception that AI destroys jobs/work. It's hard to say for sure because a lot is happening at the same time and no one has the whole picture yet, but very few cuts in the job market can be traced to AI at all, far below the baseline growth. AI still requires a lot of work, by a lot of people, and a lot of skill. It IS touted by many companies as the reason for their layoffs, but just as with ending Home Office, a lot of it is layoffs the companies were expected to do anyway, the AI is just a nice tale to tell to shareholders.

                                                Overall I think a more nuanced conversation is more likely to give us actionable answers to some of the many problems those unethical actors caused. And by any means it doesn't mean not being firmly against everything being effectively done. I won't personally pay for anything AI made or AI related that hasn't been painstakingly proven to be ethical (and I know how limiting that is) if I can avoid it in any way - and I'll be very vocal against any situation where I'm not given a choice.

                                                Definitely I don't want to make this yearning for decent discussion in any way a negation of the very real need to fight against everything that's happening, and that is exactly how you said: a huge, sweeping financial dependence led by unethical actors faster than anyone could reasonably react, and now we have a helluva mess to deal with.

                                                What I can give a modicum of credit to the "will come anyway" argument is that there's a lot of high-level political and military interest in keeping this tech going. Generative AI, and particularly LLMs, are in the strange position of being technologies that need test data to such a degree that having "everyone" using it isn't a seller's dream, it's an R&D necessity. One that would make any tech unfeasible were it not for the strategic relevance it has for the only people who really have enough power and resources to bypass the problem: governments.
