                        My parents actually had a great way of handling that. They taught me words aren't good or bad, words are just words, but there are two kinds of words that should be treated with great care. Some words, like fuck or shit, offend some people. You can usually tell whether or not those words are okay to use based on the situation and the kind of language the people there are using - if you hear someone else say "fuck" you know it's ok to say fuck around them. Then there are other words that have teeth and claws. Words like fat, stupid, ugly, any word that bullies use to make people feel bad about who they are. The thing about those words is that it doesn't matter if you're trying to use them to make someone feel bad or not. If you've been bullied by those words before, even hearing them in a different context can remind you of when you were hurt by them before, and it still hurts. Those words have teeth and claws, and they'll attack people even if that's not what you mean for them to do. You can never tell if someone around you has been hurt by one of those words before, or how badly, so it's almost always better just not to use them.

                                                    I've never heard it brought up by any of the First Nations people I've met, even though they've shared with me a number of words and other cultural things... And frankly, I'm not entirely sure how much of that culturally has survived... I mean, not the rough concept, but understanding of concepts is shaped by your sensibilities. And the cultures (for better or worse or both) have changed. For an example... I was reading an article about the intent of the term "natural-born citizen"... which is almost certainly not what we would think of it as, because we live in a very different world from that of when it was written. The meaning to us is going to have none of the same emphasis by recent past and current events (for those who wrote it).

                                                    It's human nature to view the world from your own place, even if realistically the things that made something important were super different back then. At best, we have educated guesses, but we don't KNOW.

                                                    I'll also say I would see it as cultural appropriation if you took a term like that without... you know, being part of the culture, or at least having it in your family history.

                                                            when I finally reached the point where I knew I had to transision I came out to a few close friends, after that I made a rule to come out to one new person each week, and that went on for several months before I sorta accidentally came out to everyone at once. I walked into my local game store during friday night magic busiest the store gets outside of huge events, wearing a floral skirt and simple black top. There was some hugging and positive reinforcement from those people who already knew, and some questions and weirdness from others but it all resolved itself quickly, especially because I didnt let it end there, for the next two weeks unless I was going to work I was presenting as myself and after those two weeks I was full time. I went from my first time out as myself to full time in 16 days. So the coming out thing was quick.

                                                            One of the things that I have found remarkable is just how quickly things stop feeling new, diffrent, and special. When I first started being me it was constantly scary, exciting, nervous, half paniced happiness. Even with friends, I had one who sorta freaked out.
