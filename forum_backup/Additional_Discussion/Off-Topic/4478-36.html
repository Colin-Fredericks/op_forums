                                            Tonight, I found myself thinking about a patient I saw a couple weeks back. The guy was in his 90s, and despite being in phenomenal health (he was on *zero* medications) and having no worrisome exam results, he repeatedly mentioned feeling ready to die and was insistent on a DNR. (Because you're thinking it: "no worrisome exam results" includes a depression screen.)

                                            The reason behind it was that, well...he had outlived everything in his life. His spouse, his friends, his siblings, even his children - all gone. He'd been retired for ages, too. The story of his life had long since reached its climax, but he was still here.

                                            I can't fathom what that must be like being the last man standing, the final survivor in your own story. It's a disquieting feeling even in fiction - the protagonist standing alone after everyone else has fallen away. And at that age, what do you do with yourself? What new chapter do you open up? You are, with each passing day, more alone in time.

                                            I still have to think it'd be preferable to dying early. But it is a sobering thought.
