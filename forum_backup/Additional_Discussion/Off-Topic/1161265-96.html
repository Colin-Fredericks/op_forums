    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Eh, not my bugbear. I’m not a fan of arbitrarily limited resources anyway, so I’m cool with it.
    Sure, but they're making their own job a lot harder for no good reason. Just look at how fiddly Stunning Fist became because it has to be usable at-will now. Since even things that were limited in PF1e can't have any resource limits.

    How so? I didn’t see anything in the ranger class preview that specifically benefited dual-wielding. Sure, the article talked up how the reduced multiple attack penalty from Hunt Target and Masterful Hunter stack with agile weapons. But nothing about that actually helps dual-wielders any more than it helps anyone else. +5% chance to hit is +5% chance to hit, that feature doesn’t get better just because you are getting another +5% from a completely unrelated feature. And if you want to take advantage of both Hunt Target and an agile weapon but don’t want to dual-wield... you can. Frankly, the tiny decrease in average damage you’ll see for not making your first attack with a non-agile one handed weapon is practically worthless compared to a shield anyway.

    They mentioned a chain of class feats that can improve your two-weapon fighting, I guess. But those come at an opportunity cost. If you aren’t interested in dual wielding, you can take animal companion feats instead. Or snare feats. The fact that the ranger has class feats to improve their chosen weapon style doesn’t encourage them to dual wield any more than the Pirate archetype encourages them to sail.
    Yeah, but the article goes out of its way to reassure us that rangers will still benefit from their "traditional" combat style, and then off-handedly mentions that, well, if you want to do something so exotic as fight with a greatsword, you can do that too, I guess. And then, yeah, there's a feat chain for dual-wielding. It's not required, but it amuses me greatly that they're still not willing to let go of this.
