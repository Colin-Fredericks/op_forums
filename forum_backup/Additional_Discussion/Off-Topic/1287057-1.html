        I think we're missing a point here, though.
        Lewis Carrol and Micheal Jackson were known (allegied) pedophiles, Picasso was an abusive father and a chauvinist that said some of the worst things I ever read about women, Umberto Eco was an arrogant sack of shit, Walt Disney was a fascist and an abusive employer.

        But should we really say "don't go see Guernica because Pigasso painted it"? Had people thrown him away, refused to host showrooms or buy his paintings because of his behaviour, would this world be any better? Maybe we'd have condemned him to a mundane underpaid job, but at the same time we'd have lost his best works - perhaps he would have stopped after his Blue period and become a forgotten author.
        And some times it may not even matter. Whether or not Carrol was a pedophile, "Alice in the Wonderland" has no trace of it. Would there be any point in discarding the book?

        Now, Zak Smith is no Picasso, no Carrol. The world is probably going to go on just fine even without his contributions to the RPGs.
        We could lose some good supplements, though. As loathsome as he might be he's still a good author - or rather, exactly because he's a loathsome person he can be a good author, since a manipulative 300 years old vampire just comes out better when it's created by a manipulative sex addict with low morality. Honestly, I believe that Smith is perfect for the LotFP game line and he wouldn't be if he were a different kind of man.

        In the end, people make their own choices and if you don't want to contribute to something because you despise who's behind it then fine, God knows I've done that in the past too. But as it's wrong to keep the work apart from the author is also wrong to judge the work as if it was the author; yes, the author is going to be embedded there, somehow, but it doesn't have to be the part of him that you despise and it does not need to compromise the overall quality of the work itself.

        Sexual abusers should be put on trial and sentenced, but as a gamer I see no issue with them writing game supplements from behind bars.

                                                            I believe each piece of creative work should be evaluated on its own merits, regardless of who created it or why. This is especially important for projects involving many people, each of which influenced the final product in their own way.

                                                            Then, separately from that, each creator should be judged on their actions and the consequences of those actions, regardless of their stated intentions. This includes whatever processes they used to create their work.

                                                            In any situation - I cannot condone starving a person to death (the logical outcome of denying a person all forms of support forever) because of their misdeeds. If someone is really abhorrent enough that they need to die in order to protect everyone around them, there are quicker and more merciful ways to kill.

                                                            My reasoning is that if we discard everything ever made by anyone who did something we find abhorrent, an indeterminate-but-probably-very-large fraction of human culture will be cast aside, including much that people today find helpful, beautiful, and uplifting. Moreover, if we carry that principle of zero-tolerance forward, our evolving standards for morality will gradually erode more of our past and present culture.

                                                            Besides that, I lack confidence that boycotting a creator's work will change them, not least because not all creators live off of royalties. It feels like a very passive-aggressive kind of punishment, undertaken to reassure the boycotter of their own morality rather than actually making a measurable positive change for anyone. Some more proactive options include:

                                                            * Staging protests, attempt to get the creator fired or banned from a venue
                                                            * Start or contribute to a support program for the creator's victims
                                                            * Publish competing work - especially work that riffs on the original in a way that highlights the creator's failings.
