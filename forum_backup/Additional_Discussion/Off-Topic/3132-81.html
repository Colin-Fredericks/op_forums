The rest looked like I might have dribbled mustard on my shirt. Didn't smell like that, but hey.

        I don't bring this up because I hate missing groceries for one day. I don't bring it up because I hate a man whose body is beyond his control had a bodily function he couldn't control. I bring it up because I don't hate those things, and I think it's important for you to know that to provide some context for what indignities I can and will suffer.

        At some point in the undefined future, my work will be buying us phones. They will have GPS systems in them, not for us to navigate to patients' homes but for the office to track us. It has been decided we cannot be trusted to be where we say we are when we say we're there, so the company is going to tag us and track our movements. We are being told that we are all of us liars, and that the correct solution to this problem is not to fire the people who have been proven to lie about their whereabouts (which is, incidentally, a federal offense) but to track us electronically so we will quit stealing from them.

        Calling me a liar is an insult I cannot let pass without response, and my response must be to leave this job. Being shat on wasn't enough to do it; being exposed to patients with deadly and wildly contagious bowel infections (as I currently am) wasn't enough to do it; having to spend two hours a week making polite conversation with a patient who thinks my being white means I welcome his attempts to educate me on the difference between black people and niggers (as I currently am) wasn't enough to do it; them refusing to give any staff raises while promising us bigger Christmas bonuses every year that never come wasn't enough to do it; but calling me a liar right to my face...I've been angrier, but only rarely have I been more offended.

        So, yeah. What I hate is that a company where I've worked longer than anyone else currently employed there has decided I can only be trusted if they implant a chip in the back of my neck like a fucking dog.
