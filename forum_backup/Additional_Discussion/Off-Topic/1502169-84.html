                                                        My vent now probably won't be very popular with the US members of the forums, but I have to do it.

                                                        Of course as a Brazilian I'm biased towards Santos Dumont instead of the Wright brothers as the forefather of aviation, but studying the history of aviation only made this bias into a full rage against how the brothers are treated as heroes by many.

                                                        The history of aviation is mostly a history of people collectively working on the dream of flight. Since the 19th century several people were working on techniques and improvements for flying with all kinds of contraptions. Ideas big and small that were then taken by the next one, everyone sharing their works and discoveries. Even Santos Dumont was just a piece in the great work of getting to the skies, and he knew it. His designs were made publicly available, and his Demoiselle went on to be not only the first series production aircraft (and one with zero fatal accidents to this day), but one of the main influences on future aircraft greatly because Santos Dumont licensed it for free to everyone, all versions he made.

                                                        But the Wright brothers were first and foremost selfish and narcissistic assholes. Their work was done in secret and the first thing they did was to not only patent everything they did, but make the patents in such a way as to cover for things they didn't create and to be as generic as possible and so cover any other possible innovation that even vaguely resembled their tech. They had the guts to write their patent in such a way that they could even claim patent over the aileron, a popular (and eventually vital) piece for airplanes that not only existed before, they didn't actually made or used it.

                                                        For years airplane makers were forced to either pay them or fight them fiercely in court for the right to make something, anything at all, as the US got engulfed in the Wright Brothers' Patent Wars. Things were easier outside the US, but still not ok, as they fought for their patents everywhere to try to force everyone to pay them. This was their actual public declarations, by the way, that they would make the world recognize their vital role in modern aviation and pay what they were owned, all the while ignoring how their work was also heavily based on contributions even by the very people they were suing, and how half their patent claims were bullshit (including the aileron that went to be the main piece of tech they made money with).

                                                        It got so bad that by the Great War the US aircraft industry was lagging heavily behind Europe and the US military had to simply buy airplanes for the war because nothing worth even trying was available domestically. Pressed by the military, the US government was forced to create the Manufacturers Aircraft Association to pool patents and finally unblock aircraft development and production. Several historians still argue that their Patent wars not only stalled the US aircraft industry, but in fact jeopardized (not impeded, but greatly slowed) the evolution of aeronautics for a decade.

                                                        What really makes Santos Dumont the father of aviation in my opinion isn't the 14-bis (the first airplane to fly publicly in a presentation widely recognized), but Airship Nº 6, then the Demoiselle, and more importantly his many contributions in this process that went on to be freely available for everyone to build upon and create the future of human flight.
