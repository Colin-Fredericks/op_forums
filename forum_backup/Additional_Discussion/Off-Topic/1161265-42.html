                                Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                Also, because I have nowhere else for this hot take: I hate halflings, and the only gnomes I like are 4e’s explicitly-fey ones.
                                I whole-heartedly agree with you re: Gnomes, although I didn’t care for their visual design in 4e. My problem with Gnomes is that their identity as a race has always been really weak. They’re always either shorter dwarves or tricksters, and don’t get me started about D&D treating “trickster” as a complete character concept and personality. Tinker Gnomes tried to give them a more distinct identity, but ultimately they’re still just shorter dwarves, this time with a side-helping of zany; sure, they focus on engineering rather than craftsmanship, but that’s basically the same thing only less thematically appropriate in a technologically limited setting. 4e Gnomes finally felt justified in existing as a separate race.

                                My Gnomes are basically 4e Gnomes with a different visual design, and instead of fleeing to the material plane to escape slavery in the feywild, it was to escape genocide. Mainly because my world borrows from Scarred Lands’ take on Halflings, and I didn’t want both Small races to be defined primarily by being former slaves.

                                As for Tieflings, I adore them. But only the 4e version. 4e gave them a shared history and place in the world, as opposed to pre-4e Tieflings, which were pretty much just people with Fiendish heritage. And as I said earlier in the thread, I’m not a fan of mixed-species races. 4e Tieflings mamage to get around the breeding compatibility question by making it a cursed bloodline, and also give them a clearer, more defined identity than just “part-fiend.”

                                    For me personally, the appeal of the fiends stems from the possibility of becoming one. There's an esoteric allure in the knowledge that most of the villains you slay over the course of the campaign, regardless if they're major threats or simple canon fodder, are sent to the lower planes to join the hordes of the Abyss, or legions of Hell.

                                    Transmigration (changing from one race / species into another) is one of my top favorite concepts in any fantasy setting. Lycanthropes, Undead, Aberrations, Fiends, doesn't matter. I just love the idea of being able to transform your character into a new creature. And if that transformation comes with some powerful benefits, then all the better!


                                    I would personally describe myself as Chaotic Neutral. My mind is cluttered with so many conflicting ideas that I can never commit to any of them before losing interest and chasing the next object of my interest. Some days I feel like being a villain, other days I feel like trying a hero, and some days I'm just "meh, let's see how this plays out".

                                                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                                        I actually hated that change, because it made the vast majority of Tieflings, if not all of them, hail from Devilish descent. I prefer Demonic freedom over Devilish tyranny, thank you very much. So when they decided to lump the whole race in as having sworn a pact with Asmodeus, my interest in them dropped a great deal.
                                                        At this point, I don’t really think of it as a change, any more than Requiem Ventrue are a change from Masquerade Ventrue. 4e’s setting is very different from Forgotten Realms, and its Tieflings are a completely different race that happens to share the same name. I just don’t find the FR Tieflings particularly interesting as a race. There’s not really much to them, beyond “has some Demon or Devil ancestry.” Nentir Vale Tieflings have a clearly defined role in the setting, and explore themes of inherited sin and self-defined identity. That they are specifically connected to devils as opposed to demons is a secondary concern to me at best.

                                                        To clarify though, Nentir Vale Tieflings don’t hail from devilish descent, nor did they all swear a pact to Asmodeus. It was the Turathi Aristocracy who swore a pact to Asmodeus, and for that, their bloodlines were cursed. Everyone with even a drop of Turathi noble ancestry bears the curse, regardless of their own deeds. Hence the theme of inherited sin - for the deeds of a few power-hungry nobles thousands of years ago, all of their modern descents, no matter how distantly related, face predjudice due to their appearance and the reputation of their ancestors. To me that’s much more interesting than “my grandma fucked an Inccubus.”

                                                        Nothing against the pre- (and post-) 4e Tieflings for those who prefer them. There will always be those who prefer variety over specificity, and there's nothing wrong with that. Just explaining where my own preferences lie and why.
