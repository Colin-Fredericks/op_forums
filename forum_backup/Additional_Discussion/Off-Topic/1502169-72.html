                                                        Eh... that's a fairly skewed look.

                                                        First, Snow White has been out longer (movies always drop in the box office sales over time) so another movie coming up and starting to compete with it isn't exactly special. A Working Man has only had a better daily domestic box office half of the days its been out, and only one of those days ways on a weekend. Again, no shock there, an action movie aimed at adult audiences is going to do better with the after-work folks with free time on weekdays than a family orient film, which does better on Saturday and Sunday when it's easier to wrangle the kids to an activity and so on. Yes, Snow White speedy domestic decline is an embarrassment for Disney, but Snow White - despite all the negative criticism about if from pretty much all sides of American life - had a far better opening weekend.

                                                        Second... maybe something to consider around "does Disney know what they're doing?" is to look at the international numbers. Snow White is still going to be a financial dud considering how much they spent on it, but when you look at the global box office instead of the domestic one... it's an entirely different set of numbers. The US is not the only country that buys movie tickets... and a lot of other countries don't have the same cultural norms around remakes (esp. the level of automatic nostalgia for the "original*" films that you tend to see in US audiences). It doesn't look like the international audiences are flocking to see it, but there also doesn't seem to be a serious impact from the America-centric controversy it's had. Meanwhile A Working Man isn't even in international distribution.

                                                        So, yeah, hard data is that Snow White was mishandled. But at the same time, it's not nearly as bad as some random anecdote like comparing it to a US-only action movie makes it look either (also a decade ago we were still in the era of things like the Taken franchise where these sorts of action movies were making huge numbers at the box office and were not direct to video mid stuff). Disney has probably long since crunched the numbers to know how to offset a big domestic backlash with international sales in case the movie doesn't do well broadly.

                                                        And, of course, never lose sight of the fact that those most responsible for the decisions that ensured Snow White was going to land with a thud (since Disney keeps plowing through with these "live action" remakes and they keep doing worse instead of audiences coming around to them), are rich and protected from the backlash, while lots of working folks trying to make ends meet are the ones that shoulder the burden of working on a box office bomb.

                                                        * - Lets remember that Disney built a huge amount of its animated catalogue on public domain stories, including Snow White, it's not something the company created from scratch.
