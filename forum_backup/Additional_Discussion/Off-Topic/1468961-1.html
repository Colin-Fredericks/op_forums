    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    I don't remember if it was mention in Van Richten, but the concept of soulless people took a lot from the setting without giving much in return, imo
    I'm inclined to agree, despite not being so bothered.

    But that's also a lot about my own philosophy. For me the idea that causing them to suffer is ok just because it is their defined purpose doesn't hold, and I think this is kind of the deal with them: their nature does not make them less deserving of kindness, and using it to justify your actions is itself a hallmark of evil.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    they have also decided to officially stuck Ravenloft in the Shadowfell because of, well, reasons?
    Actually as a natural development of previous decisions.

    WotC has been trying to shove Ravenloft out of their way for a long time, to be actually honest here. They made the Shadowfell to cover the same general role in 4th Edition and had been avoiding the setting itself like the plague. This actually goes back to 3rd edition, when they decided to not renew WW's license for the setting despite ongoing publication schedule then published the Expedition module in lieu of the rest of the Gazetteer series.

    Officially, despite nothing in the license impeding the module's publications.

    They made the Shadowfell and thoroughly shoved the concept of Domains of Dread there, since 4th edition. They published already eight Domains of Dread since then without mention to Ravenloft, and republished a number of Ravenloft Domains in the same manner including Barovia, Kalidnay and, IIRC, Stauton Bluffs.

    Yet, here we are. The setting have a strong following and the Mistipedia is still maintained and updated (although not every entry is of the same quality). At this point the structure of the setting is already anchored at the Shadowfell, though, so it is understandable that they preferred to keep it there.

    But enough of my ramblings, we have a tour to continue.

    BORCA

    I'm very satisfied with the overview of the land. Everything seems in place, and although they didn't mention that it is the birthplace of Ezra's faith, the Great Cathedral is still there, it's alliance with the Morninglord changed to the new Ulmist Inquisition. Excellent writing, no complains at all.

    Jumping to the session about Adventures and following tips on using Borca, it's also very good. With the sole exception of Intrigue in Borcastarting out kind of confusing and aimless, the write-up closes with very good advice, this time with many helpful tips to use other villains and plots than the Darklords.

    But I left those two for last for a reason. The Dark Twins have been a fixture in the setting for quite a long time. And here they got significantly changed. Some of it is just for the sake of change, or rather for the sake of defacing all previous editions as it seems. Some of it though, for good or ill, was to tweak the ways the two can be used in the table.

    Changes for the sake of change include making their history so that Borca is their shared domain from start. It doesn't really use up less of a word count than necessary to instead quickly summarize Ivana's inheritance from her mother and previous Darklord, and the merge with Ivan's original Domain of Dorvinia. Those details change nothing really, but help give a sense of dept to the place.

    Now, Ivana has a good chunk of her history reworked and is turned into a very different person in terms of how she relates to people. Her whole trope is changed from the Borgia-style Black Widow to the business woman that's never taken seriously. The poison is still kept as her signature MO, though. I'm torn on this because I do miss parts of her backstory, especially the role of Camille as the Legacy Villain, but on the other hand I am tired of the whole this woman is evil because romance shenanigans stuff. Now it does keep her love story as trigger, but her motivation is more interesting.

    On Ivan things are far more complicated. The changes are almost complete, and I don't like the new backstory that much. It abandons his original specific but exquisite murders (murdering the mother for the giggles, murdering the sister and her family when she have a son, making the incestuous overtones more clear), in favor of plain, sweeping mass murder. Cute, but Strahd already did it, and this time Ivana did too, it's getting old.

    The new Ivan is unrecognizable, but the old Ivan was too similar on practice to Ivana. In a sense they worked better as obvious counterparts, but now they offer more varied opportunities. He moved from being an inferior copy of Ivana with Gluttony instead of Lust as trademark to being like a Batman villain, Penguin mainly. Not sure if I like it, but among the changes made, it could easily be far worse, and the Domain itself can still work in the old style.

    THE CARNIVAL

    Oh, my goodness... my poor Isolde, what they did to you...

    So, given the constraints of being a short write-up I can totally forgive not delving into the tragedies of the troupers backstories, and the open-ended short description for each one isn't a bad thing. The Litwick Market is a new feature, and since it adds conflict I'm not against it. The Adventures section is short and very basic, though, and no mention goes to the Skurra or the Twisting, which is understandable but sad.

    And at the core of it Isolde got completely revamped. The new Isolde is just a retired Paladin paying attention to the antics of a sword that isn't even all that committed to evil to justify being a Darklord.

    I mean, Nepenthe isn't a bad character. It would make a fair addition to the wandering pseudo-domain by itself. But in the end, they should just not had put Carnival at all in the book, not to make it like this. With the themes they actually used, they could either have revamped Scaena in similar ways (I do like that Domain, but it has been abandoned for a long time and needs in fact a revamping to have broader use and appeal), or the old Shadowlands cluster, since Nepenthe is a mashup of Ebonbane and Elena Faith-hold anyways.

    DARKON

    Azalin Rex, the oldest and most powerful Lich in Ravenloft, disappeared at the culmination of a magical ritual infused with insane amounts of energy collected at the end of a long and complex conspiracy, in an event known throughout the land by a grandiose, ominous name. The land of Darkon is suffering under the swift and terrible energies released, magic scars left behind and causing havoc. No one knows whats happening or what if any future awaits for the most expansive kingdom in the Mists. The vampires of the Kargat conspire in the shadows and crush the population under their ruthless bullying and incentive to betrayal and violence. Castle Avernus is the center point of danger and death. Huge swaths of this land that weren't a problem in previous editions just became inhabitable.

    In other words, Thursday.

    Now, that's not to say that the domain isn't changed or that I liked it. Huge swaths of land are missing, although none that really matters. The Kargat is decentralized in their current infighting, which adds conflict but removes the sting of their efficiency. I think the domain could have taken other turns.

    Yet, Darkon is one of the simplest domains to use because you can throw any fantasy staple of D&D there and take it at face value, no need to adapt. Is a pretty standard fantasy land, minus warring nations, plus more undead. It's a refreshing presence sometimes, and its size contrasts with the sense of claustrophobia and isolation typical to other domains. All that remains as true as ever.

    The option of your players visiting Azalin's castle frozen mid-explosion was a nice touch, so much better that he isn't home. Il Aluk is back without any explanation, which is bad but expected. The sample contenders for his throne are interesting, diverse and cover multiple styles of game. I liked the new Baron Metus pretty much.

    My only true complaining, the nitpicks I mentioned, first that Azalin was also unnecessarily changed. He has always been presented as a Dr. Doom-like king: a ruthless tyrant, but also a competent ruler that aims for the benefit of his kingdom. Unlike Strahd he's not the worse evil for his subjects, as his torment is actually his inability to part ways with his duties for fear of seeing his legacy destroyed by less competent hands. Azalin is a good king 'cause he hates being the king, yet other threats in Darkon keep him in the throne.

    Second and a bit more serious, the Adventure section focus completely on stories revolving around Darkon's current ongoing apocalypse. Considering that this is an ongoing matter that can actually take centuries to come to a resolution, and they actually implemented changes that give so much to do in the mean time, I find it a disservice to their own work here to not give a good look at other options and let the Shroud be.

                Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                As long as we got this thread up, out of curiosity, what's everyone's favorite Dark Doman/Darklord?

                I've always been a fan of the Wildlands and King Crocodile. It's kind of got that evil fairytale/Disney vibe with all of the intelligent animals.
                That's a hard question for me, very hard. But I have a weak spot for stories that make power level irrelevant, approachable and interesting for everyone. Things like low level characters having pretty mundane audiences with Strahd or Azalin.

                So my particular favorite is probably the cursed theater Scaena and Lemont Sediam Juste. You can go wild with it, challenge your PCs with whatever impossible task you want, and still have them survive. Until Lemont decides to burn the place, that is.

                DEMENTLIEU

                What the hell. That was… interesting?

                They thoroughly changed Dementlieu, and the result is a mixed bag. This is one of the original domains from the 1990’s Black Box, and Dominic D’Honaire one of the most classic villains in Ravenloft. Also, I think one that would serve better at their current style of focusing more on the Darklord, as one of the main shticks here used to be the sheer reach of his web of manipulation and conspiracy (or that of his enemies).

                But this time we have a sweeping change with the place dotted with references, good and bad. The first joke is the whole thing with building the new Dementlieu completely around the title Masque of the Red Death (the Ravenloft-related setting of Gothic Earth). Yeah, funny… the domain is far more single-note and ultimately rid of the old theme of conspiracy and its cosmopolitan air. One thing I understand is that as a whole old Dementlieu would be more hurt than most by becoming an Island of Terror. The whole “Sinderela With a Twist” is too much on the nose, both on Saidra’s backstory and on the part of the Three Odd Gables. And of course, the domain hold little surprise after a time, since it makes a point to not have many real secrets, in opposition to old version.

                On the other side, the new theme definitely isn’t bad per se. Actually, it isn’t bad at all, just a little repetitive. This whole thing would work wonderfully as a new development along with old-style Dementlieu… and we may have it! Ok, kinda. For the trained eye the hints of the old domain are all over the place, and they made a good job in their selections of places to present, adventure advice and guiding the GM to use the Grand Masquerade as an event and plot device without making it solely about the duchess. Indeed, I must praise how much they put in it tools and ideas for adventures unrelated to Saidra even while keeping her presence overwhelmingly relevant. It is genuinely good.

                My specific observations: the domain went through the same thing Zherisia went through after the Conjunction, shrinking from its former size to just the capital. Yet, the map is almost the same. Port-a-Lucine now occupies the whole map of Dementlieu, and the two bays are still there. Some features of the kingdom were integrated into the city. The ditched Chateufaux from the map, which is a shame, but they added a wonderful mystery with everyone still referencing a place that doesn’t exists. The Cathedral are still in place, expected and ok. Lord Von Aubrecker is absent from the domain, but it seems he went back to Lamordia, and by the second idea in the Adventures Table we get that Dominic is actually still present! He’s a patient in a hospital and still have power to manipulate and dominate.

                I have to part ways saying that although I disagree with the narrower scope of the domain, I ended up liking it a lot. A few details are the difference between utter disrespect and a change that can actually pick the interest of an of fan, and here the latter was accomplished. Despite a little on the nose, Saidra is a good character, and the idea of a place where evil doesn’t even hide, yet people are whiling to mingle with monsters to pretend for a better, but normal life is priceless. I won’t be so kind in our next step, Falkovnia.

                    Originally posted by Amethyst View Post
                    I liked Van Richten’s overall.
                    I suggest you look for old Ravenloft books. Not in the sense of not using the 5th ed one, just because they're also good to add stuff to your games and most of the good parts aren't bound to any particular game system.

                    Particularly anything else that start with "Van Richten Guide to". Those are in-character books about several subjects and full of really interesting ideas on how to apply their themes to games. They're wonderful.

                    This new one is, curiously, the only book with such title that isn't in-character.

                    Now back to the review.

                    FALKOVNIA

                    The original Falkovnia would be the Domain most hurt by becoming an Island of Terror. Vlad Drakov is a failed conqueror, he thoroughly needs neighbors to conquer. Of course, the Island could provide this on itself anyways and still be smaller than Darkon, but other domains are a better fit. Drakov never realized his own predicament.

                    Now, the new Falkovnia does works well in this Island state. I just don’t like it.

                    Seriously, an entire domain the size of Falkovnia dedicated solely to a constant theme of Zombie Apocalypse? This could too easily be inserted as a plot without dedicating a main domain to it, much less sacrifice the place that best had the themes of totalitarianism and military state.

                    I don’t have much to add. The domain is well written and the hooks work well. The section on conducting the monthly siege is worth reading all by itself to conduct similar operations, and reminds me a little Legend of Five Rings 4th Edition’s rules for mass combat, which are pretty good for their focus on the PCs.

                    Vladeska will, by itself, hurt a lot of players. Screw them. She has the exact same backstory as Vlad, nothing changes beyond the domain focus itself. She would work fantastically well as his daughter and potential successor, maybe holding against this apocalypse in a remote area of the domain or a new domain all hers. And the art of her fighting the undead is very good, not perfect, but definitely good.

                    HAR’AKIR

                    Another old domain from the Black Box, another batch of deep changes I don’t quite get. First of all, why they changed the pantheon? I mean, it can’t be for safety reasons, they already have the Egyptian Pantheon in 5th edition and they say it out loud here. Ankhtepot’s story also don’t had anything particularly problematic. At the worse it had standard vilifying of Set, but that was nothing too relevant (it only mattered a little for his enemies after becoming Darklord, actually).

                    That puzzles me, but aside that it doesn’t matter a lot. They really delved into the theme of false religion here, but it isn’t hard to change it back and still use most of the write-up. Or not change, it isn’t criminally bad, just weird. That aside, the domain itself is better defined and described than on most previous iterations, and this description is very good. Places like the Breaths and the Bent Pyramid add a lot to it, as does the Labyrinth and the four oases instead of just Muhar. Muhar’s population soared ten fold: it’s now 3 thousand souls. Less quaint than before, but more useful as story device.

                    But not the same can be said of the characters. Those should be the meat of any Ravenloft write-up and we got changes that made everyone boring and same-ish. Some old names are still here, likeIsu Rehkotep, Senmet and Nephyr, but now they’re all bland and most are just Ankhtepot’s servants. The Darklord’s history changed completely, also. It isn’t completely bad, but it kind of defeats its purpose. The new lore have him first a far more empathetic soul and hardly Darklord material, then he gets a punishment that’s out of proportion for reasons, and finally he decides to create a false religion for reasons, since he isn’t actually getting much out of it, it goes counter to his depiction in the background and current, and plays no role in his ultimate goal of facing his gods judgment again.

                    Finishing this analysis, the Adventure ideas and the advice for using Ankhtepot’s Ka are good, but not by much. It’s flatly below the standards from most previous write-ups. The Ankhtepot Reborn table is especially bad, four suggestions for ending the quest that are either anti-climatic or predictable and avoid any actual change. Either Ankhtepot remains in power, leaves power but nothing changes, must be reinstated to power because change is bad, or the GM pushes the Apocalypse butom.

                    HAZLAN

                    Ok, this is the Domain that suffered more changes so far. Others had sweeping changes but are still arguably the same or similar place, Hazlan is completely unrecognizable and Hazlik almost as much. On the good side, I can say that the rules on wild magic are interesting and the general idea of a kingdom-size arcane laboratory gone wild is interesting. As in Har’Akir, a lot of useful places for adventures were added.

                    But they stripped the domain of its most interesting social problems. Hazlan dealt with oppression and racism, and Hazlik was hell bent on committing trans-universal genocide. Not anymore. His complex relationship with genre and transsexuality is also gone. At least he’s still gay, but outside that he’s just a simplified version of old Azalin in everything but body. And his tattoos are now golden and mean traitor or something.

                                        That would make sense and it is probably the reason behind some changes in it and in other domains, but for Har'Akir it doesn't actually add up.

                                        Again, the problem is that they outright say it was the Egyptian Pantheon. It is in a side box in the domain's write-up. Ankhtepot was a priest of the Egyptian Pantheon and the Akiri worshiped the Egyptian Pantheon when the domain formed.

                                                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                        The writers of the Domain have mentioned that it is meant to "gaslight" the players, to make them say "it doesn't make sense- or is it?" and overall confuse them. Now, I am not saying they did a good job with it- in fact, I agree that the execution is dubious saying the least- but that is the logic behind it.
                                                        I would like to see where the authors gave interview, I'm really curious about all of them. And while I believe the amount of change was probably a top-down command, I would like to know their reasoning on their decisions for execution.

                                                        Anyways, the next batch of domains is here.

                                                        I’CATH

                                                        This was a seriously unexpected entry. Even counting every use in supplements, novels and adventure modules, I’Cath has only been published twice, in the first Islands of Terror of 1992 and in the 1997’s Domains of Dread.

                                                        On the other hand, we can pretty much say it still stands, because this iteration is the same only in name. Even Hazlan seems more akin to the original, almost nothing from previous I’Cath was kept, just few names and the Palace of Bones. Everything that made Tsien Chiang tick as Dark Lord was stripped from her and replaced.

                                                        Which is actually good, because I’Cath was horrible. I’Cath was basically a roofless dungeon with a linear plot and no people to meaningfully interact, even Nightingale was too shallow and had a simple role in putting the adventurers on the top of her tower, otherwise it was like a dumb re-enact of Legend of Zelda with less palaces. Tsien Chiang had a wonderful premise as a powerful evil lord and wizard before it was common to give such roles to women without caveats, but it was wasted with nonsensical misandry (she did had a reason to start, but it didn’t actually went anywhere or did anything aside being repeated ad nauseum) and generally being evil for the sake of it in an extremely cartoonish way. She was like a combination of Cruella DeVil and Maleficent, but distilled of any interesting quality.

                                                        New I’Cath combines several almost-abandoned old domain concepts into a new, cohesive whole. It is a bit of the Nightmare Lands (just a bit), a lot of old Timor (especially the background before the Marikit Queen) and, of course, some I’Cath (actually less than Timor), along with the more-than-once used trope of despair against possible starvation (strong, but not extreme as in G’Henna).

                                                        As a whole the domain is well written and the dual aspect of ream and reality works well, with good rules on how to navigate them. The new daughters of Tsien Chiang (the only thing that still significantly numbers four) are simple (by design) but interesting. I only miss better illustrations, both the daughters and the dream-city streets begged for visualization in place of the Jiangshi attack. The city also have several factions that can be approached in more than one way, with the Adventure hooks including a Jiangshi as quest giver, a nice touch.

                                                        Chiang is well written, but as other in the book her new backstory is maybe too much sympathetic. It is hard to see her as the same level of evil as Strahd or Azalin, as she not only have a good start, but actually many good intentions without so much of a falling arc that entirely justifies her predicament. She seems more delirious than really evil, vengeful streak aside. I miss some aspects of old Tsien Chiang a little, especially her obsession with the number four, but all in all, good riddance.

                                                        My only real criticism and parting thought is that more than a hint of the old one-tone of I’Cath was maintained. The write-up is pretty explicit that whatever adventures you run on I’Cath, the party will have to disturb Tsien Chiang’s Dream City to escape I’Cath, and little is given as adventure material outside setting to that as greater goal. It isn’t impossible to use I’Cath in other ways, but the book won’t help you.

                                                        KALAKERI

                                                        Here we have a domain that changed even the name! But I must say it makes perfectly sense. The problem with the old name Sri Raji is that it is, actually, the Hindi equivalent of naming your domain “Mister President”. So they found a compromise in changing the name to Kalakeri, then in the text saying that outsiders gave it several other names through the ages, including “Sri Raji”. A clever solution to a stupid problem. From now on on this text I’ll use Sri Raji when talking about the old domain and Kalakeri when talking about the new version.

                                                        Now, Sri Raji was a special case in old Ravenloft, in the sense that it was the worse offender in being a British stereotype of a non-European culture, as if it came directly from a 19th century Gothic Horror book written in London. It had zero traits that didn’t boil down to that standard racist description of India. While I’m against the way they choose to change things by unannounced retcon, I have to agree that changing this domain was a necessity. I’m not so sure about Arijani’s background, though.

                                                        Simply put, the history of the three Vasadavan siblings fight over the land is good, but making Ramya the Darklord doesn’t work so well. She’s mad, but she is the wronged party and the lesser evil of the three all things considered. She’s an interesting character and definitely need to be put down to rest, but she just don’t make the cut for Darklord, much less under the shadow of the guy who made Rakshasas seem like decent citizens. It is evidently worse with the new version of Arijani, albeit strange, being still a worse person and cut for Darklord, maybe in conjunction with Inajira (I’m not sure of what to make of this new version of Inajira, but since they changed Strahd’s pact, lets put it to rest for now).

                                                        Aside the Darklord identity, Kalakeri works remarkably well. The trio of villains and their constant conflict make for a great background for any number of adventures without need to make them the actual focus, despite still having a strong impact. The use they make of the Renown system is simple but very good, giving GM and PCs alike the tools to figure out how to manage their relation with the two warring factions.

                                                        KARTAKASS

                                                        They kicked up a notch the whole concept of “domain of music” here. Where Kartakass used to be a collection of quaint villages in the woods, now it is Big Time Show Business! I’m not sure of how much it works as domain, but it surely is unique. Even the beasts and trees are artists here, and the horror comes from the ugly parts of the entertainment industry. And werewolves, of course.

                                                        On one hand, small villages and folk music are far more my thing, so I can’t say a lot about the change myself. On the other hand we have more settlements and an easy method for thinking up more and giving them unique feels. The domain have lots of opportunities for adventure without involving the Darklord and tracking reputation as celebrities is an interesting change of pace, but the single note of it may become tiring after a time and it isn’t so refreshing right after another, more complex take on the reputation system.

                                                        No more is Harkon Lukas a Wolfwere. This is a disappointment for me, that liked the finer details that made him and his domain different from lycanthropes. But then, this is such a fine detail that it may well be unnecessary baggage for some, who am I to say? Now this is a domain of both standard and slightly stronger Werewolves, and Lukas curses people with Lycanthropy on purpose. Changing back probably wouldn’t be a big deal, though, just re-purposing the werewolf statistics.

                                                        It makes more of a difference on the characters presented. Harkon itself underwent less changes than many other Darklords, the biggest one being that instead of detached due to living in a rural area nor worth of his ambitions, now he’s fully engaged with it and yet is cursed to be forgotten in short notice, buried beneath the sheer amount of new artists. His backstory had a few tweaks to be simplified, which I thought unnecessary, but the base is the same.

                                                        Greater difference is in his children, for anyone who cares. Akriel and Casimir Lukas are back and have some role in the domain, even if minor. They are human now, and work more like references to their previous versions than filling the same roles in the story. Still, they can be used for a range of purposes, so it isn’t a big loss.

                                                        Next time we'll have Lamordia, and a huge lot of rant, then Mordent, the first I just skipped because I decided to write this review, so it will be a complete surprise, hence I'm not promising to do Richemulot in the same batch.

                                                        With this we're getting near to the final run of domains. Tepest and Valachan I had an en passant reading and at least they seem to follow through their plot more than retcon them, keeping the legacy of the old Darklords. After that, a collection of old and new domains in brief descriptions, and we finally move to other parts of the book. Maybe the beginning, maybe the end, I'm open to suggestions.
