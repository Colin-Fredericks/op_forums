                            Injection is going well. A Warren Ellis story, basically a think-tank was created in the UK to predict the future of the UK and the world and see what they could do with it. They all predicted a sort of technological flatline with no real advances for a long time. One of the members is a 'cunning man' and after great reluctance decides to help them inject something into the system that should keep things from going stale. Everyone involved regrets it. Very interesting story, but Ellis is good at that, sort of out there at points. There's unfortunately going to be a hiatus for a while, presumably they'll be gathering the issues into a TPB.

                                                            I remember when I tried to give Captain Marvel a chance. This was before that run, technically she was still Ms. Marvel, but I'll spoiler it anyways.
                                                            It was during or after the Civil War, and she thought a captured Skrull on a SHIELD helicarrier had some information about her boyfriend. It wanted to be let out of its power-dampening cell in order to give her the answer. It was against regulations and, if I recall correctly, others on board said absolutely not. She went off and did something else, then came back and pulled the Skrull out and demanded the information.

                                                            The Skrull blew up, causing the helicarrier to blow up. Workers fall and die graphically, and it was over a town, no one was able to act to save it in time so they just had to clean up the wreckage afterwards. Captain Marvel was sad about what happened. So the next night she went to Wonder Man's apartment, explained it, then they had sex and she was better and went to help the Avengers. It was the end of the story.


                                                            I just didn't get it. I mean, I get that Marvel, at least some of it, tries to make characters who are people first, heroes second. But it seemed so surreal. She had even backed Tony's side in the Civil War.

                                                            Anyways, writing female protagonists seems to be a near-insurmountable issue for most writers. When I was getting back into comics I asked around and I'd hear things like, 'So-and-so is great! But don't read issues 1-200, just read 201-206 and don't read the next fifty.' Which didn't give me much confidence in the characters.

                                                            All that said, I really enjoyed Batgirl's entire run. That's Cassandra Cain, who had the first Batgirl ongoing. The ending was a bummer but I thought it was written well, even if it got jerked around by events. And Madame Xanadu's Vertigo run, though I'm not sure if that would count. There must be more, I just can't think of them at the moment.

                                                            People have been raving about Gwenpool and Kamala Khan, though it's not my thing.
