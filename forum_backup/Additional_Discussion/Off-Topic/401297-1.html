                    From what I hear that's the big issue. A lot of people seem to live in towns where no one else plays these games and there's no store, or they've always wanted to play but don't have any friends who share the same hobby.

                    I started playing with some guys in my neighborhood when I was a kid, then in the late 90s with some people online, then with them in meatspace, which is how we do it now.

                    I've heard of other people just meeting up at their local gaming store, or actually starting meetups and other social gathering things. Colleges of decent size probably have at least one club revolving around role-playing games. I know personally some groups of people who just met in college and stayed relatively close afterwards, they still play and occasionally invite new people. My latest local gaming store was near a high school, and there were often gaggles of teenagers there after school ended so I figure they've spread the hobby among their coterie. There was a not-quite Renaissance fair around here too, more of a fantasy exposition where they had gaming, and of course there's conventions.

                            Word of mouth/active recruiting. I have a few friends who never played a TTRPG in their lives before I met them, and got them to play campaigns with me just to see what it was like. They, in turn, know other people who "might be interested", and over time you can put together a small community of people you can hypothetically tap for games.

                            Mind you, that doesn't entirely solve the problems of people living too far apart or having busy schedules.

                                I've not played much either in person or online, really, but I hope to be able to get into it a bit more this year. I'm a bit of a mixed bag. Ideally, I would have a gaming session that's completely like an in-person group, but where it was possible to sometimes switch to textual communication. I love hanging out with people I like, and I've no problem goofing off or chatting in character, but as soon as it comes to describing scenes or complicated events like planned speeches and stuff, I have a really hard time.

                                Online has other advantages as well, for example that I've (unfortunately) lost contact with most of my old friends as we scattered across the world and so don't have many connections to get into a game through. Finding a group is much easier online, with the trade off that you have less of an investment.

                                I'm going to get into a university gaming club as soon as I get around to it, and I hope to get to play in at least an online game once Exalted 3e finally materialises.

                                                        I play in person when I do play. It is what I prefer, but a compatible group and the right game is important to me.

                                                        I have tried forum/email based RP, but while it in theory should fit me like a glow, it just never worked. In part because my available energy and time tend to fluctuate, and if I'm not sure about how to write a post when time and energy is sparse, it might take long enough for the game to die.

                                                        I have been thinking about trying out text, voice or video based chat to RP. Either as a mean to not be geographically limited at all when putting a group together, or just to be able to have a bit of RP in between the times when a group can meet up in person.

                                                        We have a fairly active online community for roleplayers here in Sweden, and there is a monthly forum meetup at a pub in the city I live in. So beside talking about RPGs with people around me from time to time, which in part is a long shot recruitment strategy, the online community makes it more of a matter that I probably shouldn't be so picky of what games I want to run or whom I game with
