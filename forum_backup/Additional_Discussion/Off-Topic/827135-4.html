                You're shifting goal posts a bit there.

                1) Pushups (which are focused exclusively on muscle groups high testosterone individuals have an easier time building) are a horrible metric of overall fitness, and yes, the dominance of using them as part of fitness metrics is sexist.

                2) You didn't mention sit-ups and mile and a half runs. I've seen plenty of women easily beat 85 and 9:45 of both. You mentioned sprints and weight-lifting. "Explosive" strength tests (aka ones that rely heavily on short duration and high exertion) heavily favor individuals with higher testosterone levels (aka are biased towards male sexed humans). High testosterone levels lead to increased muscle mass, which is good for such exercises. Individuals with lowers testosterone levels have an easier time building up muscle tone, which produces lower "raw power" but greater efficiency in removing lactic acid build up (aka is better for endurance).

                3) That age gap is also significant. An 18 year old any human is generally not at their overall physical peak. They're not even done fully going through puberty (most people don't totally finish puberty until 20-22); even if most of their development is neurological at that point. It takes time to develop a high level of physical fitness. A 25 year old should outperform an 18 year old assuming they're dedicating equal efforts towards achieving a set of fitness goals. There's a reason why individuals that pass special forces fitness testing average in age ~30. It takes years of training to achieve (without going past the point of when the human body starts to down turn in physical ability). In most sports - especially ones considered highly taxing because they require a number of aptitudes - the average peak performance for professions is 25-30 years old.

                So yes, the fitness tests our military uses are sexist. This isn't even controversial. It's clear from military records that this was done on purpose when women in uniform were going to be allowed in combat zones, and it's not exactly something the DoD contests in their attempts to fight allowing women into combat roles.

                                        Originally posted by Piff View Post
                                        Oh, take my word on it, this is NOT unrelated to sex.
                                        It is correlated to sex, because testosterone levels are correlated to sex. As I was saying above, whether naturally occurring or artificially stimulated, it's high testosterone levels that actually dictates how the body responds to weight lifting exercise (building muscle mass vs. building muscle tone); though type of exercise matters as well.

                                        When it comes to the records you're citing, average body size also plays a factor (hence weight classes) because the moves in question (since it isn't just "can you do this lift," there are specific regulations around form) are designed to have a biophysical structural limit where even if someone could get the weights up they couldn't keep the stance without sufficient body mass. The full moves in question require overall high levels of body strength, but the postures in specific are calibrated to high levels of upper body strength. It actually wouldn't be that hard to design weight lifting events that are more balanced between different body types, which would close the gap between the men and women in the same weight class in the current records.

                                        Dudes are stronger than women in lots of ways, but more or less the most amazing differences are in arm stuff (like bench pressing).
                                        Yep. Though again it's testosterone levels, not sex (though those correlate strongly) that determines things like distribution of muscles to the arms and pecs which leads to the perception that male = greater upper body strength.

                                        And, of course, this is still only measuring strength in terms of "what's my maximum output in a small number of short efforts." If you take people that have all been weight training at roughly equal levels, and figure out what the maximum they can bench-press at high reps and high sets per unit of time, the difference shrinks fairly rapidly.

                                        Those big muscles that allow such impressive maximum bench presses suck at endurance because of basic physics. Their size makes it harder for the body's methods of clearing exercise by-products out, and replenishing necessary nutrients and chemicals, This is why marathon runners aren't muscle bound. Big muscles add weight that makes running more work, without adding endurance.

                                        ------------

                                        There's also the distribution of fast and slow twitch muscle types. This is explicitly not sex-related. A given individual's distribution greatly influences performance in various athletic events. A person with significantly higher than average fast-twitch distribution is going to cream someone with below-average fast twitch distribution at things like sprints. This transcends sex. You just don't see the impact of this when you do things like look at Olympic sprinters because they all have extremely high fast-twitch muscle distributions to get their in the first place. Likewise you can't be a world class marathon runner without hitting the non-sex related genetic lottery to slow twitch muscle distribution. And there's plenty of sports where a balanced distribution is more optimal.

                                        And you don't turn into a crazy muscle monster either, I still look slim and feminine!
                                        So... getting back to terminology over biology...

                                        This is not helpful language. This simply perpetuates the idea that cis-gender women's average biological reaction to weight lifting is what a "real woman" should look like, setting another meaningless standard for trans-women to try to live up to for society to accept them as women.

                                                    Originally posted by garhkal View Post

                                                    This is why I go back to self-selection. The US military forces don't recruit women for combat roles (consider that we've barely scratched the surface of forcing them to allow women to test for them), so they don't actively recruit highly athletic women, while they have much heavy recruitment efforts aimed at athletic men. This is partially enforced by cultural elements such as very few women perceive athletics during academics as path towards a career so they have career plans outside of sports. Men are encouraged to see sports as a possible career, so there's a much larger group of male athletes coming out of higher education that don't make the cut for professional sports and don't really have something else they're dedicated to.

                                                    Since military recruitment efforts are gender disparate in the first place, it skews their expectations and subsequently the tests they put people through. The military recruits women with an eye towards jobs that don't have high physical requirements (at least not ones they test for, talk to nurses about the physical stress they have to put up with...) because their idea for where to put women is in support roles. Men are recruited for combat roles (up until recently all combat role recruiting was aimed solely at men), which informs the physical requirements.

                                                    This is the thing with institutional sexism. It's build down into the core assumptions of the institution. You can't just change one thing (open combat roles to women) and expect it to fix the whole system. You have to change the whole attitude of the institution to see women and men as individuals, and recruit them based on their individual strengths rather than use sex as a filter.
