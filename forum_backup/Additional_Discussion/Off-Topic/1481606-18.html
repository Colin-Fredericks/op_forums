                                            I saw two movies that I liked recently:

                                            The first one is Conclave, directed by Edward Berger. He did that recent "All quiet on the western front" WWI movie. It was... okay. I wasn't the biggest fan but I know some people really liked that one. Conclave is a mystery-thriller about the election of the new pope. And what makes it work is that it has this nice airport-book kind of pacing where you got a central mystery, a lot of twists and turns and things just keep moving. It's very pulpy in that regard. And to be honest the movie ended up being far more idealistic than I had expected. But the thing that elevates it is its cast. Ralph Fiennes, Stanley Tucci, John Lithgow... and the rest of the cast. All great actors and this movie wouldn't work without them. There's a lot of talking and a very limited cast so this movie heavily relies on the actors making this work - but they succeed. Really loved this one.

                                            I also saw Clint Eastwood's newest movie Juror Nr. 2. You definitely notice that it's a Clint Eastwood film with some decisions he made... like making the most prejudicious, gung-ho members in the jury the two black people in the room. And Toni Collette plays a prosecutor who's running for office but every time people talk about her becoming a politician, there's so much condescension in the word. Stuff like "You used to be a lawyer, now you're a... 'politician'." That's obviously Clint's direction at work there ( lol ). But there's some great direction as well. The essential trolley-problem is that the central character, a nice family man, accidentally ran over a woman at night in pouring rain. He didn't see that he hit her and her body was flung into a gorge and in the darkness he didn't see her. So he just drove off. But the reason she left the bar was because she was in a fight with her boyfriend. A boyfriend who's a criminal and who has been to prison before. So he gets blamed for the death. And at some point, the main-character talks himself into thinking that maybe the boyfriend should just go to prison. He's a criminal after all. But then the movie does this great thing where the main-character tries to make the case for this and Nicholas Hoult sells this well because he sounds so cowardly and desperate while arguing his case. I would recommend that one as well.
