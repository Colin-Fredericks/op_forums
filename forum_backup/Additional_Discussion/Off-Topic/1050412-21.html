    The anthologies (there were three total in the original Dark Destiny series) were released in 1994-1995 and were edited by Edward Kramer, who at the time had already edited a number of similar projects and was a founder of Dragoncon (which was the big con in Atlanta, where the original WW was located).

    He was first formally accused of sex offenses in 1997, arrested on different charges in 2000 but was let out on house arrest due to a severe injury that left him home bound for years, this allowed him to be free enough to be caught by the police with a minor in a motel room in in Connecticut in 2011, in 2013 he was finally taken back to Georgia to face trial for the 2000 charges (and some additional tacked on stuff) and plead guilty (though is appealing the plea now).

    So... there's in terms of a timeline, you'd have to not even be looking to know that Kramer was a child abuser when WW republished the work with his name on it. Kramer didn't actually write any of the stories IIRC, but WW failing to acknowledge his crimes at all if they're adding content to the book (it's not like it was just another piece of the original WW backlog finally making it to DTRPG, Martin added a story) - or doing anything like pledging some of the profits to a relevant charity or whatever gesture to show some sensitivity towards it - I think understandably leaves a bad taste in some people's minds.

    It also adds to the continuing narrative that the new White Wolf has no idea how to respectfully treat the subject matter they want to address through their art, as they are so far 0/2 for their handling of it in a PR sense, and the quality of their releases so far doesn't exactly seem to be having people rushing to not care about shitty PR.

                                From an Onyx Path writer who wishes to remain anonymous:

                                Hey folks,

                                Let’s talk about the weird and wonderful world of TTRPG publishing. Sometimes it can be really difficult deciding whether you want to support a given project. Like, say, right now. The confusion around World of Darkness branding and White Wolf company structure is confusing to me, and I’ve created content for Onyx Path! And right now seems a great time to talk about that, and maybe clear up some confusion about who gets your money when you support a project - because it’s not as straight-forward as you’d think.

                                So. White Wolf is now owned by parent company Paradox Interactive. Paradox Interactive is the umbrella company for both Paradox Development - their video game studio which develops games like Europa Universalis and Crusader Kings. The people at Paradox Development have nothing to do with hiring or content decisions made at White Wolf. At all. Nor do they have any ability to influence what happens with White Wolf strategy decisions. So you can support Paradox Development projects without endorsing decisions being made by White Wolf, or vice versa.

                                Onyx Path is a completely separate company, albeit composed of many former White Wolf freelancers who wanted to keep making World of Darkness content after their tabletop operations folded. Onyx Path licenses World of Darkness IP from White Wolf to create content, such as the Chronicles of Darkness and the 20th Anniversary line of games. While Onyx Path isn’t perfect, and they’ve had some notable missteps, for the most part their policy is to pursue diversity in hiring. The work that I’ve done for Onyx path remains some of the work I am most proud of, and the people I have had the privilege of working with are fantastic, thoughtful people whose work is well worth supporting.

                                Just as with Paradox Development, Onyx Path has no say in decisions that get made at White Wolf. In fact, they have even less say, because Paradox at least has a business relationship beyond “licensing an IP from them”. So you can support Onyx Path content without endorsing decisions made by White Wolf. And honestly, the 20th Anniversary and Chronicles of Darkness content created by Onyx Path so far should keep you up to your eyeballs in quality, thoughtful, and diverse WoD content for years to come. (Also notably, Pugmire and Cavaliers of Mars are also Onyx Path properties, not White Wolf.)

                                Further. It’s also important to address the fact that there were quite a few projects in the content pipeline when the current change of leadership took place. Game development is slow, friends. One project I wrote for Onyx Path took a good two years from start to publication, and I know others have taken longer. It’s also crucial to remember that White Wolf uses freelancers and other non-employees to produce content. So when considering what to support or not support, remember that freelancers on one project have absolutely ability to influence decisions being made by White Wolf. I say this specifically because I do know excellent people currently engaged to do content creation for White Wolf, and while they are certainly unhappy about the current state of affairs, they’re also unhappy about being punished for decisions that had nothing to do with them.

                                Everyone has to make a decision about where to draw the line when deciding to support or not support a company. But in this instance, I would urge people to look at the timelines, and inquire the content creators if need be, and make an exception for content that was in production when a given decision took effect. The people creating content during the transition don’t deserve to be punished for the decisions made by people hiring them to work on contract, and there are some great people with content in the pipeline that deserves to be engaged with.

                                [If you want to spread this open letter, please copy-paste and spread the word!]﻿
