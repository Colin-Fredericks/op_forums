                                    Originally posted by Accelerator View Post

                                    OK then. Are there any list of spells you give to newbies for wizards?
                                    Getting back to this one, because no one seems to want to answer it.

                                    I can only speak for 5e, but there are a few spells that are advantageous for Wizards (specifically Wizards, since every class's spell list is different):
                                    • Mage Armor: An essential pick for Wizards, since they're pretty vulnerable otherwise. They have no proficiency with normal armor, and casters can't cast spells while wearing armor they aren't proficient in. Mage Armor gives the character an AC of 13 + Dex Mod, while by default a Wizard's AC is going to just be 10 + Dex Mod.
                                    • Magic Missile: It scales poorly in the late game, since it shoots three magic darts that do very little damage apiece. But in the early game, that's often all you need. The main benefit of using Magic Missile over other spells, though, is that it's an automatic hit (unless the opponent used a Shield spell). When you absolutely HAVE to do some amount of damage, there are worse options.
                                    • Sleep: It puts creatures to sleep. A'doy. You roll some dice to get a quantity of hit points, and then that many points worth of enemies fall asleep, starting with the lowest HP creature and working up. Good for taking a bunch of weak (or weakened) foes out of a fight. It's also good if your character doesn't want to go around killing people as a first recourse.
                                    • Chromatic Orb: Does a fair whack of damage against a single target. Main advantage to it is that you decide what elemental damage type the spell uses. Fire, Cold, Lightning, Thunder, or Acid.
                                    • Disguise Self: Change your appearance for an hour. I don't think I need to explain why this is useful.
                                    • Detect Magic: Allows you to see magical effects or sense magical items, via auras. Good for gathering information. Lasts ten minutes. Further, it's a Ritual spell, meaning you can not only Ritual cast it without expending a spell slot, but as a Wizard you can do so without Preparing it that day. Wizards can Prepare (or, in previous editions, Memorize) only a certain number of spells per day (technically per Long Rest). But Ritual spells don't have to be Prepared (if they are Ritual casted), because the Wizard references their spellbook.
                                    • Alarm: Another Ritual spell. Rigs up a magical alarm around an entrance (doorway, window, trapdoor, etc) or around a 20 square area. When creatures not excluded by you cross these thresholds or enter the area, it either makes an audible alarm or silently alerts you in your mind (that can wake you from sleep), your choice. A single Alarm lasts 8 hours, which conveniently is exactly the length of a Long Rest.
                                    • Shield: Is cast using a Reaction, allowing you to use it in response to a coming attack. You erect a barrier that raises your AC by +5 (and makes you immune to Magic Missile) until the start of your next turn. If you absolutely have to avoid damage, this will often, though not always, save your bacon.
                                    • Find Familiar: You summon a small animal companion that is connected to you magically, and does what you want. It's not strong enough to attack, but you can cast spells through it that have a range of "Touch". More importantly, you can use an Action to see through its eyes and hear through its ears for a turn, allowing you to scout out locations. A Familiar, once summoned, stays around indefinitely, unless it dies or you dismiss it. Killed Familiars can be brought back by casting the spell again.

                                                    Yes, this was very much a problem with 4e. The math was so tight that if you didn’t build your character efficiently, and/or if the DM didn’t provide the right magic items at the right pace, it was easy to end up falling behind the expected curve and making the game too hard for yourself. This was why I got into character optimization, and why I was really scared to start DMing.

                                                    Originally posted by ElvesofZion View Post
                                                    The only other issue I have had with the power system was that for all my newer players (that came from games like WoW) it gave them a very gamey way of looking at DnD; one that has carried into 5th actually. Its why DnD in general has actually fallen in my list of games I truly enjoy; so much focus on combat and maths.
                                                    I can see how this is a problem for a lot of folks, though personally, I don’t mind. D&D is a game, and as such I think it’s appropriate to look at it in a gamey way. For sure D&D can be overly mathy, especially 3 and 4e. 5e has taken some steps to simplify the math, but it’s still more mathematically complex than I think would be ideal. And I get wanting to focus more on making decisions in-character than on damage per round and other combat statistics. But personally I think that comes down more to DMing style than rulesets. You can DM 4e in such a way that encourages that kind of roleplay, just as you can DM 5e in such a way that combat becomes the main focus.

                                                    I also find that a lot of people’s problems with any edition ultimately stem from trying to run it like they did a different edition. 4e does not work well if you try to run it like 3e, which is a big part of why I think it frustrated so many people. You can sorta run 5e like 3e, and it won’t fight you as hard as 4e did for it, but it’s still a better game if you discard all assumptions from previous editions and run it as it advises you to.
