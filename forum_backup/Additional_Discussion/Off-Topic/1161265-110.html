        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        Eh, I wouldn’t worry too much about that. Yeah, it is technically a True Polymorph effect, but it’s a highly specific example of one, and has no mechanical impact at all. That seems like exactly the sort of design space Common magic items are meant to cover - cool narrative effects with little to no mechanical impact.

        I’d say a consumable item like a potion with a one-time, permanent spectrum slide effect would be well within the reasonable bounds for a Common item, regardless of whether or not the same effect can be achieved repeatedly with a high level spell.
        Or, alternatively, you could make it an item like a ring (whose effect lasts as long as it's worn), but it's also a cursed magic item that can't be removed. Obviously, if the character wants to have their sex changed, it's hardly a curse, but for everyone else it's baleful enough. And it can serve as a pretty funny scenario...or not so funny, since an unwanted sex change can potentially be traumatic, or at least distressing, depending on the person. Either way, a good engine for stories, humor, and/or drama.

        It's also generally a good way to introduce an unwary party to the concept of cursed magic items. The mechanical effect is limited to nonexistent, but can be punitive enough from a roleplay perspective to deter players from putting on any random ring they find. Then again, if you're going to use cursed items a lot, I'd also recommend making it so you can't automatically figure out what magic items are just by examining them during a short rest. Makes the Identify spell actually useful, too. But that's neither here nor there.

                            Also, like... forced sex changing aside, I find the core idea behind "teaching players a lesson" by sprinkling your dungeons with "funny" cursed gotcha items highly suspect. Like, you as DM are in full control of what magic items your PCs find. "Don't just go around putting on magic rings" is only a lesson that needs to be learned if you decide to include magic rings that shouldn't be put on. Now, there's nothing inherently wrong with games in which players need to be cautious about magic items. That creates a certain tone and feel that might be desirable for you and/or your players. But if you do want to run such a game, why on earth would you think springing a cursed magic item on them with no warning is a good way to "teach that lesson?" I'd think a much better way would be to simply tell the players "in this world, some items carry curses, so you should always be careful when you find magic items. Using them before you've had a chance to remove any potential curses is a big risk." Lesson learned, and no one needed to be surprised by a shitty thing happening to their character with no warning.

                                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                        Also, like... forced sex changing aside, I find the core idea behind "teaching players a lesson" by sprinkling your dungeons with "funny" cursed gotcha items highly suspect.
                                        That's largely the result of a very different attitude towards gaming back then. Dungeons and Dragons was built out of an antagonistic war-gaming system rather than a cooperative storytelling game. So early D&D was expressly designed as a game of players vs game master. The game master was explicitly out to kill the players in a "fair" way while the goals for the players were to successful delve through the dungeon, avoiding the traps and killing the monsters. Player death was common and even expected, and cursed magical items were a part of that game design.

                                        Of course early D&D was heavily flawed in a lot of ways, and RPGs have evolved quite a bit since then. Still, I think there's plenty of room in settings for cursed magic items in games, even cursed items that don't immediately kill or harm players. It's certainly not hard to imagine a kobold tribe creating a few cursed magic items to leave around their lair in case of an adventurer attack. Once word gets out that the kobolds have a bunch of cursed magic items nobody is going to be much interested in invading that lair for kobold loot.
