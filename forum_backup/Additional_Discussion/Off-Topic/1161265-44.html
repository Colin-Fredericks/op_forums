                        Following rules as long as they benefit you is neutral. Anarchy isn’t following rules as long as they benefit you. Anarchy is no rules, which is chaotic. Civil anarchy is chaotic good.

                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                        There's a reason why the road of the (earlier) Paladin is supposed to be so difficult. So, for the sake of maintaining order and stability, the Paladin is forced to set personal feelings aside and wait for another chance to put the villain on trial.
                        Again, this is why I track ideals separately from actions. Vigilantism is by definition not lawful, but the Paladin who turns to vigilantism still holds lawful ideals, even if he allows his actions to betray his own ideals. If you’re running an old-school game where a paladin loses his powers if his alignment shifts, then that betrayal would probably cause such a loss of power.

                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                        Chaotic Good characters, however, operate with good intentions, but the way in which they go about fulfilling those intentions is morally dubious. If somebody who they know for a fact was guilty was found 'innocent' in court, they would be the person waiting outside the court house for a chance to shoot them in the face as soon as they stepped through the doors. Think about characters like The Punisher, or Spawn, both of whom have no problem resorting to excessive violence, torture and murder as long as it helps them take down the villains.
                        First of all, it’s not moral dubiousness that makes a character chaotic. The law/chaos scale is about policy, not morality. Second of all, chaos as an ideal isn’t about ignoring the rules when they’re inconvenient, it’s about believing that you are better off without rules at all. Lawful as an ideal isn’t about following laws even when they get in the way, it’s about believing that laws are necessary for a functioning society.

                        If you believe people should be expected to give up some personal freedoms for the benefit of society as a whole, you hold lawful ideals. If you believe that you are better off retaining those personal freedoms, even at the cost of societal welfare, then you hold chaotic ideals. Neutrality is by definition a lack of ideals, so if you don’t have a strong opinion either way on the matter, you are idealistically neutral with respect to law and chaos.

                        But humans (and in fantasy settings, humanoids), don’t always act on their ideals. Batman, for instance, is a classic example of a character with lawful good ideals, who acts outside the law on a regular basis. If he were a character in one of my games, he would write Lawful Good on his character sheet because that’s what he believes in. But his “true” alignment - the one that that I keep track of, that shows up with detect alignment spell, that serves as the determining factor in a paladin keeping his powers, and that is determined by a character’s actions rather than their ideals - that would likely end up neutral good. To turn to chaotic, he would have to start actively working to dismantle societal structures, rather than just acting outside of them when they’re inconvenient for him.

                                    My take on chaos is "do as thou wilt, shall be the whole of the law."

                                    The problem is that when most people think of Chaos, they immediately bring up the image of acting out with reckless abandonment. No plan, no sophistication, just mindless indulgence. But that's not strictly the case.

                                    Acting chaotically is about putting your own personal desires above everything else. If you're following the law, or adhering to societal expectations, it's because you choose to do so, not because somebody told you it was the right thing to do. And if you suddenly decide that you don't want to follow those rules anymore, then as a chaotic character, you have the freedom to stop doing so.

                                    Hence, a Chaotic Good character might want people to treat each other like decent human beings, but when trouble breaks out, they'll do just about anything to make sure those responsible pay for their wrong doings. And if that means acting like the villain, then so be it. Why should the hero show the villain the mercy they never showed their victims?


                                    Lawful Good characters on the other hand are unwilling to go to such extremes, because in their mind, it would defeat the purpose. How can they be expected to uphold peace and justice if they behave as selfishly as the very criminals they fight against? Nobody respects a hypocrite, and in order for the government's laws to have any meaning, those in charge of the government must be held accountable to its own rules.



                                    Your idea of trying to change the rules that don't seem to function properly sounds like it would be in theme with Neutral Good. These kinds of characters include good natured souls who agree with the idea of treating people fairly, but don't necessarily feel compelled to operate within the strict borders of the law. They might not pick up a sword and rebel against the local monarch, but they would probably take in refugees who are fleeing the destruction of their homeland caused by a war their country is involved in. Their judgement is impartial and (usually) fair to both parties involved. And if they feel like the status quo needs to be updated, they're probably the ones to bring it up.

                                    "I see what you people were going for, and you had a good base to go on. But times are changing, and we need to think of a way to make this system work better."
