    Originally posted by Odd_Canuck View Post
    Ballsy yes. Making threats of illegal actions that trash the economy of the country are very much in that range.



    Well yes, that is fully understandable. Believing the promises of politicians and corporate representatives when they shake hands and say 'this will bring many jobs' or 'this is a really good thing' is never a good idea. They sell ideas that sound good, not ideas that employ people.

    America already had a lovely go-around with the protectionist trade practices of jacking up import taxes on products that compete with american goods and production... it results in all those other countries slapping import taxes on american goods, causing the economy to slow down and american companies being devalued. The reason the jobs that used to be heavy in the rustbelt and elsewhere went overseas wasn't a lack of taxes being slapped on stuff coming into the country, it's the very simple fact that with modern infrastructure, modern shipping, and modern organizational technologies it is considerably cheaper to employ people elsewhere in the world and ship the goods to the US and across the US than it is to make them there.

    China is the example of the reverse in action. Dropping trade barriers and taxes, suddenly an influx of outside goods, localization of the products, and they become a powerhouse selling to both the domestic and global market.

    Slapping taxes that the US has agreed internationally NOT to apply (under threat of large taxes being slapped on them) will not help. At best it would drive down demand for the products of that company as the prices get jacked up. Which just means they move their offices out of the non-profitable US and relocate elsewhere. Meanwhile the worst is that it establishes the US as a nation even less likely to honor the treaties it signs. Which means nobody trusts it. If you want to see a country collapse in a hurry, make it so the international banks don't want to business with that government because they're not considered as trustworthy anymore.
    Exactly this. There's this oversimplified conversation about how neoliberal Democrats are pushing globalization and populist Republicans are trying to stop it. That's not how it works. Globalization isn't an agenda. It's an inevitability spurred by dramatic technological shifts and the growth of smaller nations out of colonialism. Its not about pushing globalization or halting it. It's about recognizing it as an unstoppable shift in global policy and altering your policy to match that. Those jobs aren't just going to come back when there are other nations better equipped to fill them, and punishing businesses for taking advantages of these opportunities will just further cripple our economy. What we need is to adjust our economy to stay at the forefront of the tech revolution, to be the pioneer in new industries, but our woefully inadequate education system, stymied political structure, and parasitic corporatism makes us ill-equipped to train our people and promote those kinds of jobs. It's a complicated issue that neither party (and no one person, really) has the answers to. I can't blame people for being angry. I can understand why they latch on to a demagogue. But that doesn't make that attachment any less toxic for our country.

    You can recognize that someone is justified being angry and too uneducated to understand the implications of why they're angry, and you can still recognize how disastrous that ignorance is.

Without the Clintons being in the push to say that gays in the military should actually be allowed at all, we'd probably be far less along the path towards full LGBTQ civil rights.

                                                    And there's also the issue of turning such things into a Catch-22 for politicians. If "evolving" on an issue is a bad thing... what exactly is a politician supposed to do? Should Clinton revert to outdated and bigoted LGBTQ policies that she says she doesn't believe in anymore so she can avoid looking like she's changing for political expediency, or change her position according to whatever thoughts convinced her to change and look like she's been an opportunist?
