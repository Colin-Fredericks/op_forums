    Originally posted by Drake View Post
    Right, but my point is that those people wouldn't have been in the position of needing to define themselves, their identity, and the way they felt, if not for all the people insisting that a label was needed.
    As someone who has been in the position of needing to define myself, I can say from experience that you are wrong about this. It's not a matter of people insisting a label was needed for me - I was fortunate enough to grow up in extremely open and welcoming environments. The issue is feeling differently than your observations indicate is the norm, and wanting to understand why you feel that way, and that it is ok to do so.

    Originally posted by Drake View Post
    I wouldn't have to have wondered what to call my sexual preference if no one was throwing around labels that didn't fit it - it would just be my sexual preference. Much like how everyone has a favorite color, or a few favorites, but there aren't any labels assigned to anyone that likes a particular set of colors.
    This is a deeply flawed analogy because there isn't a stigma against people who don't prefer the most popular color, people aren't brought up with that color as an assumed default with other preferred colors being something everyone avoids talking about and says they'll explain when you're older, there aren't religions that teach people it's wrong to like different colors, with holy texts they point to to cite why those colors are sinful to enjoy, and people don't get beaten to death for liking the wrong color.

    It's all well and good to say "if nobody cared about these arbitrary categories, we wouldn't need labels for them", but that doesn't account for the reality that a lot of people do care about those categories, and will commit violence against people for being a part of those categories whether we make labels for them or not. It ignores the reality that society does not treat people of different categories equally, making them functionally not arbitrary. It doesn't account for the reality that we are social animals genetically programmed to form communities around common traits, and to have an averse reaction to not being included in communities, even if we don't share common traits with them. In a perfect world? Sure, we wouldn't need labels. I hate to break it to you, but we don't live in a perfect world.

                                                    Speaking of labels, this is a label related "Do you know what I hate?"

                                                    For a less loaded example of labeling, I am lactose intolerant. When someone wonders why I am not eating their delicious cheesecake* they have spent hours making, I have a good excuse. If a gaming group wants to order food, I suggest options beyond the usual pizza or bring my own.

                                                    Yet I have learned recently, in this era of food sensitivities, it's just better not to mention it. I have some friends with food sensitivities who go out of their way to make me food without dairy. Frankly, I find most dairy-free versions of food to be disappointing, so I either avoid the food all together or just eat small amounts of the dairy version. I know they are being thoughtful, but I rather them not go though all that effort just for me to disappoint them. My intolerance to Lactose is a very minor food sensitivity compared to Gluten intolerance or peanut allergies. I can hard cheese and most yogurts. I can eat things with cooked with milk and butter, as long as Paula Deen isn't cooking it.

                                                    *Have you ever tried to turn down homemade cheesecake? People act like you killed their puppy until you give them a "valid" reason.
