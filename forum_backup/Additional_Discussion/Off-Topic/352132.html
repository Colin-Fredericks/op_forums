    Can I rant for just a few to ten minutes?

    12-27-2014, 08:50 PM
    How much is too much direction from the ST? I try never to force my players into any sort of linear progression. I like to leave things open and generally, okay, always. Whenever they have an idea in their heads I let them go about it unless it's just a huge waste of time. And I mean an epic waste of time; and that's at the point where I'll flat out say that there's nothing on that lead.

    Tonight's game session was a huge flop, I'm on my second Demon Chronicle with players who have all been under me before (Minty Phrasing!). They wanted to travel to a city by boat (We're playing on a chain of islands). That was where we ended it last week, I assumed that's where we were going today and that's what I set up for the game. Along the way the players run into a boat that ran aground on the coast of the Island that the city they were heading to is located.
    The boat was one they dealt with the session before and one that intercepted an item one of the players was transporting. The board the boat, find everyone dead or near dead and explore the vessel. The item is gone. The boat they pulled up on get's stolen (it's a speed boat one person can easily man it), and they deduce that the based on the direction the person went he could make it back to the Island they just came from, based on the fuel left in the ship. So they fix the boat they're on and head back to where they came, the previous island.

    Here thing go to hell, I'm pretty good at coming with stuff on the spot. I'd be a horrible st if I couldn't. And I tried to adjust things so they got things done and it was worth their while. The whole time I tried to point them back to going about their original task. But they just weren't getting clued in, all of them were getting frustrated and by a few hours in one of my players actually threatened to punch me in the face because she was tired of the fact that nothing was getting accomplished.

    Sorry this is so long, and I'm not looking for who's right and who's wrong. But at what point do I have to just say "No you're doing it wrong?". Basically we ended the game two hours early because I just lost my interest in running it.

Or, deus ex machina (if done sparingly) can always be done.

                          Originally posted by miss_minty View Post
                          I may go with the dice roll. I was just thrown off since they veered off their original intent and they got upset when they didn't follow through with it.
                          There's a couple easy ways to handle this type of problem (addressing the situation as a whole): adjust certain aspects so that the PCs can feel that they accomplished something on the island they stopped off at, regardless of whether it actually was anything you had in mind or not. (The couple of hours wasted trying to "drop the hint" that they obviously weren't getting could be used to create a mini-episode where something else occurs; with just a little narrative tweaking, this spontaneous, made-up thing can thread to the main plot later on, giving the PCs a sense of both having "caught something" -- again, regardless of validity -- as well as making the whole storyline feel more organic.) The second way is to literally just tell the players "Guys -- the story is elsewhere -- this was just an unexpected pit stop. You can explore the island/whatever, if you want, but I'll toss you a freebie and tell you all you're gonna find is Gilligan and a bunch of cocoa nuts...". Which of those is the better option completely depends on the type of group you're running for and what the salient mood is.

                          Don't be afraid to go meta-narrative every once in a while. It really does help to scoot things along when reality bogs down, even if only used sparingly.
