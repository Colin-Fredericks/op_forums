                                            So I happen to be fond of the food truck scene. Dallas is slowly adopting the format; there's a park about a 10-12 minute walk from my current workplace that pulls up about a half-dozen trucks every lunch break, and a couple days a week the square right across the rail line from here gets two-three.

                                            So, backstory: there's this foodstuff called arancini di riso. It's a sort of meat-stuffed rice ball, breaded up and fried. Given the Texan tendency to fry anything and everything we can (I'm looking at you, fried butter), you'd imagine this would be more common. But sadly, this is not to be: the local Korean barbecue place has something kind of similar. The state fair itself had fried jambalaya.

                                            And there's a food truck that serves them, but since nobody knows what they are they're listed as "Fried Risotto Balls." But they don't have a route that comes near where I live, so it's usually a bit of luck of the draw--or knowing that they're going to be over at the food truck yard on a given day, but that's a bit of a drive--to get them.

                                            Or, most commonly by far, I make up a batch at home.

                                            There's a point to all this, and I haven't gotten to it yet.

                                            There's a thai food truck that's going to be at the nearby square. I haven't had them yet, but they have something called "firecracker balls." After discussing it with the guy running the truck, he agreed absolutely that it was a sort of thai fusion arancine, but again, nobody understood that and the name didn't have a place on the menu.

                                            So this afternoon that's on my lunch menu. Report to follow.

                                                    So!

                                                    According to an Italian woman I knew back in college, you have Neapolitan arancini and Sicilian arancini. I generally favor--and make--Sicilian, while per her definition Neapolitan tend not to have stuffing, just being spare rice or risotto rolled up and fried.

                                                    (I just remembered that Kroger occasionally carries a four cheese arancini in this style in their freezer section, but I've found it underwhelming).

                                                    This latter style seems to be closer to what I was served.

                                                    Don't get me wrong, it was still good! It was clearly a snack, though, and not a meal: the two rice balls were maybe a bit larger than chicken eggs (albeit spherical), with a spicy mayo topping like I've seen on some sushi and a carrot-and-something-I-think-might-have-been-daikon along the side. It was panko-breaded, with a delicious crunch to the outside, and a spicy red-curry heat to the jasmine rice itself that was just propped up against the upper levels of acceptable spice and stayed there. It didn't overpower the hints of basil or lemongrass mixed into the rice, and while I'm not a fan of carrots in general, I found the slaw provided a nice acidic contrast to the fireballs (ignore what I said above--they're fireballs, not firecracker balls) and a little extra texture.

                                                    Not the best I've had, but I'd certainly have it again.

                                                    But like I said, we get two or three food trucks. While I was promised a shaved ice truck (we have a few down here that are pretty damn nice), none showed itself. But there was one styling itself "The Great Australian Meat Pie Company" and that certainly sounded promising.

                                                    I ended up getting a shepherd's pie that pretty much stole the show, with rough-cut mushrooms and savory, tender beef in a rich gravy, sprinkled with melting shredded cheese. On top of the whole thing was a towering, airy crust of puff pastry--it definitely looked a little weird as it sat there--it's a sharp visual contrast to the rich brown gravy and its contents, and was probably put on top moments before serving--but the textural juxtaposition was marvelous. I really wish I could go into as much depth on this as I did on the fireballs, but this was ultimately a bit of a simpler dish. Could have used a bit less salt, but that's about it.

                                                    My lips were still tingling from the fireballs after I had the pie, but I think the latter was the serendipitous find of the day.
