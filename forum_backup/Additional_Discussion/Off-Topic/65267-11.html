                                    The "grown up and mature" thing I believe was a comment about how Marvel tended to take a less kiddie view in comic books and grounded things in more emotional adult themes. When FF first came out the Thing had a huge chip on his shoulder and spent years dealing with his appearance, and the stories were never quite wrapped up so neatly at the end of every issue. And then there was the infighting between him and the Human Torch showing that the "family" was a bit dysfunctional at times. Such things didn't exist in DC in the 60s where most issues (I believe) where most issues ended in friendly laughter or a wink to the reader.
                                    And then there was the "power" problem. Marvel gave limitations as were seen in the strength category, which would later be referred to in the 80s as "Class 100" for those that can routinely bench press over 100 tons. (Marvel would undo that later it seems) But what were the limits in DC back then? The readership that DC aimed for were pretty much children, where as Marvel was shooting for an older demographic, one that can relate to heroes with personal problems, self-doubt and had their own...all too human failings.


                                    Mankind was once an endangered species. It will likely be so again. And mankind will only have itself to blame.
