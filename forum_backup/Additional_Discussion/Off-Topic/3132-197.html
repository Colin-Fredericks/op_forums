                                                    Here's two important things:

                                                    1) Nobody is born a good ally. Being an ally is something you have to learn to do. Like anything you learn to do, you're probably going to fuck it up. College students are learning a lot of things at once, and that means they also tend to not be the best at anything of them. But that's the whole point! School is a safe place to mess up, learn, and be better when you get out into the rest of the world. It's better to have them be a bit overzealous in their youth while they have time to experiment and learn, then to not have them later. Bad allies aren't ones that are imperfect. Bad allies are the ones that don't want to learn how to be better ones, and then take those lessons to heart. The vast majority of people you're talking about will be good allies, because they're allies-in-training right now, and they need to learn, not be attacked for trying.

                                                    2) You don't know who is and isn't a minority. Yes, some minorities are easy to see, because people get discriminated against in obvious visible ways. A lot of minorities also have various levels of passing. Oh, and being a minority is it's own learning process. If you think being white and middle-class in American means you'll never know oppression or systemic discrimination... you really don't have a leg to stand on. You display no empathy or understanding of how the fear of losing your passing privilege, while wanting to use your status to help others that don't have it can lead to people picking "safer" (aka First World) problems to advocate for over ones that put their own feeling of safety at risk.
