                              I don't have a dog in the whole GamerGate controversy - I don't really play video games much these days, but I find it fascinating.

                              It's a wonderful example of how a small minority, thanks to the power and anonymity of the internet, can radically reshape and rebrand a larger message. In all likelihood, the number of "assholes" (the people sending death threats, doxxing, etc) is comparatively small - a few thousand trolls who get off on seeing themselves, or at least their actions, get discussed in online forums, or in news columns. Yet, despite the small number, they've completely taken control of the GamerGate movement, and indeed, in many respects are becoming the face of "male video game players."

                              I think GamerGate started off with, at least in theory, some points that merited discussion involving the integrity if video game journalism(it's no secret that the video game companies that spend the most on advertising in magazines tend to receive best scores for their games, for example), but certainly by now any earlier merits to the movement have been totally eclipsed by the assholes whose actions have painted male gamers in such a negative light.

                                          Having looked into it a little bit more (and thanks for the link, Papa Bear), I'm starting to get a feeling that I might have attributed the discussion too much credit. From where I currently stand, it really looks a lot more like the usual kind of sexist bullshit than I had at first assumed.

                                          I do think there's something to be said for having a discussion about video game journalism transparency and interindustrial relations, that can't be a bad thing. But that discussion can't be had while associated with GamerGate, because there's so much wrong going on under that label that it would overshadow everything.

                                          First of all, of course, the harassment. I mean, you don't have to be any kind of social justice warrior to realize that's not a valid mode of discussion, not if you were really looking to address any issues. This is rule by fear. I saw someone call it terrorism, and I would agree.

                                          Secondly, the misogyny. Even from the very start, there's an androcentric perspective being produced. The as far as I gather disproven accusation against Quinn is that she slept with Grayson in exchange for a good review (the Wikipedia page says Grayson never reviewed her game). The accusation could have equally easily been that Grayson agreed to trade a good review for a lay. The rhetoric here is clear; despite the implicitly reciprocal nature of both a sexual act and a trade of services, the woman is foregrounded as the one responsible, while the man is never questioned. The same pattern can be seen in, for example, victim blaming in rape situations. The whole premise of the accusation is misogynist from the start, and naturally, the proceeded harassment of multiple female personalities only makes this more obvious. I couldn't help but smile at the tragedy of it all when Sarkeesian got pulled into it again, kind of just because.

                                          Thirdly, the reactionary stance taken to video games as an art form. The GamerGate supporters seem to represent the opinion that video games should not be examined in a social context, but only in terms of their form (mechanics, controls, graphics and so on) and compared against a certain number of given "golden ideals", essentially like it is today. I believe that video games is an art form, and if so, to gain credibility they must be examined in the same way we examine our other art: socially, absolutely, but also formally (in that case, not compared to a set of "golden ideals") and in relation to the individual player. A new academic field? In time, I hope so. Not so the GamerGate advocates, it would seem.

                                          In that light, I have a feeling I might agree with the "death of the gamer" articles I've read about. I'll have to read them in their entirety myself, but there is something to be said for a term starting to lose its meaning once it gets applied to too many obviously non-homogenous elements.

                                          I gather that the general opinion all lean the same way here, but does anyone know of any side which may produce a counterargument? Any link to their main hunting grounds, so I can see it firsthand?

                                          Oh, and in the same vein, on a sidenote: the term "social justice warrior" is a non-defined term, as I've ranted about earlier on these boards. It gets applied so broadly that it really carries no meaning beyond its negative charge. The views of people who get called it, I've come to experience, are often very moderate in their opinions and they are often more about common sense than anything more drastic. Beyond that, social justice seems like a great idea; why would we not want that?
