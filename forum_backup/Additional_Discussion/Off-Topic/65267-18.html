                                                Now, for something else: For anyone who enjoyed the Incredible Hercules book (that one with Amadeus Cho as Herc's sidekick), I highly recommend the current (written by the same guy, Fred Van Lente) Valiant title Archer & Armstrong. Just don't try to take it seriously as some sort of political statement, because it has over-the-top ridiculousness like

                                                "The One Percent" as an actual cult of greed-demon Mammon, complete with cultist masks, conspiracies, capitalism/business-based jargon and so on. A secret society within the Catholic Church with ninja nuns descended (or at least taking inspiration) from Lilith instead of Eve and other stuff like that. I guess some stuff can feels offensive depending on one's background (for instance, Archer comes from a fundamentalist christian community and his upbringing is not portrayed is generally portrayed in a negative light, though other, more open-minded religious characters are there to provide contrast)


                                                but as I said everything is so over the top...I didn't get anything but comedy and wacky comicbook hijinks out of it. The pseudo political-statement sort of plot feels just like the flavor of the story to me. Satire at most.

                                                Anyway the book is fun, funny and takes things more from history, as opposed to mythology in Herc's book (except for issue zero, which is an origin story for Armstrong that came out around the time of regular issue 10, in which it is revealed what Armstrong and his brothers have to do with the Epic of Gilgamesh), mostly in the fact that Armstrong is a really old immortal poet, drunkard and party-animal and has personally met a lot of famous people throughout history.

                                                And in case anyone is wondering, yes, the book does present credible challenges to the heroes, despite one of the titular characters apparently being immortal immortal, not just ageless.

                                                I've read the first arc with the religious/political stuff...and it seems the next arc involves invading Area 51 and getting mixed in alien/military-based shenanigans while trying to find-out the origins of Archer's powers. So, you see what I say about it being mostly wacky ^_^

                                                I'll finish by saying that the book is fun enough that I'll probably give a chance to the other titles of this Valiant revival, and that includes the uber-90s Bloodshot, despite me having grown to hate the 90s style of super-hero comics.

                                                    Death of Wolverine

                                                    Wolverine was immortal, or close to it thanks to his regeneration and slow aging. He has escaped certain death countless times, and not even writer Mark Millar and artist Steve McNiven’s disturbing, future-set Old Man Logan could finish him off. Today, however, Marvel has announced that the Ol’ Canuknuckle Head’s final card will be punched this September.

                                                    EW reports that Wolverine will be killed off in an upcoming 4-part Marvel limited series, appropriately title Death of Wolverine. Issue #1 will premiere September 3rd, 2014. The series will be written by Charles Soule and drawn by Steve McNiven. The “3 Months to Live” arc of this June’s Wolverine solo series will culminate in the character’s demise on September 24th. According to Marvel executive editor Michael Marts:
                                                    “For a long time, no matter who Wolverine was battling, he’s been the eternal victor. He almost always comes out on top. Now he finally comes up against an adversary that he cannot win against, he cannot fight. What does that mean for this character who’s been around for hundred years?”
                                                    How permanent will the Death of Wolverine prove to be? Could this be a Death of Superman-esque attempt to not only revamp the character but make him relevant to the comic book audience in a new way? As impermanent as comic book deaths has proven to be in the past, Marts asserts that Marvel’s creative team has made this decision “from a standpoint of finality, of closure.” He elaborated about the ripple effect this will have, saying: “You’re gonna be entering into a world without Wolverine. That affects not only his teammates, but also the Marvel Universe at large. We’ve got a lot of things in the works already.”
                                                    ---------------------------------

                                                    In other words, he won't be dead for long...

                                                    Mankind was once an endangered species. It will likely be so again. And mankind will only have itself to blame.
