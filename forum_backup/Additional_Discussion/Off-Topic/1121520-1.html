                Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

                I've met a fuckton more of any social group I can think to name online then I ever would've physically.
                That's a part of why I said the internet did wonderful things, but took away a lot too. It's a boon and a curse, in my eyes. Yes it makes finding groups (rpg, or any kind of social ones, or sport-related, or literally anything) a lot easier, even from the other side of the world. In the meantime, I don't think living the majority of one's social life on it is a positive thing. I'm saying that as someone, who did exactly that, in the past several years, due to a panic attacks. You could have people to talk to, even to play rpgs with on the internet, but, depending on where they're living, you can't take a walk with many of them, can't gather for a physical session, drink a beer, go out to buy some clothes together, going to a concert, etc.

                I might add, I like online gaming (I'm even a big supporter of it, trying to convince our group constantly to try it, because we could have more frequent sessions and try a lot more things, but they're resisting staunchly and I get it, gathering for a gaming night is just a special thing), because it gives a lot of options, however, I also think physicality is important, whether it's meeting with people personally, or a special space like-minded people would naturally gather (like a shop, club or bar, or anything else), or the simple joy of browsing physical books on the self. I remember the first time when I got into an rpg shop. It was (or is, as far as I know, it still stands) a very small, one-room bookshop, with rulebooks and novels on every shelf, up to the ceiling and even on the floor, miniatures, dices, cards, some board games. It was truly a magical place and it was also like home. From that on, and even today, I just like going into a shop and spend some time around. An online shop just doesn't offer that experience. Also, no, even the most smartly layouted pdf doesn't offer the same wow effect of a physical book.

                Also, one problem I have, with the hobby going online is that it puts even more distance between existing fans and potential ones. You could wander into a hobby store, see a sign about the weekly organized play on the front and try it on a whim. You could, back then pick a Vampire book from the shelf of a totally average bookstore, because the green marble and rose looked cool and you wondered what could be this vampire-thing? With pdfs and online shopping you have to know what you're seeking, to find it and it's a model which is catering a lot more to existing fans, which isn't the best thing long-term. I think that's why WotC wants to keep books in shops and support game stores and I think they're somewhat right.

                If nothing worked, then let's think!

                                                            The price was always a factor for me. Back when I was a freshmen in college, my friends and I got a used 3-in-1 octagonal traditional gaming table (so flat, poker, bumper pool) for around $200. Sure, brand new they're closer to $1K these days, but that's what... half of a what Geek Chic was looking for at their low end?

                                                            The octagonal table was more than big enough for having 6 people around. We figured out how to take the bumpers off,so we had a big felt lined empty surface for wargames (which we could cover and put games on hold). The poker side was our default RPG side, because each player had a well for dice or play aids (and a drink holder) leaving the middle with plenty of space for maps, minis, books to get stacked for reference, a music player for our GMs that liked to use that, and so on. Then we still had the flat side for meals, and casual/party games.

                                                            It was bulky, sure, but I don't think you could have convinced us to pay more for something "dedicated" to RPGs when it meant paying way more for a slightly increase in features.
