                                    In the wake of the most recent mass shooting: I hate how I see people react to this stuff.

                                    There isn't a mass-murderer lurking around every corner or anything, but it is a problem which needs to be solved. And for that to happen, you need to understand the problem. You need to get into these peoples' heads and grasp what it is that leads them to pick up a gun and start shooting people. You need to suspend your anger and extend empathy toward the killer in question, even as you condemn their actions.

                                    I feel like people don't do that - or perhaps can't do that. They regard any attempt at understanding as a betrayal, as tantamount to condoning the murders. As far as they're concerned, the killer was just crazy, a monster, something Not Like Us. (As though "crazy" is something obvious which sets people apart - nobody you know and like could harbor those kinds of thoughts, right?) And instead of making a good-faith attempt to understand the problem - or preferably, asking professionals who have done so - they just blame whatever convenient villain they hated anyway.

                                    It just feels to me like a lot of moral pageantry.

                                                In the wake of the most recent mass shooting: I hate how I see people react to this stuff.

                                                There isn't a mass-murderer lurking around every corner or anything, but it is a problem which needs to be solved. And for that to happen, you need to understand the problem. You need to get into these peoples' heads and grasp what it is that leads them to pick up a gun and start shooting people. You need to suspend your anger and extend empathy toward the killer in question, even as you condemn their actions.

                                                I feel like people don't do that - or perhaps can't do that. They regard any attempt at understanding as a betrayal, as tantamount to condoning the murders. As far as they're concerned, the killer was just crazy, a monster, something Not Like Us. (As though "crazy" is something obvious which sets people apart - nobody you know and like could harbor those kinds of thoughts, right?) And instead of making a good-faith attempt to understand the problem - or preferably, asking professionals who have done so - they just blame whatever convenient villain they hated anyway.

                                                It just feels to me like a lot of moral pageantry.
                                                I started on a reply to this a couple days ago... then reload nuked it.

                                                There's a few angles here. We always like to separate people into Us (right) and them (bad/wrong/evil). This is what we as humans are geared to do. It allows us to establish mental templates and apply them and thus save ourselves effort (and risk) in the future. Us are the good guys that never do anything too wrong, and They are evil/bad/wrong. Because we have some significant in-built empathy towards others of the same species (and indeed of other species as well), we distance ourselves by making them (in our minds) not like us. Not really a human/good person. They're a thing.

                                                And that's why "they"'re treated almost as a disease rather than people, because as you pointed out to treat them as people and to attempt to understand them reveals that we're very similar. We're all very close to being monsters. We fear.

                                                But because of that, people are freely able to apply whatever flavour of "monster" template they want on someone. This is especially true when the subject is dead. So they become the all-monster.


                                                Now the pageantry... why would anyone want to stop it? Monsters make news, news sells airtime and makes money. Monsters distract people with a big obvious target to hate. Monsters with the right template thrown over them can be used for almost any political drive. Want to justify cutting public health care? This guy was getting mental health care and clearly it failed to prevent anything. Waste of time and money, budget cut! All in how you spin it.

                                                And it's all very very human.
