            First off, I'm very, very, very aware that next to some, or even most, people on this thread, my "problems" are so tiny as to not really warrant the term. But I want to vent, and this seems a semi-appropriate venue. Also, this is more-or-less stream-of-consciousness, so coherency likely won't be high. I may go back and edit, see how I feel at the end.

            You know those situations you sometimes find yourself in where someone has clearly made a colossal mistake/is a giant idiot, but you honestly don't know who? That's increasingly becoming my default state. I've never been great with people at the best of times; between the increasingly-strong stress attacks I have when in a crowd, to having serious difficulty interacting with people due to knowing appropriate responses, and apparently my bizarre beliefs (I'm measuring this against the people I generally interact with. Imperfect metric, I know: they seem reasonable enough to me), it's just easier to stay at home and read or watch movies. I'm solitary by nature anyway, so that works out. But I think it's getting worse. I'm a research student in psychology, working on what will be my PhD project. However, I really have very little idea what I'm doing. I get the concepts I'm working with well enough, but I'm constantly feeling that it's a surface understanding, that I should have a better grasp of how they relate, the dynamics, and suchforth. Every other student I talk to at my level seems to have a pretty solid grasp of their concepts, whereas I feel like I'm mostly parroting and drawing tenuous links based on nothing. My supervisor seems OK with my progress, but it wouldn't be the first time I've catastrophically misread someone over an extended period of time. That, together with the fact that my last supervisor (different project) wanted as little to do with me as humanly possible, while apparently being great for all his other students, doesn't make me feel overly secure. Plus, I'm into the second year of a program that they're apparently enforcing a three-year limit on, and while I was on track in terms of time, I've hit a giant wall that I have no idea how to get around without a total redesign, which could easily take six months in itself (oh, by the way, I have like two weeks to do this, or I'll have to wait a year since my project is tied to the school year). I would ask my supervisor, but he's taken sick leave for at least a month. In combination, he was the convenor for the course I'm tutoring, and I keep getting this idea that the idea of having to see me more than once a month literally drove him away. I have no evidence for this, but it's in my brain and damn it I can't move it.

            At the same time I'm conducting interviews as part of a pilot study using the undergrad student pool, which is just reinforcing just how little I 'get' people. I mean, I know, thing about learning is you know how little you know, but most people seem to have this semi-intuitive grasp of how other people work, whereas I'm having to run entirely off half-baked academic theories and behavioural models. It's like how I play Go: I don't study or have much of a sense for the game, I've learned what little I have through trial-and-error. But in Go, I mess up, I lose a game and feel stupid. With people, I mess up, I screw up my one shot at my desired career and potentially worse. Plus, if I don't manage to pull out something meaningful from the data, then I will have that much more of a difficult time, administratively speaking, and so far I've got diddly. And not even useful diddly.

            So, I hear you say, why not talk to your friends? Well, I don't really have anyone I would call a friend. Oh, I have people I'm friendly with, a coffee every now and then, listen to them bitch about their boyfriend, but for me the title of friend implies a degree of reliability and reciprocity. I'm not expecting them to bend over backwards, or keep exact score of who owes who what, but literally every time I ask them to do something, from find something out for me to read a draft article I'm trying to publish to make sure it makes even a little bit of sense, inevitably they don't. Oh, they say they will, but nope, never follow through. Ever. I sometimes get a result if I nag, but I hate to nag, and even then it's not a great percentage. Plus, I am generally of the view that they have their own problems, listening to me whine about issues won't make my life any easier as it doesn't solve the problem, and may well make them more miserable. So, what's the point? Also, I'm kind of private. Not super-secretive, but I don't raise things about myself unless asked; just don't see it as any of their business (no, they don't ask). And yes, I see the contradiction: I like to keep things to myself, and I'm venting on a semi-public forum. Was it not clear this was a rant long on emotion and short on logic?

            So, overall, I'm pretty stressed. And when I get stressed, I get angry. Which normally isn't a problem, I'm used to having to control my temper, since it seems to be a bit more hair-trigger in me than in most. But the fact is that my willpower is not unlimited, and I'm pretty sure the anger is getting worse, and I can just see myself exploding all over something that is entirely stupid and a normal person would just laugh off or ignore. So, being the semi-intelligent person I like to think I am on my better days, I drew on my existing knowledge base and thought "you know what? Buddhism has some pretty on-topic things to say about anger, maybe they can teach me something." So I go along to the local Buddhist temple. (This next part is going to sound really racist/xenophobic/generally nasty. Not intended, or at least not as nasty as it sounds.) Apparently, their grasp of the English language is, shall we say, limited? So, that wasn't any use, and my understanding of their perspective is purely academic, so limited ability to apply it in a useful fashion. And while I completely agree with the value of learning languages, I refuse to learn at least two languages to fluency to get feedback on a perspective which may or may not help, from people who live in a country where my first language is the dominant one. So, that didn't work out, and the fact that I live in a heavily Asian area (which is fine, lots of good Thai food) where only like 30% of the people speak English to any appreciable level (which is less fine, but understandable: my other languages aren't fantastic either), just makes it that much easier to form these associations between certain language/ethnic groups and irritation, which is really not a fine thing to do (have you ever tried to not form an association between paired stimuli? It's really hard).

            So, I'm lost, I'm stressed, I'm apparently alone and not in the way I like, and I'm constantly being shown that I'm even more stupid than I thought, or at least totally unable to apply my knowledge, which is basically the same thing in a practical sense. And I have no idea what I'm supposed to do, but I do know that if I don't do something then something really bad will happen.

            So, yeah. End rant. Like I said, I know full well that this doesn't even count as a problem, but it's irritating me, and hopefully venting will help. I very much doubt it, but it's not like I'm overly burdened with options here.

                                            Dude, I whine about my problems all the time on here and I'm WAY not worse (apparently) that a lot of other folks.

                                            We're sharing, not balancing scales. No one's story has more weight than someone else's.

                                            Everyone's story is worth it's weight in gold to someone else.

                                            EDIT: I give example.

                                            I'm sitting here working in behavioral health scheduling. I'm getting calls ranging from parents trying to make little Johnny (who's 17) quit smoking pot (which is legal here in CO if not for their age, but no worse than the booze the parents were having when they were kids) up to the lady to called to cancel the appointment her husband had the next day because he committed suicide the previous day.

                                            The other day, I take this one call. This lady is going on about how she might be bi-polar and how she used to have it so together and then a bunch of stuff happened in her life and made her lose it. How she used to be so brave and now she was almost having an anxiety attack just trying to make the phone call to me. And how soothing and calm I sound and how much that helps and how I must have to be so together and right in the head to hear all these people's problems all day long. Meanwhile, I'm about to cry because she's telling MY story about how she feels right now and I had also just been diagnosed bi-polar and that my manic phases are RAGE-fuled and how I can fly off the handle over the stupidest shit.

                                            And that call helped us BOTH get a little bit better.


                                            So, never hide it. Tell your story, even if it bites you in the ass later (which mine has a lot lately). The more people QUIT hiding their mental problems like they're a wanted criminal, the more people get to NOT treat it like a horrible thing to be turned against you.

                                            K, done preaching, go about your business.
