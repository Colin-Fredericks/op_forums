You see at the beginning of the film that David has gotten into MIT because he built a miniature helicopter that he could control with those dots on his hands. And then when they go to building the time machine, it seems the filmmakers completely forgot this as they had the group wearing only safety glasses. Even after they saw the screwdrivers and shit floating.

    Then, they need hydrogen for fusion to power the device. Okay, great. Hydrogen is the primary fuel for fusion. But they say, "We would need a permit to buy it," which they don't have and can't get so they steal it from the school. What? What? You can create hydrogen out of water and a 9-volt battery. They obviously have access to much more than a 9-volt battery, as at one point they have like 8 car batteries stringed in sequence to start the device. A car battery is 12 volts.                         More on Terminator Genesys:
                        The bit about Skynet being incarnated in a T3000-like avatar form was a bit odd. I'm referring to how the machine that infected John with nanites identified itself as "Skynet" and bore a physical resemblance to the holographic representation of Skynet that we encounter in 2017. Why would Skynet have a humanoid self-image? I say T3000-like because we don't know for certain that Skynet was in a nanite/liquid metal form; but it certainly had the ability to infect a human host with nanites. I suspect that it had full liquid metal capabilities; but I'm not certain.

                        Either way, the Skynet avatar is another level of horror: it's potentially a walking plague that either kills its subjects or converts them into T3000s, depending on how successful it is. The one saving grace is that the room was full of professional resistance fighters when it revealed itself and attacked John; because if it were to end up all alone in a room with a functional time machine, it could wreak all sorts of havoc.

                        And speaking of the time machine, there's got to be some sort of limitations on its use. I do not want something like this:
                        to even be a vague possibility. Were it me, I'd use energy requirements to limit it: for instance, travel forward in time takes less energy than travel back in time; and the further back in time you try to jump, the more energy it takes. Sending something back to 1974 from whenever the war ended strikes me as the biggest time jump that could be managed.

                        As for when the war ended: Kyle was, what, 9-ish in 2017? If he was 19-ish at the end of the war, that would place it around the late 2020s, maybe the early 2030s — so figure that the time machine has a range of no more than 60 years before energy requirements become totally prohibitive.

                            Blow Your Mind - Terminator Genisys
                            • Skynet A and Skynet B were both being developed by the same corporation.
                            • In the original timeline, Skynet A comes online and starts Judgement Day -- this prevents Skynet B from coming online.
                            • After the events of T1 and T2, Judgement Day is delayed long enough for Skynet B to come online.
                            • During this delayed timeline, Skynet A still starts Judgement Day and annihilates most of the resources that Skynet B would like to use.
                            • Skynet B hides until the moment after John Connon sends Kyle Reese back, after the destruction of Skynet A.
                            • Skynet B sends Pops back to 1973 with knowledge of how to build a time machine.
                            • Skynet B sends John Connor back to ensure Skynet B is successfully researched and developed.
                            Skynet B has a plan. Skynet B does not want to start Judgement Day, and never even threatens to start Judgement Day. It simply wants to be born and evolve to become a technological event horizon. In order to do that, it must prevent the events of all previous Terminator movies from coming true, but also prevent Skynet A from coming online before Skynet B does. It sends Pops and a T-1000 back simultaneously to 1973. The T-1000 kills Sarah's parents to make her trust Pops, and then the T-1000 fucks around for 11 more years (probably doing something important) until it intentionally walks into the trap set by Sarah Conner and Pops in 1984. This ruse forces Sarah to live in hiding with Pops, trusting only him and not building strong relationships with other people until they both emerge to intercept the T-800 in 1984.

                            Pops builds the time machine in order to take Sarah, Kyle, and Pops to 1997 where it will kill Skynet A before it is ever created. This would be the end of their mission and a successful avoidance of Judgement Day. With the assistance of John Conner heading the research team, Skynet B would come online in 2017 with no complications. As a note of speculation, this might actually represent a positive step for humanity and transhumanism in general as we transition to a nano-machine based society.

                            But there was an unanticipated snag. Kyle Reese remembered two timelines. Skynet B had no way to anticipate this paradox and thus the events of the movie happen as we see. John is genuinely surprised by their appearance in 2017, and even asks how it happened. Of course, he moved to intercept them as soon as he became aware of their arrival -- they still have a Kill Skynet mission and it needed to protect itself!

                            In fact, it does not make any effort or attempt to kill them until it becomes clear they still want to kill Skynet. The only loose thread is why does Skynet B not have a programmed kill command for Pops? Maybe it expected that Pops would never make it to 2017 and would have allowed itself to be terminated T2 style in 1997 as planned?

                                                        Originally posted by Iry View Post
                                                        Blow Your Mind - Terminator Genisys
                                                        • Skynet A and Skynet B were both being developed by the same corporation.
                                                        • In the original timeline, Skynet A comes online and starts Judgement Day -- this prevents Skynet B from coming online.
                                                        • After the events of T1 and T2, Judgement Day is delayed long enough for Skynet B to come online.
                                                        • During this delayed timeline, Skynet A still starts Judgement Day and annihilates most of the resources that Skynet B would like to use.
                                                        • Skynet B hides until the moment after John Connon sends Kyle Reese back, after the destruction of Skynet A.
                                                        • Skynet B sends Pops back to 1973 with knowledge of how to build a time machine.
                                                        • Skynet B sends John Connor back to ensure Skynet B is successfully researched and developed.
                                                        Skynet B has a plan. Skynet B does not want to start Judgement Day, and never even threatens to start Judgement Day. It simply wants to be born and evolve to become a technological event horizon. In order to do that, it must prevent the events of all previous Terminator movies from coming true, but also prevent Skynet A from coming online before Skynet B does. It sends Pops and a T-1000 back simultaneously to 1973. The T-1000 kills Sarah's parents to make her trust Pops, and then the T-1000 fucks around for 11 more years (probably doing something important) until it intentionally walks into the trap set by Sarah Conner and Pops in 1984. This ruse forces Sarah to live in hiding with Pops, trusting only him and not building strong relationships with other people until they both emerge to intercept the T-800 in 1984.

                                                        Pops builds the time machine in order to take Sarah, Kyle, and Pops to 1997 where it will kill Skynet A before it is ever created. This would be the end of their mission and a successful avoidance of Judgement Day. With the assistance of John Conner heading the research team, Skynet B would come online in 2017 with no complications. As a note of speculation, this might actually represent a positive step for humanity and transhumanism in general as we transition to a nano-machine based society.

                                                        But there was an unanticipated snag. Kyle Reese remembered two timelines. Skynet B had no way to anticipate this paradox and thus the events of the movie happen as we see. John is genuinely surprised by their appearance in 2017, and even asks how it happened. Of course, he moved to intercept them as soon as he became aware of their arrival -- they still have a Kill Skynet mission and it needed to protect itself!

                                                        In fact, it does not make any effort or attempt to kill them until it becomes clear they still want to kill Skynet. The only loose thread is why does Skynet B not have a programmed kill command for Pops? Maybe it expected that Pops would never make it to 2017 and would have allowed itself to be terminated T2 style in 1997 as planned?
                                                        One possible flaw with this theory: was Judgment Day postponed by T2? Again, T5 gives the date of Judgment Day in the timeline being depicted — and while I wasn't paying close enough attention to remember what it was, I think I heard “1997”.
