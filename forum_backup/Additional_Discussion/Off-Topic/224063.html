      The city where I am currently living is Rexburg, Idaho. And overall, my answer to "do you like your city?" must be "no".

      Don't get me wrong, there are things that I like. It's a very safe community. Violent crime here is essentially non-existent. You can walk around town at any time of day, no matter who you are, and have no fear of anything bad happening to you. People are friendly and helpful. I do not have a functioning car, and twice I have been walking home with hands full of groceries and gotten rides from a stranger. Several other times I have turned down rides.

      There are some things that are a mixed bag. Everything is within walking distance. The farthest you would ever want to go normally is only a half hour walk away. (There are reasons to drive out of town, but for day to day living, you can walk around easily.) This is a downside (for me) because it really emphasizes the small-town feel.

      Downsides: the biggest one is that there are too many damn Mormons! Now, I am a Mormon, so that may sound a little funny, but seriously. Rexburg is a college town, built up around a Mormon school and the population of the city is something like 98.7% Mormon. (This is partially because the students outnumber the locals here on at least a 1.5:1 basis.) What this means is that the cultural Mormon stuff, and particularly the unique BYUI things, are sometimes overwhelmingly shoved down your throat. The first year I lived up here there was an article in the local newspaper. In this article, they quoted a police officer who was saying, "High school students shouldn't drink alcohol because it's against the BYUI honor code." That is so asinine that I can't even fathom it. He didn't say, "Because underage possession of alcohol is illegal," which is the area that an officer of the law should be concerned about, but because it was against the BYUI honor code... which shouldn't matter to him at all.

      Furthermore, as most of you probably know, Mormons tend to be very conservative. During the last presidential election, the newspaper published the number of registered voters in Madison county (the county in which Rexburg resides). There were something like 8,000 registered Republicans and 127 registered Democrats. The person receiving the county wasn't even a contest. Statistically, Madison county has been the "reddest" county in the nation for some amount of time I can't remember.

      And another thing on the political climate here: the local paper published an article maybe 6 months ago. It was on the front page and it was a profile piece on a young gay couple who were engaged to get married. I read the piece. (In fact, you can read the piece. It's right here.) It's a pretty standard profile piece as far as I can tell. I cannot adequately describe the homophobic outrage that resulted from this article. There were many, many people who said that, because of this one article, they were going to cancel their subscription to the paper. The editor was fired. There was a slew of letters that were subsequently published by the paper saying this was an offensive piece and people couldn't believe that it had been published. It was ridiculous.

      Also, when you put a group of people who have many similarities in one place, they tend to become xenophobic. This is certainly true of Rexburg. If you don't fit into the cookie cutter Mormon mold, you are judged. (One point that kind of fits here that doesn't really fit anywhere else, and is an annoyance is that the local theater doesn't show any R-rated movies.) Each week, on Tuesdays, the school holds a devotional. It's broadcast on the radio and on the BYU-TV channel. There are many people (whom I have spoken to; this is not hearsay) who think that if you are a student and you don't go to devotional, you are somehow less righteous than you could be. If you don't dress a certain way, or style your hair a certain way, or if you grow a beard, then you will receive sideways glances and people will usher their children past you faster than they might another.

      And finally, another thing that drives me nuts is that the down rolls up its carpets around 10pm. Walmart is open late: it doesn't close until 11. There are two places in town that are open 24 hours: Maverick (a gas station) and McDonalds. (Each of them actually has two locations, so maybe there are 4 total places that are open 24 hours.) I'm not used to that. I come from suburbia where our grocery stores and Walmart and convenience stores are routinely open 24 hours.

      There are many more things I could say, but that will suffice for now. I imagine Arc might add some things here, and I don't want to steal his thunder. :-P

              Okay, so I should say a few good things.

              1)It does look quite nice in the summer.
              2)THat entire walking thing is nice, even when it does remind you how much of a rat in a cage you are.

              Okay, that's enough of that.

              I suppose I need to start off with the fact that I'm inherently biased towards places like Rexburg, in a lot of ways. Even when I lived in the rural parts of El Paso that were about as filled as Rexburg was, the fact remains it was part of a bigger city, and whenever Mom and Dad needed to go do something, it was away from the boondocks. As such, from a young age, the city has always been the 'magical' place, it was where everything was, and where we lived was just beyond it. Then I actually lived in the city, and then lived in Colorado Springs next to the mall, then I lived in the suburban part,and that emphasis of "everything is in town" hit again. The end result? I like big cities. I hate small towns with a passion.(Also I associate small towns with bad people, but let's not get bogged down here)

              Then you need to add in the fact that while I stay with my faith, I have always had trouble with the people in it. I once said I never really had a role model within the church, and I think that's still mostly true, though I think some people came close. Church was never really about community though, it was never about camaraderie. No, it was like most of the rest of the world for me growing up, in that it was largely me versus everyone else. As a kid, it was me versus the other kids, as a teen it was me versus the other kids and the dumbass adults(and seriously, there were dumbass adults). I think it might speak volumes about either the power of "baby's first religion" or something of the compelling logic, if not the actual power, of the LDS Doctrine that I stayed with it all these years in spite of knowing predominantly crappy members. The short hand of this is that I have a real tendency to not like Church members.

              I might be biased against

              Little wonder, then, that I tell people I came to Rexburg out of desperation.

              And that's true-I needed out of my house and way of life in Colorado Springs. I haven't improved like I hoped I would, but that's my own damn fault. Still, it is a good thing I got out...but only just, because Rexburg is a unique American Hell. Oh, there might be worse places to live in America, let alone the world, but that doesn't change the fact that this place is still a hell.


              First off, the 'physical' properties of the town itself. It's limited as shit. With no car to escape and everything being nicely compacted together, you very easily get stuck in the same old place with the same old streets with the same old shit all the time. The physical sense of being caged takes only a short bit to sink, and considering I'm used to feeling quite free, thank you very much, that's maddening. Then you add in the time constraints. Lots of other college towns like to stay open pretty much all night, to say nothing of actual cities where nightlife is a given, but thanks to that BYUI Honor Code and the fact that this is a place where people raise their dear little children to be safe from the evils of the world, nothing is open at night. Shops close up by 8, pretty much-even if they're open, expect people to be sore at you for even daring coming in at 8:01(unless you're SKeeters, which graciously keeps a fun atmosphere until midnight, when they will actually kick you out). Of course, going back to that small town/BYUI Honor Code/Save the Children thing, there's no reason to be out at night because there's nothing here. If you want to eat out, your options are limited, because most everything is an inbred sheltered white man's interpretations of foreign cuisine or fast food. Some gems of good food exists, but because they are few, they also get worn out really fast. And that's just eating out! There are two movie theaters, both of which don't play rated R movies because [your salvation is mechanized], there's two shitty arcades, one which has an okay but overpriced laser tag arena, and....that's about it. Their hobby stores are pitiful, and that's about the only other thing that can be said. All of this builds into one simple thing:

              You are trapped here, and this is little better than prison.

              That might be overselling it, but it's god awful.

              And then...there's the 'culture'.

              Two moments, I think, really encapsulate this.

              1)So Br was in this class on China. It was god-awful. It hurt to hear about it. Not the point though. So he was in this class, and they were talking about China's child-limit laws, and discussing why China did it and how that they were actually in a position where, yes, the birth rate was hurting the state and causing the suffering of families all over. Family is kind of a big deal in the Church, so you think this might register.

              On cue, one girl, pride of her people, said that she thought it was wrong that they had the limit because the Church said that families are important and that bringing children into the world so one of the most important and sacred duties people could have. She would not back the fuck down.

              Lots of people silently agreed with her.

              2)So I was in a class called "Fundamentals of Research and Presentation", and we were peer-reviewing work-I forget what the work actually was, I just remember some of the things that were being talked about. A lot of it was dull, trite, boring, standard stuff you'd expect from a Mormon audience in the English field trying to take the marketplace seriously. Who cares.

              I remember one guy was talking about using graphic novels in the education fields,and what they could contribute to an English Education. The guy's work in question is kind of the product you'd expect from him-he was one of those people where comic books were his life, unhealthily so, but it was still minorly interesting.

              One of the critiques/comments read "I'm a little worried about the use of graphic novels-is this for a Mormon audience or for a general one?". See, she thought graphic was sort for pornographic.

              What. The. Fuck.

              Here's the thing. This sort of shit happens all over the place. Rexburg, and BYU-I, is filled with mechanized, slackjawed, glass-eyed, copy & pasted automatons who spew church primary answers as state law and bend all things to a limited view of the church, knowledge/intelligence/creativity/wisdom be damned.

              If the New World of Darkness was real, I'd accuse Rexburg of being a God-Machine experiment for it's own sterile Heaven-On-Earth.

              Part of this is that while the BYU programs are designed to bring people together, if not from around the entire world, then at least from all across America, BYU-I has turned into the Church's dumping grounds of sorts. Not to belittle the smart people or real talent that I know of here, but for the most part, if the church school program thinks you're actually worth something, you go to Provo, if not, you go to Rexburg.

              It might be related, it might be not, but BYU-I operates like Idaho's Community College. I don't have the actual stats, but about half of the people I have met are from Idaho. Most of these people are the really dumb ones. Dumping grounds indeed, and I think the fact it makes them feel secure ensures they stay in the sate, and breed more automaton babies.

              And it doesn't help that the school-and the city- promote adherence to the Gospel Doctrine to an almost police state level. As someone mockingly pointed out twisting Joseph Smith's own words, "I teach them correct principles, and they govern themselves...unless they sign a little piece of paper to get education, then they're to be governed by everyone else". Not only does the school's Honor Code go above and beyond what actual church commandments ask for(have a hole in your jeans and can't afford new ones? TOO BAD, EVERYONE'S GONNA GIVE YOU SHIT FOR IT!), but it actively demands that if someone is doing wrong, you tell the honor code office and they'll come in with their whips and knives and thumbscrews and a threat of expulsion. Very fortunately, most people, even the most zealous of Mormon-machines, don't do this, but it hangs over your head like the god damn Doom of Damocles all the same.

              And that's the biggest problem here. For a place where the Church should be, I dunno, at it's strongest, it's at it's falsest. They don't care if you actually are seeking to improve your testimony. They don't encourage love, they encourage marriage and babymaking. They don't encourage understanding, they encourage obedience and conformity. They don't encourage kindness, they encourage defending the home in this crumbling age. They don't encourage the united efforts of individuals to exalt themselves and others-they just want things to look like Main Street, Disneyland.

              It's like....if the God-Machine and Idiocracy had a baby delivered and co-raised by Zachary Comstock from Bioshock Infinite, and that baby was a backwards town in an unloved part of a confused country. That's what Rexburg is.

                                      I will not say I like my city, because it's not about the city here in Colorado. It's about the state. And I would never live anywhere else.

                                      Here I have mountains, lakes, rivers, trees....lots of places to hide from people. I don't really like people all that much, so I take whatever opportunities I can to get away from them.

                                      Few natural disasters around here to worry about. Live close enough to the mountains and you avoid the tornadoes. All the volcanoes are dormant. The earthquakes are far enough to the south and minimal enough that most of us don't know they happened until they're reported on the news. No hurricanes here, too far inland. If the polar ice caps do melt, I won't have to move. Yes, we do get a few ice storms and the snow will bury you from time to time and you don't want to live too close to the rivers in case of flooding or too far into the woods in case of fires. However, anyone with a bit of sense knows how to avoid or combat those situations.

                                      As far as a city to get the most of those advantages at once, anything west of Denver proper usually does the trick. Lots of little suburbs around there, plus a few towns up into the foothills. You can be in downtown Denver (if that's something you care for) from most of them in an hour or less.

                                      And Denver itself really isn't that bad at all. The air pollution is a lot lower than most cities that size, as are the crime rates, unemployment, etc. Denver isn't usually at the top of any of these stats, but it's usually not at the bottom, either. Colorado frequently gets the "healthiest place to live" award, but I contribute a lot of that to the fact that most urbanized places have bike/walking paths within a few yards from the houses and the rural places are full of people busting their asses with physical labor all day.

                                                  PBear covered things pretty well. I'll add a few things.

                                                  I grew up in the Denver metro area. Specifically in the Westminster/Thornton area. I think that it's a great place to live. As PBear said, the mountains are close. They were about a half hour drive from where I grew up. This gives you lots of opportunity for outdoorsy stuff. You can go hiking, backpacking, camping in the summer, skiing/snowboarding in the winter. They're beautiful and allow you to get away from the press of civilization when you need to. You can go hunting, if you're into that kind of thing.

                                                  As PBear said, Colorado is the "healthiest place to live" and I'll add on to that that, statistically, people in Colorado exercise more than any other state in the union. I do think that he's right in that this is helped by the plethora of paths to walk/bike on. Also, getting out to the mountains for the weekend is easy. Like sun? (Most people do.) Colorado statistically has more sunny days per year than any other state. Yet, at the same time, it's not oppressive like it is in other places. Colorado is a very dry place, which some people consider to be negative, but a dry heat is usually considered to be better than a humid heat. A typical forecast for the summer when I was growing up was highs in the 90s with a chance of afternoon thundershowers. I'll second PBear's saying that there are essentially no natural disasters. I do remember twice when I was a kid that we hid in our basement because of a tornado warning, but never did it amount to anything. Flash floods are probably the biggest cause of concern for most people, but the area where I lived had adequate overflow control that it never was a serious problem for us. If you live too close to or in the mountains, there is the danger of landslides or wildfires.

                                                  The Denver area has many attractions. There's the Museum of Nature and science, which I thoroughly enjoy. Right next door to that is the zoo. Elsewhere in Denver is the Center for the Performing Arts which has high quality plays/musicals, orchestras, dance etc. In Denver you have the 16th Street mall which has a few restaurants with divine cuisine and has just an interesting cross section of humanity wandering through it. In Boulder you have the Pearl Street mall, which has some, admittedly, strange people (contortionists for one).

                                                  Denver regularly has a Renaissance Faire. I've never been, but I've heard good things. There are numerous LGS that might become your FLGS. Black and Read in Arvada has a good selection of books.

                                                  Really, I think that the Denver metro area is a great place to live. If you're looking to move, which I suspect you are, it would be a good place to consider.

                                                  Plus PBear is there are you guys could sit down and have a nice, cold beer and talk philosophy (or whatever). :-P
