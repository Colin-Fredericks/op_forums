                                  Ah so it’s gameworlds as well? Well then to continue;


                                  I’ve already expressed that living in anything D&D (or D&D-adjacent) isn’t any better and might be worse depending on the setting.

                                  So moving on, the WOD is completely out of the picture as god has literally left the building in that setting. Even if you are a Mage the whole of reality is constantly punching you in the dick, so it’s not worth it.

                                  Living in the Trinity Continuum means I’d better have powers or it’s not worth it. And if I DO have powers I’m not just going to be taint-battered, I’m gonna be deep fried in it till I ascend. That or my psiad powers will have me standing further and further away from baseline humanity and all of their grotesque stupidity. That goes for the setting of Mutants & masterminds since it’s too close to comic world, and we already know that’s insanity.

                                  Scion may be fun to play, but it would still be awful to live in that setting. Being quasi-divine doesn’t make this better, it just makes it worse. And the prospect of getting swept up into a fatebond because some jackass can’t keep their “powers” in their pants makes my butt hurt pre-emptively.

                                  I remember a day in my far-off youth where I was a big Star Wars fan, but now if you told me I could live in that universe I’d tell you to quit smoking rocks. Not merely because of all the licensing idiocy lately, mostly because I can’t recall a single world in that setting that was worth living on. They’re all wasteland outposts or wretched hives of scum and villainy, and I get enough of that in the American midwest.


                                  If we move into video gameworlds it gets worse!


                                  All of the Final Fantasy multiverse (since it’s established that all those worlds are separate dimensions) may have a semi-consistent set of jobs and skills, but they are also prone to being infested with evil empires, irate godlings, and cosmic horrors disguised as “secret bosses”. I don’t need to have to level up to level 12, or go impotent from a Mako infusion JUST to pick up my amazon deliveries.

                                  The world of the Mana series seems to have a very unusual tidal magic axiom that I find...distasteful. Which is too bad since ti seems pretty peaceful there.

                                  The world of Suikoden is extremely war-prone, so is that of the Shining franchise, so life will be harder than normal due to constant wartime economy fatigue.

                                  SMT & Persona games may be charming, but living in those worlds means literally walking into a materialized crack dream and/or psychosis. I don’t have enough psychology background to deal with that every single day.

                                  The worlds of Xenosaga, Bioshock, and Mass Effect prove that post-scarcity is not only a myth, it’s a pipe-dream. Borderlands is prolly just a few decades away from real life.

                                  It should go without saying that any world infected with the corporate horseshit of Weyland-Yutani, Umbrella, Mobius, Shin-Ra, or frankly ANY other megacorporation isn’t worth living in.

                                  Fargaia, the world of Wild Arms exists just to prove that it can, and is even more pre-destroyed than FF7’s world is. Plus, the American Wild West was NOT a good time, no matter what some dumb-ass tries to tell you. Adding magic to that mess would just make a BIGGER mess.

                                  The setting of Ultima was an isekai dumping ground last I checked, which is all the more reason to leave it alone.

                                  Phantasy Star has the interesting background of an omnipotent cosmic horror deciding it has nothing better to do than utterly destroy all things vaguely human (parmian) for the sake of completion. All the hyper-space age tech in the universe doesn’t make up for that.
