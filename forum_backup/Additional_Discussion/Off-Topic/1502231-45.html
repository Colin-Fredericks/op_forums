            Yeah, acting skills are important, but there are a lot of other attitudes that can make or break your role in a movie.

            Van Damme is bad, but he doesn't shy from whatever the movie requires from him. Even his worst scene, the Street Fighter speech, was mostly the fault of whomever wrote it, and you can say the director should have just cut it or put another character to do it. It had no reason to happen except American Exceptionalism. He was perfect in Universal Soldier, as it was a mix of a story almost tailor-made for his lack of acting skills and his willingness to be what the movie needs, even if it is someone vulnerable and scarred.

            Seagal is terrible to work with. He only accepts very similar roles, overly badass veterans of half a dozen government agencies that never die. He already threatened to sue a director that refused to make an extra scene in a movie for his character to survive. Even apart all the dubious shit in his life, he's an asshole to work with, and that only limits him. And he simply thinks his skill with martial arts make him perfect at what he does. Good movies with him happen sometimes because some directors know how to use him as he is, and not a bit more.

            Rock and Diesel both have better acting skills than the old crew could get away with in the 80s and 90s, but they also have a lot of the smug attitude of Seagal. They also want Schwarzenegger's fame, and that's horrible because they try to do things they're not really good at. They have other strong suits than basic muscle-action, but comedy isn't one of those.

            AFAIK Stallone is pretty good, especially taking into account that his expression and speech are literally physically impaired. But he's actually even more accomplished as writer and director, as he wrote most of his films. Overall he's a more complete artist, so to speak, and that has an effect on his acting as most of his movies are literally tailor-made for him. And yes, he wrote or co-wrote every single Rocky and Rambo movie.

                        Saw "Predator: Badlands" and it was way better than I expected. To be honest, the idea of making a Predator-movie where the Predator is the protagonist seemed a bit dubious to me but this movie works surprisingly well. The best part about it is that it's just fun. Especially in the second half when it becomes clear what the movie is really about. It's pretty light on story and character but it's all pretty straightforward. The thing that still makes it entertaining despite that is the action-scenes. They are actually quite good and well-choreographed. The movie's also pretty good in the design of the alien flora and fauna. This is the kind of movie I wish Tron: Ares had been. Just a really fun sci-fi-action-movie.

                        Also saw Kathryn Bigelow's new movie "House Of Dynamite"... Let's just say towards the end of the movie the US-president actually says "I've heard on a podcast once that our world is like a house of dynamite." - and this is supposed to be a profound moment. That gave me a good laugh. And others in the theater were laughing too. So, that was a good moment. To describe the movie a little bit: It's a very detail-oriented, realistic movie about the US-government-response to a nuclear warhead that's headed for the US. It's very realistic. It's also one of the most empty-headed movies I've seen in a while. Like, think about the premise. Would it shock you to imagine people being stressed about it or people worrying about loved ones? Yep, that's in the movie. And that's it. The movie has nothing else to say about the topic. Seriously. The last minute of Christopher Nolan's Oppenheimer had more to say about nuclear weapons than this two hour movie. That certainly was an interesting experience.

                                                Finished Outer Worlds 2. It was pretty good and I liked it more than the first one. Also liked it more than Obsidian's other game, their attempt at a Skyrim-esque game Avowed (still can't believe they decided to force you to play a god-touched). It's a pretty good sequel in the sense that it has improved stuff from the first game, especially when it comes the game's skill-system and the flaw-system. But having finished it, I feel like some skills are more worthwhile than others (putting points in Speech felt more like a must than an option, for example). The option to go third-person this time around felt nice but the sneak-animations looked just silly. Maybe they will fix that later. The story was... okay. At the end, it does come off like one of those stories that could've been solved immediately if everybody had just talked to each other.

                                                But I did love the addition of the radio-channels. Lots of creativity there. Each of the three main-factions has a radio-channel and some of the best writing, in my opinion, was right there. And the music was also great. The math-obsessed Order making a song about how great prime-numbers are and the capitalist Auntie's Choice channel's promoting a musical about how the company got founded, for example. Stuff like that. Definitely had a good time. I wouldn't put it on my GOTY-list but it was pretty good.
