                    I'm sure when you work that stuff out, the payoff is great. Just way too high a learning curve for me.
                    If you are looking for superheroes you might want to look at Top ten and Tom Strong. Watchmen revolutionized the Genre unforunately it was a step back. Miracle man and Authority are pretty much much more grittier and dark versions of the Genre. If you just want normal Comics, Lucifer, Books of magic, Sandman all by Neil Gaiman. The Last Man. Man, I have a lot of recommendations. Mostly I would recommend you look far away from the big two except for specific stand alone stories. Getting invested in such a payoff is actually BAD.

                    Look at Cosmic marvel. From 2007-2010, there was a series of interconnecting comics. It was easy for anyone to follow because it was small. IT was an amazing Space opera. It had Highs and Lows (Never REad Wraith). Then the movie was announced Bendis took Over and RUINED the Guardians of the Galaxy. It's completely unrecognizable. That's the problem with the Big Two. They give good things to bad writers while the Good writers struggle to sell. Invitably the longer you stay in the more you start to despise what's happening to characters you love because it's not even done well. Granted, sometimes Good writers make really good works. New Ghost Rider, New Warriors, Blue Beetle before the reboot. That are just fantastic but get cancelled because of sales. It's just a Roller Coaster and I have problems getting on anymore. It's much better to stick to limited projects with set runs. At least then, if you liked it you know that the quality won't change.

                                        As to Superhero looks. You have the start of many where they were trying to at least some what distance them from the every day. When you get to the 80's and 90's they kept to that line for the most part but adding in things that would update. Which often came from the more every day. Still trying to meet the expectations of the reader though and separate that this is a SUPERhero. You of course got new heroes that didn't go that line but the reason for that was more due to the expectations of that heroes universe. Too, many of those are really comic heroes rather than superheroes which is an important distinction some are failing to see. That isn't a quality distinction, but a distinction of category. I've never seen people get their undergarments more in a bunch then when you try to say that their favorite hero really isn't a SUPERhero.

                                        The problem for me in comics as a whole is that now you have more artists in charge so you get whatever they want shoved down your throat. The mentality has changed from trying to get the reader in and enjoy to the expectation that the reader has to enjoy whatever you put out there. Accompanied by the ever popular comforting thought that if you don't succeed it is because "they just don't understand you". For me it seems it is because there is no longer the balance for that artistic temperament. The greatest things of my generation came out of that balance, that need that both sides had to argue to get what they wanted. Anytime one totally overrules the other is usually when bad things happen.

                                                    Originally posted by Allan53 View Post

                                                    With the understanding it's a categorical difference, not an implication of quality, what would you say is the difference between a comic hero and a super hero?
                                                    It is a genre like anything else. That doesn't mean that it can only be that, but people are coming to it looking for an experience. While people expectations will differ some you still have a mean to work from. If half way through Inspector Gadget the Claw starts ripping out people's entrails and hanging them by them you stepped far afield a comedy(yes extreme example). If Batman runs around defeating villains in a baseball cap and trench coat your kind of loosing that Superhero identity. That might be good for a noir run or side story, but isn't good for a new sole focus identity. Just wearing a jacket doesn't really do that but it starts stepping in that direction.

                                                    Spandex(just to simplify the example) is part of the uniform of the Superhero like a policeman or Fire Fighter has a uniform. They can still be that without the uniform, but their identity is in large part wrapped up in that. The difference between the two is that a Superhero is supposed to be a beacon(that isn't to say hero vs anit-hero or Superman vs Batman vs Spawn) and that outfit helps that identification, helps them stand out in that needed way. I wouldn't want to see Constantine in spandex. It might be as cheesy as the white hat/black hat in old westerns, but that is part of the experience. The Superhero is wrapped up in that identity created by the outfit.


                                                    In this specific case theres more a why bother. It acomplishes even less than Clark Kent taking off his glasses to become Superman. The line from the link in danelsan's post kind encapsulates all of this for me.

                                                    "To operate on the level she wants to, she needs something that can pass for semi-normal and she can turn it on to crazy if she needs to."

                                                    First in the aknowledgement that a superhero should "turn it to crazy" and second in the oblivious mindset that closing her jacket and extending her shades does "turn it to crazy". Seriously? That is like say a banana pepper and a hobniario are just as hot.

                                                    The other one that got to me was: "She's sexy but not in a spandex way — in a modern, unique way." More like in a 90's biker wannabe kind of way. I'm at a loss how it is unique or modern. I can picture them sitting around at a Marvel roundtable discussing this and patting themselves on the back about how visionary and "hip" this is going to be. It would be like sitting in the eye of a hurricane saying how nice the weather is today. Its being oblivious to anything else going on around it.

                                                    What they seem to say they are expecting and what they've created are at totally opposite ends of the spectrum. So to be a detective she had to loose the outfit, her figure, and her age? Its more like they were looking to make something like Batgirl and decided Spider-Woman was the way to do that rather than try and make a new character or use some of the characters tailor made to already do that. She looks more like some 16 year old Spider-Girl. It would be like trying to turn Superman into Superboy and say it is the natural progression of the character.

                                                    It feels like its what too many of these failed movies have tried to do. They think the existing fans are some type of safety net. Its possible as it has worked before, but it has never done anything for the longevity of the franchise they do that to. The novelty will wear off and usually by the time it does the fans that have allowed it to carry are gone by then.
