    My West Marches campaign idea so far:

    Rather than being a “pure” Westmarches game where the region is largely unexplored until the PCs get there, I am planning to place it in (my home brewed version of) Nentir Vale. The Nentir Vale map from 4e, which can easily be found online, will serve as the rough map of the region, which will be more-or-less accurate in broad strokes, but will not match the true terrain 1:1. I’m hoping this will give the players enough of a baseline to work from, while leaving room for surprises. The PCs will be part of an organization I’m currently calling the Wardens of the Vale, who much like Tolkien’s Rangers of the North, act as defenders of the region against the encroaching Darkness. At time of the campaign’s start, the Wardens will be largely defunct, save for a few scattered Wardens who refuse to give up the cause despite it being seemingly long lost. My hope is that the PCs will take up the mantle and work to restore the Wardens of the Vale to their former glory.

    Of course, a key selling point of West Marches is that it is player-driven, and I wouldn’t want the few remaining Wardens to steal the players’ thunder, which is part of why I want them to be dying out. But I want a minimal old-guard presence primarily to deal with an issue that I have heard about plaguing West Marches games: treasure hoarding. Since there is no regular party, it is entirely possible that players might in some sessions find magic items or other rewards that none in the current party have any use for, but may want to keep in hopes of trading with other players, which could take the group dynamic from merely competitive to hostile, which I don’t want. So my idea is that any magic items found on expedition are considered the property of the Wardens of the Vale - use them while on expedition if you can, but when you return, you turn it in, and everyone in the party that recovered it gets an equal share of its gold value in credit with the guild, which you can use to maintain your living expenses, upgrade your equipment, stock up on healing potions and the like, or save up to buy magic items recovered by the guild. I’m also considering allowing players to sort of rent recovered items, but I’m not sure how I want that to work yet.

    Another common problem in West Marches games I’ve heard about that I think my plan will help address is when players out-leveling the zones nearest to town, but still having to wade through all that to get to more level-appropriate zones (or just deciding to faceroll through the early zones forever rather than making longer and longer expeditions into the wild as they level up.) By placing the campaign in a Points of Light setting rather than an uncharted frontier with only one settlement of the free peoples, I can have the different settlements act as sort of checkpoints. When your character out-levels Fallcrest and its surrounding areas, you can make your way to Winterhaven, whose surrounding areas are a bit higher level. When you grow past that, you can go to Harkenwold. Etc. I’m not 100% sure what I want the actual progression to be, except for the fact that I’m pretty sure I want Hammerfast and the Dawnforge Mountains to be the endgame zone. Or At least, the endgame of exploring the Vale proper. Depending on how things shake out, I might consider pushing beyond the Vale and maybe even into planar exploration for the true endgame. But I’m getting way ahead of myself, the base concept is ambitious enough as-is.

    EDIT: Actually, come to think of it, planar travel, as well as Underdark exploration, would make for a great way to introduce more traditional West Marches exploratory play into a setting with a relatively well-charted base map.

        One way to handle this is to make one of the goals of the campaign to re-establish routes of travel between settlements, and/or liberate settlements from whatever evil force/military is dominating it. The more characters accomplish in an area, the easier it is for people to come and go without getting accosted. Eliminate enough monster lairs or fortresses controlled by evil humanoids, the more the area is considered "safe". It sort of feeds into the points-of-light idea, where characters work their way throughout the map, dispelling evil and bringing peace one "zone" at a time.

        This could even be used to scale earlier points of interest for later parties. As adventurers run roughshod over a particular part of the countryside, monsters and enemies that are sufficiently badass will start concentrating in what areas they have left, bolstering the ranks (and thus the encounters) of a location. On the flipside, foes from higher level areas start sending "reinforcements" to these lower level places, as an attempt to shore up flagging defenses and potentially make counterattacks.

        Even powerful monsters - the stupid ones, that don't really do "plans" or "allegiances" - can elect to move into areas that were previously replete with other strong predators, but that are suddenly lacking in competition due to all these adventurers slaying the resident monsters. Say a zone used to have a Manticore, a Troll, and a Hill Giant, but PCs killed or drove those all off. Well that's great for the Young Red Dragon, who can move in and take all the prey creatures for itself without interference. Especially since all the adventurers seem intent on moving on to other areas.

        Magestuck: The Ascension
