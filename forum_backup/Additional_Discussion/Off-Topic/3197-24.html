                                S5E8 - Lost Treasures of Griffonstone

                                Really took some time for me to see this episode, and then when I finally did, I didn't have much to say about it.

                                I mean, it's really good! I loved it. The adventure formula worked wonders, and I'm inclined to believe it's due to the fact that they have to find out what the problem was when they go there. That added an incentive to interact more meaningfully with the place they were put in. I'm not sure the map as a quest dispenser won't wear thin after a while, but right now, it seem to be working.

                                But yeah, Pinkie and Rainbow are colourful and interesting, Gilda is back again, we get more worldbuilding and Twilight being all salty. Sweet!

                                Also this.

                                There were some things I had a hard time to swallow. Are we really supposed to believe that everyone is a douchebag in Griffonstone? Also, I get that they wanted to visually represent the disarray the place is in, but at least clear the rubble out of the houses, right?

                                Disregarding that, I liked the episode, it was refreshing and clever while still being a really solid, narrative-focused staple of a series.

                                                        S5E9 - Slice of Life

                                                        Having rewatched the episode, I now hope I can formulate some more coherent thoughts on it.

                                                        Not on the content, though. That'll just have to stand on on its own. You will either like it or not, and I think it's as simple as that in this case. Personally, I liked most of it, but I felt like the parts where they dragged out material that the fandom had already done at length was easily the weakest. I mean, cool to see Octavia and Vinyl living together. Less cool to see them doing the classic/dubstep mix thing, since the fandom has already done that, and much better. Picked up again when they went for a ride through Ponyville, though that stretched my suspension of disbelief juuust shy of breaking. I think Lyra and Bon Bon was really well handled: they incorporated fanon but added their own (crazy) spin on it too. You really kind of do have to take this episode at face value; some things will work for you, others won't, and that's really all there is to say on the matter.

                                                        Is this a "love letter to the bronies"? It certainly strokes our egos, no doubt, see in "this thing that we made" being retold back to us. I can relate to that. On that note, yeah, one episode like this every five seasons sounds about like the right dose. Not only because the fandom is really the best at doing fandom stuff, but also because the best love letter we can get is a continued original, high quality production of the show we love.

                                                        The question that I keep asking myself is how the episode looks to someone who's not in on the jokes. It's really internal. For a single episode, that's okay in my mind, but somewhere a bunch of 10-year-old might sit around wondering what the hell just happened.

                                                        Hell, I'm wondering what the hell just happened.
