                                Nobody's saying that all technological development in the Star Wars universe stopped. We're saying that the rate of advancement is fair slow due to technological limits being hit in a large number of fields, and the inertia of any military-industrial complex (that is, if you have a Star Destroyer factory already, it's much easier to retrofit existing Star Destroyers and build knew ones with updated packages, than it is to build an entirely new ship design, the new factories with it, and stop using Star Destroyers completely).

                                None of the developments you've mentioned represent innovations that would drastically impact what spaceship designs would actually look like. The Death Star? It's a giant beam weapon that needs a station as big as a moon to operate. You're not going to redesign TIE Fighters because of it. A forked lightsabre? Nothing indicates this is actually new tech (forking plasma and energy is something we can do IRL), and nothing even really shows it's all that practical or beneficial. Only one person is using it. New tracking tech? No reason to redesign the entire ship, it's just a new sensor package.

                                The complaint is, "why are they using ships from 30 years ago, instead of brand new ships?" which some technological developments that don't have any impact of ship design doesn't address.

                                                The thing about predjudice, sexism included, is that it’s pernicious. Very, very few people consciously dislike TLJ because they hate women and hate seeing women in prominent roles in the film (and even fewer of them would admit it.) However, most of the world is built with male-centric bias. Our baseline expectations about gender relations are informed by our cultural norms, which are rooted in a lot of biases and predjudice. So you may not think “I don’t like Rey/Admiral Holdo/Rose/Rian Johnson’s depiction of Leia/etc. because they are women who excercize authority over men and that makes me uncomfortable,” that doesn’t mean it isn’t the case.

                                                The thesis of the Pop Culture Detective’s video is that The Last Jedi is all about subverting expectations, not only of the story of Star Wars, but of the space fantasy genre, and especially of the lone hero narrative and the view of masculinity it presents. The idea that a significant part of the hate for the film comes from people who are unfomfotable seeing the characters they expect to be archetypal masculine heroes being challenged by women is really just a side-note to his argument, necessary because it’s a big part of the discourse surrounding TLJ, but not necessary to support his thesis.
