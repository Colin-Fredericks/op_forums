    For me, this comes as a... mixed bag of feelings.

    On the other hand, Paradox is the spiritual successor to Target games who created the first roleplaying game I ever played... Drakar & Demoner. That they now have bought the company who has produced perhaps the second best rpg I've ever played makes me shiver in my timbers.

    I'm very afraid that they'll axe nWoD simply because masquerade and the oWoD is more familiar to most Swedish players. heck, I've had to inform people were to buy requiem and such books at our local "game store" in Stockholm.     On the OTHER hand, I'm extremely pumped that WW is getting back in business and that this might lead to even further nWoD releases. I love the game and my sincere hope is that things go well.

    And on the Swedish hand, as Swede... yeah, go Swedish Paradox for making bacon moves!

                                Originally posted by branford View Post
                                Here's the link to the discussion where Martin made the comment.

                                https://www.facebook.com/permalink.p...18&id=13735128

                                He's seems like a good guy with an appreciation for both the classic and new WOD.

                                I just hope his comment means the people of OPP, either in-house or as a licensee, will still be heavily involved in WW products, and that both WOD's survive as viable game lines. Sadly, if Paradox want to focus all its attention on the classic WOD or even a new new WOD, his comment could be interpreted to mean that he'll feel really bad when they cancel the game lines many here have grown to appreciate.
                                The thing is, you don't have to be in any way a "bad person" to end a product line or drastically change an IP. RPGs are a great illustration of this - most of the people involved in making them are very cool people - but they sometimes have to come to decisions to cancel your favourite thing ever. That doesn't make them bad, but it also doesn't that, because they're not bad, it won't happen.

                                I think that as more information has come out, we've seen a fairly clear pattern where Paradox's guys, particularly Martin, have clearly expressed their love for the WoD (and the nWoD here), and to a lesser extent Exalted, but that is not necessarily a good thing if you want to maintain the status quo, or build from the status quo to a sort of status quo+.

                                The status quo is OPP are putting out really solid products, on a regular basis, for both the nWoD and oWoD, and other game-lines, but are having to use PoD rather than trad-publishing, and aren't reaching as far as WW once did.

                                The status quo plus would perhaps be that they were empowered to use trad publishing for some products, get them back on the shelves, get some marketing going, some cross-sales with computer games and so on.

                                However, given that Martin is a huge fan of the WoD, and is planning to be a hands-on "Lead Storyteller", as he puts it, i.e. directly in charge of the brand - "stewarding it" as he puts it, there can be little question that the status quo or a status quo plus can continue, at least for the WoD (I could see it for Exalted and other lines). It cannot. That doesn't necessarily mean bad things for OPP, but it does mean big things, good or bad or, more likely, neither.

                                I still think the most likely scenario, going solely on what Paradox have said, is:

                                A) Near-complete nWoD/cWoD products allowed to be finished.

                                B) Further-out ones politely cancelled.

                                C) A new WoD that's neither cWoD or nWoD but contains the bits of both that work best and, importantly, are most marketable (what those are is open to discussion, of course), with a real focus on being accessible and getting new players in (much like 5E D&D has gone for, quite successfully).

                                D) WoD actually able to be marketed again, back in stores, etc.

                                E) nWoD/cWoD stuff still sold as PDFs - perhaps with branding to make it even more clear it's not "current". I don't think they'll get rid of them, as it would just cause bad feeling.

                                F) Games from Paradox using their new-new-WoD lines.

                                If we're very VERY lucky Paradox will use the fact that they have a lot of coders at their disposal to develop what WotC has always failed to properly develop for their games - proper, branded, full-on apps to help play and run them. I mean, we can always get dice-rollers and the like, but there's so much more that could be done, and Paradox, whilst not in a perfect position to do this (they're not mobile developers, which is probably a good thing) are in a good position, and have the basic expertise.

                                I'm just really hoping they're happy enough with the current Exalted to let 3E play out properly (I imagine they are, given it's quality - if they want to re-do the art or whatever, well, that's probably fine!), and that whatever the new-new-WoD is, it's cool - I'm pretty sure it will be, though.

                                (So long as Ericsson doesn't randomly decide that the "best rpg devs in Sweden" are better than the people who've been working on these products for years, of course. My wife, a long-time Paradox fan, points out that Paradox typically leaves the current team alone when they take over places, and is good at working with other teams but I must say the whole "We're making sure we're working with the best people" combined with comments like "the best rpg devs in Sweden" combined with the fact that presumably Ericsson is friends with all the guys at Paradox does raise a creepy spectre - hopefully nothing like that will happen, though)

I can't see how the fanbase of both worlds could be reconciled to such a radical change.
