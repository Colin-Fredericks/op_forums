        I know I'm really late to the party on these, but I finally got to watch The Dark Knight Rises and The Man of Steel.

        For The Dark Knight Rises, using spoiler in case there's anyone who's not been able to see it even longer than me:
        I thought it was a good finish to the series, and I like how it could be taken into a new series with "Robin". I'm probably too aware of the mythology for Talia to have been a surprise, so I had her pegged early on. Making Bane less super-solider and more devoted nut-case from the League of Shadows was a nice way to make him fit the established mythology. I'm still waffling on the part about Bruce fixing the auto-pilot: on one hand, it's a classic Batman move; on the other hand, this Batman always seemed more willing to die executing the crazy-ass moves. It may have been just a tad over hyped by the people I know who'd seen it in theatres, but I'm in Colorado, so there was that fun, extra level of "I might die seeing this" tension over the movie. All in all, not a bad movie.


        For The Man of Steel, same as above, spoilers:
        Now I see why the had Russell Crowe as Jor-El. This is not the gentle Marlon Brando style Jor-El. This is a Jor-El who's seen war and needs to be believable kicking ass and taking names. Kevin Costner as Jonathan Kent also made more sense as the movie went on, especially once he let the tornado take him to show Clark he meant what he said. Henry Cavill had a good look for the character and pulled off the performance pretty well. It's not easy to be that sincere and upstanding without being cheesy. Christopher Reeve had a harder time of it with each sequel. Henry Cavill did good this time, we'll see if he has the stamina to stay that way. The way Krypton was shown I think worked very well for it's early sci-fi pulp roots, somewhat over the top, but just enough to make it "not just humans in space". Zach Snyder was also a good choice because this movie needs to be kind of over the top, it's friggin' Superman, after all. I didn't like how quickly he had a costume, though. I know it's just a red & blue suit in the established Kryptonian style, but still....too quick and easy. My favorite part has to be how they made sure Lois Lane is not a blind idiot. She already knows who he really is and she has to PRETEND SHE DOESN'T (which would have made the whole "romantic comedy" style of Lois & Clark about 50 times funnier, I think). Makes about 10,000 times more sense than this smart reporter being fooled by a pair of glasses. Again, all in all, not a bad movie.

                                                    I just saw two films at the local dollar theater, and I'm glad I didn't pay more for either.

                                                    Thor: the Dark World was sadly kind of forgettable. Usually I leave one of these films wanting to talk about some part of it, but most of it just sort of slid out of my mind as soon as the house lights came up. It criminally under-used the acting talent assembled. The best parts of it were Loki and Thor verbally sparring. The worst parts were... well... the entire behavior pattern of the Dark Elves, who are bad guys just because. What exactly would "returning the universe to darkness" do? They didn't actually seem particularly uncomfortable in the world as it was, so what was so great about the old setup that they felt it worth wrecking everything else to reproduce? Also, Thor and Jane's "romance" is at the "do you like me? check yes/no" note-passing Jr. High phase, not the Swearing-Devotion-Across-the-Cosmos-and-Eternities phase. But that hasn't exactly changed from the first movie.

                                                    The other movie was Ender's Game. My initial prediction was correct - way too much of that book happened inside of its protagonist's head for an easy screen translation. But even the stuff that wasn't Ender's internal struggles was pretty under-developed. I can forgive the drop in emphasis on Peter and Valentine, who only got their sequel-establishing sub-plot when the short story became a novel. But the other kids at Battle School and the Battle School Game itself were practically footnotes. Everyone seemed to be trying very hard, but it was just a bad script to start with. It was nice to see Harrison Ford actually giving a crap about his part, as a lot of his recent movies have felt pretty phoned-in. I liked the visuals a lot, but wish they'd used them to tell more actual story, instead of just kind of shuffling us from one set piece to the next as quickly as possible while the kid playing Ender looks like he's going to cry because the writers haven't provided him anything more interesting to do.
