                                    So a couple weeks ago, I quit my job. As it turns out, I have an anxiety disorder and had been having regular panic attacks. After spending the night in Emergency, my boyfriend and family asked me to quit the job that was causing me a lot of stress. After talking a lot about the financial end of things, I agreed. I'm now jobless and on my first week of anti-anxiety medication which is making me feel really weird, but I know to expect that for the first few weeks until I get used to this stuff. I have to say, though, it's nice to not have my heart racing all the time. My chest no longer feels sore and the quality of my sleep is improving. I go back to see my new family doctor next week and at that time I would like to ask him to refer me to a therapist so I can begin CBT with the goal of re-entering the workforce in the new year.

                                                            A relief of long term depression can seem like mania to someone that is 1) not experienced with mania 2) forgot what it feels like to not be depressed. The thought of becoming manic is much more disturbing than depression to someone that suffers only from the latter. I imagine it would be less of a problem for someone that is aware they suffer from both. The loss of emotional range frequently associated with anti-depressants and mood stabilizers can also be unsettling. I think it is really hard to say that "sane" people feel that way most of the time. It would be more important to find a mental state that allows a person to avoid the problem symptoms while maintaining as much range of feelings as possible than to create a state like someone else. Sometimes a compromise must be made as to how much a person needs to feel and still be productive and safe. Often adjustments need to be made throughout a person's life. Mood disorders are treated or managed, never cured.
