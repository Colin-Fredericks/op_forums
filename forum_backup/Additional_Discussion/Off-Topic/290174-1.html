    I've been playing with the same group since I ever discovered RPG back in 2002. And fortunately, we are four guys: 1 is a good ST, 2 are brilliant STs, and I can't ST even to save my own skin. I'm the ugly duck of the group :P

    The only bad STs I knew were from RPG Meetings that take hold in two cities around here. They are supposed to captivate new players but I guess they do the opposite. Some examples I recall:

    - One ST was doing a VtM session, my friend had a Toreador with dots in Performance which he justified with his character's hobby as Mime. The ST spent 15 minutes asking him why. I was trying to understand what was the problem of someone being a Mime as hobby! Is it strange? Perhaps but so is playing RPG to most people. He finally accepted.

    - Same ST saying the session would take place at some ghost town (metaphorically, there weren't any Wraiths there). Then he asked us why we were there. Well being a one shot game as a demo for new players, everyone did the char on the spot so no backgrounds besides one or two lines. Then he got mad because no one had a plausible explanation to be a vampire in a god forsaken town -.-' "Dude, it's your story, you're the ST, you prepared the session. How about ignoring that or come prepared next time?".

    - Other ST started disallowing every rule that favoured the PCs and enforcing to the full extent of the rule all that made the PCs life harder.

    - Another ST was a rules freak, everything was done by the book with figurines and calculators whenever needed. Boring session as hell where the RP aspect was completely killed.

    And this is why I stopped going to RPG Meetings. Although I had loads of fun in one playing OG

        There have to be more bad ST stories out there. Maybe I should create a "Praising ST" thread, balance things out?

        This story is kind of an odd one. I was introduced to Exalted through this group I still play with, and this guy, who I shall name Adam, ran a game where we were Solar, nominally part of a mercenary company in Nexus, but we mostly did our own thing, and he did a great job of incorporating our goals and making us feel relevant. Even my character, who was a bit vague to be honest, but he did a really good job. I thoroughly enjoyed that game, and still rate it as one of the best games I've played in.

        So that game inevitably finished, and we moved onto other games. A while later, he asked if we wanted to come back to the setting. The way things worked out, most of us ended up with new characters, but I didn't mind too much. This game... was not good, at least in my opinion. A lot of other people seemed to be enjoying themselves, so I want to stress that this could just be me.

        The game mostly revolved around dealing with the Primordial that was released at the end of the first game (it sounds terrible, but it really worked as a climax), and our new characters were resisting it. The game started strong, although there were a couple bumps, but nothing terrible. Then we met up with the other Exalted who were fighting the Primordial, and the primary flaw of the game started to show itself. Now, it's inevitable in that context that something of the players characters will lose something of their special-ness, I get that. But every Exalt apart from us had a 300+ experience, and perfect knowledge of the fluff as well as the STs executive decision about what was "right". One Twilight character, for example, was played by a player whose knowledge of the books was largely limited to the core and Manuals of Exalted Power. If it wasn't in one of those, he didn't know it. But when he contested the NPCs idea of how X worked due to what seemed to me to be totally valid reasoning, he was called stupid and useless IC, with the ST OOC saying that it is clearly spelt out how X works in "one of the books". This was consistent: NPCs were always correct, and disagreeing with them therefore indicated that our characters didn't know what we were talking about.

        This NPC-driven nature of the story was continued, and in fact got worse. No matter what our specialty was, if we disagreed with direction taken by the NPC of our area, we were "shown to be wrong". A Warleader-type Solar was set up in opposition to a Manipulator, who employed and worked with the Solar who was responsible for unleashing the Primordial in the first place. The Manipulator, who had a Sorcerer working for him, apparently had at least 5 points in all Twilight Abilities, plus 3 dot specialities in everything, plus Solar-level sorcery, plus god knows what. Basically, if he wanted to do it, he could with trivial effort, which put both the sorcery-focussed and magitech-focussed PCs right into the redundant pile, which he did, a lot. The Manipulator, who was the biggest offender, not only knew about the Great Curse and everything including the ins and outs of the Ebon Dragons plans and expected everyone to just believe him (which of course all NPCs did, and any PC that didn't was treated as paranoid or stupid), also had 3 Exaltations of different kinds somehow, and all his plans always worked out, no matter how many assumptions were built into them.

        In short (shorter, at least) we were not at all relevant. We either went along with the NPCs, in which case we weren't relevant because there were more skilled NPCs already doing the stuff we should do, or we didn't, in which case we were outside the expected plotline and nothing we did ever came to anything. I found this particularly annoying, because I was playing a sorcery character who the ST had described as "probably the best sorcerer in Creation"; he was my old character who I later picked up again, and had spent the last 4 to 5 years improving his Solar Sorcery. Not that you'd know it, because sorcery was treated as totally useless. Shadowland closing? There were like 3 in Creation, and all in enemy territory. Creatures of Darkness hurting? Pfft, drop one combat NPC into the area, or the CoD had some way of shielding themselves. I even considered researching the spell to withdraw someones soul, to deal with the Warlord who, in Adams words rolled "all the dice in the house, and got even more automatic successes". Adam said it wouldn't work; not because he was an Exalt, but because it would take too long to research, and the Sidereals, who up to this point had been basically the running excuse for how the Manipulator knew literally everything, suddenly decided to not be helpful to my character because his support of the Manipulator, who had hired the releaser of the Primordial, I remind you, was qualified. Adam also said that it wouldn't work anyway; he was of the opinion sorcery was useless, and he was enforcing this view just as hard as he could without actually breaking the written rules. This was after I had gone through two characters, with one dying due to not having a surprise negator in the stand-up fight that wasn't supposed to happen in the sneaking mission (playing a non-combat-focussed Infernal: they don't have as easy access to surprise negators at best), and me choosing to abandon another due to being totally irrelevant.

        The game was awful. If it had gone on any longer, I would have pulled out. I wasn't enjoying it, I wasn't relevant to the story, the story was fine (just don't pretend you're going to affect it one jot). I pointed this out to Adam several times, and he always acted like it was my fault. I have no idea what happened; his first game was great! This one though...

        My Commandments for GMs My Commandments for Players

                So, this one's a bit different : it took place in a larp. A French Larp in fact, which I'm told is the equivalent of an "hardcore larp" in the US.

                It was inspired from the Warhammer universe : the story took place before the corebooks, we were playing during the early years of the empire. In fact, Sigmar only became a god during the last larp. The story was fine, and so did the players. My character's backstory was a blast : I trusted the head ST to write it, and I had the pleasure of discovering that I got the role of the eldest son of the emperor, but not his heir, since I was also a mage, which means that I was officialy banned from any position of power (but by exploiting a bunch of loopholes, I quickly got a siege in the noble assembly).

                Anyway : long story short, we find my father dying from a wound that looks magically incurable. After much heated debate between players about who will be the next emperor, we learn that almost all of the main Lords in the empire (basically, those who ruled each tribe) had been murdered by the Queen of the Asobornes, an amazon-like tribe., thanks to various corrupted artifacts, and she planed on marching on us next (we were in a secluded refuge for my brother's birthday, which meant that many high officers of the realm were here, including me). So, we scrambled for a solution, and some players actually have the idea of calling her former lover for help : a champion gladiator and former slave. And who's playing this role? The head ST.
                So, we got treated to a scene between the Queen and her paramour, where we could literaly do nothing but watch. In fact, when most of us yelled insults at the queen (understable since, you know, she killed our leaders), the slave got pissed off and berated us for behaving like dumb peasants and interrupting his love talk with her. He even threatened us to strike down the next one who would speak up.
                And of course, I did. After she mocked us one last time, I yelled that she was just a corrupted puppet. And thus, the ST came toward me, swords drawn, and struck me down. I stood my ground, since I'm a mage, I didn't know how to fight in a duel and, oh, I don't know, I thought it felt right to insult my parents' assassin?!
                My friends managed to heal me, with the help of a priest of death and a priest of life, but then the scene was over : basically, it was a cinematic, and we couldn't interact with it. But a few hours later, while I was helping with a ritual, the St came to see me and asked me why I wasn't dead : I explained to him that I got healed quickly, and he got mad, saying that I should have been dead the moment he hit me, and that I did a mistake. However, it was too late, and I was too much involved in the story to correct this, so he just stormed off mad.

                And that's just the most glaring example of this larp's problem : the ST was an attention whore. basically, when he had to play a NPC, we were supposed to stand back and watch as he performed. And his story was planned from the beginning, with no hope of deviating it, we had to endure it. The monsters were WAY too strong, despite the fact that we were supposed to be badass : we were 5 mages, and we supposedly had huge powers that could kill one-shot people at a huge cost. Except that it didn't work on the most basic of orks. So, we were forced to retreat and resist until a convenient squadron of super-rangers led by, you guessed it, the same ST, saved the day. AGAIN.

                Worst part of it? When I went to talk to him afterwards, I couldn't even have the chance to say my feelings. He greeted me with sarcasm, jokingly calling me a cheater for my "stupid death-defying stunt".

                Woaw. It sure feels good to rant!

                "And for my next trick" I said, "anvils!"

                                    A little wisdom I've picked up over the years which I call The Rule of Vader.

                                    The Rule of Vader stipulates that if the GM of a game set in a pre-existing universe includes a big-name villain, the party will drop whatever it is doing and put all of its efforts into killing the bad guy, utterly derailing the plot.

                                    Named after Darth Vader, whose inclusion into your Star Wars game will guarantee that the party will move heaven and earth in an attempt to kill him. If they're smart they'll not run at him brandishing their weapons. If they aren't that smart... well, TPK awaits. And if they do kill him the story that the GM has written will be so utterly derailed a new one needs to be written up.

                                    A variant would be The Rule of Caine, which is when the party cannot actually kill the person in question.

Turned out he made a level 20 character when the rules hadn't even come out yet. The game only had rules up to level 5. Not only did he force us to act a particular way but he bent the lore of the world to glorify his own character and previous characters he'd made, often making NPCs change important political stances to glorify him and then insult us with the opposite view.                                         We revolve around STs to protect against burnout, so it really isn't his fault, but one of the other players enjoys running games but they almost always end in us intentionally/unintentionally getting ourselves killed. He sets up whole plots with defined sequence of events. If we don't know what to do, nothing happens. He gets annoyed when we don't like the NPCs he has scripted into allying with us and it becomes a long slog of poking things until he reveals the exact action we need to progress the plot. It's a grind. One player refused to go to the hospital after getting hurt because that was the one thing he could control. Eventually we try to hide our hit points from him so he will accidentally kill us, and eventually pick on the big bad directly in what we know is a suicidal attempt. It's hard because he is new and the only way to get better is practice, but it's grueling and a slog to deal with.
