                            I have, over the decades, toyed with various ideas of alternate variations and histories for Deadlands. (It's one of those games where I like in theory, but a number of its parts always bugged me to varying degrees. Things like the Civil War lasting for a generation, the South just up and deciding to end slavery - which always felt a little bit like a cop out - and so forth.) At some point, I mostly settled on the idea of moving the catalyst to 1862 and the Battle of Antietam (which was the single bloodiest day of the war). Rather than a single spell caster, I went with the idea of multiple ones around the globe, usually reflective of whatever region they were in. This included your evil Chinese wizard (who has probably been conducting mini rituals throughout the Taiping Rebellion, especially during and after the battle of Shanghai), your evil British druids and/or hellfire club type cult, your evil Egyptian mummy awakened during the early years of Egyptology, Dracula having been kept alive through the black magic of the Scholomance, the New Orleans voodoo bokur whose name I'm blanking on, some sort of Aztec sect in Mexico, a New England witches coven (one in the Hammer Horror film mold), a European mad scientist, and so on. And yes, this probably includes a Navajo skin walker and some other Native villains. So, this sort of weird horror adventure is a global phenomenon that reflects a lot of the tropes of those regions.
                            The Civil War turns into a stalemate, and in 1864, George McClellan with the US Presidency as the Democratic candidate on a peace platform and the war is soon over. Lincoln goes back west, eventually turning into a 19th century version of Daredevil crossed with the Lone Ranger or the Shadow, being a crusading lawyer by day and masked vigilante (and monster hunter) by night. Meanwhile, in the South, a modern day Moses appears, gifted with divine power, and leads almost all the slaves to the new promise land in the West (probably the Cascades region or there about). The Southern economy pretty much collapses, and the British and French offer economic assistance only on the condition of ending what little slavery still exists. Texas eventually succeeds from the CSA, with the idea of establishing its own empire in the west at the expense of Mexico, various Native lands, and eventually California. (This proves harder than expected, even with the technological advances provided by strange artifacts found around Roswell. The Comanche and Apache are especially difficult to "tame", as are Mexican freedom fighters originally organized by the late Benito Juarez. The dinosaurs are also an issue.) The CSA is still struggling, effectively starting to turn in a banana republic (or would that be cotton republic) under British and French business interests, and even efforts to gin up a war of conquest against Cube isn't really helping. A lot of Southerners have gone west. A lot of these were pro-Unionists who wanted to live in US territory, while others are soldiers who feel that they were used in a pointless cause, by landed gentry who have sold their states out to foreign interests. But most of them are just looking for work and a new start. The Union, meanwhile, has embraced industrialization, taking on this weird and at times grimy steampunk look and feel, complete with exploited immigrants and labor movements (as well as suffragettes) contrasted with a gilded age upper class. New Orleans is effectively an independent city state, largely run by its black and mixed race population (who strangely didn't follow the new Moses west).
                            Out West are a number of Native nations, including the Sioux, the Comanche, the Apache, the Navajo, the Sequoyah Confederation (Oklahoma), and others. Actually US control runs from the Midwest through Kansas and most of Colorado before bumping into a now independent Mormon Deseret. After the Great Quake, Baja California and part of Southern California's coast have split off into a series of islands, sometimes referred to as the Great Maze. (This is entirely an excuse to have cowboys fight sea monsters.). San Fransisco and the Bay Area are still intact, and while damaged by the quake are mostly rebuilt, with a massive influx of Chinese and other immigrants. However, California is almost entirely independent as its own Republic, owing to how difficult it is to get help from back east.
                            The British North American Act never passed and the Dominion of Canada doesn't exist. The Maritime areas are still their own thing, as is British Columbia. The Quebec part of the Provence of Canada is growing increasingly rebellious and independence minded, taking some cues from the successful Red River Rebellion that established a separate independent territory. The Cree, Blackfoot, and other First Nations also have their own lands. Alaska is still Russian.
                            Mexico is still the Empire of Mexico under Maximillian, but has been in a constant state of civil war with nationalist republicans since its creation. Incursions by the Republic of Texas only complicate matters, as both sides are technically allied against the invaders, but in practice tend to constantly betray and undermine each other. The Yucatan is an independent region, largely under the control of its native peoples.
                            Elsewhere, the Meiji Restoration continues with an increasingly steam tech feel to it. The Qing Dystasty has mostly reassured control over China after the rebellion, but at the expense of allowing further foreign concessions in exchange for aid. Both Britain and France are adapting ghost-rock steam tech, but no where near as quickly or as totally as Prussia. (Neither the Franco-Prussian War nor German unification has happened; Instead, Prussia is just becoming increasingly militant.)

                            Also I call ghost rock "Unobtainium" or the like, just to give it a scientific name. It also comes in a liquid form, easily processed into lightweight new materials by companies like DuPont, which seems to be fairly common in places like Texas, Sequoyah, Mexico and parts of Pennsylvania.
