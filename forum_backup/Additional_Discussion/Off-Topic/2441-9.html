                    Really, to answer the question of what makes Attack on Titan stand out, what makes it more than the gore and the angst, and what makes the twist fail to undermine the tone of the series is because it's not about gore and angst, nor about fighting Titans. Hell, it's not even about the mysteries.

                    It's children huddled in a refugee camp struggling for food before their families are sent out to die to relieve resource shortages (and then joining the military in order to still have access to food). It's huddling in fear, shaking, praying and cursing before being sent out into battle. It's corruption and division and people putting themselves through the wronger and climbing over the others just so that they can get one level further in. It's the note that, if the second wall is breached, then hiding behind the last one won't matter, because it can't provide the food for anybody.

                    But it's also a young girl being assured that the world hasn't ended despite losing everything by having a scarf wrapped around her head. Of facing something overwhelming and unstoppable with nothing but fierce determination out of the determination to live for the sake of a loved one's memory. It's a throng of terrified rabble holding together in the face of Hell when they're told that falling apart will leave their families exposed to that Hell. Of people throwing themselves into the fray just to create an opening that their one, slim hope can get through at the cost of their own lives, and leaping into the literal jaws of death in order to drag a comrade out even to take their place. And it's a young man who only ever wanted to be exemplary so that he could escape to a life of ease taking up the greatest danger and difficulty in the world because to do anything else would disgrace the memory of a friend.

                    It's a setting, a scenario, and a cast of characters who are in every way worthy of those words in my signature.

                                                Originally posted by Janissary87 View Post
                                                Sooo, anyone catch Knights of Sidonia? Pretty entertaining play on a mecha show, and definitely looks like it would be great inspiration for a Gunstar Campaign. Hmmm?

                                                I've been following the manga for awhile now. It's by Nihei, the same fellow who did Blame! (one of my all time favorite series). Knights of Sidionia is rather fun with some surprising twists. The anime was a very good adaptation, with a few changes or alterations here or there (for the better for the most part). The one issue I have with it is that it's CG. I don't necessarily dislike CG shows, but in this case, I felt that doing it in CG ruins a lot of the atmosphere.

                                                In the manga, Sidonia itself has as much personality as any of the characters, thanks to Nihei's artstyle. He manages to make the place look ancient; it's supposed to have been flying around for hundreds of years. With the CG however, everything looks too crisp and too new. So you lose the sense that humanity is living in this ancient, rusting hulk. It's not a huge problem, but I found myself pining for Nihei's artstyle. I think the animation staff acknowledged this as well; during the commercial breaks they'd have pages from the manga showing off Sidonia's scenery.

                                                On the plus side, it had my favorite opening song in years. I really loved the way they took the oath of the Knights of Sidonia and turned it into the lyrics for the song.
