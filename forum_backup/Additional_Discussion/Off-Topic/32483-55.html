                                        The brain's pretty tricky. There was a problem with contrast sensitivity at night that caused a ton of car accidents, I think, in the 70s and earlier. Basically if you were driving straight down a road at night, and a semi-truck with trailer was ahead of you, perpendicular to you, blocking the entire road, a wildly surprising amount of people wouldn't really perceive it. So they'd just run into the trailer and get their heads smashed off or in. I think it was because most people focus on the area where their lights illuminate, and the darkness beyond that wasn't sufficiently broken by the trailer, so they would just understand it as part of the road ahead until it came into the light and it was, usually, too late to do anything about it.

                                        They added a reflective strip along the bottom, and now I think they've upgraded it to LEDs. They break up the darkness enough to poke your brain into awareness.

                                                            Hello everybody.

                                                            I've been lurking these forums for a long time but this thread is what made me create an account for the first time.

                                                            A couple of years ago I lost my father. He died of cancer and I watched him slowly wither and die. I've lost my mother to cancer too 10 years ago so I was pretty close with my father.
                                                            I was an only child and was always an overprotected kid, teen even adult. He tried to take care everything, all responsibilities were on him and the result was that when he was gone I was, and still am, lost.

                                                            When he was gone I was devastated. The economic situation here in my country is pretty bad and the unemployment rates are high. I was one of the unemployed. Not that I cared at the moment. For the first time in my life I was living alone but that led to getting a cat that I love so much!
                                                            Thankfully.my father had some money in our bank account. Money that started using to buy expensive stuff, and eat junk food everyday. These things were giving me temporary relief.

                                                            After two years of living like this, I almost emptied the reserves of money I had and while I've always been heavyweight, now I'm straight up obese. Thankfully I've found a job but its really stressful and the payment is not good enough to be able to live decently but it's so really hard to find a job nowadays that I don't have the luxury to leave.

                                                            During all that time, my only real escape was pnp RPGs. I'm socially awkward, terrible with other people and social situations but I'm blessed with a handful or really good friends. All the years that I've been playing RPGs I played exclusively with them, and the last few years were no exception.

                                                            The problem now is that they started scattering all around, moving on with their lives. Some have already left for other towns or even countries for studies or work and the rest of them are planning to. We've stopped playing RPGs now and that hurts a lot. My social awkwardness makes it so hard to me to come close to new people at work and while I was always a recluse I'm so afraid of being all alone.

                                                            I think that I'm depressed, but I really can't afford a therapist right now, but even when I could afford one, I didn't bother.

                                                            Well, that's my situation, comparing to others here it sounds small but getting it of my chest for the first time feels good.
