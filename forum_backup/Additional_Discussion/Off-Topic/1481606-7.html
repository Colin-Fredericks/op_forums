    So, I thought Lisa Frankenstein was pretty good! Wasn't really sure what to expect (I don't think I'd even seen a trailer), but it was pretty fun for the most part. It has some nice horror movie nods too.

    The story is fairly simple too, and follows a highschool girl who spends a lot of her time in a cemetery, and after being drugged, wishes to be with a Victorian man (whose grave she spends a lot of time at) and his reanimated and seeks her out. As they become friends, the killing begins to restore him to more of his former self...

    The horror elements are pretty good and it has some great humor too.

    Anyway, it was pretty good and I'm glad I got to see it.

                                                        Could be that it's one of those where someone came up with an original movie and the movie-studio was like "Only way we're gonna put this into production is if it's tied to an existing IP." and so the original script was rewritten to now be a Crow remake.

                                                        Also, two other things stood out to me. The first one is that I wonder how well the action plays if the bit they're repeatedly doing in those action-scenes is that the protagonist literally cannot die. Not a lot of suspense left when the bad guys come after him with guns, knives and whatnot while the audience is fully aware that this will be completely useless against the undead guy. The second thing is it's a bit surprising how many shots we saw of the dead wife in the trailer. Neither the original Crow nor John Wick really delve that much into the dead wife character. That's of course because the movie is about the revenge so the dead wife is more a symbol than a character. It's more about what she represents than who she is. There was this moment of realization when I and my friends were going to watch John Wick 4 and I asked the round what John Wick's dead wife's name is and nobody knew (me included). But here the trailer hints at this movie having this whole sub-plot of her meeting the protagonist, falling in love and then her also stumbling upon the bad guy doing bad guy stuff. But I just can't picture that working. The inciting incident is the protagonist and his wife being murdered and the movie is about him taking revenge. And if they do it as flashbacks somehow, it comes off like just more exposition to explain what's going on. Even though the murder-part is more than enough to justify the revenge-part.

                                                        And this trailer also made me think it's one of those where you pretty much get the whole movie already. What are the odds all the sequences in the opera house will be the literal finale of the movie? Yeah, not a good trailer... at all.
