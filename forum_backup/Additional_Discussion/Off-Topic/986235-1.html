                Bringing up Japanese media stuff is an interesting part of the equation. For cultural reasons, very few anime, manga, etc. are explicitly Christian. While this most frequently leads to a lot of widely inaccurate portrayals of Christianity, a lot of the material still paints Christianity in the positive. Though reactions can vary widely since it can often come off as very superficial (that is the trappings of Christianity are shown in a positive light but there is no depth towards Christian beliefs), and indulging in the exotic.

                There's a good number of works that deal with the early period of Christianity in Japan (ex: Rurouni Kenshin and Samurai Champloo), where Japanese Christians aren't the protagonists, but are shown in a highly sympathetic light due to the incredibly harsh persecutions of them at the time. Kenshin is an interesting example since the main character's story is extremely similar to many stories of Christian converts struggling as they attempt to reject their former lives and find solace in teachings that promote peace, harmony, and tolerance.

                There are explicit subtext parables as well. The most well known is probably Trigun. While most people tend to focus on Wolfwood and his not-really-Christian thing, Vash himself is very much supposed to promote Christian ideals by being a technical pacifist, promoting peace and love, and constantly willing to suffer for the sake of others.

                And we have examples of completely unsubtle things like Blue Exorcist. It suffers a lot from the "getting Christianity completely wrong" thing, But at the same time, the Church in the story manages to pull off one of the greatest conversion/redemption feats imaginable in Christian theology: getting the explicit antichrist to choose working for the Church to protect the world from his father instead of helping his father defeat good once and for all. "Even the antichrist can find the light of Jesus by being shown true loving kindness," is a pretty pro-Christian message to me.

                    Originally posted by dxanders View Post
                    I'd say, even if he isn't explicitly stated as such, Peter Parker (Spider-Man) tends to be a good Protestant boy.
                    This is pretty common in a lot of comic book superheroes even as comics expand to include things that make holding on to real -world belief systems hard.

                    Matthew Murdock/Daredevil is a practicing Catholic and his faith is an essential part of his moral code. Steve Rodgers/Captain America, and Clark Kent/Superman are both similar to Peter Parker as being not terribly religious but holding strongly to mainstream US Protestant morality. Kurt Wagner/Nightcrawler is another character frequently portrayed as being driven towards heroics in part due to being Catholic. John Constantine is a shitty Christian, but a lot of Hellblazer's supporting cast include positive Christian characters that try to get John to at least be a bit less of a dick.

                    Heck even Spawn, which is one of the number of comics from the 90s/00s that heavily indulges in heavily subverting Christianity and painting Heaven and Hell as equally bad forces, has the most moral and positive character be Spawn's ex-mother-in-law, a devout Christian (I don't think they specify what sect). For at least the first 1/3 of the comics she's the only character that can get through to Spawn and get him to walk a better path that Hell has in plan for him, because she's pretty much the only character that has any faith that there's still good in him.
