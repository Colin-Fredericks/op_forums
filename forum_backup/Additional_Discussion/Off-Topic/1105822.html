    An Odd Request: What's your roleplaying story?

    06-20-2017, 04:13 PM
    Hey all. I’m doing an ethnography project for a summer class. Since one of the criteria was to conduct my research within an online community that I am involved with, I figured these forums would be an interesting place to start. I’m also just curious to hear people’s stories and generally just get more involved in this community. Keep in mind that these will be used in a project for college, so your posts may be quoted, though I do not intend to publish my findings (and if that changes I will contact everyone to make sure it’s ok). All that being said if you don’t want to post a response in this thread, for whatever reason, you can also contact me through the PMs. Any responses would be greatly appreciated. At the moment I do not really have a focus for my research, aside from this subset of the tabletop roleplaying community, and mostly just have a few general questions.


    How did you get into this hobby?
    What attracted you to it?
    What attracted you to Onyx Path/White Wolf products specifically?
    What are some memorable experiences you have had in this hobby, this forum, or this particular part of the roleplaying community?
    What do you think separates this community from other ones as far as the overall culture and attitude?
    What are the best things about this hobby and this community?


    For me VTM was one of the first RPGs I ever owned or played. I was raised in a pretty conservative family, most of whom still held onto the whole "satanic panic" notion that things like Harry Potter and Dungeons and Dragons were evil well into the early 2000s. So naturally when I was old enough to question authority I dived headlong into most of it, sneaking what I could past them. I ended up picking up a copy of VTM second edition at a half price bookstore when I was about 13 because the art looked cool and the opening fiction was cool. When I found out it was a game I immediately was intrigued by it and wanted to give it a try, so I gathered up some friends (most of whom I had met at school or at church) and we decided to give it a shot. Most of those friends are still part of my gaming group. Even as many of my ridiculous teenage attempts at edginess fell away, VTM and other games stayed and I started to appreciate them more for their actual value as entertainment. My games became more nuanced and interesting and I found out I had a love of writing and storytelling that pushed me into writing fiction. This community and these games mean a lot to me because they mark a big change for me where I started to think for myself and make friends based on what I actually liked rather than what I thought I was supposed to like. For me that's what makes this community good, that there's an abundance of creativity, from fan generated content to awesome chronicles, and it's just interesting to see.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          How did you get into this hobby?
          One summer in my youth, I was with my grandmother at a Waldenbooks store in a mall. My attention was caught by this book on the shelf with an image of a Conan-esque figure bursting through a door (the AD&D 2nd edition revised players handbook, to be specific), and when my grandmother asked if I wanted anything I picked that up. Reading it told me it was a game and more parts were required, so I talked my grandmother into taking me back to the store the next day, and ran my very first session that second evening.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          What attracted you to it?
          Before learning about the D&D game, I was a big fan of adventure and role-playing video games like Final Fantasy and Legend of Zelda, novels like The Hobbit, and films like Conan the Barbarian and Wizards. Being able to take the thoughts inspired by those and channel them into even more entertainment was a huge draw.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          What attracted you to Onyx Path/White Wolf products specifically?
          I'd managed to never actually hear about White Wolf until I'd been playing other games for nearly a decade. What brought me on board was a pair of co-workers at a job I'd just started finding out I was into table-top gaming and asking if I wanted to play Vampire with them. I like trying new things, have always enjoyed vampire-centric forms of entertainment, and new from playing Call of Cthulhu for years that horror role-playing can be awesome fun, so I said yes. I was hooked in one session.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          What are some memorable experiences you have had in this hobby, this forum, or this particular part of the roleplaying community?
          Perhaps because I am tired at the moment, I am unable to form words to share any single memorable experience. This forum (and the forum its regular users migrated from) has more left me with feelings than specific memories, because the people here are (in general, at least) open, inviting, welcoming, and understanding.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          What do you think separates this community from other ones as far as the overall culture and attitude?
          The higher volume of folks here who have not only experienced the harshness of others, but are are also not blindly continuing the cycle. Geek culture in general has plenty of folks that have been ostracized and treated poorly for being different, but many of those people go on to treat others in the same poor ways without realizing what they are doing.

          Originally posted by cobweb View Post
          What are the best things about this hobby and this community?
          Too many good things to choose a single thing to declare the best, but being able to see the views of such a diverse crowd is definitely in the running.

          Not so noble anymore.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  How did you get into this hobby?
                  I had a vague cultural awareness that Dungeons and Dragons was a thing that existed from as early as 3rd or 4th grade. One of my friends had an older brother who played D&D with his friends. My friend and I always wanted to get into it, but they insisted that it was too complicated for us (really I think they just didn't want us younger kids hanging out with them). We did our best to kind of imitate things we picked up from them in our own games of pretend. It wasn't until 8th grade that I met someone my own age who had played RPGs before. She and I played a couple of one-on-one games, starting with a fantasy d6 game before moving on to D&D, and later to Call of Cthulu. We got a couple of our other friends involved as well, but it was mostly small group one-offs. My senior year of high school I got involved in my first long-running D&D games. It was such a positive experience I continued to seek out similar experiences, which is how I got involved in the Requiem LARP scene in college.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  What attracted you to it?
                  I've always been a big fan of roleplaying. As a young kid I would of course play pretend with my friends all the time. As I got older I started getting involved in theater and improv. RPGs were just one more way to indulge in roleplaying and improvisational storytelling, but they gameified it in a way that freeform roleplaying never could.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  What attracted you to Onyx Path/White Wolf products specifically?
                  I first became aware of World of Darkness in high school, when I would play Magic the Gathering at my local gaming store on Fridays. They had a lot of Chronicles of Darkness (or New World of Darkness as it was called at the time) books on the shelves, and as an emo kid who liked roleplaying games, they seemed like the perfect confluence of my interests at the time. Of course, I didn't have any money to spend on them, so they were always this cool and mysterious thing just out of reach. When I went away to college I was of course actively looking for other roleplayers, and I quickly discovered the local Requiem LARP scene. I came for the vampires, but I stayed for the community. Actually, after my first night observing a Vampire LARP I very nearly decided the whole LARP thing wasn't for me, but I happened to meet a guy there who invited me to another game with some of his other friends. I'm glad I took him up on it, because I met some of my best friends of my college days there, and found my love for Chronicles of Darkness.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  What are some memorable experiences you have had in this hobby, this forum, or this particular part of the roleplaying community?
                  It's hard to pick one specific thing, as Requiem LARP was kind of my life for about four years. I spent my weekends playing Vampire, I spent all of my free time that I wasn't actively playing thinking about the game, I got involved with the broader WoD/CofD community, all of my friends were my fellow LARPers, and when I made friends who weren't, I'd invite them to our weekend game and then the would be.

                  One story I think is worth sharing though is that I did take my gender identity for a test drive through roleplaying. I'm a feminine-leaning genderfluid person, and when I was beginning to come to terms with this, I decided to try playing a transgender character in our vampire LARP. It gave me a chance to behave as a person more confident in her identity as a trans person than I was in my own at the time, which helped me develop my own confidence (fake it till you make it and all that). It also gave me a chance to deal with predjudice on the basis of that identity in a safe way - there was a degree of separation between myself and my character, and I knew that the predjudice I was dealing with was in-character, and that the players portraying those characters were my close friends, who I knew would support me when I was ready to come out.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  What do you think separates this community from other ones as far as the overall culture and attitude?
                  This community as in the RPG community in general, or the White Wolf/Onyx Path community specifically? The Chronicles of Darkness community has been one of the most positive, healthy online communities I have experienced. The RPG community in general can be a mixed bag, but if there's one thing that sets them apart from other communities, it's a willingness to lose themselves in play. So many adults are closed to the idea of playing pretend, but the RPG community embraces it wholeheartedly, and that's something I think is extremely valuable.

                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                  What are the best things about this hobby and this community?
                  I think the best thing about the hobby is, as mentioned above, it gives adults permission to play. Ultimately all of the rules and structures are just there to make us insecure grownups feel more comfortable using our imaginations. And at our best, roleplayers can have an unparalleled capacity for empathy, specifically because we spend our leisure time willingly putting ourselves in other people's shoes.

                      Originally posted by cobweb View Post

                      How did you get into this hobby?
                      Reluctantly!

                      I have a friend from university who, given enough time, can basically make you do anything, and he wanted me to be in his Sabbat VtM game. Before that, I'd played half a session of Requiem (same friend), and some D&D I only vaguely remember from when I was 11. I owned a lot of RPG books as a kid, but most of the ones I had were too complicated for me to figure out, and I didn't have enough friends interested in playing, so mostly I read them for fun. I'd lost interest in RPGs by the time my friend finagled me into his game, but I pretty quickly got into it.

                      What attracted you to it?
                      I've done theatre and writing almost all my life, so it was a natural fit.

                      What attracted you to Onyx Path/White Wolf products specifically?
                      I like vampires.

                      But actually I'm very attuned to the "personal horror in a modern setting" mood that permeates the WoD brands. I'm also not a huge fan of sword & sorcery style RPGs, and I get hives from any game that requires me to do more than basic arithmetic. I'm actually less into RPGs and more into WoD/CofD specifically, come to think of it.

                      What are some memorable experiences you have had in this hobby, this forum, or this particular part of the roleplaying community?
                      One of the most fun was when I was in a Mage game where we ended up in a Groundhog Day scenario. My character managed to extend the problem for at least two more cycles because he dramatically failed opening a door with magic.

                      The one Demon: The Descent game I've run is probably my favourite thing I've ever done as an ST. The characters and story really came together perfectly; I've never quite been able to recapture it.

                      What do you think separates this community from other ones as far as the overall culture and attitude?
                      We have our ups and downs, but the OPP forums are significantly more positive and less poisonous than a lot of forums I could name.

                      What are the best things about this hobby and this community?
                      A bunch of people coming together to be creative and to have fun. It's hard to beat.

                                  Necro!

                                  Originally posted by cobweb View Post
                                  How did you get into this hobby?
                                  What attracted you to it?
                                  What attracted you to Onyx Path/White Wolf products specifically?
                                  What are some memorable experiences you have had in this hobby, this forum, or this particular part of the roleplaying community?
                                  What do you think separates this community from other ones as far as the overall culture and attitude?
                                  What are the best things about this hobby and this community?
                                  When I was a kid, I was a fan of both Spider-Man & His Amazing Friends and the Dungeons & Dragons cartoons. My older cousin had some of the AD&D sourcebooks, and I would read the Monster Manual and Fiends Folio. But what especially got my attention was the Legends & Lore book (the one with the painting of Odin on the cover) with all the gods and pantheons. He would also have issues of Dragon Magazine, which at the time would include articles for the Marvel Superhero RPG. I think the first actual RPG I played though was TSR's Star Frontiers. A few years later, I came across Palladium's TMNT RPG, which eventually led me to Rifts and their other stuff.
                                  As for White Wolf in particular, When I was, I think a sophomore in high school, I saw a copy of the first edition Brujah Clanbook for Vampire, and while attracted to the Bradstreet cover, what really hooked me were the pre made character templates in the back. My reaction was, "you can play that? Cool!" Soon after, I picked up the 2nd ed core book and Brujah, and a little later, the first edition of Werewolf. (One of the first horror films I ever saw was Frankenstein Meets the Wolfman, and have had an enduring fondness for both types ever since. I think the second was probably Blacula, which has always influenced some of my view of the genre. But what really turned me into a fan of horror and dark fantasy was the Friday the 13th TV series, with its intersection of Satanism, historical curiosities and tragic character flaws.) I think those initial Brujah templates are what always made me such a big booster of their use in White Wolf's various splatbooks, and why I was so disappointed to see them go away with the new incarnation.
                                  I think the most memorable experience has been the diversity of folks I would meet. This includes a number of people who worked in the industry. I got to meet Geoff Grabowski, Bruce Baugh, Clayton Oliver and Richard Danksy at different conventions, all of whom were cool people. (I think Richard Dansky may be the single nicest human being on the planet.) I also got to make some written contributions to White Wolf, which is not something I really advertise as I don't consider them all that important.
                                  I don't think there's really that much that separates gamers from other fan-based communities. All have their assorted cliques, factions, cool people, asshats, innovators, orthodoxies and drama.
                                  I think the best things about the hobby are that it's always given people who would otherwise be misfits, outcasts and square pegs (such as myself) a place to find others like them and make friends, as well as an outlet for creativity and imagination.

                                  What is tolerance? It is the consequence of humanity. We are all formed of frailty and error; let us pardon reciprocally each other's folly. That is the first law of nature.
                                  Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)
