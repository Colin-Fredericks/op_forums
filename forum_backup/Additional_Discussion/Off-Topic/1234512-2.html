    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Many would redirect their conflicted feelings at the showrunners for “pandering to the SJWs” or “trying to push a feminist agenda.”
    Honestly, pandering is exactly what I think is going on. Last time I had my Prime subscription active, I finally got caught up, mostly to see what all the fuss was about and whether the criticisms of season 10 were truly justified.

    I'll be up front about one major bias I had. I love the fuck out of Peter Capaldi, the problems with his tenure aside he's my favorite Doctor since Tom Baker, and I felt Moffat should have departed the show with Smith. I don't take the show as seriously as most, but on the other side Moffat's refusal to step down despite declining ratings, quality, critical reception, and fan goodwill, left me with a bad taste in my mouth after season 9.

    Michelle Gomez was fucking amazing as Missy, though. I was initially angry they made her an incarnation of the Master, rather than the Rani who I felt absolutely deserves a place on the show, but her screen presence and chemistry with Capaldi made me a very quick convert. Still pissed they haven't brought back Romana, though.

    Anyhow. The thing was, before I started season 10 I specifically watched season 3. I'm a fan of Tenant but strongly dislike the Tenth Doctor, and I really like Martha Jones, but the reason I chose season 3 was because I felt it had the strongest social commentary of any nu-Who season I'd watched to that point. It's not a terribly popular opinion, but I felt season 3 was by far the strongest of RTD's tenure even though "The Sound of Drums" and "Last of the Time Lords" were hot garbage.

    I also got side tracked and re-watched the first two seasons of Torchwood, but that's neither here nor there.

    The differences between seasons 3 and 10 were as clear as night and day. The commentary in season 3 is unmistakable and thought-provoking, and the storytelling is confident, nuanced, and succinct. The biggest, most identifiable, contrast to me was how Martha and Bill were handled in "Human Nature/Family of Blood" and "Thin Ice", respectively. In "Human Nature/Family of Blood", the writers showed Martha as subject of institutional prejudice, the indignity of her cover identity, and the banality of the people around her. By putting a human face on institutional classism, racism, and sexism, the writers challenged the audience to consider and confront casual prejudice and systemic bias in a meaningful and productive way. Not to be flippant about it, but the best "Thin Ice" had to offer was throwaway lines about how racism and sexism are bad, the capstone of which being the presentation of a moustache-twirling straw man from whom the audience could all too easily dissociate.

    That last point is the important one.

    That was pretty much the general trend with season 10. The storytelling and commentary was neither nuanced, subtle, clever, or at all thought-provoking. Frankly, it came off as a parade of chauvinist 21st Century morality plays. Based on the buzz about Chibnall, the season 11 cast, and the writers' room, I have no reason to believe the upcoming season will be anything but more of the same, and I don't have time or patience for that shit. If I hear otherwise once the show starts airing I'll be happy to give it a shot, but at this point for me personally the burden is on the showrunners to show improvement first.

    [EDIT: Oh yeah, Jodie Whittaker as Thirteen. Forgot all about that. Totally okay with it, I just hope she's given scripts worth a shit. I'm more offended by that fugly-ass Mork from Ork cosplay they had her in, what happened to that badass grey hooded peacoat?]

                Maybe you're upset by the fact literally every other regeneration of the Doctor since the series began has been male, and the idea that Regeneration could alter somebody's gender has never been referenced?

                This isn't about being sexually biased, that's complete bullshit. This is about taking a well established series that the fans have loved for many years, and changing it up just to appeal to a wider demographic.

                I mean, the Doctor has said multiple times that when it comes to Regeneration, you never know what species you'll look like (I still believe that his final regeneration will be a ginger without legs. As a sort of inside joke to the fans), but he's never suggested that becoming the opposite sex was a possibility.
                He never mentioned he could 'be renewed' before 1966, never mentioned he was an alien and a Time Lord before 1969, the original limit of thirteen lives wasn't established until 1976,that he could change species wasn't established until 1979 with Romana's regeneration, 16 years in...

                Then there's the rewrite of Dalek history in Genesis of the Daleks, the Time Lords' overhaul in Deadly Assassin....

                This is what Doctor Who does. It makes itself up as it goes along.

                (As to the history of gender-changing regenerations: in End of Time, Eleven thinks he's become a girl, he references the Corsair and their gender-changing regenerations in The Doctor's Wife, we have our first gender-changing regeneration in Series 8, and our first on-screen gender-changing regeneration in Series 9, so at this point, it's been part of the show's idea-space for over 8 years. The idea of a female Doctor has a longer history externally - Tom Baker jokingly suggested his successor might be a woman back when he left, and it's been floating around fandom ever since, including at least one well-received series of fan films, but it didn't get taken seriously until Moffat's era.)
