                            So, according to a reporter from Variety, Scarlet Johansson accepted the role of Major Motoko Kusanagi on an adaptation of Ghost in the Shell by Dreamworks.

                            I like her, but I wish that the studio was willing to take a change on a Nipponic actress (Rinko Kikuchi already looked a lot like Motoko in Pacific Rim, for instance). Still, if they must have someone with enough star power to get butts on seats (because Ghost in the Shell may be well known among Anime fans, but that is not exactly enough to make Hollywood-grade returns on a movie, I suspect) and who can do well on an action/espionage/sci-fi movie, I'm sure she can do it. Still, the movie will likely get some bad press for the whitewashing, so they better hope Scarlet Johansson's and whoever else is in the main roles can bring enough people to compensate for that.

                            It is a vicious circle of not having sufficiently famous Asian actors for leading movies (Jet Li, Jackie Chan, Ming-Na Wen, Lucy Liu and so on are getting pretty old for action movies) and not having Asian actors in movies making them never sufficiently famous to lead movies, though.

                                            I know I'm WAY behind on this one, and maybe it was just me who felt like this while watching it, but did anyone else expect Spike Jonze's Where the Wild Things Are to be that uncomfortable to watch?

                                            Not because it's a badly made/acted/written film. Just the opposite. It is so damn accurate to how a lonely boy from a broken home would feel and react it was making me squirm while watching it. The monster were so nonchalant about eating him and tearing down everything as to be way scarier than they seemed in the book. And their personalities: the reflections of the angry little boy who acts out, the insecure little boy who isn't sure what to do and the other one who never gets listened to, the abusive female (his sister? his mother?) who berates him and tears apart everything he tries to build, the other one who's trying to be nice to him but makes no sense to the others and just seems to agitate everything, the single voice of reason in the middle of everything else trying to keep peace and quite the angry one.

                                            I'm glad I watched it before letting the Baby Bears see it. I don't know if I'd want to let them watch it until they're older and can understand some of what's going on better.
