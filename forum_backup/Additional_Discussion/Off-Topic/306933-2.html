    Only real experience I've had with "pushy" religious people was one guy who belonged to a particularly evangelical church in my city and worked with the Red Frog Crew (for those that don't know, they're a group of [mostly] Christians who you can ask to show up to events who provide free water and red lolly frogs to people, the idea being to act as positive role models by not drinking, as well as general support and education. Vast majority are incredibly cool people, and they never push the religious angle) at a party my college threw. Got to talking to him, and he convinced me to come to one of his churches services. I was very clear that I was not a Christian, and much though I respect the faith, I do not share it, but was willing to come along as an educational experience. Also because he wouldn't leave me alone about it; I have my suspicions about why, but they're not relevant here.

    Anyway, I went along, and, well, it was an experience. It was one of those 'musical' churches, the ones that try to stimulate a sense of... well, religious ecstasy is too strong a term, but I can't think of a better one. All the way through the initial conversation, to the trip there, even his behaviour while there, it was clear he expected me to fall over and convert on the spot, which of course I did not. Even on the trip back (I didn't have my license at the time, so he drove me), he was still trying, even though I pointed out that even if I did agree with his perspective, faith isn't something you can easily choose or turn on or off.

    On the scale of "pushy religious people" he's pretty mild, I know. But I've known a lot of Jerkoff AtheistsTM, so naturally I find they annoy me more, simply due to me being exposed to them more. In comparison, most religious people I've known have been very cool people, so naturally the distribution of cool to jerk is kind of skewed towards that end of the spectrum. But I've known some cool atheists as well.

                                            Gnostic Atheist - I firmly believe that there is nothing to believe in.

                                            I was raised Orthodox Christian, but at around 7 it became absolutely, undeniably clear to me that when I die, there will be nothing left of me - no empty void, no recognition of there being nothing, I'll just completely cease to exist. And I'm fine with that.

                                            Since then I've had a few other realizations about patterns and the nature of the universe, and it all reinforces my one core belief: we are the universe trying to figure itself out. We don't just live in it, we are it. Not in a "spiritual new-age The Secret the-universe-listens-to-you" kind of way, just in a matter-of-fact "our atoms are the universe's atoms and there is no real distinction" sort of way. That we think, that we move, that we recognize and produce patterns, that we build and transport resources from point A to point B as firing neurons or vascular systems do, like some sort of ... God-Machine (pun very much intended). All those things are the cosmos, as well as being galaxies, black holes and supernovae. That we walk around thinking we aren't part of a greater system is akin to skin-cells or brain-cells not understanding that they live in a body, and that they are that body despite their own perceived individuality.

                                            People usually try to argue with "But we're more than the sum of our parts!" to which I can't help but answer "then you don't understand how they fit together". The Cosmos is more interesting, complicated and fascinating than any magic we try to layer over it.

                                            Despite the humor, that actually is my current working theory.
