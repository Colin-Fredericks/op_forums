                                                Personally, there are quit a number of aspects of 4e which I like, especially from a fluff point of view. I loved how they detached the "bloodline=race" kind of thing, presenting the Tiefling and their like in a much fresher way, and also the addition of things like the Primordials and the Dawn War and the new gods which joined the "d&d pantheon". I also liked how some of classes were presented, with the different Warlock Pacts (Star Pact especially) and Sorcerer magical origin, the addition of Primal Classes and some aspects of the new presentation of psionics, not to mention some of the new races and classes which were added (the Invoker, for example). However, I really disliked some of the core mechanics. The way that everyone got Powers and some special feature while Wizards simply got "some better control" over their powers practically made me lose interest in playing my favorite class, which by its own was enough to make me lose interest in the system, but it was still deeper than that. Every time I looked at the rules, I felt as if I was meant to make a computer game character. The move from the "traditional art style" to one which felt more "digital", with glowing weapons and their like, also ruined the experience to me- but I assume it is more of a personal taste kind of thing, not to mention all the mess which happened when WotC practically killed Dragonlance, which was my favorite setting.

                                                In short, I do think that 4e had some good changes and concepts in it, but it also did some thing which I didn't liked, and that- together with some outside effects (the art change, DL), got me to dislike the edition in general. Still, I did once made an extensive thread about converting concepts from 4e to Dragonlance, and I wouldn't have done so if I were to find no interest in the system.

                                                    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                    Personally, there are quit a number of aspects of 4e which I like, especially from a fluff point of view. (...) However, I really disliked some of the core mechanics. The way that everyone got Powers and some special feature while Wizards simply got "some better control" over their powers practically made me lose interest in playing my favorite class, which by its own was enough to make me lose interest in the system,
                                                    So, I’m curious about this. This was a fairly common grievance folks had with 4e (“everyone’s a spellcaster” and all that.) but I’ve never understood it. What is it about Fighters having pre-packaged, exceptions-based maneuvers that they can perform that diminishes the fun of playing a Wizard? I can’t imagine your fun playing a spellcaster hinges on being the only member of the party with self-contained packets of mechanics they can pick from, so it’s got to be something else. But I’m not seeing what it is.

                                                    Also, to clarify, Wizards in 4e didn’t have more control over their Powers, they were “controllers”, meaning that their Powers were geared towards battlefield control. More AoEs, stuns, forced movement effects, and zone control related abilities, as opposed to a Striker, who’s Powers were more gears towards high single target damage, a Leader, who’s Powers were more geared towards party buffing and healing, or a Defender, who’s Powers were more geared towards drawing aggro and punishing enemies for going after other party members.

                                                    Which, I gather felt too videogamey for a lot of people. Personally, I loved that because it actually made characters feel different in play. Instead of the issue where anyone who isn’t a spellcaster is limited to “Attack the most threatening enemy with my weapon” turn after turn, everyone had a distinct role in combat and abilities that helped you fulfill that role in different ways. But I understand this is a taste thing, and lots of people don’t like the tank/heal/dps/cc combat style.

                                                    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                    but it was still deeper than that. Every time I looked at the rules, I felt as if I was meant to make a computer game character. The move from the "traditional art style" to one which felt more "digital", with glowing weapons and their like, also ruined the experience to me- but I assume it is more of a personal taste kind of thing
                                                    Yeah, I definitely didn’t care for the art style either, but for me that’s a pretty minor issue. I think one of the things that contributed to the videogamey feel was the Power format, which I personally loved. They were so well-organized and codified, it made it really easy to understand at a glance exactly what a Power did, and eliminated a lot of parsing confusion that 5e unfortunately brought back.

                                                    Also, the uniformity of Power progression seemed to be one of those maligned “videogamey” elements. Every Nth-level character has X At-Will Attack Powers, Y Encounter Attack Powers, Z Daily Attack Powers, and R Utility Powers (which could be At-Will, Encounter, or Daily) regardless of Class, which was great for balance, but ultimately really boring, even for me as a huge 4e fan. Fortunately, the Essentials line finally broke free of this uniform structure, but unfortunately the damage had been done by that point. The folks who didn’t like 4e had already stopped paying attention to 4e products for two years by the time Essentials came out, and the abysmal decision to market it as 4e lite instead of 4.5e (which it effectively was) really bit them in the ass. I assume it was probably sold that way because 3.5 was itself really bad PR, and they misinterpreted the critique against 4e as “too complex” instead of “too dissimilar from previous editions.”

                                                    I’d love to see the alternate universe where Essentials had been sold as 4.5, they hadn’t worried as much about trying to preserve back-compatibility with 4.0, and they had really pushed the “we fixed 4e” angle instead of the “this will be easier to learn” angle. Maybe it would have won back enough folks to have kept the 4e train rolling a few more years, or at least salvaged more of 4e’s ideas in the eyes of enough of the community for them to have made it into 5e.
