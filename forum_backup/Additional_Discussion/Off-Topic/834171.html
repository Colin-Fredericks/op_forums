    Realism and Stylism in WoD and CofD

    02-20-2016, 09:22 PM
    So recently in another thread, a point was made that some fans find certain aspects of WoD preferable to CofD because they are more realistic. This struck me as odd because that's one of the big reasons I and a lot of people I game with prefer CofD over WoD. I had always perceived WoD's appeal being based on its stylized alternate universe. The gothic punk aesthetic, the 90's attitude, the global supernatural conspiracies and epic, world-spanning meta-plots. Conversely, CofD's appeal, in my view, was based on its more subdued approach. The universe more closely resembles real life, the monsters hide in plain sight, the scale is (generally speaking) more personal rather than epic. I always saw WoD as a more developed alternate universe of its own, while CofD was more of a "what if there were monsters in our universe?" So this got me thinking about the role of realism and stylism in WoD and CofD - clearly both have a place in both settings, but they are utilized differently, and I am interested to read others' thoughts on the matter.

    Now, before we go any further, this discussion needs a disclaimer in a big way. This kind of discussion comparing WoD and CofD and peoples preferences for each has a tendency to lead to some serious flame wars. I'm aware that bringing up this topic is playing with fire, and I'm going to be keeping a close watch on this thread. If it starts to devolve into "edition" warring (for lack of a better term, since they're not actually editions of the same game, they're different IPs with similar subject matter), I won't hesitate to close it. Ways you can help avoid this are by phrasing things in clear terms of personal preference. "I like this about WoD because..." is fine, as is "I didn't like this about CofD because..." but "WoD sucks because..." or "CofD is better because..." are not. Respect the games and respect other people's preferences, and I think we will be able to have a mature discussion about setting elements in these two games.

                                  I have covered most of my initial impressions on why WoD is more realistic than CofD in the interview thread. One more thing I would like to add is CofD's plot hooks. Boy, oh boy, this one's a backfiring paradox. Keep in mind that I noticed this when comparing CofD to V20 (might not be the case in WoD revised etc.), and also that I started with Requiem back in 2004 with no WoD experience whatsoever (so I am biased in that I've had too much of Requiem, so VtM is the exotic, shiny new thing to me).

                                  So, in CofD, your characters are most likely the focus of the entire chronicle. NPCs are bland, the tools allow for very powerful and interesting characters (both PCs and NPCs), and there's just so much potential unhindered by the overwhelming environment of WoD. Again, this was such a great idea it's a shame it doesn't work in practice. When Beckett's your Status 5 Cainite, you better be ready to up your roleplaying and make sure your character does something extraordinary at an already extraordinary and epic conflict that is Jyhad. Unfortunately, my experience with both LARP and tabletop Requiem means there's a lot of complacency. Average stories, about average characters, who just like it that way. Tell a bunch of players there's a Strix on the loose, chances are they'll pronounce it wrong (true story). Tell them that Caine, the mythical progenitor of all Cainites, is rumored to have awakened, and they'll type Book of Nod in drivethrurpg's online store faster than a Brujah with 5 dots of Celerity.

                                  So, the plot hooks are meant to fill in the blanks, give characters side quests in what seems to be one long side quest. Unfortunately, it takes a lot of effort on the ST's part (and more so for the players) to make the most out of Requiem. Which is such a pity I feel like we've failed the game (not vice versa). All I have is anecdotal, of course, but after taking the difficult decision of switching my ancient Athens chronicle to Masquerade, my suspicions were proven correct, at least for my troupe. I had been begging them to read the Requiem corebook, or at least their respective clan sections for 6 months. Only when I mentioned Masquerade and Gehenna, and explained what they were, did my players put some effort into it. They felt scared of the setting, and that they needed to prepare. Makes my job as ST easier... *insert evil laugh*

                                                      The big difference to me between the two is there's a sense of a "firewall" between the supernatural and the mundane in WoD that doesn't seem to be there in CoD. CoD kinda feels like one way you could explain how our world could be so awful... because there are literal monsters out there causing bad things sometimes. Sure, sometimes it's people... but sometimes we tell ourselves they're ALL people because the truth is way worse. Because of this, every person matters. Every person is special and priceless if they die or are lost.

                                                      By comparison, like... there are a bunch of world-spanning conspiracies in WoD, but it feels like they're a dime a dozen, and at the end of the day... this one negates that one and the overall results are null. People, including PCs, are mostly worthless and meaningless. It's not Lovecraftian, but it has a lot of the sense of pointlessness... in the end, no matter how mighty a mage or vampire you are... you're meaningless. All will crumble and fall, and it will happen sooner than later.

                                                      To me that's the difference... the sense of EVERYTHING mattering to NOTHING mattering. And based on people who I knew who played, that was the reason they liked WoD... because they could play the worst most horrible characters and it didn't matter... maybe it says something about the WoD fans I've known, but...
