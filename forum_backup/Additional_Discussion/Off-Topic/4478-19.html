        Originally posted by JimB View Post
        The best character I ever made for an RPG is an underprivileged but basically good kid who accidentally summoned a demon and found his soul intertwined with it, his goodness tamped down by its viciousness and selfishness while its evil was tempered by his compassion. His defining characteristic was his struggle to be a good person while still full of primal and violent impulses instilled by an immortal spirit. His primary conflict is internal, trying to discover who he is and to either change it or to make peace with it.

        I was thinking, though, why couldn't I flip the roles? What if a basically evil person accidentally summoned an angel was improved by the relationship even as the angel was dragged down? What if his struggle was to try to remain heartless while saddled with a new conscience, and the power of an angel was being used to selfish ends? The defining characteristic and the primary conflict would be almost the same, so why does the second character seem so much less compelling to me? Why is a conscience forcibly impressed by a (somewhat) external source less interesting to me than a conscience forcibly suppressed by a (somewhat) external force, if both characters still have roughly the same level of ruthless pragmatism?

        Is it just that good is more boring than evil?
        Honestly? I think you're just more comfortable with the first idea because it fits the culture and stories that have always surrounded you. The narrative of a man struggling against his baser impulses is a familiar one - a cornerstone of Judeo-Christian morality. The concept of a character who dabbles in the occult and gets more than he bargained for is similarly familiar (and has some of the same roots.) Not criticizing here, by the way. You could definitely make the second character just as interesting, though, if you preferred. Let me pitch a couple ideas to you.

        The first thing that comes to mind for me: you could go the whole "Evil Superman" route. (If you saw "Chronicle", it's a similar idea.) This guy's soul just got mixed up with that of an angel - that's not a trivial thing. He has power now. Maybe he was a hardened criminal, maybe he was just a young man angry at the world, but either way he has a lot of frustrations and selfish desires, and normal people can't hold him back anymore. And yet even as he takes what he wants and avenges his life's indignities, he finds himself feeling guilty and regretful. Will he find a way to forgive others and reverse his course with his newfound power? Or will he push down the guilt, savagely clawing his way to the heights of power like he'd always dreamed of doing?

        Or for a more dark-fantasy twist: maybe that "basically good kid" is, deep down, just as petty, cruel, and selfish as everybody else on the planet. But now that his soul is part angelic, he can't lie to himself about it anymore - he can see with perfect clarity every ugly thing he does for what it is. And moreover, he can see it in other people too. From the angel's perspective, the people around him - even the people he loves! - aren't "basically good." They're a pack of dishonest, bloodthirsty, self-serving hypocrites. Will our hero embrace the angel's purity in his actions, knowing that doing so will estrange him from the world? Or will he retain his ordinary human nature, even as the angel's righteous truths gnaw at him? Or - most fearful of all - will seeing the ugly nature of humanity harden his heart, inspiring him to new heights of cruelty?

        You get the idea. I do think the idea has potential, and I love when a story can subvert itself.

                I'm am just going to say this right here and now, for the benefit of educating everyone:

                If you hear about Elliot Rodger having Aspergers, do not automatically assume that it was the cause of his rampage.

                We know, from the news reports, that he was being counselled. We know that he posted misogynistic, disturbing content online before this all went down. We know he has Aspergers Syndrome, which is a form of high-functioning autism, and very recently which the DMV (think a giant mental health book/diagnosing tool/catalog for doctors) recently put underneath the autism spectrum instead of its own entry. MANY OF THOSE WITH ASPERGERS SYNDROME ARE NOT VIOLENT PEOPLE, BUT HAVE TROUBLE WITH SOCIAL CUES, SENSORY OVERLOADS AND UNDERSTANDING EMOTIONS.

                Please do not think every autistic person or Aspie is going to hurt you. We don't know the full details yet; this has just happened, the story just broke. If you are curious about the condition, please do some research and educate yourself on what this condition is. If you have a friend with Aspergers or autism right now, they may very well need your support. Nobody likes to be ostracized, and people with this condition often find themselves "left out of the group".
