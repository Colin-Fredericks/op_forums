                Trichonephilinae, they changed classification of the jorō.

                Orb weavers make a mess, but isn't as bad as it looks, around here most spiders are orb weavers. Just clean it up with a broom, preferably one you use just for that (because getting the silk out of the broom will be tedious).

                Spiders in general are harmless for humans. Our natural fear is actually a biology mystery, because all primates share the same system: a specialized, super-fast pattern recognition for araneiforms that identifies a spiderlike thing faster than you can recognize your eye is working, that tends to activate anxiety systems.

                But almost no spider is dangerous to primates at all, much less humans.

                Fun fact: Brazil is one of the countries with the greatest number of potentially lethal spider species.

                Funnier fact: that's a whopping total of 3. From more than 4k species in the country, the life threatening ones are 3. And the most dangerous has a killing rate of 2,6% if the bite does occur and goes untreated.

                Even funnier fact: in the US, a study in California IIRC found out that from every 20 patients that go to the hospital because of a spider bite, 1 was, in fact, bitten by a spider.

                Anyway, just clean their webs, it's usually faster than it seems because they look way denser than they actually are. And it's not as if you had much of a choice. Otherwise I hope against hope that doesn't become an ecological calamity.
