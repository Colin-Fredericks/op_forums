                            Bernie Sanders is definitely a real candidate - he's filed the paperwork, and everything. The question is, just how viable a candidate is he?

                            Right now, there's a lot of suspicion that his poll numbers are due primarily to the "Anyone But Hillary" camp. There's a sizable portion of the Democratic Party that's horrified at the idea of the Clintons back in the White House, either for their moderate/"triangulating" politics, or their (perceived) lack of ethics. In 2008, despite being a fresh candidate, with a powerful organization behind her, (then) Senator Hillary Clinton lost to both Barack Obama and John Edwards in Iowa (the first election of the '08 presidential campaign). In fact, its well-documented (if not widely known) that (then) Senator Obama was specifically recruited by the Democratic establishment to run against her.

                            And there's a lot of suspicion that Senator Sanders is just picking up those same anti-Clinton votes. He isn't even a member of the Democratic Party - he's an independent who sides with them on organizational votes. And career-wise, he's best known as a "talking head" on cable news - a good source for a liberal quote, not so much for getting policy implemented.

                            That doesn't mean he can't win the Democratic nomination - he's setting record crowds, after all. But a lot of pundits still believe his poll numbers are "soft", and liable to melt away if another viable anti-Hillary candidate gets into the race. No one's sure who that'd be (although there's a lot of talk about Vice President Biden jumping into the race), and the window to launch a successful campaign is closing fast . . . But the doubts are there.
