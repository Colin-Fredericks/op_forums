                                    Let's tackle some of this...

                                    Originally posted by AzraelFirestorm View Post
                                    I'm legally disabled. I might be a little "off" forever! I don't want to wait till I get "better" to start dating again if that takes me more than a few years, decades or the rest of my life! I'm not a monk!

                                    So, with the "stay single until you feel happy, even though [insert terrible fact about reality]" idea thrown out the window, my problem is threefold:
                                    The goal of that particular line of advice is that unless you are happy with yourself, and willing to be who you are, you are always going to be searching for the person "who will complete me". You don't have to be perfect, but if you go out looking for someone because you NEED someone, then you're setting yourself up a lot of dependency issues and high abuse probabilities in the future.

                                    As for not being a monk, do you not have hands? Even if you don't there's other options thanks to technology.

                                    Originally posted by AzraelFirestorm View Post
                                    1. I'm extremely emotionally attached to a person who is, for all intents and purposes, unavailable to me. Yes, it does hurt.
                                    Okay, this does indeed suck for which you have my sympathies, but there's little that can be done that I am aware of other than time, distance and/or seeking to find the flaws that would make said person less attractive to you.

                                    Originally posted by AzraelFirestorm View Post
                                    2. I'm extremely physically attracted to a friend/acquaintance (or two), and I have no idea how to both ask if they're attracted to other men in general, and if they might be attracted to me personally. Advice would help.
                                    Well... there's the direct approach of just outright asking them. Assuming you don't want to be that direct, you can jokingly reference all the wild parties and wild women they must hang around with. If you're willing to take a middle approach and don't mind a semi-slight lie, you can approach them for advice. Tell them that you've got some friends that were talking about coming and visiting and you're collecting recommendations for fun/interesting places they've visited. From there if you want to feel the waters out mention that a couple of the friends are a gay couple, do any of the places tend to have a problem with that? From there you can possibly extract information if they're gay, or open to same-sex adventures.


                                    Originally posted by AzraelFirestorm View Post
                                    3. SHOULD I TRUST THE INTERNET AT ALL WITH MY SEX LIFE? I have no idea. I've had a few online relationships, but they've always ended in some sort of disappointing crash. Do I keep looking for love online, or do I unplug and go completely local? I'm not a hugely social person, outside of gaming, so bars and clubs don't really appeal much as venues for meeting romantic partners, but where else do you go? I've rarely seen the inside of a bar.
                                    Okay, let's start with flipping the question around... Who else are going to trust with your sex life? You can be fairly certain most of us are largely impartial in the matter, since due to distance there's not much likelyhood that we're going to tell you not to date X because we secretly want to date X and don't want the competition. A large number of use are also not huge social people outside of gaming, so we have similar experiences on which to base our suggestions. And they're just that, suggestions based on our own experience.

I've never had a partner that didn't either call my hobby weird, or at best just didn't get it. I've rarely had the luxury of having a partner know what tabletop rpgs even ARE. It would be nice to date someone that would actually enjoy spending time together doing something we both love. That sounds like it would be awesome.

When you're a GM you have that gooey urge to help your SO more than others in the game, and friction between the two of you at the table can translate to friction or lack of friction elsewhere in the relationship.
