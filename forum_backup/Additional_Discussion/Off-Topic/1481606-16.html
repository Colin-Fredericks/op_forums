                        I saw Beverly Hills Cop: Axel F this weekend and surprisingly it was pretty good. It's one of those nostalgia-sequels with Eddy Murphy reprising his role as an older Axel F who's helping out his daughter (who's a defense attorney) and there are a lot of cameos and references from the first three movies. But it's a fast-paced Action-comedy that holds up. For one, the characters they bring back are still the same great character-actors they were before with people like Paul Reiser, Judge Reinhold and John Ashton reprising their roles. And the new cast is good as well. Joseph Gordon-Levitt as a younger detective is pretty good. Taylor Paige as Eddy Murphy's daughter is okay but that's more because the whole plot is a bogstandard paint-by-numbers affair. She doesn't get to do a whole lot. The movie's exactly what you think it is. But it's fun for what it is.

                        The MVP on the cast is definitely Kevin Bacon, though. He plays the sleazy corrupt cop villain in the movie and man, he nails that role. If anything, they should've given him more scenes. Because he takes that archetypical character and really elevates it with his performance. He brings the right energy to that character to make him so much more compelling than what he's like in the script.

                                                Yesterday I saw Kill, directed by Nikhil Nagesh Bhat. It's this Indian action-movie that's a mix between John Wick and Die Hard (but on a train). This is the premise (from Wikipedia): "When army commando Amrit finds out his girlfriend Tulika is engaged against her will, he boards a New Delhi-bound train in a daring quest to derail the arranged marriage. But when a gang of knife-wielding thieves led by the ruthless Fani begins to terrorize innocent passengers on the train, Amrit takes on the thieves in a death-defying spree to save those around him - turning what should have been a typical commute into an adrenaline-fueled thrill ride."

                                                The reason I saw it is that I had heard of the John Wick producers already planning an English remake of this movie. And once I saw it, I can kinda see why. The plot and the characters are all pretty formulaic but what absolutely stands out is the great fight choreography and the use of practical blood-effects. The action is absolutely brutal. And there's a turn in terms of plot in the middle of the movie that surprised me. I had not expected this movie to have the guts to go there. I still think I liked it less than movies like Raid: Redemption or John Wick but it was definitely a fun watch.

                                                But now I'm wondering how they're going to handle a remake of this because there are two important parts to this movie: One, a lack of guns and two, the societal context of India.

                                                        I saw Longlegs yesterday and it was way better than I expected. It stars Maika Monroe as an FBI agent who's part of a manhunt for a serial killer. It's a very slow-paced movie but it makes up for that by excelling in atmospheric storytelling. The direction, the soundtrack, Maika Monroe's performance... This is one of those horror-movies that's all about vibes. Nicholas Cage is the serial killer but he has way fewer scenes in the movie than you might think with his name above the title. But man, it's a great performance. When he's there on the screen, he's nailing it in terms of creepiness. I can't think of a past performance I would compare it to. But that's typical for Nicholas Cage, I guess. You can always bet on him making interesting choices with his performance (both for good and ill depending on the movie in question ).

                                                        I'm still wrestling with myself whether I like the ending or not. I certainly didn't expect the movie to go there and the explanation also went further than I thought. It's like the ending offered too much information and not enough at the same time. I know, that's contradictory but it's like I either would've liked it to be more ambiguous or offer even more of a conclusive ending.

                                                        But it's a good movie. I definitely gonna watch this one again when it gets its streaming-release.
