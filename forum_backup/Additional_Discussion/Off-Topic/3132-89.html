                We used to host big Fourth of July parties at my dad's. He had the land for it, way out in the country where fireworks are legal. It was the only thing we ever really did with my mom's side of the family, and we kept it up after she died.

                We didn't do it this year. Dad's dead and too many of Mom's family are off getting divorces. It didn't bother me much. I didn't have much to say to most anyone involved.

                Today, I went out with some friends. We played cards, our host bought a PS4 and Bloodborne, we watched him fuck it up a lot and say bad words. It was fine.

                Driving home just now, I watched all the fireworks being lit off by my neighbors and as I looked in the direction of Dad's house, I discovered I was furious with every person who was setting off fireworks tonight. They didn't notice the darkness or the silence at my dad's place, and why would they? What did it matter to them that his patch of the sky had gone forever dark? I wanted to go door to door, grab each person in turn by the lapels, and shake them while I screamed at them about how they should know a man who prided himself on his displays will never light up the sky again because his own light has been extinguished. I wanted to yell at them about the hurtful, hateful bluntness of the metaphor life had crafted today, and to tell them to shove their fireworks up their asses.

                I didn't. I'm not a psychopath. I just wanted to.

                Instead, I came home and wrote this.

                                                I was driving down a moderately busy road today, away from a nursing facility and back to the office, when I saw a big box, maybe eight inches tall and a foot long, in the middle of my lane. The road I was on is a five-lane, so I checked my mirrors, saw no one coming up behind me, and swerved to avoid it. The old woman in the SUV behind me did not dodge it. She slowed down and hoped her front bumper was high enough to miss the box. It wasn't.

                                                About thirty feet away, I stopped at a red light at the intersection, checked my mirrors again, and saw no one was coming. I punched my hazards, got out of the car, and ran to the old lady's to extract the box from her bumper. She smiled great big at me and waved her thanks. I nodded and scurried back to my car, because the light was turning green. I still had the box in my hands, but now I knew I had no time to pop my trunk and throw the box in there for transport to a garbage can, and I certainly wasn't going to huck it onto the sidewalk where the wind could blow it back into the street again. My only choice left was to toss it into my passenger's seat.

                                                As soon as my door closed, I could smell the stink coming from it, a stink I identified immediately from long years of professional experience. I gagged, turned off my AC (the temperature today is nearly one hundred degrees, or for you European types, slightly hotter than a healthy human's body), and rolled down all four windows so maybe, just maybe the stink wouldn't get caught in my car and amplified by the baking it will receive as it sits out in the parking lot today. It didn't work, so I left my windows cracked when I got to the office, and I hope that will air the car out some.

                                                ...Oh, I'm sorry, have I neglected to mention to you what the stink is? How silly of me. The box that was left in the middle of the road had been shat in and left on a 120F road to bake.

                                                Fuck human beings.

                                                            No one ever told me I work for a Christian company. I'd still have taken the job even if they had because I was dead broke at the time, but still, I'd argue that being a Christian company is actually a betrayal of our patients and their own beliefs; that death respects no religious denomination and therefore neither should we. In eight years, the issue hasn't come up, though, so I guess it hasn't been a big deal. I only found out about it when one of our new nurses was complaining to the boss last week that the insurance rep told her "It is what it is," about what I don't know but presumably about her being disqualified from getting insurance this early in her hiring period, because that offends her Christian sensibilities and she was told we're a Christian company. I thought about writing one of these posts but decided against it. Didn't seem worth it.

                                                            Yet here I am anyway, because there was a flyer in my mailbox at the office this morning for a seminar on defense of Christian marriage, which is being attacked by the American government, and which will have a presentation by one of out office's chaplains.

                                                            As I sit here, trying to comprehend the kind of mindset necessary to believe that your right to have a Christian marriage is diminished by anyone else not having one, I am again struck by that sense of exhausted futility I sometimes get in these kinds of issues when I realize exactly how wide the gulf is and how I do not possess anywhere near the skill necessary to bridge it.

                                                            It's eight in the morning, and I already want to give up and go to bed.

                                                            This will be a fun day.
