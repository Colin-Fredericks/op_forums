                                      I sort of do, but I have some worries about how far this might go.

                                      As a minor answer, I gotta say that it's tough to declare what some people should learn immediately going into the various strictures because the entire damn thing is vague as hell. Nevermind that the old religions aren't really codified in a way a lot of Western spiritualists are set to understand easily, and that therefore the entire definition of them and their crafts becomes immensely fuzzy and diverse(without even touching on neo-paganism), and nevermind that the people writing books have their own opinions about what's good opening fodder for initiates and beginners-the entire subject is very in tune with the idea of the individual's rate of progression, so some people are ready for more, some for less, some for certain subjects and not for others.

                                      As a general rule though, the philosophy and attitudes and broad metaphysical viewpoint of each branch of spirituality make for good starting points as a general rule. People who want to know more about the craft can easily build from a good beginner book on their own(although they should really seek out the wise in their community, if there be any).

                                      I don't think the craft needs to be rushed.

                                      EDIT: And again, all of this is a minor answer because I still don't think the question is particularly clear.
