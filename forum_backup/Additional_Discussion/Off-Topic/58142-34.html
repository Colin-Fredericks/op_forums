So you're not avoiding that awkward conversation with the kid, in fact you're increasing the odds. You're not eliminating seeing people that fail to match your gender expectations in the bathroom, you're increasing the probability.

        Of course there's still the question of people that present as a mix of gender traits, and what do you tell the kids? Well, if you don't want to explain just use the solution everyone else does when they kid asks "Oh, they're just going to a comic convention and are dressed up."
        I can offer my experience here as a genderfluid person who presents different mixes of masculinity and femininity at different times. Generally speaking, my decisions of restroom use depend on my presentation on a given day. If I feel like my appearance reads generally male, I'll just use the men's room. As long as I don't feel like my appearance is going to be confusing, I feel like the men's room is the safest option. If I've got some mix, I'll use a single-occupancy or family restroom if one is available, and if not I will hold off as long as possible, and if absolutely necessary I'll use a men's room and try to get in and out as quickly as possible. When it comes down to it, I would rather put myself in a situation where I might have to defend myself physically than put myself in a situation where I could make a lot of women feel unsafe. If I'm presenting totally feminine, I will again avoid using public restrooms (apart from single-occupancy and family restrooms) at all costs and I will not use a men's room. If my partner is with me I might go into the women's room with them, if I feel like I'll be able to pass, but I won't go alone. I will leave early if I have to and deal with whatever I've got to deal with once I'm out of public space.

        That's the thing, my restroom choices are always based on safety. I would love to be able to chose based on where I would feel most comfortable, but that's just plain not practical. And that's exactly the problem. The college I went to made the decision to remove gender identifiers from restrooms in my third year. Restrooms were labeled "Stalls" and "Stalls and Urinals" and anyone could use which ever they wanted. I can't tell you how liberating that was, I could just use which ever restroom was most convenient (usually stalls and urinals, cause that's just easier most of the time) and not feel like I was invading anyone's space or putting myself in a dangerous situation. And there were no issues with people abusing the gender neutrality of the restrooms to assault anyone, sexually or otherwise in the time I was there. I went to a pretty liberal school, but that's my anecdotal evidence of gender neutral restrooms doing nothing but good.

        EDIT: Apologies if this post was TMI for anyone. Restroom stuff can be uncomfortable to talk about, but I feel like it's also an important issue to be able to discuss openly.

                                                            So far in the 9 months I have been living full time as myself I have been using the ladies room and no one in person has had a problem with it. most of this hysteria is a blow back from the legalization of same sex marriage. Scared people reacting to one thing that scares them by attacking another more vulnerable group.

                                                            I will say that the current push for theses law scare me. Kansas is trying to put a bounty on trans people, as well as passing a law that declares us criminal and a threat to cis people. The laws are not written that way but it's there intent.

                                                            I just wonder how far it will go, will I need papers to travel, perhaps wearing a rainbow star on my clothing so everyone knows, or mabye they will just put us all in a camp somewhere for our protection. That all seems insane but we have done as much and worse to others in the past when fear and hate controlled people. Given our very limited numbers and even more limited power there is very little transgender people can do to stop something like that.

                                                            But I get up every day, get dressed and face the world because I have to be me or I would be dead.
