                                                        OK, you're overgeneralizing here. Generative algorithm models do not, inherently, require a constant influx of data. Bullshit like ChatGPT and all those do, because of how they're being applied. They're the digital version of corporate executives that want to keep making the arbitrary line going up and up despite how ridiculous that is. Just like you can run a company without being focused on making the magic money line increasing every year, you can run a generative AI on a limited data set; and your product will probably be better off for it.

                                                        When we look at the good things people have done with generative algorithms (something that's actually been around since the 60s), it generally comes from highly curated limited datasets because they're using the right tool for a specific job. They don't need, or want, extraneous data because that would make the results worse.

                                                        If we want to get back to movies, want to know why the CGI in movies like Jurassic Park and the first Matrix looked so good? Interpolation. Digital interpolation in film editing is the process of digitally creating frames that we never filmed to smooth out the interactions between filmed and digital elements. The Matrix is a better example. The first bullet time rigs had one major problem... there were gaps that made the initial filming look really choppy because the technology at the time forced them do use a static array of cameras and each gap between lenses lead to a gap in the film and visual chops. So, digital interpolation was used to look at frames before and after each break, and digitally fill in frames to create the illusion of a seamless camera movement.

                                                        How? Generative algorithms. A computer did that work, not a human sitting there drawing each extra frame digitally. It's the exact same technology that the new wave of "AIs" use, and one digital artists have been using for decades, but appropriately used to a specific application and highly curated dataset to ensure the best possible results.

                                                        In trying to make the algorithms better, they're gonna run into a brick wall.
                                                        First, just to note, you linked to an ad for a company.

                                                        Second... this is kinda my point? The potential dangers and consequences are inherently constrained by the limitations of the technology. How worried should I really be about ChatGPT when ti's eating itself into oblivion from scrapping up all the data? I'm not worried about these systems replacing human artists because these systems generally produce mediocre work at best. I'm worried about things like the massive amount of e-waste and energy production going into powering them. I'm worried about the exploitation of data scrapping. Though most of these are problems with every tech-fad now.

                                                        The future is customer service calls where to consumer frustration it's harder to tell if we're talking to a machine or a human because we'd rather know, even as companies tell us that the user experience is better the longer it takes to figure it out (or the ruder we are to living people trying to pay rent and buy food to test them). Because it's a limited data set. "Customer service voice" is a thing where call center employees are already encouraged to homogenize their voices. They only need to be able to make small talk and then be able to answer questions about the product the company sells/services so they don't need to care about regurgitating all of Wikipedia on demand.

                                                        And if you don’t deliver even more data to refine the algorithms… well, then these algorithms just start cannibalizing each other and start hallucinating all over the place.
                                                        Again, it's about application. Using them to speed up research into protein folding was highly successful and the protein folding algorithm didn't go off the rails because of it's limited data set and start making up new molecules that defy the laws of physics. It kept to what it was made to do, because nobody tried to make it do something else.

                                                        Computer scientists have known this for ages. This is part of the plot of 2001: A Space Odyssey. HAL 9000 didn't just flip out because AI's inherently go data-crazy without proper inputs. It got contradictory inputs and processed them to the best of its abilities. The scary thing about HAL wasn't that it defied orders... but that it followed them as optimally as possible.

                                                        Garbage in, garbage out. That's the phrase for a reason. Data set size is not part of that phrase for a reason.

                                                        My position is that you can't rely on this technology at this moment. It's not reliable enough to be usable for professional scenarios.
                                                        And my position is that people that know what they're doing have been successfully using this technology in profession scenarios for decades, and for even longer we've know the primary source of instability in automated functions is the humans, not the algorithms. Fad generative algorithms are made by people that aren't trying to do something specific, creating a design-side gigo problem, and were then released to the wild with minimal controls creating a user-side gigo problem. Ones designed by professionals for professional use consistently perform to expectations by reducing gigo as much as possible.

                                                        The fact that Elon Musk can't make a successful line of trucks does not mean trucks are a flawed concept nobody should be using.
