                I also bought nWoD after I had played Bloodlines. It just was what was available in the store at that time. It wasn't until after I started to read the book, noticed how a lot of things were different, that I googled around and figured out there was an oWoD as well, and that they were different.

                I don't mind metaplots, and if done right they can be a source of plot ideas. What I however have a very strong dislike for is advancing meta-plots; so I'm glad it was nWoD I got my mits on. If there should be a meta plot, I want one that sets up the pieces and tells me in what direction they plan to move and what their agenda is. It is just chaos-theory at its core; the second there is an unknown factor, however small, it will diverge. In my opionion, the only way to really handle an advancing meta-plot is to remove both the players and the ST.

                When it comes to WW having new owners... One of the Employees at Paradox Interactive is Marco Behrmann. He has been heavily involved in the Eon line (the fourth edition was quite recently released), the Swedish RPG Noir, and is part of the development of the new Kult game. At PI he has some form of Project Management position; and while I don't know how much P.I. have had him involved, his comment at the main Swedish RPG forum when the new dropped was "I love my job." Everything points he is involved, and it is a guy who understands what it means to create and maintain a large RGP line.

                My own speculation is, I think OPP will get new directives and frames to work within. Then, as long WW and OPP can agree on the details, and on the costs of licensing, it will be business as usual. The main opportunity, and the main threat, as I see it is that WW/PI gets more involved in the RPG making while letting OPP do it. That would be great if the people at WW/PI and OPP gets along, but could damage things completely if they don't.

                        I can see an advancing meta plot in computer games and fiction, as they are tighter controlled medias. An enWoD (even newer WoD) might work as a merger between classic and new, if they make anything new meta-plot agnostic, but with the option to jump on and deviate from the game/fiction meta-plot at any time. So a "Cleveland by Night" would both have alternatives for different times in the meta-plot and information on how to use it in a nWoD like way without meta plot.

                        Then, there will of course be people who will strictly following a meta-plot, without wanting to have one. While I fully understand those who did while there was no option, because the idea never occured to them; I don't think there is any point in bothering with those whom will even after being told how to do it another way.

                        While I think it is likely they will have a tabletop version fairly compatible with a computer game line, I also have a hard time seeing they wouldn't allow someone paying a license fee to keep an old line alive. So as long people are willing to pay for them, I think there will be oWoD and nWoD compatible product to buy somewhere, and I think OPP will be the most likely party to produce them.
