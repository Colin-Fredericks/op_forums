                            I think I’ve figured out why PF2 is so appealing and yet so frustrating to me. It fixes most of the problems I have with 5e’s player-facing options, but it throws out everything I like about 5e on the DM side. Or, I guess since it’s an evolution of Pathfinder rather than of D&D, it doesn’t throw them out so much as it declines to adopt them.

                            I love 5e’s core mechanics. It really boils the d20 system down to its core essence and makes a really intuitive, easy to run game out of it. But I find all the player-facing subsystems and character options really oversimplified and boring. Pathfinder has the exact opposite problem, where it has really rich and interesting options for players to tinker with, but its core mechanics are so clunky and overwrought that it’s a chore to DM. I just want a system that combines modern D&D’s user-friendly baseline with Pathfinder’s wealth of options, it would be my ideal fantasy RPG. But alas, I don’t know of any system that has managed to walk that line.

                                            I've been thinking about the Abjuration school in DnD 5e, and how it doesn't get a lot of interesting options. Like, in general, there are very few Abjuration spells. But especially the Cantrips are lacking. You have Blade Ward and Resistance (and spellcasters usually only get one or the other). Both can certainly be useful, but they also aren't amazing. Just now, I thought of a homebrew Abjuration Cantrip that could fill a different niche.

                                            Barrier/Minute Barrier
                                            Abjuration cantrip
                                            Casting time: 1 action
                                            Range: 30 ft
                                            Components: S
                                            Duration: 1 minute (concentration?)

                                            You erect a transparent wall of force on the ground or in the air within range, up to 5 ft square and 1/4th inch thick. The wall has an AC equal to 10 + your spellcasting modifier, and 5 hp. When the wall is reduced to 0 hp, it shatters and dissipates. It also dissipates if you cast this spell again.

                                            The wall's hp increases by 5 when you reach 5th level (10 hp), 11th level (15 hp), and 17th level (20).
                                            My idea is that the caster (Sorcerer, Wizard, Artificer) erects a minor barrier, one that is fragile and unmoving (if the caster wants one elsewhere, they must cast the spell again). The Cantrip is a way to create cover on the fly, and to block small pathways. It's see-through, so certain spells can be cast through it, but not attacks that pass through the intervening space (like most attack cantrips), and it is as much cover for other characters (including enemies) as it is for you.

                                            Initially I thought of making Barrier have 10 hp per "level milestone", but decided that if a 1st level spell like False Life only gives 5 hp of protection (when cast with a 1st level slot), a cantrip at base shouldn't do more than that. The main advantage of other protection spells (like False Life or Blade Ward) is that they travel WITH the caster, whereas the Barrier is fixed and external (enemy spellcasters can cast "around" it in certain cases). Even at higher character levels, the increasing hp of the Barrier shouldn't be that powerful. 20 hp, at 17th level, isn't really all that much protection. Even the resistance given by Blade Ward can end up mitigating more damage overall.

                                            I'm not sure if I want Barrier to be a Concentration spell or not. I definitely only want casters to be able to have one at a time. But I don't know if it needs to be Concentration, or if I would just say in the spell that the caster only gets to have one up at a time. On the other hand, if Barrier's hp increasing DOES make it more potent than a cantrip "should be", Concentration might be a necessary balancing measure. Like, sure, you get this neat bit of cover, but you also can't have other Concentration effects up and running at the same time.

                                            What does everyone else think?

                                            Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                    Originally posted by Willowfang View Post

                                                    You have a point about the AC. Most starting level spellcasters will tend to have a spellcasting ability modifier of +3, which only gets higher as the character goes along. A Barrier in that case will have better AC than some starting PCs.

                                                    I see two possible ways to handle this. The simpler one is to make it have a static AC 12 (maybe as low as AC 10), no modifier. The other way is to drop the AC to 8 + spellcasting ability. It still starts at AC 11 for most dedicated spellcasters, and rises to AC 13 later, assuming no "breaking Ability score limits" shenanigans happen.

                                                    Any competent monster won't have much trouble getting through an AC 13, 20 hp wall when the game hits high levels. It just might take a bit of time or a lucky set of rolls. Which is mostly what the user of this cantrip would be hoping for.
