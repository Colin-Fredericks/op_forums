            I really hate the cycle of self-doubt I'm in with regards to my own writing. I'm confident that I can create a story which has a sense of artistic unity and that I can portray character archetypes well, but I feel completely incapable of writing anything which doesn't strike me as enormously derivative. Half the time I'm constructing a plot or a character concept the inspirations are so clear cut in my mind that it feels like I'm stealing parts of them and that what I'm making ultimately has little value thanks to how derivative it is.

            The problem's compounded by how I like to play certain classic types of stories/settings. For instance, I want end thing more than to make a setting for Vampire the Requiem with an Invictus prince and a strong Carthian opposition, with all the usual suspects operating as they typically do in the fiction. I see people calling that sort of thing boring and typical all the time, but for me? It's super fun and interesting. I guess part of it is that I haven't gotten to play Vampire all that much and never in that "typical" setting so its exciting for me, but I find the same thing to be the case in to other games too.

            I want to play a Jedi fighting the Sith in a Star Wars game because I've never gotten to, etc. Its definitely a first world problem, but it messes with me. It makes me feel less confident in my writing.

                                                        Here's something that's irritated me today - it's going to be heavy on political stuff, fair warning.

                                                        The backstory first: there's this community I'm part of. The administrators announced today that they'd have a booth and a float at their city's Pride Festival in June, and invited like-minded members to join them.

                                                        The reaction was strangely mixed. A sizeable group of members were in favor of the action, and in favor of gay rights in general. Another sizeable group opposed the action...but (at least ostensibly) not out of overt homophobia, as you might expect. No, they said, they just wanted the community's administrators to remain officially neutral, so as not to alienate people or stifle ideas and so on.

                                                        * * *

                                                        So here's what bothers me about that:

                                                        My first grievance - and I know it's cynical - is that frankly, I don't believe many of the stay-neutral crowd had the motives they claimed. And if I'm right on that count, I wish they'd just say outright that they don't want gay marriage to be legal and get it over with. It's a selfish desire on my part, admittedly - it's ugly when people are open and obvious about their prejudices, but it's also less threatening in a way. If someone's angrily chanting "Adam and Eve, not Adam and Steve!", most of society will write them off...but if they cloak their goals by talking about religious liberty and free speech and so on, people you know are more willing to listen, and then that prejudice becomes a problem for you personally.

                                                        My second grievance is that even if these people are being truthful, I'm not sure it's much better. One of the most frustrating trends I see in the world, especially in popular media, is this determined centrism - this idea that the truth is always somewhere in the middle, that only the overzealous and narrow-minded take firm sides. And it particularly annoyed me in this case, because of all the conflicting ideas to which you could apply that principle, the decision of whether gay marriage should be legal is not a super-nuanced one. There's no complicated expert arguments in play here, full of uncharted gray areas. It's about as binary as issues get, and I can't fathom how you could hear both factions' rhetoric and end up thinking "Well, both sides have good points and bad, I guess nobody's really in the right here."

                                                        My bias is pretty obvious here - LGBT rights are important to me. And the idea that a community shouldn't show support for those rights, on grounds that it might make opponents of those rights uncomfortable...it's difficult to convey the anger that comes with that without sounding like a bitter zealot. But I hope I've done okay.
