      While not 'slang,' an important term to recognize is cis/cisgender; it refers to anyone who isn't trans or non-binary, and neatly solves the pickle of describing that without the unfortunate implications inherent in calling such folks 'normal.' You'll sometimes see described as cishets - cis individuals who are heterosexual.

      Many folks don't bother saying the entire LGBTQA+ acronym out loud, because it's a bit of a mouthful; queer is concise enough as an umbrella term, though it does have a lingering reputation from its days as an insult.

                              Heteronormative isn't slang, but says a lot. It's the idea that being hetero is the norm, so framing everything in the world in that context. Words shape our worldview, so how you frame things matters.

                              Erasure is a pretty big one too, both for trans and bi people. And... those happen from both cishet and LGBTQ communities. I'm not bi, but I know quite a few, and there's this perception that paints bi people either in a very broad negative light or just being het or not on who they are in a relationship with currently. For trans, there's that even events where trans people were important, they are left out or ignored. Stonewall was a really big example of this (though in fairness, we weren't the only ones who weren't given the credit as a community we deserve).

                              I think the main problem here is... even if something has more a clinical nature, when referring to the majority, it's treated like it's a slur because "We're normal. We don't need that." See how some people react to cis.

                                              Q is an umbrella term for people who exist outside the gender, sex, and/or sexual binaries, but don't fit under the categories Lesbian, Gay, Bi, or Trans (or Intersex or Asexual/Agender, which are now included in the acronym as well). There are as many gender identities as there are people, and it's important that people have their identities acknowledged rather than erased. At the same time, there comes a point where the acronym just gets to be alphabet soup. Adding Q is somewhat of a compromise between those two conflicting necessities. It acknowledges that a wide variety exists beyond the other letters, while keeping the acronym from ballooning out of control.
