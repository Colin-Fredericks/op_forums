    The situation with the relationship between the LARP community and White Wolf was a lot more complicated than that really sums up. There was a massive falling out between White Wolf and the Camarilla fan club (the largest LARP group because of its close ties to the company) that really wasn't about the move to the nWoD games, that had already drove a huge wedge there.

    The nWoD certainly was the last straw in that mess because WW was tired of their "official" fan club that was getting all sorts of perks and access not treating this as a mutual deal that would lead to the Camarilla to promote official products, while the Camarilla didn't want to be shackled to official stuff for their games (understandably). But that bad breakup was a long time coming before that.

        Unpopular opinion, but I don't think nWoD killed White Wolf. I think "personal horror" killed White Wolf.

        Or more accurately the whole toxic mindset of "Personal Horror vs. BadWrongFun" and that whole shift began in Revised Edition.

        Contrary to what a lot of V5 fans (and devs) will tell you, the first edition of Vampire was very much "Trenchcoats and Katanas/D&D With Fangs badwrongfun" but it had the whole "Storytelling Game of Personal Horror" schtick as window dressing. That window dressing was largely a marketing gimmick to try and sell this new game to wider audiences. V1 was very much the most D&D-esque of all the editions of Vampire, and 2e was merely a slightly tweaked and expanded upon take on 1e.

        The Humanity meter was less of a way to enforce personal horror and more of a way to copy the Sanity meter from Call of Cthulhu, as Mark Rein-Hagen and Andrew Greenburg were both huge fans of CoC as well as D&D. The "woe is me, I'm a monster!" angsty shit was merely some fluff to justify this mechanic.

        So much of VTM 1st Edition was just taking stuff from other games they liked and reworking them to fit this modern Gothic-Punk milieu, as the core seven clans all roughly correspond to classes from AD&D 1E in addition to corresponding to various pop culture vampiric archetypes.

        Don't believe me? Once you see this list, you can't un-see it.

        Brujah - Fighter
        Gangrel - Ranger
        Malkavian - Cleric
        Nosferatu - Thief
        Toreador - Bard
        Tremere - Magic-User
        Ventrue - Paladin/Cavalier


        1st Edition was the edition that had Alien Hunger, Ashes to Ashes, Awakening: Diablerie Mexico, magic items in the Players Guide, and all the weirdness in both Chicago and Milwaukee, to say nothing of the stuff in The Hunters Hunted that everyone always seems to just gloss over (Kyber Crystals, the "Fist of God" merit, Caiaphas Smith, Greek mages consorting with Elvish warlords in NYC, a vampire-hunting Penn Jillette expy, The Children of Osiris)

        All 2nd Edition did was just pick up where 1st Edition left off. And it was the most successful edition of Vampire for the longest time.

        It wasn't until after Andrew Greenburg had left and more importantly, Justin Achilli had risen to prominence within the company that this shift towards so-called "Personal Horror" really got going.

        The VTM metaplot stopped being this weird little thing that pretty much began and ended in the Chicago city limits and instead became this massive overbearing force that policed the setting of anything Achilli didn't like. The whole false dichotomy of "Supers With Fangs BadWrongFun vs. True Gothic-Punk Personal Horror" also really got going under his watch. Achilli was the guy who saw all the early marketing promos for V1 and took it at face value.

        Then with the Revised Editions of the other game lines, they tried forcing personal horror on the game lines that never even had it as a pretense. The edition wars over Mage: The Ascension were infamous for a reason.

        The only game line of the original World of Darkness that was truly about Personal Horror from day one was Wraith: The Oblivion. VTM only had the flimsiest pretense of personal horror as a marketing gimmick to sell their game to goths prior to Revised. Wraith actually walked the walk. And it sold like shit.

        Everyone forgets that most of Wraith's fandom and acclaim is largely in retrospect. In the 90's, Wraith was considered a failure on all levels and it sold like shit. It bombed so badly that it was the only game line to get a hard cancellation before the Time of Judgment. Even goddamned Changeling: The Dreaming sold better than Wraith, as it only got a soft cancellation around 2001 and at least got some sort of closure with its own segment in Time of Judgement.

        The later WW devs and the more toxic elements of the WoD fandom hitched their wagons closely to Justin Achilli's "True Art Gothic-Punk Personal Horror vs. Icky Trenchcoat & Katana Supers With Fangs BadWrongFun" mindset and helped push the false dichotomy and alienated a lot of people as a result.


        This push for thematic ideological purism reached its nadir with WoD5 and the controversies over that are still raging as I type this post

                                    But also, and finally, personal horror insistence is the sort of thing that only affects a small slice of the audience. It's really only a certain slice of those who read and expect to play the game but never really do where you get any actual audience where the personal horror becomes a turn off, because they neither have the experience to know how something like personal horror actually affects gameplay (ie, never as much as they think it does) nor the relaxed set of expectations because they have to imagine everything will follow the tone of the books.

                                    Like, yes, Rose Bailey's Second Edition for Requiem is better than Justin Achilli's First Edition, and yes, a big part of that is that Rose understood how to balance personal horror with style, class, and energy in a way Achilli isn't prone to doing, but that doesn't mean Requiem First Edition had the power to kill White Wolf, because it pretty clearly didn't. White Wolf had ambitions and needed help for fulfilling those ambitions, and sadly the people they partnered with were not actually helpful.
