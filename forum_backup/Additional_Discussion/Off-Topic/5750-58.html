                        So I saw the Green Inferno. I felt like I had to, as I enjoyed much of Eli Roth's work, even though I don't like Italian cinema, or the films the movie is based on. I wouldn't recommend it to just random people on the streets, it wasn't great. But anyone curious about it and interested in those movies would probably like it. Roth's movies have a tendency to delve into the face of evil among us when their characters are faced with evil or danger, and this movie doesn't disappoint in that regard. Some of the acting faltered at points, a fault, I think, of an unbalance in the writing. Some of the characters were written very realistically, others were written with more of a dramatic flare one would expect in this kind of movie and it does stand out. Still, I thought some of the main offenders, notably Sky Ferreira and Daryl Sabara, ended up really killing it, even though Sky had a relatively minor role.

                        It was nagging me throughout the movie but I recognized Lars, the character played by Sabara. Afterward I checked and found where I recognized him from. He was in Teen Lust, a sort of coming-of-age story about the protagonists race to lose their virginity, with a twist. The families of the protagonist Neil and his friend, played by Sabara, are members of a Satanic Church. And Neil has been specially groomed by this church to be sacrificed, as a virgin, to empower the forces of Darkness and, when realizing this teenage baptism is going to end in death, make a last moment escape and spend the rest of the night doing what they can to ensure that Neil survives. Since the church is spread throughout the power structure of the town and calling for help is not an option, they decide to lose Neil's virginity. It's a silly premise, and the movie's entire concept is about ten years too late, but overall it is a slightly fun movie and where the writing fails the acting picks up the slack. As a bonus, Cary Elwes plays the hunchbacked High Priest of the Satanic Church.
