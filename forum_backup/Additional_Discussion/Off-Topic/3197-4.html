    I'd think segregation is the wrong term, since that implies institutional enforcement. It might just be a matter of trends, possibly deriving from certain cultural differences deriving from difference in focus and aptitude.

    Something like unicorns having a higher tendency to be urbanised, considering the academic and manufacturing matters suggested by magic, while earth ponies might be more rural considering not just their proclivity for farming, but also how the general ecology needs to be directly managed.

    It could be different in major cities (although Canterlot still seems to trend towards a predominance of unicorns in high society), but I could see Ponyville as unusual for having those characteristics in such a small town (albeit one that seems to have been a bit of a trade hub, for capitalising on a unique resource and possibly proximity to Canterlot). Or possibly even cities might trend towards different pony tribes kind of coming up and living separately (like, there won't be earth ponies in magic school), while Ponyville seems to have people mixed as a matter of course.

    Heck, it even has an earth pony couple with unicorn and pegasus children; that's got to be kind of weird.

            S4E6 - Power Ponies

            Super heroes. Why did it have to be super heroes?

            Okay, I'll actually admit. The episode is surprisingly good. They don't do the stereotype with saving babies and stuff, like The Mysterious Mare-Do-Well did. For that, I'm eternally thankful. For a show that starts with them all getting sucked into a comic book, it was pretty solidly done. The new villain was pretty well-made, and it helped that I really enjoy people with prehensile hair like that. The Power Pony alter-egos were funny commentaries on the characters, and I won't go into all the subtleties right now but the fact that there were subtleties is very encouraging. Oh, and Rarity had best power.

            But. Super heroes?! I'm just... I realise many people may enjoy super hero comics, but to me, the super hero played straight is stupid. The costumes, the side-kick, the whole premise of their existence... I can't take it. I don't read super hero comics, and I would frankly appreciate if they stayed the heck away from the shows I watch that allegedly are not super hero shows. I am at least grateful that this time, they had the good taste of putting the super heroes in a very literal own universe, and not in the real Equestria... y'know, like The Mysterious Mare-Do-Well.

            I didn't hate this episode, in fact it was better than I had expected for what it was. I just question the justification for its existence.

                                        S4E7 - Bats!

                                        I didn't get around this until today. I blame Christmas.

                                        On the one hand, I really liked this episode. On the other hand, I don't have that much to say about it.                                                         S4E8 - Rarity Takes Manehattan

                                                        ...then she takes Berlin. Anyone? No? Okay.

                                                        Even if few and far between, Rarity episodes are usually really good. I find that she is the pony who best carries her own episodes, the one who can do best without the support of the ensemble. Of course, this time she did have the rest of the mane six around, but that didn't stop her from putting her own personal spin on it.

                                                        I will have to disagree with Scutarii about how Rarity's character felt. While the song was a bit in-your-face about it, I found the overall impression to be quite natural. It's just these little almost subconscious acts that I think best drives home how the mane six examplifies their Element. I also, in fact, quite liked her breakdown in the middle. It didn't feel out of place at all, and it helped making her feel real to me. The best part was that they didn't make excuses about it, either. It'd have been so easy to go "no, no, it was all right", but Applejack's "Yeah, you were pretty rotten" makes it clear that it wasn't okay, but that they'll overlook it. Much better handled. In fact, I found that Rainbow Dash and Twilight also were really well-written this time. It strikes me that I may have to grossly rearrange my Best Pony List.

                                                        And that rainbow thread. I absolutely love how they drop little hints that I've no idea what they mean like that. They might mean nothing, though I don't think so. They might even drop a few fake ones, just to shake us up. Sooo much stronger continuity in this season.

                                                        Yeah, I loved this episode. One of the strongest yet the season (and that's in a season which has started out particularly strong), and among the upper tiers ever.
