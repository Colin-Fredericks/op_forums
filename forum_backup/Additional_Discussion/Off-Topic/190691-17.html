                                    Thought of the day:

                                    While this is clearly not a perfect analogy and there are other circumstances to be accounted for, there is still some truth to it.

                                    The other day, someone posted a meme-style pic about how working for a wage that does not allow one to support themselves is just a few steps away from slavery. That's not what I'm arguing, though. If considered a bit, it seems slavery could be a better choice.

                                    I look around at how much time, effort and money put into some of the expensive things they own, like cars, homes, luxury items, etc. As a slave, you are not a cheap commodity. You're an investment. As such, they would have to feed you sufficiently to stay healthy, clothe you appropriately for the environment, house you well enough to stay healthy and safe, see to your health care so that you can continue to work, and (potentially depending on the laws governing ownership) since you've never done anything else with your life they could have to tend to you in your old age (don't roll your eyes, they used to do it all the time). As a slave, you don't own anything, so there is no mortgage, no car payments, no big ticket items, no depressing crush of seeing your back account empty almost as fast as it fills. Granted, yes, you may have to take a few lashings of the skin. But are those really that awful compared to the lashing of the spirit working the daily grind for what amounts to nothing creates? That's up to you to decide.

                                    How sad is it that living in Middle Class America can actually look like a less preferable situation than being a slave? Personally, I could never do it, but I think I have ODD, so defying authority is practically a reflex action for me. I'd be beat to death inside of a month. But, hey, not a lot of worries left in that state of existence, either. Worrying is for the living.
