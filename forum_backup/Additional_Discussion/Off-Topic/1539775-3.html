        There is this odd subgenre of 1990s science fiction films that I greatly enjoy, that being the "the end of the millennium is going to be violent and crazy and slightly absurd, especially in Los Angeles" movie trend. The opening scene of Demolition Man is part of it, as is Predator 2. Another is Virtuosity, a quasi-cyberpunk thing with Denzel Washington and Russell Crowe that I think is unfairly forgotten. Released about the same time was Strange Days, which sadly underperformed at the box office thanks in large part to a really terrible and obtuse trailer. (And it continues to infuriate me that neither this nor director Kathryn Bigelow's other cult classic Near Dark have ever been released on blu ray, not even after her Oscar win.)

                                                    My layman's understanding is that True Lies was shot on a specific type of 35mm camera set up that was designed to make it easier to adapt the footage for television and VHS release at the time but ends up having some issues when doing a transfer to more modern DVD/BR and wide screen TV formats. Bigelow's Point Break was shot using the same type of camera, and apparently the recent Shout Factory release shows those issues when you try to do it unaltered. But the True Lies 4K ends up overcorrecting to the point where it looks like a movie made in the past five or ten years instead of a film made in the 1990s.

                                                        Regarding guilty pleasure TV shows, I have a lifelong fondness for Friday the 13th The Series. Part of the slew of late 80s/early 90s syndicated science fiction tv shows, it has nothing to do with the more famous film series other than sharing a producer, Frank Mancuso Jr. Instead, it follows the adventures of a pair of cousins who have inherited their creepy uncle's antiques store only to find that most of the stuff he sold was cursed by the devil at leave death and misery in their wake. So they spend most of their time trying to recover cursed antiques.
                                                        It is a cheesy series, with a lot of the dodgy practical special effects and melodramatics of both "Technicolor Gothic" era tv and episodical sci fi shows. However, this show helped nurture my lifelong enjoyment of both horror and history.
