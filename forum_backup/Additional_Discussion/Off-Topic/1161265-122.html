            To be honest, I’ve never seen push/pull/lift values actually come up in game. Carrying capacity I could see being lower, but also their equipment is going to be smaller, so may as well just abstrat it as, the differences roughly balance each other out and save yourself some math.

            Originally posted by PMárk View Post
            Well, since rage is heavily leaining on str, it's still not ideal.
            This is exactly my point though. A Halfling makes a strictly suboptimal barbarian because of strength modifiers. Whereas in a system where size differences are expressed through active and passive abilities only instead of through ability modifiers, a barbarian that cant effectively use oversized weapons can still work around that, and “I dual-wield instead of using a giant axe” is a more interesting difference than “I have 10% less chance of hitting and do 2 less damage on a hit.”

            Originally posted by PMárk View Post
            And anyway. Half-orcs getting a bonus to str still indicates that they are better at things str-related, thus halflings are worse in those than them. Effectively, it's the same situation than having pluses and minuses, just, somebody somewhere thought up the notion that people don't like minuses, which might be ven true, it just never was a thing for me.
            Ideally, half-orcs wouldn’t have +2 strength either. You would have no racial ability modifiers, only active and passive racial features that affected your gameplay in a way that reflected your physiological advantages and disadvantages.

            Originally posted by PMárk View Post
            As I said, same with PF2, some people are complaining that now untrained is -4, while I was happy about it.
            Thats kind of a different matter, and I’m personally fine with the untrained penalty. It’s about racial mods invalidating certain race/class combinations. I prefer race features that affect how a member of a certain race might behave as a certain class, rather than numbers simply making certain races worse (or better) than others at being a certain class.

A new player might be someone, who is just new, but invested and a casual is more like someone, who just finds it fun and an opportunity to hang out with friends, but doesn't care about any of the game or story beyond that at all. Don't want to read the story in their free time, don't care to learn the rules even into the 10th session. And that's not even a problem, if they're an enjoyable company and good playing buddy. Unless they deliberately don't want to spend time and energy on it, at which point I question their interest. It's not like it's mandatory, nobody cares, in a sensible group, if you didn't read this module or that supplement, but it's there, if you want to dig deeper. Regardless, the corebooks are usually quite beginner-friendly and those are all you need, really, everything else is optional.

Instead, what about adding an alternate ruleset to the game, like a quickstart? Some people are perfectly happy playing with those. I'm absolutely not against having alternatives and easier avenues, but I'm against losing good things.
