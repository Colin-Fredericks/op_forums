                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                    Could you not? Just because you didn’t like it doesn’t make it bad.
                    I don't have any feelings towards any edition of D&D. I'm mainly a Shadowrun guy. So as not to clog the thread, I'll list my percieved flaws of D&D 4e and move on. Suffice to say that I find your statement "you call it bad just because you don't like it" to be disingenuous and insulting.

                    1. 4e is bad at handling out of combat stuff. It fails to meet its stated design goals, which is a topic vast enough to be a multi-page rant. It is also mathematically broken, with your chances of success at "average" task for your level swigning wildly from "always fail" to "always succeed" depending on the level.
                    2. The combat is incredibly slow because everything is a damage sponge.
                    3. Not only is combat slow, but it's also boring, There's pretty much never a situation where daily -> encounter -> other is not the optimal attack sequences.
                    4. On top of that, it's also hard to track. 4e abilities end or start functioning at extremely varying times, some at start of a turn, some at end of a turn, some in the middle, etc. which becomes a pain in the ass to track.
                    5. A host of other, smaller problems, like magic item design. Reducing magic item dependency was one of the stated design goals, and 4e failed to meet it, in many ways making the dependency worse.

                    Originally posted by Bill Slavicsek on Skill Challenges
                    From the first discussions about D&D 4th Edition, we knew that we wanted a mechanical subsystem as robust as combat that could handle the other things PCs do in an adventure—namely, social encounters and challenge encounters. We didn’t want a system that reduced all the intricacies of a situation to a single die roll; we also didn’t want a system that failed to add to the fun of an adventure. What we did want, for the situations that called for it, was a system full of tension, drama, and risk… the very essence of any D&D encounter.
