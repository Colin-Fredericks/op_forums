                            Okay, so now I want to switch away from my prior points about defense of people who would dislike Kill la Kill in favour of more stuff that I myself actually liked about it.

                            Or anything else on the planet besides what this thread is doing right now, I'm not too picky...

                            So, one of the things that initially drew me was in how the protagonist was a woman, though still with basically all of the attitudes of your typical shonen protagonist, but part of what kept me very closely drawn in was how it had a variety of prominent (and incidental) female characters, with a diversity in personalities and relationships, and good development in each, without relying very heavily on stereotypes. That, really, is what helps me over some of the more overt visuals, more than the idea that they're running with a coherent theme under it all; whatever they're being put through, there are real characters underneath it. The Bechdel test isn't as utterly comprehensive as some people who point to it might imply, but the series still does entirely well with it; I mean, bonus points for how almost every female character has a connection to and conversations (or at least exchanged words with) every other female character.

                            This even extends nicely to how, although romance in general isn't really prominent, and is mostly left in implications, the most prominent implied romantic connections between characters are between women, but without it needing to be all showy and titillating.

                            And it gets to do all this without also making it a story that excludes men entirely, as many such seem to feel the need to. Like, there are women, and there are men, and the women get to just happen to be more prominent in the overall story.

                            This is all a matter relevant to my interests, so to see a show do so well with it in terms of character and story is very satisfying to me.
