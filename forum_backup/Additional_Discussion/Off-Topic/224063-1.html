        Every place has it's advantages and disadvantages, the trick is them lining up with what works or doesn't with you.

        Small towns are great if you're into the very rural activities such as hunting, fishing, RV riding or some combination of them.

        Cities can start to develop problems with crime but also due to the concentration of people allows for specialty shops and events, like game stores.

        I like the San Francisco Bay Area. The city itself was too expensive/crowded but the transit system made it so you didn't need to deal with traffic.

        I like Calgary because even though it's got annoying winters, the downtown core is built around the fact and interconnects the buildings, plus the transit system is decent (not great but okay)

        Sacramento is kinda take-it-leave-it for me. Transit system needs a lot of work before it's viable, game stores generally not that great.

                        Plymouth is a small, somewhat dull town that's about a twenty minute drive away from the Twin Cities, and yet still has a lot of farmland around. The neighborhood I live in is safe enough, I've walked around during any given set of hours without running into any sort of trouble. Except maybe a Cop asking me what I was doing walking around in the middle of the night

                        We've got a Supermarkets, Fast Food places, Retail Stores and a dozen other buildings that close at like 6. Except for a Cub Foods and Taco Bell, and the Mann's Cinema during midnight showings. Not a lot of nightlife really. Still the Neighborhoods always seem to be dark enough that one can generally see the stars on a clear night. And there is something nice about walking or biking alone through them. If you're quiet enough, you might see a deer.

                        There aren't a lot of Public Transits places. Dunno why, probably because everybody can afford a car, bike or decent running shoes. Which is felt all the more during the winter when everyone fucking forgets how to fucking drive.

                                            Am I the first non-USA Resident to post here? :O

                                            I love my city, Lisboa (Portugal). And to be honest I guess most of the world, we've been winning a lot of prizes in past two years, including the 4th Most Beautiful City in the World

                                            It's a cosmopolitan city with a huge influx of tourism. Nevertheless, being a city of 3 million people (which is what most people in other countries would call a Village eheh) it keeps it's identity and mystery.

                                            It is almost 800 years old as part of Portugal and much older if you consider it was founded before the Roman Empire. Therefore we have no short amount of monuments, historical ruins, art and a piece of History in every corner.

                                            The topography gave the city it's nickname "The 7 Hills" which all face the river. Given the 1755 Earthquake and Tsunami that razed the city, the rebuilt used modern techniques like geometric construction and streets intersecting with avenues which blend with the older more medieval neighbourhoods that are more of a chaotic random building with maze like narrow streets.

                                            We have amazing climate with the Winters never being bellow 5.ºC at their worst and great Summers spent in nearby beaches.

                                            The architecture in itself is also something to see and finally the Mayor has implemented a policy to preserve the historical and traditional façade of the buildings even if people completely rebuild the inside. We also have big gardens, one of them named after an English King (Edward VII).

                                            No lack of amenities as theatres, cinemas, shopping centres, concert arenas etc.

                                            On the cultural side there's an infinity of typical foods, it was the city that gave birth to out traditional music "Fado" (Bill Clinton being a huge fan), where our golden age of cinema was based (during the 20's-50's nowadays it kind of sucks), Universities and it's the country's Capital.

                                            I could spend the whole day writing everything in detail so I tried to put it just in topics for a quick read. I welcome all to visit, we are also known for being the best hosts

                                                            Do I like the city I live in?

                                                            More than most places that I have lived, yes. - Meridian, Idaho feels a lot like a small town out in the country (I drive past crops, livestock, and agricultural equipment pretty much daily) but is actually, thanks to be immediately adjacent to Boise, a large enough city that I have a choice in movie theaters to visit, countless restaurants to choose from (specifically, there are 14 different pizza places within only about 1 mile of my home), and all of the varieties of store which are not found in actual small towns out in the country.

                                                            It's quiet, has a low crime rate, and is still on most major U.S. (music) tour schedules.

                                                            The downsides?

                                                            The state is ranked 48th in education, has a ban on same-sex marriage, and is chock full of people that don't actually seem to realize exactly how racist, sexist, and hateful they are (though I have found a decent number of genuinely alright people to spend my time with).

                                                            Not so noble anymore.
