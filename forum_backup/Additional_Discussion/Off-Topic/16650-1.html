                            Yeah there's been other multi-Doctor stories, most notably the 10th and 20th Anniversary specials, The Three Doctors and The Five Doctors respectively* and they have the same general thing. Actually the 11th Doctor had some inkling of what was going on at the beginning while in those older stories the oldest Doctor present had no idea what was going on. At least if I'm remembering it correctly, it's been a while since I've seen them.

                            *It's kind of a bad name for both specials as they both were largely short a Doctor. In the Three Doctors the 1st Doctor only appeared on a screen a couple of times to talk to his future selves which was because William Hartnell (the 1st Doctor) was old and in terrible health and couldn't really handle a more hands-on role. In the Five Doctors the 4th Doctor only appeared in a clip from an unaired episode as Tom Baker, the longest running Doctor, felt that it was too soon since his departure for him to return. Which he later regretted.

                                    There was a gag short between 10 and 5 a few years back where 10 remembers how to solve the paradox because he saw 10 do it back when he was 5.
                                    But note that 5 *wasn't expecting* 10 to remember that, and was actually surprised that he did!

                                    It's never been stated, but my working theory was always that the Laws of Time enact a kind of amnesia on Time Lords who cross paths with themselves this way; it only makes sense, otherwise in the Five Doctors, they all would have known who the actual villain was from the Second Doctor on! Once they leave, the oldest Doctor present (5, in the case of TFD) suddenly finds that he can remember the adventure from all POVs involved. DotD came pretty close to confirming this theory by the end (10 remembers what happens in Time Crash because of the unstable nature of the temporal anomoly that brought him together with 5).

                                    One wonders how the story might have been different if Eccleston, the 9th Doctor, had signed on. Maybe he didn't because it retconned the numbering of the Doctors, and he was revealed to be the 10th all along? I'm still waiting for the fandom to split in two once that revelation really sinks in... Hm, wonder if they would have called it "The Four Doctors"?
