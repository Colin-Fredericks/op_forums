                        Confusion is pretty common. Doctors aren't the only ones telling people what to believe. And when you have two people you trust telling you conflicting things, choosing what to follow isn't that simple, especially when your health is at risk. The result is people trying to make what they think are the best decisions they can under a barrage of conflicting data. And Doctors aren't completely in the right, either. For example, vaccines have problems, they have risks of all kinds and limitations. Symptoms can begin anything between a week and several months after infection, and when they do, you're a goner. The single treatment available has been around since 2004 and saved a whopping number of 5 people. 5, total, in 21 years. Without this treatment your chances of survival are above 0% only in the strict sense that it is technically impossible to affirm, it may be that you can survive Rabies, we just never saw a single case even in legend through the whole human history. So that's it, we have vaccines, the Milwaukee Protocol, and a statistical imperative as options.
