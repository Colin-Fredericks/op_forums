                        I saw a couple movies last weekend. The Pyramid, a found footage-esque film about archaeologists who find an ancient pyramid that seems to predate the more prominent ones, buried beneath the sands. There's a father/daughter old/new dynamic as the daughter is a big believer in using modern technology including sonar and scanning to do a lot of work for her, while the father is a 'traditionalist' who wants to get down in the dirt. They want to explore it but the riots in Egypt have the State Department telling them and the film crew trying to make a documentary on the find out of there. They decide to stay long enough to explore a little and regret the decision. The acting was good, despite some script issues, but the movie was pretty bad. I don't normally care about this sort of thing but the film quality are pretty good, though some of the special effects suffered. It had a budget of over six million, and while that's not a lot compared to some huge blockbusters it's still a lot of money. While it's obvious that money and special effects don't make a good movie I think some movies really drive the point home, this being one.

                        It's not about mummies.


                        Then there was The Hybrid, apparently originally titled Scintilla. More of a sci-fi approach to horror, though just a little bit in the future. When it started I was expecting some horrible C-movie from Asylum or something, but it managed to surprise me by being a solid B movie. I don't think anyone will put it on their list of greatest movies of all time, but it's pretty good for what it's about. A mercenary operator pulled out of some hellhole of a prison by a corporation who needs him to do a job for them. They were led in his direction by some of his former team, who seem to have fallen on hard times in his absence, though they don't fully appreciate the more 'honorable' leader. They're directed to go behind the lines of an Eastern European revolutionary front and get one of their scientists who seems to have gone rogue. No zombies, they took a less common plot for what's going on. Action, tension, nice twists, it's all pretty good. Even the special effects, used sporadically, were well handled. I really don't normally care about that sort of thing, but I think because I watched these movies so close together, the quality of the Hybrid stood out a lot more against the more shallow but showy experience of The Pyramid.

                                So last night I saw the movie Spring, and a while ago Clown.

                                Spring was gearing up to be good, but it failed near the ending. To me pretty hard. It's about this California guy, Evan, who was trying to go to college but had to go home when his father died of a heart attack, then stay home when his mother developed cancer. And he stuck with her to the end and sort of realized he had no reason to stay there afterwards. So after an old wish his father had he decided to go to Italy where he bummed around with some other guys he met in a hostel and then bumped into this woman in a small tourist town. The woman, Louise, is a bit odd, right off the bat you can tell she has a secret, and while it gives frightening glimpses of what the secret is you don't get the full blow-by-blow until later. But that's the problem part, for me. I'm not going to say it's a bad movie, it just fails at this one essential part. Otherwise the acting is pretty good, the cinematography is good, the special effects sort of bounce between lousy and well done but it's more of a character story so that's easy to overlook. There's a nice atmosphere to the whole thing, they really took advantage of the environs.

                                Without going into details, there's no vampirism or anything but she's effectively immortal. They can be together, but he doesn't want her to live her immortal life anymore. He wants her to become mortal and live a finite life with him. Even overlooking the fact that they've been together about a week at this point, I'm annoyed the movie portrays this as the romantic thing to do. Maybe it's just me but, 'I love you, and if you loved me too you'd die with me,' seems less like love and more selfish. There are some other reasons that they might not be able to live together if she stayed immortal but they could easily be sidestepped. It was a good movie but afterwards I couldn't help but think about how different Romeo and Juliet would be if Juliet told Romeo that if he loved her, he'd commit suicide with her.


                                Then there's Clown. It seems like a ridiculous story, maybe something you'd see on Syfy on Saturday night. It even has Peter Stormare. It's about this timid real estate agent who's son has a birthday. The wife calls and, apparently, the clown they scheduled isn't coming. Desperate to deal with it, he looks around the house he's checking out and sure enough, there's a clown costume. So he puts on the outfit, the make-up and wig and nose and entertains at his son's birthday party. It's a blast, and he's wiped, so he falls asleep on the couch afterwards. When he wakes up, he has trouble getting the clown costume off. Not just the outfit, but the make-up, the nose, the wig. It won't come off. He tries hacking it off with power tools, and it won't even tear. His wife, a dentist, manages to rip off the nose with a lot of elbow grease and takes a decent chunk of his nose with it. But everything else seems like it's there for good. And he starts getting these strange hungers. First for sweets, birthday cake, but then something else...

                                It's a surprisngly good movie. Very dark, as perhaps all clown movies should be, but it takes an idea and runs with it. The husband is explored, he's a harried guy in a loving relationship but hated by his wife's family and friends. The wife doesn't really get this, at first, but she's shown to be just as in love with him and trying to do what needs to be done given their situation. Most of the characters are well thought out, the script is pretty solid for a horror movie. The script takes some nice twists that really make it a better movie than it could have been.

                                It's not a clown costume. It's a clown skin.


                                I'm going to have to get better at talking about these.
