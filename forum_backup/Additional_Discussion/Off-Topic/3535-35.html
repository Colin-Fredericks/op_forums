                                                Originally posted by Odd_Canuck View Post
                                                Let me know what the results of the inquires are. I am curious as well.
                                                Well, I finally got the chance today.

                                                Let's get this out of the way: The protesters outside the Planned Parenthood in my town do offer services to refer women to alternate medical and counseling resources to support them in the choice to keep their pregnancies. I cannot, at this time, testify as to the quality of those services, but my biggest concern--that the protesters want to restrict women's choices while accepting no responsibility for ensuring the only choice left is a useful and productive one--is at least partially answered. They do offer services to women. Whatever other complaints I have against the position or their methodologies, the protesters take steps to support women.

                                                That said, my cynicism is really blaring on this one. The group protesting is one called 40 Days for Life, which the spokesperson (by which I mean one of the two retired women walking around outside) seems to base its entire premise on religious grounds. According to the literature I received, their entire plan is to pray and fast for forty days, at which point God will...hang on, let me quote the pamphlet..."heal our land." Heal it of abortion, presumably. This is a really sore issue for me for reasons I won't go into fully because I don't want my anger to seem like I'm directing it at our Christian friends here on the boards,* so I'll just say it does not sit well with my beliefs that they would rather pray than, I don't know, contact their state and federal representatives. I'm not Christian, but I was raised Christian, and I was taught that grace and healing are gifts God gives, not objectives that can be purchased at the price of prayer. It feels insupportably arrogant to me to think that I can hand God a voucher for X amount of people praying Y hard for Z period of time and haggling that into a payment God is obligated to accept and repay.

                                                Despite the protesters' complete focus on religion, though, I did my best to find out what they actually do for the women and the children they target, and I must admit their position seemed rooted in compassion to me. They mentioned the tragedy of abortion a lot, which, fine, yeah, I gotta give them that. Every woman who's ever told me about her abortion has described feeling guilt and regret for years afterward, so wanting to spare women that is a pretty noble cause.

                                                I just wish I didn't get such an air of sales pitch about it, you know? The constant repetition of religious beliefs and invitations to pray seemed like clumsy attempts to indoctrinate me, and I couldn't help noticing the protesters did not ever once ask me a question about my beliefs, because they didn't care what my beliefs were except to train them to match their beliefs. It's a bummer, but fine, whatever, I guess I can't really fault people who seem abortion as murder for not asking me what my stance on murdering babies is. You probably start from the assumption that no one likes killing babies.

                                                Anyway, the story is pretty anticlimactic, since most of it was about religion and I don't care to hear that as the basis for legal arguments. The protesters have more actual procedures in place than I expected, but fewer than I'd hope (seriously, "ask God to make it all better" is just not a political ideology I can get behind, whether I agree with the issue at hand or not). So while I remain opposed to their goals, I do feel that fairness requires me give them more credit than I did in the day.
