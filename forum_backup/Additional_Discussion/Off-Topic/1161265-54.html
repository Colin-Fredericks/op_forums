    Originally posted by Morty View Post
    Sure, but the ranger's combat styles are a particularly flagrant example of it. Would the fanbase really have reacted badly if they'd been just removed and never spoken of again? I don't think so. 5E removed that and no one seems to have complained.
    But 5th edition didn't remove it. It's still there. In 3rd edition Rangers got two-weapon fighting feats for free. In 3.5, starting at 2nd level, Rangers could pick a Combat Style from either Archery or Two-Weapon Figthing. In 5th edition, at 2nd level Rangers pick a Fighting Style from Archery, Defense, Dueling, or Two-Weapon Fighting (interestingly they don't get a Great Weapon Fighting style for using two-handed weapons). It's basically the same thing they had in earlier editions, they just have a few more choices than they did.

    So no, nobody complained about it getting removed because, rather than getting removed, it's still there and improved a bit from what it was in the earlier editions.

                I'm just really happy that Paizo is innovating. The fantasy gaming scene has been so heavily defined by 4e backlash for the past 10 years. Paizo spruced up 3.5 and sold it as Pathfinder, while WotC tried to save 4e with Essentials, and when that didn't work, designed 5e by committee. I understand there's only so much innovation Paizo can do - they cultivated a fandom based on aversion to change, so changing too much would undoubtedly be shooting themselves in the foot. But it looks like they're actually trying to make PF2 a better version of Pathfinder, instead of a better version of D&D 3.5. That alone is enough to catch my interest, and even if it ends up not being for me, I think it's a sign of good things for the hobby. I hope this gives WotC the kick in the pants they need to start pushing the envelope a bit more with 5e. 5e is doing way too well right now for them to think about moving to a 6e, or even a 5.5e, but if they start to feel threatened by Paizo again, maybe they'll be encouraged to finally start providing some of those much-touted modular options that never turned up for 5e. I think some competition will do both IPs some serious good.

                                                Originally posted by Morty View Post
                                                First off, there's no "improving" Ranger Combat Styles. They shouldn't exist, period. And maybe I'm giving 5E more credit than it deserves, but it seems to have done it. Rangers pick a combat style just like fighters and paladins do.                                                 I think you are giving 5e too much credit in this case. Rangers in 5e very much have combat styles. They get access to a limited pool of Combat Styles (no Protection or Two-handed weapons for example). Paladins get access to a different, limited set of Combat Styles (no Archery or Two-Weapon fighting) while Fighters get access to all of the styles. It's honestly not much different from PF where Fighters get to pick from any Combat feat for their combat style, but other classes like Rangers, Monks, etc, have a more limited list of what combat feats/styles they can choose from. In both cases though it's still very much an improvement over where Rangers were in 3rd edition and 3.5 edition.
