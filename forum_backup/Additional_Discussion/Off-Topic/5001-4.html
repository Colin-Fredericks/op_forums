                    I posted a thing (in this thread), then I deleted it because I was like "meh, nevermind, nobody cares anyway" (ironic as that's one of the things bullying does to you -- self-dismissal). Is there a way to get it back? (at the time it said there was a way to undelete because it was a "soft delete" but I can't find the option anywhere)

                    Anyway, I'll restart I guess. My life in high-school was comparable to being caged in with sharks or hyenas - they'd poke and nibble at you to test you out, and when you offered any resistance / fought back, that's when the real feeding frenzy would commence. Usually with you being outnumbered (they'd get you at times when you are alone, no matter how many friends you have.)

                    Some excerpts of my school experience (Quebec, Canada) include the following (all day, every day, for 5 years):

                    - Being called an ugly worthless gay weak idiot while being slammed into lockers and getting slapped upside the head.
                    - Having rocks thrown at me by 5 guys that would follow me partway home most evenings and beat me if I would offer any resistance, just because they were bored.
                    - The guy sitting in front of me would slam his chair into my desk, in turn slamming my desk into me. The guy behind me, his friend, would do the same with his desk.
                    - Getting spitballs and erasers thrown at the back of my head (sometimes the teachers would get them too, when they weren't looking).
                    - Just the never-ending stream of insults, of shoves, slaps, spits, etc. Every day for five years.
                    (Not to mention the numerous times kids were busted for drugs (usually pot and mushrooms, but often ecstasy and lsd, rare times cocaine) and for hiding handguns in their lockers (once or twice).)

                    Amongst other things. On top of that, adults (parents and friends' parents, not teachers) telling you to stop being such a girl, to suck it up and ignore them because they'll go away if they get no reaction (hint: no, not at all, not ever). They don't help you to deal with the sheer amount of fear, sadness and blind rage that it induces. They just tell you to ignore it, because if you do it'll go away.

                    The only time those things subsided even a little is when I snapped and choked a guy halfway into unconsciousness (one of the chronic bullies), probably wouldn't have stopped until he was out cold if a teacher didn't pull me away.

                    A lot of kids walk away from that pretty damaged later in their lives - trust issues, alcoholism, flashbacks that keep you up at night where you keep analyzing the situation, uncontrollable rage outbursts. Some people, now grown adults, can't even walk by a group of teenagers without feeling a rise of defensive adrenaline and hatred, as though they were about to get attacked.
                    There were a couple studies that examined the phenomenon and found that it was remarkably similar to post-traumatic stress, from all the years of unrelenting abuse, similar to how soldiers would still expect aerial strikes or guerrilla squads to come get them even after the war they fought in long passed. (I'm not trying to say that it's exactly the same, clearly war veterans have it much, much rougher, but the phenomenon is similar.)

                    If you had a horrible time in high-school and you've noticed you exhibit some of the things I mentioned above (mistrust, rage, paranoia), you're not crazy, it's a thing and it can be helped. Mostly just by talking about it to someone that cares (friends, family, significant others) and won't dismiss it as "it's all in your head". Yes, it's in your head, and it won't go away. That's the problem.

                                            My middle school years were horrendous, but by the time of high school things had evened out (for me). I was never one of the *popular* kids, but I joined the music and drama department early and had enough status as a hanger-on that I was mostly just ignored.

                                            Really, that was most of my experience for most of high school. I wasn't incredibly popular, but I wasn't *un*popular either. I didn't have the tormentors I'd had in earlier years, but I didn't have very many friends - at least, 'til senior year, when I joined the Sci Fi club and met the (essentially) family of friends I still carry to this day.

                                            Now, if you want to talk about junior high, *that* time sucked. I'd be clotheslined by two guys holding a backpack between them, or have someone pull on my own (heavy) backpack to knock me down. One time it was raining when I got to my bus stop, but the kid whose family owned that house refused to let me onto his porch, so I got soaked (as did everything in my backpack). That one, the teachers caught onto, though I didn't see any reprisal toward the other kid.

                                            Oddly enough, that's the one kid who came up to me years afterward (in high school, actually) to apologize for being horrible to me when we were younger. It's weird, just how good an apology can feel.
