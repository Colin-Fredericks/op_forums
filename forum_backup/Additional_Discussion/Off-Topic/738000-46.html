    It's weird being on the other side of cultural bias. Being from the United States, it's usually my own culture putting itself in front of the rest of the world, but it seems in this case that's getting flipped around, based on reports that Scandanavia hasn't really taken to nWoD, and phrases like "long neglected community" and "best developers in Sweden".

    However, there's one thing to consider, and that's the way Onyx has been doing things. If I go to my local game store, I see D&D, Pathfinder, the 40K RPGs, three different flavors of Star Wars, Shadowrun, Dresden Files, and a bunch of others. I do not see anything World of Darkness-related, neither new nor classic. That means that if I don't take particular care to research it, I will not be aware of any new WOD products.

    Of course, the people frequenting this site know about OP and how they're doing things, but those are the people who are already heavily invested in the games. I don't have any numbers, but I don't think OP is picking up much in the way of new players with POD. It would be interesting to compare the sales of Vampire the Requiem 1st versus 2nd edition, for example. Or, for that matter, Vampire Revised vs Vampire 20th Anniversary.

                The problem is that since the 1990's, there are fewer and fewer FLGS's and mass-market bookstores that carry RPG's. Whether we like it or not, the industry has changed and shrunk over the past few decades, largely due to the cost savings and convenience of the digital publishing model and competition from other media like video games.

                    It's also worth remembering, regarding which RPGs you still see in stores, is that all the RPGs prominent in stores are only there because they are made with companies that are in gaming stores for non-RPG products.

                    D&D is own ultimately by Hasbro, but even sticking with WotC, D&D has a miniatures line, and WotC has multiple CCGs as well.

                    Paizo, to compete with D&D, also does Pathfinder minis.

                    FFG is heavily involved in board games, CCGs, and minis, on top of their RPG products.

                    Catalyst is also still heavily involved in minis with Battletech as one of their two flagship properties.

                    Privater Press is also a mix of RPG and minis.

                    So that covers: D&D, Pathfinder, the new Star Wars games, WFRP, Iron Kingdoms, Mechwarrior, and Shadowrun.

                    Pretty much all of the companies that are just doing RPGs (or at least RPGs are their primary product financially), have moved to the model Onyx Path is doing. If you see a FATE book, or a *World game, in a local store, it is more than likely the owner is a fan more than they're actually distributing to physical stores on a general basis (for example, nobody near me carries the Dresden Files RPG).
