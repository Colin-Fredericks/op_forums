      The following is a short story I wrote, called Mundanities of the Most High. If you don't get the references, then you don't play the best game ever.

      ---

      "You're late," he said to her as she walked up to the doors of the building that was not technically their place of business but might as well have been. He said it blandly, the way he might have said any essentially meaningless fact: "There's a vendor over there," or "Nice weather we're having."

      If he'd been hoping for a reaction to the accusation, he would have been disappointed. She just nodded and said, "I had things to do."

      There were a lot of responses he could have made to that, because he had a very good idea what things she'd had to do; but most of those observations were beneath him. Instead of any of those, he just asked as he strolled toward the front doors, "Your lover's back in town, huh?" Then, when he noticed she wasn't following him inside, "What?"

      "Don't you 'what' me," she didn't quite snap. "I heard that tone. What did that tone mean?"

      He raised his hands, palms out to show they were empty as he protested, "I didn't mean anything bad by it. It's just...you two have such a good relationship. It makes me think, is all. She loves you, and you love her, and that puts the two of you on the same side of every conflict that's come your way. Love is enough for you two."

      She gave him a look, held it for just a second longer than was necessary to be sure it sunk in, and then elaborated. "I'm going to ignore how drastically you're oversimplifying the amount of work we put into our relationship, and instead just wait for you to get to the point you're trying to make."

      He let that one pass, mostly because he was just a bit too morose to care about her lip and partly because he wasn't sure he didn't deserve it. "I just mean...Love wasn't enough for my father and me. We loved each other, but not enough to be able to...to bend. Maybe if I'd been more like you, we would have found some way to compromise, and I wouldn't have had to..." He trailed off, but it hadn't really been necessary for him to continue.

      The silence stretched out for two or three seconds while she was uncharacteristically quiet. After a moment, she ventured, in the cautious tones of a student who isn't sure she has the correct answer to the teacher's question, "So that tone means you're in a funk because you just now noticed that you're you instead of me?"

      There was a grain of truth in that assessment beneath the sarcasm. He couldn't deny it without lying to her, and he couldn't agree with it because it would hurt his pride to do so, so he shrugged, then asked, "What's so funny?"

      She flapped a hand at him while she tried to get herself under control, which she accomplished with great speed and greater difficulty. Carefully, as not to burst out laughing again in the middle of her sentence, she enunciated, "I have never seen you shrug before, and now that I have, I know why you don't do it, because you do not have the body for it."

      He thought he should probably be offended by that, but mostly he was just confused as to what she could have been talking about. He looked down himself for the answer, and when comprehension dawned, he heard a laugh of surprise bound out of his mouth. "Oh. Oh! Shut up," he suggested, though the commandment lost some of its weight because he was laughing as he issued it.

      "How about you try and make me?" she offered sweetly, and that time they laughed together, because no matter who you are (and sometimes because of who you are), sometimes it's fun to be childish.

      It wasn't that he didn't know how she'd manipulated him into laughing. He did; no matter what his mood, he couldn't help being aware of things like that. It was just that he didn't care. A laugh is a laugh no matter how much planning went into it, and anyway, that she could trick him like that at all was a big part of why he liked her. It made her much more than rare and only slightly less than unique.

      (She had her reasons for liking him too, but they changed so often there was no point in enumerating them. The list would be inaccurate by the time it was done.)

      His momentary melancholy dispelled, Luna and the Unconquered Sun walked into the Jade Pleasure Dome.

          The following post is from 2010 in a thread titled, "You Know What I Hate?" I feel the subject matter of the thread is obvious. I've edited this post somewhat to provide context that depended on the rest of the thread.

          ---

          You know what I hate?

          You may have heard of a bill before Congress to provide long-term health care for first responders to the Twin Towers, who are now suffering health issues related to the toxic fumes they inhaled on the job. It's named after someone who died from these medical problems, someone with a Russian-sounding name I have no hope of spelling or even remembering correctly. The Zdroiga Bill, or something.

          Many of the first responders have lost their jobs because they are unable to work, and most of those can't receive workers' comp because the bureau is fighting every claim. How can anyone prove that the throat cancer he has is caused by exposure to the toxins in the air at Ground Zero? As such, an unconscionable number of the people suffering these illnesses are becoming destitute because of the prohibitive costs of treatment.

          All that is a shameful indictment of our society, but it is not what I hate.

          This bill was up for ratification, but the Senate could not vote on it because of the Republican filibuster over extending the Bush tax cuts. The Republican party--the one that has made the last nine years standing on the shoulders of the tragedy that is 9/11--abandoned the people whose image they've been co-opting since 2001 because the Republican priority is protecting the wealthy and empowered. Since this bill will be paid for by closing a tax loophole that permits American companies in foreign countries to avoid paying taxes, I suspect they will shoot this bill down as well because their sponsors will demand it.

          The avarice here is so intense it's offensive (I'm tempted to call it blasphemous), and the blatant disregard the Republican party shows for its constituents is all the proof anyone needs that our government is willing to trample anyone and everyone who might possibly deprive the privileged few of their luxuries. I am not limiting my contempt to the Republican party, either. The Democrats love gay people when it's election season, but they've done precious little to grant gay rights, haven't they? The people we elect to represent us have no intention of ever doing so. We are paying them for services they will never render in return.

          All that is nauseating and makes me cringe to admit I'm American, but it is not what I hate.

          I feel completely helpless in the shadow of this staggering, monolithic evil. It is a moronic kind of dull malevolence unstoppable in its senselessness; a half-retarded bully belching troll-like laughter as he kicks kindergartners in the ass to send them sprawling whom the teachers ignore because he's been in the sixth grade for two years already and he'll probably be back next year. It is predation upon the American public, plain and simple, devoid of even the pretense of principle or higher purpose to mask the sheer greed for power held no longer as a means but rather a self-perpetuating end unto itself. As I contemplate how big and entrenched this kind of debasement is, a sense of futility so great I could fairly call it despair sweeps over me. There's no changing this, no way to spark outrage or rouse the public--in whom true power lies, if we'd only roll over on the metaphorical couch and do something--and in my darkest moments, I give serious thought to going Tibetan monk on it all and committing seppuku on the steps of Capitol Hill to try to draw attention to the issue. Hell, I've even composed the first draft of my suicide note in my head.

          All that is a kind of frightening commentary on how self-destruction is apparently the only way I can conceive of to express myself, but that is not what I hate.

          What I hate is that there are real problems in the world, in the country, in my state, in my city; real people suffering; real issues that need to be brought to light and discussed, but all pales in comparison to the physical immediacy of how miserable I feel because I can't get to sleep at night over the sound of the voices in my head arguing about whether going down on Supergirl would be too dangerous for your teeth because of her unbreakable pubic hairs shredding your teeth.

          Yeah. I hate that.

              The following post is also from the "You Know What I Hate" thread, though it's rather less old than the previous one.

              ---

              Originally posted by Me, to my representative in the Missouri State Congress
              Subject: A plea from a constituent

              Dear Representative Cauthorn,


              My name is Jim Ballard, and I am writing to you with a great sense of despair in my heart, an almost insurmountable amount of "what's the use" brought on by shame I feel for my home state right now. This is coupled with an admitted and sad ignorance on my part as to the proper procedure here; I'm honestly not sure that you're the correct person to be talking to about this issue, or how to find out who is, so if this is not in your arena, I hope you will be so kind as to inform me of the correct person to contact, that I may participate in the democratic process to try to salvage some sense of pride I otherwise might have had in being Missourian.

              This, then, is my grievance: I am staggeringly dismayed to learn that our state is considering passing a Don't Say Gay bill. Regardless of one's feelings on the morality or lack thereof in being homosexual or engaging in homosexual activities, which is a debate I think both sides are bound to lose because neither side will consider the other viewpoint, I believe that such a bill grossly misses the point of the government's role in society. The sole purpose of the government, I believe, is to act as a mediator in the event where two people exercising their rights come into conflict; to borrow a famous example, to decide whether your right to swing you fist supersedes the right of my nose not to be punched. If that's the case, then we can argue that the entire purpose of the government is actually to limit the rights and freedoms of the people it governs. Therefore, in order to pass laws morally, the legislative body must give grave and somber consideration to the amount of harm each law inflicts by limiting the rights of those whose actions would break that law, so that the greater right in the conflict is preserved.

              Representative Cauthorn, I submit to you that my rights as a straight man are in no conflict with the rights of a public school to discuss homosexuality. Nothing about a social studies class on gay culture takes anything away from me, nor threatens anything I possess. As such, passing this bill can only be immoral, because it restricts the rights of others to protect nothing at all; and if you'll forgive me for moving into a more personal area, I find it unbearably shameful that our state could stoop so low, could be so mean-spirited and flatly cruel, as to further marginalize an already trod-upon minority of people who, no matter what anyone thinks of their lifestyles or sexual practices, remain citizens of our state and nation and should therefore be afforded every protection the rest of us enjoy. Restricting the amount of information available to children in order to instill certain prejudices is a job is for parents, not for employees of the state, except where a demonstrable threat of harm exists, and no one has demonstrated that threat to me.

              Mr. Cauthorn, you are the representative of my district, so please, represent my interests in this. Defeat this ugly bill and restore some of my faith in the Republican party, in the democratic process, and in American nature in general. After watching the systematic and insidious disassembly of the freedoms we enjoy, sacrificed in the name of fear, I am emotionally exhausted from the effort of trying to believe that people can behave decently. I need some kind of validation of that belief to keep nihilism at bay. A person can't go on without, if not the sight of a light at the end of a tunnel, then at least the hope that such a light exists. I just--I need one sign that reasoned, ethical behavior can be rewarded. I am not challenging you or daring you but begging you when I ask you to please reward my faith here.

              I eagerly await your response, sir, and thank you for your consideration.

              Jim Ballard
              [contact information redacted]
              Originally posted by Representative Cauthorn, in response to me
              Mr. Ballard,

              Thank you very much for your heartfelt input. It will certainly give me something to consider for future discussions.

              [his signature]

              State Representative

              John Cauthorn

              District 021
