        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        I've had this nugget of an idea for a while now. What if you ran a DnD game where all the basic rules remain functionally the same...but everything is scaled down?

        A game where "Tiny" sized creatures are considered "Medium", and you scale everything else accordingly? Basically, what if you ran a DnD: Borrowers Edition?

        Playable races would be as follows:
        -Lilliputians: The aforementioned Borrower type people, who use the Human stats. Size: Medium.
        -Pixies: Basically Elves, but tinier. Hell, you could just MAKE them "Elves", but in the sense of pre-Tolkein elves that make shoes or cookies. Size: Medium.
        -Mice: Sapient mice, ala Redwall, Mouse Guard, or The Secret of Nimh. Thinking of using some of the Halfling stats. Size: Small.
        -Rats: Like Mice, but bigger, and probably with different abilities to differentiate them, according to popular conceptions of them. Size: Medium.
        -Sprites: Like Pixies, but with wings. All their racial traits are going into purchasing flight, with Ability scores geared towards spellcasting. Size: Medium.
        -Birds: Common small birds, because BIRBS. Might use Aaracokra stats. Size: Medium.
        -Frogs/Toads: Because I know some folks like Arnold Lobel's books about Frog and Toad. Imagining frogs and toads will be two subraces of the same creature. May or may not tap the Grung stats for this. Size: Small or Medium.
        -Toy: Humanoid children's toys, brought to life. Because we have to have someone fight the rats above, for Nutcracker-style antics. Size: Medium

        From there, it's just a matter of skinning existing mechanics to fit the smaller scale of everything. Monster stat blocks will employ logical conversions, like using Giant sized animal stats to represent normal sized critters. A Human being is just a Giant, to a mouse or Lilliputian. Nor did anyone say a Beholder needed to be measured in feet, rather than inches. Really, though, thinking about the various player races and what influences they draw from made me consider that this kind of campaign would draw A LOT from children's stories. Borrowers, Frog and Toad, Redwall, The Nutcracker, etc. Seems like I'm already running off a stylistic trend, which may bias tone, mood, and story.

        Then again, no one ever said some of these children's books weren't frightening and intense. And besides, DnD has never been one to shy away from action fantasy, which many of these works are or can be geared towards.

        Because of the scale, an entire campaign could be run in and around a single house. The interior walls and floors - depending on the nature of construction - could easily serve as an enormous and sprawling dungeon, complete with interesting use of verticality. Traveling down a human-sized street could be a big journey. And, given the tech level available to such small characters, it could technically be set in any time period. A borrower is going to swing around a sword (or rather a repurposed pen knife or sewing needle) to fight, whether he's in a medieval cottage or a modern urban apartment complex. The kind of vermin that will be their bread and butter opponents also don't change overmuch when industrialization kicks in. It's only really when folks start getting computers and cell phones that a tiny character like that is significantly straying from the level of power they can use. If only because a borrower who gets a smart phone could conceivably wreck some havoc on the macro scale as easily as a human with the same device.

        Thoughts and feelings?

        Here's A thing about sizes. The game is medium centric. You have your HP and the weapons list and spells do damage built around that scale. You make everyone small, and their weapons do less damage, but the small and smaller creatures still have the same HP, so a fight would take twice as long or more (no point yet, just saying)

        To get your flavor, do you want to just be small, or have the characters have to fight twice as long melee wise? (spells still do full damage)

        Seems like it would be easiest to keep the mechanics and character sizes as listed, but just double or more the height of everything around them (and maybe add a little HP to the new size catagories above them, but not reduce melee weapon damage

        This seems to me would be tons less adjustment to the stats for stuff as is, rather then completely change the medium centric rules on almost every page of the books

        Otherwise, you got a fine idea for a campaign

                                Age
                                Note your character’s age. The description for your
                                character’s ancestry in Chapter 2 gives some guidance

                                that, you can play a character of whatever age you like.
                                Your character’s age is a major factor that shapes how
                                she interacts with the world. There aren’t any mechanical
                                adjustments to your character for being particularly
                                old, but you might want to take it into account when
                                considering your starting ability scores and future
                                advancement; for instance, an old and wise character
                                might have a higher Wisdom score, so you might want
                                to make sure to put one of your free ability boosts in
                                Wisdom. Particularly young characters can change the
                                tone of some of the game’s threats, so it’s recommended
                                to play characters who are at least young adults.
                                So, appearantly, in the world of Pathfinder, aging doesn't have any detrimental effects on physique. You are just as hale and strong and flexible and fast in your seventies, like you were in your twenties...

                                Riiiiiiiiiight.

                                Look, I get it, aging sucks, I hate it too. But things like this seriously break immersion and suspension of disbelief for me. And don't come at me with "but there are dragons too" arguments, this isn't taking into account any magic.

What this is adding to the game? That some people could bump knuckles saying "yeaaaaah, down with agesim"? On the expense of believability?

                                Caveat: I'm fully aware that on some settings and in some games, some non-human races are/was like that, but these things are exceptions and actually important parts of those races' portrayal, because this phenomenon changes social and cultural dynamics a LOT.

                                            Yeah, the question should never be, "what does leaving this thing out of the game add to it?" but, "what does including this thing in the game add to it?" and, "Is what this thing adds to the game worth the additional cognitive load that comes with it?"

                                            Personally, for me, age-based stat modifiers arguably add some immersion, in that it mechanically reflects the fact that older folks might not be as strong or as nimble as they were when they were younger. They also harm immersion somewhat, in that it implies that older folks have keener senses than they did when they were younger. So, mixed bag. On top of that, it isn't really relevant in the vast majority of campaigns. I don't think I've ever played a D&D game where characters started out in one age bracket, and reached another over the course of play, unless timeskipping was involved. So, PF2's advice seems perfectly sufficient to me. If your character starts out old, you can choose to build them with an 8 or 10 in Strength, and/or Dexterity, and/or with a higher value in Wisdom and/or Intelligence than you might otherwise. If there's a timeskip, and you feel adjusting the stats would be an appropriate way to reflect the effects of your character aging, you can ask the DM to let you shuffle some Ability Boosts around. So, whatever immersion may be gained from age-based stat adjustments, to me, isn't worth the cognitive load. Your mileage may vary, of course.
