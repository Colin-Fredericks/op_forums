                                                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                    It's not hate, more that I'm curious about the whole thing with thanksgiving. Why do Americans commemorate the day their ancestors pretended to not be complete assholes about ethnicity, culture and religion to endear themselves to natives?

                                                    Being clear that my ancestors did pretty much the same thing here in Brazil. We just don't have a day to commemorate it, here it would be the First Mass in the country I think. It is literally the whole thanksgiving holiday that puzzles me.
                                                    So maybe a little historical context would help.

                                                    Abraham Lincoln made Thanksgiving a national holiday in the middle of the Civil War (1863) as a way to try and bring the Northern states together. The US had spent several years locked in an extremely bloody civil war, one that had ended up dragging on and being far more difficult than it had originally been sold as. Morale among many people in the North was pretty low at the time. There had also been some fallout over the Emancipation Proclamation (which had ostensibly freed all of the slaves in the rebellious southern states that were fighting against the northern states) that he had issued earlier that year.

                                                    There were quite a few politicians and others in Northern states who, while rejecting the southern states leaving the US, also didn't want to see slavery ending. The issue of slavery was more complicated than, "Northerners hate it, southerners love it." At the time, there was a small but slowly growing and vocal movement among people who rejected the federal government outlawing slavery, especially among various northern business owners who had been hoping to see slavery continue in some form in the south after the war ended. Some of these people were suggesting a second seccession and that some of the Northern states should leave the Union and join the Confederacy or at least that the US should end war and accept the existence of the Confederacy. Of course those ideas never went anywhere, but Lincoln was keenly aware that there were rifts growing among the people who were still part of the United States.

                                                    Also, at the time, there weren't very many "United States" Holidays - there were things like Christmas and Easter but those were religious holidays that had been brought to the US from Europe. George Washington had issued a couple of "Thanksgiving proclamations" asking people to take a day to show thanks to God and come together, and a few other Presidents had done so after that. But it hadn't been an official holiday. Lincoln looked back to this and decided to create a 100% United States holiday that traced back to an event that happened in the United States would help to create more of a sense of brotherhood among the remaining states of the US, which had until that point, been facing an extremely difficult and bloody war with the South. There was this myth, even at the time, about the "First Thanksgiving" that Native Americans and settlers had put aside their differences, come together in peace and celebrated together, and that myth sort of worked with what Lincoln wanted, which was for Northerners to put aside their disagreements and come together in the middle of a war.

                                                    You can see it in the transcript of Lincoln's proclamation when he declared Thanksgiving to be a national holiday.

                                                    By the President of the United States

                                                    A Proclamation

                                                    The year that is drawing toward its close has been filled with the blessings of fruitful fields and
                                                    healthful skies. To these bounties, which are so constantly enjoyed that we are prone to forget the
                                                    source from which they come, others have been added, which are of so extraordinary a nature
                                                    that they cannot fail to penetrate and even soften the heart which is habitually insensible to the
                                                    ever-watchful providence of Almighty God.

                                                    In the midst of a civil war of unequaled magnitude and severity, which has sometimes seemed to
                                                    foreign states to invite and provoke their aggressions, peace has been preserved with all nations,
                                                    order has been maintained, the laws have been respected and obeyed, and harmony has prevailed
                                                    everywhere, except in the theater of military conflict; while that theater has been greatly
                                                    contracted by the advancing armies and navies of the Union.

                                                    Needful diversions of wealth and of strength from the fields of peaceful industry to the national
                                                    defense have not arrested the plow, the shuttle, or the ship; the ax has enlarged the borders of our
                                                    settlements, and the mines, as well of iron and coal as of the precious metals, have yielded even
                                                    more abundantly than heretofore. Population has steadily increased, notwithstanding the waste
                                                    that has been made in the camp, the siege, and the battlefield, and the country, rejoicing in the
                                                    consciousness of augmented strength and vigor, is permitted to expect continuance of years with
                                                    large increase of freedom.

                                                    No human counsel hath devised, nor hath any mortal hand worked out these great things. They
                                                    are the gracious gifts of the Most High God, who while dealing with us in anger for our sins,
                                                    hath nevertheless remembered mercy.

                                                    It has seemed to me fit and proper that they should be solemnly, reverently, and gratefully
                                                    acknowledged as with one heart and one voice by the whole American people. I do, therefore,
                                                    invite my fellow-citizens in every part of the United States, and also those who are at sea and
                                                    those who are sojourning in foreign lands, to set apart and observe the last Thursday of
                                                    November next as a Day of Thanksgiving and Praise to our beneficent Father who dwelleth in
                                                    the heavens. And I recommend to them that, while offering up the ascriptions justly due to Him
                                                    for such singular deliverances and blessings, they do also, with humble penitence for our national
                                                    perverseness and disobedience, commend to His tender care all those who have become widows,
                                                    orphans, mourners, or sufferers in the lamentable civil strife in which we are unavoidably
                                                    engaged, and fervently implore the interposition of the Almighty hand to heal the wounds of the
                                                    nation, and to restore it, as soon as may be consistent with the Divine purposes, to the full
                                                    enjoyment of peace, harmony, tranquility, and union.

                                                    In testimony whereof, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the seal of the United Stated
                                                    States to be affixed.

                                                    Done at the city of Washington, this third day of October, in the year of our Lord one thousand
                                                    eight hundred and sixty-three, and of the Independence of the United States the eighty-eighth.

                                                    Abraham Lincoln
                                                    So yes, the concept of Thanksgiving was extremely politically motivated due to an ongoing civil war and it specifically used and reinforced a myth of American exceptionalism and also pulled in some ideas about different groups putting aside their differences and coming together peacefully and then tried to present that as a basic foundational element of what America was about.

                                                    *EDIT* Sorry, this post ended up a bit longer than I had intended.

                                                    Also to note, Thanksgiving, as originally envisioned, was an entirely religious holiday for giving thanks to God for the blessings the citizens and nation had received. If you were to go back and look at the proclamations from Washington and others, I don't believe you'd find a single mention of Pilgrims or native people's. At some point, Thanksgiving became a secular holiday. I can't say why for certain but if I had to guess, I'd point at the US education system.

                                                    In the US public schools are ostensibly secular. And Thanksgiving was a 100% US original holiday. But there are a lot of limits to how you can teach and use the holiday in schools if it's religious. So educators began pushing the idea that it was a holiday commemorating a lunch a couple of native Americans and some Pilgrims had. Over time, this is how people have learned about the holiday and at this point most people think it's about commemorating a specific event, even if that wasn't how it was originally envisioned.

                                                        This reminds me of a similar holiday we have here, at least in function. Tiradentes.

                                                        Brazil never had, during colonial times, a notion of being a singularity in any sense. As a result we didn't had a unified front for independence. For a time we had many such movements across the place with different colonies trying to achieve independence for themselves in the model of the Spanish colonies.

                                                        One such movement, the Inconfidência Mineira (Minas Conspiracy), failed royally, in the crib, but under trial only one member took full responsibility for the whole plot, Joaquim José da Silva Xavier, known as Tiradentes (Tooth Puller). He was sentenced to death, hanged, cut, and his pieces exhibited in the areas where he used to proclaim liberal ideas.

                                                        When the independence in fact came, by the hands of a monarch, Brazil was declared an Empire, so the idea was still of a unified monarchy governing many distinct lands without a national identity.

                                                        So when we became a Republic, separatist tensions ran rampant. The many states had a lot of disagreements, there was civil unrest in several parts, the government did favored some regions over others, small sedition movements blossomed overnight. The transition from Monarchy to Republic by itself had been somewhat pacific, but that also means that mostly the administrative and government institutions remained in place instead of giving true independence to the states.

                                                        At some point they decided that we had to promote an actual Brazilian Identity. This is something that didn't quite existed until then. That's indeed still ongoing project, which involved many interventions through the education system, the media, and the creation of a narrative of Brazilian heroes. On the holiday front, along with the Proclamation Day, they made Tiradentes' death into a holiday as Martyr of the Independence.

                                                        Yet this is quite a secular tale anyway, and never developed into a meal or something, we kind of just don't work in this day, which is our standard for holidays. I think yours too, AFAIK.
