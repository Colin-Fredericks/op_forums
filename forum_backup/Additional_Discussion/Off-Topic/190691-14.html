                                        Had this idea for a superhero game this morning in the shower, wanna get it down someplace before I lose too much of it.

                                        AFTERMATH - part Watchmen, part Incredibles, part Wanted.

                                        PREMISE:
                                        There is a gene that gets activated in some which grants them superpowers. Others just put on costumes and go out to make the world a better place. Since there's only a 0.5% chance that someone's "meta gene" will activate, either spontaneously or thru exterior influences (random lab accidents and exposure to unusual substances, y'know, the usual origin story stuff), the superhero population has always remained small. Superheroes and costumed adventurers have been around since just before WWII. During the war, they were very popular, going out and fighting the Axis and such. Afterwards, they still remained favorable in the public eye until the mid 1960s. With the Baby Boomers came that rejection of the establishment and some of the heroes were seen as part of that establishment. Others seemed to be on the side of the anti-establishment. By the mid 1970s, enough incidents with the heroes had occurred that they were very much out of favor with the public. In 1982, the President and Congress passed the Superhuman Restriction Act. The heroes had 3 choices: retire, become part of a governmental organization that could use them (law enforcement or military), or become a criminal. One of the smarter super-scientists had even developed a drug to suppress the powers for those who wished to retire or were arrested and imprisoned. The bulk of the heroes simply retired, most already up there in years or simply soured by the public hatred. Many took the "legitimate" route and joined police or military where they could continue to serve. Some decided that if they were going to be branded criminals anyway, they might as well be super-VILLAINS.

                                        And that's where the whole mess went wrong. There were already super-villains and they didn't care if they were breaking one more law. Sure, a lot of the ones with just silly gimmicks were taken down quickly once they had the full might of the National Guard and such sent after them. But the old line about "survival of the fittest" proved itself out. Those that remained were the smartest, toughest and/or craziest of the bunch. They were more successful and frequently left a bigger body count behind since they knew what was in store for them should they get caught. These were the guys that simply were too good at being bad to be stopped with even the help of the remaining heroes in their now legitimate roles.

                                        And this is where the player characters come in: A handful of heroes took a fourth option - keep fighting the good fight, just keep out of the public eye. While the chances of a meta gene activating are small, if either or both of the parents have/had an active meta gene, their chances skyrocket up to 40%. So, the children and grandchildren of the heroes are out there with powers of their own and a choice to be made: let the bad guys keep making a mess of things or take the risk of becoming another Most Wanted by fighting them.

                                        - Those that can give up essential liberties in exchange for temporary safety deserve neither liberty nor safety. -- Benjamin Franklin

                                                Hummm,,, Since we're discussing Superhero games/ideas.... Here's one I had a couple years ago now.
                                                edit: unfortunately the system is automatically splitting the last chunk into two spoilers for some reason, can't seem to resolve, sorry about that.

                                                Intro
                                                You are sitting in a second rate lounge of a south american rat-hole bar in June of 1955. Around you sit an assortment of drunk, run down and exhausted people, from former housewives to former soldiers, but you all have three things in common, you're all here to join up with a mercenary group long on pro-America and short on sense called Black Wolf, you're all "super" powered, and you have little choice but to be here.
                                                It's so hot you can see a plastic hula-girl decoration slowly melting into a radio blaring an American news broadcast from weeks ago behind the bar. You've had showers less humid than the air in this place... and you're hoping it will be your new home. The Freak Registration Acts passed last month, that's why you're here... hell it's even what the radio is talking about. Oh sure, it has a big long name, and variations in every western nation of the world... The Mandatory Extraordinary Entities & People Logistical Enumeration act... but that doesn't change what it is. It's a leash and a kennel... and maybe a bullet to the brain.
                                                Ten years ago, people like you were war heroes, saviors of the world. A time when a handful of American Super Soldiers brought World War II to a close with a decisive attack on two major Japanese cities. Two years ago most the world hated and feared you after the events of the Korean War. Now, you're all registered like good little pets, and required to work only for approved companies, most of which work hand-in-glove with the US or other western governments. Working with regular people is asking for trouble for a "super". Any crime you're around is likely your fault. If someone tries to beat you up in a bar, it's your fault. Someone smashes your windows and eggs your car? Yep, your fault. This is one reason you opted to seek employment as far away as possible. Better to keep moving, and stick to areas where news doesn't travel so fast.
                                                The Freak Registration act caused a second problem though, the other reason you're here. A market glut. So many super people, so few jobs... even the government can't absorb you all. Your kind can't go on the dole anymore, you can't work most jobs, and if you become a "burden to society" you are likely to end up in a lab somewhere being studied. Hence this place, this last hope, and not a great one. They're only taking a handful of people. There are at least 20 people in this bar in various states of getting drunk, all just as desperate to work for a third rate mercenary outfit.


                                                Radio broadcast
                                                The following is a transcript from the NBC evening news broadcast of Thursday March 31, 1955 from their studios in New York City, New York, USA.


                                                Good evening viewers, and welcome to the NBC nightly news. Tonight's top story is the one that is on everyone's mind, the Mandatory Extraordinary Entities & People Logistical Enumeration act, or as it's more commonly called the Freak Registration act goes into it's one month grace period tomorrow. As a result, there has been stepped up security around the country as protests and celebrations divide this nation on the future of those that are now being called Meeples. Both of these public displays are likely to continue until the end of April when the grace period closes and indeed even beyond.

                                                But why is this topic so divisive to our nation, one founded on freedom and equality? Why was the act created and what does it mean for the future?

                                                To understand the love-hate relationship the nation has with Meeple, one must look back over a decade to the end of the Second World War. During the war, all nations became focused on developing better weapons technologies, this of course reached it's peak with the American development of the now infamous Alpha Strike and Beta Blast strike teams who were deployed to Tokyo and Kyoto after the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki failed to produce a swift end to the war. This was widely hailed as a fantastic success, and even marked a milestone in women's rights as it was the first time a woman was deliberately deployed as part of a front-line combat unit, and the fame of Lieutenant Mary Stars of Alpha Strike carries forward to this day. To this day we know that the Japanese surrender on September 2nd 1945 could have been much delayed to the loss of Japan and America alike had we not deployed them.

                                                However, much like the fearsome Nuclear Device, development of super-soldiers continued on after the war, from the attempts of American Scientists to focus on forces that could defend America to the illegal and highly dangerous experiments of the Soviet Union to develop unstoppable chaotic killing machines continued, sometimes in public but often in secret.

                                                During this time, a number of catastrophic events happened to the world that were largely hidden from the public at large, only for information about them to emerge in the last two years, from contact with alien species, to strange magic and even miracles happening around the world. Many of these events turned more people into these Super Men.

                                                Over the years following the end of the war, many of these Super Men took to donning disguises, to promote vigilante justice and in some cases, crime. Apparently inspired by pulp comics, an overwhelmed nation passed the now famous Anti-Cape laws, that ban such actions, as well as the production of the pulp comics that encourage them. This resulted in significant pro-cape backlash, which in turn spawned new waves of clamping down on supporters, leading to the now famous arrest of comic writer Stanley Lieber for aiding and abetting criminals and promoting communism with his underground pulp comics.

                                                But where does the love of the people turn to hate for a whole class of people? For that we must look to the Korean Conflict. Like all true citizens, the Super Men volunteered for service when the Conflict came around, many others joined through the selective service process. Expecting a quick end to the War, America deployed the Super Men in force after the communists threw the hoards of China behind North Korea's bid to take over the peninsula. Expecting to be able to put down the fighting quickly and liberate the country the American Super Soldiers lead by General Stripes, former leader of Alpha Strike encountered the very soul of terror, Communist Super Mutants. This brought the war to a close quickly, but not in the liberation of the country. North Korea and South Korea are now forever separated by a wasteland, a demilitarized zone hundreds of miles across, that serves are a grim reminder of the loss of life.

                                                This lead to the tide of support for Supers in western society to sour rapidly. This was greatly accelerated with the post-Korea Scarlet Witch trials in which the ability of such beings to not just do great feats of strength or military might but to warp the very minds of anyone was revealed. This reached it's peak last year and led to the unopposed passage of the Freak Registration Act as well as similar legislation in most western nations.

                                                Where do we as a nation go from here? What does this law mean for the MEEPLE? We'll tell you after the break.

                                                {Commercial Break}

                                                http://www.oldtimeradiofans.com/old_radio_commercials/

                                                {end break}

                                                Welcome back to the NBC Nightly Radio News. Before the break we discussed the origins of the events leading up to the MEEPLE act. Now we'll answer what exactly IS the MEEPLE act and what does it mean for the average citizen and for the MEEPLEs? Simply put, the MEEPLE act is the mandatory registration of unusual powers with the government and an international body, as well as the organization of government and private organizations that have proven themselves trustworthy enough to employ such potent forces. This helps to safeguard the citizens from undesired attacks from criminal MEEPLEs as well as providing guidance and organization to the MEEPLEs in what is frequently a very difficult time in their lives, as their powers emerge. This is of course no more invasive to a MEEPLE's freedom than having to fill out a draft card, with the added benefit of having a dedicated job hunt organization working for them.

                                                What does it mean going forward? Broadly speaking, any MEEPLE refusing registration is considered a criminal, and will be treated as such. However thanks to the far superior numbers of civic-minded MEEPLEs there are people with Extraordinary capabilities to respond to such threats. More importantly, thanks to such experimental policing efforts such as New York City's new Special Weapons And Tactics force, composed primarily of MEEPLEs, even the every day citizen is expected to gain the benefits of having MEEPLEs protecting them, even while the government shields you from the dangers that they may present through carefully engineered systems of checks and balances.
