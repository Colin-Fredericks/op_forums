    38) ​Marnodil Starcrown (Fallen Aasimar Warlock, Old One Pact):

    Okay, so what happens when a Celestial pledges fealty to a denizen of the Far Realm in exchange for great power? Of course you don't know the answer to that, because it's never happened before. But that didn't stop Marnodil from wanting to find out! Fascinated by the power of his divine heritage, Marnodil became obsessed with exploring its upper limits, most notably how well that power measured up to the denizens of the multiverse.

    Being half outsider, himself, Marnodil had surprisingly little effort with contacting the outer planes. Sometimes, these encounters would result in pleasant experiences, and other times they would be not so pleasant ones. But whether good or bad, Marnodil always managed to pull through, and that degree of luck (he insists that it was skill) only continued to feed his ego. Until finally, his pride and curiosity grew beyond his ability to control. Marnodil's last attempt to explore the planes brought him to the chaotic, incomprehensible reality that was the Far Realm. Generally speaking, Angels are creatures of lawfulness and order, so when Marnodil's found himself surrounded by a form of reality that was completely beyond his comprehension, it would be safe to say that his grasp on sanity was noticeably shaken. Had he been completely human, he would probably have collapsed into a drooling, catatonic mess and remained that way for the rest of his life. But this was not the first time Marnodil had encountered entities of chaos, and he was miraculously able to endure the experience.

    That was when his soon to be patron first spoke to him and offered the Aasimar a bargain. All the power and knowledge that he craved would be granted to him, in exchange for an oath of service. Before he realized what he was doing, Marnodil voiced his agreement and turned around to see... actually, he doesn't remember what he saw, because he was now plummeting through the sky of the Prime Material Plane, impacting the ground with enough force to leave a smoking crater in his wake! A couple hours later, Marnodil woke up, climbed out of the hole and dusted himself off, his mind endowed with forbidden knowledge that neither man nor angel were meant to possess!

    Despite his fall from grace and his bargain with the incomprehensible being from beyond the stars, Marnodil clings to his Aasimar sense of goodness. It just happens to be clouded over by a twinge of insanity. Marnodil's alignment is Chaotic Good, with an "ends justify the means" philosophy towards accomplishing his goals. His patron has also granted him the Pact of the Tome, recording his eldritch knowledge within a Book of Shadows.

        39) Sebek-khu, Returner from the Deep [Human, Shadow Sorcerer]:
        The means of entering the Egyptian afterlife is kind of unfair, really. If one's body is not preserved, one's spirit - one's Ba - has no place to reside. One is barred from the Western Lands - a paradise in the form of idealized Egypt itself - and must therefore dwell permanently in the waters of Duat. Mummification is needed to avoid that dark, watery fate. Thankfully, even the poorest Egyptian can have their body buried in the hot desert sands after death, preserving their corpse well enough.

        Assuming, of course, one had loved ones to dig the hole. Sebek-khu was an orphan by circumstance, and a hermit by inclination. He had no parents, wife, children, friends, or even neighbors to rely upon. So it took several weeks before anyone discovered his moldering cadaver where it had expired, due to sickness. Naturally, Sebek-khu was hosed, and his spirit dwelled for a time in Duat. Blind, paralyzed, and waterlogged. Unable to move or speak or even breathe, for no one had bundled spells with his burial place to allow him to do so. He could merely float among the other damned souls, and wait out eternity.

        That is, until one night. Ra's great boat sailed down the river towards the East, as it did every day. It was the only brief light the drowning dead could enjoy. And as always, Ra and Set were engaged in battle with their eternal foe, Apothis. As the boat drew next to Sebek-khu, Set was knocked senseless, and Apothis saw the opportunity to attack Ra. Some part of Sebek-khu yet believed in goodness - or perhaps he simply knew that Ra's death would result in the loss of what little light Sebek-khu could look forward to every night. With a titanic force of will, Sebek-khu broke through his paralysis with improvised magic, and crawled into the prow of the ship. His ineffectual flailing was sufficient to distract Apothis, long enough for Set to regain his footing and drive the serpent away. In recognition for his bravery and service (however slight), Ra offered Sebek-khu the chance to escape his fate, by becoming a guardian for the living world. Naturally, the dead hermit leaped at any opportunity to avoid returning to the waters of Duat.

        Amid the remains of more "proper" guardians, Sebek-khu's mummy appeared quite suddenly, his wrappings stained black from immersion in Duat's waters. Moreover, the years in Duat have painted Sebek-khu with its qualities, endowing him with mighty shadow magic. He may even summon his Khaibit - or shadow-soul - in the form of a black jackal, that hounds and harries the enemies of Egypt.

        More than anything, Sebek-khu dreads the idea of returning to Duat. He would do anything to avoid going back. However, his time in Duat has also afforded him insights into it, and to the many monsters that dwell there and gnaw on the spirits of the damned. Moreover, his personal dread of Duat is extended to others; where previously he wanted nothing to do with his fellow inmates in the household of humanity, he now seeks to aid them.

        Specifically, he goes to great pains to preserve mummies, as he would not wish Duat on anyone. For this reason, he takes personal offense to the English-speaking world, when time progresses far enough that he encounters it. The Egyptomania that grips Europe and other places will do great harm, with callous "modern" people defiling mummified remains, making them into paper, medicine, and even fuel for steam engines. Countless souls, consigned the dark waters, for the amusement and convenience of foreign bastards! By this point, of course, Sebek-khu will have grown into a powerful Sorcerer. Woe to the 19th century, that this dark mummy will arise!

        (Yes, in Real Life, the idea of mummies burned as railroad fuel is likely a myth started as a joke by Mark Twain. In the DnD version of history, though, who's to say it wasn't true?)

            40) Bek, Simple Cupbearer? [Human, Way of Shadows Monk]:
            Bek's inclusion into the ranks of the guardians seemed dubious to most people. He was just a cup bearer for royalty. He bore cups for the Pharaoh. That was it. And if the Pharaoh needed someone to hold his cup, mummifying a servant was unnecessary. That's what shabti were for.

            The choice begins to become clearer when one is privy to the knowledge that Bek was also a covert bodyguard, spymaster, and assassin. Trained from a young age in martial arts and stealth, Bek's duty was to act as the formidable fighter that hid in plain sight. Everyone paid attention to the Pharaoh, and many paid attention to his guards. No one noticed the servant in the background, bringing the Pharaoh his drinks. No one cared where in the palace he roamed. And no one bothered to quiet their voices around servants when speaking of treason. When necessary, Bek could spring to his Pharaoh's defense - for he was always armed with his fists - or quietly remove threats to his rule in the cover of darkness.

            So when it came to pass that Bek outlived his master, the priests (who knew most than most) offered to allow him to continue protecting the Pharaoh for all eternity. Bek, who had known no other existence except this, agreed.

            It's a choice that he comes to question as centuries of service turn into millennia. Did he choose correctly? Or was he merely afraid of change? For a man who had no fear of death, the thought of living frightened him more. Bek comes to ask if service unending is really what he wants to do with himself. Or if he'd rather look for a way to follow his own path, and really live for the first time in thousands of years. Maybe he wants to cease being a servant, and become his own master.

                41) Dedyet, Not Dead Yet [Human, Necromancer Wizard]:
                Ever since her parents perished in a fire, Dedyet was fascinated by death. Fascinated, and slightly afraid. When she was taken in by an educated friend of the family, her fear turned to obsession, as she was instructed in the ways of magic. Studying the ways of Anubis and Thoth, Dedyet became a court magician due to raw skill and intelligence, despite the majority of magicians in the imperial court being Arcane Clerics (for indeed, in Egypt, priests and wizards were often the same thing). Just as she began to make a name for herself, though, Dedyet began to suffer a wasting illness, that would almost certainly be her end. The priests of court, jealous of her rising star, deliberately avoided curing her...or at least, that's what she thought. In her weakness and frustration, she was willing to blame anyone.

                It wasn't fair. She still had so much more research to do. So many mysteries to tackle, so many magic spells to learn. It wasn't fair, gods damn it!

                Knowing her body would soon give out, Dedyet sought a stopgap measure. So she made the radical choice to volunteer to become a tomb guardian. With any luck, service unending would buy her the time she needed to find a more permanent solution to the problem of mortality. A way to not cycle between life and death, but to suspend herself somewhere between. Maybe then, she could actually get some work done. Her only regret is that, as a mummy, she only gets resurrected when the Maat needs her. So many centuries of time wasted, that she could be spending in research.

                Dedyet is sharp, ambitious, and possessed of a sardonic, black humor. She's also a fidgety sort, her hands constantly needing to occupy themselves with something. It's rare that she isn't absentmindedly playing with amulets, the hems of her clothing, locks of hair, or her necklace of teeth (not all of which are from animals). The disease that plagued her in her first life was extinguished in death, but the damage to her body was already done. The wizard is a frail and sickly sort, though she refuses to allow her poor stamina to stop her from seeking knowledge. If she has to, she can always animate the dead to carry her around.

                Given that completing a mission means being drawn back to the tomb, to return to the sleep of ages, Dedyet does what she can to stall or drag her feet. She'd never get the time to do research if she rushed from job to job. That's a waste of time, especially since the knowledge she wants could be around one century and be lost the next! No matter the setbacks, though, Dedyet persists. She WILL conquer death, one way or another.

                    42) ​Ahsoha: It is common for purebloods among the Yuan-Ti to be deployed among warm blooded society as covert agents, but few approach this assignment with the same level of flair and style that Ahsoha demonstrates. As a way to personally amuse herself while on the job, Ahsoha joined the entertainment industry and quickly rose to stardom. She may have also had a hand in sabotaging the careers of other aspiring performers, but nothing has been officially proven, yet.

                    As both a Yuan-Ti and a member of the College of Satire, Ahsoha presents herself around others as if she were a higher being. Frequently speaking in the third person and making outlandish demands from those wishing to hire her services. She once threatened to cancel a major performance because the house's staff had failed to separate the mixed assortment of candies into their own different colored bowls, which they were contractually obligated to do! Ahsoha's true performance is mocking the lofty perceptions with which those among high society view themselves. Unfortunately, Ahsoha is herself a member of high society entertainment, which means the joke is often missed in favor of her other talents.

                    Due to her profession, blending in with normal society is no problem for Ahsoha. She has plenty of makeup and skin care products which can be used to hide her features from casual inspections and she always has some form of dark tinted eye wear while in public. Without the aid of cosmetics, Ahsoha's skin is covered in tiny, green scales, while her eyes are pale blue and have slit shaped pupils. Her silky hair is jet black in color and reaches her shoulders in length. Yuan-Ti physiology has given her quite the sex appeal, with a slender, curvaceous body that seems unnaturally flexible when performing on stage.

                    Background: Entertainer (Singing, Acting, Dancing)

                    Alignment: Neutral Evil

                    Race: Yuan-Ti Pureblood

                    Gender: Female

                    Age: 22

                    Class: Bard, College of Satire

                        43) ​Zushim Whitelock: What separates gods from men, when both wield the power of creation? This question burns within the mind of Zushim, who has devoted his life to the study of transmutation and how it may be applied to the world around him. Who could possibly deny his claim to divinity, once the power to reshape the world is at his finger tips?

                        Zushim's early years were spent under the tutelage of an arch mage by the name of Omaceran. Being an elf, his master had centuries worth of magical experience to draw from. Unfortunately, Zushim was human, which means it would have been impossible for him to learn all of his master's secrets within his natural life span. Instead, he decided to focus on teaching him a narrower spectrum, one which Zushim seemed particularly keen on learning. Much to both their surprise, Zushim turned out to be a prodigy in the art of transmutation and quickly developed an obsession with it. As he grew older, Omaceran eventually deemed Zushim worthy enough to graduate from his studies and take to the road as a journeyman wizard. Although he was initially reluctant to leave, Zushim realized that, while powerful, Omaceran did not share his philosophical views on the subject. Thus, he struck out on his own, seeking to hone his craft beyond the limits of his master's teachings.

                        Since leaving his master's tutelage, Zushim has fallen in with the adventurer crowd and has worked with a couple parties in the past. Completely devoted to the pursuit of knowledge, Zushim cares little about the party's moral compass, so long as their actions lead to further discoveries he can benefit from. As such, Zushim has found himself working alongside both the forces of good and evil depending on what side he deems appropriate for the situation.

                        Background: Sage (Wizard's Apprentice)

                        Alignment: True Neutral

                        Race: Human

                        Gender: Male

                        Age: 21

                        Class: Wizard, Transmuter

                            44) ​Otixan Nalmic:

                            The only thing more dangerous than a rampaging psychopath is one who knows how to focus their wrath on achieving a specific goal. Otixan is one of these rare individuals who has learned the secret of focusing his rage towards satisfying his objective, instead of lashing out at everything around him in a maddened frenzy.

                            His claim to fame and power started amidst the criminal underworld, where Otixan worked as a contract killer. So long as his employers were both willing and able to pay the coin, he was willing to eliminate problems on their behalf. It may not have been very glamorous, but it was a steady stream of income for many years. That is until the sudden, but inevitable betrayal that typically befalls people in his line of work, when a particular client sought to use him as a scapegoat to take the fall for the death of one of their major rivals. The problem isn't that Otixan was innocent of the crime, for he actually was the one responsible for killing the target. No, the thing that upsets him is that he was never paid for his work, because the client had alerted the authorities and have him captured at the scene of the crime. After spending 10 years in prison for murder charges, Otixan managed to escape and pay his old client a visit, where he proceeded to dispose of them in a gruesome fashion.

                            Afterward, he decided that working on his own was no longer as beneficial as it had been in the past. So he began to ally himself with parties of adventurers who didn't mind performing illicit jobs in exchange for coin. The next time a client decides to renege on a contract, Otixan is going to have some back up on his side!

                            Background: Criminal (Hired Killer)

                            Alignment: Lawful Evil

                            Race: Black Dragonborn

                            Gender: Male

                            Age: 34

                            Class: Barbarian / Monk

                                45) Kulnar:

                                Since the day he was born, Kulnar never really fit in with the other members of his tribe. While most Orcs grew up to be huge, burly warriors, Kulnar seemed to be the runt. He wasn't as big and strong as his half dozen siblings, nor was he prone to reckless displays of aggression. Indeed, Kulnar seemed to be remarkably intelligent when compared to the majority of his tribesmen. The only ones who seemed to possess more insight were those with mystical abilities. Believing they could shed some light as to why he was so different from the others, Kulnar decided to spend time around these magically inclined Orcs in hopes of learning their ways.

                                They revealed to him that Kulnar was not a true Orc, but rather a half breed who would never be as physically gifted as the more pure blooded tribesmen. But recognizing his capacity and willingness to learn, they acknowledged his potential affinity for magic and began to instruct him in the ancient secrets passed down throughout the tribe for countless generations. Kulnar learned how to commune with the land around him, drawing power from the natural world and using that power to offset his lack of physical might. For a time, life was good, as Kulnar had finally begun to make himself useful to the tribe, instead of taking up space and resources. But as is usually the case with Orcs, this happiness was nott o last.

                                Kulnar's tribe had made a recurring habit out of raiding nearby villages for supplies and territory. Eventually, they had run out of any villages within the nearby area and were forced to expand further and further into civilized lands to maintain their resources. That was what prompted the lord of the land to finally take action. When the tribe foolishly attempted to raid a fully populated town, they were met with a militia of soldiers and knights who decimated their forces and pursued those who attempted to retreat back to their camps. Kulnar was not present at this time, as he had gone deep into the wilderness to pursue a vision quest. By the time he returned, his tribe lay in ruins, the camp had been burned to the ground and the severed heads of their leaders were placed upon pikes surrounding the perimeter. Some of them had even been his teachers, now turned into gruesome warning signs that raiding would no longer be tolerated. Gripping his spear firmly in hand, Kulnar turned from the camp and set off into the heart of civilization, hoping that someone would find his magical talents to be of some use.


                                Background: Outlander (Tribal Marauder)


                                Alignment: Neutral Evil


                                Race: Half Orc


                                Gender: Male


                                Age: 16


                                Class: Druid


                                Druid Circle: Circle of the Land (Grassland)

                                        47) Lavender:

                                        The story of a puppet with its strings cut, Lavender was constructed to entertain the masses by competing in grand duels against other fighters in the arena. She didn't always win, but her artificial body performed well enough that her master saw fit to keep her in operable condition for over a decade worth of service. And yet, no matter how skilled she was, Lavender was still only a construct. She had no personality outside of what her masters designated for her. That is until the day she drew the attention of the fairy folk.

                                        Even now, Lavender still had no idea what she did to draw the attention of the Archfey, but while she was in the repair shop from one of her more recent battles, she was visited by a woman dressed in blue robes. With a simple touch of her finger, Lavender was endowed with self awareness, obtaining free will in the process! As can probably be expected for a mindless entity who had suddenly gained the capacity to think, Lavender became immediately obsessed with exploring the full scope of everything life had to offer. Unfortunately, her master did not feel the same way, angrily trying to reassert their dominance over her. Reacting on instinct honed from years of battling in the arena, Lavender retaliated, killing him in the process. After which, the blue clad woman graciously offered to help guide her along the path of life if she would agree to perform the occasional service for her. Lavender, having no alternative options that she could think of at the time, agreed to the bargain and became a Fey pact Warlock!

                                        Unlike the stern and regimented members of her kind, Lavender's personality is wild and passionate. She is quite literally a teenager who has only recently begun to explore the world she lives in. As such, it is safe to say she has poor impulse control and is prone to making brash decision based on her emotions. Of course, it doesn't help that her patron is a member of the Archfey, who aren't exactly known for adhering to common sense, either.

                                        In terms of appearance, Lavender is mostly humanoid in shape, with a body composed out of pale colored wood and encased inside of composite plating. The signature plating is lavender colored, from which her name derives. She stands 6 feet tall, with an athletic, female body type of 265 lbs with glowing blue eyes. When invoking the gifts of her patron, Lavender's pact weapon takes the form of a bastard sword.

                                            48) ​Tinares Stormhorns:

                                            Once a figure commanding great respect and prestige, Tinares has now become a bastion of terror and barbarism. A former knight of his homeland, Tinares grew increasingly frustrated by the rules and regulations those of his station were expected to abide by. He was, in some official capacity, a member of the nobility, yet the tenets of his order forbid him from truly enjoying the benefits that such a position might otherwise afford him. It didn't make any sense, none of the nobles who abstained from the field of battle had to follow a similar conduct, so why should he? It was questions like this which drove Tinares into the clutches of the demon lord, Baphomet, who tempted the young knight with a life of self indulgence.

                                            Tinares' descent into evil was gradual, but mortifying once others began to take notice of his shifting behavior. At first, he would tempt fate on a monthly basis, then move on to weekly acts of unlawfulness. After a while, his crimes became daily, then hourly, until he eventually threw aside all pretenses of honor and gave himself over to whatever whims of the moment he had. After finally managing to arest him, Tinares was stripped of his official knighthood and sentenced to death for his crimes. But the demon lord of the endless maze had promised Tinares freedom, so freedom he would have. Rather than accept the court's judgement, Tinares lashed out like a wild animal, slaughtering the judge, the jury and whatever guards tried to stop him. He then stormed off towards the city gates shouting that he would no longer be restrained by the laws of society.

                                            As a minotaur, Tinares believes himself to be especially favored by lord Baphomet, although the demon's actual opinion on the matter remains unknown. After all, nobody is particularly willing to contact him for the purpose of asking about his views on one random servant. Tinares has fully embraced the chaotic, bestial ideology that Baphomet seeks to spread across the planes. He lunges at the opportunity to indulge his darker pleasures, only hesitating when he suspects that the risks might outstrip the rewards. With his connection to the civilized world now ruined beyond repair, Tinares has begun to align himself with other evil creatures, especially monster races such as Orcs, Goblins, Kobolds, Bugbears and the like. Such alliances, however, are purely made for the sake of convenience, rather than any true companionship and those who are foolish enough to shake his hand can expect to be cast aside the moment Tinares feels they have exhausted their usefulness.

                                                49) Gorgeous Garaford [Elf (Eladrin), Oath of Conquest Paladin]:
                                                Gorgeous Garaford first entered the Prime Material Plane through a mountain spring, at a spot where the mortal world touched the Feywild. In those days, she vacillated between Spring and Autumn, for her love of pleasure and her generosity, respectively. Gorgeous came upon a village near to where the two planes met, and immediately fell in love with the humans who dwelled there, living their simple lives. She, in turn, came to be loved by them, for she was learned in story and song, and could carouse with the best of them. The days of plenty Gorgeous enjoyed with these people, and when times were hard, she would retreat to the Feywild and return with exotic foods to keep the locals from starving.

                                                One year sported a particularly poor growing season, however. Gorgeous left to obtain foods for the villagers. By the time she returned, the village had been decimated by an invading army. The famine wasn't limited to the single village, but rather afflicted much of the continent. Nations went to war over what resources could be had, and the countryside was further filled with marauding bandits, the results of starving and displaced citizens.

                                                Gorgeous Garaford - who had until then known only joy and frivolity - was heart-broken by the destruction of her beloved second home. In her grief and rage, she pitched Summer, and swore a Paladin oath. She saw the country descend into chaos, and found that no amount of talking or the like would stop people from hurting one another. She determined that peace could only be achieved through force; if she wanted to stop the fighting, she would have to MAKE everyone stop.

                                                The Eladrin wanders the land, finding trouble and solving it at the end of a sword. She'll bring peace and order to the country, even if she has to conquer it to do so.

                                                    50) Severity Praise-Gods Plainscloth [Tiefling, Lore Bard]:
                                                    Yes, that is his actual name (he usually goes by "Sever"). Severity's family were part of a community of Puritan-esque Tieflings, who focused themselves on simple, austere living and religious devotion. They trace the infernal taint in their blood to a formerly wealthy trade city, that became so decadent its inhabitants graduated from "mundane" indulgence, into outright devil summoning and infernal masses. In the generations since the fall of this city, the transgressions of their ancestors are worn on their sleeves. In an attempt to regain favor with the gods and wrangle what they see as infernal proclivity towards sin, these Tieflings have adopted their plain, ascetic lifestyle, and a focus on uncomplicated faith.

                                                    Into this world, Severity was born. A life where pleasures of the flesh were abhorred, frivolity was discouraged, and creativity was quashed. As a boy who seemed born with a song in his heart, it was a poor fit. There were no musical instruments in his family's isolated village, and the dry church hymns were dreary. Plus, he finds the farming life a real drag.

                                                    On his 15th birthday (not that his community put much stock in celebrating such things), however, he accompanied his father in delivering grain to a neighboring, less-than-puritan village. While his father negotiated prices, Severity was lead away by an unearthly melody. He found a bard performing in the village square, piping out a musical piece that enthralled Severity. And when the man sang, the words rang through his soul. After the performance, Severity began talking with the Bard. Upon hearing that the Tiefling boy had no instruments, and that it was his birthday (for which he would otherwise receive nothing), the bard gifted him a flute. It was compact enough that, when his father came yelling after him and to pay those damned musicians no mind, Severity was able to secret it back home with him.

                                                    Severity practiced piping and singing in secret, sneaking off to the deep woods to avoid detection. On this sixteenth birthday, he followed his father to the other village again, and again met the bard. They talked for a time about the boy's progress, but Severity could not demonstrate there, for he feared his father hearing and seeing him. The bard understood, and agreed to meet the boy later outside the Tiefling village, so he could teach Severity in secret. Another year went by, and Severity grew in skill under the senior bard's tutelage.

                                                    Alas, one day, Severity was discovered practicing. His parents were furious, saying that Severity pursuing music would surely lead to his damnation. By this time, the boy had grown tired of his family's narrow, restrictive view, and left. He met his teacher again traveling away from town, and finished his training on the road.

                                                    Sever finds life as a performer enjoyable, stimulating work. He enjoys the simple pleasures of the outside world, like food and drink and festivities. He is not, however, so hot about how openly these societies talk about "adult matters". As a boy barely become a man, who was raised in a puritan household, matters of sex are foreign and overwhelming to him. His greatest embarrassment occurred when, while performing in a sailor bar, he supplied the room with a melody on which the patrons could engage in sea shanties. When they filled the air with lewd, raunchy lyrics, Sever immediately froze up, his blue skin flushing a deep purple. When he saw everyone looking at him, he promptly fled. His teacher taught him many things - how to work a crowd, how to make a camp, how to bargain prices, and how to recognize when someone is trying to rip him off - but the old bard simply could not prepare the sheltered babby child for the rigors of adult society. While Sever is getting better at faking being comfortable in such situations, he's still, at heart, a farm boy thrust out into a cosmopolitan world.

                                                    Homesickness plagues Sever, from time to time, despite how stifling the village was, or how poorly the last meeting with the family went. He knows he will have to return, one day, to reconcile with them.

                                                        51) Gunter Creek [Human, Abjuration Wizard]:
                                                        Apprenticed as a scribe, Gunter was only a few years away from becoming a journeyman when his talent was spied by a wizard, who had a business interest in those learned in the ways of the quill. Gunter was offered instruction in the ways of magic, so that his existing skills could be put to a more high paying use: scribing spell scrolls. Indeed, Gunter's new wizard master was Arnold Dimm, of the Dimm & Bright Magic Company. Dimm & Bright specialized in producing magic items - spell scrolls, potions, wands, and the like - for sale to high paying clients. Gunter was put to work in a well-appointed office, where scores of his fellow low level wizards (as well as some Sorcerers, and a couple Gunter suspected were Warlocks) churned out spell scrolls. It was fairly tedious work, producing identical copies of spells like Mage Armor, Alarm, Comprehend Languages, and Mending. But tedium hardly surprised a working scribe like Gunter, and payment was ample.

                                                        Naturally, such cushy positions rarely last. Just as Gunter began to dream of a greater position (where he'd receive training in 2nd and 3rd level magic, the then highest grade of spell scrolls the company produced), Dimm & Bright fell to financial ruin. An unfortunate confluence of an office fire, gold-strapped clients, and the loss of a ship (and cargo) bound for the front lines of the kingdom's latest (costly) war, left the company without recourse but to close down. Subsequently, Gunter Creek - a young man accustomed to the quiet life of a tradesman - was left high and dry, without even having officially completed either his scribe or wizard apprenticeship. Nor were there any business connections in place to efficiently sell the wares he could produce; Dimm & Bright had been unique in the regard that they were magical wholesalers. Put off by the change in fortune, Gunter brooded a while, until it became obvious that he could no longer afford his comfortable apartment in the city.

                                                        Departing the nation's capitol, the young wizard pondered his next move. In time, he decided that, if no one else could employ him for the work of scribing spell scrolls, he would simply have to found a new business, that could fill the void Dimm & Bright once did. But to establish a new company, he would require capital. A lump sum of gold, with which to get the venture up and running. There was one problem: the country itself was embroiled in a deep recession, the end result of a series of costly wars that depleted the national treasury, spiked taxes, and choked trade. Every door Gunter came to asking for funding shut itself; none had the reserve wealth to spend on chancy projects, especially given how poorly the last such venture turned out.

                                                        No, if Gunter was going to make his dreams a reality, he would need to seek alternative sources of revenue. With great reluctance but great ambition, Mr. Creek took to that course common to errant wizards: adventuring. This despite having few, if any, combat-oriented spells in his book. A risky plan, to be sure - a city boy like Gunter dreaded the mere idea of camping outdoors, let alone trudging through dank dungeons and monster-infested wilderness. Still, there were a million stories of forgotten ruins and buried tombs, replete with monsters and traps, but also hidden caches of gold and magical items. So despite being remotest from the picture of an adventurer, the adventurer's life he did pursue.

                                                        Now, if he could surround himself with burlier allies, that would be something...

                                                            52) Baruch Halfteeth

                                                            A weak, cowering sycophant, that's the impression that many receive by socializing with Baruch and witnessing how he bows and scrapes and pleads his way into the good graces of those with more power and self respect than he could ever hope to possess. But, as is so often the case with evil, his apparent weakness is nothing more than a devious ploy to lure his enemies into a false sense of security. From behind the scenes, he plots and schemes until his victims let their guard down. Then he lunges from the shadows to deliver a swift, lethal blow and slinks back to the security his master's provide.

                                                            Baruch is a natural born wererat and has therefor carried the disease of lycanthropy throughout his entire life. From a very early age, Baruch learned to embrace his curse and used its power to augment his physical prowess. By now, he has become a gifted monk, disciplined in the Way of Shadow and striking down his foes before they can even sense his presence. To this day, Baruch does not know the identity of his father and more than likely will never know for sure. His mother worked at a brothel, after all, and was bedding several random strangers on a daily basis. His biological father could be almost anybody in his home town, or even some random adventurer who's probably half way across the world by now.

                                                            As for his appearance, Baruch Halfteeth is a scrawny, haggard looking man with a slouching posture and a subconscious habit of wringing his hands together. His brown hair is cut very short and is constantly sticking out in matted tufts as he fails to properly groom it. He garbs himself in an old, tattered robe that looks like it hasn't seen proper maintenance in over a decade. He has dark brown eyes and sickly pale skin that makes him appear to be suffering from some kind of chronic health defect. True to his namesake, roughly half of Baruch's teeth are missing, though the circumstances for losing them are known only to himself.
