    I just dreamed a Wes Anderson film. Edward Norton stars as a man struggling to make his way in the world of high finance with a chronic illness that occasionally results in explosive diarrhea and/or vomiting. The movie opens as a documentary team is finishing up filming a biopic of his life story. The film goes on to be a critical success but a financial failure, the construction crew that is renovating his house competes to see who can purchase unopened copies for the lowest price (the winner is the man who spackles the ceilings, he scores a copy for 61 cents from a video store whose water main broke. Not wanting to relive some of the more embarrassing moments in the film, Edward Norton's character avoids watching it, only to discover that everyone else in his life has seen it and, empowered by his struggles and the moral the director included in the film (don't cross or avoid mountains, create valleys), they are surpassing his every success. When he can no longer afford the construction crew and goes to fire them, they are ecstatic because they have since tripled their rates and all married Russian brides as a result of being in this film, and were only completing the job at their earlier rate out of loyalty to him.

    Penniless and desperate, he watches the film only to discover that a glitch in the crediting has resulted in him receiving a much smaller share of the film's meager profits than originally contracted. He uses this seed money -- and his own moral from the experience of being in the film -- to seek out a second attempt at success.
