        So, looks like Ancestries in PF2 will be giving a bonus to two Ability Scores (presumably +2 to each), a penalty to one Ability Score (presumably -2), and a floating bonus that can be added to any Ability Score, including the penalized one (but presumably not the ones you already got a bonus to), as well as additional hit points, base movement speed, starting languages, and vision. On a side note, it may have been a mistake, but the article seemed to imply that Darkvision might just straight-up let you see as well in the dark as you do in the light.

        On top of the basics every character gets from their Ancestry, you get Ancestry Feats at certain levels. They haven’t said what levels yet, but reading between the lines on the Leveling Up blog, I’m confident in saying that you get one at 1st level, and at each subsequent odd-numbered level you alternate between a General Feat and an Ancestry Feat (so you get a General at 3rd, Ancestry at 5th, General at 7th, Ancestry at 9th, etc.)

        The Feats they previewed for the Goblin Ancestry were nothing particularly inspiring, and I’d expect them to be about the average for Ancestry Feats.

        Interestingly, there’s no mention of gaining weapon or armor Proficiencies from Ancestry, which could just be because Goblins don’t get any. But I’m hoping this is a sign they’ll finally remove Proficiencies from Races and shift them to Backgrounds.

                    So it sounds like your objection isn’t to the concept of Sneak Attack, it’s to its design. Since 3e it’s pretty much been locked in as +1d6 x [half level, rounded up] additional damage on attacks against flanked or unaware targets, and I agree that isn’t necessarily the best mechanic to design a class’s combat capabilities around. Personally, I would keep the concept of the Rogue being more effective against surprised or otherwise vulnerable targets, but I’d scrap the assumption that adding a bunch of d6s is the way that should be expressed. I’d make something more like the PF Unchained Rogue’s Debilitating Strikes the baseline. When you Sneak Attack a target, you can apply one or more status effects to the target, and as you level up, you get more options for what debuffs you can inflict. Maybe more damage could be one option so you can play a Skirmisher Rogue, but it’s not the default and the Class doesn’t have to be designed around the huge damage spike. And the exact amount of extra damage can be determined based on the rest of the system math instead of just using half level d6s because that’s the way it’s always been done.

                                                            Mike Mearls works for D&D, not for Paizo. My comment about that quote making me eager to swap from D&D to PF2 was meant to indicate that his expressed dislike for concepts that I would consider essential for a game designer to think about, like a well-developed action economy, are pushing me away from D&D.

                                                            That said, I do agree that the direction Paizo is taking PF2 does seem to be going away from the expectations and desires of a lot of entrenched Pathfinder fans. There seems to be a lot of positive interest in the game outside of the Pathfinder fan base, and a lot of negative interest coming from within the fanbase. I think Paizo is well aware of that though. My speculation is that they recognize that being "not 4e" isn't a viable selling point in a post 5e world. A lot of the people who adopted PF1 because it felt more like what they wanted from D&D than 4e did have already gone back to D&D for 5e. 5e feels enough like D&D that they can't sustain themselves just on feeling like D&D anymore. They're trying to distill a more distinct identity for what it means to feel like Pathfinder, and embracing that, even if makes it feel less like D&D than PF1 does. The fans that just want to play a game that feel like D&D have already left. The fans that love the company and will buy anything they make aren't going anywhere. The fans they're likely to lose in this change are the ones who want to play a game that feels like D&D 3.75. And their hope is likely that they'll gain more fans that want to play a game that feels like whatever they decided Pathfinder feels like than they lose. It's risky in the short term, but I think it's really their only option in the long term.
