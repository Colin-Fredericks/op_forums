Their limbs were broken and they were stuck, but they were alive and struggling because even in the pain and the fear they still wanted to live.

                                                        They had made their nest in the tall grass around what used to be the perimeter of the aboveground pool in my yard before I destroyed that pool so I could throw it in the Dumpster at Dad's while we had a Dumpster rented. The grass was so tall and thick because Dad had always wanted to be the one to trim that grass, not trusting me to not ruin the pool. That the grass was so tall was therefore my fault; that the mice were in is was therefore my fault; that the mice were suffering was therefore my fault.

                                                        I got a trowel and dug them out of the earth. They were too broken to move, but they still tried to get away from me, crawling away on their remaining limbs or, in the case of the one whose limbs were gone, by trying to worm-wriggle away.

                                                        I wanted them to live. Though they're repulsive, invasive vermin who foul everything with their shit, they were staying outside, minding their own business, harming nothing and no one. They did not deserve what had happened to them, and I wished them no ill. On the contrary, it broke my heart to see them terrified and in pain.

                                                        So I took a cinderblock and smashed them one at a time. Each one of them squirted. They were full of some kind of white foam. It went everywhere. I now know mice are full of white foam because I was too irresponsible to properly tend to my land.
