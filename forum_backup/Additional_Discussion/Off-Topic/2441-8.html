            Eh. If AnubisXy says that a surprise development atroughly the quarter-mark ruined his enjoyment of the series, I’ll take him at his word. I don’t share his experience (if we’re talking about the same surprise, it didn’t ruin my enjoymen in the least — especially since they didn’t follow up on it in the manner that analogous reveals in other anime tend to be handled.
            Erin’s return as a Titan-hating Titan. Traditionally, when the main character gets a “special power” like this, everyone else gets relegated to the role of glorified cheering section. Not here: Erin’s unique talent gets recognized by his superiors as a stce; but the actions of his companions and superiors have proven to be every bit as important as anything he has done, before or since.

            As for the pacing of the reveal: you may be making the mistake of thinking that it’s the show’s Big Reveal with insufficient build-up, when in fact it’s merely the first step in the series’ buildup to its big reveal — which hasn’t come to fruition yet even in the manga.

                            Originally posted by cerealkiller View Post
                            Why didn't the humans just burrow underground
                            Because mining habitable spaces is really difficult in terms of logistics and engineering? Also probably wouldn't really keep the Titans out; the walls work because they're too thick to break through and too tall to climb; getting something with the same kind of integrity for underground would be more difficult, I imagine; you'd get 15-metre classes digging and tearing their way through and 3 to 5-metre classes getting into the holes. Come to think of it, you'd mostly have Titans constantly crowded around the entrances to such habitations (much as they crowd around the wall gates) and they're not so sessile at night that people right next to them while unrestrained would get through unscathed. The gates can at least be cleared when expeditions need to head out because of the advantage of higher ground.

                            Originally posted by cerealkiller
                            go above at night or whenever the titans aren't active to farm and get their animals seeing as they know they don't mess with livestock and other animals.
                            I don't think farming at night would be very effective, considering how photosynthesis works. Livestock also seems fairly likely to wander off if left unsupervised for half the day (or scared off by the giant monsters), and not really effectively managed while sleeping. And even if people are away from those places during the day, I imagine they still attract Titans just from the scent of them, and that would be likely to lead to them being trampled.

                            Yes, the image of some intrepid farmed adventurer returning to their secret crops and livestock enclosures at night only to find that they've all been crushed seems quite suitable to the usual milieu.

                            Originally posted by cerealkiller
                            Why can they develop the technology to fly and have some kind of natural gas to power it but can't develop something that would destroy the titans much easier than cutting the back of their necks.
                            I wouldn't really call 3D Maneuver Gear flight, and even then that doesn't really matter, since technological progression doesn't work that way, and even if it did, the way Titans are set up makes me feel like there aren't that many ways that would be more effective.

                            These aren't plot holes, by the way, they're just nitpicking, of the kind that no setting can stand up to simply because any reasonably intelligent person can find a way to logically propose how anything in a fictional world can be other than how it is. Neither can real life, for that matter, anything from international responses to the growth of the Nazi movement to the very fact that life exists at all (something so chemically and mathematically improbable that it actually creates a credible basis for the idea of alien progenitors, even if that's only really pushing event zero back a bit).

                            Minor spoiler for the general tone of the series as a whole:
                            Still and all, between what happens in the show and some vague things I've gathered from information about the progression in the manga that I'm otherwise deliberately ignorant of, there's a gradual implication that there's something sinister behind the social system underlying the series.


                            Originally posted by cerealkiller
                            I think part of what made it unappetizing for me was that the twist felt lazy. Instead of having that happen they (the characters) couldn't have gone with an innovative plan? Hell, I would've been okay with the story shifting characters at episode 6 or 7 w/e it was when that happened.
                            Was what they did in the supply depot not innovative? Or the full details of how they ended the battle? Dataweaver is right, it's not as if all other characters become props and spectators from there on in.

                            Spoiler for episode 7 onwards, related to previous spoiler:
                            It's not as if Eren's transformation constitutes much of an instant win button either, considering that he can barely control or understand it, and everybody around him is really damn testy about how much he uses it; as far as I've seen in the series, people seem continuously on edge as to whether each time Eren transforms will be the time where they lose him completely.

                            It also contributes to the tone of the series because nobody has a clue how he's able to do that, and it throws all kinds of assumptions into confusion and doubt, especially as certain things unfold in the story. The revelation of Eren's transformation is an asset, but it's mostly a disturbing mystery.


                            People have compared Attack on Titan to a zombie story, and while I don't quite think it's the best thing to try and sell people on the series with, one basic tenet carries through; zombies are freaky in a manner that contributes to tone, but they're not really interesting antagonists, or even really antagonists at all, they're a force that binds people into situations that bring social complications to the forefront, and occasionally something of unknown and uncanny nature and origins that need to be unraveled. The manner of the so-called twist is part of playing into the premise of just how powerful the Titan are, but it doesn't really redirect the focus or tone of the series, because if the series was just about fighting the giant monsters it wouldn't be nearly as interesting.

                            As for being foreshadowed, what did one think that those injections were about?

                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                    Still and all, between what happens in the show and some vague things I've gathered from information about the progression in the manga that I'm otherwise deliberately ignorant of, there's a gradual implication that there's something sinister behind the social system underlying the series.
                                    Absolutely.
                                    There’s more going on in the series than “giant humaniod monsters go on a rampage”; and in fact the Titanic rampage is merely a symptom of the real problem. The series has, from the very beginning, asked the question “just where did the Titans come from in the first place?” — and Ehren’s transformation is a vital piece to the puzzle, without which humans would continue stumbling around blindly.
                                    If you don’t like the twist or what the story is actually about, so be it; not every anime is for everyone. But the premise isn’t poorly thought out, nor is the twist a cheat.
                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                    Was what they did in the supply depot not innovative? Or the full details of how they ended the battle? Dataweaver is right, it's not as if all other characters become props and spectators from there on in.
                                    Far from it. Traditionally, when something like SnK’s twist takes place in an anime, the Ehren counterpart starts calling the shots. In SnK, Ehren has not displayed any sort of leadership, tactical, or even unusual fighting prowess; and he’s very ignorant of what’s going on. Others with more competence in these areas continue to take center stage where these things matter; and Ehren only takes the spotlight when brute force is called for — which is surprisingly rare, considering the brutality of the Titans.
                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                    As for being foreshadowed, what did one think that those injections were about?
                                    Indeed. Frankly, I’m not sure what more they could have done in terms of foreshadowing, given that the whole point of the reveal was that nobody involved knew that such a thing was possible. As I implied before, the reason why the reveal came so early was precisely because it’s not the “big secret” of the series: it’s merely an early indicator that “humanity vs. giant zombies” is an oversimplification.
