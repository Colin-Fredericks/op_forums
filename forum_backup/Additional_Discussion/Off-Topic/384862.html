    'Quality Guardian' Rant

    01-29-2015, 05:02 AM
    Just looking to let off some steam about something that bugs me. If you have something constructive to say, I'm happy to hear it, and I'll do my best to take it on board. But please don't try to convince me I'm wrong; I may well be, but I do not much care.

    I've had quite enough of this anti-Twilight, anti-Friday, anti-popular thing that seems to be everywhere. It's not new, I'm sure, but it drives me up the wall how people pick something seemingly arbitrarily, and declare it the "Worst X EVAR". Twilight, particularly, seems victim of this. Now, I'm not a fan of the books; I find them dull, characters one-dimensional at best, and I'm not entirely sure if it has a plot. But that's really irrelevant outside of my own head and decisions. Lots of people like them, and hey, good for them, they found something that makes their lives a bit brighter. I know I could use a bit more of that. But no. "We can't have that!" say the Quality Guardians. "People must only like what we say they can like! Game of Thrones* for all!" So they bitch and moan, insulting everyone who dares have a different opinion than them. Anne Rice took more liberties with the vampire mythos than Meyer, nobody's calling her work unforgivably crap and insulting people who like them. Or at least it's happening a lot less and I don't have to deal with it. So everywhere I turn, if the word 'twilight' is mentioned, regardless of context, you're looking at minimum of 20 minutes bagging on the series and movies, with implicit or explicit calling of anyone who disagrees with them mentally retarded or totally lacking in taste. Because it's totally impossible to have different opinions and not have one party be stupid, right? So when my father thinks my fondness for fantasy novels is silly and boring, and I find military books overblown and stupid, who's stupid? Come on, break out your magic qualitometre, I've got a sippy cup to give and voting rights to withdraw!

    What's really annoying is that, by a lot of measurements, Twilight is good. Do I like it? No. But a lot of people do, it's hugely influential, massively successful, and it is actually kind of innovative in its own little way. Vampires sparkling sounds silly, but so did vampires being burned by sunlight back in the 1920s or whenever Nosferatu was made. Certainly wasn't a part of Dracula, and hardly universal in the mythos predating it. And hell, at least it dealt with the thirst; Underworld didn't even do that in any significant fashion, and that did really well as a movie. Given that quality is, for the most part, entirely subjective, those are as reasonable metrics to apply as any other. Nobody was saying superheroes were good literature back in the 30s and 40s, yet look at the impact they've had up to now. Same with the noir detective thing, psychedelic movies and arty innovations of the 60s, and hell, even The Matrix. Do I like them? No, not really. But they were successful, had a huge impact, and a lot of people did, and they've stood the test of time and are still relevant today.

    It also happens with music. Now, nobody is seriously claiming Rebecca Blacks "Friday" is the next big thing in music. Her career is pretty much non-existent, and her song wasn't that good. Wasn't even really meant for distribution. But oh no, someone is trying, can't have that! Let's send her death threats and threaten to rape her and utterly devalue her as a person because she sang a substandard song that she didn't even write! I don't care if she was 14 at the time, I know I've never done anything silly at 14, and everyone who does grows up to be a horrible, horrible human being who strangles puppies and burns orphans! After all, every time someone likes or makes a bad song (because that can be determined empirically with 100% reliability and validity, after all), the Earth moves a little closer to the sun, speeding our mass extinction.

    I thought we'd come to the conclusion a while back that quality was largely subjective? You know, that whole "beauty is in the eye of the beholder/one mans trash is another mans treasure" thing we went to all the trouble to come up with proverbs for?

    We see plenty of discussion about inclusiveness and acceptance in our hobby, and maybe that's a discussion that needs to happen. I haven't seen these issues in actual play myself, but I'm willing to accept that they're out there. But you can't say "we need to be inclusive of people, regardless of their sexuality/race/gender/favourite Pringle flavour" and then turn around and belittle people because they like a different book series than you. Screw worrying about whether Prince is a misogynistic term, how about we focus on the little stuff? If we start accepting things that are totally subjective and opinion-based, maybe it'll be easier to accept things about people that they can't change.

    But of course we all know that the works themselves have nothing to do with this. It's just people trying to make themselves feel better by banging on someone elses efforts. "I can't write my name, but at least I didn't write Twilight!" "I suck at guitar because I can't be arsed practising, but at least I'm a better musician than Rebecca Black!" "Why would I try to be a more moral person, I'm better than Mel Gibson and his anti-Semetic rants!" Bashing people is just way easier than doing better, and accepting that other people have a differing opinion requires so much work. Gotta quiet those nagging voices of inadequacy somehow, after all.

    So, while I can understand the appeal to bitch and moan to make yourself feel better, please don't fall into the trap of appointing yourself Quality Guardians. Someone likes something that you don't, that's fine. You can disagree, even discuss why. I know a lot of people don't really like Evanescence, but I thoroughly enjoy most of their music. That's my right. And it's your right to disagree. But it is not your right to attack people for having differing opinions.

    You know. Unless you're God. Then I suspect you have more important things to be doing, and hopefully aren't so petty as to worry about people seeing a silly vampire romance film. And if you are, then I think you have deeper issues you might want to look at.

    * Chose Game of Thrones because I don't see the appeal and it's popular. You like it, good for you. It's an example, go with it.

      I have the feeling I mostly agree with the gist of your rant, but that you didn't choose the best examples. Twilight, for example, from everything I understand about it secondhand, is not good, but not because supernatural teenage romance with vampires who sparkle like diamonds in the sun is intrinsically bad. Rather, because there's a lot of creepy codependence going on in it that Stephanie Meyer didn't intend to convey. (Even then, I have to add the caveat that I haven't read the books or seen the movies. Just because supernatural romance isn't a bad thing doesn't mean it's my personal thing.) That said, I'm pretty sure a lot of the people expressing reflexive resistance to the idea of shiny vampires don't really know much more about Twilight than that.

      Friday is hilariously bad, but you're right that a lot of people misplace the blame for it. Rebecca Black isn't the perpetrator of some offense against taste, she's the victim of some skeevy music producers who misrepresent themselves to teen girls and their parents with pie-in-the-sky dreams and bleed them dry for vanity projects with slapdash writing and direction. She deserves sympathy; the exploitative, lazy dude who wrote the song does not, and has gone on to pretty much continue to do the exact same thing. I hadn't heard that she'd received death or rape threats, but I absolutely would believe it.

      Edit: At a second glance and catching some bits I hadn't noticed, I'd also be very careful about contrasting this idea with issues of inclusivity. People feeling left out because society excludes them and erases their identity as a person, and people feeling left out because they like a book or a show that the subculture turns its nose up at, are two very different issues, and if you think the latter is a more pressing issue, I think you ought to consider if you're venting enough heat that you're saying things without thinking, or otherwise, reconsider your priorities. Besides, it's a false dilemma, and one that's often used to devalue arguments toward inclusivity, to imply that only one of these issues can be worked on at a given time. There's not a single well of nonrenewable social justice that has to be rationed carefully.

              Is 'Friday' any worse than anything, say, Megan Trainor has put out? Or Jason Derulo? Or, hell, most pop stars? Yeah, it's shallow, repetitive, and massively over-autotuned, but there's at least 10 songs on the Hot 100 at any given time that those criticisms can be levelled at just as easily. It's dump pop, really: just noise, but pleasant enough noise, easily ignorable. But yes, you're right: if there has to be blame, which I am not convinced of, then it rests on the writers and producers: it is their job to sell this stuff, after all. And yeah, she mentioned in an interview I think that she's gotten a lot of threats, to the point where her family was seriously considering hiring a bodyguard.

              And we can go back and forth on what Meyer intended; unless she actively says, we'll never really know. And it's not really important anyway. I'm sure people read all sorts of things into the media I enjoy; god knows roleplaying hasn't been exactly criticism-free from that angle. Hell, I liked the Infernals splat; disgusting as it was, it was appropriate given the context (they are in Hell, after all. Not meant to be nice), and maybe the writers intended all that stuff, maybe they didn't. The Lunar splat has certainly got a disjoint between intent and how it reads, and I like that too. But nobody would seriously entertain the idea of censoring me for my opinion on that, or accuse me of being mentally retarded. So why is it OK to do it based on teenage girls liking a book aimed at teenage girls? Frankly, if you wanted to get upset about that, I'd get upset about the lack of quality writing aimed at that demographic, but that's not something you can really blame any individual work on.

              As to your inclusivity point, you're right... sort of. Is it the same thing? No, of course not, the sheer scale alone differentiates them. But when you're around a gaming table, people insulting your gender/race/sexual orientation and people insulting your taste in media, while one may or may not be more upsetting (depends on the individual, I suspect), they're both insulting. So by focussing on one, which is affected by millions of factors and mostly is the result of social and cultural factors, while the other is, in theory at least, more easily addressed? Sexism/racism/etc is complex. Saying "Everyone who likes X is stupid" is more simple, and is easier to address, and might help create that more inclusive feeling people are going for. Might not, who knows. Won't fix the other problems, obviously, but I doubt it'd hurt.
