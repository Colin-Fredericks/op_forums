    I wouldn't think that too many RPGs would really have much of a left or right wing bias.

    Probably more left wing if anything, almost purely as I think left wing is where you would tend (tend) to find more of the intense people who like sitting indoors with friends rolling dice, experiencing intense emotions about imaginary things, rather than the more outgoing, lighthearted and enthusiastic personalities who are well adjusted and at home in their communities, outdoorsy and sportsy, open to meeting new people, keen on experience and common sense more than theory, who you would tend find on the right wing.

    Myself, pretty centrist-independent is about the only way to view my politics. I'm very skeptical about both the markets and enlightened self interest on the one hand and about left wing movements and class identity on the other, and I'm an agnostic (one who believes that at least all religions are myths, and at most there is no deity) who is very secularist in politics.

    I would say if anything I'm on the right wing side, because I tend to view my responsibility as much more of a defender of the groups I belong to against myths towards them than to be self critical towards my own groups and celebrate other groups. I tend to be more critical of people who believe denigrating myths about their my group than people who believe overly glorifying myths about them.

                                Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                Quoted for truth. I wouldn't go so far as to say that it's the most right wing game ever — the Traditions are generally all over the map in terms of political persuasions, but tend toward moderate/liberal views — but yeah, Sergeant Brother is dead-on about the Technocracy. Ironically, I know a number of liberals who are convinced that the Technocracy represents conservatives, rather than everything conservatives despise. I can only explain it as them buying into stereotypes of conservatism.
                                Hm. There's definitely some truth to that, and I think the Technocracy can definitely be filtered through the lens of conservatives' worst fears about neo-liberalism, "nanny states", and globalization; but I think the problem with looking at any game through a lens of conservative/liberal warfare is that our understanding of those words are so oversimplified.

                                It can definitely be perceived through the lens of a globalist One World philosophy that reflects the worst fears of libertarian leaning conservatives. It could be seen by Christian conservatives as a shocking indictment of scientific rationalism. Reagan era conservatives might see it as a stark reflection of the sort of bureaucratically heavy conceptions of China and the USSR. Communal anarchists might feel the same way. But liberals concerned about social rights and personhood could see it as a reflection of what happens when private enterprises are allowed to consolidate power at the expense of the people (the Technocracy could definitely be seen through the lens of Randian utopias). Others might even conceivably see it as a reflection of social conservatives wanting to subvert personal freedoms for the sake of a Christian-values state (with scientific rationalism as a contrary metaphor).

                                It can be about those things, but I don't think it inherently is, and I think that's the strength of the game. It's so prone to flexible interpretation. If the core game is inherently about anything, it's the risk of totalitarianism (which I think most conservatives and liberals are afraid of) and the banality of evil that comes from believing that your ends justify the means. Wanting to shape the world in your image because you believe you're right and everyone will benefit is not a value exclusive to conservatives or liberals, and it's not a core value of either ideology. The fact that the world the Traditions ruled over was a clusterfuck too suggests to me that it's not about conservatism vs. liberalism. It's about the threat of unwavering power and self-assured self-justification regardless of ideology. The Technocracy's attempt to steamroll over marginalized groups (including pagans, an indigenous alliance, sexual "deviants", and counterculture hackers) for the sake of a pure ideology could similarly be seen as an indictment of the xenophobia expressed by right wing nationalists.

                                But I think the perspective that Sgt. Brother posits isn't about the values of conservatism vs. the values of liberalism. It's about the values most people share filtered through the most paranoid, straw man ideology of the most radical conservatives around. Demonizing your opponent is the easiest way to convince yourself of the absolute truth of your core values. Most progressives don't want to eradicate religion or make abortions mandatory, and they believe strongly in the right to free speech (along with the right to criticize the free speech of others). Viewing it through an "Info Warsr" prism doesn't mean asking any serious questions about your own ideology. It just reinforces the ideology you already have, and that strikes me as counterproductive to the strength of roleplaying games like this.

                                    I think there's an inherent problem in taking a line that's 15 - 20 years old and filtering it through the perception of the modern, deeply divided political spectrum. It's particularly easy to do when you're working with an exaggerated version of a particular group of people. But just as a thought experiment, I think I can adapt the Technocratic Union to fit the most exaggerated and demonized view of modern conservatism.

                                    The Technocratic Union isn't a neoliberal global order. It's privatized business. These aren't people who have been elected or appointed by elected officials. They're the sort of exceptional individuals that, according to Rand, have the right to cross boundaries because of their exceptionalism. What they practice isn't science either. It just resembles science, and they're happy to game the numbers in their favor, knowing that the parasite masses are too dumb to know better. Of course they aren't poisoning the environment. These statistics confirm it. Our schools run better than those supported with tax payer money. Trust us. We have a documentary to prove it. Iteration X promotes the establishment of a purely mechanized work force, and even though you'll be laid off because a robot can do your job cheaper, it will be fine. Trust us. The Wall Street Boys in the Syndicate aren't parasites. They're daring proponents of the free market. Ditto the Progenitors. Public health care creates slum hospitals. We can replace your heart, your brain, anything, just as long as you can afford to pay for it. The New World Order promotes fair and balanced news. The Void Engineers promote imperialism abroad for the good of everyone. Why haven't we exposed the rest of the world to our wonderful technologies? Because they just aren't ready for it yet. Sure, we're populists, but we're the only ones visionary enough to understand our toys.

                                    The Traditions are the disenfranchised, bulldozed by the three headed beast of social, cultural, and economic conservatism. The Akashic Brotherhood? The Chinese who are not our friends. Cultists of Ecstasy? Queers and cucks. The Euthanatos? Fuck you. If you're going to die, it's going to be because the medicine was too expensive. Suicide is a sin. The Celestial Chorus? Hippies and pacifists. The Dream Speakers? You mean those fucks marching on our pipelines? Run them over with your heavy machinery or lock them up for acts of eco-terrorism. The Order of Hermes? Satanists. Ditto the Verbena. The VA? Digital terrorists.

                                    After all, they don't despise the Traditions, but they don't think that "minorities" should be given preferential treatment over "regular people". Sometimes marginalized people need to be sacrificed for the good of everyone.

                                    Is that what I believe conservatives are like? Hell no. But it's about as nuanced a view as liberals being God-hating, baby-killing, free speech censoring conspiracy globalists. That said, I do think the Technocracy more accurately reflects a massive global, vertically integrated, free market conglomerate mixed with a conservative think tank than it does a shadow government. But YMMV.
