                Today's thought: Am I the only one who played in groups that seemed to pay attention to the Golden Rule of White Wolf, often paraphrased by said groups as "so long as it makes it a better game, fuck the rules!"?

                There have, of course, been numerous posts about this and several other posts supporting the idea and talking about the games in which it doesn't happen. But here's my thing about it........

                First, this is after having read a post in the Forsaken forums about the Idigam Chronicles development and how they're trying to work into the book a way for the pack's totem to be raised not just by experience points, but by things that actually happen in the story. Now, the actual details of this don't matter as much to me as the fact that there seems to be a need to write this shit into the book. Really? People don't feel comfortable just saying to their group "Hey, your characters just did something seriously kick-ass! You're all gonna get this cool thing because that's how the story played out."

                Second thing, all these Beats and Conditions and such seem like they're ways for putting mechanics on shit we already did all the time, anyway. So, again, people really need this spelled out in the books? Did I just happen to get some really great players and STs to learn from, or is everyone else waiting for the WW/CCP/OP police ninjas to swoop down on them to tell them that it's BAD WRONG FUN!!! Did these players get grief about "their way" being BAD WRONG FUN from so many sources they actually believed it? I've been playing these games (shit, lemme think.....1994, maybe 1995 or so.....fucking hell 20 years, shit I'm old!) for a while......and I've never had those ninjas show up AND everything I always read encouraged us to play the game to make it fun, and if that meant bending, breaking, bedding, or abusing the rules SO BE IT!

                And now in a very obvious burying of the lead, this is one of those moments when I feel like the whole game and systems are leaving me behind because I don't get what the purpose of some of this stuff is and so many other seems to be so excited by it. Hunter, Geist, Mummy and Demon seem to get so much (nearly fanatic) appreciation for things that I don't get from them. People seem to love the Tier system, and I look at it and say, "um, yeah, didn't we just get away from those global conspiracies in the Classic WOD?" The Krewes seem to be nothing more than an elaborate system for making the player group. There's supposed to be a deep, horrific mystery in Mummy that I simply cannot find. What little I've read of Demon has not grabbed my attention. My recent acquisition of Masquerade 20 and Apocalypse 20 have just kicked me harder in the ass with nostalgia and I'm regressing back in anticipation of Ascension 20. I still don't have enough time to take for a game, and I know it's my choice because I'm putting my Baby Bears first, but it's still depressing.

                tl:dr - "My games", though I know I have no right to call them that, are moving beyond me and I'm sad.

                                                        For lack of a better title, and based on what's established, it would have to be something like "Wraith: the Remembering/Fading/Anchored", I think.

                                                        A post-survival horror game.

                                                        Similar to Mummy in that there would be a serious focus on remembering who you are (were?), why you continue on and what you have to do to keep going.

                                                        Underworld geography would remain the same, but the Wraiths (like Geists and Kerberoi) would have special advantages for their unique state.

                                                        Some of the things that would make them different from the other ghosts is that they have not tied themselves to a single Dominion, they are not anchorless, and their powers are based around using/applying memories they have from their previous life. For example, if they want to cross one of the Rivers, they don't just dive in, they remember themselves going on a long trip. This allows for adding new powers as they manage to dig out more memories from their souls. That's another thing, it's not just a reflection of what they once were, it's their actual soul and thoughts and memories wrapped up into an ectoplasmic form. What they risk, daily, is losing all that, which would be measured by the Morality/Integrity stat. Their anchors would be much more important as they'd be a place to replenish memories and gain new ones. Interacting with the living world would also help build those things up, but it brings with it the risk of running into mages, sin-eaters, kerberoi, etc, etc, etc that can mess with ghosts. As a default, and to show their stronger connection to the living world, they would all have Manifest as an automatic, built-in Numina.

                                                        And my short burst of creativity has ended for now.
                                                        Importing this over here so I don't take over the thread it came from. Just latched on to this idea: Wraith: the Post-Survival Horror game

                                                        Expanding on above:

                                                        While the living world has it's fair share of perils, Wraiths are not safe in the Underworld, either. Generally, the ghosts and the Kerberoi will leave a Wraith to his deathly existence, unless the Wraith does something to draw a lot of attention to himself. The ghosts will take notice. They could be curious about why he can do what they can't and they will react to that knowledge in various ways. They will ask, they will insist and when further denied, they will demand. They will claw, weep, tear and rage at the Wraith to get it if they feel like it. Other could just clamor all over the Wraith in great numbers like a Deathlands Messiah, looking for a miracle to get them out of their awful state. If all of this disrupts the Dominion sufficiently, the Wraith could find himself with a Kerberoi on his ephemeral ass. Driven out, carried out, thrown in prison, etc, etc....whatever they decide is the appropriate punishment for being too alive in the lands of the dead.

                                                        I'll keep coming back to this.

                                                        EDIT: A little more.

                                                        No matter how much the plead, beg, bribe or demand, there is no way for a Wraith to make a ghost into something like itself. Once a ghost has lost enough of itself, there is no going back. This is one of the threats a Wraith deals with daily.
