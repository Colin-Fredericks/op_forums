                        I think income inequality is the bigger source of problems among minority communities (and among white communities) than race, and tackling income inequality would have a much larger, much more profound and much more immediate effect on minority communities than any sort of campaign to "end racism."

                        Unfortunately in the Democratic Party has always been more interested in race baiting to various degrees, and you can't blame them for that. It's a subject that gets passions up and gets people to the polls to vote. Income inequality is a more cerebral issue that doesn't get people as emotionally invested in it as problems revolving around race or sex (and the Republican Party has always race baited in its own ways as well).

                        Plus at the time of the Primary races, race was dominating the headlines - as an example, look at the Black Lives Matter movement. Many members very publicly hijacked the stage from Democratic candidates (and Republicans to a degree) demanding they say "Black Lives Matter," and hurling insults at anyone who suggested that, "All Lives Matter."

                        Unfortunately the BLM movement largely flamed out because being so based around race meant that it was incapable of making inroads to members of other communities (as well as being a leaderless movement and so not having anyone with the power or authority to disavow people who do dumb stuff like riot and loot). But not being able to make inroads with other communities was a big, big problem that BLM has never been able to overcome.

                        As an example, I live in a predominantly Hispanic part of the country with very few African-Americans. Black Lives Matter had almost zero effect here - the one rally that BLM tried to hold was largely overwhelmed by Hispanic counter-protesters.

                        Many Hispanics (in this area at least) felt "Black Lives Matter" meant that Hispanic lives didn't matter - that BLM wasn't interested in what happened to Hispanics. Which is funny because Hispanics often face the same sort of racial problems from police officers. Hispanics should have been flocking to the movement. And yet, because BLM was a black racial movement rather than a movement around police corruption, Black Lives Matter was never able to form the multi-ethnic coalition that it really needed in order to be a success.

                        Whites and Latinos and Asians and others who said, "Yeah, Black lives matter, but our lives matter too," were largely met with jeers and accusations of racism from Black Lives Matter. And unfortunately due to that, the BLM movement came across to many whites as less of an anti-police corruption movement and more of an "anti-white" movement.

                        This was just another example, like various PC issues, white privilege, etc, that helped set the stage for the 2016 election a stage on which many white people in America feel like they were under attack because of their race.

                        And that also helped set the stage for why many people on the left weren't terribly interested in Bernie's message - that income inequality was a bigger, easier to solve issue that would help people a lot more than yelling about race.
