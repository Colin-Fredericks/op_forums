There is also Isaiah, our first male gay character, who also is a POC. Whilst Maas is making progress in the diversity of her novels, these characters barely feature in the narrative, but instead occupy the margins."                                             I think the threshold is also influenced a lot by the very broad spectrum of what a "supporting character" can be. In the LotR books, Samwise, Merry, Pippin, and Gollum are all supporting characters to Frodo's status as the protagonist of at least one part of the story. But they are not equals in their narrative weight, and what details about them mean to emotional reactions. If you were Yesterday style sent to a world where LotR was never written but was also otherwise basically the same as ours somehow, making Merry and Pippin more that good friends would feel a lot more token-representation than it would mean if Sam was romantically in love with Frodo and part of the whole journey is Sam coming to grips with Frodo just not really caring about that sort of thing and Sam needing to find fulfillment with someone that's actually going to reciprocate his feelings (and lets be honest that's not exactly a huge stretch from the original anyway). I think most people would also get the backlash if Gollum was somehow the only minority coded supporting character.

                                            I feel like most minority groups that want representation appreciate if an author just says, "I don't think I can authentically write my central character as having experiences that I don't really understand." But if you can only do representation via Merry and Pippin supporting characters where that basically never really matters, it's not a good look. You should at least be able to create a character that has deep emotional bonds with people of very different backgrounds than your protagonists... because humans in general have deep emotional bonds with people that are different from them.
