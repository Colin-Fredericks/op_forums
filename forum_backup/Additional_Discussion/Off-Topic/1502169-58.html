                                        O...K... but most of the public face of politics is theater and performance because humans aren't very rational in how they select their leaders in aggregate, and humans really don't like watching the legislative process (because it's slow, mired in procedural and traditional complexities, and just generally boring) actually happen. But in a system with even the trappings of democracy, politicians need to get votes, so they need to do things that appeal to their human constituents to get those votes. So a lot of theater happens because that's what works for the getting votes part and keeping their cushy jobs, even if it means their actual job performance suffers for it.

                                        Democrats are plenty theatrical as it is. The DNC put Bernie Sanders denouncing the billionaire class on stage right before JB Pritzker, a billionaire who used his status as richer than Trump to make jokes at Trump's expense, got up to talk... and while some people found that a bit odd, for the most part the theater worked and the seemingly disparate performances didn't turn people off.

                                        What's unlikely to happen is the Democrats getting more bombastic. Their theatrics are going to stay more subdued because that's the current brand: the Dems are the "normal" party that does normal things that will make everyday life better. That's a hard act to pull off if they start getting as off the rails as their opponents have been getting. It's a hard act in general because, "we're the party of vital changes to fix things," and "we're the party fighting for the normal status quo stuff everyone likes," aren't really compatible even if you can convince enough people that they'll get whichever they want if you sell it right. NFT digital trading cards, signed bibles, and limited run shoes aren't the kind of theatrics that do that sale.
