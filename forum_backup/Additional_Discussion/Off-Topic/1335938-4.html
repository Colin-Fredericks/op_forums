        On topic:

        Consent, and formal ways of easily getting consent across, is important to games because we all have our problems, and we at the same time can't always know how those problems are going to manifest for other people. We should definitely try and intuit and empathize as much as we can in the first place, but clear communication helps to support those efforts, and starting on the premise of believing those with the problems when they clarify why it's a problem expands our understanding and gives us essential lenses for viewing the rest of the world. In time, some of those lenses may reveal themselves to only be quite particular, but in most cases they help to create a highly useful work station for ensuring that people feel comfortable working with us individiuals, and maybe even open them up to challenging their limits because they know they do so in a safe environment.

        So, things like the form in the OP are highly useful, and I really like them.

                                                My issue with the "in a fantasy context" argument is that it seems to suppose what happens in an imaginary context doesn't have to pass through the real minds of real people.
