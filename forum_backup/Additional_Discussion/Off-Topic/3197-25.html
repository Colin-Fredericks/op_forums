                                                    Perhaps I mispoke myself...I didn't really mean a calvacade of fan refs, per se, but just a different approach to storytelling. I've been saying for a while now that, as muchas I enjoy the show, I wish the makers would allow it to change and develop with the acknowledgement of its older audience. Most people seem to think I mean making it "darker and edgier", but that's not what I'm talking about.

                                                    When I read the comic book (some pages of which I posted earlier in this thread), I practically shouted,"YES! That's it, *that's* the tone I'd like to see it the show!" And the100th was much closer to that. It's still very appropriate for kids, but more of an "all ages" feel; I don't think changing MLP, *if done correctly*, will somehow automatically destroy The Formula and/or alienate kids.

                                                    Part of The Point of the show that Faust wanted to get across was that kids are smarter, tougher and more sophisticated than TV allows for, that TV doesn't have to be simplified and dumbed down for their ages, and that they will not shatter like fragile glass if you challenge them by pushing the boundaries. If anything, most will thrive on that!

                                                    That's what I would like to see more of from FIM, and this ep was really a step in that direction. I don't expect the show to stampede in that direction, no, but I do hope it keeps making baby steps there, at least...

                                                        S5E10 - Princess Spike

                                                        Kinda... boring? I don't know, the first half was kind of lackluster to me. The "run around and obsess over your job" is rather overdone, and while it was done pretty well and it did tie back in later, it was still meh for me. It did pick up in the second half, where Spike gets to confront the reality of his newfound power.

                                                        I did like how the episode wrote Spike. Real Spike episodes are few and far between as it is, and this time he came into his role really well. The way he drifts from wanting to help Twilight to using her name for his benefit felt smooth and gradual, and I think it can be used to speculate about his character. For example, how does his dragon nature play into wanting to manifest his power and acquire favours? He never really stops wanting to help Twilight, though, so maybe his dragon birth doesn't play in?

                                                        It's a pretty good episode, no real faults except a sense of having seen it before, but executed well enough that it at least didn't get tedious. The accents were funny too.
