            Originally posted by Zironic View Post
            If you look at the global comic industry as a whole, you'll notice that this problem is localised to more or less only Marvel and DC since they're the publishers that want to have their cake and eat it too.
            To an extent. The issue, as I put it, is money.

            The south east Asian comics (I know I'm lumping a few fairly disparate things together there) do avoid the problems Marvel and DC have creatively, but have plenty of their own. But they're simply not as valuable as intellectual property. One Piece is a really fun manga and a really fun show. It's earned it's extremely long run and hasn't fallen for the "status quo shake up trap." But when you look at the numbers... Spiderman is flat out way more profitable despite it. One could argue that in many ways Spiderman kicks Luffy's ass in profit because of it. Though there are also plenty of comics that don't managed to keep from the status quo issue that don't come from the big two. Japanese markets just usually kick in and end series to start new ones to avoid this being common.

            One Piece is also something of an outlier for comics from that region that manage to last that long. Bleach is still zombie trudging along and suffers plenty of the same issues as Marvel/DC status quo. The Gundam franchise could be seen in the same light (even if they're more anime focused, they do plenty of manga, and both are really vehicles to shill what makes the real money: model kits) as the repetition of plots is a bit obnoxious over time. In the long running single continuity area, the vast majority of them fall into the inevitable trap that Marvel and DC did. It's just that in many other regions there is little expectations of most comics going that long in the first place.

            I think we're also seeing more and more independent artists rebelling against the Marvel/DC model and breaking out into their own through webcomics/kickstarter with the most notable example being Girl Genius. I wouldn't be particularly surprised if in another decade or so the Marvel/DC comic divisions are completely wiped out and they're left to just making action movies while the independent comics conquer the market.
            While I think the current status of the industry allows indie comics successes that they just couldn't get access to before, they're not killing off Marvel and DC anytime soon. They make too much money despite all their problems. Girl Genius is profitable, but she isn't pulling in a few million dollars a month in sales. There isn't going to be a massive blockbuster-fest of profits from a bunch of Girl Genius movies.

                                                        Originally posted by Shadowflame View Post
                                                        All the examples you've mentioned so far...I've never heard of before. If they are considered a "success," then it's one they've carved out in some niche that doesn't come up in on the radar of the mainstream of public awareness.
                                                        Dude, Donald Duck is like the biggest comic in Sweden. No contest even. Marvel has a healthy presence, DC less so, and One Piece is coming out regularly, but Donald Duck is what people think about when they hear "comic book".

                                                        Or well, when they hear "serietidning", but you get what I mean.

                                                        Looking at the attention Thor's gender change gets and comparing it to similar attention for Donald Duck is not really a very good way of measuring popularity. This is mainly because the two comics are very different beasts. Not only do they differ in tone, subject matter and intended audience, but there very assumption of the format is different. Donald Duck comics are not concerned with a canon, at least not in the sense that every issue progresses the story and those events will have to be taken into consideration later. Donald's canon is more of a set of basic assumptions: he has an uncle named Scrooge, he's competing for Daisy with Gladstone, and his three nephews live with him. Beyond that, few things will have permanence. I can't count all the times Donald has been scared by ghosts or been abducted by aliens, and yet when it happens the next time he's still equally surprised at their existence.

                                                        If someone wanted to switch Donald into a woman, they could probably do that. It's just one more of all the strange things that happen and are promptly forgot for the next issue, or hell, the next story in the same issue. The way the authorship works (at least in the Swedish comic books), with several different authors all doing their own thing with in most cases very little collaboration, there's no way something like that would stick. Therefore, the lack of such controversy regarding Donald Duck should not be taken a sign that Donald Duck is less popular than Thor, since the circumstances make is much harder to actually effectuate said controversy.
