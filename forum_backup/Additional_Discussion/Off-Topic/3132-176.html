        Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post
        ...when you make yourself rare and exclusive people tend to find it more compelling to be part of it than when its always there and can access it whatever they can.
        That's true. When I started making it clear to my groups over the years that anyone not actively adding to the enjoyment of the group playing the game was going to get booted, and they saw me follow through, people generally started putting in more effort to make the game as a whole work well. Then most of the shitty times ended up being as a result of building a new group after moving somewhere new (and thank fuck I don't have to do that so much anymore).

        Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post
        That said I still get players who don't read the books, which pisses me off to no end.
        I've found going the opposite route has actually managed to work out better for me. I used to get bent out of shape by a player not knowing how their own character works or the basics of how to play a game, but at some point I realized that them not knowing their character wasn't just putting a damper on the fun for me, but also for them, and for anyone else at the table because they have to wait while the player that doesn't know their shit figures it out and/or gets verbally reprimanded by me. And since telling someone who obviously can't figure shit out on their own "Figure your shit out" wasn't reducing the problem in any way, I started expecting every single one of my players to have a complete lack of understanding of their characters - giving every player, no matter how long they've been playing, the exact leeway I would give a player who was having their first table-top role-playing experience.

        And when I am mentally prepared to have to tell every player every detail about how their character works mechanically or how they fit into the world around them, I am either fine with it - as anyone would presumably be fine with teaching the game to the uninitiated - or pleasantly surprised because the player is exceeding my expectation that they don't even know which die to roll or what part of their character sheet to find appropriate information on by actually knowing their shit.

        Not so noble anymore.

            I've managed to work around this just 2 times before. In the NWoD/CoD book Mysterious Places, at the end (IIRC) there's a scenario called "The Empty Room". The first time I used it, the game fell apart in 2 sessions, so it was no big deal. the second time, the game only ended because I was moving too far away to justify the drive, but I left all my notes with one of the players to continue if they wanted. Here's why it works to avoid the wasted work: you let them play thru that while you watch the characters play out and dream up the game you want to create. I was able to stretch the scenario for about 4 to 6 sessions, giving me time to figure out what direction I wanted to take them in. I warned them upfront that this was not going to be the whole game, so I still took their desires into account.

            BUT, this was only 2 times out of I-lost-track-how-many-times! So many I walked away from gaming for awhile!
