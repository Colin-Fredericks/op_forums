            I figure this is as good a place as any to vent a bit and hopefully find my mood some measure of improved.

            However, what I need to vent about kind of necessitates some establishment of history if I want to even imagine that anyone will understand where I am at right now. So here's the shortest version: I've had bipolar depression for as long as I can remember, but I didn't know what how I have always been was called until about a decade ago. My parents got me help because I was living with them again after about a decade living apart and they realized that it wasn't "just normal teenager stuff" that I was shutting myself away in a darkened room and seemingly flip-flopping between sleeping all day and not sleeping at all for days at a time. The medication I got onto evened out my activity cycle, and it did even out my mood too - but it did so too strongly, and my friends, girlfriend, and family had to tell me to stop taking it because I didn't even notice that I didn't give a shit about anything anymore (no anger, no irritation, no excitement, no laughter, nothing).

            I decided at the time that I would just continue on as I always had, dealing with the lows and channeling the highs into something productive.

            It turns out that I've also had fibromyalgia since my early teen years, and was not in fact a "wimp" that couldn't handle the normal pain that everyone goes through as my ill-informed father had convinced me. Only very recently, within the last year, has this been confirmed as well as can be by medical professionals and treated with medication.

            So that gets to the part I need to vent about - I lucked out big time; the first medication my doctor picked for me to try out to see how it would help (chosen because it is alphabetically first on the list of medications she put together that both might be helpful to me and will only cost me $4 a month) has worked wonderfully. I no longer spend every day dreading the pain of simple things like taking a shower, or getting into and out of a car so I can go places a person needs to go. I still have pain, but it's a tiny fraction of what I used to feel all day everyday, and it's also entirely explainable by the fact that I am significantly out of shape from all of avoiding the pain I started doing once I stopped self-medicating with marijuana, alcohol, and any opiate I could get in my body without a needle.

            The medication, an antidepressant by design that incidentally has been found to help some people with fibromyalgia, also manages - the absolute miracle drug I lucked into it being - to help keep my activity cycle more even.

            But now I don't know how to live. By which I mean I don't how to get shit done, not that I've considered ceasing to be alive (which hasn't crossed my mind in nearly 20 years, and I am thankful for that fact). I sleep more than I have ever slept - I'm used to being able to function on like 4 hours of sleep a day, and now if I sleep 4 hours and then wake up to take the wife to work, I get home from dropping her off and sleep another 6 hours. And if it rains, I'm useless with how tired I get. We had a couple days back to back last week of light rain, and I think I was sleeping 10 hours at night and still drifting off into an 2-4 hour afternoon nap.

            And my moods still have highs and lows like before, but I think the lows are getting worse, or maybe I'm still just adjusting to life on medication and my irritation at loss of my "super powers" that were my manic periods is compounded into making the lows seem worse. The point being that I fucking hate some shit now that wasn't such a big deal to me before. I'm straight up pissed off to the point of thinking insults at myself that I haven't been able to get on top of house work, and that I haven't started making progress at getting back in shape, because my old excuse of the pain being too much to bear is gone and what is left is what feels like bullshit to me even as I sit here literally struggling not to dose off at my desk typing of "I'm too tired right now."

            And I've been angry. Like I'll ask the wife "What do you want for dinner?" and she'll ask me "Do you want to fix something at home, or pick something up?" which I know logically isn't a problem because she is participating in the process of us figuring out what our dinner will be, but it isn't actually an answer to my question. And that's a common thing she'll do where she won't actually answer the question I've asked, and it seems to me like she's trying to give me the answer I want to hear. And I've mentioned in the past that I don't like people trying to tell me what they think I want to hear, I just want them to answer the questions I've actually asked (I've got a think about power dynamics - I hate people putting themselves under me, but I also hate feeling like people don't listen when I speak to them, so it's kind of a double-dose of "ugh" when someone tries to put their own opinion below mine in importance by not actually answering the question I asked). But lately when she does that I have the urge to scream. I try not to raise my voice too much - I don't give in to the urge to scream - but I do raise my voice because I can't keep the frustration bottled up inside.

            I know it's not her fault, and I know that I'm not the only one suffering from my affliction - it's hard on her too, seeing me in the dumps starting to lose my shit. But I can't stop myself feeling the way I feel in an instant, and I can't help but to be frustrated and to feel like when she comes to tears because she feels bad about me feeling bad she is taking something away from me - because I'm not expressing my sadness/frustration/whatever and receiving comfort or support from her, I'm giving the comfort and support and she is the one that gets to cry it out. And no, I really can't figure out why we can't just both cry it out and be giving support to each other in doing so together - the instant the first tear streaks down her cheek, all the things I was wanting to let out and be comforted in regard to turn into the hurt and anger that is the feeling that she just took away my right to be the one needing a shoulder to cry on.

            Through all of this, there is one other thing. Fear. I'm scared shitless that this is just how life on this medication is going to be from here on out, and similarly terrified that if I ask my doctor to switch me to another medication that I'll go through the shit my sister has gone through where she is on something new every few months and dealing with a new shit-show of side effects or things just not helping at all because the only thing she's been on so far that has actually help and not had unbearable side-effects are the narcotics that her doctor doesn't want to leave her on long-term because of their addictive nature - so I kind of feel like I don't even know what the fuck I want in this whole ordeal, and that makes me angry too.

            I don't even have enough give-a-fuck left in my tank right now to go back and read this post to make sure it is intelligible, so sorry if it isn't. But hey, maybe I got something out of me. Maybe after a shower and a nap I won't be quite as pissed off at everything and nothing as I was before I started typing.

            Maybe I'll make it through this weekend's trip to the grocery store without becoming that guy that's clearly losing his temper about trivial shit in public - because I fucking hate that guy.

            Not so noble anymore.

                A couple of tips from one semi-medicated bipolar sucker to another:

                Maybe the doctor mentioned it, maybe not, but sometimes those manic phases can come out as anger instead of boundless energy, especially while on certain medications. Also, caffiene can cause those anger phases to come quicker and easier on those same medications. My doctor this time said that wasn't a problem, but I remeber it being an issue when I was on the same stuff as a kid. I cut out caffiene and I feel like I have more energy and have less rage issues.

                Get a punchng bag in your home. When the anger comes, go hit, yell, kick and scream at it. When fatigued, strap on the gloves and take some swings at it. Take a bit of self-dicipline to do it, probably sting more with your other issues, but getting the heart rate up pushes that fatigue away. When you get up to take your wife to work, make sure you put on something to work out in, but that might bug you if you tried tosleep in it. I found these work out shirts that are very good for letting air thru, but the collars are too tight and it would drive me nuts trying to sleep in it.

                We've all seen it and heard about it before, but it's still often dismissed easily: autumn and winter can make someone who's already down feel worse. I've had a hell of a time doing my own exercises, skipped it for two weeks now using excuses like "I'm too busy." and "I'm too tired.". Tomorrow, hell or high water will not stop me. I'm tired of my own excuses. Sometimes, you can channel that anger into getting mad at yourself for not doing what you know you should be doing. Just don't let it go too far or you'll be back into the depression spiral.

                Anger turned inwards is depression. Anger turned sideways is humor. Watch some 3 Stooges, listen to Christopher Titus (a comedian who uses some very messed up things in his life). Whatever you find funny, go for it. The Addams Family is great if you're already in a dark mood. They are right there with you. Robin Williams always worked blue, but his later stuff revealed just how hard a time he was having. He truned his open heart surgery into some really funny shit.

                I cannot emphasize this one enough: TALK ABOUT IT! Don't hide your moods. Don't be afraid of your problems. Tell everyone. My employer company has both of your conditions listed as legitimate disabilities. I have to put down being bipolar for any changes I make in my employment status, and they have to accommodate me. And, of course, you always have us here.

                Try it out, let us know, stick around and keep talking to us. Good luck, my friend.
