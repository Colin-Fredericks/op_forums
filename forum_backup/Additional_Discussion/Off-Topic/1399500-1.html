                I don't have a proper overview, but guess I play about 50/50, with periods of playing a lot more female characters than male. Like, can I imagine what it's like to play someone who's never known the Internet? Or the Simpsons?                                                 While not to get too tangential here, I feel like this is one of the reasons why purely fictional settings are so popular in RPGs. You don't have to keep up with trends to a play a teenager in D&D, or know about things that happened 60 years ago to play an old fart in Traveler. Even if it isn't canon to a given game, in a high fantasy setting, you can play with gender norms enough where Dragonblooded have fairly unified mannerisms from their dynastic and militaristic culture that focuses far more on class than gender in how people are expected to behave, speak, dress, and so on. You cut out a lot of these worries when you're not trying to do something close to the real world.
