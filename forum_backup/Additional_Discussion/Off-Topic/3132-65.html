    Having not read the thread for some time i wanted to touch on this and hope what information I can give is useful.

    Whenever I see this, usually but not exclusively in conversations involving discussing morality between Christians and Atheists(I am an atheist as point of reference). What first comes to mind is that we have to understand that this conception simply does not correlate to reality, and specifically the reality of humanity as a social animal. In addition it also contains a misunderstanding or perhaps you could say, miss-application of what "subjective." means in regards to it's use as subjective morality. On the latter point, it should be understood that "subjective" here does not mean arbitrary but is simply being used in the vast majority of cases as the plain recognition of median moral ideas filtered through individual, familial, tribal, cultural, national,(insert all varieties of peer groups here), perspectives. That is particularly important because the concepts of morality, ethics, are anti-thesis to the concept of being arbitrary.

    On the former there is the idea of morality as being something that is a byproduct of evolution. I know some who think that this would be a controversial idea but I do not think that is a controversial idea but I understand that many do.(Btw if the previous part here seems stilted, I apologize, I accidently cut the post and I'm not sure how to undo with this forum tech) Setting that controversy aside even a lay person can see the idea that there are benefits in regards to maintained survival in a species acting in accord vs a society that indulging overly in murder, destruction,etc. It's not simply a matter of "collective opinion." but of shared practical experiences as a species and our ability to survive in a particular environment. There is a weight to that we shouldn't dismiss too flippantly or think is something flighty. And in regards to that it's part of why morality as a concept is capable of change, growth and survival because subjectivity is it's strength, not it's weakness. A so called "inherent" or immutable morality would not be able to survive ecological, technological or cultural shifts if it was a reality. While it's distasteful to see all the horrors that have happened in the past, or are happening now as a result of failures in our subjective morality to adapt to the time.While the the pain and suffering those failures have caused, are causing and will cause should not be dismissed. It's still I suspect better than what we would see from immutable, revealed, etc morality.
