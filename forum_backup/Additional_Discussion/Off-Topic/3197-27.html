    S5E13 - Do Princesses Dream of Magic Sheep

    Fluttershy: "Mmmm, it's so nice to be the pet for once."

    Do you hear that? It's the keyboard clatter of a hundred slashfics being born.

    Pet peeve, but could someone explain to me why it's "magic" and not "magical" up there? You have three perfectly useful and distinguishable forms: "magic" is the noun, "magical" is the adjective, "magically" is the adverb. Still Merriam-Webster lists "magical" and "magic" in the adjectival sense as synonyms to each other. Why use the noun form as a second adjective? I'll never get that.

    Right, the episode.

    Luna fans will orgasm over her getting so big a role on her own. I'm personally of the opinion that Celestial is #BestPrincess, but sure, no harm. I had some issues with the presentation of her, though. Not only did I roll my eyes at her having created the Tantibus in order to punish herself, but also that she could apparently have saved herself years of self-imposed nightmares by asking the others if they liked her? She sure came around real fast at the end there, and it all felt pretty plastered on. I'm also worried Luna will get typecast into being the "dream princess" exclusively; the fact that even her own episode was about that is a bit worrying. But maybe she hadn't shown up enough to have to worry about that yet.

    The dream sequences were pretty cool, though. A few references, some callbacks to previous episodes (Flutterbat was neat, but the Power Ponies did not have to return however). I particularly liked the return of their, uh, Rainbow Forms? Y'know, these things? I liked their design and it's nice to see them again. Princess Big Mac and the giant Derpy was maybe a bit on the nose, but I felt like it worked without coming off as super forced.

    All in all, pretty fun episode, but the biggest thing about it was that Luna was in it, and I'm not sure I like where they seem to be taking her. They are making more of a character out of her, however, and that is rewarding to see. Here's wondering if they'll do more with Celestia as well, of if at least one sister needs to stay mysterious.

                        We back? We back. Actually looking forward to the movie coming out next week, and up until then...

                        S5E14 - Canterlot Boutique

                        Rarity was once my favourite character. She had all the opportunity to become the stock antagonist socialite, but it was turned around in a way that really impressed me. She and Twilight was best pony for season 1 and 2, because they both seemed so counter-trope. On the other end of the spectrum was Rainbow Dash, who was an obnoxious douche with a flat portrayal. It's interesting to see that when I assess my 2015 Best Pony list, the top spot is shared by Twilight and... Rainbow Dash, and while Rarity isn't at the bottom (sorry, Flutters, but you're just so boring to me), she shares a split fourth place with Applejack.

                        What happened? Well, Rainbow Dash got a lot of episodes that developed and challenged her, and Rarity... didn't. She's not quite as bad as Fluttershy for being a one trick pony, but she's probably the pony which is the most thematically framed-in. Canterlot Boutique suffers a bit from that, because while it's not really a Suited for Success 2: Electric Boogaloo (instead of having to stress to accomodate the wishes of her customers, she is understimulated by all her customers just wanting the same thing), it's also really easy to see the similarities (that song, for example, which for reference was mediocre). It's just always about dresses with her, isn't it? It kind of annoyed me that once she finally got an episode, it's about the same thing she's been doing in the background for the whole run of the show.

                        That said, if you get over all that, the episode was good. I liked the dynamic between Sassy Saddles and Rarity, for example. It's clear that they both want for the shop to succeed, and both of them have personal stakes in that, but they have different ideas of what that success looks like, and Rarity gets pulled into Sassy's version before she rediscovers what the core of her craft is. That doesn't make Sassy bad, per se; instead, it makes Rarity an inspiration figure for Sassy, which I think was great since Sassy showed no signs of needing guidance at all, in fact quite the contrary. The characterisation of Sassy was easily the best part of this episode, though that doesn't mean I like her much.

                        Solid and really good for many reasons, but not among my personal favourites.

                                    S5E15 - Rarity Investigates!

                                    Short on time, so gonna be blissfully brief. Liked the episode doing new things. The first part was kind of slow, but I also appreciate that they held off a bit on the trenchcoats, so that the detective part didn't overstay its welcome. I also felt that Rarity came off as getting her "fashion thing" plastered on a bit at the beginning of the investigation sequence, but it felt more natural later on. Rarity and Rainbow Dash is a rare combo, but fruitful since they cover so little common ground, provided the writers manage to find some ground to base them both off. That happened here, I felt.

                                    The episode cast Rarity in a new light, which she sorely needs. On the other hand, it also didn't really dare to not include the same old fashion thing we've seen for ages. I think there are more themes in Rarity than the ones she's stuck in right now, and this episode helped to bring that out. I liked this one more than Canterlot Boutique.

                                    On a side note, Mane6 just launched an Indiegogo campaign for the new incarnation of Fighting is Magic named Them's Fightin' Herds. Check it out if you're curious.

                                                S5E16 - Made in Manehattan

                                                I swear someone on the writing team ships Charity/Applesack OTP. They've had more coupled episodes than anyone else, I think. And they do go well together: as I've said earlier, while they are very different, they're actually in some fashion the mane ponies with the most in common as well.                                                         Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
                                                        I imagine she's done the flying thing to death. I do like that the general approach is to have the main cast turn up and catalyse others to action rather than solve everything themselves.
                                                        Yeah, that's one of the things that make this season interesting, and also kind of hard to compare to the other seasons. I can't really decide if I like season 4 or season 5 better, because they kind of do things sufficiently differently to be hard to compare.

                                                        And speaking of doing different things, there was a new movie premiered yesterday! It's been a pony-filled weekend for sure. I caught the US premiere on stream, but I've been told that it was cut to make room for commercials. I'll have to watch it again once the full version comes up up somewhere. Nonetheless, I think I got the gist of it anyway. So without further ado...

                                                        Equestria Girls 3: Friendship Games

                                                        Okay, let's be honest. This is the third time they use this structure: there's a thing at the school, they have to team up to compete in it, there's some complication in the meantime and in the end there's a big boss fight. On the one hand, that can come off as feeling bland and uninspired. On the other, it makes it kind of easy, and interesting, to compare the three and see what they did differently.

                                                        One thing to compare is main character. In EG1, it was clearly about Twilight. She was the one we experienced the world through, and while Sunset Shimmer was the new and interesting one, it was still a very traditional Twilight-and-her-friends-but-mostly-Twilight-saves-the-day affair, complete with friendship rainbow laser at the end. In Rainbow Rocks, Twilight was still kind of in focus, but Sunset was the one who got her story told. It was a redemption arc, where she got to be accepted by the humane six (that's a term now) and in turn again accepted the tutelage she had rejected from Celestia from Twilight instead. Rainbow Rocks probably contained more character development than any other FiM feature has ever done, and it did get a much better reception.

                                                        Friendship Games, in turn, is the story of human!Twilight. In a way, human!Twilight is a Moondancer character, since the comparison between her and pony!Twilight are unavoidable. Even if it's not quite as traumatic as in Amending Fences, and human!Twilight seems to even yearn for something to change in her life while Moondancer was very guarded to that, the progression from seclusion to openness is clear. When human!Twilight unleashes the magic at the end, the transformation shows that even in those with only academic interests, seclusion and ego-focus can corrupt as surely as "evil" intentions, and highlights the importance of friends to ground and check you. This development is is one of the strongest parts of the movie. We get to see human!Twilight in two separate situations in the span of one movie, and the events that brought her from one place to another, and that alone makes her character more useful than pony!Twilight ever was in EG1. Pony!Twilight couldn't develop here, because EG is a spinoff. Human!Twilight is on home turf, however, and her character is stronger for it.

                                                        Likewise, there's much good happening for Sunset in this movie. In EG1, she was defeated as a demonic villain (no offence), and in Rainbow Rocks she was redeemed into friendship with the humane six and accepted a position of tutelage. Here, we get to see her become the one to offer redemption and, in a sense, become the tutor for human!Twilight in the ways of friendship (and yeah, I fanboyed when she turned phoenix-alicorn, I fanboyed so hard). At the end of the movie, she has three different kind of relations tied to her, which makes her remarkably complex, not only for FiM or children's animation, but for much of popular entertainment media. When it comes to character writing and development, Friendship Games is some of the best we've had from the franchise yet.

                                                        Which is good, because otherwise it's really kind of bland.

                                                        In terms of the school event that frames the movie, this one was by far the least interesting one. In EG1, there's a tight tie between the real villain Sunset and Fall Formal, and the tropes regarding proms and teen highschool drama lurk in the background. In Rainbow Rocks, the villains were also very tightly tied to the Battle of the Bands, and the music scene in the community provided another touchstone. The Friendship Games... doesn't manage that. There's not a strong enough idea of what the Games are for them to become significant, I found, and they mostly felt like something to drive the plot forward rather than something that was actually important. Hell, not even the characters knew what the Games were actually about before they happened (but they were still expected to train?). The only part that really had any punch was the second one. The first one was only there for a song (which admittedly was among the better ones) and the third was cancelled entirely due to sudden demons. Given that the main story about human!Twilight deals with her everyday life, I think setting it in a more everyday surrounding would have benefited the movie more. Either that, or the Games could have taken a stronger profile, like saying "this is a football game!" or "it's a swimming competition!" and not just been a bunch of events bundled together.

                                                        And speaking of event relevance to the actual threat, let's talk about villains. Sunset was a functional, if very traditional, villain. The Dazzlings stepped it up a notch, not only being threatening but also carrying personalities and... they were just cool, weren't they? Come on, they had style, they had allure. They managed to show the thrilling side of being bad, which was what they were supposed to, naturally. I know many considered them and their music the highlight of Rainbow Rocks. By comparison, Friendship Games has Principal Cinch as main villain. That... just doesn't cut it. Sure, you dislike her, she's actually really horrible, but she's just so bland. Now, there's something to be said for a down-to-earth bad guy without high-flying plans, but the main problem is that she's not a villain so much as an obstacle. You know she's not going to be the one transforming into a demon at the end. The lack of a proper villain doesn't have to be a problem, but when they choose to cast the movie in the same form three times in a row, the comparison becomes obvious, and so does the shortcoming. If they want to go another route, they'll have to change more than the one thing.

                                                        Okay, quick round. The music was pretty cool, but Rainbow Rocks' was better overall. I liked Twilight's song at Crystal Prep ("I know there's more that's out there; another me I haven't met"), but damn did it remind me of Let It Go. Also, Unleash the Magic is a nice villain song, but it reminded me of "Stop the Bats". Just sayin'.

                                                        The Crystal Prep students that were supposed to be counterparts to the humane six were interesting for a gimmick. I think those will show up in fanworks but probably not get developed more elsewhere. The movie could have pushed their angle harder, made it about individual rivalries to lend relevance to the Games, but alas. Opportunity lost, I think, but then again, some of the gimmicks might not have stood up to closer scrutiny anyway. Also, don't call her Indigo Zap, writers. That should have totally been Lightning Dust and you know it. She even has the earrings!

                                                        The way Shining Armor looked at Dean Cadance, I wonder what happens in her office after hours. In other such news, they really have to decide about Flash Sentry. Either make something of him or just cut him and free up another minute for something useful. I don't hate him as such, and I think he might be a cool romantic interest for human!Twilight, but please make him something more than a human roadblock.

                                                        Final score? Well, I do like it. When I watched it the first time, I thought it was really good, and from some perspectives, it really is. But then I watch it again, with a bit more distance, and I see all the things that Rainbow Rocks did that is just missing here. I would say that Rainbow Rocks is still the best Equestria Girls movie. This one isn't all that far behind, but it is behind. Still totally worth seeing, though.
