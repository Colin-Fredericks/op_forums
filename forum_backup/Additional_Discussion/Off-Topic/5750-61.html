                                                        I could talk for days about how important a character Superman is to me, and I may yet make such a post, but I want to talk for a second about Batman.

                                                        The creator perhaps most directly responsible for the vision of Batman we will see in the upcoming movie is Frank Miller, a writer and artist heavily influenced by the noir genre. He always struck me as a writer with more style than substance, but his style was entertaining, and since I read to be entertained, that was fine.

                                                        But something changed inside Mr. Miller on 9/11. PTSD has made of him nearly a self-parody whose characters are an author-insert (for they are all the same character) who is an ultra-violent, racist, xenophobic fascist proposing brutal Final Solutions to make the world safe from scary foreigners. A lot of people find his post-9/11 work offensive, and I suppose I do too, but mostly I pity him. He is a wounded man who needs healing.

                                                        I go into a poor fellow's psychiatric history because it occurred to me today that I can say nearly all of that about Batfleck, too: At least judging by the promotional material, Batman seems to be a dark-minded man who was changed by the movie's equivalent of 9/11 into an ultra-violent, racist, xenophobic fascist who want to enact a brutal Final Solution against an alien to keep the world safe from scary foreigners to the point that he becomes self-parodic.

                                                        I tend to suspect this parallel is unintentional just because I do not credit the movie's creators with the savviness to make such allusions, but even so, it seems savagely tone deaf to me to make such a character choice; to make Batman an expy of one of his most famous creators who is now perhaps most famous for being sick. It feels like a bad satire.

                                                        That is not to say there is no room to tell stories about people traumatized by a city being destroyed and thousands of people dying in the tragedy, but...I dunno, man. In this case it just seems super uncool.
