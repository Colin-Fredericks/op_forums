                                            I hear that a lot, and I get where they're coming from, but the big thing for me is that that first part informs the final part(you know what I'm talking). The similarity between the two confirms the reasons and the ideas at work in case people missed the massive buildup throughout the entire series to that point(and beyond it). It is important to establishing perfect(or near perfect, or at least better, depending on who you are) clarity.

                                            I bring this up namely because it seems like most people(Not you, Anubis) I read talking about the opening also have problems with the sharp turn of the ending.

                                            As for it's pacing, asides from what they have to cram in there, admittedly, it almost felt too long for me. THe gears shifted right when I was starting to go "Okay, stop lying to me", which is actually a really good time to do so, if not perfect(and clearly it was for some, hence our conversation). For me it worked out.

                                            My final thing on the beginning is just that I loved the homage as well as the fan skewering. All talk of "the opening explains the ending" aside, it is fanservice-y...but for me, not in a bad way. I love the nods to the genre and the loving acknowledgment of it-it was like the guys at Shaft and in the Quartet were trying to say that they really did like magical girls and all the standard things about it even if they were then going to proceed to rip it all a new one. Urobuchi said he wasn't deliberately deconstructing the genre, and even if he is lying,I liked how this felt like a nod to the fact that he had still done just that by giving the normal presentation of the genre its' dues.

                                            The fan-skewering was also great-I'm sorry, but I have this horrible thing where I love the audience getting slapped(playfully or lovingly, or at least parentally, rather than malevolently or spitefully, BURTON) by the creators, and the opening also did that. It positively rolled around in fan stories and opinions and gleefully covered itself in fan culture like a pig in mud, and then looked at the audience and asked "Now come on, did you really think your stories would actually work?" with a big mischievous grin on its' face. It went on just long enough and with the right tone that I wasn't really that bothered by it.

                                            WARNING: The spoiler doesn't give away specific details, but it does allude to the nature of the ending which will of course somewhat inform your viewing if you haven't seen the movie yet. I don't think it hurts knowing the nature of the ending in the sense I'm talking about(as people might get a sour taste in their mouth otherwise), but just in case I put it in spoilers. Just being cautious, I'm speaking in the vaguest of terms, but it does allude to a definitive quality of the ending. That said, it does inform the main reason I like Rebellion, which has less to do with itself and more to do with Madoka on the whole.
                                            Of course, the big reason I like this movie is that while it is anywhere from a good-if-at-times-tiring film to a really solid piece on the whole(and I am in the latter, as you can tell), it's what it does for the whole of the series. It clearly needs the final installation for me to say anything concrete, but it's natural progression of and challenge to the ideas at work really elevate what the series is asking and semi-answering, and they way it does that just about nears perfection on the technical grounds.

                                            It's not what I would call the best(that goes with all my bias to Utena), but the Rebellion took an already impresively told story and took it just about the pinnacle of actual story craft. What it does it greater than what it is, is what I'm saying.
