        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        Ok, pipe dream here, but picture an open-world single player game (I imagine Bethesda style, but Ubisoft style would work fine if that's more your cup of tea). You've got your big city sandbox, populated with humans, vampires, werewolves, mages, the works. You start out as a human, and the initial questline has you stumbling into the mysterious and spooky things that walk the city at night. And from there, the sky is the limit. You can play the whole game as a mortal caught up in the machinations of these monsters you just learned existed, take to hunting them, or even become one of them (obviously for splats that are tied to heritage there would have to be a sidequest where you discover your great grandpa was a werewolf, or whatever). Different quests become available or impossible for different splats, so there's a ton of replay value.

        EDIT: Sidenote, I think this would work better set in nWoD, just because nWoD is generally more crossover-friendly, and the modular approach lends itself nicely to sandbox play. But I definitely wouldn't say no to such a game set in oWoD either.
        I think we've all dreamed about that idea at one time or another, but I think there's some serious practical difficulties in getting it to work. I see technical problems with adapting the Obsidian model to a dense open world. Both Fallout and Elder Scrolls are about worlds that are rich with mysteries to discover but sparse of population. There's definitely an issue of how you pack that many characters into a more densely packed urban setting, but I also worry that it undermines one of the core gameplay advantages of the Obsidian model. For me at least, the most addictive appeal of these games is the question of what's over the next hill, and the gameplay loop is predicated on venturing out into the wild and loading yourself down with loot. I'd be concerned that adapting it to a modern day metropolis would strip out a lot of advantages that come from these games and result in a game that shows the seams more readily.

        There's also just the question of practicality regarding budget and manpower. It strikes me that fitting nine different breeds of supernatural into the game and trying to make them distinct would be an overwhelming task, and even if it was possible, the rest of the game would suffer. There would be so much gameplay balance to achieve and balancing all the cross-splat narratives would be a nightmare. I suppose you could simplify things by stripping down the importance of clans/tribes/etc. and reducing it to the core Vampire/Werewolf/Mage model, but even then it would be a pretty ambitious project. And that's not even taking into account managing the distinct politics and morality systems that make each of the game lines unique.

        Maybe the best way to swing it would be to build a game about an individual splat (probably Vampire) and then release DLC expansions for the other splats that are set in the same system? That way you could start with a more approachable model and then use the preexisting market and asset design to create what amounts to pseudo-sequels set within the same model, modular stories that can interact with the status quo of your existing city.

        Originally posted by The young man in the cafe View Post
        My idea of the perfect WtF game would be kinda of like Fallout New Vegas, but with a "hunt planning" feature similar to the preplanning from Payday2 for the more important quests, where you can call in favors from mortals, spirits and other being that you have helped through out the game to help crack the tougher targets of the sacred hunt, by having allies sabotage the enemy defenses, gather intelligence and leave supplies for the pack and other such things.

        and if you play as an Elodoth of the mastermind face, a Rahu of the tactician face or an Irraka of the scout face you get some sort of bonus to preplanning, like help costing fewer favors for the mastermind, or a discount on intelligence for the scout and a discount on more assault style assistance for the tactician. However, calling in favors requires you to help others in order to be owed those favors and some forms of help are only available if specific sorts of people or spirits owe you favors.

        and like I said, to make things manageable for the developers, this detailed hunt planning will only be available on certain quests, including main story quests and the more important side quests, including main story and major side quests in DLC.

        what do you guys think of that?

        EDIT: and of course you would have to manage your terrority, but I am not sure how to go about including that in the game.
        I think it's a neat premise. I loved the heist feature in GTA V, and it sounds like Payday 2 did a solid job with it too.

        I'm not too versed in WtF, but when I think "territory management", the first thing that comes to mind is State of Decay, a pretty great sandbox zombie game predicated on protecting, scavenging, and defending territory. It could potentially be a great jumping off point for a Werewolf or Hunter game.

        Also, I know it's been done to death, but I think the episodic adventure model Telltale has been following could be adapted to the crossover nature of Vampire pretty adeptly. It could be cool to take a singular city and tangle together narratives from a bunch of splats. The have their own stories that might be loosely, tangentially, or not at all related, but the decisions made in one have ripples across the metropolis' infrastructure and shows how all these vying groups of monsters impact one another even when they've never met.
