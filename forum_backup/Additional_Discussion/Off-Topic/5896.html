                  11-28-2013, 08:00 AM
                  As some of you may know, I´m currently making a role-playing game and I´ve started initial playtesting. Well, let's call it a fantasy heartbreaker. So far we've made characters and we did a short play session yesterday. There was no combat but some dice-rolling took place. The system is fairly simple, you roll as many d6 as dictated by your Trait and count any die that shows 4-6 as a Strike. Yesterday showed that success rate can turn very high, especially if you spend an Effort point to add a Skill rating to the roll (more dice). The reason you spend Effort to add Skills is because this way you can spend more Effort to add more Skills (you are not limited to a single Skill in this system).

                  About the characters they made, they stuck to a "traveling musicians" theme:
                  Embris violinist (premade racial template, patronizing attitude)
                  Rok lutist (also premade template, 4-handed eagle-folk)
                  Gnome cleptomaniac/flutist (he made the racial template, outcast from his own people)
                  ANIMAL (drummist, one-of-a-kind creature, based on the Muppets' Animal)

                  The idea was that you can make the character you want to play. So far, the game seems to have succeeded on that front.

                                          I just finished version 4 of my Exalted character-sheet and have uploaded it to my website for your enjoyment.

                                          Biggest changes are structural, since I redid the sheet nearly from scratch in Illustrator and gave most sections of the sheet their own layer. The sheet has nearly 50 layers. By doing this, I hope that making changes to the sheet will become much easier, since I can select everything within the same layer with a single click (so changing fonts is a breeze).

                                          I also replaced the Exalted logo with my own. It is not as cool as the official logo, but it will be easier for me to alter it as needed.

                                          I made one sheet with the material from the Scroll of Errata and one without. Both can be found here, under version 4.
