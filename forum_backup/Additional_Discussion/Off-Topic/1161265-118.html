                                I have to agree that I don't see the logic behind making weapon styles class feats. Those shouldn't even be general feats; they should be handled by proficiencies. The proficiency tiers seem perfect for this kind of thing, but for some reason they don't do anything for weapons other than increase your to-hit bonuses by tiny amounts. Why? I just don't see any rhyme or reason behind it.

                                As for lightly armored fighters... yeah, you can play one, if you feel like wasting one of your few class features. But, once again, why is it there in the first place? Isn't fighter supposed to be this blank slate, versatile class that covers many martial concepts? So why does it have a class feature that supports heavy armor? It would be easy to just make it apply to all armor and thus give fighters good AC no matter what they choose to wear.

                                "You can do it, but it'll be a pain in the rear and/or waste some of your features" has been the litany of people trying to play less than standard concepts in D&D for decades. Feels like this is something a new edition should try to improve on.

                                Of all the things that people say are "4E like" about PF2E, this is for once something I actually think is true... but not in a good way. 4E doubled down on some of 3E's restrictions, and 3E was already a hide-bound system to begin with. Like you couldn't really dual-wield if you weren't a ranger or tempest fighter. But if you were a ranger you couldn't not dual-wield if you wanted to melee - at least 3e/PF allowed you to take the archery style and switch between a bow and two-handed weapon.
