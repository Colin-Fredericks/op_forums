                The PF2 Alchemist looks like a lot of fun, and I’m glad to see that Alchemy will be a thing any character can dabble in with a Feat, though the Class will of course be better at it. I’m really, really glad to see an Alchemist that isn’t just a spellcaster by any other name. And it sounds like they’re putting a lot of thought into what exactly constitutes an alchemical item. For example, alchemists fire, acid flasks, thunderstones, and tanglefoot bags will all be alchemical bombs. Poisons will be alchemical items. And they’re drawing a clear line between potions (which are magical) and elixirs (which are alchemical.) I love all of this.

                Also, my concerns about the other classes we’ve seen having their abilities come at later levels is starting to be alleviated a bit. Between the classes we’ve seen and the Ancestries, it looks like they’re really trying to spread out features that in PF1 are extremely front-loaded, so instead of getting most of your features right away and spending the rest of your career increasing the numerical bonuses, you’ll continue getting new unique abilities right up to 20. And with the revised XP System, it might well be that playing a character from 1st to 20th isn’t such a monumental undertaking as it has been in the past. That’s my hope, anyway.

I don’t see how mutagens at 1st level would fix that though, they really don’t offer much more in terms of round-by-round decision points.

                                        Originally posted by Morty View Post
                                        Spreading out the abilities concerns me. We've already seen fighters and rogues get basic things on fairly late levels. They're avoiding &quot;bottom-loading&quot; the classes, but why? 4e did it and it worked well. Why shouldn't you get your class's iconic features on a low level?
                                        It’s not just classes. Races are getting spread out too. At 1st level, an Ancestry seems to give you between 6 and 10 HP, +2 to one physical Ability Score, one mental, and one floating, -2 to one ability score, two languages, and a movement speed. All the other stuff that has traditionally come at 1st level like Dwarves’ poison resistance or Elves’ keen senses are becoming Ancestry Feats, which you seem to get at 1st, 5th, 9th, 13th, and 17th.

                                        There are a number of design reasons one might want to spread out abilities more. For one thing, it allows you to get something interesting every level, instead of having a bunch of blank levels where all that happens is your numbers go up. It also helps work around the 1-level multiclassing dip problem. I’ve heard that Alchemist 1/Barbarian X or Alchemist 1/Barbarian 1/ Fighter X were pretty game breaking in PF1 thanks to Mutagens. Another reason is that it can help with the scaling problems at high levels. High level adventuring has always been a bit of a shit show, so they seem to be trying to extend the low and mid level experience up through the higher levels. And it’s really not a surprising shift, 5e did the same thing, Starfinder did it, 13th age did it. The fact that so many individual developers are coming up with the same solution to d20’s high level problem would seem to me to indicate that it’s probably a pretty effective solution.

                                        Now, like I said, I’m still leery of this change. If it still takes a year of weekly 4 hour sessions to get to 15th level, only now you’ll have the abilities of a 7th level character when you get there, I’ll be right there with you in saying it was a terrible decision. But if they speed up the pace of advancement as well as spreading out the abilities, I think that could be a great thing. It would actually be really nice to be able to count on getting to 20 in a 6-month campaign, especially if the scaling follows a fairly reasonable curve during that time rather than being pauper to demigod.

                                                        So, with goblins, elves, dwarves, halflings, and gnomes previewed for PF2, leaving humans, half-elves, and half-orcs to go, and knowing how Ancestry ability score adjustments will work, I’m beginning to see what my first house rule will likely be if I run it.

                                                        It’s confirmed that most Ancestries will get two fixed +2s, one floating +2, and one fixed -2. Humans and half-humans will probably just get two and floating +2s instead. All the races shown so far get one of their fixed +2s in a physical ability and one in a mental ability. Oddly, all of the small ancestries get +2 Charisma, which is weird especially for goblins.

                                                        Now, I’ve never really liked mental ability increases from race. The idea of some races just being inherently smarter, cleverer, or more charming than others due to biological factors just feels wrong. Normally I just chalk it up to “race in D&D also includes an element of culture” since race can also give you things like weapon Proficiencies. But PF2 is taking steps to separate physiological Ancestry features from cultural ones. Specifically, the physiological traits will be the base traits (size, speed, ability boosts and flaws, special vision types, and starting HP), while the cultural traits will come from Feats. There will even be a few character creation only “Heritage Feats,” which are physiological traits like poison resistance for dwarves. But for some reason, mental ability boosts are still part of the biology side of Ancestry, as are starting languages.

                                                        So here’s what I think I’m going to do. I’m going to split Ancestry into Heritage and Culture. Heritage will give the size, speed, special vision types, starting hp, and the fixed physical ability boost, and qualify you for Heritage Feats. Culture will give you starting languages, the fixed mental ability boost, and qualify you for non-Heritage Ancestry Feats. For the ability flaw and floating boost(s)... I’m not sure yet, since some flaws are physical and some are mental, and the floaters can be either... Maybe I just drop them both. Or maybe I assign the physical flaws to Heritage and the mental ones to Culture and have any Heritage or Culture with a flaw also come with a floating boost. It’ll require some thought, but this would be a much easier and less intrusive house rule than trying to divest culture from race in 5e.
