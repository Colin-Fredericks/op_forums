                                                    Originally posted by Morty View Post

                                                    The idea that drow need a good goddess to vouch for them in order not to be terrible is kind of silly to begin with, though.
                                                    I agree that the idea that the drow need a goddess to not be terrible isn't among the best, but they desperately need nuance and variety. It also makes sense that their pantheon is not wholly evil, given their history in the Realms.

                                                    That said, Eilistraee is not there just to make the drow "viable", nor she was added merely as a player option. Her concept is really unique and interesting,                                                     and an integral part of the drow history in Faerun. She also shows an aspect of that drow race that is quite deep and thought-provoking, and she is a valid character on her own.

                                                    In the FR, Eilistraee has been a presence in the drow's lives on Toril even before Lolth--she was even patroness of a major dark elven kingdom (that was exterminated by the elves, rendering her powerless for millennia). She is one of the goddesses that Ed Greenwood created for his own setting, long before TSR asked for more drow gods (he only used the opportunity to make Eilistraee official), so her current role in the setting doesn't come out of the blue, nor it is forced.

                                                    In her story, she was caught in the conflict between Lolth and Corellon (manipulated by her mother), and in the aftermath of such a war, when she was but a girl, Eilistraee's first thought went to the dark elves themselves. She knew that a time would come when the drow would be alone and surrounded by strife and cruelty. She was scared of the future that she had foreseen, yet she chose to forego the comfort, luxury, and safety of Arvandor in order to love her people, to share their fate and be one of them.

                                                    As the drow                                                     were poisoned by Lolth and forced to live an existence when even affection was taboo, Eilistraee shared their battles, their struggle, and desire for a better life. She took the role of a loving and nurturing mother for them, and even as she collected wounds after wounds in her battle, even as she was an underdog, alone against overwhelming forces, she turned those scars into lights for her "children". Not even once she gave up on them: she kept being by their side, bringing them her light, caring for them and offering them a different, hopeful path. In all this, she suffered in witnessing and experiencing their decline in spite of all her efforts, but also learned to know them like no other.

                                                    While the world sees the drow as monsters, Eilistraee knows that all of them are sufferers of abuse, neglect, and lack of affection from those that should love them the most. They abuse each other, they abuse their children, but--most of all--each of them is abused by Lolth, and the innocent tortured until their innocence is swallowed by cruelty. However, no matter how dark their souls can become, Eilistraee still sees them for their lost beauty, their shattered hopes, the part of them that was silenced by hatred: she treasures and nurtures it, to make it flourish again. The Dark Maiden gives to all drow who live in such a situation the pure, warm, and vibrant love that they were denied their whole lives. One that the goddess herself longed for but never received: the love of a mother.

                                                    And so, to a people who have been taught that they have no value except for their "status/power" (or except as "meat of Lolth"), who grew up without knowing genuine affection, Eilistraee shows the strength in caring for each other, in sisterhood/brotherhood, in unity, empathy, and compassion. She gives them value, appreciating and treasuring them for what they simply are and makes them unique (including the "demons" that they have to face every day--the evils that they have to defeat to take back their life).

                                                    As the drow are forced down the strife-filled path of Lolth's dogma, Eilistraee sings to each of them to make them *feel* the sheer joy of existence that they have been denied, of casting off their chains to freely chase their forgotten dreams and follow their hearts (her focus on joy and the arts is tied to this, to let the drow free to embrace and express their true and intimate selves after being constricted by Lolth's censorship, spontaneously and without fear. There are even moments like this among the rituals dedicated to this                                                     goddess, when she simply listens to her people letting out their emotions and experiences of the day as free-form messages).

                                                    In the end, Eilistraee does her best to gently lead *all* drow to rediscover life anew, to see all that they have been missing on, what their existence can be. She sets the spark within their souls alight once again, she lures them out of their comfort zone, to embark on a journey to see and marvel at what life has to offer, to open their hearts and make them understand that a different existence is not only                                                     possible, but that it leads to actual happiness and liberation. She's there in all the important moments of this journey, she helps her "children" in various practical ways (especially to thrive on the surface) nurturing, protecting and teaching them about this new surface world that is their forgotten (but rightful) home, like a mother would. However, she is careful to never impose anything on them or to forcefully intervene in their choices, as she wishes for her children to find their own path, and she knows that she can only support them in this.

                                                    That's her concept of redemption, and a relationship with her people that makes this goddess unique, and so valuable in my eyes: Eilistraee doesn't feel that the drow need to "atone for their sins" or something along those lines. In her eyes, they don't: they need to be saved and rescued from the horrors of living under Lolth's thumb, to be shown that things don't *have* to be that way.

                                                    I also find her concept,                                                     in general, to be inspiring. She's a woman who, despite all her wounds, never stopped seeing and healing the beauty in the broken, never stopped dreaming, smiling to life, and bringing hope to those who are lost in the cold of the night. Eilistraee *is* indeed that kind of beauty that can blossom even amidst suffering and despair, that no matter how much you wound and hurt her, still shines for all those who are lost in the cold of their nights.

                                                    So, in the end, she isn't just the token good team-mate, or a deity tacked on to satisfy players. Those were the reasons that led TSR to ask for such a deity, but the result is something entirely different.
