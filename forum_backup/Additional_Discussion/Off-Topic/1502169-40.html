        Madame Web does this weird thing where the movie is so hesitant in just getting to the good parts of its premise that it feels like you're watching the boring prequel of another movie that hasn't been made yet. It reminds me of that George Lucas bit from Patton Oswalt mocking the idea of whether you really need to see Darth Vader in the past when he was a little kid. There was this movie Dracula Untold (2014) which was supposed to launch the franchise and so this movie instead of being about Dracula as a vampire, it's a movie about how Dracula became a vampire. Totally unnecessary and uninteresting. Similarly in Madame Web, you have these three teenagers who are destined to become these spider-super-heroes but the movie is essentially about them before they become those superheroes. First of all, it's weird that it's three. Dakota Johnson mentioned that the movie went through some drastic re-writes. Maybe they signed contracts with those three actors because, in that version of the script, that made sense and then they couldn't just boot them again after the script changed. But it's the same issue: There's no need to have a whole movie about those three teens before they become Spider-super-heroes. It also leads to another weird thematic issue with the movie regarding Madame Web's precognition because the movie isn't quite clear on whether it believes in predestination or that individuals can change their fate. Lots of things in the story point to the former belief which is a weird message.

            The problem seems to be that Sony thinks it can just force another "spider-verse" franchise into existence that's competing with the very well received MCU Spider-Man, and the very well received animated Miles Morales focused Spider-Man movies... when they can't use Spider-Man.

            They keep trying to build up all these villians/anti-heroes and supporting cast... without a main character. Whether or not superhero franchises are even a good idea right now, people can forgive a certain amount of "building up a larger film" eating into films if the pay off is something that seems intriguing. What's the pay off with the Venom (who's apparently not even in this universe anymore?), Morbius, Vulture (from the MCU though) and Madame Web? Where is it going? When Fury mentions the Avengers Initiative in Iron Man 1, people starting being very forgiving about some of the "MCU world building" intruding on individual stories because the pay off was getting an Avengers movie at the end of it.. and that worked out pretty well. There's no thread in the stories to follow to see where it leads. It's just, "Spider-Man supporting cast and rogue's gallery characters" going nowhere.

            It's also kinda weird to me because there's a Spider-Man antagonist that's basically been completely ignored for decades outside of the comics, who's big thing is that he can't take Spider-Man out on his own, so he's been behind the scenes manipulating both conflict between Spider-Man and other heroes (or at least potential allies like Morbius), and tormenting Spider-Man by helping a lot of his regular enemies out: the Jackal.

            The Jackal is basically the bad-guy version of Nick Fury for Spider-Man. While he's heavily associated with the much hated Clone Saga, that's skipped a mine-load of stories before that (and him cloning himself to constantly "die" or get captured while the original gets away was never really a problem).

            Setting up a "behind the scenes manipulator" showdown between The Jackal and Madame Web (who did not need her own origin story movie) while picking someone like Ben Reiley or Jessica Carpenter as their central Spider-Person could actually be interesting. Even for people that don't know the comics, they can get invested in the stakes of a battle over the fate of a hero (esp. one that's easier to sell as having potential to just go bad and stay there than Peter Parker) as one person in the shadows is trying to guide them onto the good path, and another is constantly creating enemies to throw at them until their too overwhelmed to stay on the straight and narrow. Bad guy stand alone movies like Morbius and Kraven can work a lot better if there's an expected pay off of them not being simple villains to beat up when the Jackal manipulates them into fighting the new core Spider-Person who has to struggle with want to do with complex moral questions around where the line is between hero and violent vigilante that talks big.

            But Sony would have to care about writers and directors more than short term financial lines going up to get to something like that.

                                    The thing about "judging people on the content of their character" is that it's an aspirational goal. We don't live in a world where people are treated equally based on things like color, sexual identities, or religious affiliations. In an unequal world, fairly judging people means you have to see the parts of them that advantage and disadvantage their lives. Identifying those aspects of society and working to eliminate them, or at least mitigate them, is what gets us to the just and equal society where we can only be concerned with the content of people's character and not have to take more into account.

                                    The line is from a speech referred to as the "I Have a Dream," speech because it's a dream. It wasn't reality then, and it isn't reality now.
