    I didn't mean to imply older settings even if two of my examples were definitely older ones (much older than Exalted which isn't a baby either), and there's a reason I picked both a fantasy and science fiction examples.. The applies equally well to more recent fantasy settings that keep the advantage of not trying to replicate real world cultures. Onyx Paths new The World Below will have the same advantage in being easier to RP away from your comfort zone as D&D, as does something like Lancer from Massif Press.

    There's also games like Kids on Bikes or Masks where age is less of an option, but there's more room to play around with other dials. Kids on Bikes is meant to be set when the players were kids (or at least try since you can have a fairly mixed group) so that everyone has a more shared idea of references and so on. Masks also focuses on younger characters, but as team of super-teens, you can do things like be an alien who doesn't get Earth cultural stuff, or be raised from childhood by an established hero in a not so health fashion that leaves you very out of touch with normal kids your age when you finally leave to join your own team, etc.

    There are certainly ways games and influence us away from our normal pick (which I'm prone to as well as I play a lot of games that don't do much to push you away from that if you don't want to).

                                                            That level of "historical accuracy" always feels weird to insist on. By '43 the German army had relented to the demands for more troops and softened it's stances on an all-male military. There weren't (openly) any women tank-gunners. But the German anti-aircraft gun battalions became fully integrated, so thousands of German women were part of gun crews by that point in the war (as well as a number of non-combat units like women taking over nearly all of the non-combat piloting roles because Hitler was obsessed with women not firing weapon more than he was with them not serving in the military). And while the war ended before it mattered, as it went on, even Hitler finally relented and signed off starting full integration of women into German's military forces as the Allies were starting to encircle Germany.

                                                            It is such a small deviation from history, and a big deal for the player's fun. I'm glad if everyone was happy with a compromise reached, but these sorts of appeals to historical accuracy always ring really hollow to me because I've never seen a historical game that's ever that consistently accurate (and of course history is also always a bit vague because history is full of holes and biases).
