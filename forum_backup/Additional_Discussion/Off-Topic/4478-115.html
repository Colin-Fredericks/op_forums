                        It lines up with numbers I've seen, as well as what I've observed personally.

                        Guns are a tool, they make violence more deadly and easier to inflict. Apparently going against the currently held belief, possession or non possession of a weapon of any kind does not induce or prevent incidents of extreme violence.

                        Likewise, the difference between what is considered illegal and what is considered legal in the US for firearms when used in an extreme violence situation is relatively minor.

                        Tracking guns would allow an identification AFTER the fact in shootings, but would not prevent such incidents. Significant new restrictions on gun possession would reduce the incidents in which LEGAL guns were used in violent incidents, but (IMO) are not likely to have a significant impact on the incidents given the readily available black market for weapons. I can see no way to reduce the black market for weapons in the US to a low level without draconian weapon sweeps given the established culture and manufacturing infrastructure.

                        Purely IMO, chasing gun ownership and restrictions, while needed for general order and improvement of the existing systems, is not directly or effectively indirectly addressing the issue of the number of people that commit such acts.
