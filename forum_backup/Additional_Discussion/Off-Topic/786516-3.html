    Hmm, favorite trans characters? Not really. I have to mirror Baroness Nerak. Again. And of course she's goth as well. WTF's my fetch doing in the US? Anyway, Lesbian/Bi characters do it for me. What I self-identify as is a bi-sexual woman, not transwoman, so... Ah wait! Got one! I quite enjoy the Misfile webcomic.

    Like I already mentioned, over here, for the last 2 years, there has been somewhat of a media offensive regarding the trans topic. Visibility and representation are at a all time high. Articles in all the popular magazines and newspapers, documentaries on tv, one of the characters in the most popular local soap has been outed as trans, etc. Let's see, this week it's 2 articles in the 2 most popular women's magazines, oh, and a transwoman won Holland's Next Topmodel (ok, not really Belgium, but we share media to an extent). It's actually all getting a bit grating. Like we're being hyped or something. Still, it's for the best, I guess.

    Much of the above is thanks to the efforts of the LBGT community. So, in principle, the community is very supportive. In practice, honestly, at the big get-togethers, it's a bit segregated. If you conform to the gender binary, and actually don't look like a trans, it's of course very easy to mix. Anyway, I do conform, am very passable and I actually go 'stealth' as much as I can so I can socialize with others without the baggage. I am rather relieved I can do this, and yes, I am not really proud of this tendency at times. But like I said: I self-identify a bisexual woman, not a transwoman. I'm not ashamed to be a transgender and I can and do talk freely about it, but it feels so good to be treated as just a woman, you know? So, in short: it's still far from ideal if you don't conform to the binary standard, even in the LBGT community, because if you don't, you can stand over there with the trans-clique.

    Now, let's move on to the medical experience. In the university hospital in Gent, they have the 'Gender Team', a multi-disciplinary team, and they can provide everything, from diagnosis, counseling, legal help, HRT, epilation, FFS, SRS, etc. It's an all-in experience, as it were, and a good one at that. They even go as far as to make the things that are not covered by social security, such as FFS, affordable. I simply have no words to express my appreciation for these people.

                            I'm a bit late to the game, and it's been rough lately, but I'd like to give my own thoughts if that's ok?

                            I realized I was trans probably around 5-7... but due to home life and other stuff suppressed it a lot, to the point that I didn't think of myself that way till I was 21. North Dakota and my father are not exactly kind towards those who are different, and in my home town I already had problems because it was very small, they didn't like "outsiders" (true story, over 80% of my grade school class were related to the rest, as cousins generally), so... I consider myself a lesbian woman.

                            I'm going to mostly mirror Baroness here that I identify/enjoy lesbian and bi characters more than trans, BUT... I think that's in part due to sometimes a real problem with the presentation of them. I'll second Misfile though.

                            On treatment by the umbrella groups... I've had some pretty awful experiences with gay men. And considering I have a bit of fear towards white men for past history, though I try and keep it reasonable, and living in a very white state... I've not taken that sort of thing well. I am not holding a grudge or anything, but it makes me hesitant. I feel like there's a real set of problems not just for trans, but younger people and those who aren't white (especially if you mix these). Look at pride events and how much they can cost, or look at the recent history of Christopher Street Pier in New York City. I don't think it's a universal thing, but there are those groups who through ignorance or malice can cause harm (more often ignorance, but malice can be real, such as in the case of some TERFs).

                            To me, I find sex kinda not nearly as meaningful a term as gender, and frankly what I'd say has no criteria that makes sense. I studied a lot of biotechnology which has a lot of genetics and such, and generally take a strong interest in biology (and had to take as much biochemistry as any pre-med or pharmacy student at the uni), so I feel I know a bit about what I'm talking about. Genetics has so many cases where people don't fit into the neat boxes of male or female, so it doesn't work. You can't look just at physical structure externally or even really internally, because there are many many things that show it doesn't work (as a few, intersex, issues where someone who is assigned a female gender develops male genitalia in puberty, etc...). Hormones are so wildly variable as to not work. In the end, gender is the only one that can be identified reasonably well, and that's because the person can say (publically or just to their own self) who they feel they are.

                            I don't know if this is helpful at all and hope no one minds.

                                I think that most people probably don't eroticize their own junk. If it's all in working order, it's just sort of there, and you probably don't think about it too much except when something is not working properly.

                                Also, to add to that: expressing yourself in a more masculine or feminine manner doesn't have to have implications for your identity. We're all somewhere on a spectrum between completely "male" and completely "female," and male and female anyway are imprecise social constructs that only hint at the underlying reality, which is that what we recognize as gender is a complex set of loosely-linked traits that each exist on separate spectra. There was a recent (often-misquoted) paper [http://www.pnas.org/content/112/50/15468.abstract] on brain sex indicating that most people have brains that are a mosaic of "male" and "female" traits. Likewise with other gendered traits.

                                It's the same with gender expression; we all have personality traits that are stereotypically male and female, especially when those categories are so broadly construed as to define bravery as "masculine" and empathy as "feminine." Yet, we (almost) all can be brave, and we (almost) all can empathize with others. Being able to switch modes of expression makes you flexible, but it doesn't make you (for example) "not a man."

                                However, if on the other hand, something about being a man felt "off" or "wrong" to you, then there might be an issue with your identity. But I don't get the sense that that's the case, from what you've written.

                                It reminds me of a bit from Susan Stryker's essay [https://tidsskrift.dk/index.php/KKF/...w/44377/84150]. We're obliged to construct our identities around ideas of an essentialist masculinity/femininity divide, but those ideas are themselves constructs. Which, I think (if you'll let me drift even more off topic) is where a lot of the fear towards transgender people comes from, because if you think too long about the implications of trans-ness, you're at risk of (as Stryker says) discovering the "seams and sutures in yourself."

                                            This thread has been really helpful for me. I feel like I should share my very messy story. I've typed up variations of this for this thread over the past few days, but never posted it. A bit because it may be off topic.                                             And out of shame, because I have done things I regret.

                                            I had a lot of difficulty going though puberty. It took me a while to realize this was less because I wanted boy parts, but because my family's fear of me becoming a victim of sexual violence - taken advantage of- had a negative impact on how I viewed both genders. My family was very much against me moving away for college, because girls shouldn't move away for college. They wanted me to go to the same local fourth tier university my mother did (Thankfully my mother supported me going to the college I wanted). Later, when I kicked my college boyfriend out and decided to live alone, they worried because it was dangerous for a young lady to live alone. I brought up joining the Peace Corps, and got stories about how dangerous it was to be woman overseas.

                                            I did not want to grow up, because growing up meant that fifty percent of the population would cease to see me as an individual, but rather something to fuck. I hated that. I hated being a woman because it was barrier against doing what I wanted with my life. Because of this, I had negative views of feminine woman. How stupid do you have to be to want to be treated as a thing, instead of a thinking individual? The bar for my expectations of men was set so low, that I made a lot of poor choices for who to be in a relationship with.

                                            Now, I'm 33. I realize that my earlier views on gender were colored by my upbringing. I still have a negative reaction when someone brings up little girls being princesses. Once I expressed an interest in websites that have sciencey clothing for girls, someone else expressed a need for pink Dora the Explorer clothing items for boys. I had to stay quiet, because my first reaction was "WHY! Why would you do that to your son!?!" Not rational, very awful. I'm ashamed to post these thoughts in a Trans-Gender thread, because I feel like I've been part of the problem sometimes.

                                            The majority of my RP characters start out in my head as male. I switch them for real life play, but on forums, I keep them male. When I was planning on my Exalted Quest Game, I briefly considered making the main character female. We already had a male quest character on the Exalted forum and I have often said that there was a general need for more female characters in stories. I had such a negative reaction to writing about a female child going though puberty, I kept the character male. I couldn't bear going though female puberty again. I knew I couldn't deal with someone spouting the same "Girls should be Careful" crap I got as a teenager. I did not want my character to be limited by their gender, not for a first writing project. Now I know that the odds of that happening on this forum are zero, but I did have a nasty flame war with one of the more conservative members about sex. The fear was not easily extinguished.

                                            Yet, when I make an MMORPG character, they are female. As a child, I identified with gender neutral characters like Data or Odo. As an adult, I still identify with them (see Gir Avatar) but also with female characters. The Agender Identity intrigues me, but the label never seemed to fit me right. I don't care if you call me sir/ma'am, he/she, but if you ask, I'll tell you I'm female. I think Agender expression fits me better and I am very happy to discover that was a thing.
