                                                        Everyone does this to some degree. The vast majority of what people perceive as "taste" or "flavor" is actually aroma stimuli not something coming from the taste buds in the mouth. Smells we associate with specific taste-experiences help prime the body for the assumed type of interaction. Your brain making you experience the sensation of eating your favorite cookie when you smell it stimulates increase salivation to increase the amount of enzymes in your body that break down the simple carbohydrates in sugars and simple starches. Your brain triggering a similar response when exposed to high sulfur odors (whatever nasty food that gets linked to, rotten eggs frequently) primes the gag reflex because many harmful bacteria you don't want to eat give off sulfur gases even if sulfur itself is generally harmless to ingest.

                                                        Children that are very sensitive to this tend to grow out of it as their palates expand and they eat more diverse foods as that forces the brain to assume less based on initial scent impressions.

                                                        I could also tell if a TV was turned on 3 rooms away, by sensing the output of static electricity from the screen.
                                                        This is a reasonably common phenomenon as well. Children hear more of the higher register, meaning they can pick up on electric whines adults don't. We literally grow out of this as our skulls mature and our ear canals change shape until that range goes away for the vast majority of people that experience this for a significant part of their childhood.
