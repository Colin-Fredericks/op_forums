                            Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
                            Japan seems to want to keep that language and cultural barrier as high as possible with Western fans. That despite the wonders of international simulcasting, the product is ultimately still made by the Japanese and for the Japanese...a first-world country that still remains behind on gender politics, not just in government policy but to even have the conversation in the first place. That they're willing to brush off any criticism and declarations of boycotts as the word of silly Westerners that don't know any better. Or the blunt deflection of "you're going to read/watch/buy it anyways".
                            I don't think it's so much an anti-western attitude as it is simply a general cultural attitude that encourages more passive resistance than active resistance. For the most part in Japan, they have the attitude of, "If you don't like something, then don't buy it." If there's a book (or a game or a TV show or a movie or whatever) with material you find offensive or don't like, the attitude is, then just don't spend your money on it, problem solved. Rather than protesting and complaining and boycotting, go find something that you do like and spend your money on that instead. If you can't find something you like, then make it yourself.

                            Japanese media has a lot of material that people, even in Japan, find fairly offensive. But rather than trying to boycott or change it, people there just avert their eyes and ignore it. If something starts introducing elements into a series or whatever that a given individual doesn't like, then that person will simply stop following that show or whatever and find something else to watch instead. That's not to say that people don't complain, they certainly do, but they tend not to take communal action or lodge protests with companies or individuals over it. When they do (such as more recently with Kemono Friends and Kadokawa) it tends to be fairly exceptional and is still somewhat frowned on in the overall community.

                            This does create something of a cultural gap in Japanese people dealing with American/Western mindsets, where if there's something we don't like or find offensive, we'll complain and boycott the company until they finally change and start making something that no longer offends our sensibilities. Japanese media companies and creators find that attitude incredibly arrogant and it's one of the reasons why many of them aren't terribly keen on doing business with other western countries.

                                Anyways, on to a more happier subject, I went ahead and wrote up some custom Quirks for My Hero Academia!


                                Name: Rawhide
                                Type: Mutant
                                Effects: A primarily defensive type quirk that causes the user's skin to develop into a thick, leathery body armor. This quirk provides the user with increased resistance to damage, but because of their tough skin, it can be difficult to properly treat whatever injuries they do manage to suffer.

                                (-)

                                Name: Redirection
                                Type: Emitter
                                Effects: If utilized to its full potential, this quick can be nearly invincible. It allows the user to alter the vectors of their surrounding environment, changing the direction in which things move, while still retaining its full momentum and power. This quirk's only noticeable weakness is that whatever they're trying to manipulate has to already be in motion, first. If their reaction time is too slow, or they are unaware of an incoming attack, it is possible to bypass the otherwise perfect defense system this quirk provides.

                                (-)

                                Name: Pardon Me
                                Type: Emitter
                                Effects: A quirk that definitely should not be in the hands of the Villain Association, but probably will be anyways. It grants the user a weak form of mind control, which is activated by issuing a formal apology to the person or people you wish to affect. Once under the effects of this quirk's power, the subject will feel compelled to forgive the user of whatever wrongs they feel they've committed. The weakness of this quirk is that it doesn't give the user the freedom to walk around doing whatever they feel like without question. If the user robs a bank and gets caught by the police, they will still have to return the money they stole, but forcing the cops to forgive their transgression may prevent them from being arrested because of it.


                                (-)

                                Name: Random Selection
                                Type: Hybrid
                                Effects: A versatile, if somewhat unreliable quirk that was created from the combination of a mutant quirk that granted the power of shapeshifting and an emitter quirk which granted the power to duplicate other abilities. The user of this quirk possesses unstable DNA, causing their quirk to fluctuate between powers. Every hour, their current ability is replaced with a different one from a selection of 10 possible options. True to the quirk's name, the choice is completely randomized, with the user having no idea what kind of power they will receive next. The different options provided by this quirk include Enhanced Strength, Rapid Healing, Wings, Retractable Claws, Energy Blasts, Forcefield, Super Speed, Telepathy, Power Dampening and Physical Invulnerability.


                                (-)

                                Name: Magnetic Rail
                                Type: Emitter
                                Effect: An exceedingly deadly quirk for those specializing in ranged combat. It grants the user the power to create rings of magnetic force, which dramatically accelerates anything attempting to pass through them, much like a rail gun. The weakness of this quick is that the projectile is traveling so blindingly fast, that it is virtually impossible to control its trajectory once it's sent through the ring, so the user must take careful aim before using them.
