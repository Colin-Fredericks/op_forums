            Glad to know I’m not alone in that.

            For my own setting, I have dwarves live about as long as a hypothetical human who never got sick. Which is tricky to estimate, but from what I can tell, 100-120ish is a pretty good ballpark. Accordingly, I also say that humans can theoretically live that long with the aid of healing magic and alchemy. Most common folk don’t have access to the necessary resources for that, so the average is still somewhere around 80, 85. But some kings are said to have ruled for over a century. I do like elves to have long lives, but not as long as is typically seen; I think double the human lifespan at around 160-180 average with as long as 240ish being theoretically possible with the right treatment makes for a good, long-lived people without getting into territory where they’re still young after a century. Halflings I like to keep at pretty much normal human lifespans, with maybe a slightly higher average due to them being pretty hardy. 90-100 without magical aid, 120-130 with it. Same for gnomes. The one place I actually go longer than standard D&D assumptions is with dragonborn, who in my setting exhibit negligible senescence. It’s not clear if they have a maximum lifespan, but if they do it is sufficiently long compared to other humanoids that they may as well be ageless. However, there aren’t a lot around any more, as most of them were killed in the war with Bael Turath.

                Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post

But when you look at the PHB races, you’ve got dwarves living 350 years, elves living 750, halflings living 150, gnomes living 350 on the low end and 500 on the high end, and half-elves living 180. I can’t be the only one who thinks that’s a little excessive?

                Not really. I think having one of the shortest life spans further emphasizes the human's drive for diversity and improvement. They don't have the luxury of time on their side, so they have to work fast if they want to stay ahead of the game. If they continue to drag their heels through life, they'll wake up to find that they're past their prime, while their former rivals and allies are still operating at peak performance.


                But even then, not every race is as long lived. Half Orcs only live up to about 75 years, while Dragonborn only live around 80. And those are just from core races, the expanded list of races have life spans measuring similar in length to humans, with some being only a few years longer, or a few decades shorter.


                Also, I think the "long view" provides a rational incentive for why the more long lived races tend to look down on humans, despite their improvement. What with humanity's tendency to leave the consequences of their more reckless behavior up to the next generation to solve. But in the case of Elves and such, they will most likely be around during the next generation of humans, and the generation after that, and possibly the generation after that.


                From their perspective, the worst examples of humanity show up, create big problems, then die off and leave it for everybody else to clean up years later. And by the time they've finally started making progress towards recovery, the new generation causes some new problems, then dies out and the cycle continues to repeat itself.


                If I were an Elf, or a Dwarf and had to constantly deal with that crap, I'd have a pretty low opinion of humans, too!
