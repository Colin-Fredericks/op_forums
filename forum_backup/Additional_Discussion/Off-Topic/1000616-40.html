                I think the system, as it is right now, is essentially broken. For a democratic republic to function, you well informed voters who come out to vote. And unfortunately, America does not really have this. People don't vote and a lot of people frankly don't care. "Any politician is the same as any other - they're all crooks," is an old mantra and lots of people believe it. What that means is that special interests end up holding more sway with many politicians than the actual voters do. That's what's wrong with America's system - too much of the electorate simply isn't engaged and doesn't give a damn about the political system.

                I can't really blame people for that. I mean, what I keep coming back to is that before Trump got involved in the Republican primary, the prediction was that it would be Jeb Bush vs Hillary Clinton. And at the time I was considering just not voting at all and just giving up on the political process if it came to that. And, while I think Trump is a terrible scumbag, he has opened a lot of people's eyes. People are realizing that voting does matter, people are regretting they didn't vote, and a lot of people are waiting to be heard in 2018.

                So in a way, I would point towards someone like Trump as being an excellent method of fixing the system. By screwing up so badly, he's going to get people much more passionate about politics. They'll pay more attention to what's going on and be more willing to go vote. In theory, that's how you fix the system - by using your vote to remind politicians they work for you, not themselves and not special interest groups. Or at least that's what should happen.

                Unfortunately the political system in the US has been so heavily hijacked, rigged and gerrymandered that in many respects, having more people vote may not actually change much of anything. Even if more people show up to vote, in many places you already know who is going to win. And this means that the people who have to change the system are the very people who gained (and retain) power due to the system, which makes them the last people who will want to change it. And they have the power to limit what other people can do and how they can respond.

                So from where we're sitting right now, at the end of the day, the voters have to vote and hope that the people who have gained the most from the broken system are going to turn around and change things. Maybe burning down the system really is the only solution. At least sometimes it seems more reasonable that hoping the SOB's who broke the system in the first place, so that they'd get more power, will suddenly do an about-face and fix everything.

                I don't know. I'm feeling pretty cynical today. Maybe next week I'll be more bright, hopeful and cheerful about things.
