    Would you leave this world for a D&D world if given the choice?

    10-19-2025, 02:06 PM
    If you could leave this world to live in a D&D (or pathfinder) world, would you do it and why?



    In my case; I absolutely would since ALL D&D worlds, even the really hostile and shitty ones like Dark Sun have a factor this world does not; linear progression. Any commoner who has the simple gumption to do so, can better their lot in life by leaving the quotidian script society applies to their lives and venturing out into the unknown to find their fortune. The instant you start doing this, you start earning EXP (unless your DM is one of those milestone people), and you start seeing measurable improvement in yourself and you life. It sounds ridiculous until you realize that D&D worlds are lousy with buried ruins, lost keeps, monster lairs, and all manner of magicked insanity waiting to be discovered. A simple snatch and grab can set you up for a year, and a large enough score is a total quality of life upgrade.


    Here in our world you can work your ass off your entire life just like the aforementioned commoner, but if you step one foot out of society’s script you’ll face everything from social shunning to jail time. Barriers to your success will materialize like magic to make sure you stay mired in whatever social stratum the bastards in control arbitrarily placed you in. And despite claims of meritocracy, no amount of work will ever make a difference since it’s all about social standing and clout. Yes in D&D there’s things like bandits/brigands/pirates/thieves, but those already exist here in our world and have existed for all of time. Yes D&D worlds have plagues and such, but we just got out of a pandemic without any clerical healing to work with. There’s entitled and self-absorbed nobles and a feudalistic system, but what do you think the billionaires are trying to do with this country now? There’s monsters that can raid smaller settlements and infest cities, but weigh that against hate-groups, psychos, and gestapos running amok.

      I understand your frustration, but it wouldn't work out as well as you think.

      You're assuming everyone follows PC rules in D&D settings to begin with, but that's not the case.

      Most people in the setting don't get XP or follow level progression rules (level progression is universal only in 3rd edition, but even there XP isn't). Adventurers are exceptional people even at level 1. They get better stats, better abilities, better HP rules, better equipment, better everything.

      General society in D&D isn't all that far from the shitty situation in our world. Most people don't have regular access to Clerics and spells in general, at least not in a way they can control. Living as an adventurer, even if available to you, is hardship after hardship and most of your money going to things that may be beautiful, but aren't there for your comfort, but for your survival.

      Working under the conditions we do IRL is hard for a long list of reasons, and meritocracy is an offensive joke. But have you ever actually went hungry or faced physical danger? I did, and even being just a fraction of what D&D characters (or a lot of real people, actually) go through, I don't want to be there again.

      That's actually what makes the people in power so powerful, and lock us in shitty jobs: this is far from the situation our society could and should afford us, but is still far better than the alternative, even a fantasy alternative.

      Don't waste your time and energy wishing to live in a escapist fantasy. Use the escapist fantasy to help you cope, but otherwise take this anger and turn it into political action, as small as it may be, to improve your life. It's also a frustrating path, but the only one that can really pay up to any degree.

              You didn't ask if there are fictional worlds people would rather live in, you cited as specific style of fictional world and someone responded with very clear reasons why it's not actually better to live under those conditions.

              There are plenty of examples of fictional worlds where, yeah, I'd love to escape to them if that was somehow a possibility. This world does, in fact, suck. But D&D style fantasy worlds? Fuck no. I know this is a controversial piece of fiction, but if you want to understand why... go read/watch Goblin Slayer because part of the core themes of the show is that D&D style fantasy worlds are inherently screwed up in horrific ways. When your fate is ruled by dice, eventually the gods roll badly on your behalf... and unlike a player of a TTRPG you're going to experience it all for real.

              Someone wants to send me to the core worlds in Lancer? Cool. I'd love to be part of a post-scarcity near-utopia where the vast majority of humanity lives comfortable lives free to pursue their intellectual desires without worrying about jobs just to get by. People that want to change the cosmos in a grand fashion can go join the Auxiliaries if they want danger and excitement, but the rest of us get to be safe and sound doing whatever we find productive for society in our own ways and follow our motivations as we see best. A universe where the government isn't perfect, but is honestly trying its best to balance its vast population with a democratic system that could (and has in the past) voted in tyrannical governments as the most pressing political issue and is doing a decent job? Yeah, sounds good.

              Of course, since we can't just all hop in a portal and get zapped over to that, nor are trucks actually blessed with the power to yeet us into other dimensions... we're stuck here in reality and have to real with it. Part of reality is that real lasting change? It's slow. The idea that history is defined by these singular moments (or worse people) were special things happen and blam... everything changes? That's the scam. Real change is slow. It's frustrating hard work. It can take decades, even centuries, to actually get humans to change on a mass scale that's no longer going to be at serious risk of backsliding. Your grandchildren might die of old age before the fight you care about finally bares fruit. But it's never going to happen if nobody ever starts.

              There's an interesting bit of folklore where it seems like pretty much every agrarian civilization has it's own version of a "Rip Van Winkle" style story. As far as most academics conclude, these are not related as if some myth got passed around, but an independent phenomenon as it serves a distinct purpose for orienting early agrarian cultures towards long term social thinking. Building a successful agrarian society takes time. It takes generations to cultivate land until it can fully sustain the people you have, let a lone expand the population. People need to develop strategies that encourage activities meant to benefit later generations: good water infrastructure, planting long gestation crops, communal mills or presses, etc. So stories about men falling asleep only to wake up and see how far things have come along seem to be common because it says that yes, eventually, the hard work will pay off even if not for you.

              Modern highly technological societies (like anyone posting here probably lives in) reach a point where those stories get a bit lost because technology lets us build things up so much faster. We don't have to wait decades for certain cash crops to finally become profitable but if maintained will be profitable for generations forward. We have, in many ways, lost touch with that mindset and thus with a fundamental aspect of what makes civilization function. Our world isn't going to get better if we accept sorrow that what we build today isn't going to make our lives better at the cost of the joy of building good things for the future we never see ourselves.
