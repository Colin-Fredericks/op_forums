                    Originally posted by etherial View Post
                    At what point do the children's beliefs become important?
                    Nobody's born with beliefs, beliefs come from being educated and having experiences. Those beliefs should overwhelmingly come from the nfluence of parents.

                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    How do you do you feel home-schooling parents should have to balance raising their children with the skills they'll need to succeed past what homeschooling can offer, with this? What happens when teaching one's beliefs and teaching vital life skills come into conflict?

                    As a note, I have nothing against either home-schooling (in theory, I've met a few individuals that should not have home-schooled their kids because they really did not have the skills or commitment to do so) or teaching children beliefs. I had a religious education and do not regret it in the slightest, and I had a lot of friends that were home-schooled (my private religious school was very small, so I ended up doing a bunch of after school classes with the local home-schooling group for things my school and those parents couldn't effectively do). But I have also seen a lot of home-schooled students struggle intensely in college/university, or get sucked into unaccredited higher education that meant they had higher &quot;degrees&quot; that were meaningless to most employers.
                    Originally posted by Jachra View Post
                    So &quot;being able to teach their beliefs&quot; should include &quot;making them totally unfit for functioning in the real world&quot;? You don't think the state has a responsibility to protect those kids from their parents?

                    What about health care? Should parents be able to refuse to get their kids treated?
                    So, these two questions are similar so I will answer them together. Do I believe, in theory, that there is any degree of bad teaching where the government should step in and force kids out of home schooling and into public schools? Yes, probably so, but it isn't a matter of parenting teaching their children things I disagree with, but rather gross educational neglect - like children who are functionally illiterate or unable to do basic math. That sort of thing is often brought up by opponents of home schooling, but in truth it is exceedingly rare and numerous studies have been done that show that home schooling students do very well.

                    As for the possibility that some aspect of home schooling might lead a child to less success than some ore option - that may be true. Then again, who gets to define a successful life? Is it just about money? I think that there are more important things than just money and I don't think that the government should be deciding those things for everybody else. It's not as though there aren't literally millions of public school educated kids who end up in educational dead ends, have terribly dysfunctional lives, or end up woefully ignorant of basic information. I don't trust the government to decide what the absolute truth is to the degree that it should force it on every child in the nation, I see that as highly dangerous. So if somebody wants to teach their children that the earth is 6000 years old, I may not like it and I intend to teach my kids evolution, but I wouldn't think of forcing evolution in their children just as I wouldn't want those parents forcing young earth creationism on my children.

                    It's easy to say that the government's version of the truth is the one that should be mandated when you agree with the government. Imagined you were a parent that lived in a nation where something you found morally abhorrent was mandatory for all public school classes, you might be a little more sympathetic then about parents who want to opt out.

                    As for health care, that a different topic and education is complicated enough without clouding the issue wih something else.

                                                        Since we are identifying, I consider myself a libertarian-leaning conservative. For most of my life, I considered myself Republican, my family has a history of involvment with the Republican party as activists, volunteers, etc. I stopped identifying as Republican around 2010. I'm planning on voting for Gary Johnson, as he seems to be the sole reasonable candidate. I'm not 100% happy with his positions, but I don't need to be. Kasich was my first choice.

                                                        I personally feel the primary issue with both major political parties is their tendency to ban things they personally dislike. The assumption is made that government should act as a sort of moral force that prohibits actions simply because they are objectionable from one viewpoint or another, rather than the action causing actual harm to others. We've got one party that wants to keep loving adult couples from being married, and another that wants to force people to bake cakes. I find both equally reprehensible, and ultimately senseless. Prohibition doesn't work, whether it's gin, joints, or sex.

                                                        For all that, though, I'd be tarred and feathered at a Libertarian party gathering; I'm a "statist." I believe in things like basic social safety nets, effective and well-funded public schools, and a robust, if restrained, foreign policy. I think government sucks at doing just about everything, but some things only government can accomplish. Sure, governments have trouble getting roads built, but I can't imagine turning that responsibility over to private citizens. Half the time neighborhood associations can't agree on the proper height for grass.
