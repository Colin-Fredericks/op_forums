    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    While it's informative to study history, including how major nations rose and fell, there are limitations in such comparisons. Rome in particular feels like a very weird choice because Rome and the US never really had that many similarities. The Roman Empire took longer to fall from its height under Trajan, to the division into the Eastern and Western Empires than the US has existed.

    If we want to look at Roman history, the Eastern Empire/Byzantium is a more interesting comparison. While Byzantium lasted a very long time after its significant decline after a few extremely prosperous centuries not having to send resources to prop up the western provinces any more (the Roman empire's agriculture centers were in the east), it's decline was driven by the rise of new empires that changed the focus and balance of power between nations (compare the US during WWI to WWII vs. the post Cold War era), a plague (compare to COVID), and increasing ideological divisions (Byzantium becoming a Christian nation followed by Islam's rise in power leading to serious diplomatic tensions, and isolated Byzantium from later Christian nations that arose to their west, compare to the US having to handle diplomacy with BRIC despite major cultural issues, and how the US has diverged from the course of liberal democracy in Europe).

    It's not a rosy picture (of course, we're assuming a comparison to a nation in irreversible decline is valid), but probably more informative to what's really worth considering about what lessons from history can help inform decisions now.
    I disagree with the conservatives on this one, but there are some similarities. America is a Republic where both parties are saying that if the other party wins, it will be transformed into a despotic empire. Also, from the height of the empire under Trajan to the division under Diocletian is only 170 years. The U.S. has existed for almost 250 years. However, from the time of the decline of the Roman Republic and the age of Julius Caesar to the eventual fall of the Empire is over 500 years. A very long time indeed.

    Sorry Heavy Arms. I’m mostly just taking pedantic issue with your dating system while agreeing with your general conclusion.
