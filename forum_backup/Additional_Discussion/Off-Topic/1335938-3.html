    I don't see the issue then if you and the others agree that its important to having Trigger Warnings. Oiii internet squabbles make my head hurt.

    I have to say its one of my issues with DnD, I hate the morality system and the homogenous evil species. In games I'm fine with things like spirits hard coded to be evil, but if they are biological beings then I am more inclined to just have it be more the odds are stacked against them because they grow up in an overwhelmingly evil setting, rather then they have to be evil. So say you adopt an Orc or Goblin they can grow up to be good an Noble, or evil. They may have a biological aggressive streak but they have the potential to be nice. But you seem to agree acceptions can be made regardless of the nature of exceptions.

    I mean it seems like an artifact of days gone by that Half-Orcs are presented as default rather then just having Orcs in the core. And they whitewashed the implied heinous acts that spawned Half-Orcs (that an orc forced themselves on someone) and instead went out of their way to say noooo barbarian tribes sometimes have arranged Orc Human marriages. They should just have Orcs be the default rather then Half-Orcs, like how WoW does it.

    The main reason that I see people arguing against the morality system seems to often boil down to people aren't robots who are compelled to act in accordance with a specific set of principles. And to this, I would somewhat agree. However, this in absolutely no way means that the alignment system should be tossed out completely.

    I view the alignment spectrum as a set of guidelines for helping to establish your character's personality. Instead of being hard coded into your character, they influence how your character would normally choose to respond to a given situation. While changing somebody's alignment is possible, it shouldn't be as easy as Light Side, Dark Side is for Star Wars.

    "Oops, I acted on impulse, instead of allowing wisdom to guide me. Guess I'm a Sith, now!"

    Changes to one's alignment should be a gradual process that occurs over the course of the story. The character is routinely faced with situations that lead to them questioning their own morality and ethical world views. Ultimately leading up to them perceiving the world in a brand new way that falls in line with their new alignment. And this change should be restricted to a maximum of 2 alignment shifts per character during the entire campaign. Any player who tries to shift alignments a third time should just become Chaotic Neutral by default, since I imagine their mentality would be too fractured after that to truly believe in any sense of right or wrong.

                                                        I've been debating with myself over whether I should even get involved in this long off-the-rails discussion. But now seems like a pretty good time to put this on the table.

                                                        I cite Lindsay Ellis's video essay on Bright, specifically the section on Allegory and Coding (in a section called, funnily enough, "Allegory and Coding"). [Relevant section begins at 24:03].


                                                        The important take-away being that, while Allegory is intentional, Coding is not, and crops up even when the author of a fictional/fantasy work didn't mean to. That just because a writer didn't intend a message does not mean a message isn't an applicable reading of the text. This being a direct result of the writer working within existing frameworks - including mythical or genre frameworks - without examining the assumptions with a critical eye, or considering how other people could read the text.

                                                        Magestuck: The Ascension
