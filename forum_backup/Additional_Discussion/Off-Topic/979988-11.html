                                                Originally posted by Shinjo View Post
                                                How should vocal activists on the left approach more moderate conservatives with regards to the deplorable beliefs of their fellows?
                                                I'd say that's more for self-identifying conservatives to answer.

                                                However I think any reasonable answer would need to start with being willing to engage with conservative philosophic assumptions. The vocal left frequently pushes too hard against conservatives that might agree with their goals by forcing a specific philosophic framework (aka things like privilege and representational politics). I think perhaps looking at some of the US Supreme Court rulings where Justice Kennedy (the "swing" member before Scalia's death, but considered a moderate conservative) sided with the liberal Justices and look at how he approached things and how he phrased his reasons (ex: Kennedy is very big on the concept of personal dignity as a more conservative/libertarian rationale for his rulings over the liberal reasoning you'll get from Ginsberg).

                                                As I mentioned earlier, my reasoning for being pro-abortion is a conservative philosophic structure (and a faith based one at that) even if it doesn't follow the same logic most conservatives do. A lot of left/liberal leaning individuals would probably have some issues with my logic behind it (esp. since it really isn't based at all in any feminist principles on female body autonomy). So being able to frame seemingly liberal positions from a conservative philosophic framework is something that can be done.

                                                This belief has permeated US conservative thought for decades, to the point where the notion of fact-based journalism is seen as inherently against conservative policy.
                                                Personally, I think the problem is that the US conservative movement and conservative media have long since abandoned actual conservative principles. A huge number of right wing politicians cloak themselves in the label of conservative, but then push far right radical agendas. The conservative media has openly pushed this same issue by encouraging the concept of "purity" to conservative movements like the Tea Party where you have to always be pushing the most far right platforms to be considered a "true" conservative while centrist and moderate conservatives were decried for their lack of ideological purity.

                                                I think the majority of right leaning Americans are not actually on board with trying to conflate conservatism with reality denying policies, but the polarization in our politics makes it hard for them to feel like they have a place; which I'm aware is a running theme in my feelings on US conservatism.
