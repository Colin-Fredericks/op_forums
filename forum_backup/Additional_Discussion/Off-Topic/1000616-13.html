        On the Electoral Collage:

        It's needed reform for over 200 years. Hamilton - one of the key authors of the concept - wanted to amend it almost immediately after seeing it in practice as the electors immediately starting gaming the system rather than acting as a check on the every-day citizen. The electors were meant to counter the ability to sway what we now call low information voters by having a group of high information individuals be who actually makes the final decision. Madison and Hamilton actually tried to amend the Constitution to mandate that the vote was clearly for electors, not Presidential candidates, and that you would vote for your own district's elector directly instead of as a whole state. Obviously they failed.

        I don't think we could scrap it, since that would take an amendment; and the people doing the amending would be going against their own self-interests; as well as many philosophic beliefs about how to approach the Constitution. But I don't think how it works now is any good since it isn't the explicit intent of the Framers (since they said so and tried to change it), nor is it representative of a more modern ideology of direct democratic selection of the executive.

        If we're stuck with the current state of the electors being robots who's job is to be symbol and fulfill the letter of the Constitution despite the spirit of it, there's a simple solution that some states have already actually implemented: bind electors to vote to the national popular candidate instead of the state popular candidate (either in total, or in part). Just write them completely out of the picture and get rid of the ability for what happened this election. If every state agreed to bind 50% - 1 of their electors to the national popular candidate, we don't get this problem any more. Winning the national vote nets you so many electors that there's mathematically no way you could have won the most votes nationally and not end up with the most electors as well.

        I also think the "let the small states matter more" argument holds much weight. They matter to a ridiculous degree since the number of Representatives is locked and the minimum is set at 3. This already has a huge impact on the make up of the House. As well, swing states matter more. Delaware and Wyoming don't get Presidential attention despite their proportionally more valuable electors, because both are so solidly locked in by one of the parties it's not worth it. California and Florida have similar electoral strength in relation to their population, but California is Democrat country, and Florida is a swing state... so Florida gets way more attention. The point of the Electoral College was never to give smaller states more power in the presidential election anyway.

        On the DNC:

        The DNC's only real issue is an image problem. Rep. Ellison will most likely be the new head. He's younger, a Sanders guy, a Rust Belt guy (from Detroit, represents a 2/3s white part of Minneapolis, etc.), and a double up on minority status. Howard Dean might have more experience bringing the Dems back from this same situation, but that's effectively also a negative in this political climate. The Dems are pretty agreed already on what the path forward looks like, so the biggest thing to do with the DNC is make sure the new head of the DNC is a good messenger for what they want to accomplish.

        Of course, the Dems have always been good at talking about the right way to move forward, but their history in midterms and smaller local elections shows they've got an execution problem that's much more complicated. Even Obama's highly energized electorate wasn't there in the midterms. But the DNC also has a great leverage now: the Trump administration is what happens when Dems slack off for four years between presidential elections.
