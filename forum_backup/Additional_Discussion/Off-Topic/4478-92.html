                        When I was eighteen, I had my wisdom teeth removed. I don't know if the process was much more involved back then or if my teeth in particular were problematic, but I was under sedation for some time, and when I came out of it, it was my dad who took me home. I remember apologizing to him for the waste of time, a waste of time because I felt fine and was sure I could have driven myself home with no problems. My dad disagreed with my assessment of my abilities, though, and I don't remember arguing with him, not least because it wouldn't have made any difference; he'd already driven me there, after all, so it's not like I had my own car to take home.

                        I'll say again that I remember feeling fine. I don't know now how true that was, though. I know Dad wouldn't even let me go into the pharmacy with him, and I seem to remember him being alarmed when he came back out and found the car door open, me hanging out of it. I explained to him I was just doing that in case I had to throw up because all the blood I was swallowing was making me queasy, and I think that satisfied him, but as I look back on the events of that day, I wonder how much of its haziness is because of the two decades of time between now and then and how much of it was a drug-induced goofiness. How was I acting that day, anyway? Was Dad being cautious, or was I like that kid on the YouTube video, feeling fine inside but disconnected from the drugged behaviors I was engaging in?

                        I can't ask Dad that any more. There's no one I can ask any more. My parents are gone, and because we were a very solitary family, the only witnesses to a lot of my life are gone. I can't even guess how much of my personal history is lost now, beyond anyone's ability to recall. So long, childhood memories; so long, anecdotes of when I was young.

                        Maybe I wouldn't feel this way if not for my personal thing about not being able to answer a question (I hate it, I hate that so much, it gets under my skin like tunneling bugs), but there is a horror to me in contemplating how much of my history is gone, and as I recognize that horror, I understand for the first time why my dad gave a crap about genealogy. I never cared about it--and still don't; as much as I'd like to be different, I cannot care about the lives of people I've never interacted with--but I think I get now his search for a history, his desire to know about his past. For the first time, I can look back at my life not in terms of my own experiences but in generational ones and really consider the humbling immensity of the chain of people who lived and died to make me exist. From a certain perspective, all of human existence prior to 1978 had been building up to me, culminating in me, making me the first and foremost of the species upon whom the weight of all our hopes and potential rested.

                        I look back at that chain now and still just can't care about much of it. Only the two links of the past that generated me, my mom and my dad, mean anything to me. But when I consider everything that had to happen to get here, and how much of that was not random chance but their concentrated efforts, I feel a sense of obligation to try to live the life they'd have made for me rather than the one I've made for myself. I feel like I might owe it to them to stop being miserable and be happy.

                        I have no idea where to begin, but motivation is a start.

                        Also, I kind of enjoy the irony of how it took the loss of my past to make me care about the future. It's a funny world sometimes.
