                    I love stories in any form but the written word has always been my favorite medium. I recently ditched my bookcases because I realized that they actually took up space I could use for more books... So now I have huge stacks of books just lining the walls, on the stairs and to make impromptu tables, chairs and footstools (it sounds terrible, but books are surprisingly durable!). I used to be a librarian and have also worked in bookstores. Now I'm the 'head' of a book-club which really isn't as lame as it sounds (we just did Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream and are now reading Roald Dahl's Sometime Never: A Fable for Supermen. Its a very eclectic group and the books we read reflect that). I also write my own stories but I just don't seem to find that special something that makes me feel like they're actually good. I think I have good ideas but don't execute them very well...
                    There's no genre I dislike, though there are plenty of authors I think that don't deserve to have their work published (I'm an elitist that way). Most of the books I read are classics, if only because I want to know what makes them classic and because they rarely disappoint. I've recently discovered Haruki Murakami's What I Talk About When I Talk About Running (I'm a runner too, so...) and have since voraciously read most of his fiction work. I'm looking forward to his next work as I thought IQ84 was relatively weak compared to his other works. I'm currently rereading The Wind-Up Bird Chronicle for myself while reading Sometime Never: A Fable for Supermen for the bookclub. Next up is probably The Sirens of Titan by Kurt Vonnegut.

                                        So, I'm rereading Jurassic Park after about 15 years. It's not nearly as good as I remember it being.

                                        The writing just seems lacking. Most of the characters seem kind of one dimensional; you don't really get much insight into why they are the people that are. That might be the style of book, though.

                                        Tim is supposed to be 11, but I feel like he acts more like a 14-15 year old. Lex is supposed to be 8, but acts like she's 6.

                                        Doctor Grant magically knows that frog DNA will allow the dinosaurs to reproduce while Doctor Wu, the chief geneticist, can't fathom why Grant asked about frog DNA. On the other hand, Doctor Wu is giving pointers to Arnold, the engineer, on debugging the computer system that Nedry sabotaged. (Maybe that's why he can't figure out why the frog DNA is relevant: because he skipped that class and took a programming class instead. :-P )

                                        Also, the book was published in 1990 and really shows its age with the way it describes the computer systems.


                                        That being said, as is to be expected, the book is a lot more detailed than the movie, and does a lot of things better.
