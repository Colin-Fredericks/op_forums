                            So, I've been watching, and for the most part, enjoying Mirai Nikki, aka Future Diary. Essentially, it follows Yukiteru, your standard milquetoast, weak-willed anime protagonist. He compulsively keeps notes on everything around him, and occasionally talks to God (he's not insane, he just pretends). Until it turns out that God is real and is running a game where the last survivor takes over His station. Yuki is pretty useless, until he hooks up with Yuno, a classmate with an obsessive psychotic streak a mile wide. She loves him, in the "one day she will kill him and wear his skin as clothes" tradition. It is played every bit as horrific as it should be, and most of the series is awesome.

                            However, the last couple episodes I've watched (11 and 12) were really weak. Characters were acting really OOC for no readily apparent reason, I'm pretty sure the plot itself didn't hang together, and it really seems the main draw of the show has flipped without any particularly clear reason. I'll keep watching, but I'm kind of disappointed with the turn of it.

                                Future Diary was one of those shows with a ton of potential that just horribly fell apart as it went on. Unfortunately it felt like the author didn't really know where to take things. Too bad, the first 1/3rd was pretty amazing.

                                So, for this season, I'm finding it pretty slow. The only show I'm watching is Pandora in the Crimson Shell: Ghost Urn. It's by the author of Excel Saga, who teamed up with Shiro Masamune of Ghost in the Shell.

                                Kokaku no Pandora (as it's normally abbreviated to) is basically a girl meets girl story, though one of the girls us a full body cyborg while the other is a robot. The show is chock full of references to Ghost in the Shell, so many you can think of it as a semi-lighthearted prequel. It's also got a few references to Excel Saga hidden here and there.

                                The show obviously has a low budget, but the animation team does a great job animating things in ways that are fun and can work within the limited budget they have available. It's pretty clear the animation team enjoys working on the show and they manage to push that energy through to the viewers.

                                The show is rather lewd (one of the characters, Clarion, has a port in her lower abdomen that pops out and the other girl has to insert her finger to link up) though since the characters have bodies like Barbie dolls (complete with obviously artificial joints) I find it tends to be a bit more humorous than overly sexy.

                                                    Hai To Gensou No Grimgar (Grimgar Of Ashes And Fantasy) is a comparatively somber fantasy experience in this anime season that does a lot of good things. The devil is in the details - how logical consequences follow, how the characters are akward and fallible and lost, how they're a bunch of scrubs trying to get by, how the monsters have as much personhood as them, and how they deal with loss and pain from the harshness of the world. It's got some lovely waterfaded backgrounds and climactic music too. Of note is its slow and thorough pacing that works really well with the atmosphere. I could go into detail, but describing it in many words may be more difficult than in few. So instead I just recommend giving a few episodes a watch to understand the experience.

                                                    On the flip side of fantasy anime, Kono Subarashi Sekai Ni Shukufuku Wo, or KonoSuba, is hilarious. Not only because every character has a broken personality of the ridiculous sort that all interact in an explosive fashion, but because the comedy seems intent on ridiculing cocky premises quite handily and nothing ever seems to go well for anyone. Of course, humor is subjective, but when KonoSuba comes to life I know I'm going to enjoy a shameless comedy of errors.

                                                    More standard fare but enjoyable in the ways they're not:

                                                    Ao No Kanata No Four Rhythm pleasantly surprised me because I wasn't expecting the main female character's lovable spunk (in addition to her airheadedness), and despite looking kinda typical (it IS a visual novel adaptation), all the developments and important characterization in the cast have been really well spread out among their various relationships. This is not a "only the male MC engages with the issues of the other characters" situation. Much to the contrary, he has a strong supportive role but the limelight has consistently been on the female members of the Flying Circus club, the people they meet, and the issues they have and how they help each other (and are helped by the supporting cast).

                                                    Shoujo-Tachi Wa Kouya Wo Mesazu (Girls Beyond The Wasteland) is an interesting and kind of self-parodying sort of story, given it's about a group of friends working to make a visual novel, and is itself going to be released as a visual novel soon. It seems pretty typical and I've heard people say they weren't interested because it seemed like a show with harem tones. Much like similar comments about 4Rhythm, I don't know how they got that idea. There has been an episode focused on a character's hesitant romantic interest in her friend, but there's a lot more to the relations of the cast - and indeed to how they work together and manage to organize themselves - than romance. The difficulties they face in a project like this, both internal and external, are given a fair bit of importance, both on their own and in how they affect the small cast of slightly quirky characters. And although that quality isn't always there, the show has shown to be extremely good at "show, don't tell" as early as the very first episode, when we got to know our main character and his defining traits by following his day.
