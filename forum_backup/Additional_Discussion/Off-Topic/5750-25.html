    The first two X-Men movies was the breakout and top of the list and I'll probably keep viewing them that way.
    X-Men First Class undid the amazingly shitty "Last Stand." And after that Wolverine Origin movie I thought the whole franchise should have been tossed in the trash.
    First Class and Days of Future Past are tied to me for 3rd place. "Future Past" would likely rank higher if this wasn't just another Wolverine movie. (as I see it)

    As for maturity, this is why I made an unfair comparison to Captain America; the Winter Soldier. Winter Soldier had themes that were pretty adult, as epitomized in the character of Alexander Pierce. And not just adult, but those themes were pretty much alive today and very current. (I'm looking at Drone attacks, Snowden, NSA surveillance, etc...) Whip out a newspaper headline today and no one would be surprised at the "putting a gun to everyone's head and calling it protection." It was thought-provoking.

    "Future Past" didn't really require much thought, despite the space-time quandary. And it was made to fix the crap that went on earlier with the prior movies. We pretty much knew what was going to happen, and in that it was kinda tame. I had a bit of fun with the movie but that was all. Had they stuck with the comic-book stuff, such as scenes like this one which shows Wolverine's ambiguous ethics or scenes as shown on this cover where Wolverine gets blasted down to his bones. Or scenes such as this one which evokes the level of desperation that I genuinely felt reading it, but didn't feel it from the movie.
    Had they simply put the comic-books in movie form it would have been better in my opinion. But I understand that they had to fix the prior mistakes, and they wanted to throw a few nods towards characters that became popular later on in the series that reflects the younger fanbase. I get it, but to me it made a lesser movie than it could have been.

    Mankind was once an endangered species. It will likely be so again. And mankind will only have itself to blame.
