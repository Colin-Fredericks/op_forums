            I don't know that WotC necessarily needs to put "#notallorcs" in big bold letters across their books. I think it's always been understood that, for example, the vast majority of orcs grow up in societies where chaotic and evil ideas are valued, and therefore most orcs tend towards being chaotic and evil because that's how they've been raised and taught, but there are and always have been exceptions. The same is true for goblins, and derro and drow (especially drow, so many examples of non-evil drow).

            And then, of course, you have entire settings where "monster societies" tend to be very different, such as Eberron, where orcs, goblins, drow, etc don't live in societies which value chaotic or evil ideas so they have very different alignments from what you might get in the Forgotten Realms or Greyhawk. Generally speaking the only beings who are innately evil (or good) are Outsiders such as Angels or Demons, but even then they can can change their alignment based on their actions, and good angels can fall to evil and the most evil of demons can redeem themselves and become good.
