    I'm just saying it now, completely with bias: Best. Season Finale. Ever.

    Not only was it a hell of a spectacle, but I actually really liked a lot of the ideas and character growth that happened, and the best part is that the growth I'm talking about has been building for the entire season(or at least since the Discord tries to screw with Twilight and Cadence episode), not just in this one shot. It was simple, but really well done given the time frame they had.

    And you know, despite the rocky start(a lot of the early episodes were bad, and I nearly stopped watching), Season 4 has had a lot of powerhouse episodes, and in the end and on the whole it's as good if not better than Season 2. Still not as good as Season 1 in my opinion, but I really started feeling the show's spirit burning again by the end.

    If nothing else, the finale is really damn spectacular.

    EDIT: Also, Tirek is now best villain. He kind of always was, to be honest.

                S4E25-26 - Twilight's Kingdom

                So I came into this wondering just how exaggerated the reactions to this episode was. I found they weren't. I can only concur, best season ending. Hell, even the things I could complain about aren't actually bad. Not only does it (as we knew) pick up the thread with the locked box and the Keys of Harmony (which I've decided they are called), but it also ties into many previously planted plotlines. Discord's redemption, for example, which I only now feel is actually worth putting some score into. The fact that Twilight is a princess without any actual authority over anything. And, perhaps most importantly, the fact that the show did not return to the status quo and returned the Elements of Harmony. They seem to be actually gone now? And I find myself thinking this was the best thing they could have done. Move forward, keep developing. That's what make shows stick around.

                In fact, I think I lied. The best thing about this episode is that it does what it proclaims to do. It shows that this show dares believing that little girls actually likes watching a female character beat the shit out of giant monsters. That makes them more progressive than the majority of the gaming industry, and I really, honestly believe it's the right call. I've not seen any demographics lately, but for all the space we bronies take up, I still think the show's biggest audience is the "intended" one. And that is what will make this show stick around.

                Seriously. It better be, or Hasbro will pull it. Can't mess with them.

                Did I have some complaints? Well, there's only one real one, and it has to do with the Twilight's combat scene with Tirek. First, let me preface: I freaking loved that combat scene! As an Exalted fan, do you know how long I've waited to see some disgustingly overpowered equine godqueens unleash their full fury? They are both so freaking powerful that they can't even hurt each other, it's beautiful. But therein lies the problem, too. That fight was never going to solve anything. As much as I loved it, I knew it would end in a draw, because one-on-one violence can't win the day in Friendship is Magic. I felt like it was easily the weakest part of the episode. Which admittedly isn't saying much, given the rest.

                Eh, I guess it's not really a complaint, but I feel inordinately sad about Twilight's tree-library. I guess I'm supposed to, but damn, that place was iconic. And all the boooks!

                Right, I don't have any other ways of saying "everything was awesome", so I'm just going to go right on into...




                Some collected thoughts on season 4

                You all know I love this season by now, so I won't go on about why I love it again. Instead, I will relate my perspective on what I think this season represents for the development of the show. And it is this: this season has been important because it shows we don't need Lauren Faust.

                Before you collectively lynch me, give me a moment to explain.

                In the history of My Little Pony, it makes sense to talk about a Faust and post-Faust era. In season one, Lauren Faust laid the ground foundations of what FiM was, the core tenets and the tone for the entire show. I don't believe FiM would have come about without her, and we must recognise that. She was directly involved in the first season, and on-and-off in the second season, and those season are also widely beloved.

                Much as Faust is to thank for the event of FiM, fact is that she won't be coming back. Season three had to prove that Faust's absence would not have detrimental effects on the quality of the show, and I think it's not controversial to say that it failed. By the end of season three, I had at least to some extent prepared myself for the start of decline. The golden days behind us, we'd see the show falter and lessen until some executive at Hasbro decided Gen 5 was in order. It would all be Equestria Girls from here on out, oh yes.

                And then season four came along and showed us that not only can FiM manage without Faust, it can actually make many parts better. For example, I honestly suspect we'd never have seen a season-long subplot if Faust had still been on the team. Even in season three, note how they tried to do it but rather failed; it's unlikely to be a coincidence that they attempted it the moment Faust was gone. But conspiracy theories aside, season 4 has been a cavalcade of success, with a few blemishes but hey, that's how it goes. We've seen that the crew can carry our beloved series into the post-Faust world not only in name only but also in spirit and execution. That is the significance of season 4, and it's damn significant.

                Glory days ahead, bronies. Glory days.

                                                        I've been thinking about Tirek, and how he really is firing on all cylinders as a villain. For one thing, it's great that they introduce him early and then have a double length episode to develop him; I wouldn't exactly call him a complex character, but between the way that he's able to manipulate Discord (with the sense of, if not a history, than a familiarity of reputation), his vengeful attitude towards the Princesses, and his persistent references to his brother that demonstrate a lingering bitterness over that whole deal, he's got a good deal of nuance when it would be so easy to just leave him as a generic villain. That thing with the brother in particular... he keeps being brought up, to the point where you'd think it might be setting up his presence, but instead it just serves as a way to characterise the antagonist (as well as inform his motivations and factors that make for his eventual downfall.

                                                        Also, he fucks around with unicorn horns and cutie marks, which I've always thought was the easiest way to show something off as severely messed up with ponies.

                                                        His voice is great too, initially quite creepy and unnerving but also growing suitably powerful sounding as he grows stronger. And he's got a great design, the whole bull-baboon centaur thing, and the particulars of how he grows out, all warped and stretching and his horns curving out.
