                                                            I can't not reply to this thread after reading it, but most of what I want to say has already been said, perhaps most succinctly and pithily by Mr. Gone. I'll just sum up what I think I can add.

                                                            On the issue of Vice Admiral Holdo's competence, I'll agree that she was an awful leader, choosing neither to share enough information for her chief pilot to trust her judgment nor to put him in the brig when he showed open insubordination. She was generally incompetent in many ways, just as the First Order's officers were generally incompetent. I interpreted the space-navy scenes in The Last Jedi through the lens of understanding that these were the "junior varsity" admirals left over at the end of several decades of pan-galactic war, and the rebels had all but run out of resources to fight. Keeping that in mind, I can understand how the rebel bombers were so fragile that a single crashing X-wing tore through their hulls like they were paper. I can also understand how, when Admiral Ackbar and everyone else in the helm of the rebel flagship were killed, besides Leia, who was too disabled to take command, and the job fell suddenly to an unprepared vice-admiral who may have been promoted beyond her "level of incompetence" (see for reference the Peter Principle), she didn't command very well.

                                                            The First Order had less of an excuse to have crappy officers, but it seems they were even more incompetent than Holdo. Again, chalk it up to their being the remainder of a space navy working under the dominion of a megalomaniac with awesome power in the Force but terribly little wisdom or self-awareness, seconded by a young man whose awesome power in the force was hindered by his ongoing identity crisis. Under these conditions, the officers in the First Order's space navy seem to have been promoted more on the basis of their excellent diction and eagerness to be yes-men than any real talent. I didn't like it but it seemed like a plausible state of affairs.

                                                            On the issue of Snoke being a "wasted" character, well, maybe he wasn't as awesome as we all wanted him to be, but think about this: for all his power in the Force, Emperor Palpatine didn't consider him a serious political threat. If he had, Palpatine would have crushed Snoke long ago. In that light, we shouldn't expect Snoke to be as awesome as Palpatine or even Vader. In the power vacuum that followed the end of E6, Snoke had the opportunity to emerge, but ruled with arrogant hubris on raw Force power rather than with any wisdom, discipline, or sense, which is why he created the motive for Kylo Ren's betrayal and then didn't see it coming.

                                                            I'm also glad that RJ had the courage to make E8 "not what we expected." I honestly came away from E8 satisfied that the desperate gambit undertaken by the supporting cast turned out to fail (in reality the vast majority of desperate gambits do fail), glad that Rey is truly a fresh face with no important family name, and ready to look forward to E9 telling us the story of Rey and Ben, which is what the new trilogy has wanted to be all along. Yes, there were some wasted characters, and yes, Vice Admiral Holdo should have programmed the autopilot and gotten into an escape pod before her flagship launched itself kamikaze-style through the First Order's flagship, but generally I enjoyed E8. Now that it's on Netflix I'm going to watch it again and see if my mind changes on the second viewing
