                    So I spotted some statements from TotalBiscuit on the issue. First this and then this. In summary, he's taking a middle stance, condemning the harassment from both sides and asking the GamerGate people to show their hand and actually talk journalism ethics.

                    He makes some statements I don't agree with, like suggesting that reciprocating with the GamerGate harassers has only "fuelled the fire" and allowed it to continue; while this may be true, I think it's more important to speak up about what kind of thing happen to people, on the internet and otherwise, who deal with these subjects. I'm also not sure it's more important to talk ethics than to actually help those getting harassed, but it seems TB thinks the one will bring about the other. I'm skeptical, but I suppose we have different outlooks.

                    I'll not deny I watch TB, mostly because he's a good caster and like hearing his voice (it's not creepy >.>). He's opinionated, but mostly factual and has an ability to keep two thoughts running at once. I don't think he'd ever call himself a feminist, but he does support women in gaming and has an anti-harassment stance. He's just not very active about it. More like he's not standing in their way. Much as I lament it, it's not strange that he'd take this stance; he's very much a "mechanics-guy" and I can see him sympathizing, if not with the brogamer attitude or the oppression of females, then with the idea that mainstream art criticism isn't interesting for games.

                    TB's views on these things have always been kind of iffy for me. He tends to be able to argue them down to a level where it's hard to disagree with the things he does say, but often feel there are further implications that should maybe get more consideration. For example, I personally agree, as I've said above, that journalistic ethics in the games medium is a valid discussion topic, and I don't think it's wrong to discuss it with GamerGate-associated people - we shouldn't be "afraid to touch". Putting them to task might be a the best thing we can do; let them prove that it actually is about ethics.

                                Ah, video games as art vs video games as entertainment...

                                Personally I think that video games are first and foremost a form of entertainment - thus the word game.

                                That doesn't mean that you can't have a game that also teaches you think, or causes you to reflect on the world or whatever, but ultimately games are about being entertained (whether having fun, getting scared or having some other emotional reaction).

                                Certainly it's possible to have an electronic media that doesn't focus on entertainment and instead on say, teaching or some such. For example, virtual tours of ancient buildings or such aren't really "games."

                                Ultimately though I think that if you're playing a game and aren't entertained then, as a game, the product is clearly a failure, even if it's a critical success on some sort of abstract artistic or social level.

                                So I would argue that, when making a video game the priority is (or should be) based on entertaining the people who will play it. And if the priority isn't on entertaining people, then you shouldn't call what you're making a game.
