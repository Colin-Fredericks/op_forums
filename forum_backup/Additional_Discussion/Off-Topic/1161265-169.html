        Originally posted by Second Chances View Post

        I can see that. It’s one of the reasons I vastly prefer Eberron’s fixed timeline that never advances from 998 YK, the problems don’t get solved as the timeline advances.
        I think D&D settings could actually do with adopting a CofD model. A nebulous “present day” (in the sense that it’s the assumed default era, not that it’s technologically modern) with lore detailing the setting’s history in broad strokes but no advancing metaplot, and a number of “dark eras” for playing in time periods detailed in the setting’s past.

        I realized this when prepping to run Waterdeep: Dragon Heist in my Points of Light setting. The idea of a city as large, populous, and prosperous as Waterdeep just doesn’t fit into my setting, I thought. But I realized it would have fit pretty comfortably in Turath before it’s collapse (and in fact, before its corruption had spread beyond a few noble houses). This lead me to realize, there’s no reason I couldn’t run the games during other eras in my settings lore.

                                Originally posted by Second Chances View Post
                                Got some undead and dragon plothooks for everyone!
                                I like this idea a lot. Having more types of undead enemies is always useful, especially in an undead-heavy game. Otherwise, it helps vary the kinds of minions a dragon can command. (It's not just kobolds or dragon cultists all the time, fun though they are).

                                My quick guide for adapting the Dragonscarred Dead to DnD 5e:
                                • Start with the Knight stat block as a base (MM p. 347)
                                • Undead, probably Evil aligned
                                • Choose damage type of the breath weapon that slew it (Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, or Poison)
                                • Add staple Resistances and Immunities of undead creatures, as well as an Immunity to its chosen damage type; don't forget things like Darkvision and the like
                                • Make the melee attacks do an extra 1d6 damage of the chosen damage type
                                • Optional: give the creature a kind of fear effect (recharges on 5 or 6) that frightens targets who fail their save. As if the Dragonscarred Dead have been endowed with a measure of their killer's/maker's own essence
                                This will probably put the monster at CR 4. I don't know, I'm just eye-balling it based on the increase in damage output. I haven't run the numbers in the DMG or anything.

                                    If I had to think of a plot hook for the Dragonscarred Dead, I'd probably fold it into a plotline I came up with called the "Heirs of No Life". In my homebrew setting, the continent was once threatened by an undead warlord, called the No Life King. He's an aspirational figure among intelligent undead in my setting, with many seeking to become the next No Life King. Leading to a variety of such monsters and villains scouring the land, making (and breaking) alliances among themselves and trying to amass minions and magic artifacts. All in the service of increasing their own power, so one can declare itself the new No Life King and rally all other contenders under its banner.

                                    For the Dragonscarred Dead, I'm imagining a warrior who got killed trying to free the ruined city of Vetrice (capital of a great empire) from the Black Wurm that occupies it, an ancient, evil black dragon. This warrior is super pissed about having his flesh melted off, and wants to gain the title of No Life King so he can march an undead army in.

                                    While destroying the Black Wurm would be a net boon for the world, this undead warrior won't let anything get in the way of attaining great undead power. When everything else is melted away, only the HATE remains.

                                    Magestuck: The Ascension
