                                        Picking one at random:

                                        Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                                        Yeah, I don't see a potential rapist going through the trouble to "disguise" themselves as a woman just so they can follow women/little girls into the restroom to rape them. That's a bullshit, fallacious argument if I ever saw one.
                                        Who said anything about sexual predators disguising themselves as women? The generic "trans-friendly" bathroom policy doesn't allow you to interfere with anyone from entering the women's restroom. In theory, its to prevent transgendered individuals from being embarrassed, or having their gender identity questioned. In practice, that means anyone (trans or not) can walk right in - and if you try to stop them, it opens you up to charges of harassment and/or discrimination.

                                        And if the girls or women inside feel threatened or uncomfortable? The issue came up . . . oh, about a month ago at a Planet Fitness. They're a gym chain that has a trans-friendly locker policy, and a woman complained to the management about an individual claiming to be transgendered in the women's locker room. The management's response was to throw her out, not the suspect individual.

                                        So you can't stop a suspicious individual from entering the women's restroom. And, once inside, the girls and women can't appeal to a third party if they feel threatened, or believe the person to be a possible sexual predator. That nixes any sort of preemptive effort to head off a sexual assault - no one can interfere until an attack is actually happening.

                                        Health insurance. Seat belts. Fire alarms. From guard rails on bunk beds to car seats to babysitters, we do a lot to make the world safe for kids. A policy where you can't intervene to stop a sexual predator until a little girl is screaming in fear is a terrible one. There's a reason the Houston anti-discrimination bill went down over the bathroom issue, despite having a populace and government considered friendly to the gay/lesbian/transgendered community.

                                        Implementing a policy in the real world is always messier than pondering it as an abstract concept - and there are often ugly tradeoffs that have to be made. The policy that you favor may not be the best for society as a whole, or it may have unacceptably bad side effects on other groups. Thus there may well be pushback when you present your proposed policy to the general public, and thus having alternate proposals is a good idea.

                                        Having a third (non-gender specific) bathroom seems like a reasonable option. PenDragon's children-only idea (although meant facetiously) could work, too - still would be a safety issue for women, but the details could be hammered out later. Requiring transgendered individuals to flash some form of medical certification might also be an option (although I suspect there wouldn't be a lot of support for that proposal).

                                        Opposing transgendered access to the bathroom isn't about bigotry (although I'd be surprised if that wasn't a factor for some), or somehow accusing them of being sexual predators. Its about the side effects - such as how it opens a huge loophole for actual sexual predators.


                                        Edit: Oh, and with regards to some of the other posts - "Think of the children" is a classic rhetorical device, not a logical fallacy. It has a strong emotional pull on the audience, and often addresses real concerns.

                                        An argument ad hominem, on the other hand, is a logical fallacy. The moral/social failings of your opponent in an argument have no bearing on whether they're raising good points - and (as Cicero noted) such a criticism usually implies that there are huge flaws in the argument you are trying to advance.
