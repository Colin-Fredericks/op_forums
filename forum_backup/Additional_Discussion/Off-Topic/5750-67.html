                                            Was going to go see Ghostbusters on wed, but then saw that a local theater was showing a marathon of Star Trek, Star Trek Into Darkness, and Star Trek Beyond, all in IMAX, with the last in 3D...yeah, I'll spend a day doing that!

                                            For the record, I loved both of Abram's takes on ST, and never really got what the hardcore fandom's problem with them were. STB, however, feels pretty *different*...it's clear others are behind the camera. I dunno, I feel like it was *good*, but not great, and I *liked* it, but did not love it...maybe it'll grow on me more with repeat viewings...

                                            I will say that it actually did feel more like the classic Trek series, probably because as they're well into the 5 year mission now, Chris Pine's Kirk has observably grown, matured and settled down some, to the point where he feels a LOT more like classic Kirk...he even has William Shatner's hairstyle from TOS, which I actually found a bit distracting at times! The movie actually inspired me to watch some of the classic eps on Netflix afterwards, which none of the other movies have ever made me do...

                                            Word to the wise, btw, apparently some of the TV spots spoil a big plot reveal about Idris Elba's villain, so...consider yourselves warned (Simon Pegg is reportedly furious over this).

                                            One of my pet peeves with the Star Trek film series has been its need to target the Earth, no matter how far in space the story is set, as though audiences can't truly be invested unless our world was endangered (Generations was particularly clumsy with this), so I was really hoping this film would avoid that pitfall. It did, sort of, in a clever way that didn't directly threaten Earth, but still had a large target that we were invested in, in the form of one of THE most amazing space stations I've ever seen put on film! Seriously, this alone was almost worth the price of 3D (this is also where we meet Sulu's husband and child, giving us a personal stake to worry about)...

                                            There's also a really over-the-top action sequence at the climax that might have people wondering, "Is this really Star Trek?? Should ST go there, and have this type of action in it?" Personally, I loved it, and appreciate pushing the boundaries like that...although I might NOT like it if I saw something similar in a Star Wars movie. Hmm...

                                            Anyway, I recommend it.
