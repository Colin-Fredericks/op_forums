    Originally posted by Paradim View Post
    If so, I'm very conflicted on it. I like students being aware and caring about politics. It IS their future. But I don't like hearing of these outbreaks of violence due to differing politics. It's polarizing the discussion and normalizing these behaviors. And I can't see that as being good.
    I've been thinking this for a long time. It seems to me that our national political discourse has been growing more divisive over time, but it really came to a head this year. And if we don't make a concerted effort to change directions, it will get worse.

    See, violence is the natural outcome of demonizing anyone who disagrees with you. If you've already settled in your mind that 1) this person's ideas have no value and 2) there is no point in you trying to impart your values to them or negotiate a common set of values, what else is there to do except act unilaterally?

    If people are trying to be civilized, this means trying to do an end-run around whatever your opposition is to achieve your desired goal. In politics you see this principle enacted by whoever is in power using their legal authority to force their will on the unwilling, and the powerless trying to undermine that authority. No negotiation, just social battles for control.

    If people aren't trying to be civilized, this means you straight up kill anyone you don't like. Without a greater power threatening punishment, or a moral doctrine against violence, there's little incentive not to use force.

    On the frontier of the Wild South, there's only one woman with the grit to take on its most dangerous outlaws and bring them Back Alive, or Maybe Dead.
