                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                            I’m scared folks are gonna be like “that’s a True Polymorph effect, you can’t make that readily available!”
                            Eh, I wouldn’t worry too much about that. Yeah, it is technically a True Polymorph effect, but it’s a highly specific example of one, and has no mechanical impact at all. That seems like exactly the sort of design space Common magic items are meant to cover - cool narrative effects with little to no mechanical impact.

                            I’d say a consumable item like a potion with a one-time, permanent spectrum slide effect would be well within the reasonable bounds for a Common item, regardless of whether or not the same effect can be achieved repeatedly with a high level spell.

                            Also, in my experience, the 5e crowd is generally not so stodgy about “um, actually, this effect should require X-level magic to achieve” type rules pedantry. A big mantra of 5e is “rulings, not rules.”
