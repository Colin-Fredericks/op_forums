                                                It is, however, fairly consistant with populism in the face of sudden democracy.

                                                One of the failings of how various countries and organizations have tried to spread democracy (and specifically liberal democracy) around the world is that suddenly adding democracy to a culture pretty much doesn't work. It tends to just destabilize a country and let strong man dictator of whatever stripe whip up a populace and take over.

                                                Consider Germany in the decades leading up to the Nazi (democratically elected) take over of the country. Despite there being latent issues with bias against Jews, Catholics, Romani, and the LGBTQ+ communities, the late 1800s and early 1900s were a time were Germany and the countries in its sphere of influence were some of the most progressive and inclusive in Europe. My great-grandfather grew up in this period outside of Munich. He felt fully integrated into Germany society. Being Jewish never hurt him as a businessman, or lessened the respect he earned from his Christian neighbors as a proud German. When WWI was starting, he considered himself a patriot, and volunteered to join the army. Being Jewish didn't stop him from quickly becoming an officer on merit. He survived and came home a war hero even with the loss, and being a Jew was never part of how people gave him respect for his service to his country. In the 20's, never in his wildest nightmares even with the Nazis around, would he think that it was possible for Germany to turn on him... until it did.

                                                Look at all the countries that the CIA fucked up. How quickly old resentments that seemed buried in the past and barely part of mainstream culture sprang to life in horrifically violent ways.
