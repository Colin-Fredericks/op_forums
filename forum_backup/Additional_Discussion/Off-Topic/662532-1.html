                    Generally an issue 1 won't continue directly from the end of a previous ongoing unless there's some sort of massive change, like the New 52 stuff or this upcoming Marvel thing. A lot of comics are going to appreciate that you know the basics, like Bruce Banner was exposed to gamma radiation and when he gets angry becomes the incredible green Hulk, so they won't always completely change a character. But there are usually some style changes and a new start. It's more of a soft reboot. Some obvious things may have changed, but it usually just won't mention them, so some later writer can pick up the slack and get some nostalgia fandom via recurring bad guys if they want, but alternatively you're not tied down to decades of previous continuity and fans worrying about continuity inconsistencies. That's what they did with Extremis, I think. It was like the movie, but basically appreciated that Tony Stark has been Iron Man, he's done some unspecified superheroing, otherwise its a new start. The big two did this with a lot of lines after the 90s.

                    First issues sell, though. It's one of the few things you can really count on. If it's a big name and the first issue, it's going to sell a lot. So if they're on issue 30 of a run that sells poorly, and no one's really talking about it, some writer comes to you with a new take on the character, why not go for a reboot?
