                                If it helps, here are some of the other authors and groups working in this vein:

                                * In his Felix Gomez series, Mario Acevedo is exploring what would happen if the oWoD Camarilla discovered there were aliens on Earth, doing . . . something. The main character is a veteran who got Embraced during the Iraq War, and now works as a private eye. Roughly half the books focus on the aliens and vampires slowly becoming aware of each other (tensions rising all the while), while the rest focus on Felix's other cases.

                                * Mur Lafferty, a former White Wolf author, is working on tour guides for supernaturals. Or rather books about writing travel guides for the supernatural community, and the hijinks that ensue when they start asking questions. Starting with The Shambling Guide to New York City, her protagonist (a blackballed travel writer) gets recruited by a vampire (much to her surprise) to create the first travel guide to the hidden supernatural community in NYC. Strong nWoD/CoD influences, with some great excerpts from the travel book (I really, really want to open a bank account with the ravens).

                                * Seanan McGuire's October Daye series reads almost exactly like a Changeling: the Dreaming series. Sure, there are some house rules thrown in . . . but it would not surprise me at all to learn that the protagonist used to be her PC in a Changeling campaign. Her InCryptid series is also pretty good, showcasing what would happen if a group of hunters suddenly went all Greeenpeace on behalf of the supernatural community.

                                * Kelley Armstrong also works in a World of Darkness-ish vein. Her Otherworld series has a wild mix of supernaturals hiding in the shadows, where the multi-splat Council roughly counterbalances the sinister Cabals (dark mages who ruthlessly exploit other supernaturals). The first book, Bitten, was recently adapted as a SyFy series.

                                * Carrie Vaughn, author of the Kitty Norville series, has an interesting series about a werewolf DJ who accidentally brings an end to the secrecy of the supernatural world - and kickstarts a war with an ancient Roman vampire. Chloe Neil (Chicagoland Vampires) and Charlaine Harris (Sookie Stackhouse/True Blood) explore similar ideas, albeit from the vampiric side of things

                                * L.J. Smith's Night World books are a young adult series with a strong World of Darkness vibe. The end of the world is coming, and everyone is scrambling to find/control/kill the four Wild Powers who will determine whether the world is saved . . . or destroyed.

                                A couple of others worth mentioning, albeit in a different framework:

                                * Cubicle Seven is working on a series of variant Call of Cthulhu settings, where the PCs are spies recruited by the mysterious "N" to oppose the Mythos . . . as well as the enemies of the Allies/NATO/etc. The first, Achtung! Cthulhu, occurs during World War 2, while World War Cthulhu occurs during the Seventies (a.k.a. the height of the Cold War)

                                * Tanya Huff has two overlapping series: Keeper Chronicles and Enchantment Emporium. Oddly, the two series have exactly the same plot outlines, but the books in the Keeper Chronicles are more . . . fun, while Enchantment Emporium skews far darker and more adult (as in "cannibal witches who fuel their powers through bisexual orgies" dark).

                                * And Kim Harrison's Rachel Morgan (Hollows) series is basically World of Darkness meets Shadowrun
