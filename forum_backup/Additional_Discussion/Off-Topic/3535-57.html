                                        Originally posted by Kammerer View Post
                                        Honestly, Lovecraft gets a worse rap than he deserves. The guy started out with opinions fairly average for his time and progressed as he matured. A lot of the reason why Lovecraft comes off as double plus ultra racist is that people he corresponded with tended to be on the cutting edge of social progress, making him look a neanderthal by comparison. And because many of his works are open to shallow racist readings, like taking Shadow Over Innsmouth as a screed against miscegenation rather than biological determinism - an issue Lovecraft was struggling with at the time.

                                        What I'm trying to say is that yes, Lovecraft was racist, but he wasn't this Jim Crow-esque pillar of xenophobia modern pop culture likes to paint him as.
                                        Trust me, you don't need to explain that to me. My "I'm a huge Lovecraft fan" is less "I read his books and like them" and more "I read his books, his letters, what his peers wrote about him, what his peers wrote in general, essays of all sorts about him, the S.T. Joshi's biography and my college thesis is about him". I don't bring that up when speaking casually about him, because, honestly, I'm a nobody and who cares, but Lovecraft is pretty much the only thing out there I'm really confident about.

                                        Since I brought Lovecraft up in the first place though: I agree his racism is not a core element of his works as such as pop culture paints it to be, I agree that there's more to him than that and that his contributions to literature and modern culture are huge and owe little to racism (except Horror at Red Hook, but screw that story) and it irritates me that's almost impossible to discuss Lovecraft without having to consider the topic when many other famous authors were just as bad and seemingly immune to this.

                                        But Lovecraft was a pillar of xenophobia. Not of the Jim Crow-esque kind, but of his own, more intellectual and reclusive kind. I mean, it's hard to deny. It does not feel good when you have to face it personally. And I got it easy! I can't blame Samuel Loveman for having repudiated Lovecraft after he discovered how anti-semite he was.

                                        The thing with Lovecraft is that his racism is weird. He was, and that's the exact term I always wanted to use when I discussed him academically, a fucking dork. He was a man with issues that grew up mostly alone and built his personal perspective on impersonal absurdities. The kind of fucking dork that as a teen writes (paraphrased) "I am ashamed that the colonies gained the independence when we could still be part of the glorious British Empire" when his family is proud to having had a role in the US independence and that consequently hurts their reputation.

                                        Several of his most problematic personal views developed in this little, isolated bubble, and Lovecraft could not reconcile them with reality when the occasion called for it. He was also stubborn as a mule, which does not help. In turn, this lead to these awkward situations where Lovecraft was perhaps not a practical racist but remained a fiercely intellectual one.

                                        "Sure, I married Sonia Greene, a Jewish woman, but now that she's my wife that's part of her past"

                                        "Sure, I'll hang with Samuel Loveman, he's Jewish but he's a good one, unlike those I don't know"

                                        "Sure, I'll become the mentor of the young Robert Bloch. He's Jewish too, you say? Who cares, Robert's great. He killed me in one of his stories. Hilarious"

                                        "Sure, Zelia Bishop is a fine young woman. I enjoy our interactions and she's a brilliant mind. So glad some young ladies manage to enlighten themselves like that"

                                        "Sure, now that Robert Howard mentions it, perhaps I might consider that not all the african-american people are that bad. But only some"                                         (Speaking for the times, at least. Bob Howard did his best)

                                        That sort of stuff. Lovecraft always had trouble reconciling his lifelong convinctions with what happened in the actual world. He wrote both "I know he's a clown, but by God, I like the boy" when talking about Hitler (which is a tough one to forget) and "I distrust fascism. One can't tolerate much a system whose followers declare "Enemy of Mankind" whoever disagrees with it".

                                        I think the thing is that Lovecraft's bizarre racism was more a symptom of the issues and inner conflicts the man. Lovecraft felt out of place, out of time and thought to belong to a mankind that was not there. He saw the cosmic horror because he lived it. Which is actually the real source of its best works and the actual origin of his greatness, I think.

                                        I can go on for hours but I think that proved my point. Things with Lovecraft aren't easy. Some say "I liked Lovecraft because he hated (terrible epithets)" (no really, I know several who said this) and others say "I hate Lovecraft because he's the absolute incarnation of white, American racism". Neither is right, but racism was a rather big, cumbersome element of ol' H.P. personality. Not as important as many people think, but it's hard to blame them for it. They have all the reasons to dismiss him when the racism thing arises.

                                        Some did not. Neil Gaiman, Stephen King, Alan Moore, Thomas Ligotti, Mike Mignola, Guillermo Del Toro...the list goes on. More recently, awesome stuff like Lovecraft Country and Get Out happened when Lovecraft fans decided to confront with the man's racism. As for me, I just wrote terrible lovecraftian pastiches that nobody should ever read.

                                        But I owe to Lovecraft a lot. When I was just a kid and some teacher picked on me through all the middle school, turning a boy who loved to read and into an emotional wreck that could not bear to open a book and hated both himself and all things school related, it was Lovecraft that brought me back from the edge. So I am rather biased. Guess I love that, just to say this is on topic?

                                        And we got here from a bit of confusion between Joseph and John Campbell.
