                                            Originally posted by ahather View Post
                                            where is the stuff for pathfinder 2 on dying and resonance, i cant seem to find a relevant blog post
                                            Sadly it wasn’t in a blog post. It was only brought up in episodes 3 and 4 of the Glass Cannon podcast.

                                            Basically, your Resonance is equal to your Level + Charisma modifier. At the beginning of the day you have to do something called “Investing” in any worn magic items like cloaks and rings, spending a Resonance to make them work for the day. Wielded magic items like weapons don’t require investment to work. Any time you make use of a magic item’s activated effects - say, by using a wand of Cure Light Wounds to heal yourself, drinking a magic potion, or using your magic sword to shoot a beam of force energy, you have to spend a point of your Resonance. If you run out of Resonance, you can still use your magic items, but every time you do so, you have to make a check. Just a straight d20 roll with no modifiers. The DC starts at 10, and increases by 1 every time you use a magic item in excess of your Resonance limit. On a success, the item’s effect works, on a failure it doesn’t, and on a fumble (which again is a failure by 10 or more), you can’t use the activated effects of magic items for the rest of the day. The passive effects of any wielded items or worn items you invested in still continue to work for you.

                                            At least, that’s as close as we can tell. There are some things that are still a little unclear, like is armor considered worn or wielded, do triggered abilities (like say a weapon that deals extra damage on a crit) cost Resonance, and is Investment more involved than simply saying you’re investing in your cloak and spending the Resonance for it. But that’s the basics as near as we can tell so far.

                                            Also, someone on the Paizo forums ran an automated simulation, he found that after around a million trials, he said was succeeding at about 2.3 checks on average before a fumble.
