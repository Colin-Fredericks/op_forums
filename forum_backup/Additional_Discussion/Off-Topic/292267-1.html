                                        Mind you, you should still expect bugs in largest cRPG in history area wise, but here, this may help with running it:

                                        General:Playing DOS Installments under DOSBox - UESP Wiki - The Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages
                                        http://www.uesp.net/wiki/General:Playing_DOS_Installments_under_DOSBox
                                        Earlier installments in the Elder Scrolls series—Arena, Daggerfall, Battlespire, and Redguard—were developed for DOS and consequently do not run under modern Windows (nor non-Windows systems, for that matter). This page deals specifically with running these games in DOSBox, and is applicable to all of them.


                                        Daggerfall:Running under Windows - UESP Wiki - The Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages
                                        http://www.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Running_under_Windows
                                        There is a serious memory issue when running Daggerfall under a modern Windows-OS such as Windows-XP. This issue causes the quest object to be invisible, hence it will be nearly impossible to find or kill a quest object, since it cannot be seen. Once the quest is active, switching to DosBox or similar applications doesn't solve this problem, since the quest data is already stored in your savegame, you have to start the quest from the scratch. Always keep that in mind because, if you embark on the main quest or a questline, you will have serious troubles. In addition to the problems with the quest object strafe, backward moves, climbing, swimming and jumping are almost impossible to perform. Considering all these facts, it is highly advised to use DOSBox when running Daggerfall under a modern Windows-OS.

                                            For the curious, my first character was a Redguard sword-and-shield heavy warrior (though in retrospect, I probably should have gone with light armor considering how much sneaking I did). He grew up in Hammerfell, raised on stories of how his people drove off the elves after being abandoned by the weak and callous Empire. Hearing of the Stormcloak Rebellion, he joined up with a crew of piratical adventurers who were on their way to join the fight against the Empire (and, by extension, the Aldmeri Dominion). They were shipwrecked, though, and my character was the only survivor. He followed one faint lead after another and was finally able to join up with Ulfric--right in time to be captured with him.

                                            As for the stopping point, well, all the major plots are wrapped up: the world is saved, Skyrim is an independent nation, and my character has returned to Jorrvaskr to take his place as Harbinger of the Companions. It felt like a good place to fade to black and roll credits. For my next character, I'm going to take the Empire's side in the war, and I'm leaning toward a Dark Elf "spellsword" type.
