                Originally posted by Kat View Post

                To be honest, with growing population and depleting resources your ideal becomes more distant from realization with each decade. It would better for mankind if the population stabilized and stopped growing rapidly. Thankfully that is happening in Europe.
                That's actually the interesting thing. While there's no obvious direct single point of causation we can point at, pretty much all western countries seem to have stabilizing and declining birth rates, largely in proportion to availability of birth control and general health and wellness. So helping people to be educated, not starving seems to be an effective meta-method to stabilize or even decrease the population.

                As for depleting resources, it's a highly subjective thing. The whole Peak Oil thing has been a standard "it's going to start happening in the next 5 years!" battle cry since the '70's, and continues to be based the rather unrealistic assumption that even as extraction costs rise, the cost of oil and fuel will remain relatively low. There also tends to be a lot bias towards using the same things in the same ways and ignoring a continually changing marketplace.

                Right now, we have insanely cheap oil, over the coming decades it's expected that natural gas will take over much of the demand. Can we continue for the next century relying on just oil or even oil and gas? No. However that's why we have science and engineering. We easily have the capacity to provide power for the next few thousand years at current and projected levels of consumption, with the technology we have now. And that's with the technology we have now, there are reasons we invest in fusion research and development, as well as the bio generated hydrocarbon researches, because with those we are sorted out for our needs for even longer.

As for general minerals and metals... well, adjusting for inflation, the costs to produce have done nothing but go down.                                     Water... water is another thing. The big problem with water right now is much the same as with food... it's not that there isn't enough in the world, it's that it isn't where the people need to consume it. That is mostly something that can only be addressed by either mass migration of populations, or using technology to produce clean water. Unfortunately the cheapest/easiest way to do that is with desalination plants and the technology there is in flux. It's a low-profit field because nobody wants to pay for water right now, and there are a lot of highly disruptive technologies being developed... building a 20 million dollar plant today might mean it's got the same costs as something that could have 10 times the capacity for the same cost in 5 years.
