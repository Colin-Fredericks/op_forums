          Most of the CWoD games have been around a little over, or a little under 20 years. People learned them in their youth, and keep playing them because they already know them. Sitting and reading a new 400+ page book and setting with splat books is just too time consuming for many people these days. The hobby of Role Playing is waning in general, it's just how things go. There's all sorts of things for people to do with their free time now that don't requiring syncing ever busy schedules for 6+ hours a week. So role playing is a high investment low reward kind of hobby when people tend to flake more and more, or simply don't have time given real world circumstance.

          My point is, people are refusing new world cause they plain already know old world. The people who can make time just to play a game (which is already a hell of a commitment today) don't also want to learn new systems for the most part. Especially when it comes to niche games. World of Darkness is great, but it's far from the main stream in terms of RPGs. It's easier to find a new gaming group of high schoolers than it is to find an old timers group. Those kids have the time do pick up the books and read them and get invested. However very few kids are doing that compared to the 90s. So all in all. Unless you want to play DnD with teenagers, finding any roleplaying group seems to be pretty hard.

          Those hard core enough to keep up with it all often turn to online mediums these days to find the rare like minded individuals. Check roll20 sometime and you might get lucky. Sometimes those people turn to play by post, which is easier to plug into a busy day to day, but is ultimately generally fairly slow paced. Check rpol. A local gaming store might help, but they're fewer and far between. Sometimes you can post LFG add there though.

          I would argue NWoD is no less hard to get into than CWoD, but why learn something new when you already invested the time learning the old stuff back in your youth. I think that's most people's general point of view. I have tons of gamer friends who can't manage to work together to get a game going, and when the scheduling does work out, we bust out our old favorites for ease of play. I've practically given up expecting anyone to read new books. The only game I will run is Star Wars or DnD cause everyone who is a geek or roleplayer already knows the setting, themes, and moods.

          The only other advice I can give you is to check to see if there's a local chapter of the Camarilla or Minds Eye Society or whatever it's called these days in your area. There are LARPers who love all these games, and they will play with you, but my experiences with the Cam LARPs have been overall negative, but you might find a table top group that way.

          Lastly I will say this. And I don't mean to offend anyone. Finding new players is often a total crapshoot as well, as for every okay role player you find (AKA, someone who likes to game like you do). You find at least one person who isn't in it for the same thing you are, and you find one person with limited social skills who does not mesh well with a mostly social game at all. Finding a working cohesive group of people to sync schedules and RP together is insanely hard without it all blowing up over conflicts of personality and game style.

          All that said, I wish you luck,

          Cheers,
          Mark

                                          That seems a bit off. He might have gotten them confused.

                                          In old World of Darkness you have a threshold that changes. In new World of Darkness, outside of special circumstances, the threshold is always 8.

                                          Put down simply, almost every roll is Attribute + Skill + or - Modifiers, which may include other stats like Renown or a Discipline, countered against a target's Defense as a - modifier and so forth.

                                          8, 9, or 10, you've got a success. If you have a 10, you roll again. If you get 5 successes, you've generally got an Exceptional Success, possibly for some added benefit.

                                              Yeah it sounds like your player might have their WoDs mixed up. In OWoD each task has a "difficulty" which is the number you need to roll or beat to get a success, and some tasks also have a minimum number of successes required. So for example, one roll might be difficulty 6 and require 3 successes, which means you'd need to get 6 or higher on at least three dice. On another, you might only need one success, but have a difficulty of 9, so if you roll at least one 9 or 10, you succeed. In NWoD, the "difficulty" and the required number of successes are fixed, you always need an 8 or higher to get a success, and you only ever need one success on a roll to succeed. You adjust the difficulty of a roll with bonus dice and dice penalties.
