                    I've been thinking about The Princess and the Frog.

                    I like that movie quite a bit; to me it's a genuinely good Disney animated movie that I could watch more than once.

                    There's always been something that bothered me about it, though. I never quite could put my finger on it - I always chalked it up to my typical eyerolling at "what you really need in your life is a boyfriend!" stories.

                    Today though, I randomly came upon the real why.

                    There's a moral I want to see in this movie - one I dimly believe is the one the story was actually going for. Like most protagonists, Tiana has a character flaw driving her conflict - in her case, quite obviously, her obsession with "hard work" at the exclusion of anything else in her life. Her father worked hard too, but he managed to strike a balance: hard work, and a loving family. But Tiana only saw the work, and got the wrong lesson - that working harder and harder was the only way to live. She's supposed to understand that she needs the same balance her father had; hard work to fulfill her dreams, and an actual personal life - not just a boyfriend, but friends, family, a life beyond her job.

                    That's what I want to see in the movie. But when I actually watch it, the way it comes across is... different. The movie seems to be saying that her father's hard work failed; that in the end, it was his family, not his work that mattered. He didn't achieve his dream, he died young, and he accomplished nothing outside of his loving, happy family. Which is great! But his family is treated as an outside thing; as if there were two sides of his life, "family" and "work," and work never amounted to anything. "He never did get what he wanted, but he had what he needed." It seems to be saying that Tiana is not unbalanced, but just plain wrong, and that having a lover - not even the abstract of "love" you can find in friends or family, but specifically a boyfriend - is what she really needs.

                    And I don't like that.

                    Bits and pieces of Exalted setting.
