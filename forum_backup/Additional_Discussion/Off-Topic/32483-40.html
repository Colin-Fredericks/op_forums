                                Don't worry Scelesta, I'm about to one-up you in the Complaining About Trivial Crap stakes. At least your issue has a legitimate stressor behind it (one of the main reasons I never want to have kids is to avoid exactly where you are now). And from what infinitesimal amount I've been able to glean, just being stressed out and wanting some time to yourself doesn't a bad parent make, it's what you do as a result. Hanging in there, taking care of the screaming brat as best you can, even when massively stressed? That makes a good parent. Although it does sound like your fiance could take a little more of the load, even if it's only handling the kid for a couple hours while you chill out.

                                EDIT: Re-reading this, I don't know if I communicated my point properly. I did not mean to imply that your issue was trivial, it's really not. I cannot imagine dealing with that kind of ongoing stress day after day, constantly.
                                A while ago (a few weeks) I realised how cripplingly lonely I am. I won't go into details, but essentially barring a not-really-a-relationship when I was 17 (over 10 years ago), I've never had a serious girlfriend. This is hardly surprising; I'm reasonably intelligent and I do try to be friendly to everyone, but I've got the physical attractiveness and personality of a stump, my social abilities are near zero, and I'm fairly solitary by nature. Not surprising no woman is interested and that's perfectly all right. I wouldn't want to spend time with me either. But it does mean that this damn need goes unsatisfied, and I'm still trying to work out a way to deal with it beyond ignoring it or getting angry; one only lasts so long, and the other isn't constructive. I've managed to get a little distance from it, so it isn't occupying my every waking thought, by really knuckling down on my thesis, but, well, something more embarrassing has started happening.

                                Whenever I watch/listen/experience something that elicits an emotional reaction from me, even if it's positive, I'm overcome with the urge to burst into tears. Even if I'm watching an early episode of Madoka Magica, which aren't really all that tragic and actually kind of heartwarming. I have to turn it off and just breathe for a goodly while. This is particularly troubling because my thesis, as with much of psychology, is at least partially guided by emotion; granted, mostly recognising it in my interviewees, but still. So having to take a Spock approach is really affecting me there as well, although I am making some progress.

                                I've considered going and talking to a psychologist, but that requires me to get a referral from a GP (and apart from that being a gigantic pain in the neck, I don't want to go up to a stranger and say "I'm being a giant cry-baby"), or find a way to adjust the insurance to allow me to just go to them, but apparently unless you want to check yourself into a hospital they're no help. And yes, I'm well aware of how rare health insurance is in the US; chalk that up to a combination of me being really, really spoiled and it actually being kind-of common in Australia.
