    Damn, I’m late again with what I picked up.

    Astonishing Ant-Man#9: Still doing the foreshadowing Scott going to prison again, but still don’t know why? They need to wrap that up soon as I’m starting not to care.

    Batman #1: Good issue, good art. We get introduced to new characters Gotham and Gotham Girl, (more like this generation’s version of Gangbuster.) Don’t see them lasting in ten years.

    Civil War II #2: A set up issue to define the battle lines a little more. Best moment, the Hulk, (not Cho,) is coming back to kill them all, Thank God!

    X-Men Civil War II #1: Storms Extraordinary X-Men face off against Magnetos Uncanny X-Men that feels more like a simple disagreement than true conflict. Plus Nightcrawler jumps ship to Magnetos side, so a little added drama there.

    Dark Knight Return-Last Crusade: The intermission tale for DK3, that takes place before The Dark Knight Returns and focuses on Joker and how he killed the previous Robin in Frank Millers Batman Universe. It’s a cool story, but not necessary if you read The Dark Knight Returns you already know what will happen, but still nice to have.

    Descender #12: The sad song of Tim-22. I see a lot of issues like this lately where we get full issues dedicated to antagonist of why they are doing these horrible things. I guess to build sympathy for the bad guy? We saw it with Negan from the Walking Dead and a few other books. Just interrupts the flow of the main story.

    Devolution #5: Mini finally ends and..just went down left field and not sure how they were trying to end this.

    Green Arrow #1: Wasn’t too bad really.

    Green Lanterns #1: The Reds are back, but still wondering why they had Larfleeze on the cover.

    Justice League #51: Where in a rush to get to #52 So we can get back to #1 so We will tell a little tale of Robin working with the League.

    Superman #1: Superman’s kid maybe teaming up with pops. I’m curious, pretty good story.

    Swamp Thing #6: This Mini finally ends, and the villain was who we all though he was is reviled. Poor Mathew, (same one from Sandman,) just can’t catch a break. But all is set right again with this issue. I love Swamp Thing, but this wasn’t his greatest story. So my hope for a decent ongoing Swamp Thing book is dwindling.

    Titans Rebirth: Setting things right again and it feels good.

    Uncanny X-Men #8: Still my favorite X-Men book. The story is still fun to read even though the Event as a whole sucks.

    Side Note..Is anyone else following the Apocalypse War Event? Is it even an Event? It just feels like each X-Book has their own little “Flavor of Apocalypse” story but don’t see the connection to ALL the books. And I hate Hopless’s All New X-Men so I don’t even read that.

                                    So I gave She-Wolf, an Image comic allegedly about a girl reacting to a werewolf bite, a try. I say allegedly, because honestly if I didn't read the blurb I'd be totally baffled as to what was supposed to be going on and what was real and what wasn't. Now, that can work, but I get less “existential horror” and more “seriously, what the hell is even going on?”.

                                    This can be illustrated more clearly by example than explanation. This is two pages, one occurring directly after another. Context doesn’t really help.





                                    Is the girl in the bed the same as the girl who was scratched? Is that the same as the girl by the window, now magically dressed? There are parts of semi-coherent narrative, but it doesn’t take long before it flips back to just weirdness. And they rarely establish much. We don’t know the character, we don’t know the situation, and we don’t know who the werewolf was (her boyfriend? A friend? Some innocent recipient who she cursed for unknown reasons?) Why is it presented this way? It’s just frustrating and artsy for the sake of being artsy. There’s innovative, and then there’s just bad storytelling. Later on she spontaneously turns into a werewolf in front of people, and they seem shocked, but the next panel people are just talking to her as normal, with no transition.

                                    So, yeah. Like the idea, very poor execution.

                                    Project: Nemesis recently finished. I’m… not sure whether I like this or not. It doesn’t really give us many answers, and the ones we do get tend to more undermine the story than help it, but it does a really good job of making us feel how the characters do – there’s a big monster rampaging and there’s basically nothing they can do about it. They’re totally out of their depth, and desperately trying to stay alive.

                                    The last issue does tie up the plot threads, and it’s not a bad plot as they go, but, I dunno, it seemed a bit ham-handed/smultsy to me.

                                    The art is pretty much always gorgeous, and I do like the characters – each has a consistent and clear motivation (mostly variants on the theme of “stay alive”), and the basic idea was potentially very interesting. Worth a look, I’d say, but that’s just me.
