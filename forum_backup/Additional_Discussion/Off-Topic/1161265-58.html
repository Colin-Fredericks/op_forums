        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

        That... is... not the point? At all? "Small thematic overlap" isn't the issue. It's about classes operating on completely different levels of specificity and narrowness in concept and design. I mean, you've said it yourself. When you play a ranger, you know you'll have a cut-up druid spell list, a hand-me-down druid animal companion, terrain-related abilities and combat feats. When you play a fighter... that in itself tells us nothing about the character. Likewise for rogue. Barbarians are even worse than rangers, here - playing one means you're a raging berserker using strength-based melee weapons. Period.

        You can draw a sort of spectrum in how much your class says about your character, really. Fighter and rogue are so generic as to mean nothing. Clerics, wizards and sorcerers are more specific in that they tell you what kind of magic your character uses, out of several well-defined kinds. Druids are more specific than those three, because they're not just divine casters, but nature-oriented divine casters. Bards I'd place in the middle, appropriately enough. Then we have rangers, barbarians, monks and paladins, who basically each cover one concept, with some variations.

                                        "Weapon master" is absolutely generic in a combat-focused game where almost everyone will use some kind of weapon, somehow. Every class that primarily attacks with mundane weapons will become a "master" of them by any definition. "Weapon master" doesn't tell us how the class uses them and why. It's not about a lack of magic, either. Consider the barbarian. It doesn't use magic, but it's got strongly-defined character. Too strongly, because it swings the pendulum too hard in the other direction. But it's not directionless like the fighter.

                                        If you took the barbarian, renamed it "vanguard" or something and defined it as an aggressive martial combatant who can periodically push past their limits in a variety of ways (one of which can be a berserker rage), I feel like you'd be approaching a good middle ground.

                                        As far as Batman goes, he's not a D&D fighter. If you had to pin him to a D&D class, it'd be rogue. And his contribution to the Justice League is heavily predicated on being loaded with obscene amounts of money and gadgets.

                                            Pathfinder's Vigilante class does the Batman thing well. You have a social side, which tends towards being renowned, but who can be an artist or craftsman or other sort of celebrity and they get some neat abilities in that state but nothing really good for combat.

                                            For combat they switch over (having to do it in private, generally), and you decide between being an avenger or a stalker. The Avenger is more of a fighter type, the stalker more of a rogue. The 'vigilante' side could have a different alignment within one step per axis. So you could be lawful good and neutral good, or chaotic neutral and neutral evil. The stalker lends itself towards secret assassin types just as much as it does mystery men.

                                            I think it pays to have the mixed kind over the standard class. Or at least retool fighter to something. Soldier, sellsword, martial artist, whatever. What distinguishes a fighter from anything else?
