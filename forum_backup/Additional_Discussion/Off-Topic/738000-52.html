                            You have this chronicle that you're running, see? And it's set in the World of Darkness. Then a new supplement for the World of Darkness is published in which some sort of event happens; and in the wake of that event, the setting is noticeably different. As the Storyteller, you're put in the position of deciding whether to adjust your chronicle to conform to this new development, or to ignore it and risk not being able to use portions of future supplements that assume that the new development has taken place.

                            That's metaplot. The Original World of Darkness was (in)famous for this sort of thing, with the events of the Year of the Reckoning being the most egregious example. But the only example I can think of where the new World of Darkness did this was in the Promethean series, when the ongoing Water of Life featured chronicle visited New Orleans. Between the time Vampire: the Requiem described New Orleans and the supplement in question was published, Hurricane Katrina radically altered the city; and the Story in question incorporated Hurricane Katrina into its plot. Other than that, I can't think of a single bit of metaplot in the entire New World of Darkness.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                Ehh, I wouldn't mind a series of metaplot books. The Invictus proclaims itself to be the Camarilla? A new covenant rises that possesses its own, unique Blood Sorcery? That's all nice and well, if they're neatly packaged in the series, where each major event is its own book and contains its own changes. Seeing metaplot developments in the main gameline? No, simply because it would shift the focus of the game and make bookkeeping difficult.

                                                History and setting depth, however, I'm all for. It makes reading the books fun, like revealing hidden truths about the world I'm about to explore in my game. That's because in case I actually want to heavily modify parts of the setting and come up with my own, unique mix, I will do it anyhow. I don't see the difference between "What are the Strix's relationship to the Ventrue? * Maybe [a]. * Or is it [b]. *Wait, [c] looks interesting too!" and a proper backstory. We can get both, as V20 demonstrated. As a paying customer of OPP, I really can't imagine why I'd ever request that I am given less material for the same $$$, simply because more might make me feel uncomfortable about picking/changing/erasing bits here and there.

                                                        Originally posted by PMárk View Post
                                                        About metaplot: I know it is a totaly superfluous argument, if sometone don't like it, then it is. But I did never understand, why someone hating it with so much enthusiasm. Yes they have good and bad stories in any setting, it's inevitable, but for me one of the CWoD's strong points are the metaplot. Things do happen, characters do things, it's making the world breathing and alive, create history and gave a storyteller a mine of hooks and ideas. If it is not for my taste, I won't use it. If I want change things, I can. Besides it makes novels, which is a good thing. It only will be restraining, if I let it be and it's always better, IMO, to have opportunities and devices and don't use them if I don't want than don't have them.
                                                        The problem is when the metaplot changes things (and if it doesn't, it's not really meta-plot, is it?). My "favorite" example is the nation of Tethyr in the Forgotten Realms. In the 2e FR boxed set, Tethyr was described as being in the middle of a civil war, with various nobles all trying to beat each other up on account of the power vacuum left when the royal family was assassinated a few decades back. So when I learned that there was a boxed set called Lands of Intrigue that covered Tethyr and the neighboring nation of Amn, I thought "Hey, that sounds like a really cool place to run a game" and bought the box.

                                                        Except in the meantime, they had released a novel where the long-lost heir to the throne, who has (I kid you not) been living in hiding as Elminster's scribe, returns, marries, and he and his new Queen set things right again, so now it's a nation where nobles intrigue at the royal court instead of battle each other in a civil war. To quote the VeryBritishProblems twitter: That's not quite what I had in mind (translation: what the bloody hell is this?)

                                                        Metaplot also reduces the value of old books. TORG (a game about other dimensions invading Earth and bringing their realities with them) was a game that relied heavily on meta-plot, and even had players influence it through surveys in the Infiniverse magazine. At one point, one of the invaders lost about half of their territory - so any previous material that was based on the stuff that was there is now nullified.

                                                        I much prefer the Eberron approach, which is to present a world where all the material takes place at a particular point in time, and at that point any of a number of things are about to happen. Perhaps the Lords of Dust manage to free one of the fiendish Overlords trapped beneath the world's crust. Perhaps an army of Warforged comes boiling out of the Mournlands. Perhaps the Last War gets re-ignited. Perhaps the Inspired make a play for greater influence in the court of Breland. Any of these things could happen, but the setting presents a point where they haven't happened yet.

                                                            Another FR example could be the role of the drow as a whole. Due to events in novels, the drow did gain a much more active role in the world, much more prominent presence on the surface than earlier. I like it, because I like the drow. I mean the drow drow.

                                                            The thing is: naturaly, not everyone will like any random story changes in any setting, that's for sure. However, I'm still holding the opinion, that an inner storyline's making the setting a living one, and that's good. As I mentioned, one of the positive side of that is it makes novels/comics/etc and I (as a lot of us, I think) did come to the hobby through these and still love to read them. Besides I like to read cool stories in a setting book, with events, characters and so on. Sandbox is great and needed, but stories too, and for me, the ideal is some balance between the two.

                                                            In fact, while I like a lot of NWod things very much, in the end I did stick with the CWod because comparing the two, I was feeling the NWoD's settings a little bit... connectionless I think. Like things are floating in the air, everything is isolated, only local political games and so on. Clans/tribes/etc. are archetypes, with no expanded history. Overall the whole games are very "here and now" and "just here, just now". I totaly can understand why one prefers that style and why found the CWoD (or any setting with an ongoing story) restrictive as a storyteller. I just doesn't feel that way to that extent and clearly a lot of us also didn't. If I want to change, I change. If some player's plot-lawyering me, I simply state, that in my game, now, this is the situation. All of the books saying I can do this, so I will do this if I feel the need. If one cannot deal with that, well it's not compulsory to play. However, I also think, as I wrote earlier, that if NPCs are the real heroes in any story, especially in modules, that's also equally bad. Balance between the two are essential and I hope the old and new guys and girls in OPP and in WW/Paradox will find that fine line!

                                                            All in all, I don't think that either the no-metaplot, or the pro-metaplot opinion is better than the other, only different tastes and different preferences. Live and let live I say. I hope the future of NWoD is really fully secure, because clearly a lot of people are prefering that style and it would be sad to lose that community.
