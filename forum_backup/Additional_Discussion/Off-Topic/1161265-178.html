                            Originally posted by Morty View Post
                            I haven't read a lot of Dragonlance, but I don't think it stopped anyone from portraying goblins and ogres (I don't think there are orcs in DL) as inherently evil.
                            It should be mentioned that in Dragonlance, Evil is much more legitimate than in other settings- like, sure, it is still evil, but evil has a place in the world, with balance between good an evil being the most important feature. Sympathy for evil characters is very, very common, and following dark gods and using dark magic (as well as being a member of an "evil culture"), does not make them unplayable in the general setting.

                            Also, that is actually not the case- sure, ogres were created by the gods of evil, but the modern ogres are those who were cursed by the gods of good for their evil actions. Those "ogres" who went against their culture (the irda), have escaped the curse, and it was a matter of their choices. In the late Age of Mortals, we even see how a civilization of ogres tries to grow beyond the evil limitations of their society, and the ogres are presented as a playable race in DL. Goblins, while also seen generally as "evil", are known to have produce a few heroes, and have a grew to become more and more complex and developed as time progressed and the setting evolved. Heck, Toede, originally an antagonistic comic relief, have became an hero during a bet between two devils on his soul.

                            And of course, we have the draconians- a race which was outright stated to be evil from birth, only to slowly find their p[lace in the world, and become a true nation, growing beyond what their creators imagined. Not to mention the minotaurs, or even dragons.

                            So yeah, between the fact that being "evil" is much more legitimate in Dragonlance, and that the evil alignment has been presented as the influences of dark gods and the culture they came from, and the fact that most of the races have produced heroes which show that your race does not choose your alignment, and that the greatest racists in the setting are humans and elves, I don't think that the claim is that valid in Dragonlance. Only ogres have the vast reputation of being inherently evil, but again, that mostly their culture and religion than the fact that they were created by the gods of evil, for it is a cosmological truth of the setting that every race has the freedom to choose whether to be good or evil.

                            In short, no, it is objectively false that any of the races of Krynn is evil by birth, as it is objectively true that any soul has the right to choose between right and wrong.

                                                        Been meaning to talk about this for a while, but didn't really have the energy to do so. They dropped another Unearthed Arcana early this month, for another pass at some subclasses. They are the Phantom Rogue (replaces the Revived), The Genie pact Warlock (replaces Noble Genie), and the Order of Scribes Wizard (replaces Archivist Artificer; yes, really).

                                                        Phantom Rogue
                                                        • Reflavored away from being a Rogue that died and came back to life, and instead just a Rogue who learned to bridge the gap between life and death in a nonspecific way. Could have learned it from a Necromancer or be a disciple of a death god. It's probably a good change, because the Revived had a very specific theme that not everyone might be interested in. You could certainly go with the character having learned their death powers from having come back to life, if you really wanted.
                                                        • Instead of using "Bolts of the Grave" as part of a Cunning Action, instead you use "Wails from the Grave" to do half your Sneak Attack damage to another creature when you use Sneak Attack on a different one. Puts more focus around the Sneak Attack - which the Rogue will want to do every turn anyway - rather than forcing the PC to jump through hoops to get their damage.
                                                        • The "Tokens of Past Lives" have been rebranded "Tokens of the Departed". Instead of being a poetic way to referring to the additional proficiencies you get when you finish rests (which can now be swapped at the end of Long or Short rests, not just Long), you get actual, physical tokens when something dies within 30 ft of you. As in tiny objects you can carry in your inventory, that look like whatever. (Amusingly, the article suggests looking up the Trinkets table from the Player's Handbook to decide the appearance of the tokens). You get some passive benefit while you have one on you, and you can destroy one to ask a spirit a question.
                                                        • The above bit about destroying a token to ask a question is Phantom's replacement for the Revived's ability to cast Speak With Dead. Personally, I think Speak With Dead was more useful (since you could ask any remains you find questions, plural, rather than just that of the creature you happened to get a token from). Plus, my homebrew "Skull Scholar" archetype relies on Speak With Dead, and now we have one fewer character class that could accommodate it. *Sad face*.
                                                        • Instead of being able to teleport at 17th level, the Phantom can go incorporeal at 13th level, allowing them to phase through objects/creatures and have a 10 ft fly speed. While I kind of miss being able to bamf around the battlefiend, phasing through walls isn't bad, and you get it earlier. (You can also recharge this feature by spending a token). The Phantom instead gets a buff to their "Wails from the Grave" damage at 17th. Good, but nothing a character can't afford to skip on, if they want to multiclass.
                                                        The Genie Warlock
                                                        • Makes what kind of genie patron the Warlock pacted with more important, due to what elemental resistances you get, AND what spells are added to your Warlock spell list. Half of the spells given by this subclass are general to genies, while the other half are particular to whether you made a pact with a Dao, Djinni, Efreet, or Marid.
                                                        • Unprecedented among Warlock patrons, all Genie Warlocks also get one 9th level spell added to their list, in addition to the normal complement of ten: Wish. Which makes perfect sense, as a highly valuable servant of a Genie should be able to get a Wish from them every once in a while. Moreover, I can easily see the Genie granting the Wish having their own ideas about what is and is not a proper use of that power. (So, no using Wish to wish yourself free from a contract).
                                                        • Dropped the previous version's tethering mechanic. Instead, you can use the Vessel you get as part of the pact (which still doubles as an arcane focus) as a mini demiplane. At first just going into it yourself to rest or achieve safety for a number of hours, and then later being able to bring in the rest of the party. The Warlock gets to be slightly more self-sufficient, but can still help out the party. Moreover, at higher levels, resting in the Vessel lets the party gain the benefits of a Short Rest in the span of 10 minutes, rather than an hour, giving the Genie Warlock a bootleg version of the Catnap spell.
                                                        • At 6th level, you can give yourself a flying speed a number of times per day.
                                                        • The capstone ability is a "Limited Wish", letting you cast any one spell of 6th level or less, from any spell list, once per 1d4 days.
                                                        • Really like the changes to this subclass. Makes it seem more thematically consistent, and less like it only exists to test out a novel game mechanic (tethering). I hope the fourth kind of pact boon gets reintroduced later.
                                                        Order of Scribes Wizard
                                                        • Replaces the Archivist subclass of Artificer. Leans into the idea of a Wizard as being book-centric. Not sure I like the abandonment of calligraphy as an art form, and spell scrolls as a craft.
                                                        • Can summon a magic quill that has its own ink (in whatever color you like). Can erase anything written with it as a bonus action (erasable ink pen). And halves the time and money needed to copy spells into your spellbook, no matter its school. This last bit seems slightly powerful in comparison to the base Wizard subclasses, that can only get a discount on spells within their chosen school.
                                                        • Scribe Wizard can turn a spellbook into an intelligent spellbook. Can be used as an arcane focus. Can replace damage types of spells cast with that of another damage type in another spell recorded in the book. Can just waive the added time requirement to cast Ritual spells, once per long rest. Can recreate the ascended spellbook if it's lost or destroyed, making it act very much like a Tome Warlock's tome.
                                                        • Gets the Archivist's ability to manifest a tiny floating "familiar", except without half the subclass features that added onto it. Mainly for spying, illuminating, or casting spells from its space. No using it to cause psychic damage.
                                                        • At high levels you can teleport with the manifest mind thing, and can also come back from death by spending spells - not spell slots, SPELLS - written in the ascended spellbook. They disappear from it to power the resurrection, and you can't ever cast those lost spells again unless you use Wish spells, one per spell. I find this part very punitive, since if you needed to just recopy spells to your book to get them back, it would still cost time and money per spell. (Albeit not as much, given your magic quill). Wish spells used for purposes other than replicating other spells tend to carry the risk of something bad happening. Some would say the risk of losing spells forever is a small price to pay to avoid death. I don't know if I fully agree.
                                                        • Generally I think the Order of Scribes is find - flavorful as hell - but I don't know if I like losing the Archivist to get this Wizard subclass. Especially since Wizards already have many subclasses, whereas the Artificer has very few.
                                                        • It's confirmed that the Psionic and Onomancer Wizard are official dead and will have no more support. *Sad Face*.
