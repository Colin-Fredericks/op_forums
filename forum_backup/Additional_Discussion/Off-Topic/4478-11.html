                                                Currently on my mind:

                                                In March 2012, after finishing Mass Effect 3, I started following a new rule with the media I consume: when a series I'm following (in any medium) reaches its grand finale, I don't go in blind. I may have been following the story breathlessly, taking in each new installment the day it becomes available - but for the series finale, I will be waiting a couple days, then going online and intentionally seeking out spoilers that summarize what happened.

                                                The reason I do this: my experience has been that people are terrible at endings. Especially when the series was popular or critically acclaimed. Maybe the ending makes a sudden shift in tone, or leaves a bunch of stuff unsatisfactorily resolved, or screws up what we know about the characters. Or (as has happened too many times) the creator decides they need to get cute and artsy with the ending for whatever reason, probably doing all three of the above in the process.

                                                When a series I love ends that way, spoiling it for myself does a surprising amount to help. There's less of the shocked disbelief, and none of the irritation-building-to-anger as I wait for closure that isn't forthcoming. Instead, I can quickly see the basics of what happened, acknowledge to myself yes, that is just a godawful ending, what were they thinking and adjust my expectations accordingly. And if on the other hand the series managed to wrap things up in a satisfying manner after all, then I'll still enjoy watching it.

                                                Anyway this is mostly a roundabout way of complaining that a TV series I enjoyed ended in a kinda stupid way this week, and I'm disappointed about that. But still, less disappointed than if I hadn't pre-spoiled it.
