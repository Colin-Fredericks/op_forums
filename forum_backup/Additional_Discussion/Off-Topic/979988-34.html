            Originally posted by ElvesofZion View Post
            Post
            There's a lot here, and I don't disagree with all of it by any means. I would like to share my own position though, for the sake of a back and forth.


            The ‘Big’ part of those points mainly refers to the idea of them having a powerful lobbying arm, an are a that I would argue needs an extreme amount of reform.


            Do you have a position on how you'd like it to be reformed?

            I don’t believe that substances should be banned for sale because it is my business if I want to buy lead paint, not the governments. But it is their job to ensure that I am not sold something that claims to be lead free but actually does contain lead.
            The issue is that there were unsafe levels of lead in our entire environment. The lead lobbying groups and scientists claimed this was entirely natural, only for scientists who weren't on the payroll to explain that it wasn't. The argument that it doesn't really hurt anyone if you buy products with lead in it and everyone else doesn't - the rest of the population will still feel the effects of unnaturally high levels of lead in the environment. This is especially true because lead is so cheap and easy to work with, people will still use it, even if it is harmful.

            Another example would be cigarette companies, their product is highly addictive, often engineered to be more addictive for the sake of getting customers and keeping them. At some point, I feel, we need to step in and regulate the business to keep industry from taking advantage of consumers, because on paper it's nice to think that everyone is an informed objective consumer, we're not, we're animals who suffer cravings and lapses in judgment. A more or less identical situation is happening with sugar now, especially as children are targeted and marketed to before they're of an age to really understand what's going on, doctors get to deal with 10 year olds who have type 2 diabetes, and it's pretty terrible.

            Then we have things like global warming, which basically isn't going to be corrected if we let the free market have its way, it's had plenty of time to prove otherwise.


            I am very torn on the pharma/medical front, I tend to disagree with conservatives and may libertarians on it. I generally think that the right to live should likely be protected. But the actual implementation of it leaves a lot of issues for me. As I said above, it is my right to use lead-based paints if i want to, but is it then the government and thus the peoples responsibility to take care of me if i get sick from it? I also understand the argument (in theory) that private industry helps drive innovation and progress in the medical field.
            What about people who 'just get sick'? What about people who were born sick? What about people who were born sick because their parents used products with lead in them?

            It's also worth pointing out that places with a single payer healthcare system still have better healthcare than we do, and not just in cost but in terms of quality. Private Industry has not lead to America beating other countries in terms of quality of care or life.

            Banking is also a very large topic, my general feeling is that the government should step away from banks, way more then the currently are. But that the focus on a contract driven society would help create a culture where people are more wary of bad options like payday loans and sub-prime mortgage scams.
            I don't think a contract driven society would do much to change things. People don't do payday loans because they're ignorant to the danger, they do it out of desperation. It's sort of like signing a contract with Ursula or whatever, people would still do it - but the thing is, should we allow people to prey on the most desperate like that?

            Social Security
            I don't really have any arguments for this. It's just a matter of personal preference for me - I think it's okay for me to pay taxes so that the widow down the street can get a social security check, and so on. It's a matter of solidarity for me, and paying into Social Security is a way for me to help others and myself. For a really good example of why I feel this way, you can watch the documentary 'Requiem for the American Dream', it's on Netflix, unless they took it down.

            Technological progress is an inevitability, it has always happened. The invention of the printing press removed the need for scribes, cars replaced horses, etc. People will find work or leisure will become work: the rise of e-sports for example. I am not scared of progress.
            There are many articles talking about it, and it's an issue of debate among experts, here's one example: http://harvardpolitics.com/world/automation/ - I'm sure you can find dozens and dozens more with a Google search.

            Right, so the issue is that in the Industrial Revolution and in similar leap periods, the technology only eased the burden on labor. Things are quite a bit different this time. We don't just have self-driving cars, we're looking at doctor's being replaced by diagnostic machines, therapists being replaced by artificial intelligence (already being tested in some areas, and they're, unfortunately for me, a huge hit. People much prefer confiding their darkest issues with a machine rather than an actual human person). Food service jobs go away, and so to do customer service jobs. It's a leap where the thing that set humans apart from beasts of burden or machines - our brains - are being the equipment outmoded.

            And I don't mind the idea of everyone using the leisure time to become artists, or athletes, or esports stars, but if you had any experience as one of these things you'd know that expecting everyone 'to make it' with careers like that is nuts. Sports athletes and esports stars alike have to take drugs to remain competitive, and run themselves into the ground. For every one successful writer, there are hundreds of us who can't quit their day job because they will never make ends meet.

            That's the purpose of a minimal standard of living. Corporations are going to profit immensely from automation, the dwindling middle class will not. A minimum standard of living gives people the money they need to survive without becoming homeless, and if you get a job as a writer or a League of Legends steamer, that's fine, it will raise your income above the bare minimum - you make it big like Neil Gaiman? Then maybe you don't need the minimum standard of living anymore. At least, that's the idea.

            The true center of capitalism really has nothing to do with promoting a better product, at least not directly. The central tenet of capitalism that I think tends to be ignored is that ‘a person deserves just compensation for their labors’.
            The actual central tenet of Capitalism is that means of production be privately owned, and that these private interests be in charge of its use for the means of profit. That's kind of the defining principle of capitalism. However, it's an argument that requires explanation, and the explanation for why capitalism is a good system is that the government, or social interests, cannot best determine how the means of production should be used. Private interests, driven by profit, are most likely to succeed and produce a superior result because they are motivated by survival, in a perfect capitalist system without monopolies, the superior product will out, and inferior products will sink. In other words - Progress will follow Capital. That is the argument that capitalism makes.

            I'm not sure I've ever seen any economist or historian claim that Capitalism means just compensation for their labor. In fact the only time I've ever heard that is from FDR while proposing his New Deal, which were effectively a number of steps taken to redistribute wealth and insure that the people had more bargaining rights. In other words, a series of measures largely considered to be Socialist.

                I would add for Solana's post (and ElvesofZion's benefit):
                1. One thing technology has always done is concentrate capital (defined as the means of production) into the hands of the wealthy. Essentially, every labor-saving technology allows a factory to be more productive, which means that fewer laborers are needed or the same number can produce the same amount. This means that the laborers have less collective power, very slightly, for each technological advancement For instance, this can have the negative effect in the future whereby a government can deploy its robot soldiers to oppress a populace without that pesky problem of the army turning on the government. This is not necessarily a bad thing - previously it served to get people out of shitty jobs and into good ones, but see point 3 below.
                2. No social action has ever prevented a technology from coming to market. Ever. So the question is not 'how do we stop it?', because that's impossible, but 'how do we adapt to it once it's here?' This is meant as an answer to Elves'
                3. As Solana indicates, what will happen is that technology will eliminate substantial numbers of jobs without creating enough jobs to fill the gaps that they left behind. There will be structural unemployment that people have no ability to do anything about on a substantial level.
                  1. People often comment "Well! We'll have jobs taking care of the robots, right?" Sure. A few. Ones that themselves will increasingly vanish as robots take that over, too. And then when riots come from people not being able to feed themselves, we'd better hope the government doesn't already have a sufficient mass of effective robot soldiers that they can just push a button and say "So long." - Something, by the by, that a libertarian government could not really guard against (indeed, if anything, there'd be greater pushes to it to cheapen the cost of maintaining a military.)

                                        Being Pro Gun Control is also not a politically conservative stance. Just pointing that out. Overall the opinions expressed in that post are on the conservative side of things with a few very notable exceptions.
                                        BTW, the part about there being an "Atheist Dogma" is an inaccurate use of the word dogma. There isn't an atheist authority that has set down a set of rules about what constitutes atheism, and if a better scientific theory about the creation of the universe comes about atheists don't have a codified belief system that needs to be abandoned in order to accommodate the new scientific theory. Being an atheist requires only one thing, a lack of belief in the existence of gods. To them, God and Santa Claus have the same level of validity. Some are smug about this, others are willing to just keep their opinion to themselves and let others choose to believe what they wish without being jerks about it. As long as someone's religious views aren't being used to justify harm towards others then I'm quite happy to be part of the latter camp.

See the "time is a circle" monologue from True Detective for one hypothesis, and we already have mathematical models that demonstrate how time could move backwards relative to the perceptions of the observer.
                                        If one can believe in a God that has no beginning or end then they should be able to apply the same thinking to the existence of our universe and the material that makes it up. Something not tethered to Time in the fashion humans are would not have the same type of causal relationship to reality as we do, nor the same type of understanding of cause and effect as us (in regards to a cognitive entity). To that being the cause could very well be generated by the existence of the effect. The effect exists so the cause would have to (have) happen(ed) but would exist in an indeterminate state until observed. This is the state of being that Dr. Manhattan existed in the comics (to give an example of what this hypothetical being could be like), Yog-Sothoth from the Lovecraft Mythos would be another possible example.
