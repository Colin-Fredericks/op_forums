                Just finished a book about the ancient Maya; it was written by an anthropologists, not a Mayan-historian. so, it's more technical and more detailed with lots of science to back up the facts; for instance, the Mayan didn't starve when their civilization-collapsed; oxygen-isotopes on skeletons confirmed they were as well fed as those in the past.

                What gets me the most, that applies a lot to CoD, is their ritual blood-letting. Doesn't that scream vampire? But, what if, there were no vamps there during that time? I mean, what if, vamps were strictly Old World, and their blood letting so for something different? Someone like a Blood-bather?

                Sorry Shadows of Mexico, but, the old-Mexico may not have been as Foresaken/Requiem paradise as depicted in the book. Vampires would have a real hard time getting to the Americas*. Even Gangrel would have difficulties even with Earth-Meld, unless they crossed the Bering land-bridge (which would make them old, really old). Or, to avoid the Sun, one walked, under water. The People, not so much. They could have gotten by boat, or, really did cross the Land-bridge.

                And while reading the book, I couldn't help but think they'll be America's future (USA). A collapsed civilization with ruins everywhere, with people living in isolated tribes. I mean, we're using the resources of the entire planet to support our lifestyle; this can't go on forever.

                                        I've just finished Hamattan Season by Tochi Onyebuchi. It's sort-of a fantasy-noir-mystery but the most interesting element was its setting of colonial West Africa (under French occupation at the time). I think this one was really a case of "lesser than the sum of its parts". I've liked pretty much all about it, the characters, the setting, the story... but oh man, the pacing of the plot. To describe the plot as moving at breakneck speed is an understatement. There's even a moment in the middle where the protagonist thinks about how he can't believe that this all started with a dead girl. Looking back on it, I really did feel like the protagonist couldn't walk five meters without him rushing headlong into another plot-point.

                                        But I'm really excited to dig into Hemlock & Silver by T. Kingfisher. I heard good things about the writer but for some reason I've never picked up one of their books before.
