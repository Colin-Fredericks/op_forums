    On the usage of "she" instead of "it"

    07-13-2014, 03:32 AM
    Could someone explain me the rule behind it? I see it frequently, but as I really like English (not my mothertongue), I'd like to know when to use it and when not to.

    Originally posted by Blood & Smoke, Protean
    Rubbery Flesh: Warping her flesh into a rubbery substance and her bones into flexible cartilage, the vampire can stretch, condense, and contort her body in impossible ways.       Describing someone as 'it' is impersonal and can be read as rude. English doesn't have a good singular, gender-neutral pronoun and using 'they' and 'their' as the singular is clumsy.

      In Onyx Path products, the writers try to personalise the writing by using 'he' or 'she' in examples when referring to an unnamed example person - from your quote, 'the vampire' character could be male or female, but the description sounds nicer as 'warping her flesh' than 'warping its flesh', or 'It can fit through, or 'shooting at it', or 'Protean dots to its dice pool'.

      Writers I've worked with try to vary which gender is used between examples, but as we're working on individual sections and don't know as we're writing how they'll flow with all the other drafts, we trust in the editors and developers to make the usage relatively even.

                                          Originally posted by Jesuncolo View Post
                                          So there is no hard-fast rule. It's just &quot;because I don't like to use it for people&quot;?
                                          No, "it" is only used for objects, not subjects. People are either "he" or "she", and technically "he" doubles as the grammatically correct gender-neutral term for people. However, that rule is pretty out dated, as it doesn't take a genius to realize that "he" is a gendered pronoun. Some people use "they", and this is technically incorrect because "they" is plural, but it is generally accepted because for one thing, not all native English speakers know all of the rules of our language, and because it's preferable to the unfortunate implications of the male gendered pronoun being used as the default for people of unspecified gender. "He or she" is technically correct, but is very clunky. "One" correct in some situations, i.e. " Warping one's flesh and one's bones into flexible cartilage" is fine, but "the vampire can stretch, condense, and contort one's body in impossible ways" doesn't work. There isn't really a gender-neutral pronoun that works in all situations besides "it" which objectifies the subject, so there is a lot of inconsistency between different writers. There was some effort to introduce a singular, gender-neutral subject pronoun to the language (xir, as Katsura mentioned), but it was largely unsuccessful, in no small part because "xir" just sounds silly. White Wolf and Onyx Path alternate between "he" and "she" every time the subject changes, which is in my opinion the best solution, as it is entirely grammatically correct.

                                          Vampires in my tongue are male gendered, as well as everything non-gendered, but I thought that everything that is non-gendered in English was always referred to as &quot;it&quot;. It's not an Onyx Path exclusive either: it seldom happened to me that a D&D book referring to a &quot;spellcaster&quot; as &quot;she&quot;, though the caster itself was not gendered.
                                          Every thing is referred to as "it" in English, but people are not things. As a vampire and a spellcaster are both kinds of people, referring to either as "it" is objectifying.

                                                      A bit of important linguistic history:

                                                      The singular they is first recorded as being used in the 1400s.

                                                      The gender-neutral he was actively started as a movement in the 1750s, and wasn't commonly accepted until later.

                                                      As English has no official body of authority for what counts as officially correct (which most languages do have), we're stuck with something we've been using for over 600 years or something that some academics convinced the British parliament to start using a bit over 200 years ago. There's no actual way to say which is "more grammatically correct," because there's actually no such thin in English besides popular usage. But the singular they has both the longer standing usage (it has been part of English before the full transition to modern standardized English was finished), and is actually neutral.

                                                      Also, an important alternative that often gets ignored is "one" which can function as a personal gender-neutral pronoun. However one, just like they and he, runs into some issues in terms of both flow and formality.

                                                      Middle English did have a gender neutral pronoun but for whatever reason we dropped it. However one of the better invented pronouns for this is an attempt to modernize said dropped pronoun. Which basically involves dropped the "th" from the "they" equivalents since the older one essentially worked on the same principle. Though in some dialects it isn't all that clear.

                                                      --------

                                                      Anyway, the WW/OP approach of variation in using he and she is a very useful compromise position. By mixing up both you avoid the inclusive issue, avoid the language flow issue of using they all the time or having to constantly establish the gender of a vampire in every example, and avoid the tone issue of using one all the time.
