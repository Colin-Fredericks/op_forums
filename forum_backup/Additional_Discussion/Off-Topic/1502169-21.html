        I want to thank Heavy Arms for tackling the argument with much more tact than I could, and I would like to agree with/slightly expand upon one point in particular.

        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
        Human sexes are not binary, they're bimodal. Being bimodal, there's always going to be people in the middle that don't neatly fit into male or female based on whichever biological benchmark you want to use.
        My motto these days is, "Nothing is simple; everything is complicated." Sex is one of the most complicated things out there, to the point that I'm personally not even sure words like "man" and "woman" describe real things that exist in the real world, so they definitely don't prescribe how people are or should be. We just let our attachments to these outdated and overly simplistic categories become so important in our minds that we'll get angry if reality doesn't neatly suit the preconceptions we try to force upon it.

        Words like "man" and "woman" describe concepts that were thought up by cavemen who thought thunder was made by sky-spirits battling each other and that an eclipse was a demon trying to eat the sun. Those categories are not terribly accurate and are being exposed as losing their usefulness, which is fine. That's how science works: we get better at detecting and identifying the components of reality, and update or abandon old ideas to suit our new knowledge. Our society is not beholden to keep incorrect ideas around just because they're old, let alone to persecute anyone in an attempt to deny how incorrect old ideas are.

                Originally posted by JimB View Post
                Sex is one of the most complicated things out there, to the point that I'm personally not even sure words like "man" and "woman" describe real things that exist in the real world, so they definitely don't prescribe how people are or should be.
                I do think it's important to remember that just because something is an artificial human construct doesn't mean it isn't "real" even if reality is complicated.

                To get away from more serious issues: Video game consoles are real, even though humans made them up; including the idea of putting value in a highly specialized computer just for playing video games. Now, there are lots of things to discuss where the differences between consoles and PCs don't matter, or the differences between the latest X-Box and Playstation don't matter. If you, say, want to improve the video game rating systems so parents can be better informed (since the ESRB sucks at its job)... you can ignore the complex reality of hardware specs and corporate-enforced exclusivity. If you're trying to figure out if you want to buy a video game console at all, and is so which, the nuances suddenly matter a lot more.

                I think part of accepting the complexity of reality is also accepting that scope dictates how much complexity we need to include in a given discussion. If the conversation participants all accept that human sexes are bimodal, then there's no reason to go into the complexities of why we know that's the case. We can focus on how to adjust society to accept that the outdated concept of a sex binary is outdated, and better accommodate people in the fuzzy middle between the two main modes that have historically been mistreated by society.

                Human experiences of artificial social constructs are also very real. Scientifically, "race" isn't real. There's no scientific way to match the social construct of "race" to a concrete set of biological metrics. But race is very real for people that experience discrimination based on it. Women, regardless of the fuzzy edges of what "woman" means, face real problems because of that label. It ultimately doesn't matter if the line used to divide the favored and the outsider is made up or not. The urge to discriminate is real and needs to be addressed as a far more pressing matter than if people are being discriminated against over "real" things like Downs Syndrome or "unreal" things like discriminating against people with CAH because they don't fit into one of two neat boxes regard "biological sex."

                Originally posted by Darksol-aeternium View Post
                It’s probably liberating to think that nothing in life is simple and everything has its own layered complexity, until you realize that those two things strip people of the things they actually got into this whole civilization for; stability and security.

That complexity allows for specialized members of society, or bureaucracy and cross-specialty cooperation to do more than we could when we lived in smaller communities where everyone had to contribute to everything. If you want police or a standing military for security, you need complex logistics to support having people that don't actively do things like grow crops, etc.

                Once it became more than just man/woman or gay/straight you immediately lost all the simpler people who make up the general population and who don’t want to have to spend additional effort trying to understand the identity politics of each and every individual they talk to.
                1) It's always been more than man/woman and gay/straight. Your selling humans short a lot here. One of the reasons the US political system is so screwed up is that living in a low population rural area vs. living in a dense population urban area literally impacts people's brains and how they relate to other people. When you grow up in a dense population with high diversity, your brain turns on a bunch of pathways to navigate that more easily. If you grow up in a low populate and more homogeneous environment, your brain puts its energy to other things.

                2) The idea that people have to "understand the identity politics of each and every individual they talk to," is pretty BS. First, that's natural human instinct as social creatures anyway. Second, for the most part people don't expect random strangers to know or have to know all of that. People just want basic empathy and decency. I am in the ~20% of my country that isn't a member of the dominant religion. I don't expect every Christian in the US to be a religious studies major, but they could be a lot more empathetic that 1 out of every 5 people they interact with isn't going to be Christian, and doesn't want to be treated like a Christian until they have to say something. Just remembering that not everyone is the same as you, and not assuming that someone's the same as you when you talk to them is all people are looking for from their fellow citizens. PhDs and all that is for politicians and so on to have real debates about policy and law; and yes expertise should be part of all of that.

                Can’t wait to see how civilization collapses under all this bullshit this time.

                    If I can offer a bit related commentary:

                    I live in Colorado Springs, the place where Focus on the Family is headquartered, and has for the better part of my two decades has been conservative, complete with the religious types. I'm pre-op trans genderfluid and tall enough to be a basketball players, so when I go out presenting as femme, in the world of people who fear about out-of-control-wokeness, you'd imagine people would be tripping over themselves trying to make sure they properly understand at a glance that I am pre-op trans genderfluid, and also instantly know what gender I feel like regardless of what my presentation is, and what my sexuality and kink labels are.

                    It's not what I want and it's not what happens. The grand majority of people I meet either default to ma'am/miss/she/her or they/them and I run with it (because those are accurate). Some people will say sir and I get stung but otherwise keep moving on. A few, more people than I would have expected, ask me how I like to be addressed. If anyone has felt trapped in trying to address me, the quickness of response that is normal has not relayed that fact to me.

                    The one person I can think of who came close to it asked me if it was okay if they called me a guy because they had just asked me to explain it to them because they had never heard of this before, and they were a grandma well up in their years. I told her sure, I wasn't expecting her to get it right, with the usual note to myself that I didn't expect her to like it at all. She never once failed to refer to me as a woman.

                    This "labyrinth of identities" thing that people get into is a farce brought up to feed the discomfort and unease people feel with something that is new or otherwise unfamiliar in an attempt to alienate the majority from the people who benefit the most it's implementation. The outraged non-typical gendered individual is likewise a farce for the same purpose-most people without a binary gender identity are honestly more afraid of standing up for who they are, because doing so has historically been an invitation for violence in a number of different forms.

                    Gender identity isn't going to destroy civilization. Gender identity is flotsam on the actual ocean of things going on that are simply complicated in some form, nevermind actually harmful to civilization. That's why a lot of people want you to focus on it, on all side of the argument-it's for the most part superficial, and the making of it a major issue says a lot about how the actual problems are and are not is going to be handled.

                    Capitalism destroying natural resources, including the people working within capitalism, faster than we can restore them, and paligenetic ultranationalism virulently attacking others within and without the societies leaning into it are the things that going to destroy civilization.
