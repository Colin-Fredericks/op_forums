                                S5E1-2 - The Cutie Map (It seems to be called Cutie Markless on some sites, but The Cutie Map is the name in the videos and on Wikipedia, so I'm going with that.)

                                In my comments on the conclusion of the season four finale, I predicted glory days ahead for bronies. Season four had, in my opinion, shown that the post-Faust FiM was not only able to match but in some cases outdo what had come before. Going into this season five premier, I was a little bit worried. Would I have to eat my words?

                                The answer is, not yet, that's for damn sure.

                                The Cutie Map is a very interesting episode. Compared to many of the previous openers, it's very low-key. Forget about saving the world from eternal night, or primordial chaos, or a whole kingdom's enslavement to a shade-form master of mind magic (and stairs). This time, the stakes are a one-street town, literally on the end of the tracks, with a population of, what, 100 ponies, maybe? The villain they face is no world-ending monstrosity, but simply a pony who use her magic for no other (stated) purpose than wanting to feel like the biggest kid in the school yard.

                                The setting is decidedly unconventional, and not only that, but also the mane six's role in it. In all the aforementioned openers, the Elements have been the focus of the show. They (and sometimes Spike) have been the heroes that save the day, the ones to be lauded and honoured with stained glass windows and crystal statues. This time, there is no such grandeur. Half-way through the episode, they are reduced to the same level as everyone else, and they even get their powers back significantly later than every other no-name pony in the village did. Instead, their effects are much more abstract. The mane six represents an alternative to a societal norm, which doesn't have much at all to do with shooting rainbow lasers. Their influence manifests through providing perspectives, and it lets the themes of the episode be so much stronger than it would have been if said rainbow laser had been in commission.

                                And what themes we are talking about, too. Equality, uniformity and individuality and the ordering, liberating and disrupting effects of both all to some extent are discussed in the episode. Sure, it's evident that one side is clearly represented by a villain, but on the other hand, through Fluttershy, one of the protagonists shows that parts of the doctrine of uniformity exists in her as well, which I found absolutely fascinating (not to mention super clean character writing). If they will indeed spend a few more episodes in the village, as the ending scene kind of implied, I would love to see an aftermath scenario where the village ponies learn to embrace their re-won talents once more, and possibly incorporate any beneficial parts of Starlight Shimmer's doctrine that they may have learned, to show that people grow through their experiences and come out stronger through plurality and diversity. If I dare dream, that is.

                                This episode doesn't overload my senses with awesome like Twilight's Kingdom. It's hard to say it's perfect, full points, tenouttaten. Instead, and that's much more interesting, it rather demands other points of comparison. If this is a taste of the direction the show is going in, then I can see a super interesting, but probably also polarising, time as we move ahead in season five.

                                For now, though, I stand by my assessment from last year. Glory days, bronies. Glory days.

                                E1

                                New season! Yay! I can't wait to see how OH WOW isn't that a convenient magical map plot device that was just summoned now for no apparent reason and is immediately accepted without question?

                                In this scene: Spike is a boy.

                                MY LITTLE PONY *sings* New shot of the castle in the background. Product placement never fails to deliver.

                                Anyone else getting a slightly familiar feeling about this episode? The heroes are given a quest through convenient means, they go where they are told and, upon arriving, expect danger and decide to investigate by chatting with the locals? Is this actually just a session log from McCarthy's D&D game?

                                Twilight Sparkle, Sunset Shimmer, Starlight Glimmer... they like that theme, huh.

                                Speaking of theme, In Our Town is a really effective song for getting the theme of the episode across. It's actually a pretty interesting way of contrasting equality and uniformity, which are important concepts in society today. I like where this is going.

                                I mean, it's slightly lost on account of Starlight Glimmer obviously being a villain. But still.

                                "No wonder nopony's wearing anything!" - Rarity 2015

                                Sugar Belle is cute and confused. Unfortunate when your ideology doesn't line up with observed facts, isn't it?

                                Poor Pinkie. The baker's worst torture.

                                The Cutie Mark Vault: the world's biggest iPhone.

                                When Starlight Glimmer is talking about the great mage Metalbrook (is that right?), there's a second when Twilight looks away and her eyes dart a little bit. I love those frames. It's evident that she's searching her memory for something, but they could have done that with her just looking away. The eye flicker is such a beautiful detail.

                                Wait, it just struck me. Didn't Tirek remove the Cutie Marks when he stole all the magic? Twilight should have known that, right?

                                E2

                                So Fluttershy is a pretty interesting border case. The non-confrontationalism that the town represents kind of plays into her personality, and it ultimately does represent a form of kindness. I like that one of the mane six seems to gravitate slightly towards it, as it gives it a bit more ambiguity. Here's wondering is Flutters can take the heat of being undercover, though.

                                Apathy Pinkie is awesome. That is all.

                                Bottled Cutie Marks. Buy 'em in six-packs. It'll be the new coke.

                                Wow, Starlight. One pony with a Cutie Mark would destroy your entire philosophy? That's not a very rigid ideological framework you got there.

                                Woah. Action Fluttershy there. Damn, girl.

                                Starlight is so in for it. Good lord.

                                The last part of the episode is frankly great. I love that they're putting in the villagers as the active parties. By keeping the mane six's influence on the ideological plane, they don't come off as the big saviours as much. It also relates that the only ones who can break a societal norm are ultimately the people of the society.
