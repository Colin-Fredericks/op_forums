                          Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                          The "Metaverse" is one of those things that will eventually happen, Zuckerberg is just trying to push it way to soon and the world is not in a position where enough people are ready to jump in and make it a reality right now.
                          I disagree, but we have first to understand that the Metaverse isn't actually a coherent concept, but a lot of stuff wrapped in an idea of immersive experience.

                          That said, I don't think most of his Metaverse talk is BS because those things won't happen or won't happen so soon. I think it is BS because most of the aspects of the Metaverse are already a reality or are becoming reality fast, and are already starting to interact. 95% of it is already how our lives are starting to look like, he's just adding purple prose to quite prosaic prognostics.

                          Now, his true pièce de résistance is the VR as a nexus point of all those aspects of computing, economics, etc. And in that the Metaverse will never be the huge thing he says it will be, but not for any technological reason.

                          Bluntly put, immersive experiences are inconvenient, and the whole point of VR is being immersive. Immersion has a heavy demand on shutting down your perception of your surroundings, if not by design, by virtue of your own brain. How many times didn't you got so distracted with something that the whole world could have blown up that you wouldn't notice?

                          Having access to this level of immersion is certainly nice, and his predictions on when it will be possible to that degree are probably about right, I don't doubt that. But how frequently in our lives we really can afford or even want that level of immersion? Especially when it starts to be immersion on mundane experiences. One thing is to be fully on the movie for 2 to 3 hours on a Saturday night. Another is to be fully on your work 6 to 10 hours per day, 5 or 6 days per week. That's a nightmare, not a dream! Even entertainment, there are times when not being fully immersed is what makes an experience safe enough, curious enough, or even possible (as I certainly can't be fully immersed in a game during work, but I certainly can play one if I'm careful about it).

                          Augmented Reality is far more reasonable for a Metaverse-like situation than VR, as it doesn't have to rob you from experiencing your surrounding and other things, but that has its own limitations to the experiences that can be created.

                                  Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                                  I just hope that corporations using this business model won't somehow monopolize VR technology.
                                  They already don't. There's a lot of small companies and individuals doing stuff for VR, and gate-keeping access to this technology for developers isn't just hard, but also would make the goal of widespread adoption harder to achieve. The last decades demonstrated how important it is that you get independent developers onboard with your project instead of isolated from it.

                                  Remember, even Nintendo found out that opening the Switch for Indies is a good idea.

                                  But that said, when we talk about branding it is not about monopolizing the technology in any way. It is about monopolizing public interest and brand property. While Nike will hardly have, lets say, monopoly over the ability to make high quality virtual shoes, it will keep their trademark over their logo. On top of that, if the shoes are detailed enough they certainly count for IP laws, which means a Nike brand shoe can't be copied even without the logo. While it still mean the same protection applies for any content creator, what they would aim is for the notion that the only way to get that specific model, with or without the logo, is from Nike. The risk is increased by the fact that those companies already have their entire catalogs of models to use, including old models they can't risk producing for real anymore, but can offer digitally at no cost, and the fact the very medium makes finding out falsifications and illegal copies a lot easier.

                                  If that makes you feel better, though, such arrangements already exist and aren't so ugly (didn't I say that most things he's saying are far less impressive or new than he implies?). A lot of games and simulators, for example, deal with such things regularly. They break cheap IP deals with corporations to offer the virtual version of their products to the players inside the game. It is specially ubiquitous with cars and other vehicles. They are IPs, the developers did broke deals with the real companies, and a lot of them are micro-transactions in those games. Even emoji packs are already a thing, and they follow more or less the same logic as in-game purchases.

                                  So take it as it already happen as a reference. It is unlikely that you'll be without options if you don't want a brand shirt or something, and it is equally unlikely that it will cost as much as it does IRL. Whatever form it takes, it will derive far more from how people interact with the idea and the specific game or social media than from some sort of technological monopoly.

                                  NFTs are another matter entirely, and while people are saying a lot about them in tandem with the Metaverse, we need to understand what they are, before we think about what they can become. An NFT is basically a signed copy of a given digital file that can either transfer the signature or not when it is copied, but never have two copies with the signature. The purpose is to validate the file as the "original", but keep the possibility to transfer this original through any means. It also keeps a register of who's the owner of its current iteration, but that's not theft proof. There are several other fine details, but that's the gist of it.

                                  What an NFT isn't? First, the file itself isn't impossible to copy, it may not have any hinder to copy it at all, it just won't transfer the validation. It isn't of better quality or otherwise unique outside the inner signature itself. There are some stuff you can do inside the signature (a blockchain validation chain), but nothing that affects the file itself in any way, shape or form.

                                  At the end of the day, the only thing an NFT does is to mark a given copy of the file as the original for whatever it's worth. Its current notoriety as an investment asset is just due to abstract scarcity and perceived value. It literally isn't any different from an authentic painting or manuscript: it isn't the only or even better way to experience the information withing, it is just "the real deal". For investments it does matter, at least to a degree. For a digital market it may have some uses, but it is unlikely to affect the general market except as a better validation feature against illegal copies.
