                Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                That is true that technology has been a boon for conspiracy theories, bad science, bad faith criticism, and amping up the worst elements of society.
                Has it, though?

                I'm not saying it isn't a problem that has to be addressed, but as Heavy Arms said those are fairly old problems. And I'm not sure if social media really makes as big a difference as it seems at first.

                Truth is, a lot of those have always circled around quietly, but quietly doesn't mean weakly at all. Search about what kind of things people believed 30, 50, 70 years ago, and how spread the crazier shit was, and you'll see our past isn't any pristine era of enlightenment and accuracy.

                Mass media had similar problems with another spin. Sure, overall they curated things and left a lot of heavy shit out of reach, but they also had their own lies and semi truths to spread, and no one could compete with their attention to try and set the record straight.

                Social media sped up the life cycle of rumors and corrections, and laid it bare for everyone to see, but didn't create and it's hard to measure how better or worse it was when more subtle. In the end ignorance has always been widespread, it was just hidden before.

                Personally I think we should demand more political action in this regard. Modern tech can propagate problems, but also solutions. What it won't do by itself is force the companies that control it to work for a better society. We can't keep going relying on corps to limit damage out of the goodness of their hearts.

                On Nintendo: what the leaks revealed is that Pokemon was getting less than 10% of the budget you'd expect from a franchise of its size. While there are no hard numbers, games AAA usually cost hundreds of millions to make, sometimes past half a billion, just to keep up with expected quality. Yet Pokémon has drawn suspicion and criticism for slow improvement for quite sometime.

                Now we have the hard numbers. Scarlet/Violet, the most recent entry in the franchise that sold over 28 million copies, had a budget around 20 million dollars, less than a dollar per copy. This isn't a crime, but is an eye opener on how much they rely on brand and why they're so bullish with even remotely perceived competition.

                Well, with Switch 2 being officially leasing rather than sold, anyway, I'm out of this train for good one way or another, but it's a shame this got this far and I hope for whoever sticks to the next gen that it will be worth the indignity.
