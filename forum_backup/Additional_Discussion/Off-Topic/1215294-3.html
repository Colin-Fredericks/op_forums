                                        I like Comics Thanos, he's one of my favorites. Movie Thanos is different and, while I prefer the comic version, I did like him too. Besides, Ultron is another of those I love, perhaps even more than Thanos (though Doom gets the top spot), so I've survived worse translations. I do agree that his motivation in the comics flows with less issues, but I think there's enough in-universe explanations behind how Movie Thanos acted the way he did

                                        To offer some speculation, based on me trying to get into Thanos' head:

                                        Thanos, in his twisted perspective, has no intention to provide infinite resources because that would require the one with all the Infinity Stones to stay around and shower people with food, materials and fuel, to keep things in check and become the benevolent god of a thankful universe. He does not want that: Movie Thanos wants to solve the Big Problem that torments him, willing to sacrifice everything in order to do that, be sure that the universe gains room to breath for a bunch of eras and retire, not giving a single fuck about being a space overlord, the hero of our dimension or anything like that. He wants to do that, retire and rest. Even die in due time. That's why he says he hoped Gamora would sit on his throne one day. Movie Thanos' is a different kind of egomaniac from Comic Thanos, in the sense he still believes that what he wants takes precedence over anything else, but in his mind the reasons to do so are less focused on him and more on a terrible greater good.

                                        We can speculate that those half-planet murders he did were his way to reach the goal before he learned about the Infinity Stones, or maybe something he kept doing because planets where dying out there and he had to step in and solve the issue, Stones or not. The armies, the tortures, the wars and all that stuff were means to an end.

                                        Now, he does this by killing, destroying, breaking minds and committing genocides on a cosmic scale, so I think we can all agree that's he's not a good guy, but I don't think he considers himself one either. Movie Thanos probably thinks of himself of the one who's strong enough both to recognize there's a fundamental flaw in the universe and to take on the weight of the feats and hard moral choices that are required to solve it.

                                        But I do believe his characterization implies that an "unnatural" solution such as becoming a neverending wellspring of resources would look to him as a naive way to solve things that might lead to disasters , conflicts and that would fall apart without someone taking care of everything all the time with the Infinity Stones. And Movie Thanos is tired

                                        Having said that, I'm not gonna deny there's some narrative weakness in place and the MCU Mad Titan has irrationally tunnel-visioned himself into becoming the most dreaded being of the universe. That's why I said I hope we'll learn more about him in the next movie (in the other thread), because he's a cool antagonist that deserves better.
