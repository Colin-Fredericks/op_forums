                            Originally posted by JimB View Post
                            Remember the Mario movie? Critics mostly didn't like it, and I don't either. I personally find it boring, shallow, and too full of meaningless "lol i know taht gaem" moments that feel nakedly cynical.
                            This is one of the difficult paradoxes of the children's movie industry. For things like traditional TV and streaming, children's entertainment needs to pass the, "parents pay enough attention to be OK with their kids watching it while they go do other parent things," test. There's minimal expectation that parents are going to sit down and actually watch; even if the parent is in the same room the whole time.

                            A theatrical release doesn't afford parents that luxury. They've got to sit there and watch it too. Ideally, a great children's movie layers in stuff for parents to enjoy that's seamlessly woven into the film... but that's really hard to do. That's why the movies that pull it off are the "greats." If a movie can't interweave stuff for parents into the story, they're going to prioritize what appeal to kids as the primary audience. That's how you get stuff like making the game, "spot the Easter eggs from your own childhood" stuff from the Mario movie. They're hoping parents can at least engage with that if they can't get into the movie, so they're not completely bored (obviously not necessarily so successful in that example) while indulging the kids.


                            Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                            You way you describe it is capitalist mediocrity incarnate.
                            It's... Disney. That's my bar for Disney. That's what I think about the '89 Little Mermaid too. Disney is a financial juggernaut built on capitalist mediocrity. They take public domain stories, sanitize them of anything meaningful, make it bright and colorful with catchy songs, and then rake in cash because kids love it, and parents cave into it.

                            I guess the big disconnect here, is that I was never expecting The Little Mermaid to be anything more than this. There was nothing out there saying, "hey this is one of the exceptions where Disney breaks the formula and did something more." I'm cool with Disney making the merfolk multiracial and having minorities as main characters, but I know it's because their marketing department says that's in, not because Disney is a champion of multiracial liberal democracy and is trying to take a political stance about it.

                            But I also don't like people bashing stuff solely on the grounds that they're not the target audience.. I'm a SFF and horror geek from back when that was extremely uncool. One time a group of kids snatched my copy of the Fellowship of the Ring, ripped off the cover, and stuck it to my hair with duct tape and chewing gum. And those same kids grew up to complain about how wrong it was to turn The Hobbit into three movies because it would never live up to the LotR trilogy, and then try to cherry-pick quotes from Tolkien about what Hobbits look like to hate on the Rings of Power having a more diverse cast.

                            Within some social limits, I want to be left alone to enjoy what I enjoy, and not watch what I don't want to watch. And I feel that means I should be held to the same standards. I don't know if The Little Mermaid is a movie I'd like or not, and I'm probably never going to know because I doubt I'm ever going to spend the time to watch it. But other people are watching it, and loving it, and more power to them. It might be corporate drivel, but it's also entertainment that's doing its job of entertaining people. That's good enough for me to not like people trying to justify not liking it beyond personal preferences.

                            I think a big reason is changes in visual quality to create spectacle.

                            In the 80s and early 90s... TV animation was garbage. The Disney renaissance films had animation that blew the Saturday morning cartoons out of the water. Animation that's still high quality today. That said, the "Saturday morning" style shows done with traditional animation are on that level of quality now too. A kid that's grown up with 2010s animation shows isn't going to see animated movies from the 90s as being massive leaps in visual appeal...

Whatever flaws I can find in The Little Mermaid if I watch it, I can guarantee it's still orders of magnitude better visually than most of the CGI stuff spoon fed to kids right now.
