                                Maybe a bardic "lich" (untied from alignment restrictions) would need to continue performing old works for new people, or keep making new works, in order to stay "alive". So you either get someone who wanders the world, performing for new audiences and eventually returning to old places after a generation or two, or you get someone who can stay in one place so long as they constantly churn out new examples of their craft. With either path, you get a pretty different flavor to the classic lich huddled in a tower or castle away from the world except when they need to "eat" or directly act against someone else, so it might fit the flavor of the bard class better anyway.

                                Also, since there's probably no phylactery (or at least not in the same sense as a wizardly lich), you might want to think of some other way to permanently destroy one, and also what if anything keeps them coming back to "life" if someone destroys their body.
                                Maybe they need to be stopped from creating or performing anything for long enough for their works to stop being spread, and basically "starve" them? Though that sounds more like a way to make their version of a demilich...
                                Or maybe they transform into a bardic lich through a true magnum opus, and destroying any significant traces of that (in both memory and ink) is what allows them to actually properly die? That'd be a hell of a lot of work, but... maybe that ought to be the case, if you want to take the angle of their strain of magic turning them into "living" works of art?
                                Orrr you take a more "fairy-tale-ish" approach and either trick, coerce, or convince them to make another great work of art that depicts their own death, and the magic that transformed them and sustains them is what also puts them down.
                                There's um, kind of a lot you can do with the base idea.

                                            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                            How does everybody feel about the idea of a Bard becoming a Lich?
                                            Some years ago I was in a Pathfinder campaign and one of the major reoccurring antagonists (and an ally for a little while) was bardic lich (or more accurately a bardic Worm That Walks). She had been a courtesan and follower of Callistra and had been killed by an abusive client. She came back to life as an effectively immortal swarm of wasps that could disguise itself as a normal humanoid. She took revenge on her on her killers and then continued to kill people who would abuse courtesans and prostitutes, though she eventually went too far and started killing even innocent people and anyone who would get in her way.

                                            But I think a more traditional lich would certainly be possible. Most spell casters seek lichdom because they are afraid of death or because they have things they wish to do and don't want death to stop them. A bard who decided to become a lich because he refuses to allow death to stop him in his quest to amass stories or whatever seems reasonable. And of course there is always the classic "court manipulator" type of bard who might have become undead so that he can secretly play the puppetmaster in a kingdom for centuries.
