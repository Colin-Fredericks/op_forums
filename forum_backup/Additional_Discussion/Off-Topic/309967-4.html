                                    Why, exactly is in economically infeasible (why it is politically so is pretty obvious)?

                                    An underfunded lower and middle class is a massive drain on economic growth, thus increases inflation. A highly disparity in wealth such as exists in the USA increases money supply (aka more wealth concentrated in the highest brackets is generally linked to that money being put in the money supply instead of being spent); which also increases inflation.

                                    So what is economically infeasible about redistributing - through taxes - in the fashion Odd_Canuck describes? It should actually decrease inflation, not increase it, since it would draw the rate of economic growth and the rate of the growth of a money supply closer together, and therefore suppress inflation.

                                    Inflation is a bit of a boogeyman in all this anyway. Most economists agree that a low level of inflation is actually a good thing, and most developed countries have a multitude of ways to adjust inflation rates through policy to maintain the level of inflation they feel is optimal.

                                    The whole economic argument for a social safety net is that it is actually better for the economy to keep people from falling into economic hardship through government assistance than it is to let people go solely with the whims of their personal situations.

So two parents with two children would be getting ~$12,000 on top of what they make working. Just consolidating everything like that, and paying zero attention to who actually needs that money and who doesn't, the amount we're already spending turns out to be rather significant sum as it is. And there would be savings regarding reducing the amount of bureaucracy to handle all of this (hey, small government socialist solutions!).
