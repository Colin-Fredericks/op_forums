                                Originally posted by SpectacularTentacular View Post
                                Hopefully, this is a start in getting protests that go beyond papier-mâché parodies of politicians and mindless sloganeering, and get to having a coherent agenda and message without a contingent of flakes that (of course) every news/cameraperson will focus on to the exclusion of the main issues.                                 Honestly, I think that part of the problem with so many of the protest movement's we've seen today, is that they don't give enough respect to the methods of the protest movements in the 60's and 70's and to a large degree reject those kind of methods as unworkable in the modern world. I feel that the "modern" leaderless-style of movements that we've seen in the Tea Party, Occupy Wall Street, and BLM have been rather ineffective.

                                Of those movements, the only one to be considered a resounding success is the Tea Party, largely because they ultimately chose leaders to represent themselves, came up with a coherent platform and decided to focus heavily on politics. In fact I would argue that the election of Donald Trump is, in some respects, the capstone of the Tea Party.

                                Meanwhile, Occupy Wall Street largely fizzled out and while Bernie Sanders had a bit of success in tapping into the residual embers, it wasn't enough to get him anywhere.

                                And BLM has been a very mixed bag, in large part because its lack of leadership prevents the movement from being able to disavow specific people, and simply ensures that the media will listen to whichever person is the loudest (and that person is very often not a good representative for the movement to the public at large). For many people, the murders of police officers by a couple of nuts and the riots in Baltimore are what they think of when they think of BLM.

                                I think what we've seen is that any kind of social movement, if you want to enact long-term change, needs to ultimately end up coalescing around a group who can lead it and speak for it. Otherwise you just have a mob of individuals running around doing their own thing. There's a lot of movement and action to be sure, but it's not unified action and a lot of it ends up being fairly self-destructive and wasteful. That's is what happened to Occupy Wall Street and what is happening to BLM.

                                So hopefully future protest movements look back to the 60's and 70's and realize that even in a world dominated by social media, there's still some great ideas about successful protest movements that should be taken out of that time period.
