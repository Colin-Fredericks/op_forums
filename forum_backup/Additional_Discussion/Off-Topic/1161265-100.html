                                                            Originally posted by PMárk View Post

                                                            I don't know, I didn't really have problems with the mundane classes.

                                                            Thing is, being a mundane character means you'd do... well... mundane things. Which doesn't need as much sub-systems as magic and there's not much logic making most mundane stuff class-only.

                                                            But hey, I wasn't a fan of the 4e design, so there's that. I don't feel, however, that the PF2 Rogue, or fighter, is underdeweloped, in comparison, to the Sorc, or Bard.
                                                            Whereas I see no reason to give some classes special treatment just because they happen to cast spells. A sorcerer or cleric gets class features, powers, feats, skill proficiencies and spells. A fighter or ranger just gets class features, skills and feats. Some of which are insultingly bad, like a +1 to hit once if you happen to critically succeed at recognizing a monster.

                                                            Every class and every character deserves to feel cool and useful. Why should some of them have to spend a feat on every remotely interesting thing they want to do? Look at the fighter sheet. He gets to charge better, raise his shield reflexively and take 10 on some Athletics checks. And the shield-raising feat competes for reactions with his only actual class feature and the shield's blocking property. The rogue's two skill feats strike me as the absolute minimum for any character, particularly a non-magical one. Those feats aren't even that good. Taking less falling damage and being better at hiding items... as long as no one is actively trying to find them on you. Glad we dodged that crossbow bolt. Can you imagine the game-breaking potential of hiding small items on your person more easily?

                                                            I'm still not sure why they made opportunity attacks a fighter feature, by the way. If they wanted fighter to be able to lock down enemies, why not give them better OAs? Or extra options for them?

                                                            Your point about "making most mundane stuff class-only" would be valid if there was "mundane stuff" to gate away, but there's not - PF's basic combat and skill model have very little room for improvising or creativity. Changing it would require changes that'd run into the brick wall of "That's not D&D!".

                                                            4e's class design has flaws, but it's miles ahead of any other edition of D&D because it doesn't show blatant favoritism and understands that you need to front-load features a bit, so a character starts out being able to do what their class is advertised to do. Which spellcasters sort of get in PF2e - a wizard has eight level 1 spells, no questions asked, plus cantrips and school abilities. A barbarian or monk are stuck with a slow drip-feed of content and waiting until level 7 before they get a respectable assortment of tools for their jobs. Once again, rogue's supposed mastery of skills strikes me more as the bare minimum of competence.
