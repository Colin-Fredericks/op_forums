    Well then. A new start.
    Better introduce myself this time around.
    Hi, I'm Soren. I'm one of those weird Scandinavians types, more precisely, a Dane.
    Now, a bit of background.
    I've been a part of this hobby since late '99, as that was when this young impressionable soul picked up a copy of "LA by Night" and "A World of Darkness 2nd" at the local library. After that, it was a swift decent into the rest of the WOD, and a few years later, I discovered that you could actually play with other people, and soon, my social circle consisted mainly of the other role-players near me. I first joined up on the White Wolf forums under the nick "Dalerik", way back in the days when we had all those jpeg images and there actually was a online community.
    After the Time of Judgement and the following reboot of the forums (I think mostly due to the bright colour-scheme), I left, and joined the Shadownessence fan forums as "Growls-at-the-Moon", which was my nick as well when I rarely visited the WW boards, but in some great shuffle, my account was lost, but not my registrered e-mail, so I was left lurking for nearly 6 years, never bothering to call tech support.
    My interest in the WW games gradually waned, as fewer and fewer people I knew would play the WW games, and most of them had picked up either D&D-clones or indie games. Then, I finally joined a game society who appriciated the WOD as much as I. Through them, I kept the OWoD/NWoD/Exalted flame alive in my heart, even as we sought new games.
    Cue V20, Onyx Path and the following products, and it lead me here, breathing in the spirit of the White Wolf.

    Aside from that?
    I'm a writer, having published a few articles and stories in my native language, I've been considering if my English vocabulary could be good enough for writing for a wider audience.
    I'm a LARPer, as it is actually bit of a national sport here in the DK, with thousands of kids having played boffer larps at one point or another. Personally, I'm a bit more into more serious affairs, as we actually have a fair share of WoD larps in Denmark, the oldest being a 15-year old Werewolf: The Apocalypse chronicle called "Rage Across Denmark".
    Also a bit of a medieval re-enactment buff, mostly due to the shiny swords and armours.

    I run three campaigns at the moment:
    - "After Winter Follows Spring" a chronicle about a noble family, set in the "Song of Ice & Fire"-universe, with intrigues, sex, honor and backstabbing.
    - "Silk & Steel", a Legend of the Five Rings campaign about a empire recovering from a civil war, with five young samurai making their way in the world.
    (I've actually put up a Actual Play thread of this up on RPG.Net, poke me for more info)
    - "Vampires of the Caribbean", A Vampire20 chronicle set in the british colony of Jamaica, in the year 1660, with the Sabbat and the Camarilla vying for control over the New World, with a trio of vampires having barely escaped the Inquisition, finds themselves shipwrecked and 150 years out of time, and worst of all, only to find the Jyhad has found them already.

    On the more boring side?
    I work for a Computer Stress Test company and does a bit of transcribing of medical journals for a local doctor. It pays the bills.

    __________________________________________________
    Preaching the enlightened gospels of Gaming Anarchy

        Hello, I'm Kismet, and I was introduced to tabletop roleplaying games through Vampire: the Masquerade in October of 1995 by the guy I'm privileged to be living with now.                                         I'm Ian.

                                        First got involved in... 1995, 1996? Somewhere around there, when some gamer friends introduced me to Vampire once we'd wrapped up an AD&D game. During a break in Planescape releases in 1996, I started snapping up rulebooks for all the game lines, with Vampire and Mage as my favourites to this day.

                                        In 1999 I got involved in the Wolf Pack demo team program, and shortly thereafter in 2000 I was one of the original ten Storytellers on the New Bremen moderated chats, spending a year as the Head Mage ST. It was in this guise that Jess Heinig approached me for my opinion on a number of items going into the Guide to the Traditions, making that my first RPG credit (albeit as "additional contributions"). I also helped jumpstart a number of the other chats, including the Trinity/Aberrant chat, Fangsfall (Scarred Lands), Rookhausen (Ravenloft), and Jade City (Exalted).

                                        In 2001 I started fansite Wolfspoor, which started off as a WW news and rumour site, but later evolved into just a host for my numerous avatars and icons. In 2002/2003, I got together a bunch of fans to make a Trinity Storytellers Handbook. The project was never completed, but I'm really proud of the work that was done.

                                        In 2004 I got my first published work in Scarred Lands' Edge of Infinity: The Scarred Planes. I also started up the Unofficial White Wolf Wiki, which continues to this day.

                                        Since then I've had my finger in a bunch of pies. Graphics, chat site coding, videos, and so on. In early 2012, Rich asked me to be lead developer on the new Trinity Continuum. Later, after asking my opinion on what sort of thing should appear on the new Onyx Path website, given my experience building and running fansites, and my awareness of the requirements on a business end as well as the fan side of things, Eddy uttered the fatal words: "why don't we just get Ian to do the website?"

                                        That takes us to today, I think.

                                        Ian A. A. Watson(he/she)
                                        Onyx Path Community Manager
                                        Trinity Continuum Content Lead
