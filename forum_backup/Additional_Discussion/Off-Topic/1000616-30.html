    Originally posted by Paradim View Post

    For the intelligence agencies to take a stance that Russia is responsible... That takes them having access to convincing evidence of that. So, it's possible someone else is responsible, but if so, then it would probably be a case of them deliberately trying to frame Russia.

    And that starts to get a bit convoluted...
    Publicly available information ties the DNC hack to "Fancy Bear", a known Russian hacking group. There's little doubt that it was Russian hackers.

    However, that doesn't necessarily mean the Kremlin ordered this - it could have been private hacktivists trying to influence the election. That said, the Kremlin does have a history of supporting citizen groups, such as Nashi, who engage in these kinds of acts with the government's funding and tacit blessing. But then, if the Russian government supports a private organization that commits an act of cyberwar, who do you hold responsible?

    I don't know what the CIA knows. If they've gotten hold of intelligence that explicitly links Fancy Bear to any Russian official, I'd call that solid enough proof. But even if that were the case, I doubt that anyone in Russia will face legal consequences (from their own government) for this.

                            To be honest, I've never understood the panic over Trump. He's basically a Reagan Democrat, who ran on a variant of the Democratic platform from the Nineties.

                            For example, the whole "deportation" issue. I followed the Republican primaries this year, and dug into the various candidate's position papers. Under his proposal, anyone kicked out by the Trump Administration will immediately move to the front of the line for the next batch of immigration slots. Inconvenient, perhaps, but hardly a "the brownshirts are coming to get you" moment.

                            Or gay rights. Even back during the primaries, Trump was very pro-LGBT, making a point of posing with the rainbow flag, having gay speakers introduce him, criticizing the bathroom bills . . . And since the election, he's come out in support of gay marriage. I'm not really sure what else people want him to do, but my Facebook feed has been full of people freaking out about Trump and LGBT rights for weeks now.

                            So I really don't understand the collective freak out* so many Democrats have been having. Democratic pundits have been saying for years that the Republicans will have to moderate (i.e. become more like Democrats), and, well, they have. Pro-Social Security & universal healthcare, anti-Wall Street . . . Trump's probably the most moderate candidate the Republicans have fielded since, oh, the Rockefeller days. There's a reason he and Bill Clinton used to hang out, after all.



                            *now, if Sen. Ted Cruz had just been elected, then I could understand the panic, given just how conservative/right wing he is.
