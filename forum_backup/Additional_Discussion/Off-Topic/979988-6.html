                                                Economy absolutely can be managed as we do this every single day in the US. Socialism does not simply mean "economic planning", it means shared ownership. As for scarcity, I can easily make the argument that capitalism creates artificial scarcity and we can see in the US the results of the artificial scarcity caused by capitalism. So if both systems are causing scarcity how do we fix the problem and what do we replace the current inefficient systems with? Those seem to me to be the questions that need answering, and dogmatically clinging to the labels "conservative" and "liberal" we are picking sides like this is a sporting contest and we are rooting for a particular team. It prevents people from looking at solutions proffered by "the other side" because it means their particular team isn't "winning". The difference in attitudes between the time of my grandparents (WW2) and now is that people are more interested in climbing over other people than giving them a hand up. We've become a society of self interested, self absorbed narcissists who believe if it isn't a problem for me then it isn't a problem. As Carlin said "it's bullshit and it's bad for you".

                                                ​If the solution offered is "well poor people need to work longer and harder" then where does the spirituality figure in? Where is the value in human existence, where does the opportunity to further grow and develop enter into the equation? When do they get to live their lives instead of working to make someone else wealthier while they settle for whatever crumbs are allowed to trickle down their way?

                                                When do people who are exhausted from working their second and third jobs get to expand their educational development, and how do they afford an (overpriced) education when they are making choices about whether to pay rent, their utilities, or buy enough food so as not to starve (and that won't cause them health issues down the road)? I look at people now and wonder how they can afford the higher education that will afford a better quality of life, I was able to work two jobs and graduate college in the 90's without a mountain of debt and that just can't be done today with the cost of tuition, even at the state school levels.

                                                Once these issues are addressed you will have a better, fairer, more stable society. And I for one don't give a fuck what system gets us there as long as we get there.
