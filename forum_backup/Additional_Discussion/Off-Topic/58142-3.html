    I dislike most 'queer-oriented hangouts' because they're 'queer-oriented'. I have no problem with the idea of a place where you can feel comfortable and safe being yourself but too often it actually is about 'being queer'. I've been weighing the idea of coming out to my family, but as you might expect, that makes me very nervous. It was a bit of a struggle just bringing up these feelings with a distant friend, and I more or less knew she would be accepting and kind about it. Still, I feel a strong urge to get it out in the open. And I don't think telling my family would prompt any horrible backlash - no screaming, no disowning me, no cutting me off - but I do worry about the idea of being loved less, of being viewed as an embarrassment or tactfully ignored. ("Let's not talk about Cielle's situation.")

                            Any advice for how to tell my family I'm trans? Or about how you came out, or about coming out in general? I was planning a soft approach, and talking to one parent to start with - I've heard that being outnumbered is a recipe for problems - but I think you guys have more experience than me.
                            Cielle,

                            While I can't speak to the nature of coming out (I'm straight), I will speak as a parent. My wife and I have both talked about this in relation to our children (A 9-yo girl, 6-yo boy -- "physically" that is...to be determined by them as life moves on...they are who they decide they are after all...), and we have both decided that to us as long as our children are happy, then we're cool. We, as parents are doing our best to keep our "lines of communication open" (that's "olde foaggie" for talking ) them and not at them. I don't know you're family, but maybe sitting down and just talking with them might be the best? Or...maybe some sort of surprise for them (A Cake w/message, a Painting or sorts, etc??)?? You know them best, but I've always believed that being up front and honest is the best way to do things.

                            Anyway, good luck, and know that there are plenty of people out here (Of all types) who support you.

                                                        Originally posted by Loxosceles
                                                        It is a subject that I would be very interested in seeing opinions, anecdotes and ideas regarding.
                                                        I do not bring up the topic of sexuality in front of real people (as opposed to internet people; I can talk about pretty much anything with someone I'm incapable of looking in the eye). I'm uncomfortable with it. I don't bring up sexual orientation either, because even though it's not the same thing as sexuality, it's close enough that I don't want to open the door for a conversation about the sex people like to have. There are only maybe three real people in the world I trust and am comfortable enough with to feel no shame or panic at learning details of their sex lives, so between that and the fact that I have no gaydar, what I can say is made of more caveats than actual statements.

                                                        For instance, almost everyone I have ever roleplayed with has been in a heterosexual relationship at the time. That in and of itself is not proof of heterosexuality, but of the maybe fifty roleplaying partners I've had, they've all been in a straight relationship. I only know of one person who, after I stopped gaming with her (and stopped talking to her entirely because the bitch owes me six hundred bucks she refuses to pay back, instead insisting that my best friend shoulder the debt, which he has agreed to for reasons I'll never understand), declared herself gay.

                                                        I'm a little surprised that, of all the people I'd played with, I only have proof of one of them being anything other than straight. This is particularly peculiar to me since I live in a town with two colleges and one university, so we pull in a lot of out-of-towners who are, if stereotypes are to be believed, in a stage where they experiment with their sexual orientations. I assume a big part of the issue is living in Missouri, where our state policies are pretty solidly homophobic, so gay or gay-curious people either don't come here or stay in the closet. I also assume that if those experimenting kids are around, they're too busy experimenting and/or going to school to come to play D&D with me and chat about their orientations.

                                                        As a perhaps final note about the roleplaying environment I live in, this area is, as I've said before several times in the past, Pathfinder country. That is the only game in town. No one wants to play anything else. I don't know what correlation Pathfinder has with alternate sexualities, if any; the only time I've ever seen the topic brought up is in a player resource for a published adventure path which included a romance mechanic for four important NPCs whom the GM were encouraged to treat as bisexual. Compared to a game like Exalted, whose new edition will have a trans person as a signature character, I treat the lack of discussion on the topic as an assumption that players and characters are all hetero.

                                                        I'd actually like to talk about Exalted's upcoming Prince Diamond in some more detail, but later. I got shit to do.
