                    Originally posted by Sorwen View Post
                    Even more so in this situation when they could be playing up Thor Girl.
                    Without any actual practical knowledge of marketing or anything like that, here's what I'm suspecting the rationale is:

                    If you create a female Thor-alike in her own title while Thor has his own, you're going to have some people who pick it up, absolutely. Primarily, you'll have the folks who would buy anything with a Thor logo on it, and you'll probably have some people wanting to support a female superhero.

                    On the other hand, if you replace Thor Odinson with a female Thor...well, yes, you're absolutely going to hemorrhage some readers who will be upset. With luck, good writing, and word-of-mouth, you can win most of them back. Meanwhile, you probably keep the majority of the audience of Thor as it was, and add the people wanting to support the female superhero, as above.

                    So the Thor completists and whatever miscellaneous audience won't be shelling out more money, but at the same time you're not having to pay the costs to write and illustrate a whole other series.

                    Meanwhile, you're building heat for this new character. When--not if, when--she ceases to be Thor and the Odinson takes his hammer back, assuming you did your job right, you have a pretty good idea of whether it's financial sense to spin her off into her own book, and can keep the new readership you gained from this storyline while you're at it..

                    EDIT: Also, by making this character the main star of an existing comic, you're simultaneously attacking a second barrier that going straight to spinoff would run headlong into: the idea that this is "Thor for Girls."

                                        Well, a few things come t mind:

                                        1) The Thor franchise already has most of those characters in use at some point (and it is heavily implied one of them is going to be the new Thor as it is). Pulling a "new" character from the same well as Thor in the 616 Marvel-verse is pretty much impossible at this point. There are going to be legacy/continuity holdovers regardless.

                                        2) This is pretty standard comic book industry stuff. Legacy character comic titles are generally where new characters and retooled characters get introduced before they get spun off into their own thing if they do well, or just get added to the title's existing cast of support characters.

                                        3) Marvel has done pretty well by not worrying about the comics and movies not lining up. This is hardly the first time there's been a major character thing happen in the comics that didn't mesh well at all with the movies.
