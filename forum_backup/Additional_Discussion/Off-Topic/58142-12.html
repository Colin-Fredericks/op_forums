                            For most of my childhood, I only really had female friends. I just didn't get along as well with the 'other' boys. In small ways, I had always considered myself more of one of the girls than one of the boys.

                            After my parents got divorced, and I started seeing more of my dad (since he wasn't always away, avoiding my egg-donor), he started having some conversations with my brother and I. These conversations were from the perspective that he hadn't spent as much time with us as he'd like to have, and he wanted to get to know us better.
                            I was 10:
                            Dad: "So, have you started to like any of the girls in your class yet?"
                            Me: "I've always liked the girls in a bunch of ways. And yeah, I'm starting to like some of them in 'other ways', now. But, it's odd for me, since I've pretty much always just been one of them. It's like I'm a lesbian with the wrong body."
                            My father, who's a bit of a 60's conservative type, did not react well. Fortunately, he just assumed I had an awkward way of saying things, and that he didn't understand what I had tried to say. Of course, I didn't understand at the time either.

                            Other than that, there really isn't any one 'ah-ha, that's what I am' moment for me to point out. With how conservative my father is, and how homophobic my egg-donor is, I spent most of my teenage years and you adulthood in denial, trying to convince myself I wasn't Trans. Depression has been a close frenemy of mine because of this.

                            As far as adjusting to it, some of the things that I used to maintain the denial were the preconceptions about trans*folk ('Trans equals Gay' being the main one, but also a lack of understanding about how much transition manages). So adjusting to it really just came down to information exposure in ways that chewed through my ability to maintain the denial. Since I've admitted it, I've started to be a lot more proactive in making my life better, haven't had anywhere near so many depressive episodes, and the ones I have had, it has been a lot easier to pull out of.
