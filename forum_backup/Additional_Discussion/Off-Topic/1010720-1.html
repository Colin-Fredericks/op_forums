                                1. Pregnancy is a life-threatening condition. Pregnancy can cause diabetes, lactose intolerance, septic infections, infertility, heart disease, acute organ failue, suicidal depression, internal bleeding, osteoporosis, and death. In 2015, the mortality rate of pregnancy in Brazil was 44 per 100,000. That's not a huge number, but I prefer to live in a world where all of those people knew the risks and chose to proceed anyway. One of the things that keeps that number low is access to abortion.

                                2. Legalizing abortion reduces the number of abortions performed. Bringing things out of the shadows allows people to evaluate their decisions calmly and rationally, and seek outside resources they might not have discovered before. It also dramatically reduced the number of deaths from botched abortions to practically zero.

                                3. 33% of all fertiziled human ova fail to make it to term anyway. Anthropogenic abortion is a drop in the bucket compared to the natural version.

                                4. The actual ethical basis of the pro-choice argument is called "bodily autonomy". Suppose for a moment that there is a genius kid out there who is on track to cure cancer and is suffering from acute kidney failure and you are the only match in the entire world. Even if they could do it with 100% success, no one has the right to hook you up to the kid and use your kidneys to keep them alive. And even if such an arrangement were made, you would be well within your rights to walk away and let him die. And once he was dead, no one could force his family to use his body to for science or any other purpose. His corpse is his own to dispense with as with all his other earthly affects.

                                So even if you accept the premise that a fertilized egg is a person, the anti-abortion stance is that a fetus has more rights than a man, and that a woman has fewer rights than a corpse.

                                                A lot of what I would start with as already been said, so I'm going to start up a very different side of this:

                                                The anti-abortion movement as it exists in my country (the US) and many similar movements in other countries are - while not purposefully so - an attempt to force majority religious values on my religion.

                                                See, my faith has a very long standing view on abortion that does not match the "personhood starts are conception, and abortion is murder," rhetoric. Supporting reproductive choice is also supporting members of my religion to practice our faith's teachings rather than another faith's teachings (since this is where the majority of anti-abortion stances are founded).

                                                One of the important aspects of this, is that even with my faith's views of personhood, we view abortion after fetal personhood as more nuanced than it is often treated. Once you accept the fetus is a person, you accept it has autonomy. Killing a fetus might be murder, but it might also be self-defense. That's actually the theological argument my faith falls on. An abortion after fetal personhood is acceptable if it follows our concept of self-defense. As well, my faith does not exactly view self defense as a choice. Your life is sacred, and you therefore have a religious obligation to protect it from others that through malice or ignorance would threaten it.

                                                So for women of my faith, if they've carried a child to the point we consider when a fetus develops personhood (which is later than conception), and she is of the valid belief that her child is posing a threat to her, she doesn't just have the right to defend herself, she has a moral obligation to do so. Obviously, it's never that simple, but that's something my faith teaches on this matter.

                                                So I come at the pro-choice side from a considerably different direction than most people. Pro-choice for me is not just a mater of bodily autonomy (though I do consider that important) but defending the right to have different moral considerations on abortion and choose according to those different beliefs.

                                                ---------

                                                On a separate note I want to push back against the, "the mother should ruin her life for the sake of one child," argument.

                                                Consider for a moment that a mother that chooses to not ruin her life for one child who would then have to grow up living a very rough life, might then go on to have multiple children when she's ready to provide a proper stable home for them. If we're thinking in terms of the theoretical potential of an unborn child, how do you weigh protecting the theoretical life of one child, to the theoretical lives of three other children the mother could have if she didn't have the first?

                                                It's a horrible choice to have to make of course. In a perfect world she could have all four children. But this world is not perfect. Once you start arguing for the potential life of a child, you open the room to discuss the potential life of every other child that mother might have. If you force a mother into a situation where having more kids later isn't feasible (or even possible) for her, you're saying having a pregnancy to term is actually more important than all those kids.

                                                And while I am phrasing this as a hypothetical, it's really not for me.
