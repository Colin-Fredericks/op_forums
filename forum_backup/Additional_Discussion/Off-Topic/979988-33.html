                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        @ElvesofZion

                                                        Wow, that is lot of different topics... but I'll try to touch on each. I am putting my responses in spoiler tags because they got kinda long.
                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        What are your thoughts on government regulation, particularly in light of the past (Big Tobacco, Big Lead, etc) as well as events in the present, like Animal Agriculture, the food industry with sugar, the recent issues with pharmaceutical companies, economic crises created by banks, and outright predatory organizations like payday loans


                                                        Some government regulation is reasonable in general. Although the method that such regulation is administered and enforced should be the subject of extreme scrutiny.

                                                        These examples all seem to have to do with consumer protection regulations, so I will stick to those for now. Generally speaking the act of purchasing is a contract between the purchaser and the seller, and I would say that it is the responsibility of the State (general term here, a discussion about regional vs. federal government is its own topic I think, although it does touch on all of these points as well) to ensure that contracts are upheld by both parties. The ‘Big’ part of those points mainly refers to the idea of them having a powerful lobbying arm, an are a that I would argue needs an extreme amount of reform. I don’t believe that substances should be banned for sale because it is my business if I want to buy lead paint, not the governments. But it is their job to ensure that I am not sold something that claims to be lead free but actually does contain lead.

                                                        I am very torn on the pharma/medical front, I tend to disagree with conservatives and may libertarians on it. I generally think that the right to live should likely be protected. But the actual implementation of it leaves a lot of issues for me. As I said above, it is my right to use lead-based paints if i want to, but is it then the government and thus the peoples responsibility to take care of me if i get sick from it? I also understand the argument (in theory) that private industry helps drive innovation and progress in the medical field.

                                                        Banking is also a very large topic, my general feeling is that the government should step away from banks, way more then the currently are. But that the focus on a contract driven society would help create a culture where people are more wary of bad options like payday loans and sub-prime morgage scams.

                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        How do you feel about Social Security?


                                                        The Social Security program is a nice idea but shows a fundamental problem with many socialist programs. They are very often mandatory by necessity. A safe (at least in theory, it has been tampered with) retirement account that any citizen can use is a very nice idea. The problem is that it is supposedly not a tax and yet it is mandatory, and a tax must be included in order to pay for the administration of the fund. An anecdote to help illuminate:. I have a good 401k with company matching, it would be much more financially profitable for me to take all of the money that goes to Social Security and put it there. But I cannot do so, because in order for Social Security to work it must be mandatory so there is enough money in the system from current workers to pay for the current retirees and admin the system.

                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        What are your thoughts on the increasingly automated workforce, and experts who claim a minimum standard of living will need to be implemented at some point?


                                                        Technological progress is an inevitability, it has always happened. The invention of the printing press removed the need for scribes, cars replaced horses, etc. People will find work or leisure will become work: the rise of e-sports for example. I am not scared of progress.

                                                        I am not familiar with any particular papers on the need for an automatic living wage kind of thing specifically as a result of increased automation in the workforce. If you happen to have references I will gladly read them and then comment on them.

                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        Do you believe that private companies should be free to regulate themselves? Is there a point where you feel the government needs to step in and protect the consumer?


                                                        I think my first point answered this for the most part, the idea of ‘regulation’ is thrown around as a buzz word by both ‘sides’ but I think the real question is in the deeper concepts of what a business is and what the government is for.

                                                        Originally posted by Solana View Post
                                                        If it turns out that you don't believe in regulation, how do you reconcile the promise that capitalism will produce a superior product, when evidence exists to the contrary? (Fast food is of inferior nutritional value, devices are built to break down quickly because you'll need to go out and buy another one, safety and craftsmanship are sidelined when you can make a bigger profit with cheap labor, which leads to exporting jobs (bad for the local economy)).Advertisements do not create informed consumers, they often shore up an inferior product.


                                                        To start with, what we have in the US is barely capitalism at all. The true center of capitalism really has nothing to do with promoting a better product, at least not directly. The central tenet of capitalism that I think tends to be ignored is that ‘a person deserves just compensation for their labors’, anything else is an addition to that core idea and some of those additions are mutually incompatible. Laissez faire capitalism does introduce the idea that a completely free market will ‘eventually’ (key word there) result in the ‘best’ (another important one) products through the selection by the people. The ‘eventual’ is important because that part of laissez faire capitalism assumes that ‘value’ will be determined by the people with their purchasing power, and that over time the items of the most value will get bought and others will not. Value is a perception however, and it is totally dependent on the people to make that judgement. Fast Food is subjectively ‘better’ because it is convenient, the nutritional value is a different aspect. One that you (and an increasing number of people) perceive to be more important than the convenience, but that was not the case for a very long time in the US. It is changing overtime though and that is what is expected from laissez faire capitalism , the best value, as perceived by the customer base, will eventually win out by being bought while the perceived lesser product will not.

                                                        As long as people are ok with the ‘inferior’ product because it is cheaper then it will continue to sell. That is the price of freedom in the market. People are free to make bad purchasing decisions.

                                                        All that said, I am not totally sold on                                                         laissez faire capitalism myself, as evidence by me saying that there are some regulations that I agree with. The core of capitalism, I do agree with. Every person that does work deserves fair compensation for that work, if that compensation isn't to be had then society has decided that the value of their work is nothing. It is not the governments place to force me to pay for things I don't value.


                                                        Everything here is pretty summed up, since it is already really long and there is a lot to discuss on each topic. I am also not a politician or an economist so they are more stances based on ideals than true plans of action.
