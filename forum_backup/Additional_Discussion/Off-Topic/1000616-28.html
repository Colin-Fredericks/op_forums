                                Originally posted by Axelgear View Post
                                Fixing voting is a necessity.
                                Yes, though one has to agree on what the problems are before you can even start to pick the best approach to fixing them.

                                Very few of the issues with the US voting system are binary things. Esp. since the US operates in a strange balance of each state being "equals" in power and the general public.

                                It makes solving future problems possible by shattering the bipartisan divide; by making the votes of a given state represent the state. It'd break the stranglehold the Democrats and Republicans have on the electoral system and end the problem of single issue voters.
                                Which fix exactly accomplishes all that? How is that fix at all practical? It sounds a lot less like a "fix" and a lot more like trying to amend the Constitution to completely alter the way elections are handled.

                                When you only have a binary option, extremes become the norm.
                                I don't think that's as true of the course of history as it needs to be to assert this. The US political system has gone through a number of waves of polarized extremes and pushes towards pragmatic centrism despite the Two Party approach. And while the politicians are hardly blameless, it comes down to the mood of the public. When the public is unified, moderation and bipartisanship rule the government. When the public is divided, ideological extremes are the norm.

                                Everyone fears that this time the polarization will be so bad there's no turning back... but the ebb and flow of history - just as it suggests that all countries rise and fall over time - tells us this isn't the case. It's just how much damage a period of polarization does before it ends.
