    Old news, I'm sure, but looks like there's a sequel to the Equestria Girls movie coming soon (that was fast!). Interesting promos they've been offering:






    Immediate observations: I note that there's no sign of Twilight, which could be good or bad. Does the movie start in the human world, then Twilight shows up midway through? Do the characters find the human Twilight hinted at in the first movie? Or is she just not in this one at all? Either way, it seems there may be even less Pony in this one than there was in the first...

    Also, I haven't seen Sunset Shimmer, which would be a bit harder to explain. Did she go back to Equestria? I hope they don't just lazily write her off as having "moved away", as that would cheat us of her character dev, and...she really has no where to move *to*. I'd rather see her return to Equestria and show up in the next season...

    It seems that Trixie is gonna be the main antagonist this time around! More Trixie is always welcome, even if it's humanized. If there is a Pony connection in the plot, maybe we'll even get to see original Trix in Equestria!

    Aside from that, this movie reminds me, yet again, of how most cartoons are made by guys much older than their target audience. Ever notice how, whenever cartoons have popular music characters, it's always "rock stars", who are almost always done up as Kiss parodies? Do kids today actually listen to "rock music"? Can any of the groups kids listen to today even be correctly classified as "rock"? What was the last kid's cartoon you saw that had a musician character perform something that actually seemed like something kids would really listen to?

                    This may sound weird, but I honestly didn't realize that he WAS handicapped, until I read some comments about it and rewatched! I don't recall what I DID think, but I think I thought that he was pulling something (like, it was a fancy cart); maybe it was because we'd seen wheelchairs before, and they didn't look anything like that! This design works much better for the equine form, tho. And yeah, his design was pretty over-the-top "cool", so maybe that was distracting me, too. Also nice that they let him be a bit of a jerk!

                    Given he's a pegasus, I imagine he flies most of the time, but needs the support when he must spend time on the ground, such as at a stall or in line. It'd probably make sense for it to be made of extremely lightweight materials and be easily collapsible so that he can take off quickly if need be...

                    Overall, I thought that was a good ensemble ep, tho the Rarity-Applejack plot was a little weak, but at least they didn't forget about Rarity representing "generosity". Rainbow trades Fluttershy away...so, slavery/indentured servitude is legal in Equestria?! Someone could trade their child away, so long as both traders agree it's fair?? Also, I don't always look for that sort of thing, but the shipping is strong with this one; certain fans must have had a field day!

                    One thing I really appreciated; I consider it a mark of lower standards in children's tv that mandates that the heroes must always "win". Sadly, the otherwise great early Harry Potter movies were afflicted with this, where we must see House Griffindor win against Slytherin at some contest every film (even if Dumbledore has to blatantly cheat to make it so!). And this season of Pony was slipping into that mode, with Rainbow implausible getting such a high score on her test, and even little stuff like the Mane Six finding something to connect with Maud Pie over. Remember the end of Season one, where NO ONE got what they really wanted (well, except for Celestia, I guess)?

                    So it's nice to see that we can still get an episode approaching that here, with the ponies at least not getting what they were after, even tho they end up with some pretty cool stuff nonetheless...

                    Also, is it me, or did the Scary Beasts Trader resemble a ponified Kratos?

                                                Whew. Weren't those a couple of intense weeks. Now when I have time to do anything else than study every waking minute of the day, time to catch up on some pony. I can't believe I've missed three more episodes since last time. Just some short comments on each this time.

                                                S4E21 - Testing Testing 1, 2, 3

                                                To summarize the epsiode, I liked it a lot. I could see what you meant by this episode giving a boot to the educational system, and I don't think it's just the American one, either; most school systems would use Twilight's methods as the base. And yeah, it's true that some people learn differently. Still, I can't help but feel that while they may have had to approach this episode this way to make it fit the show format (which it did, and it was great; 90s Pinkie in tha house, yo), I don't think the episode got to the bottom on the analogy it was making. And it certianly failed to problematize the representation of education that it gave, but hey, that's not its job either, so I'll let that slide.

                                                S4E22 - Trade Ya!

                                                This one was a bit hit-and-miss. The kind of comedy of complications that Rainbow Dash had usually doesn't appeal to me, and it was the weakest part of the episode up until the end. I did like Applejack's and Rarity's passive-aggressive feud though, and Pinkie had some great moments. I'll have to admit, Fluttershy had some sweet moments in here as well, which surprised me: I usually think she's among the weakest of the mane six. Towards the end, it kind of escalated a bit with Rainbow Dash selling Fluttershy into slavery, but at least something happened. I liked how an otherwise lightweight episode got at least a little dramatic, if in a very silly way, and how Twilight got to follow through on her duty. It was an okay episode.

                                                S4E23 - Inspiration Manifestation

                                                I love the sounds Rarity makes! And the portly stallion's voice acting is great as well. I'm not too keen on the premise; Rarity was never one to forgo the function of a dress for its design, or rather, she made them work together. I don't see why she's so distraught at the the critique when the puppetry wagon clearly doesn't live up to functional standards. The rest of the episode is frankly a bit too magical for me to take any lesson from very strongly. I suppose there are questions of consent and hard-lining an ideal, but for me, it mostly comes across as a much better variety of the super hero theme. Bit of a cop-out at the end, though, with the memory loss. By the way, wasn't there a comic about Rarity getting corrupted a bit ago? Seems to be a thing. :P

                                                S4E24 - Equestria Games

                                                Okay, on the one hand, that was a HILARIOUS episode. As I said above, I don't really like the comedy of complications and people embarrassing themselves, but damn it, it was too well paced not to be freaking awesome. Most of Spike's jokes were exactly spot on, and that anthem... I cried with laughter. Top notch. On the other hand, it was really inconsistent and sometimes downright dumb. At the end, there were four princesses present, but heaven forbid they actually do anything? Even the competitors are faster to react than the security squad. I realise it's a dumb complaint given the obvious slapstick comedy style of the episode, but sometimes I just lose it over how damn useless the godkings are. Pretty neat lesson at the end, though.
