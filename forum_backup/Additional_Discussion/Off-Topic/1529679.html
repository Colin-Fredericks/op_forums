                      I have read Changeling: The Dreaming, although the supplement was intended to actually have a different atmosphere than that one did. Changelings are more protagonists who maintain some of the world's magic, while Cherubs are supposed to be lost fools. That intent was actually reflected in the mechanics. Changelings can lose their Changeling powers, and this is for obvious reasons portrayed as a tragedy. Cherubs can become mortal, but this is supposed to be a very difficult thing to accomplish, and it is arguably a good thing. Also, I will admit that I have not thought about interactions with Beast. I will admit that I have not read much of it, but I can see that working. That would probably provide a neat alternative antagonist to Heroes.

                          General Notes
                          I can feel that similarities to Changeling: the Dreaming also, even if you're going in a very different direction. I had the same thoughts about there being lots of room for Beast crossovers via the dark dream but those are the parts of Beast that are the most confusingly written.

                          Something that hit me over and over were the fetish connections. Personally it made me pretty uncomfortable but I get it. It feels like the work either needs to more directly address the fetish connections, or lean away from it. By just sort of alluding the fetishes it leaves some things open to (i hope) misinterpretation where people who want to feel young (in a possibly sexy way) and people who seek out youth to abuse. If you want to keep the fetish connections I'd maybe lean into something like Cherubs wanting to protect innocence and children (though that might detract from the idea of them being lost and self-obsessed). Using youth as a general catchall also feels a bit off, there are lots of people drawn to youth or what to be younger but not in a childlike way.

                          Minor Editing Notes
                          I saw a few instances of 'Their, They're, There' being used incorrectly. Maybe word search each word and just check you used the right one.

                          I noticed you start sentences with 'Also' and 'But' a lot, you probably want to avoid that since it indicates the thought really should be part of the sentence proceeding it. My first pass at writing something has the same artifacts (I think our writing styles are very similar), but it's worth it to re-read and try to get rid of those. I usually find that I'm either being needlessly precise to the point it confuses things, or I let an idea get out of control and needs to be cut into clearer pieces.

                          Writing Notes
                          In Chapter 1 I noticed you really seem to be working hard to describe what a Cherub is on a technical level, and it gets in the way of describing what it means to be a Cherub and more importantly what it feels like to be a Cherub. It makes sense, I'm sure this is a concept you've rolled around in your head and figured out the angles of for awhile, but in this chapter I think you should lean into your more evocative writing and keep the more technical discussion for rules and storytelling chapters.

                          As an example "At the height of Aspiration, the soul to which it is subjected cracks. The infinite power of the soul spills out, and twisted by the Longing, weaves the aspirant into a new state," is an amazing sentence that really sells the idea of what it feels like. The bones of capturing those feelings are there, I'd just take a hard look at the chapter and see about cutting down on technical terms. The core idea of loss and longing that consumes someone until their soul cracks and they touch something pure and are remade is really impressive, but sentences like, "This transformation rarely comes out of nowhere, and even in cases where it seems spontaneous, there is usually a latency between it and an earlier period of Longing," are really really dry and feel like a technical manual.


                          Taking notes as I read...

                          "Cherub: The Longing is a game about dreams that are fading, inspired by media of childhood magic and the hidden fictions of ABDLs." Sent me spinning, I might reference Little's here instead of ABDLs, obviously a lot of overlap but from what I know I little's or the few I've met they seem more clearly aligned with what you're discussing of wanting to preserve a childlike outlook and attitude.

                          Glossary section.
                          • Using Aspiration when it is also a core system for character advancement is confusing. When I was reading I kept expecting the two to interact or overlap.
                          • Instead of calling it a Hideout calling it a Fort might work, evoking the idea of pillow or blanket or forts made in the woods by kids. I liked the idea of Crib being a bit of language play, and Fort came to mind while trying to think of something similarly clever.
                          • If you wanted to trim back the Mage-isms snipping the bits from the way Glimmerside is described as an aspect of the Astral would help a lot, then you could put that info into a possible crossover section. Generally for sections like this its helpful to think what the characters would know, and it seems (so far) that Cherubs don't have much idea about a broader astral realm.
                          • Lodge is used in Werewolf: the Forsaken, not the end of the world reusing terms but feels like there might be a better term out there. Keeping with the childlike aspects maybe something like a fair, funhouse, carnival or something along those lines.
                          Chapter 1
                          I can get what you're saying in the "Cherubs are... not like children, like childhood, weird people" sections but it feels like you're both concerned about being misunderstood while also circling around your points without cutting to the quick, which is what you need to do in these sections. You mention the idea of religious purity a few times, which I think is a very interesting angle but you only mention it in offhand ways. Basically, I get the vibe of what you're trying to say, but it needs to be much clearer than just vibes.

                          Also in that section "Philosophizing aside, Cherubs like youth..." desperately needs some sort of clarity since it can easily be taken in some pretty dark ways.

                          Minor typo, Aspiration begins with the sentence, "Cherubs attain their condition." which feels like some editing fragment.

                          I really like the section on the Glimmerside, you did a great job capturing what the Glimmerside is and evoking childhood wonder. I'm not sure if this is the chapter for clarifying things, but you mention some of it being a lens then kind of drop that language as the chapter moves on. It might make sense to jut stick to capturing the feeling of it here, and then getting into details in the rules chapter around which levels are like a perception filter and which are places you bodily go.
