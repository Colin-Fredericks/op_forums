    All ERs do is just slap a band-aid on you and send you back out the door...it has happened to me when I could not keep anything down. 24 hours later, I was back in the ER, the EMS telling them that if the hospital does not take care of me, they would take me to a trauma center. I ended up in ICU dehydrated with IVs, getting antibiotics and who knows what else which vomiting anything that passed my lips. I know my potassium was low. I was sitting in a chair talking to one of the nurses when she handed me a soda, thinking I could keep that down. I could barely hold it because it fell out of my hand as I slip into a seizure. The hospital won't tell me what was wrong (Mom thinks I was at the beginning of sepsis which is rather nasty to have). That was when I started having intestinal trouble and trouble eating the foods I used to eat.

    We are avoiding the local ER because the way they treated our family, despite of Mom and Dad working there. My SIL nearly had a bursted appendix that they label as just an upset stomach. When Mom fell in May, all they did was splint her right leg (broken right fibula) and bandage her left ankle which was twisted. No pain medication. She was stuck in her chair for 2 months except when we took her to the bone doctor for her cast and walking boot. Medicare helped pay for a wheelchair but she had to use crutches to get to bed or the toilet. The ER did not notify her doctor that she was injured...I told him when I had to get some scripts one day. I had food poisoning one day and they kept giving me drugs that did not work. I told them Zofran several times...I know my body damn it. One hospital treated me for the flu (Tennessee before my hospitalization) and I started getting antsy, that wonderful itching feeling when you are reacting to a drug that you are allergic to.

    Most hospitals are not equipped to handle mental patients. Oh, they will admit them, get a sitter and pray that the state hospital gets a room open. If you take a closer look at mental patients in the state hospitals, they are the ones who slip through the cracks...no insurance, no Medicare so they are put in the nastiest 'dorms' where you have to guard your stuff from being stolen or have an elderly woman piss on the floor because she cannot make it to the bathroom (I learned to be a light sleeper during that time). The common area is co-ed but you have to watch yourself in case someone wants to know you better. I refused to be in any room by myself with any male, even the doctor. When they let us go to the basketball court, one guy kept bothering me. I told the attendant and he got the guy to leave me alone.

    I finally find a MHMR (who once rejected me) with the bipolar and PTSD diagnose from Tennessee in my hand and me telling them that I have to have treatment and I be damned if I go back to Terrell. The NP, caseworker and nurse worked with me and we finally find the right drug cocktail. Find the right doctor, Elgustado...if I knew where you are at, I could find a rheumy for you. Reapply for Medicaid. If the ER took x-rays, show up at a doctor's office saying this is necessary. That is how I found out that I have degenerative back problems (I overdid it again today...raking up the leaves from the trailer, making my lower back hurt again). If I could send you money, I would but I got bills and rent. All I can do is encourage you to be strong and fight.

    Bipolar vampire...can't decide to feast for fun or for rage...

                    Originally posted by Wikipedia's entry for Bipolar Disorder
                    Mixed affective episodes

                    In the context of bipolar disorder, a mixed state is a condition during which symptoms of mania and depression occur simultaneously. Typical examples include weeping during a manic episode or racing thoughts during a depressive episode. Individuals may also feel very frustrated in this state, for example thinking grandiose thoughts while at the same time feeling like a failure. Mixed states are often the most dangerous period of mood disorders, during which the risks of substance abuse, panic disorder, suicide attempts, and other complications increase greatly.

                                                Originally posted by JimB View Post
                                                I don't really feel safe posting this, and you don't want to read it. Oh well. Spoilers.
                                                I have a host of issues resulting from post-traumatic stress disorder as a result of a year long deployment in Afghanistan. Before then, I had frequent bouts of what was, admittedly self-diagnosed, depressive periods along with ideations of suicide that really only got worse after the military. Four of my friends from my unit have killed themselves since we came back. To make things even more potentially dangerous, I am an avid firearms enthusiast and have a few guns in my home and some of them are convenient enough to use that I could conceivably, comfortably shoot myself. In fact, I used to practice it.

                                                I spent a lot of time planning this, you see. It helped me feel calm knowing I had a way out and a plan to do it quick. I'd want to shoot myself in my bath tub because the drain's right there and it wouldn't stain the tile. Even if it did, tile's cheaper to replace than carpet, you see, and I wouldn't want to make a mess. I had a few notes written out. One I typed for legibility concerns but I thought that felt too clinical. I wrote a few of them out, a few in pen but the later ones in pencil because I wanted to get my word choice just right, and that requires the ability to erase clean. Eventually I got one that I liked and then went over the words in pen, but then realized I'd used notebook paper, and that felt like I'd been too hasty in my choice of paper and that I'd half-assed my last words. After I finally had the one I liked, I'd put the lid on the toilet down and set the note on it and then fuss over if it looked better askew or straight or what. I'd mess with the wash rags around my sink and tub, too, to make them look neat. I'd make sure my pets had food and water, and ways to get outside if no one came for them in time. I didn't want to leave a messy house or make my cats suffer.

                                                Finally, I'd empty my favorite pistol and look at myself in the mirror, barrel to my temple. Sometimes I'd experiment with where I held it, sometimes laying in the tub and seeing where the blood splatter would go, but usually it'd be against my temple. I'd keep my finger on the trigger and gently apply more and more pressure to really get a feel for how much I'd need to pull it, and I'd always jump nearly out of my skin when I'd hear the hammer drop and feel my gun rattle empty.

                                                The last night I did this ritual was when I'd heard my best friend from service had killed himself and at that point I felt like my misery had bottomed out, it couldn't get any worse, and that was the time to do it. I went through that ritual again, put a bullet in the chamber, got in the tub, put my pistol under my chin, and slowly put more pressure on the trigger until my senses came to me and I racked the slide before I could fire. I cried, I don't know for how long, got up, and went to bed, where I kept crying until I fell asleep. The next day I went to the VA to get help.

                                                This story scares me every time I tell it. It makes me feel like shit, it makes me want to cry, I don't ever want to revisit these emotions, but I do, regularly. Why? Because each time I tell this story, I feel a little less bad about it. Every time I tell people what I saw over there, what I did, what I've done since, it makes my burden feel lighter. I believe humans, as social creatures, benefit from a sense of community and camaraderie, and having a place where everyone can join you in mourning your problems, whatever they may be. I have my therapists, friends, and family to fall back on. If you know someone, or if you trust this community enough, do you think it'd help if you talked through your problems more with them?
