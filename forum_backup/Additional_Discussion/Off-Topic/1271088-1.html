                                    Hi,

                                    I don't know if you still feel like this, but I hope things have improved. I'm in a similar situation, but I think I'm learning how to improve it bit by bit.

                                    My biggest problem was that when I was young, I went from having shitty friends or no friends at all to having a friend that I love more than a brother, someone who made me feel alive, fearless, and connected like no one else. Then years ago he had to leave to another country and since then I can't help but judge every new relationship based on what I had with him. Rationally, I know it's neither fair nor realistic to hold other people to such standards, but that lingering feeling of "it's just not the same" keeps following me. My solution was to understand that relationships are not a matter of all or nothing, there are different types with different intensities and each one brings something different that it's special in its own way. You have family, coworkers, gaming friends, classmates, and even the people you talk with in the forums. I still miss my best friend, and I don't think I will ever find a connection like the one I had with him, but that can't prevent me from enjoying other types of connections for what they are or even find one that is similar to what I had.

                                    I hope that my words help somehow, and I wish you a happy day.

                                                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                            In my now 1,5 year Scion 2E game, I make 3rd recruitment phase for players. Like in clock, 9 months pass by - and even more stable players are going off the game. Why is it?
                                                            Probably less about you or your game, and more about life moving on.

                                                            There are many possible "legitimate" reasons for someone to have less time or focus on the game, and as time goes on the chance of one of those reasons coming to a particular player's life ceases being a statistical fluke to become a likelihood.

                                                            In practice, keeping such regular groups for long is always hard because of the many factors that can interfere, and being a group activity means you multiply the likelihood of interference by the number of participants.
