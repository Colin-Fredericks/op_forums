                            I'm glad you're still alive, Gecko. I doubt I've ever replied to you before, but seeing what you attempted to do was still painful. Getting definite news about it would be even worse. So I'm relieved that you're physically recovering.

                            I've battled depression for quite some time. It's extremely tiring, but I've managed this far and I hope I'll get an even better handle on it in the future. Unfortunately I don't always have that attitude. During my bad periods (especially back during my suicidal periods) I simply can't motivate myself to go on the same way that I do when I feel better. The way I've described it to my therapist is that I practically become a different person when I'm sad and frustrated. Things I love don't cheer me up anymore, and the reasons I have to go on and to live just seem like bullshit in comparison to how much I hate my living situation, my diagnoses, and how much I am a social fuckup. But then when I get those feelings out of my system I have a hard time understanding how I could even feel that way. I'm not bipolar, but that's how drastic the difference is for me. Right now when I'm writing I certainly feel a lot of frustration with my life as it is right now, but I can still enjoy the good parts of my life. RPGs, TV series, bad horror movies (especially this month) and so on. Not a particularly meaningful existence, perhaps, but I'm managing.

                            What I'm trying to convey is that what you're feeling right now might not actually represent what you'd feel once you've started on your mental recovery. It seems really fucking hopeless right now and I doubt you'll see it this way right now, but I'm glad I never carried through with any of my plans (though it certainly was close a few times).

                            I don't know what you're going to do now, Gecko, or what you should do in your particular situation, but I hope it works out for you.

                                        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                        Unless your advice relates to how I can live as enjoyable and fulfilling a life as possible with the knowledge that I should kill myself but can't, your advice is useless.
                                        I did already, but I can lay some more on ya.

                                        First, to reiterate: What is good is easy to get, and what is terrible is easy to endure (Epicurus). This is not a platitude, but simple adjustment of perspective. By simple, though, I do not mean easily attained as it can take constant reassurance and redundant recitation to remain within one's perspective. It is simple in that it's not complex, which means it's easier to repeat as necessary. "What is good is easy to get." There are many things in life that are good: breathing is good, eating is good, laughing is good, crying is good, walking is good. We take so many things for granted, and these simplicites of life are missed for their innate pleasure. Try taking a few deep breaths while stretching. Focus on the feeling of the air filling your lungs. Think about how amazing all those little capillaries are, sucking the useful ingredients from the air to keep you going. Feel the tension in your muscles and how nice it feels to release it. Not your thing? how about food? Make yourself a peanut better & jelly sandwich (unless you're allergic to peanuts, because then we're back to the suicide thing). Simple food that can be wonderful. Feel the textures in your mouth. Roll it around a bit. Get all the flavor out of it. Nuts and fruits squashed into paste, do you know how much work that takes? Someone went thru a lot of trouble to make that just so you can shove it in your mouth and swallow. Enjoy and appreciate the effort they put into it. "What is terrible is easy to endure". Once again, it's a shift in perspective. Name me something that can happen to you in life that cannot be endured, overcome or adapted to. Go on, I'll wait. Loss of occupation? Loss of house/home? Loss of transportation? All can be replaced with a little work or adapted to if long term. Loss of limb or family? Endured and adapted to as well. Depression, even when it's strictly a matter of biology, has methods for enduring and adapting. Loss of life? Ah, there's the one. Only life has potential to persevere. Early and unnatural cessation of said life consequently snuffs out that potential.

                                        Now, I should state that what you are asking above is nearly impossible. Someone else cannot make your life appear enjoyable or fulfilling for you. There's only one thing in the world you are actually in control of: how you react to everything around you. If you choose not to find enjoyment and fulfillment in your life, and you also choose not to change the circumstances of your life or the perspective you have on it, then it's never going to change. But if you make the decision to change things in your head, then that's your first step on the 1,000 mile journey.

                                        And if you think any of this cannot be done, I'll refer you to not just several people who post on this thread regularly, but feel free to message me personally and I'll tell you all about my fun times living with bi-polar 2 while on and off meds.

                                                            I'm willing to give the benefit of the doubt. I've been around people with suicidal tendencies and have in fact lost two friends to suicide.

                                                            I have also known several people who made light of those deaths by using it as a stick to ward off criticism way too much. I have personally had my losses be disrespected by people who think threats of suicide are "Get out of jail free" cards.

                                                            I am not saying it is wrong to talk about these feelings, how overwhelming they are and how powerless we can feel to them. We need to talk about them in order to cope, to hang on, to find meaning.

                                                            But I've been around people who are working to get better, who are trying to handle it, who are seeking help, and I know what that conversation sounds and looks like.

                                                            If you're going to talk about suicidal feelings, make sure you know what you are doing when you do so and why, lest you come across as offending those who have lost people to suicide. We know the difference between people who will do it without help and those who are just wanting to be excused.
