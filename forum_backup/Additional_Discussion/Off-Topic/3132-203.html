                                        Ok, I'll rant and try to have some fun.

                                        The thing is, I grew up with Columbus not only as a national hero, but even one that's closer to me than that. I'll explain.

                                        I live in a small town near Genoa, which is Columbus' town, in the Italian region called Liguria. We have a reputation for not only being rather fierce (to be honest, lots of regions have that) but also for being grumpy, prideful and always angrily complaining about something. The kind of people that live of tourism but can't stand the tourists and tend to give outsiders the impression they're allowed to pass through but will never truly belong. Each country has those and we're it for Italy.

                                        In all fairness, there's a bit of truth to that. Take that Simpsons sketch where Willie says "Damn Scots! They ruined Scotland!", apply it to us, and it will still work. But being this kind of prideful people that care a lot about our own achievements, one of the few things we don't argue about is Christopher Columbus.

                                        We have schools named after him, songs, museums and all that stuff. The first thing you see once out of Genoa's biggest station is a giant statue of him, people visit his home and every now and then a town tries to claim it was there that Columbus actually was born. He's not just an Italian hero: he's our hero. Which is just a fact you grow up with.

                                        And that's where the problem start. Because someday, you'll eventually get across the fact that maybe Columbus was not that great. That maybe he's not that worthy of the respect and fame he has.

                                        At first you'll ignore it, because that will just sound wrong to you. Perhaps you'll take it as slander without foundations. At best, you think it's a matter of the usual ugly side of history, nothing more than that.

                                        But then evidence starts piling up, especially if you read some more, and then you have to face the truth: that Columbus, even for historical standards, was a human pile of garbage.

                                        And that's an absurd realization to live with around here because the guy's everywhere! There's this weird dissonance, which is made even weirder by the fact that actually many people know about it. But you can't talk about it because that would break an unwritten social rule. I'm not kidding: aside from some arguments I got into, I witnessed people having trouble with their professors because they disputed Columbus in class, the kind of trouble that comes back to bite you when you're making an exam. People (history nerds, students and other teachers) learn to avoid the topic.

                                        It's frustrating.

                                        Look, I get that historical figures have nasty skeletons in their closet way more often than not and I get that people need heroes and adore the icons they grew up with, but Columbus' actions cross a line of...let's say tolerance towards the facts through awareness of value dissonance, that made me put him off the pedestal.

                                        I don't go out there to ruin people's idea of him, but if I'm taking part in a discussion about Columbus, I'll eventually say what I think.

                                        Let's say that I I wish for the conflict that comes with confronting with sanitized history and actual history to be easier to deal with
