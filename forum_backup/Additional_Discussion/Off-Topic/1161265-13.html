        Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
        I’ve never understood the complaint that the Spellplague was too much, too fast. The Realms get catastrophically fucked up so often that th fanbase literally has a name for when it happens, “Realms Shaking Events.” If you wanted the old Realms, then you play in the timeframe before then.
        To be fair it wasn't only the Spellplague. Rather, what upset people was the Spellplague, plus the introduction of an entire new world, plus a 100 year time skip. All of the metaplot and NPCs that people had been following - literally for decades, suddenly didn't matter anymore because those characters were dead and often those locations were gone or presented very differently.

        The idea, of course, was that the Forgotten Realms was too dense of a setting with too much material that had happened and this made it very intimidating for new players to get into - they had look into a setting that was almost 30 years old, which was hard. So the Spellplague + introduction of Abier + the 100 year timeskip was meant to essentially be a reset button. Older players would barely recognize the setting, and all of the old metaplot was gone, so both long-time Forgotten Realms players and players new to the Forgotten Realms would basically both be exploring this setting from the same starting line.

        Unfortunately, things didn't turn out the way the creators hoped. Older players simply dropped the setting because they felt it turned something they were passionate about into something unrecognizable, and new players still didn't get into the setting because it still had that 30 years worth of backstory and felt pretty intimidating. As a result, the Forgotten Realms basically lost of all of its old fans and didn't get any new ones. That's why they reset things again in 5th edition and are walking back many of the changes that 4th edition introduced.

        I think the one take away is that there were probably better ways of making the Forgotten Realms accessible to new players than to drive off all of the long-time players, the guys who had been loyally buying products and following the setting for the last 20 or 30 years.

                    Originally posted by Drake View Post
                    I hope that the designers will realize a concept that I've used for years at this point; that a setting's "cosmological model" need only be the way the 'learned folks' in that setting view the relationships of various planes, not a literal 'if you could get far enough away from the planes, you would see them literally in this physical shape and arrangement'.

                    And in that way, it is easy as pie to have both A) all settings exist within the same multi-verse and B) there are different cosmological models for some settings be true statements, and do so without altering established details of any settings.
                    Most of the time, I agree with you. This is pretty much exactly how I run Planescape with Ravenloft, Golarion, Forgotten Realms, Rokugan (yes, you heard me. 3e's OA was one of my first books), Greyhawk, and Dragonlance all incorporated into one cosmology. Each views the planes through their own lens and none of them (including Planescape's Factions) are able to see the whole picture because its not something morals a meant to understand. So we totally understand each other on that and I agree that this is the most practical way to do the metaphysical crossover.

                    But I disagree when it comes to Eberron. What make Eberron unique is that things are so different. Its not just the planes and their metaphysics. The outsiders that inhabit those planes have completely different metabiologies, different lifecycles, and different symbolism than the outsiders of the Great Wheel. If the approach taken was that Fernia was just what the people of Eberron called the Plane of Fire, it would be a total betrayal of the themes of both Eberron and Planescape. Fernia is nothing like the Plane of Fire! For one thing, Fernia is evil-aligned and births Balors, Pit Fiends, and Firres in addition to Fire Elementals. For another, all of the planes of Ebberon have infinite layers, similar to the way the Abyss is arranged. Even the easiest plane to do this with, the Far Realms and Xoriat, doesn't work. Why? The Far Realms is a plane beyond reality. Xoriat doesn't fill that role. It is the plane of madness (and inspiration), but it is very solidly a part of Eberron's reality, not something beyond it. The plane that actually fills the same niche as the Far Realm for Eberron is the Astral Plane, since it was not created by the progenerator dragons and technically exists outside of Eberron's cosmic schema.

                    So blending cosmological models can work, but only if you dig deep into what the planes actually mean, not just go with the surface correspondences. All of that said, I do think there is a place for Eberron in the shared universe, and its drawn directly rom what Keith said (and what Mike has said which sounds like he's talked to Keith about it). Eberron can be found in the Deep Astral if you look hard enough. If you do find it, you will find this Prime is metaphysically orbited by its thirteen astral planes. Not demiplanes. Fully on, infinitely large planes that are sealed off from the "usual" realities and are extremely difficult to reach. These planes have their own alternate ecologies which are passingly familiar to those found on the Great Wheel, but on closer inspection are completely unique. Alternatively, you could also reach Eberron through the Plane of Shadow, but again, this is very challenging.

                    EDIT: Hey! My blog comes up when you image search "Eberron Cosmology!" Sweet!

                                    Yeah, the lack of character choices after first level is one of my biggest gripes with 5e on the player side. You get to choose your Subclass, maybe a couple of your class features involve a choice (like which Skills do I take Expertise for, or what spells do I learn this level), and some Feats/ASIs. But for the most part there’s very little that differentiates one rogue from another, and less that differentiates one thief from another.

                                    This is why, while I adore the rogue, warlock is ultimately my favorite class this edition. They basically get two subclass choices (Patron at 1st level and Pact Boon at 3rd), you get to choose spells and feats, and you get to choose Invocations. The warlock has a choice to make almost every level, which is great, and although most warlocks are going to take Eldritch Blast and Agonizing Blast, there are other viable builds, and a lot of variety beyond the basic build. Plus they’re the only spellcasting class that breaks out of the same spell slot progression table everyone else uses. They remind me very much of my favorite parts of 4e.

                                    I also think they’re the best Class for beginners (sorry, Fighter). In my experience, people who are new to D&D, especially if they’re new to RPGs in general, want to play spellcasters. If it’s your first foray into roleplaying a character in a fantasy setting, who wants to be some dude with a sword when they could be a wizard? Even better if you can have both a sword and magic (and Warlocks happen to be one of the easiest classes to build a gish with). But Warlocks are easier to pilot than a lot of other spellcasters. Worst comes to worst you fall back on Eldritch Blast and you’ll keep up pretty well with an archery fighter, only you get to do it with magic beams instead of a bow and arrow. Plus you get just enough spell slots for about one leveled spell per encounter, and you never have to worry about spell preparation or spell levels. You always have all of your known spells available and you always cast them at the highest spell slot level you can. And to top it all off, the Patron acts as a built-in mentor combined with a built-in source of conflict, and is a great way to tie your character into an unfamiliar setting.

                                    I fuckin love 5e Warlocks.
