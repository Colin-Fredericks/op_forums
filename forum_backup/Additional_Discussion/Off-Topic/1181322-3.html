    53) Agorak:

    Being a Half Orc can be socially difficult, but it's probably worse for those who possess a natural affinity for magic yet lack the proper training to wield it responsibly. Ever since he completed his rights of manhood when he was 13 years old, Agorak has become the black sheep of his tribe. Orcs and humans alike both try to maintain their distance from him, out of fear that his strange powers will bring some kind of misfortune upon them for getting too close. These fears are not entirely unfounded, as Agorak has caused more than a few accidents while 'playing' with his magic. It finally came to a head when the young sorcerer burned down the hut where the tribe's chief was keeping his harem of concubines. Agorak was driven out from his tribe with a volley of throwing axes, and a seemingly endless stream of profanity by his former chief. Eventually, he found his way into more civilized lands and began to take up the life of an adventurer.

    As an adventurer, Agorak is brash and unpredictable, which he attributes to being a quirk of his Orcish heritage. Having never received a proper education in the magical arts, Agorak's approach to magic is to just unleash a wave of power and hope for the best. Contrary to popular opinion, this usually seems to get the job done! But there are still times when things don't work out the way he planned, and when accidents happen, it's not always the enemy who suffers for it.

    Race: Half Orc

    Age: 17

    Gender: Male

    Class: Sorcerer

    Alignment: Chaotic Neutral

    Specialization: Wild Magic

        54) Weaponer Krin [Hobgoblin, Eldritch Knight Fighter]:
        In ancient times, when bronze was the pinnacle of metallurgy, there existed the Hobgoblin city-state of Villangard. And this warlike nation was directed by the Weaponers of Villangard, master forgers of weapons and magical items. Their magic swords and shields and spears and rings were legendary; works of art and tools of martial prowess that would not be rivaled for centuries. Some say they even forged wings of bronze, so their warriors could take to the sky and rain lightning on their enemies with magic spears. So great were their arms, that even long after Villangard fell and was swept away into the tides of history, Hobgoblins still call their magical weapon smiths Weaponers.

        So it is with Krin, a Hobgoblin driven by a desire to study and recapture the glory of that ancient civilization. He threw himself into books and the forges, so as to develop his craft. That he might bring back the glory of Villangard and elevate the Hobgoblin race to a place of prominence again.

        Unfortunately, this would be his undoing. For there came a time when, in the course of a military campaign, he had on good authority that a town his Legion prepared to burn held artifacts and scrolls from legendary Villangard. He was ordered to burn the town. He could not. Such rich history could not be allowed to die, and so he disobeyed, seeking to salvage the artifacts instead. For his insubordination, Krin was stripped of his rank and cast out in dishonor.

        Nonetheless, he got what he sought. The objects - and the lore accompanying them - were sufficient to point him in the right direction of potentially greater stores of lost Villangard artifacts. Moreover, being banished from the ranks of his people provides a boon in disguise. Now that he's a free agent, nothing holds him back from pursuing his dreams. Now that he's on his own, he can quest for true ancient secrets. Now that he isn't creating magic items for the Hobgoblin legions, he can create true masterworks for himself, and for the joy of creation. Nothing can get in his way anymore.

        Well, nothing except that which destroyed Villangard originally. Some threats are eternal, and enjoy the advantage of being forgotten. Even Krin doesn't know what force destroyed Villangard. If his quest continues, though, he most certainly will.

            55) Marx von Grob [Human, Oath of Devotion Paladin]:
            When word came in that a faraway principality had come under the sway of fiends, the church of Marx's lands put out the call for soldiers willing to fight them. It was to be a crusade - a holy war against men that swore allegiance to devils. A righteous man, Marx von Grob kissed his wife and children goodbye, and set off to join the fight. The fighting was brutal, and the horrors he saw among the demon-haunted settlements of that land disturbed him to his very core. Yet it could not break him, for he had faith in the gods. By crusade's end, he had become a Paladin in his own right, though he'd begun as a farmer.

            Fiends remanded back to their abyss, Marx declined a spot in the new crusader state that formed in the ashes of the old one. Instead, he returned to his farm.

            Alas, though he dwelt again in his pastoral fields, and again embraced his family, true contentment eluded him. Marx took to the spring planting with diminished enthusiasm, and both his days and nights found him haunted. Not by nightmares and visions of the crusade - strangely, he had none of the shell shock that reduced many of his companions to bundles of raw nerves and bad memories. Rather, a singular restlessness dogged von Grob. Before long, he deduced the cause.

            Rather than being broken by the stresses of conflict, he'd adapted, and too well. He'd become a warrior, and could no longer turn off that aspect. Marx von Grob could only find peace in the midst of war.

            Reluctantly - but paradoxically with unspoken excitement - he gathered his arms and armor. He resisted the urge long enough to take in the fall harvest, then kissed his family farewell. Marx liked to say that he set off to protect the world from evil. In truth, such a mission was a happy coincidence. What he truly sought was adventure. A chance to plunge himself into the maelstrom of combat, even if he had to go looking for it. Even now, he longs to be with his family...but he also cannot resist the siren call of battle.

                56) Peat "Peaty" Woofrider [Goblin, Beast Conclave Ranger (Revised)]:
                Technically, he came from the "Wolf-rider" tribe. But Peat (or "Peaty" as he's sometimes called) has an affectionate, child-like disposition, and so he liked to call himself a "Woof-rider". He loved tending to the tribe's wolf mounts, to the point of preferring their company to his overly serious family. He loved to cuddle with them, and hated to beat them (a discipline method common to goblins). Indeed, Peat didn't seem to have a nasty bone in his body. A fact that frequently put him at odds with his tribe, who hedged close to the ideal of the cruel goblinoids. This disconnect led to Peat not fitting in very well; he was often found on the fringes, patrolling the outskirts of goblin territory and hunting game on wolf-back. It was on these fringes that Peat learned the ways of the Ranger.

                One day, however, Peat and his wolf mount returned from a hunting trip, only to find the village ablaze, dozens of his tribe dead. Only the youngest and eldest goblins survived, and they spoke of "big folk" (medium sized humanoids) adventurers who orchestrated the attack. Peat memorized their descriptions well, and collected what items the killers left behind. After helping the survivors pack what was left and hook up with another goblin tribe, Peat struck out on his own. Many goblins and wolves were lost - members of his family. Regardless of their relationship, Peat could not leave them un-avenged. He would track down the killers of his tribe. Not necessarily to kill them - for Peat is a gentle soul - but to understand why, and to make sure these adventurers never hurt goblins again.

                How to go about finding them, though? The world is large, especially compared to a goblin. Well, what better way to find adventurers than to become an adventurer, and travel with same? In truth, Peat had quietly nursed a desire to travel the land and go on adventures. Meeting new people and new fluffy animal friends. Moreover, his travels through big folk lands show him an increasing number of injustices and tragedies. Wrongs that needed righting, monsters that needed fighting, and darkness that needed lighting. If Peat traveled long and far enough, he'd find his tribe's killers sooner or later. In the meantime, why not indulge the desire to be, not a craven villain as goblins normally are, but an actual Hero?

                Peat is a generally friendly, upbeat goblin. When in combat, however, Peat gets serious. Any time social interactions and diplomacy breaks down, he becomes focused on eliminating all threats. Play is play and fun is fun, but even he knows there is a time and a place for tomfooleries. And despite his gentle demeanor, he's still an experienced survivalist, hunter, and combatant. When stalking prey or engaging foes, Peat coordinates with his wolf in both ranged and close quarters. Favored tactics include firing arrows from wolf-back, taking potshots from cover while his wolf sneaks around to flank, and engaging with his wolf in melee with duel short swords.

                    57) Professor Sarah Pagecount [Yuan-Ti Pureblood, Great Old One Warlock]:
                    A studious, learned woman, born to a merchant family in a provincial town. Emotionally detached and of a retiring disposition, Sarah entered into academia as a student of history. For indeed, she had strong opinions about many subjects...including race. Since her parents were committed to asylums early in her life, Sarah nursed a nagging fear and obsession with "degeneracy", both cultural, intellectual, psychological, spiritual, and hereditary. Sure, a High Elf or a Dwarf were fine, as they went, but drow elves or other variants of humanoids were obvious debased examples of their species. She extended this even to humans; Sarah long nursed a racism - though not true antipathy as such - towards those not of her particular ethnic stock.

                    An ironic attitude, to be sure, but we'll get back to that.

                    When the student Pagecount graduated university and gained a young Professorship, it was during this era that she developed an interest in the occult. This interest caused her to cross paths with other individuals, a group that eventually coalesced around the solving of baffling mysteries and the seeking of eldritch truth. They were less a party of Adventurers, and more a cabal of otherwise average, unpowered Investigators. Over the course of these investigations, the "Lamp Lighters" (as they came to call themselves) experienced danger, horror, and blows to both health and sanity. A few of Sarah's closest compatriots died, were irrevocably lost, or slid into madness. Eventually, Miss Pagecount, too, would stare into an Abyss that was as stultifying as it was personal.

                    For, indeed, during the course of an investigation into some Cult to a Reptile God, Sarah crossed paths with hostile and magic-savvy persons who, to her disgust, blended the aspects of man and snake. They were the Yuan-ti, heirs to a serpentine legacy older than recorded history. What broke Miss Pagecount, however, was what she learned after her narrow escape. Returning to her home town with a mind flush with thoughts of miscegenation, she sought comfort for personal fears by looking into her own family history. Instead of finding reassurances as to her pure bred heritage, she instead discovered that members of earlier generations in her family could be traced directly to that town controlled by Reptile Cultists. Diving through letters and diaries left by relatives revealed that they would develop the ability to commune with serpents, spit poison clouds, and influence minds. And that many acquired reptilian features as they grew older.

                    Sarah Pagecount was a pureblood all right. A Yuan-ti Pureblood. Suffice to say, the revelation drove Miss Pagecount temporarily mad.

                    What companions remained to her consigned her to an institution, as they'd become accustomed to doing when investigators broke under the weight of eldritch truth. And for a year, Sarah sat in her padded cell, struggling with her nervous breakdown. Eventually, however, the dreams that previously were filled with memories of horror and anxieties over her tainted heritage, gave way to dreams of a more...spiritual nature. Sarah contacted by an entity - a great serpent that coiled around the world. A primordial serpent from the beginning. It offered to give Sarah power, but in exchange for what she didn't expect: for her to continue doing roughly what she'd already been doing. That is, investigating eldritch forces, busting cults, and dispelling madness. For, as the serpent explained, the entities beyond the Material Plane were hardly united in purpose; even Old Gods and Outer Gods, like it, weren't necessarily allies. They were all rivals to the Primordial Serpent. Even other reptile gods - like those worshiped by her Yuan-ti kin - were rivals, and thus the Yuan-ti cults were obstacles to its enigmatic goals.

                    This being could hardly be expected to have wholly benevolent purposes for the world. But Sarah decided that one extra-planar threat working at some undefined point in the future was better than hundreds of threats acting right now. So, with reluctance, Miss Pagecount signed the pact. Shortly thereafter, her health and behavior improved to the point where the doctors let her leave.

                    Now she's on move, splitting time between getting her professorship up and running, and working to combat the horrors beyond mortal comprehension. She's even got new allies - actual Adventurers this time, who hopefully will be made of sterner stuff. People who won't crack under pressure or die off quickly. The biggest issue with them, at the moment, is both hiding the true source of her powers, and hiding the growing signs of her heritage. Scales have begun growing on her ankles, and her eyes flash into gold slits when she casts spells. Still, she's becoming less judgmental these days; her extended stay in asylum, and revelations about the family history, have forced her to confront old assumptions. At the very least, personal experience has taught her that there's far worse things to be than of a "lesser race".

                    We said she was making progress, not that she was completely changed. These things take baby steps.

                            58) Captain Efferil Cithreth [Wood Elf, Trickery Domain Cleric]:
                            A proper gentleman from a house of good standing (for Wood Elves, at least), Efferil Cithreth desired from a young age to be a sailor. To travel the seas, exploring coasts and islands, and filling in the blank spaces on the map. For a while, there, he even had the privilege, ascending to the rank of captain and running a tight ship. That is, until a rare spontaneous urge had him follow up on a purported treasure map he came into possession of. He rationalized that, even if there was no monetary windfall, they could at least chart the deserted island and set the stage for having turned to more productive use.

                            Unfortunately, his first mate and a handful of crewmen had a lust for treasure that trumped their loyalty. They were a ship of explorers and occasional ferry, so they were unaccustomed to overly profitable ventures. The lure of gold enticed their greedy hearts. So when Captain Cithreth anchored them next to the island and brought a party ashore, the mutiny was under way. First at the ship, while Efferil and his landing party were out, then to Efferil himself when they reached their destination. No sooner had they set foot into that forgotten, vine-covered temple that the betrayal was sprung. His mutinous crew didn't need Efferil anymore, and tied him to the alter while they sought booty.

                            Negotiations got him no where, and his bonds were knotted too well, as was typical of career sailors. Left alone and forsaken, the desperate captain cried out to anyone - mortal or divine - that could deliver him from what was rapidly turning into an imminent demise. Then, someone answered. From above him, the great statue of the temple's deity - a forgotten trickster god of chaos - spoke. The god found the stuffy elf a boring creature, but admitted to finding the mutiny predictable as well. So the deity offered to free Efferil from his bonds...if the elf sowed discord in his name forevermore. Seeing little other choice, Efferil agreed. And so he was gifted with divine revelation, and a Cleric he became.

                            What followed was a senseless series of events, as Efferil - goaded by his new god - dispatched his mutinous crew members in the most erratic and...interesting fashion he could muster. Though by the time he returned to shore - carrying a heavy chest of gold along the way - he found his beloved vessel quite burned and destroyed, the rest of the crew dead or lost. His god mused that "[he] tried to turn the tables on your traitors, but things got out of hand; it was hilarious". Deciding that it would be more interesting anyway, the god bid his new disciple to find his own way back. Efferil was, suffice to say, dismayed by this.

                            Weeks later, Efferil managed to sail back to civilization on a raft, and traded in his hard-earned treasure for a new vessel. But by this point, his god's demand for greater chaos meant that he couldn't return to just being a regular sailor. Every act of discord was a prayer to his deity, and Efferil eventually found himself driven from port before long for his many casual crimes and improprieties. Earning an ill reputation and being quite fed up with his string of misfortunes, Efferil finally gave up. Fine, okay. The god wanted disorder? The elf would deliver.

                            And thus, the Pirate Captain "Wild" Efferil was born. A Chaotic Good pirate captain, though Chaotic more by duty than by inclination. Despite his natural meekness, neatness, and desire for a quiet life, the elf pursues a reluctant campaign of piracy, lawlessness, and high seas adventure. Hoping, one day, that his purpose will be fulfilled, and his god will move on to other things, so Efferil can finally have a peaceful life.

                            From the outside, of course, everyone knows the legend of the unpredictable, possibly schizophrenic pirate priest. The elven man who dresses neatly (albeit garishly), charms peasants, changes his face to sow confusion, and subverts law and order. Truly a madman he must be, and a dangerous one at that.

                                59) ​Scourge Skinripper:

                                "Look man... sometimes, life's a bitch. The best advice I can offer you is to make life your bitch. Now pass me another ale, we've got a memorial to attend!"

                                Usually people who commit themselves to a life time of adventure and dungeon crawling like to imagine that by the time it's over, they'll have proven themselves worthy of some minor deity position as a reward. But Scourge has other plans on his mind than joining the ranks of the deific hierarchy. No, Scourge dreams of becoming a demon lord and establishing his own personal playground within the depths of the Abyss. There are those who warn that becoming one of the most powerful demons in existence is not so easily done, but Scourge retorts that obtaining divinity is perhaps even more difficult in that regard.

                                While Scourge himself may not actively try to spread pain and misery, such things do tend to follow in his wake. Scourge is a man who walks his own path in life, which is predominantly focused on keeping himself happy, even at the expense of those around him. While this may come across as being incredibly selfish to the point of sociopathic behavior, Scourge has demonstrated himself willing and able to cooperate with others and becoming protective of them. Although his motivations behind such actions have less to do with having a conscience and are more in line with him being curious to see where things go, as well as finding those under his protection to be a continuous source of entertainment and usefulness.

                                While other Tieflings may be known for their sleek and seductive appearances, Scourge takes things in another direction. A hulking brute of a demon spawn, Scourge Skinripper is able to trade punches with the best of them, assuming he doesn't have another weapon in his hands already. His horns are shaped like those of an antelope, with his ebony colored hair being cut short into jagged tufts. His eyes are a solid shade of vibrant gold, and his skin is the color of blood. His weapon of choice is the heavy flail, for he claims that his enemies die too quickly after being struck with a blade.

                                    60) ​Elzira Silverveil:


                                    "Look, I'm not exactly an expert here. If you want to open a portal to the Feywilds, you can either find me a ring of mushrooms, or find yourself a proper wizard!"


                                    Raised among a predominantly human community with nary an elf in sight, Elzira has always been curious about the other half of her heritage and what it means to follow the ancient ways of her elvish cousins. Alas, she had few, if any, opportunities in which to explore such questions and grew increasingly frustrated by the lack of answers over the years. This frustration would eventually lead to her sneaking out of the village and into the forbidden forest that neared its borders.

                                    Everybody in the village knew that the forest was home to denizens of the Feywild and chose to avoid the place out of either respect for their ways or fear of their power. But during her youth, Elzira had occasionally heard that elves were related in some fashion to the fey and believed that if anybody could provide the answers she was looking for, it would be them. She was not entirely wrong, but not entirely correct either. After an hour of entering the forest, she encountered an Eladrin settlement, who welcomed her with curious exhuberance.

                                    During her time in the forest, the Eladrin answered every question that Elzira posed to them, revealing to her the secrets of the elves and their ancient magics. Then, when she was done, they presented her with an offer to learn such magics for herself. She was half elf, after all, and was entitled to the same heritage as any who shared their blood. Young and eager, Elzira Silverveil hastily agreed and a bargain was struck between her and the village elders. In exchange for pledging her service to Verenestra, she would be infused with a portion of the forest's magic, granting her abilities which resembled those of the fey who dwelled there. This seemed like a great deal at first, but upon finally stepping outside of the forest's boundaries, Elzira was surprised to find that 300 years had gone by and anybody she once knew was long dead. Now, she has nobody to turn to but the fey and their capricious whims that often don't make sense to anybody but themselves.

The pages of the grimoire smell like fresh oak, and appear to have been written by a delicate hand.

                                        61) ​Inwein:

                                        "Do you have any idea how stressful it is? When everybody takes one look at you and expects you to be a shining example of piety and good behavior? I'm sick of trying to live up to their ridiculously high standards, from now on I'm doing whatever I want, whenever I want!"

                                        For most people, being a child of the heavens would be something to take pride in and live up to. But for Inwein, he's always seen it as a source of great frustration. As an Aasimar, he never got to have a real childhood, because those around him expected him to embody all the stereotypical angelic traits. Namely a firm belief in order, religion and service to the community. All the traits that a young boy typically lacks during the early portions of their life! As he grew older and was expected to take on more responsibilities, the town he was raised in saddled him with more and more expectations. This would continue until finally the day came when Inwein couldn't take it anymore.

                                        In a fit of anger that he had been holding back for years, Inwein lashed out and struck a priest in the face... within the halls of their temple... during a congregation! The supplicants were shocked and mortified, but not nearly as much as they were by what came afterward. The Aasimar proceeded to fly into a tirade of curses and insults as he vented every ounce of frustration he had been holding back his whole life. Not one person in the temple wasn't a subject of his scorn. When it was all over, Inwein stormed out of the temple and finally the town itself, leaving his friends and family gaping in silence.

                                        Since that day, Inwein has taken to a life of adventuring, traveling along the open road and indulging whatever desire strikes his fancy. Even after the tongue lashing he gave them, Inwein still has a lot of pent up aggression churning within him, and eventually learned how to channel those emotions in battle to great effectiveness. It was also through this anger that he finally learned how to access his true potential. Inwein was never meant to be a symbol of love, but an instrument of war! His heavenly light draws the spirits of fallen warriors like moths to a flame, and the potency of his rage causes such spirits to manifest themselves in the material world. With some effort, Inwein learned how to command these souls to assist him and his allies in exchange for giving them the chance to enjoy the thrill of combat once again!

                                            62) Qidiel:


                                            "Oh, no, dear, I was turning into a bear long before this happened to me. Really, the only difference I've noticed is that I turn into a bear more frequently these days. Speaking of bears, would you like to be one, too?"


                                            When your race is born of magic and you come from an entire plane of reality that's practically drowning in the stuff, it's not unlikely that you would be exposed to illnesses of a less mundane nature. Be that as it may, Qidiel certainly wasn't expecting to contract lycanthropy from a one night stand she had with a pair of extraplanar travelers. Most people would be justifiably angry with the situation, but Qidiel is a native denizen of the Feywilds, where raw emotion holds more sway than rational thought. As a druidic worshiper of Lurue, Qidiel was avidly curious about a disease that had the power to change people into intelligent beasts, and soon devoted her time to learning how to harness and control her affliction. Once she had finally managed to do so, she decided to visit the material plane, where she could play around with her newly acquired powers without endangering her friends and family back home.

                                            Despite her cheery disposition, Qidiel is an exceedingly dangerous individual. She allows emotions to dictate her course of action, focusing on whatever personal whim strikes her fancy at the moment. She also does not have a very high opinion of the short lived and magically stunted inhabitants of the material plane, whom she views as toys for her amusement. Once they are no longer entertaining to her, she casts them aside in order to pursue her next source of amusement. She also has no understanding of the concept that magic can not solve all the world's problems and is frequently confused as to why the denizens of the material plane have placed such weight behind its usage. Because of these personality traits, those who do not understand the nature of the Feywild, or the ways of those who live there, may describe her as being a sociopath!

                                            While those of elven stock may consider Qidiel to be a mere child, she is physically mature by the standards of those with shorter lifespans. As expected of an elf from the land of fairies, she is impossibly beautiful, with emerald colored eyes, alabaster hued skin and oak colored hair that drapes down to her shoulders in shining locks. Her casual attire is a contradiction of both primitive and sophisticated design. A set of robes fashioned out of oversized leaves, woven vines and dried tree sap, Qidiel looks like a noble lady of the forest when she isn't wearing more appropriate battle gear.

                                                63) Oskar Deepgrowth [Hill Dwarf, Circle of Spores (UA) Druid]:
                                                Oskar was banished from Clan Dankil for certain indiscretions, and abandoned civilization to live in the wilderness. His travels were long and hard, the seasons harsh and the food scarce. He would have starved to death, had he not been taken in by an isolated clan of Myconids, who called themselves Deepgrowth. These fungus people nursed him back to health, and the eldest Myconid took Oskar under his wing.

                                                The dwarf harbored a deep desire for adoration. Honor - or rather the perception he possessed it - was everything to him. So when he went to unbecoming lengths to maintain his reputation, and was caught out, his Clan sent him away. The Myconid elder taught Oskar to detach himself from matters that were, in time, only temporary. Reputation was fleeting, and failure as much a transient state as success. Just as the body died and fed the land, so too must a person accept hardship, and learn from it. Even when one uses wisdom to extend the time desired between states, they must all fall away, in time.

                                                During what became Oskar's initiation into druidic mysteries, the Myconids gave him special psychedelic mushrooms. The resulting trips (or "vision quests") expanded the dwarf's mind, providing the shift in perspective the elder had attempted to teach. Perceptions expanded, Oskar explored matters both physical and spiritual. As his training played out, years of exposure to the Myconid body and telepathic spores caused his own body to become a carrier for fungus. With experience, he learned to channel and control this fungus.

                                                When the elder Myconid revealed that nothing more could be learned there, Oskar departed the company of his adopted Clan. He wanders the land, seeking new experiences and the chance to bring the world into balance. And perhaps, if his understanding broadens and his deeds are great, he will regain his honor in truth.

                                                        65) ​Vindon Eldermead:

The true power was inside them all along! Unfortunately, that is not the case for Vindon, who's power actually does come from the ancient weapon he keeps in his possession.

                                                        Once a simple halfling from the Eldermead family, Vindon was looking at a profitable, yet boring future as the owner of a small brewing company. But like many young boys, he often found himself longing for adventure and would frequently wander off to explore nearby ruins and caves in the forest. It was during one such exploration that he came upon an old wooden box, half buried in a hollowed out tree stump and practically covered in moss. After wrenching it free from the ground, he cracked it open to find a beautifully decorated sword resting inside. A sword that began speaking to him the very moment he grabbed it!

                                                        Before he even realized it, Vindon had soon become a bonefied adventurer, traveling across hills and planes in search of evil to fight. In fact, he got so caught up in the excitement that he doesn't even remember how to get back home! The sword still speaks to the little hero on a regular basis, instilling him with confidence and power that would be considered impossible for others of his kind to manifest. It has introduced itself to him as 'Hogarath, the Gleaming' and claims to be the soul of an ancient dragon who came within a hare's breadth of achieving divinity before their enemies imprisoned them within the blade he now carries. Regardless if such a tale is true or not, Hogarath has proven itself to be useful and trustworthy enough that Vindon continues to heed its instructions when presented with them.
