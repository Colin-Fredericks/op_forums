            Technically it's not original, but it was heavily modified from the events in the novel. The novel presents things from the sheriff's perspective as he investigates the body (notably the gay couple in the novel are significantly older while their attackers are actually younger which leads into); which was both beaten severely and tossed into the canal, but also had a suspicious wound on his chest. The teens all claim they did harass the couple, but a psycho clown did the killing; as the adults of Derry are under IT's influence they don't normally acknowledge Pennywise's actions and rationalize them away. In the novel, since it's mostly related in-character after-the-fact, what really happened isn't fully clear (though the movie's version of events is entirely in-keeping with what the novel's imply through the other gay man Don's version of events). Obviously multiple interviews would have eaten up a lot of screen time, and the movies drop the C-plot of the sheriff starting to believe in Pennywise/IT again which threatens the hold on the minds of the town's adults slip so presenting it in the form of the investigation doesn't have the same narrative importance.

            So, in the novel, there's significant room for interpretation (though it's clear Derry has a significant homphobia problem though given when the book was written it's a bit more matter of fact as homophobia was far more tolerated in 1984 and Don is a Derry native that decided to be much more obvious about his sexuality than the police are presented as finding "appropriate") on how far the altercation went before Pennywise got involved due to it happening essentially "off screen;" though even the worse interpretation of what happened in the books is less vicious than the movies. As the whole book is an allegory for how bigotry insinuates its way into culture and adults internalize discrimination as normal and thus unremarkable instead of actually stopping the social ills that keep new generations locked in violent belief systems, the book repeatedly tells us to take the children (even teens old enough to be charged as adults in a murder case) at their word since they don't have the same blinders the adults do. Thus readers that want do not engage with the subtext of the whole story, and specifically this section of it (which in the novel comes in fairly early in the manuscript), can just take the teens' side of events that Pennywise bares the majority of the blame and the thus read the story as the attack didn't really get physical unlike the movie.

            NB: The miniseries from the 90s also completely cut this whole incident as well, which could influence people perceiving it as the movies making more of a political statement by adding on screen homophobic attacks.

                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                It's kinda like the shower scene in Psycho or the opening scene of Jaws. It feels extremely graphic and violent due to the skill in its execution, but if you watch it closely, you see how much the art is in letting people fill in the details. King's own public statements on it are also fairly blunt and unambiguous precisely because he got annoyed at people that refused to read into the subtext of the chapter's presentation and how it helps build up to the revelation of just how deep the rot in Derry goes. The attack comes up in the second chapter of the book, by the end of reading the book, what happened is fairly unambiguous, but it's part of building up the suspense and horror that early parts of the story only feel so obvious after you've finished the story.
