    The thumbs down emoji reaction

    08-22-2024, 08:17 AM
    This is not hugely important, but it's been in the back of my head for awhile now and I'd like to get it out.

    Am I the only one who's kind of bothered by the thumbs down reaction on the forum? I kind of liked when the Like feature was just a binary like or not, but the thumbs down is the one emoji reaction you can drive by and leave that's negative, and I've seen a bunch of them that just kind of leave a sour taste in my mouth. Obviously I don't expect everyone to agree with every post on the forum, but it's one thing to leave a reply expressing your disagreement, and another to just click an I Don't Like It button on the post and move on. I don't know, it feels like a less respectful tone. Is that just me?

              I think there's also a problem that the forums just had "like" for such a long time, that there's no forum culture about what the new options actually "mean."

              Are the "sick" and "angry" reactions something we interpret as an "enhanced like," that lets us say, "I like your post, but the topic of it upsets me," or do we interpret them as responses to the poster, "you make me sick/angry for posting that" when we see them used?

              We have no idea if a portion of the thumbs down emotes that seem to have gone up recently are trollish drive-by negativity, or someone that thinks a "thumbs down" to a negative post (like the Vent Thread) is actually a statement of agreeing with the poster instead of how it's taken by most of us as a sign of disagreement (though that doesn't explain some of the uses in the Gush thread... like seriously if there's any thread to not do that in... it's that one).

              Unfortunately they were also just kinda dropped on us randomly during an update with the new (thankfully much better) forum version we're using now. It wasn't a planned roll out where the admins/mods could have taken some time to address how they were expected to be used before people were already using them based on their own preconceptions on how to use them appropriately.

              I don't mind a more robust set of options then just "like." But I really do wish there was some attempt to make forum expectations on meaning clear so we all know what the default reaction will be treated as. It can be nice to just click an emote instead of writing up a post that's just a duplication of what someone else said, but "like" is the only one with an existing context most forum users here agree on from years of posting together. The rest are currently a source of uncertainty, which can fester.
