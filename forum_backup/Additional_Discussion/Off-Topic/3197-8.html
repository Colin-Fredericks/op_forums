                        S4E15 - Twilight Time

                        I see Twilight is starting to make a name for herself. I mean, she's only the 1000-year queen's chosen protégé, the literally ascended newest princess and saviour of the world like three times already (I don't count Chrysalis, because Canterlot isn't really the world), so I suppose a bunch of school kids thinking she's cool is a reasonable celebrity level, right? In all honestly, it's nice to see it at least slightly recognized.

                        What's even nicer, though, is seeing Twilight working. Yeah, I know, it's like she's a librarian or something. Huh, now when I think about it, has that ever been established in the show that it's a public library, or is that just something I picked up from the fanbase? Nevertheless, the role as teacher fits her amazingly well (I see she's taking cues from her own teacher), and I feel it does good for her character.

                        Oh, and I guess the CMC was in it too. As always, I find them rather flat. There was something to be said about showing the procedure of how social status develops, though. And how benefits beget obligation, and you either got to be able to deliver or be cynical enough to ignore it. Useful subject, actually.

                        Somehow I'm not surprised it's Sweetie Belle spearheading the episode, she must have taken some influence from Rarity. And, can I say, Pip's British accent? Is awesome.

                        I'll not call is super, but it's solid. It was logical and I liked Twilight's character. Overall, highly acceptable.

                                            Okay, let's talk about Fighting is Magic.

                                            Short recap in case someone doesn't know. Fighting is Magic was a fan-made 2d fighting game developed by underground developers Mane6, containing, well, the mane six beating the snot out of each other. The project was inspired by a couple of joke photoshop pictures, then went on to become a concept and eventually there emerged a full-fledged fighting game made in FighterMaker. It was a pretty big deal in the fandom a year and a half ago, having received endorsement from people like Lauren Faust and others working on the show. Unfortunately, it wasn't endorsed by Hasbro, who sent a C&D letter to Mane6 just a couple months before the projected release date. Cue outrage, upheaval and, most importantly, one dead game. After regrouping and acquiring the help of Faust herself, Mane6 decided to reforge their game in a non-pony form. They are currently working on that, and posted earlier this year they'll have a Kickstarter soon.

                                            Why am I bringing this up now? Well, currently on EqD, there's a news post containing a "tribute version" of the original, pony-based Fighting is Magic. It's made by an unknown group who's kept it on the low-down until completion (and EqD is currently getting some flak for blowing that cover, though I wonder how they had imagined distribution to work otherwise), and is based on leaked files from a Fighting is Magic alpha test, containing new work to fill in the content holes. This is likely the closest we're going to get to a pony fighting game.

                                            On the one hand, don't get me wrong: I raged along with the best of them over the C&D. I swear, I was honestly emotionally upset about it and it took me months to actually get over. In my opinion, it's the worst controversy in brony history, nevermind alicorn Twilight or Equestria Girls. But on the other hand, is this really smart? I have two argument for why it might have been better to just let it die.

                                            1) It might annoy Hasbro again. I'm not a lawyer, but I'm afraid they might come to Mane6, tell them they're not doing enough to keep their project dead and sue their asses. And it seems they too are worried about that, given this post from yesterday. From where I stand, I don't even see Hasbro having to be 100% correct in their charges, since Mane6 just won't have the means to dispute it. It seems to put unnecessary spotlight on Mane6 for a marginal profit.

                                            2) Let's not forget that Mane6 is actually developing a game for us. Much as they hated the C&D, they've said that it will be better in every way (and odds are it will, as they've upgraded from FighterMaker to the Z Engine used for Skullgirls), except the simple fact that it won't contain ponies. I feel like it's unhelpful to remind Mane6 that many of the fans will take a technically worse game as long as it contain ponies. It seems like it's a dick move.

                                            I'll admit, though, I never thought I'd get to hear the RC88's background music on top of an actual fight. I can't say I'm not happy about that.

                                                S4E16 - It Ain't Easy Being Breezy

                                                This episode was written by Natasha Levinger, the new writer who also gave us Pinkie Apple Pie earlier this season. That episode had the highest joke and reference saturation out of any episode yet, and was also one of my favourites this season. Can this one stand up to it?

                                                Yes. Yes, it can. But it also has problems.

                                                First, the good things. God DAMN those things are cute! And, more importantly, they add to the scope of the pony world. World building helps the show feel fresh, at least when it's credibly done, and by the standards of this show, it certainly qualifies for well-written. Also, the episode has an element of Equestria-specific culture and biology, which I adore. Things like Hearts and Hooves Day and Hearth's Warming is derived from our real world customs, but the Winter Wrap Up and Running of the Leaves spring from the fact that the ponies have to manipulate the nature around them on their own. This little fact seems to have slipped by the wayside, and I'm sort of thinking that's due to Faust leaving the show, but I'm always happy when we get some sort of quirky natural phenomenon at least. Admittedly, it's a bit strange that the Breezies would have plants which they have to go to another world to get pollen for, that really makes no ecological sense but fuck it, it's magic!

                                                What are the problems? Well... I never felt the conflict was credible. Right, Fluttershy is a nice host. That's really not enough to make anyone willing to risk a whole year stranded in another world. Sure, take a break, catch your breath, but they should be more eager to go on home. This in turn gives the "rainbow lesson" with Sea Breeze the same kind of problem; okay, he was strong-worded, but for the rest of them to just obstinately refuse? I don't buy it. And speaking of the rainbow lesson, we once more see that Fluttershy is the same one-trick pony as always. No other pony is as type-cast as her. It's always overcoming shyness, one way or the other. Admittedly, this time it's comparatively well done, but still. All the other ponies have at least a couple of typical perspectives. For Fluttershy, it's really only the one.

                                                All in all, the episode is good. I wager the fanbase will be split on the issue, but I quite like the style that Levinger is showing in her two episodes so far. I can tell a marked difference between her and others' work. Her language and dialogue is snappier and less forced than in some episodes. She dares to go new places, bring up new ideas. I'm a fan.
