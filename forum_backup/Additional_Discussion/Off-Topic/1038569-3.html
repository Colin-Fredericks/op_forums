                    While I've been hesitant to join this one (I think most of you can safely assume why), I'm giving in.

                    There's a saying I like when thinking about the cycle of violence: "The sword comes into the world because of justice delayed, and justice denied." This means that, like Charlaquin I find the Brandenburg test insufficient for the service of justice. It delays the application of justice, which will only lead to the need for force and violence later. We cannot prevent violence by delaying justice for those speech is meant to harm because the harm is not immediate. We can only delay things.

                    The "Nazi threshold standard," to me sounds a lot like, "Oh good, I might die before people care about doing anything." Remember that the US Civil War was not started to end slavery and make black people equal, and WWII was not started to stop the genocide of innocent minorities. Those were causes that were added to the propaganda of one side to prop up their moral arguments for their staying in the fight. Lincoln might have thought slavery was a moral wrong, but he still thought that black people were an inferior race. The Allies might have said slaughtering non-combatants was wrong, but that didn't stop them from denying refugees safety from the Nazis or killing German civilians. If we hit this supposed Nazi threshold, it means we've already failed people (and I'm in one of the groups that Nazis like to target).

                    For me Nazi rhetorical is not some hypothetical harm either. When someone advocates for Nazi ideology, it is psychologically traumatic to me, because they're saying that I'm subhuman, that people that treat me nicely are bad, and that people that torture me and put me to forced labor until I die are saints. They're saying that because they know it hurts me.

                    And yes, I know there's usually some point about how it's just words and I'm giving them that power over me, blah, blah, blah. Screw that. I might have those tools now as an adult. I didn't have those tools as a kid while I sat in fear of being seen while Neo-Nazi's loudly proclaimed their desire to harm people like me on a public bus, and nobody did jack shit; not even ask them to stop. That trauma doesn't just up and disappear to let me blow off Nazi shitheels talking shit now.

                    If someone was around to punch one of those assholes, well which is the lesser evil? Some skinhead getting decked, or a scared 12 year old not being traumatized for life because they saw that random strangers that didn't have to do anything standing up and doing something? Which is the worse ripple effect? A trio of Nazis realizing that a bus full of mostly white people like the idea of them getting punched more than they like them talking about beating up non-white people and Jews, or all the better things I never did because I was constantly struggling with trust issues after seeing that busload of people ignore it just like normal German people ignored the Nazis that would eventually come for my family?

                    Does violence beget violence? Sort of. But sometimes speech is violence.
