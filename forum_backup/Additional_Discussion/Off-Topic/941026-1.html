                Outfitting an entire football team is several thousand dollars and usually done through extensive fundraising. Some schools have contracts with major sporting goods companies like Nike, or advertising from others, because their games bring in a lot of people. Most of that money goes back into sports or other extracurricular activities, like band.

                That's high school, though. And not every high school by a longshot.

                Elementary school sports, middle school sports, aren't usually getting all this money.

                As for why it's allowed to happen, teachers are generally only able to teach. When a parent can't pay to outfit their child, or doesn't care enough to ensure they have the required supplies, then the burden falls on the school and most schools can't equip every student. It's not uncommon for teachers to do it, bringing in extra supplies for those who they know won't have them.

                But in the end we're talking about pencils, pens, maybe a backpack, some notebooks. Calculator, maybe a flash drive. While classes that operate entirely on computers are more common now than they were in the 90s, they're still not everywhere. Usually when they do have classes like that there's also classroom or school-use tablets. You can also bring your own to school, I believe, if you have an issue with school-supplied devices. There's an article about the shift to electronic devices, and the problems that can roadblock it, you can read here.

                Though, again, it's not something every school district could even pull off.

                    On the kids gear thing, especially when it comes to technology, other factors come into play.

                    If a school says "you must have a PC laptop of X specifications" then the first thing you're going to run into is the fact that most of the parents don't understand the requirements. They're going to go to an electronics store and say their kid needs a laptop for school.

                    From that, some are going to try to upsell the parents on the most expensive and flashy bit of gear, using the pitch that it's needed or will help the child. Of course that means an expensive thing that's going to break in a year or two, if it's not stolen.

                    Others are going to assume something completely different and sell them an apple instead of a PC, or the cool new tablet or whatever, which is going to be incompatible with the decades plus old software the school is using, making it useless.

                    Still others are going to use the parents as a way to dump obsolete and outdated gear, generally at an inflated price.

                    And that's just the price of the average level of unawareness of tech specs. You're also going to get the parents that KNOWINGLY buy the overpowered machines, can't afford anything but the third-hand decade old laptop, or have political or tech preferences that they will enforce upon their children (No child of mine is going to use a samsung product!)
