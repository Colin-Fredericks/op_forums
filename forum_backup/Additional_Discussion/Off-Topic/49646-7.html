            Azreal:

            I too have had a lack of relationships, and spent a long time emotionally where you are. I'm 27 as well, but have kind of grown out of it. The older I've gotten I've simply devoted more free time into personal development and learning. Learn to meditate, read more books, study up on philosophy. Try to see the world from different points of view. I've come to cherish my independence so much that I even portion out my time to my friends, being very selfish with it. What I do I do for me.

            And I really do get where you're coming from, I mean that, I was a wreck for years, and I -never- -never- talked to anyone about it, I bottled it up and let it simmer on the inside and it really fucked me up. I did have a series of events that kind of "cured" me, but the biggest one was learning to work on me and learning to meditate. I firmly believe that we only get what we're ready for, and you have to work on you before you're ready for a relationship.

            It wasn't until I went through my big "spiritual meditation learning phase," that I met the perfect girl, and had a lovely relationship for 3 years. That ended too though, in the end it became too codependent, and we smothered each other. From the outside we had the perfect relationship, and everyone was shocked when we quietly ended it after a lengthy discussion about how we lost our own identities to the idea of our relationship.

            Just another lesson I'm happy to have learned. Since then I've turned down two advances for a relationship because I know exactly what I want from a relationship now that would add to what I have. I no longer desire simply to be in a relationship for it's own sake, it has to be the right kind to add to what I already have going on, which is pretty enjoyable constant alone time. It's kind of like Carl Jungs idea of individualism, that through becoming the most ourselves, we eventually will see how best to integrate into our societies.

            So too it is with relationships, by becoming the best you that you can be, you will eventually see what you actually want, not just what you think you want.

            If nothing else, read up on transcendental meditation. Or if you want to low ball it, read Eckhart Tole's "The Power of Now," and try to absorb the good bits. The biggest change I had in my life was learning to turn off my brain's rampant and constant need to tell me what it though would make me happy. Imagine at minimum being about to at least stop thinking about all this shit without your control. Hey, it worked for me.

            I wish you all the best,
            Mark
