    Off the current topic of the thread, but very much in-topic...

    I keep seeing people in other boards (Exalted, WoD, etc.) talking about others in their group going through the books and stuff to look up what Charm, Power, etc., they are going to use next. After D&D 4e, I've started following the "combat card" method of writing descriptions* of all the things my character has on index cards for easy perusal during play. It's so much faster than having to go through a book all the time.




    *Resource cost, dice pool with difficulty, short description of what succeeding does, that sort of thing. It wouldn't really work with Mage (either one, from my understanding), but it's really useful for so many systems.

        You know, we’ve now seen character sheets for a 1st level fighter and wizard, so we can directly compare the two.

        Starting from the top and working down, both have the same Perception bonus. Fighter has 4 more HP, and that’s with the same Ancestral HP and Con bonus, so that gap is going to get wider every level. Fighter has 4 more AC (5 more if they both raise their shields) and two more TAC. Fighter is an Expert in Fort and Ref saves and Trained in Will saves, where Wizard is Expery in Fort and Will and Trained in Ref, so a wash there. Overall, the Fighter is significantly hardier.

        Moving on to Strikes, Fighter’s most damaging Melee Strike has +6 to hit and does 1d8+4 , his most damaging Ranged Strike has +6 to hit abs does 1d6, +1d10 if he hits by 10 or more. Wizard’s most damaging Melee Strike doeshas +1 to hit 1d8, though he has the option of a +3 to hit, 1d4 damage Melee Strike for when accuracy is most important. His most damaging Ranged Strike has +3 to hit and does 1d8. Again, the advantage definitely goes to the Fighter here.

        Wizard is Trained in 7 skills to Fighter’s... 3? 5? This part’s a little unclear from this sheet, cause I can’t tell where the Athletics and Acrobatics values are coming from. Probably an armor penalty. Either way, advantage Wizard here.

        For at-will abilities, the fighter can Stride twice his speed and make one Strike for two actions instead of 3, or raise a +2 AC and TAC, Hardness 5 shield for 1 action. As a reaction, he can raise his shield if he didn’t as an action, block 5 incoming damage up to twice before breaking his shield, or make an attack of opportunity. Wizard can spend 2 Actions to make an Attack against TAC for 1d4 +1 splash, to find out if there’s a magic item in 30 feet, or to make an unattended object glow in an 20 foot radius, or 1 action to raise a +1 AC and TAC, hardness 4 shield. As a reaction he can block up to 4 damage once before losing his shield. I’m giving this one to the Wighter, although the Wizard has some out of combat versatility the Fighter lacks, the Fighter has much better at-will combat options.

        Once per day each, the Wizard can use 1-3 actions to deal an automatic 1d4+1 damage per action at a range, and 2 actions to do 2d6 damage in a 20-foot cone (DC 5 Ref to avoid double damage, 15 for half damage, 25 for no damage). Once per day, he can use one of the above a second time. Presumably he knows other spells not listed here that he could have prepared instead. Obviously advantage Wizard here, because the Fighter has no daily abilities to compete.

        Personally, I think I’m fine with that at first level. The Wizard is more versatile, and can get a couple big damage spikes per day, while the fighter is much hardier and has a much higher baseline damage output. It doesn’t even take all that many combat rounds for the Fighter to out perform the Wizard’s DPR even with his spells. Of course, 5-minute workdays will ruin this balance, but what else is new.

        Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.
