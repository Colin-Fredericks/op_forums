                                        I have begun to think that while not every gifted person is depressed, nor is every depressed person gifted, depression is a statistically predictable outcome of being gifted. I have been blessed to know a lot of brilliant people in my life, way more than I have a right to (thanks, internet), and the common threads have led me to wonder how many people outside my anecdotal experience have been born smarter and more talented than the rest of us, only to hit a moment as they grow up where the gifts they'd put so much stock in, had at least partially defined themselves by, failed to solve some critical problem and left them with a tiny wound if self-doubt; then another, and another, and another, one piling stop the others, probably in a school environment where the kids around them hate them for being different and teach them thereby to hate themselves and the gifts that make them different, each hurtful experience made hard to forget by the basic nature of their above-average abilities to remember making the wounds still hurt like they're fresh with each recollection.

                                        I wonder how many gifted people retreat into nihilism because they know their gifts can change the world and make it better, but also know that people do not want the world to change or improve, and because they're ashamed to think of themselves as having good ideas that could help people, they learn to be silent and begin to slowly but persistently hate themselves for feeling they've betrayed the responsibility of their gifts, because they believe they could be and do better but are too weak to stand against an entire world that hates them.

                                        I don't suppose I'm really going anywhere with this post. I don't have an action to call any of my readers to, nor some bigger point to make than just wondering how many smart people are tearing themselves down. I guess I just want to say, if you're out there and hating yourself, whether you're gifted or not, I'm sorry for what you're going through.
