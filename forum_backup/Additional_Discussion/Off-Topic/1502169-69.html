                                It doesn't even help the wealthy. It might in the short term, and we know how much current economics has corporations obsessed with the quarterly bottom-line no matter what the long term consequences; since they can easily shift the costs onto other people. The whole reason tariffs and excise taxes went from the backbone of international commerce to a tool of diplomatic coercion is because when there's an economic collapse (and there's always going to be another one under basic capitalism) even the wealthy can't avoid it.

                                The rich can only escape the eventual bust of a tariff scheme if the tariffs are targeted more specifically: the more practical domestic production is, the easier it is for them to push off the costs.

                                For example, a 20% tariff on coffee imports would pretty much bankrupt huge swathes of the service industry. The US consumes 300 times more coffee that we're capable of producing (since the only state that can grow coffee at scale is Hawaii there isn't much room for expansion). Coffee would get prohibitively expensive for average folk, and there's no "bail out" that's possible because there simply isn't the domestic supply. Billions in the markets would vanish, and companies wouldn't even have the money to pay out the golden parachutes to their C-suite idiots.

                                Across the board tariffs means you can't even contain it to specific industries either. Even industries that would remain viable without lots of imports ballooning costs suffer because the investor class will skew shit and screw it all up. A rush on corn futures as investors dump import reliant items like my coffee example isn't sustainable either.

                                Like... we know what happens with this. The Smoot-Hawley Act with tried to use tariffs to end the Great Depression only made it so much worse. It didn't equalize things or anything that drastic, but huge amounts of the wealthy class's worth were wiped out in just a few years once the retaliatory tariffs started kicking in. There's history that shows what happens in response to this stuff. It's just bad for everyone.

                                The only possible benefit this has is to someone with power over tariffs and a desire to be perceived as "strong" by being a geopolitical economic bully. And even trying to make yourself feel good by bullying people around isn't exactly some perpetual happiness machine either.

                                                    I know multiple people (including family members) that have either moved to other countries either as ex-pats or on the path to full naturalization. So yeah, it happens.

                                                    The problem is most people that want to leave don't have options. It's expensive and emotionally taxing to uproot yourself and possibly family even to move to a much lower cost of living country. Most countries have very strict immigration laws as well. One of my in-laws lived abroad for two years hoping to get accepted for a naturalization program but in the end was rejected and had to come back to the US ... basically leaving her dead broke and relying on family support for years getting back on her feet here.

                                                    That's the thing with a lot of people just talking big about it online. They, broadly, have not done their research. They don't have their papers in order. They don't know which countries are actively seeking immigrants and (crucially) in what fields. Unless things get so bad that other countries start accepting US citizens as refugees, it takes months or even years just to have everything in order to even try to go somewhere else. Hence most online chatter about it as all talk. It's people that think moving to Canada is the same as moving from New Jersey to New York.

                                                    I have the dubious privilege of being from a family of all refugees. All of my grandparents (either as adults or as children) fled conflicts - sometimes enduring some severe hardships - to get to the US. I've heard all of their stories about what it took. Since I was eighteen I've been sure to have what I need to get out of the US just in case. If I finally decide it's time to pull out one of those plans, something else I know is that means I'm not announcing it online before I do. I'll talk about it after my long term residency is validated.
