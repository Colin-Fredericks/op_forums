                                                            I would need some serious convincing to consider the writers negotiating a pay raise the problem with Hollywood right now (the movie studio CEOs pay rates aren't secret, and they clearly don't feel like tightening their belts).

                                                            If there's a serious slump, there's two vastly bigger factors that Occam's Razor points to over anything else:

                                                            1) The continual dissociation between the C-suite and what the company actually does. This is something we see across multiple sectors. The people at the top have been increasingly divorced from any real connection to the products their companies make, and are thus far more focused on what they do know: making the money line go up. What's going on at Boeing is a great example: A few decades ago most of the corporate executives at Boeing were engineers or pilots at some point in their careers. Since the people that understood why certain costs were important to keep paying to keep making great airplanes were in positions of authority, Boeing maintained its status as a high quality brand. However, the company (specifically after a major merger in '97) has steadily been taken over by people who only know business. Since they don't understand why this redundancy is important, or why you can't increase the interval between checks any further without increasing risks to unacceptable levels, Boeing is now famous for planes that fall apart midair.

                                                            We're seeing this all over the place. There's plenty examples in Hollywood just looking at the utter mess of a company Warner Bros has turned into.

                                                            2) The impact of Covid on audience viewing habits and a failure of big movie studios to adjust. 2020-2023 have been the worst box office grossing year since 2005. While people not going to the movies seems to be changing, we're still well below where the trend line was headed from 2015-2019. A lot of people like streaming the newest movies at home. "Going to the theaters" is more of a thing done once a month or two, instead of a few times a month Studios have generally been failing to entice people to go back to the older ways of consuming films.

                                                            The only major adjustment (and this started during Covid, not after the recent WGA strike) is that the studios are putting out less films. Though an interesting note is they're averaging better returns. That is, with less films to go see, people are actually going and seeing more of what comes out each year. So if the lesson the big studios has gotten is less films and focus on ones that make the most money, the "slump" is just a reaction to the 2010s completely over saturating the market.
