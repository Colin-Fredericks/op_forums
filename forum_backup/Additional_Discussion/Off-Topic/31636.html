    Non-WW RPGs we play and love

    12-17-2013, 10:08 AM
    Apologies if there's a similar thread somewhere; I couldn't find it.

    This is sort of what I expected from the "Games Discussion" thread; I've contributed there, too, but I'm curious as to what other tabletop RPGs people play around here.

    I'm a big, huge TSR-era D&D nerd. I like 3e and 4e well enough, as a player anyway (playing a 3.5e/d20 L5R game right now), but it's TSR-era stuff and its OSR derivatives that really get me going. Of the original crop, I favor the D&D Rules Cyclopedia; on the OSR front I am a big, huge fan of Adventurer Conqueror King System (ACKS), which pretty much feels like the D&D RC capped at level 14 and honed to a razor-fine edge, and of Astonishing Swordsmen & Sorcerers of Hyperborea (AS&SH), which feels like AD&D 1e or OD&D by way of Weird Tales pulps and Tales From The Crypt comics. But right now I'm running Swords & Wizardry Complete for a classic, OD&D-powered, super-deadly dungeon-crawling romp.

    I am absolutely, head over heels in love with Runequest 6th edition. In a review elsewhere, I called it "the Cadillac of Runequests" and I stand by that assesment. It's become my go-to game for non-D&D fantasy. I can run pretty much any fantasy setting I know or imagine with this hefty but clear tome of goodness.

    Call of Cthulhu is a classic, I know. I've used it to run Mythos and non-Mythos horror scenarios to great effect. I've done subdued investigative games and pulpy shoot-cultist-in-the-face cheap thrill romps. I love it that the game supports both.

    Mongoose Traveller is a game I am very much new to, and I ran it only once, and I'd love to revisit it. I love the "gambling" feel of character generation.

    Savage Worlds was my go-to system for a while, and I'm a bit burned out on it, but my players have been begging for a return of my wildly successful Day After Ragnarok campaign. I've been pondering whether to do it with the "gritty combat" option from the new SW Deluxe, or whether to try something else (I know there's a FATE version around, but I'm not much of a FATE fan).

    Wild Talents I've been meaning to try for a while now. I have yet to find a supers system that I love and I suspect WT might be the one.

    Eclipse Phase I've also yet to play. I had little contact with transhuman SF before buying it and it got me reading a few of the classics; I should really find the time to run it. Maybe as a radically different follow-up to my current D&D dungeon slog.

    Last but not least, I'm a huge fan of Palladium RPGs in general and Rifts in particular. I find that the system generally isn't as bad as the Internet would have it, just abysmally presented. A few charts (like they used in the old Palladium Fantasy RPG 1e) would go a long, long way towards making the game easier to run and play, as well as cleaning up a few contradictory rulings (e.g. attacks per round, burst fire). I've tried conversions in that past but really, nothing beats having a dragon and a hobo in the same adventuring party, with the hobo roleplaying circles around the dragon by dint of a clever and glib player.

              GURPS is my preferred system. I use various editions of D&D (mainly 3.5 and 4 these days), because the systems are still a distinct part of player expectations, no matter how broken they can be.                   Fourth Edition D&D is my number one non-WW RPG. It wasn't the first edition I played, but it was the first I could call my own, and I especially enjoyed how the system of powers was set up.

Every class and every monster operates using the same basic system of powers. Under that framework, even the most exotic material (ex. psionics) could be implemented without much difficulty.

                                  I like the Warhammer 40k universe a lot, as well as the respective games. Unfortunately, I'm not a huge fan of the system. Too many charts! When I run Warhammer 40k games, I tend to just toss out the system and use something else.

                                                          I started roleplaying on Dungeons and Dragons (3.0 to 3.5) but when I came to university I absolutely fell in love with White Wolf games in particular World of Darkness. However, despite all its issues DnD has remained something of a favourite for me and I still play it semi-regularly. Though I've tried 4th Edition and while it was enjoyable I wasn't particularly sold on it over all, I have in the last few months got reading the various pathfinder supplements and absolutely adore what they've done with archetypes and the additional classes (I love Gunslinger and Alchemist in particular). I'm also a big fan of the Iron Kingdoms setting (and Warmachine of course) but I've not got a chance to try out or look at in any depth their new roleplaying game.

                                                          In the last 18 months I was exposed to and have run Eclipse Phase, which is another absolutely beautiful game that changed my opinion on percentile systems. Its one that I have every intention of running for a longer period of time in future given that I actually managed to freak my players out last time (huzzah).
                                                          I'm also a big fan of Mutants and Masterminds by Green Ronin and am still playing the second edition, my only issue is character generation is a chore and its hard to keep games from becoming completely and absolutely rediculous which often ruins my intention of running the grittier and more serious side. I've taken a break from running the game for anything more than one shots, its still one of my favourite systems and proves infinietly enjoyable so long as players stick to their concepts/themes. I'm also intending to run alongside a friend of mine a Dragonage game but I need to look at the system more.
                                                          I recently began playing L5R (4th ed) which so far I'm loving for the game itself, the interesting mechanics and the setting.
