            I can see how the message could be interpreted this way, but I don't think that's what was meant. At least, I choose not to see it that way, if only for consistency's sake, as that would be in complete contradiction with the previous (very strong) moral, where Fluttershy realizes that it's ok to keep up her pattern of skipping out on her friend's annual get together in favor of doing her own thing.

            Like with Amending Fences, parts of this one were a little hard to watch, as I've been exactly where Twilight was...hell, given that this forum is a hive of geekery, I'd bet that many, if not most, of us have! So I can see where some of these issues are sore spots, but I still praise the series for Going There, and for depicting genuine Emotion in its characters, probably more than in any previous season! Seeing Twilight finally break down like that, much like Moondancer did (and Luna in Magic Sheep) really brings home why this show is head-and-hooves above other shows of its kind.

            That said, it's still not *perfect*, by any means: All of those above examples spent so much time on the comedic filler, that the actual climax and aftermath felt really rushed; unfortunately, the makers are still playing it safe when it comes to their demographic and what they feel they can safely handle.

            As for the rest of the ep, my main problem was with Discord: He is normally Funny, to the point that his eps tend to be the funniest of the season. However, in order to get the audience to empathize with Twilights' not getting his running gags, he was given incredibly lame humor here, specifically so that we wouldn't laugh either. Which makes for boring watching, but...well, I guess that's the only way to really make this sort of thing work, maybe?

            Also, the ending, attempting to vindicate Twilight or something, by putting Discord in her position, felt really weak and labored. Also, I didn't mind the reveal that he had deliberately caused it all, but it seems like it might have been more in character for him to leave in a huff rather than just shrug and Hugs All Around like he did...

                            Wow, I swear. While I've never felt that my education has been more useful than this last month, I've also never felt it's been this tiring. Which means I've slipped behind on pony. Since the S5 finale is apparently coming up this weekend, I'll just do a blitzthrough to make sure I'm not missing anything.

                            S5E17 - Brotherhoof Social

                            I finally watched it. Why did it take so long? For some reason I had gotten into my head that it would be awful. It wasn't. At all. Big Mac's act was a bit grating at times, but it was mostly really funny and quite touching at the end. Damn, I can be stubborn at times.

                            S5E22 - What About Discord?

                            Yeah, what about him? This episode just fell flat for the first 2/3. It wasn't even bad, it just failed to engage me at all. Discord managed to be bland, how about that? True, it did pick up towards the end when Twilight started jumping to conclusions, and Discord being devious is pretty satisfying, but they could have made it at least not dull for the other part.

                            S5E23 - The Hooffields and McColts

                            Oh yeah, this is more like it. Twilight's princesshood has been a recurring thing in the background this whole season, and I love how they play with her insecurities and bookishness at once in this episode. Twilight as the theoretical diplomat and Fluttershy as the ground-level humanitarian (or... animalist, I suppose?) makes a nice contrast between them, and I think there's a relevant comparison to the current refugee crisis (even if that's not connected just to the war in Syria). Overall, the map episodes have been at worst good and often outstanding, and I think the gimmick can last another season at least.

                            S5E24 - The Mane Attraction

                            So this is the final piece from Amy Keating-Rogers, huh? Maybe I'm wrong about that and she's writing the finale as well. If it's true, however, I can see no fault in this episode being her topping off her time on pony. This was strong and emotional. Lena Hall did a great job as Ra-Ra, and while I wasn't too impressed with the song when it was spoiled at ComicCon half a year ago, it benefited from having the context of the plot around it. Also, hey, it's Applejack!

                            Very well. I am now all caught up, and ready for the finale. Bring it!

                                        S5E25-26 - The Cutie Re-Mark

                                        Sorry, just a moment. I'm looking for something. Won't be a minute.

                                        Ah, right, here it is.

                                        Originally posted by Weimann View Post
                                        did anyone really think a war threat would ever actually be realised in MLP?
                                        Huh. Would you look at that. It's funny how I'm made to eat my words on this, of all things. I thought this at least would be a safe bet.

                                        Naturally, I'm still not sure the scenes in the season finale can be called a "realised" war scenario, since it's only a few clips in an alternate timeline. But for that short clip, they did manage to show not only the combat but also the structural consequences on Equestrian life. This isn't a "good guys win in decisive battle" thing. For about a minute, we saw a drawn-out conflict between nations, and how that affected society at large. I can honestly say there's not much I would have expected less from My Little Pony.

                                        Since it seems I've started on the different alternate timelines, I might as well finish them. The Sombra timeline was easily the best one to me. It carried so much potential, and I'm sure it will be explored extensively in fanfics. Likewise, the Queen Chrysalis (they managed to say her name this time) timeline was similarly evocative, but we weren't told much of the bigger picture. Is this the last remaining resistance to Chrysalis' offensive? Or is it one of many rogue cells that fight a guerilla war against a still stabilizing new queen? This is all open to interpretation, which is a good thing in this case, but it doesn't mean I would have liked to know a bit more about the context.

                                        And from there to Nightmare Moon's timeline, which is... actually kind of lazy in comparison. My problem here is that we're not really told anything we didn't already know (except for her taking up residence in the old castle and imprisoning Celestia in the moon for revenge, though I'm sure someone had already imagined that as well). We only got to see her and seven guard ponies, with no new information about the world at all. They trusted her character to carry the whole timeline, and it didn't really do that. It felt unsatisfying in comparison.

                                        Of course, the last three ones were only there so they could say those characters had been included. The Flim-Flam one was a pure joke. Nevertheless, it made the episode stand out like few others in terms of sheer spectacle, only really rivalled by the other season endings. And all of it is fanservice. Awesome, awesome fanservice that I'm still somewhat reeling from (I've seen the episode at least five times, it's that good), but still just something to stroke our sensationalist desires. What about the actual conflict?

                                        What about Starlight Glimmer?

                                        One thing that makes this episode stand out even among the premiers and finales is that Twilight is alone. Every other time, the mane six have tackled any problem as a unit (or, well, at least all been involved). This time, it's Twilight vs Starlight. And that is because Starlight is smaller scale, so one against six wouldn't make sense. Yes, she has tremendous power and is a schemer second to none. She's still small scale; she's not after world domination, like most other villains have been. Her problem with Twilight is that Twilight wrecked her gig out in the desert where literally no one knew there were even ponies. This is all about her personal revenge. That's what I love about her. She's petty and prideful, and she doesn't even know about the consequences of her actions. This gives Twilight the possibility to be the Big Damn Hero without necessarily turning Starlight into a monster. I can honestly relate to her (note, not support her: she was clearly doing some messed up shit in that village, but I can put myself in her shoes and imagine how I would feel).

                                        The fact that we get to see Starlight's past is also a contributing factor. How many other villains do we know the history of, by which I mean their personal experience rather than a record of their actions. The scene between Sunburst and Starlight might seems menial and perfunctory (and not saying it isn't, this really could have used a minute more), but it's eminently important none the less as it establishes a personal reason for Starlight's behaviour other than "for teh evulz, lol".

                                        Sidenote here: it wasn't my idea, I picked it up from a reactor I like to watch, but wouldn't it be perfect if Sunburst had been Sunset Shimmer instead? We know she was sent to Celestia's school as a prodigy, and it's totally a plausible turn of events given her previous personality. Of course, the moment they even hinted that EqG might even resemble canon is the day when they have to hire bodyguards. But just imagine! It would be so sweet tho!

                                        The situation is resolved into redemption, which given the establishment of Starlight's character is surprisingly credible. Once again, not friendship rainbow laser. I am happy about this.

                                        Speculations have been going around about Starlight's place in the show next season. Will she be Twilight's "faithful student" and be a new member of the mane cast? Will she be a sometimes character that get an episode a season? I'm open for a closer relationship with her, and I think there are some plot seeds already sown (the aforementioned Sunburst, for example).

                                        In light of all this, I hereby dub Starlight Glimmer Best Villain. Because she managed to make me actually care what became of her without that making her any less threatening.

                                        Alright, just a few more shoutouts, then I'm done. Kelly Sheridan, Starlight's voice work was excellent. Animation team, you consistently blow me away; particularly the scene where Twilight is trapped in a crystal while Starlight is monologuing is super, because while so much straight-up talk could get boring to watch, the fact that the crystal is slipping sloooowly through the clouds makes the tension remain. Such a simple move, such a great effect. (All they had to do was suddenly and drastically change how clouds work, but, y'know, magic.)

                                        The song was meh.

                                        In the end, I think the thing I liked the best about this episode was that it still, after five years, managed to surprise me. Not only be good and meet my expectations, but actually throw a curve all out of left field. That is my beacon of hope for the next season.
