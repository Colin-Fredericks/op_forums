                    I am reminded of the last speech of Ben Franklin at the Constitutional Convention (he was too sick and old and had a friend say it for him). Part of what he said was:

                    "... I agree to this Constitution, with all its faults - if they are such; because I think a general Government necessary for us, and there is no form of government but what may be a blessing to the people, if well administered; and I believe, farther, that this is likely to be well administered for a course of years, and can only end in despotism, as other forms have done before it, when the people shall become so corrupted as to need despotic government, being incapable of any other."

                    I find myself agreeing with old Ben. The problems we are facing here in in the US (and in Italy and in Britain and so on) are not problems with forms of government. The problems are with a populace that is changing in fundamental ways, and is becoming (using Franklin's terminology) "corrupted". In a democratic form of government, people have to be willing to compromise their own ideals for the sake of the general good, but that often isn't what's happening. Powerful groups on all sides of the political spectrum in many nations have learned that while they may not have the power to force through legislation they want, they can block legislation they don't want, even shutting everything down. They learned it in the US and also in Italy and elsewhere.

                    In a despotic form of government, this doesn't happen. The despot has the power to ram through whatever he wants no matter what the other powers in society may think about it. That's what despots do. But this does not happen in a democracy. Groups must be willing to lose once in awhile, or let their opponents win a little bit once in awhile, but they refuse to do that today. So they decide to stop everything and everybody ends up with blocked government, which makes those who are currently doing well very happy indeed - because no changes mean they will continue to do well.

                    People have to start being willing to lose and let the other guy win - the Republicans have certainly been the worst offenders in this regard, but even the Democrats are guilty of this. This sense of political gridlock is the biggest problem that many Americans have with their government (and Italians have with their government and many other people with their own governments).

                    I think it's safe to say we haven't reached the point in time that Ben Franklin was thinking of when the people became so corrupted that they needed a despotic government, but I think we are beginning to get there. Many Americans voted for Trump, not so much because they agreed with his views, but because they thought of him as a strong person who would get things done - someone who could tear through the bureaucratic mess that is Washington and bring badly needed change.

                    Basically, a lot of people voted for Trump because he has the dictatorial qualities that they have started to believe are necessary for an American leader because of the endemic problems with our form of government as they misunderstand what is happening. And while Trump isn't a dictator (I'd label him a Technocrat), the fact that this is the direction that a lot of Americans are beginning to turn is rather worrisome. And as long as there is a sense of political gridlock in Washington, those feelings on the part of the voters - that the government is inherently broken, are only going to continue to get worse and worse.
