    Originally posted by JimB View Post
    I wish I had saved a link to a story I found a month or two ago. A kid was on the phone with his boyfriend one night, making plans to come out to his parents the next day after school, but when he woke up, they had left him a note on the fridge that went something like, "We overheard you on the phone last night. No worries about coming out; just bring home some OJ when you come home. Love, your parents. PS, you and your boyfriend make a cute couple."

    That note is as close to accurately reproduced as I can recall (which means I did my best but can't vouch for it), and I was wondering, how would you guys feel about receiving that note? I'm torn. On the surface, it seems fine, but I think I'd be disappointed at losing the chance to make a big declaration for my own sake, you know, to take a stand and be brave. My suspicious mind would also wonder the circumstances of them overhearing me, and why they seem to be trying to talk me out of talking about my orientation and what it means to me; like, is it an attempt to leave it an unspoken secret that's swept under the rug? I also might resent that they tried to decide it's not a big deal when it's probably a very big deal for me. People die for being gay.

    But that's me, and I'm an angry sort of person. How would any of you feel?
    I'd be incredibly relieved, to tell the truth. In my opinion the big declaration, taking a stand and being brave, are not that important, compared to having this burden lifted from you. I'd still want to talk about it, and I'd still be nervous and have to gather up the courage to do even that, just because its an uncomfortable topic to talk about with your folks. I probably wouldn't think they were trying to avoid the conversation, I'd think they're just trying to give me the space I need before I'm ready to talk about it. Frankly, I'd be glad if they wouldn't consider it a big deal, because it shouldn't be. They got it right where so many others did not. One of the few things I loathe about being gay is that every time I tell someone its almost always made into a big deal, as if it isn't utterly normal. I'd rather people just acknowledge it and move on to a different topic. I won't get mad or anything when people don't, I figure its part of the times we live in now, its just a bit tiresome and sometimes frustrating. I try not to make a big deal out of it myself. Of course, it doesn't feel that way when you're just starting coming out to people, it does feel like a big deal. Those feelings are, actually, much more so than coming out itself, and you deserve to talk about them, get them off your chest. If you're not given the opportunity to do that, don't wait, just take it.

    I'd be pissed they overheard me on the phone though and I'd definitely start inquiring about that. Nothing quite like an utterly normal domestic dispute about privacy to underline that nothing's really changed between each other. I might 'forget' to bring the OJ too, while I'm at it. Depends on if I want OJ, really...

    All that being said I'm afraid I'm an odd duck too. I've had numerous conversations on the topic and most people don't share my opinion and/or feelings.

I'm comfortable knowing the basics so I sorta figure I'm done now, until I hit upon something that strikes me as new pushes me back into researchmode. I already know I won't have the time or energy to actually do so, so I'd basically be 'monogamous by default' but I feel like that shouldn't be much of a problem.

Its hard to find the line where a friend becomes a romantic interest, but not a sexual one, but I feel like I might already be in a couple of these relationships anyway.

                            Originally posted by Whim View Post
                            Tluthal, you gave me so much stuff to wiki just now...

                            Cielle, I'm crossing my fingers for you.

                            I came out almost two years ago to my parents, after everyone else in my life already knew and accepted it. I was terrified about how they would react to their son being gay. My uncertainty fueled my imagination, coming up with thousands of terrible scenarios. My fear and imagination kept me from coming out for so long. Before I finally did I'd made arrangements should the worst happen and I had someone I trusted with me for the strength to actually go through with it. As it turned out, it wasn't really necessary to make all these arrangements, they just said they didn't like it and that was that. It was oddly anticlimactic. To this day they pretend it isn't true, but I can deal with that. I came out for my own sake, not theirs. I didn't want their acceptance, I wanted to be freed from the burden of keeping this secret.

                            Though the arrangements proved useless, in this case, I'm glad I made them anyway. I made sure I was financially independent and living on my own, I told them in front of someone who didn't live in their house, I made other plans for the day so that people, who I had told about my plan, would worry if I didn't show up, and take measure, I told my neighbors to call emergency services if they heard me yell. I kept my shoes and my jacket on, sat close to the door and kept a table between me and my parents, just in case I needed to make a run for it. I know, its paranoid, but its important to have some measure of stability and safety in moments when you feel insecure about the situation. Maybe you don't want to go overboard like I did, but the general advice is sound.

                            When you're telling them, stay calm and take your time to say what you want to say. I told my parents I wanted to tell them something important and I did nothing but stare at my feet for some twenty minutes before I finally got the words out of my mouth.

                            I hope my little story and my advice are useful to you. I wish you all the luck in the world and I hope you can draw strength, if even just a sliver, from the knowledge that there are people out there who support you no matter what happens. Stay strong.
                            This is good, comprehensive advice. Honestly, I would not call you paranoid at all. Making sure you can function independently before sharing something that has ruined other parent-child relationships is just common sense.
