                                Last night, I treated myself to a double feature of Batman vs. Superman and Civil War:

                                BvS: Not as bad as I'd heard it was and, because I'd heard about specific complaints and was looking for them, I was able to see that some of them are actually explained in the movie, but the storytelling is pretty dense, so it's easy to miss a lot of them. But on the whole, Zack Snyder's take on the material is SOOO out of line with the source that I just can't get behind it. While this movie is FAR more technically accomplished than the recent Fantastic Four, I have the same issue with it: Even if it were narratively and technically *perfect*, I would still dislike it, because that's not the FF! I can forgive problems of plot, effects, and everything we fanboys love to wank about, but when the basic foundation of Tone/Mood is wrong, the very Essence of the work is just lost.

                                Civil War: Now THIS is more like it, just the thing to wash the taste of Movie DC from my mouth! Interesting that both companies are headlining stories where the Superheroes fight each other, but CW feels much more earned and coherent. Without getting into details, the plot is almost entirely different from the comic CW, which is good, as that one relied on an established MU with decades of history and character interaction; it wouldn't have worked nearly as well in the MCU, so they wisely went with something different. I couldn't help but think of this as the next Avengers movie, since there were so many of them, including a few surprise guests (if you've been paying attention, you probably already know who they are, but I'd been avoiding spoilers and trailers, so it was all a surprise to me)! Be ready to be part of an entire theater fangasming at the showstopping airport scene! Finally, true comic book action up on the live big-screen!

                                If I had any problem, it was with the real villain, and how well his plan comes together, when...just who IS this guy? Just some random guy can make the greatest superheroes in the world jump to his tune so easily?? At least he had a decent motivation...Also, where was SHIELD? I'm still a little confused as to the status of the organization, despite watching the tv show regularly? And shouldn't Nick Fury have been at the funeral, I'm pretty sure he's now known to be alive, right?

                                Actually, I do have a larger issue, but it's with both films/companies/universes and this obsessive need to examine superhero fiction in a Real World context. The problem is simply that, if you deconstruct these stories so much, then you will BREAK them, and show why they don't really work. And it's fine that they aren't plausible, that's why they are *escapist fantasies*! If you can't get on-board with that, then maybe you are not the ones to be making these movies, guys...

                                Let's just have movies with superheroes fighting evil, *done really well*, and be happy with that, ok?

                                                            Bite was abysmal. A couple good ideas strung together by the weakest characterization and plot possible. They Look Like People was actually pretty good, but for some reason it's in the Horror category when it's more appropriately a Thriller with some Mystery and Drama, like IMDB points out. In case no one's read this thread and noticed, I like Horror movies. It's a solid story about fictional mental illness and the friendship of two guys in bad places in their lives. Uncaged was surprisingly funny and good, though I wouldn't go so far as to say it was great. It's sort of sedate and a lot of things are underplayed, but still a decent movie. Ava's Possession was a letdown, not nearly as bad as Bite but it seemed like they decided to abandon the horror side of things in favor of comedy but they didn't really do the comedy well either. It also seems like a lot of stuff was cut. Some Kind of Hate was a solid flick, a sort of throwback to the old revenge rampages, very bloody but with some nicely thought out effects, might be inspiring to Geist the Sin-Eaters fans or ghost buffs. Southbound is great, I'm surprised it hasn't been talked about more since it's release but I suppose the interest in horror anthologies is waning again, the stories within were fantastically gripping and one in particular left a lingering sense of unease.
