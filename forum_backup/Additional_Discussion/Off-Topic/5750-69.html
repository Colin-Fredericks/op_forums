                        I've seen the film succinctly described as "a watchable mess".

                        My own perspective is that it feels like a series of pieces that are strung along with no real overarching story to chain them together or narrative logic to drive them along.

                        A big deal is made out of how she's in possession of Enchantress' heart, which is supposed to be her big control and security measure, and yet Enchantress goes rogue at the earliest opportunity in the story, and we get no clear indication of why Waller does not or cannot trigger the heart to kill her. At one point Waller is stabbing it, which... is that intended to kill, or hurt? Why not just trigger its bomb? Incubus seems to do something that helps Enchantress, but we're not really privy to what or how; not even a reaction shot of Waller stabbing the heart to no effect. And getting the heart back seems to matter, so is it still her weakness?

                        Plus how the whole mission turns out to be getting Waller out of the city... what exactly was her end game?

                                My problem with recent DC Movies is that they are draining. I mean emotionally, there are few breaks in between the drama, so even if I am Not invested the lack of a stylistic break is somewhat exhausting. Marvel movies mix it up. That said, I find movies like Antman and Guardians to have relatively little dramatic break and don't enjoy them much either. I used to be a DC fan most of my life (in terms of comics) but Marvel has just been doing better recently. I got a pile of comics right in front of me and maybe 1 is a DC book. The pendulum swings. There was a time that X-Men was the only well known marvel group outside the comics.

                                I do think it is odd, since DC always used to seem more hopeful what with it's fake cities, less realism and more about huge figures. While Marvel was the gritty one that put it's characters in more real life situations. It's like they switched. But DC really took it too far.

                                The problem is also partly with Batman. He's gotten so big he's hard to recognize sometimes? The Batman I knew growing up would have trouble fighting 5/6 no-name thugs at a time. I liked him because he was normal. Now Batman has super smarts in all but name and there is a legit question on whether he could win against superman. The playboy millionaire has become a bit misanthropic. I feel like what's wrong with Batman is what is wrong with DC comics/movies. So hard core they forgot their core. When Bruce Wayne says a lame joke because he thinks it's funny, embarrasses himself flirting, and get scuffed up by normal stuff people get scuffed up by, I'll feel DC is back on track.
