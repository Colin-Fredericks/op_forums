    Bad XP-riences

    02-03-2015, 01:15 PM
    This thread is the beginning of something I've been thinking about doing for a long time, a series of essays on what I have learned from all my years of gaming. The central premise of my thread is this: I suck. I have sucked a lot, and often, and hard, and I will no doubt continue to suck for the rest of my life; but sucking is necessary because it's only when we fail that we learn to stop sucking. Here are some lessons I have learned from all my years of sucking.

    I want to get started right now but I really need to leave the office in about five minutes, so for now this thread is just a placeholder for my memory.

              Well I'm going to borrow the thread then and start talking about the bad games I've been in.
              The most quick to memory is the game my friend and I were in set in 1957 London. Pull up a seat and grab a pack of smokes cause you're going to need them.

              So this game was set in London, and I decided that I wanted to play something really really exotic, so logically I played a Swedish Toreador Androgynous party boy/girl who came to England for schooling, got embraced, and never left. This character was mostly made to see how I could roleplay with odds stacked against me, something I've realized I do constantly because it's fun or something, I like challenges. Never the less, this game would spike off into insanity, but first let's meet the rest of the cast.
              1. Myself - The Toreador with Obfuscate and Presence (Fun combination)
              2. My friend, John Smith - The US airborne turned Brujah
              3. Dio Brando the True Brujah - I really really really wish I could say this wasn't real
              4. Idon'tevenrememberhisname the Ventrue
              5. Idon'tevenrememberhisname2 the Tremere
              6. The GM - Worst GM I've ever seen

              So about half the party was English and the other half of us won the Revolutionary war but we managed to get a game going online. Game was starting where the past group failed, tracking down some hunters and spying on them. My other friend was in that game as a Baali. Yes a Baali. Let him play one... on the Path of Humanity. Yeah... Moving on. So that session ended with the Baali suplexing cars because I don't even know. But my friend and I were brought in as reinforcements to replaces the people that left from the previous group. So Ms. I'mnotaLasombra whose the new Prince of London since Mithras disappeared calls us in and tells us what to do which is to spy on some hunters. That's it. Now I know what you're thinking: "How's that hard?" Well I'll tell you.
              So we talk and say we should stop by the Elysium for a drink. We walk in and everyone in the group walks into their own dark brooding corner. It was like some 8th dimensional room where everyone has their own corner while the band is just a single girl playing this sad song on the violin. Smith and I sit there and talk with one another, GM asks what everyone else is doing, which is hiding in the corner and sulking. So 2 hours IC go by and we're sitting there and finally say "Are we going to do the job?" Everyone else says no. Alright, so we decide to do it ourselves.

              We arrive at this abandoned church with the lights on and I go over and knock on the door. The hunters open, tell me to leave, and shut it. Smith calls up his ally and has him try again. No dice. So we head back to the Elysium and chew out the others so they follow us. I tell Smith to throw a rock, and he does (keep in mind he has Potence of 2). GM says "Roll to throw the rock." Yep. We kinda just roll with it and he fails the roll to throw the rock, which hits a window and bounces off. A rock thrown with 6 strength. Bounced off the window. So a guy opens the door and yells at us to stop. Smith then replies with "Get out of town you damn Frenchmen!" And they took exception to that with a Light Machine Gun. Which he fired at full auto at him. My friend then declares that he is going to run away. "Roll to run away." We kinda go slack jaw over the mic and ask why we need to roll to do that. "Because you need to roll to do things" So he does and fails, cursing under his breath about the GM. He instead pulls out his pistol and begins firing from cover while I'm ducked into an alleyway with Obfuscate covering me.

              Long story short, we spend about 2 hours arguing about the rules. GM thought that a Vampire can't heal wounds with blood, nor could they spend blood to increase their physical attributes until we pulled out the rulebook and metaphorically smashed it into his face. Gun fire is exchanged with the Hunters and they basically soak all of it. Yes, the Hunters have and I quote "Kevlar body armor" in the 1950's. I even asked for clarification, and he specifically said Kevlar. Long story short, we fire about 20 rounds into one of them before he drops while they're spraying the street with full auto fire, and his buddy is pumping shotgun shells at us. The Hunters eventually turn and run after almost kicking our asses, and lock the door while I go to a police box and call for the police. GM said police boxes are fictional things that only existed in Doctor Who. More hitting head in with Google for a literal hour until he accepted it. So the police tell me that they're going to come and collect me and bring me to the station for question, which isn't police protocol at all. They didn't care if I was safe, no they cared about collecting me, but I had Alternate Identity so I don't care. I get taken, they question me, I BS them a story about how I heard the gunfire and blah blah blah, and they do absolutely nothing about it.

              Next night is where it got weird, which I will continue tomorrow if I remember.

                      I've written and scrapped this story a couple of times because I'm discovering it's very hard to do. In the first place, I've been trying to leave people and their names out of it, because naming the accused always seems low-class to me, but that creates for a very confusing narrative; in the second place, I'm starting to wonder how many of my observations are useful to the gaming community at large and aren't specific to the personality of the geographical area I've been confined to all my life. Well, I've decided to let the story be what it is, and you guys can give me feedback on it.

                      I've been running a game for about two years now. It started as Pathfinder but became Dungeons and Dragons when those books came out and I had a chance to read them, discovering I will never run Pathfinder again (which I will probably do an article on in the future). The game nearly ended this month when, two sessions ago, the game ended and I started getting text messages from the players to complain about the character played by my best friend, whom I'll call Lee for these purposes. I was told he's no fun, he's too grumpy, he doesn't have a personality, stuff like that. When I passed these complaints along to Lee--admittedly with an inkling as to how he'd take it--he nearly quit the game because he felt disrespected by the way our group was handling the conflict. The best he (or I) could figure, if they wouldn't approach him directly with their concerns, then either they were afraid he'd be too childish to take the criticisms or else they don't respect him enough to confront him to his face, and he didn't want to keep gaming with people like that. I wasn't sure I did, either.

                      This is not an isolated incident for me. Gamers refusing to talk to one another has been pretty much the tenor of my entire career, to the point that I've always assumed it's just how all gaming groups are. For instance, one of the very first games I tried to run, a first edition game of Exalted, ended on the second game session when two of the players didn't show up, didn't call in, didn't answer my calls, and never returned my messages. I must have done something to piss them off, but they thought the correct solution would be to finish the game session, then go home and never speak of it to me or indeed speak to me at all ever again, so I have no idea what I did wrong. Same thing happened years later when I tried to run Legend of the Five Rings. Both of those groups were comprised of people pulled from internet ads or bulletin boards at the gaming shop, though, so maybe the fact that they were strangers to me plays a part. Don't know.

                      The point is, I'm used to people not talking to each other, but this was the first time I was in the middle of it, the first time I was the fucking switchboard, the first time I was ever asked to criticize someone else while protecting the identities of the critics in question. I didn't care for it, and I don't intend to do it again.

                      As for how this issue resolved itself...shit, I'm not sure it has. The group got together and had a conversation, but I couldn't help noticing the conversation never involved any criticisms, and the critics never owned up to their parts in the brouhaha. Perhaps because of this attempt to talk about problems without talking about problems, it was decided that some of the characters were tainted by bad first impressions and their players decided to make new characters to replace them. On the surface, everything seems fine now, but I wonder how much I can expect history to repeat itself since almost nothing was said about what made those characters objectionable in the first place; why wouldn't people repeat the same mistakes if no one will identify them as mistakes? Still, the group has established that its boundaries include not being direct with one another, and until I actually see these problems recurring, I think I have to respect the boundaries set. I guess I could warn the group about why I think this might not work, but I think naysaying at this point would probably be more destructive than constructive, so I should hope for the best and wait until I have reason to start complaining.

                      So what have I learned from this bad XP-rience?

                      I do not understand respect. You guys probably know this about me, but I'm the kind of guy who bases his understanding of things by taking a personal experience and applying that experience hypothetically to another, and since I don't respect myself, I can't identify respect when I see it. It's hard to teach myself such a basic emotion, but I think I'm going to categorize respect as a form of trust: trust that you can be honest with someone, trust that they will react better than a child when criticized, trust that they are strong enough to take what you feel compelled to dish out. Maybe that's an accurate definition of trust and maybe it isn't, but that's okay, because it's not what I learned from this experience. I learned that you have to respect one another, whatever "respect" actually is, because without that, you're just a bunch of strangers making each other uncomfortable for a few hours at a stretch and who wants that?

                      Who wants that? Seriously. It's been an exhausting experience for me, and one that has had me ignoring this thread deliberately for two weeks because I just wanted to replay Paper Mario: the Thousand-Year Door so I could experience an RPG where people help and support one another instead of complaining about one another behind their backs. My batteries are recharged now, so I hope that doesn't sound more dramatic than it is (we all need a break, right?), but still, it's worrying when I discover that I am going out of my way not to think about the game I am running or the people I run it for. Bad sign.

                      But the reason I'm not sure this is a useful article for the gaming community in general is I'm a Missourian, and I have traveled just enough recently to learn how deeply, painfully clannish my people are (this is something so fundamental to me I did not have the perspective to realize it until I was forced to interact with and rely on people who are not Missourian). We form cliques like high schoolers and never break them, defining ourselves by strict social circles. For instance, the group I'm talking about have six people in it, and the stratification is visible at a glance: there's me and my friend Lee; there's an acquaintance we made online and her fiancee; there's an acquaintance we met online and a long-standing friend of his. We all talk more among our duos than to those outside the preexisting groups, and though I like to think I do better at it than anyone else--I'm the GM, so I have to--I am guilty of this kind of clannishness as anyone. Lee is the only person I'm apt to hang out with outside of the game, because he is in the Friend circle, while the other four are in the Gamer circle. Hell, writing this thing, I realized I don't even know the last names of two of the players.

                      Extraordinary as that kind of segregation is, though, I think there must be something in it a lot of gamers can recognize. If there isn't, then my willingness to be open about shit all those years ago among this community wouldn't have been seen as such a remarkable thing. I hope this article has been somewhat useful to you, but if it hasn't, I'd love to know where you think I misstepped.

                              While I can certainly sympathise with your experiences, I can also understand how difficult it can be to open up a critical discussion with someone.

                              Generally, I would like to think I am fairly good about getting along with all sorts of different people, but one time I was in a campaign (one of the few times I got to be a player) and I actually ended up having serious personal issues with the GM. There were also some points of contention about their GM style, but that was fairly minor (and probably unavoidable when a ForeverGM ends up in the player seat). The GM just rubbed me the wrong way for various reasons, but I also did not want to make a scene and disturb what seemed to be a fairly enjoyable game to the others, so I kept it to myself for a long time. It is simply really difficult to bring up criticism of a person, especially of a personal nature, in such a way that it will not negatively affect the relationship forever (exact difficulty can obviously vary depending on how open to criticism the person is).

                              Eventually, the other players started having problems with the GM as well, but more about this GM style and we actually did talk about it with the GM, on two separate occasions even... the second led to the terminus of the game. The GM was quite pissed off and not very understanding about the problems raised.
                              After a few months, it all cooled down and we are playing together again, but it had to kill an (admittedly interesting) campaign first.

                              Considering all the hassle we went through, just for the campaign to end anyway and some added real life drama, I can understand why some people might just prefer to duck out and leave quietly.

                              Long story short: Shit's complicated, yo

                              Politeness is the lubricant of social intercourse.
