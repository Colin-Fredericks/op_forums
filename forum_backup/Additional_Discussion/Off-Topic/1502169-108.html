                    First difference is that while frequently used interchangeably, Freedom of Speech (protected in the US) and Freedom of Expression (as stated in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights) have two key distinctions. One in that Expression covers all forms of communication, but the other that it protects the idea itself, not the specific words used, while Speech refers more directly to the words.

                    This is important because that's the origin of the US idea that you can say anything, and how hard it is to pass legislation or rulings limiting it in several situations. I know this is not really a legal problem, but is an actual political problem.

                    But other things differ. The Human Rights Act of 1998 in the UK has Freedom of Expression defined in article 10 with far more clear space for limitations than the US 1st amendment. The UK further passed several laws to define limits to it that are generally more clear, and as such more actionable, than in the US.

                    And finally you have the system of Common Law that's the whole of it in the UK, where not only can laws be more freely reinterpreted, but also customs and practices have legal weight without need for formalization.

                    So all together it's a way weaker protection than in the US when it comes to speech itself, and that specially hurts people trying to emulate the US far right (a thing that's happening the world around).
