        Okay, one last word:

        There can be problems with the matter of appropriation with that idea.

        EDIT: To be perhaps a bit more concise on this point, as well on exactly what the sense of ambiguity I referred to previously is: I'm never quite sure whether or not to take Ragyou as the usual lazy "rape as easy way to establish character as evil", which has all kinds of problems relating to trivializing the realities of rape, or an actual halfway decent insight into the circumstances of somebody being sexually abused by somebody they are closely associated with.

        I... extremely tentatively lean ever so slightly in the direction of the latter, based on surrounding details; Ragyou is sexually abusive towards Satsuki, but initially still kind of... motherly towards her. It's messed up, but possibly messed up in the way that sexually abusive parents might be. That Ragyou at that time seems kind of sincere in her regard for Satsuki, well, I feel it might lend a bit of credibility to the possibility that there's a bit more nuance to the portrayal than it just being an easy shorthand for Ragyou being evil.

        I guess that might be part of why I'm not inherently repelled by the scenes in question, although this whole line of discussion is making me feel very uncomfortable as I try to sort out my thoughts. There's a part of me which is examining this in terms of not wanting certain uncomfortable realities to be culturally invisible, which is struggling against the need to be share that the basic outlines aren't being exploited for cheap narrative purposes, with a dash of "I don't want it to be invisible, but there are places where visibility is not appropriate". I can't find an easy answer to it.

        (There are also problems for what it means for the writing of Satsuki as well; if "villain is a rapist" is a cheap and lazy way to convey evil, then "badass women is current or prior victim of sexual abuse or assault" is a cheap way to provide credentials (or relatability or the like) to a character, and I might be worried about that being at play as well.)
