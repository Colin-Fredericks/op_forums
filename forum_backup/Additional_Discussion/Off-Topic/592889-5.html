            We talk about labels all the time, and I'm of firm belief that they aren't going anywhere soon. A label is a cognitive shortcut, that links a set of ideas together for easy processing. They definitely can lead to misconceptions with people who assume the label is 100% accurate rather than being the shortcut it is. After all, if I ask for a spoon, when what I really need is a large serving spoon and you hand me a teaspoon, its not really your fault because I chose to not be specific, and I cant really hold you responsible for not knowing the difference, especially if you are not a connoisseur of spoons. This same concept goes for any label in the kink-world or the LBGT community.
            That said, I find that labels are not bad. Any distaste for labels would stem from the poorly formed idea that by being labeled, a person is the sum of that label, when we know this is definitely not the case. You could call me "Dominant" for example, and it would only scratch the surface for my own behaviors and tendencies inside and outside of the bedroom.
            There is a bit of a disconnect here on the behavior vs identity argument, and that disconnect is part of what I said above about the labels. How I identify is going to be based on core behaviors I have observed in myself, and perhaps how those behaviors have been observed by others and been thusly labeled. My wife told me I was dominant for 15 years before I came to terms with the idea and started to explore enough to understand what that meant, Just because I refused to see it, even refused the label, did not mean that I was not, in fact, of dominant behavior. Now, its much easier for me to tell people, even members of that particular community, that "I am dominant/I am a dominant" rather than explaining all the kinds of dominant I am. And that opens the doors with some people to have much deeper conversation on that point, because now they want to know the nuances.
