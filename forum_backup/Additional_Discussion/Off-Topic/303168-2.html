            Oh man, one dude in our HtV game...

            If his characters weren't being stupid, they were being incredibly stubborn. Whenever the rest of us tried get him out of whatever "special" thing he wanted to do (it was always stupid) so we can move the game along he always contested the rolls. Every. Single. Time.

            He got us surrounded by a SWAT team just after we came out of a vampire bar, ate some goblin fruits in the Hedge, wandered off so many times I've lost count, decided to resist not taking an injection that would prevent him from taking full lethal (I have no idea why), disregards a powerful vampire's death threat and (surprise!) someone ends up dead because of it, blurted out our mission objective right in the target's faces in one mission (yes, faces, plural, as in multiple targets), and then there's his most recent antics...

            Alright, so a bunch of cargo ships have been mysteriously disappearing as of late. Being hunters, we decide to check it out of course, so we board the ship from our makeshift boat and more or less find out what's going on with the ships. Long story short: The ships were being taken to some alternate dimension that turned the crew into dehydrated zombies. The only reason why we weren't affected is because we had a special kind of "sunscreen" on. It wouldn't last forever though, so we had to get the hell outta dodge. We did, barely. However, the exit to the dimension we came out of left us stranded in some random part of the ocean.

            Naturally, we try to navigate our way to some land to figure things out from there. As we're doing that, we come across the Lochness Monster (or at least the equivalent of it). So what's our response to our current predicament? Full speed ahead in the opposite direction of course, except for this guy. He gets the bright idea to take a dip into the waters (shark-infested waters, by the way, we know because we got attacked by one and killed it, so there's lots of blood in there, too) and swims towards the damn thing!

            Oh, it gets worse. Being a good cellmate, I decided to save him, which required a Strength + Athletics roll. Get this: My character's Strength and Athletics are both 1, so that's just a measly two dice and yes, I did know I had to make this roll before I even said I was gonna do it, but I obviously needed to do it anyways so I spend a Willpower. What does he do?

            HE FUCKING CONTESTS MY ROLL!(My character's name is Eulalia, his character's name is Ione)

            A roll I'm spending Willpower on to save his ass, and he wants to contest it (it's important to note that every time he contested my rolls when I was trying to prevent him from doing something stupid before this, he would win. Every time. So I thought for sure he was gonna win this roll, too. This is why I let out a big "Thank God!" after I won the roll). No, he wants to do something special. Why?

            Because our ST decided to make it so that our characters wouldn't die in the first season (our chronicle will have multiple seasons), so he gets the bright idea that as long as our characters are safe from death with the training wheels on, he might as well have fun with it. Yeah, and completely disregard the difficult position you're putting the ST in. Okay, so maybe our ST shouldn't have told any of us that, but she didn't anticipate the knowledge would be abused like this.

            Yeah, this guy's officially our group's Michelle...

                                            Actually, today Banality felled on me.

                                            There's this guy who I taught to be a Storyteller, and then he began to hold his own chronicles. About a year and a half ago, I needed a fourth player for my chronicle (the previous one moved away) and then he entered in the chronicle. After three or four months I realized that there was something wrong and we talked about it; it was almost as if during the sessions he was bored, but nevertheless insists on playing. Then he told me that he didn't get a good vibe from his character; therefore I tried to meet him halfway, suggesting different evolutionary paths for the character, to make him feel like his own, more personal.
                                            Frankly, the problem was also mine because I don't feel very motivated without reactive players. It's almost like playing alone.
                                            Everything ok for 3-4 months, then the same problem. We talk about it, I give others ideas. We continue to play.
                                            3-4 months, the problem returns. We discuss another time, and I ask him if he want to make a new character. No, he does not like the idea of losing the hard-won experience points, he think that would create hardships for the other players and basically he believes that a different character would overlap with the roles of the other PC (I have never seen the problem, but that's not the issue here). Eventually, after enumerating my significant deficiencies he promises that he will try again with his character, more motivated.
                                            Another 3-4 months, now. Still the same problem. I'm tired of the situation and I'm going to talk just because we are friends and I care a lot for the chronicle, as it lasted more than two years and therefore I would like to complete it. This time I decided to pressure him, at the cost of being unpleasant, because I really want to understand what the hell is the problem. Finally, after three hours of discussions, he admits the real problem.
                                            He is annoyed by how I set the chronicle (tons of NPC, relevance to the canon, metaplot, etc), he barely tolerate the system of rules that I use (essentially, the houserules I use and the rules I should ignore and do not), and basically the only true reason why he still plays with us is that he really likes the company.

                                            At this point, I must probably be angry, because he could at least trouble himself and tell me before ... but I'm just disappointed. Indeed, it is from yesterday that I have a sort of rejection for all role-playing games in general, and in particular for my chronicle. The desire to complete this chronicle I worked on for over two and a half years is completely gone, almost as if someone drained it.

                                            I feel like I lost the spark. Welcome, Banality.
