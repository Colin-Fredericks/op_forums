                Originally posted by dxanders View Post

                See, I'm in the opposite camp regarding my view on the games but of the same opinion regarding a synthesis of the lines. I got into VtM almost 20 years ago, stuck around in some capacity through the End Times books and didn't really explore the New World books until a few months ago. VtR is everything I liked about VtM in theory synthesized with mechanics I find clever. That said, I'd grown tired of the preponderance of metaplot in VtM and the almost superheroic action-adventure tone the game seemed to have developed toward the end of its life. I don't want to see the two merged, as practically every aspect of VtR is better tailored for my experiences than VtM. The other games that interest me (Demon, Wraith/Geist, Changeling) are SO different in their two iterations as to make a synthesis unfeasible.

                I think the conversion kits that have already been released are really the furthest the company needs to go in combining the two versions of the games. As far as new releases for the nWoD though, I don't mind them scaling back production to make way for new cWoD releases. It's not a metaplot heavy game, and I don't need a bunch of books to integrate it into my table. Give me cores, fatsplats covering the factions and clans/tribes/etc. and the occasional supplement up to the standards of books like Damnation City and I'm happy.
                Which is absolutely, 100% perfectly fine with me. I don't like it, myself, but it's not something I hate to the point I wouldn't want someone else to have their favorite or even preferred system or style. At best, I'd like to have the nWoD removed even more from the original WoD, giving it even more of it's own name. Even something a simple as removing the Gangrel, Nosferatu, and Ventrue from Requiem and making something new, even it they where very similar. On the other hand, Requiem does contain the Carthains rather than the Anarchs, the Lancae Santum rather than the Path of Night. Both are similar to the somewhat equivalent Masquerade concepts, neither are really the same. They allow for a similar fill in, but also allow for a lot of room outside of that. Less so with the Lancae Sanctum, who at least in first edition seemed to be all over the damn place, and like I said, far too specific in it's own niche (within the game/setting).

                I know plenty of folks that love both, and for them, the aspects that don't crossover are the big selling points for whichever particular game they are playing. I think it's safe to say that almost no one is interested in the idea of trying to mesh them all together. Saying the VtM, MtA1, and WtO all take place inside the same world, while VtR, PtC, and HtV also all take place in one world, cool. I think most of us kind of already think that way anyway, even if it does means few issues, (so God's real, but Mages can alter reality, and everything has a spirit are pretty mutually exclusive points of view). So what. Just means no one has the one true answer. Well, not until it's too late. Hell, add in a new layer or two and rebuild that link between the Exalted and the Age of Darkness. Fantastic.

                            I'm cautiously optimistic about the Paradox acquisition of WW. They seem truly interested in the material and far more personally invested than CCP. Nevertheless, I also don't believe that Paradox has made any major or final decisions about either the classic or new WOD game lines, although the classic will probably be the basis for any computer games given its history and name and market recognition.

                            My primary and immediate concern now is that the acquisition will substantially delay the otherwise imminent release of Mage 2e (as well as Promethean, Changeling, the new nWOD core, etc.) while Paradox decides on their vision for WW, how to implement it, and how OPP fits into their plans.

                            In any event. I wish Paradox, OPP, and all the great developers and writers of recent WW products, all the best of luck during this transition.
