            S5E3 - Castle Sweet Castle

            Meh, it was okay.

            On the one hand, this episode dealt with something that couldn't really go unaddressed in a well-written show. Changes, particularly traumatic changes, leave marks and it will take time to work through it. Twilight's sense of loss was relatable and the moment when she and Spike stood in front of the ruined Golden Oaks was actually very touching. The moral of the episode, that it's not the material place that makes a home but the memories associated with it, was a good one too. This was an episode that was entirely justified, probably even needed in order for the show to support the fandom's claims that it's more sophisticated than other shows aimed at children.

            But it was also pretty dull. Predictable (doesn't have to be a problem, but it kind of was this time), not super sharp writing on the script nor the song, and the mane six didn't get to play their personalities up properly: with all of them having strong opinions on interior design, Rarity's schtick was kind of invaded, and it's a pretty narrow one to begin with. The possible exception was Pinkie Pie, and she was the one who was responsible for the few times I chuckled. Speaking of them, I was rather confused as to why the character with the most relevance to the subject matter was shipped off at the start and kept out, as a plot point even, until the last few minutes again. The idea of the episode wasn't bad as such, but it would have benefited from Twilight and Spike getting a bigger part of it.

            So yeah, good to see this episode, but it's not a rewatch. Average at best for me.

                            One thing the episode did make me think about was the function and place of cutie marks in the world of Equestria.

                            On the one hand, in the first dream, Apple Bloom is shown suddenly getting really good at something she's never even heard of before because she happened to get a cutie mark about it. That's clearly just a representation of a child's fears, as it's shown on multiple occasions, not least in Magical Mystery Cure, that the cute mark isn't a talent lottery that makes you good at some random crap, but instead a representation of the talent you already got. Luna also speaks (somewhat) to that effect towards the end. In fact, it seems like cutie marks can often be quite broad, almost thematic in their application. Cheerilee, for example, has flowers as her cutie mark, which represents her affinity for cultivation, but she applies that affinity to cultivating young minds as a teacher instead of, y'know, flowers. One can even look towards the mane six for this. Rarity's cutie mark isn't about making dresses, it is in fact about finding gems in the ground. However, it can be argued that her talent is as broad as bringing out hidden beauty, which would connect her dress design with her cutie mark.

                            It is, however, pretty obvious that the cutie mark has a social dimension as well. The third dream, where Apple Bloom is afraid she won't get an apple-related cutie mark, I could imagine being reality in some cases (not with the Apples, of course, since they are Good Guys, but elsewhere). This could be said to be the case in reality as well, but in Equestria it will be tattooed on your butt, making it very explicit. I can imagine some talents not being super fun to advertise like that. Someone with talents like theft, prostitution or other socially questioned traits would probably wish they'd at least not have to wave it around everywhere. Like, even if you're not actually a thief or prostitute, it's still what you're leaning towards and that open for all to see. I can see families cutting ties with someone because of it.

                                            I've been watching the new season approximately as it comes out. I've not had much to say so far; I like it well enough, the opening two-parter was pretty good as these things go (the premise it sets up looks like it might be interesting, and the plot had a nice ring to it) and I thought it was nice to address how Twilight's home was destroyed.

                                            This latest episode though... I just have to have a word about how dull it is.

                                            I mean, it's not the worst episode in the series, but at least those ones left an impression of some sort. This one just meanders around for the first half going through the motions of its obvious dream set-up (so obvious that it feels weird that they try to play up some things for tension when it's clear that they're not real). It doesn't even really manage to do anything interesting with it; the whole Apple family rejection scene is kind of surreal, but is really so disconnected that it's not very provocative. And its surreality isn't even very interesting when it comes down to it, just basic "switch around voices and have them state Apple Bloom's already clearly stated fears". The whole shadow thing wasn't very interesting for similar reasons; it's barely there and barely does anything (and the acting for it is honestly pretty wooden) so the payoff doesn't really mean much.

                                            I see Luna show up in the episode, and I get the feeling she's less there to guide a troubled soul through a nightmare and more that she's about as ready for the episode to end as I am.

                                            And the parts with Sweetie Belle and Scootaloo... they've got some interesting implications in the idea that, deep down, those two actually recognise what their talents are, and fear the possibility of them being taken away from them, things that seem as though they could make a more substantive plot to this kind of episode, but it's kind of revealed a weakness in the character of Apple Bloom as a whole.

                                            I tend to like Apple Bloom a fair bit, and at worst I don't usually mind Cutie Mark Crusader episodes, but this episode has made it fairly apparent that Apple Bloom has basically nothing else besides Cutie Mark anxiety going on when she's not part of a group. I mean, not just in terms of hinting at what her talent might actually be (the show having never really followed up on the carpentry and construction stuff suggested in The Show Stoppers the way it has for Sweetie Belle's singing and Scootaloo's athleticism), but also an apparent lack of traits with which to give her stories on her own, the way Sweetie Belle has tensions with her sister or Scootaloo has a desire for an idol or problems with her wings. When she's not an ensemble or satellite character, it's always just some variation of "is more sad about Cutie Mark issues than her friends, has some unpleasant encounter as a result, learns a valuable lesson", and it's been going on so long now that it's becoming hard to get past the marked lack of other traits.

                                            I mean, no wonder she's worried about her Cutie Mark; I sure as hell can't tell what her traits or talents are supposed to be.

                                            If they're going to keep coming back to this well, I really think some adjustments need to be made.
