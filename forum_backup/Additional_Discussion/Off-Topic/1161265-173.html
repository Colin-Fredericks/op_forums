                                                So they put out another Unearthed Arcana article, this time a stack of spells and some rules about magic tattoos.

                                                Aside from a neat acid damage AOE spell for arcane casters, it's all about getting help from other planes. Including a bunch of ones for conjuring creatures to fight for you, which I'll discuss in a minute.

                                                Otherworldly Form lets Clerics, Sorcerers, Warlocks, and Wizards take on either a heavenly or hellish mien, with added benefits like flight and boosted AC. My minor gripe is that it had the chance to let you flavor it as other planes, like the Feywild, yet limits to JUST the upper and lower planes. If I'm playing an Archfey Warlock, I'd like the ability to sprout butterfly wings and dance around like the Kindly One I was destined to be. This spell is also one of the only ways a Wizard can really channel angelic power, as attested in folklore and medieval occultism. I'm almost certain it's intended for Wizards to only use the fiendish side of this spell, even if nothing stops you from doing otherwise.

                                                I really like Spirit Shroud and Summon Undead Spirit, because it scratches an itch I've had for a while: calling ghosts to fight for you. Personally, I would have preferred a spell for summoning different kinds of incorporeal undead from the monster manual, and also some custom ghostly stat blocks for specific classes. Like a "Condemned Soul" and "Martyred Soul" for (Archfiend) Warlocks and Clerics, respectively. But, as we've learned from the existing conjuration spells, it's not necessarily ideal to have a spell that requires additional references to work at all. So I get the logic of creating a set "ghostly undead" stat block (which it shares with a skeletal and zombie one). If nothing else, I can always flavor it how I want. Though only Spirit Shroud is available to Clerics.

                                                Like, even if you want it to be on-theme, Death domain Clerics exist. If they're allowed to Animate Dead - just as a standard part of the Cleric spell list - they should be able to conjure one.

                                                On the note of the summon spells, I generally like what they're doing here. Having a set stat block (with specific features that can be tuned to the caster's preference) is great. There are so many of them, too. WotC really threw Conjuration Wizards a bone here, since they get access to many of these spells. You know, as they freaking should. I'm not even really mad they don't get Summon Celestial Spirit, because they aren't limited to just "elementals" or "a single big elemental". Conjurers get to do their intended job earlier than 5th level.


                                                The magic tattoos I'm more ambivalent on. It's great to have them, and some of the tattoos you can get are neat. One of them is just "you get AC without armor or spending a spell slot", which is always good. Now I want to have a bunch of tattooed Wizard NPCs in my games, and have it serve more than "Ritual Purposes". On the other hand, I don't know how much I like that the Spellwrought Tattoos only let you cast the spell once. Like, we have plenty of magic items that let you cast spells with charges every day, or even just once per day, and don't have a problem. Why is it different that it's in tattoo form? Because it can't be taken from you?

                                                I also don't know how to feel about the magic tattoos being something that is applied with a magic needle that instantly forms the tattoo on the skin, and can then be removed at will later back into needle form. Kind of runs counter to the vision I have in my head of a tattoo artist painstakingly etching the magic into a person's skin, each a work of art. I suppose 5e is built with convenience and modularity in mind.

                                                    So when I first started my homebrew setting, I've decided to take a path in which there are only 10 gods, with each one governing over a single Domain. Now, that could have been an issue, as each of the gods also had an alignment- so it would have meant that at least three of those Domains would require the worship of an evil deity. The problem was even greater, as one of my players wanted to be a Tempest Cleric- an Tempest was the Domain of the CE god of the pantheon. In order to solve that problem, I have taken the path in which instead that the deities force their lessons and ideas upon their followers, guiding them towards good or evil, law and chaos, the Powers That Be are both intimately close to the world and distant in the same time. The gods do not care for their followers- they simply exist, embodying their nature in the world, and those who serve their nature can draw upon their power. It is not that they are not sentient, or that they do not commune with their followers- it is simply that the forms and idols are how the mortal mind can comprehend the terrifying image which is the gods without breaking apart. Of course, I didn't tell that to my players- I have simply stated that while the gods have their own alignment, different cultures sometimes see different alignment or character of the same deity. As such, while some deities are seen as "evil" or "good", you don't need to be good or evil in order to worship them. That's how you have evil cultures which gain access to the Life and Light Domains, and good cultures which can use the War and Tempest Domains. Some such examples were orcs priests worshiping the goddess of fertility in order to help in childbirth, hobgoblins recognizing the goddess of luminaries as the queen of heaven, dragonborn following the commands of the war god and- most important of all- how dwarves who worship their own maker, a chaotic good got of artifice, as lawful.

                                                    Now, my players really loved the concept- and our cleric have had a lot of fun playing a cleric which misunderstands the nature of his god of calamity while the gods taunts him for being unable to accept his destructive nature. While things are going great on that angle, something kept bothering me- how can the dwarves, the children of the god of the forge, be unable to accept the nature of their own creator? I mean, it makes sense for a "foreign" god to be worshiped in such a way, but having them being unable to recognize the alignment of their own maker just felt like I mocking their own culture. Like, how stupid those dwarves are? How idiotic was it on my side to make the dwarves so stupid? Should i just recton that part, under the claim that the primer I sent to my players was an "in world misinterpretation by a foreign culture"? On the other hand, I could just changed the god's alignment- the gods where already too integrated into the setting, and their place was already set. My players didn't care, of course- but I cared, and that stupid line of bad worldbuilding just kept haunted me more than a year after the campaign has started..

                                                    Until today, in which I have finally solved the problem.

                                                    As I stated before, the gods in my setting may have alignment, but they don't care that much for how their followers perceive them. The goddess of nature exists in every plant and animal, every hunter and hunted, every form most beautiful which walk, has walked and will walk the earth. Whatever philosophies and religions mortals develop around those forces of nature comes from how they interpreted those divine, and come directly from their culture. Divine magic is the act of channeling the powers of the gods and advancing their influence, not their moral agenda- but in order to do so, you still must have faith in your god. Faith is important- and it doesn't matter what exactly you believe your god to be as long as you believe. However- if faith is that important, what if it would not only allow the followers to draw upon their deities, but also to change them? What if the faith in a certain form of your god, one which deviates from the actual nature of your deity, is so strong that whole cultures are build upon it? Shouldn't that faith be accounted for anything? Shouldn't it make a difference? Sure, it won't actually change the god- but it could coalesce into something. Manifest into something. So much divine power which both accepts and denies the nature of that god could form a lesser idol, a small god, a rouge aspect of the greater whole- a mask which took life of its own. It is still the god- but is separate. Clerics are the mark that the gods have upon mortals- why should those aspects be the mark that mortals leave upon their gods?

                                                    I must say that I really love that idea- suddenly, my setting have those small manifestations of the gods which, while channel the powers of the gods, could turn against them. The god of peace is an aspect of the god of war, the goddess of tears broke from the goddess of luminaries, the god of civilization came from the god of calamity and the god of monuments is the stern aspect of the god of artifice. The dwarves may have been created by the god of the forge- but they didn't accepted him, as he wasn't what they wanted him to be. They denied their god, yet still respected him- and from that dissonance, an aspect was created, to bridge between the faith and the god, between the mortal will and the uncaring, divine power. The dwarves aren't just so stupid that they can't accept their own god- they are so devoted, that they changed their god for them, and their god complied. They are not idiots. They are amazing.

                                                    Again, this is just a very, very small part of my setting, one which I am not sure if my players would ever interact with- but I finally can sleep at night. I have managed to maintain consistency in my setting, while solving that annoying little bit of bad writing which haunted me for so, so long, and I was so satisfied that I had to talk about it somewhere- and my Facebook groups are not the place for such long posts (not to mention that a lot of people in there are mean). I just had to get it out of my system, and I couldn't brag before my players because who knows if that part would become suddenly relevant to my campaign?

                                                    Anyway, I'm going to sleep now.

                                                        Okay, so they released another UA article, revisiting Psionics.

                                                        To put it simply, I'm unenthusiastic about the material this time around.

                                                        It's really late and I want to sleep, so I'll be brief (or as much as I can, since it IS me we're talking about).

                                                        First, the Mystic is official dead. I figured as much, but I suppose they came out and said it so fans of the older UA material from years ago would stop asking about it. (Not that Mystic fans won't make a stink over this now). Personally, I liked moving to a decentralized way to handle Psionics.

                                                        Second, this UA drops several psionic spells introduced in the previous one. I would need to break out that article to check, but I don't know if I like losing all of them. The spells in this UA are down to three: Mind Sliver, Mind Thrust, and Intellect Fortress. It's a very small amount.

                                                        Third, the Psionic Wizard subclass got the ax. Don't like that. The material for them was cool.

                                                        Fourth, I'm not going to get lost in the weeds about the reworked Fighter, Rogue, and Sorcerer subclasses, but I take issue with a feature added to all three: Psionic Talent. It involves a die (starting at d6 and growing bigger at level thresholds), that get rolled to add to various things. And the die that is rolled ALSO "shrinks" or "grows" with use, in a roundabout way of making psionic power a finite resource per day. Again, I'm half asleep already, so I suggest you read the article yourselves to grasp the full conceit, but suffice to say it's a big change that makes any character who uses it to have an inconsistent and swingy resource.

                                                        To the Fighter and Rogue it's just kind of weird, but with the Sorcerer subclass ("Psionic Soul" replaces "Aberrant Mind"), it makes it objectively worse. The subclass loses its unarmored defense feature (one of the more welcome tools in Aberrant Mind's kit). Instead of having a flat list of psychic-like spells the Sorcerer just gets to know (without counting towards spells known, a precious resource) you must roll the psychic die to get A SPELL of divination or enchantment from the Sorcerer list (which, again, doesn't have as many psychic spells after this UA jettisoned a bunch) for a number of hours.

                                                        Incidentally, the psychic die - which can grow or shrink at random throughout the adventuring day and can only be reset once per day - is another resource the Sorcerer player has to keep track of, along with Sorcery Points.

                                                        And why are the designers even bothering with this psionics dice mechanic, anyway? Well, because they reworked the Feats devised in the previous UA to RELY on having the die from the Psionic Talent feature, or from a different Feat called "Wild Talent" that also gives the psionic die.

                                                        Remember in the previous version where you could just BE Telepathic, no matter your class or other choices, at the cost of a single Feat? Now you either need to be specifically one of the aforementioned classes + subclasses, or take a completely different Feat. You can't JUST be psychic in a specific, singular way (which works really well for invoking the feel of 1e, and/or of Dark Sun, a setting this UA hints at several times). We need to make a whole production out of it, tying up more character resources. You can't even use Magic Initiate to get some low-level psychic spells, because a bunch of those were excised.

                                                        ...

                                                        I am so tired. Someone else take the baton here.

                                                            While I do like the idea of the Psionic Talent Die (even though I am yet to actually test it), I too have found the Psychic Soul rework of the Aberrant Mind uninspiring. It didn't felt like a sorcerer subclass mechanically, and the flavor was too plain compared to the original IMO. It feels like they really try to NOT make a core Psion/Psychic/Mystic/etc class, while the Psionic Talent Die feels like something a class should be based around. The result feels like an hybrid creature which I really don't want to use or play- I don't find it interesting as a sorcerer subclass, and it feels boring as a replacement for an actual psion class. In short, I have no use for it.

                                                            I have no problem with the idea of psionic subclasses, of course. I think that the Soulknife did a significant upgrade compared to the original, and the Psi Knight is ok I guess (I really wish they would stop slapping "Knight" for every magic using Fighter subclass, just like it starts getting old that every time they make a new Magical Origin they call it a "X Soul", just because they don't want to be specific about how the Sorcerer got their magic. It worked for the Divine Soul, but it doesn't work all the time- and the Psychic Soul is one such example, IMO). The idea of a Wild Talent feat also makes a lot of sense of psionics. But the excuse of "Oh, we made a class which a lot of people complained to be OP, so we ditched the idea of making such class and instead invent an whole new mechanic without making a class about it because the former mechanics were OP" is, honestly, the most lame excuse I've ever heard. They should just be honest and say "we don't want to make a Psion class because we know that many loud people hate the concept of psionics", because seriously, if the PsTD mechanic is balanced for subclasses and feats, there is no reason why it shouldn't be balanced for a full class if designed correctly.

                                                            Just some rant from my side. At any case, I am going to use the Mystic as is until Wizards would finally decide how they want psionics to work, and then rework it according to their final mechanics. Again, the new mechanics seems nice, and I think that psionic subclasses and feats could enrich the game for a setting which includes such powers, but I hate hearing lame excuses which make no sense, and I also don't like the fact that it seems that either the people who design psionics are not psionic fans, or that they do the best to make it "tolerable" for non psionic fans on the expanse of psionic fans. It's just frustrating, that's all.

                                                            (and just to note- I am also perfectly fine with a potential Psion class simply using spells through some interesting mechanic, like the Warlock does. Psionics don't have to be complicated- they just need to feel different, IMO).
