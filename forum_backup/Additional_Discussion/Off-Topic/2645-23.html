            I think, perhaps, our video-game/TTRPG mentalities creep in to our narrative thought processes when it comes to looking at extra-narratives: Yoda's age, relative to the assessment at hand, is all-but-irrelevant (not *totally* irrelevant, but not far off). Age does not necessarily equate to power, nor dominating finesse. Plenty of highly skilled individuals in The Real World(tm) can find themselves upstaged by the talent of a novice. In the metaphysical context of The Force, Yoda may well have a greater degree of finesse and meta-"muscle" than Dooky, but it isn't by *that* much of a margin. A Jedi naturally attuned and/or strong with the force at a young age may well be nearly as strong as a veteran like Yoda -- he just may lack some of the more nuanced and/or finessed uses/expressions of power and understanding that the latter may have. Also, unlike in a video game, a proper narrative will show how age is as much a debilitating influence as it is one that provides the opportunity for skill and knowledge -- Yoda's the best, but that can still only be by a level of more narrow degrees rather than a video-game-esque "I'm a 143rd level Jedi Ass-Master". Yoda proved to be Dooky's superior (which is why the latter had to create a distraction in order to escape); but none of that involved Dooky being *dominated*, by any degree. It's all in the margins.
