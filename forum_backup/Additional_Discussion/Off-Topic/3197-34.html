        S6E9 - The Saddle Row Review

        There's something about this episode that reminds me of the Dresden Files. Somehow, the way more shit just keeps piling on is hilarious to me. The individual bits in the episode is pretty funny on their own, but the fact that the characters get to comment on them makes them perfect. And so great timing with many of those little bit scenes, like AJ's and Dashie's hoof bump.

        Really, the episodes when the characters just get to rub against each other like that (and not in any other way) are my favourites, because there are so many developed relationships between each and everyone that it could fill an episode alone. While it's cool that we get continuity in the form of Rarity's boutique (and is it just me, or has season 6 really pushed the long term status quo in a way previously unseen?), it's really just an excuse for the group to do something all together. And that's great! This isn't the most important episode, but it's up there among the funniest.

            Well, the mid-season hiatus is soon over, so I should probably wrap up the first half, and since I do just love the sound of my own voice (sight of my own text?)...

            And yes, I did watch them ages ago, I just couldn't be bothered to write anything. :P

            S6E10 - Applejack's "Day" Off

            Cute. AJ-Rarity has been an antagonistic pairing a few times, so it's nice to see them get along. It's also useful to point out the value of reflecting on your own behaviour. AJ is a silly pony. Otherwise, not much to say. It worked.

            S6E11 - Flutter Brutter

            Fluttershy's parents. yay.

            So... Zephyr Breeze. I kind of like him. It's clear that he's supposed to be an unwitting asshole, and they made that so clear that I kind of have to admire the craftspeople at work on the show. He managed to walk a fine line between being really exasperating and outrageous (I'm ashamed I found his hitting on Rainbow as hilarious as I did), and still making you realize that he was supposed to fill that role and that it actually made his character better. That said, he doesn't need to come back. Ever.

            The lesson kind of hit home for me. I do rely a lot on my family to stay afloat, and while it's not like I crash in their basement, they still fund a lot for me since my education has had a few false starts. It made me realize how ready I am to actually start having a proper job and make an income that I can sustain myself on.

            Since it's a Fluttershy episode, it was about confidence and speaking up. I've resigned myself to her being a one trick pony that way. However, I did think they gave it a twist with her not being the struggling one in this case. It's certainly one of her better ones.

            S6E12 - Spice Up Your Life

            Every boy and every girl! Spice up your life! People of the world! Spice up- sorry, I'll stop.

            This was... pretty bad. Really. I don't know the first thing about food criticism, but I'm pretty sure it doesn't work like that. In fact, the idea that Zesty Gourmand was somehow at fault for publishing her opinion is strange indeed to me. That's literally what a critic does. True, those opinions should be based in solid arguments and evidence, but we do get an explanation of Zesty's critical perspective in the episode (she values the subtle and rare tastes, as she sees them as indicative of a high degree of cooking craftmanship). I'd argue that the fault is with the crowd, who blindly followed her ratings instead of reflecting on if those ratings would be a good guiding tool for them. Now, it's true that at some point a critic will have to look at themselves and acknowledge the power they wield, and take that into account when writing their reviews, and that might be where Zesty is at fault. That said, in the end, I find I have to respect her for standing her ground and adhering to her own perspective of culinary criticism.

            Also, since when has Rarity been about conformity? Yes, she's into the whole high society deal, which requires a degree of adjustment to standards, but she's always been a trend setter, not a follower. I don't think she filled her role well.

            Well then, I'm ready. Bring on the next one.

                S6E13 - Stranger Than Fan Fiction

                I was guarded when I found out that this would be a Daring Do episode. I felt that the previous Daring Do episode was divided into two almost wholly separate parts: the one that was about fandom and the one that was an adventure. The first part, that was about Rainbow and Twilight was delightfully meta and reflective of fandoms in general and the brony fandom in particular, and it felt like it was talking about, and to, me. The second part was about Daring Do, and it was... a normal adventure episode. I was less than impressed by the 90 degree swerve in the direction of the dramatic arc there.

                Stranger Than Fan Fiction was what Daring Don't should have been. There's an adventure in the background, but the fandom theme doesn't stop because of it. Daring Do is there, but it's never about her (which is a personal preference, of course; she's not so interesting to me). Instead, the fandom theme is always in the foreground and the message is important, if not original. They're talking about problems that kids and adults alike have in real life, and issues that touch on communication in the internet age and, along that trajectory, political discourse. That is a solid message.

                And, y'know, Quibble Pants. This was his episode. I gather that the voice actor is well known in voice work, and it shows. Sharp delivery, clever lines, dripping with personality in a way that even the mane six doesn't reliably manage.

                Summary: This Daring Do episode was good since it wasn't about Daring Do.

                Edit: I tried to figure out which one of Rainbow Dash and Quibble Pants I was most like myself, but I couldn't really figure out if they represented any kind of dichotomy. If so, it's not very clear. Neither of them seem to hold sustainable positions either way. RD can be seen as "show committed", loving whatever had the Daring Do logo on it due to her own relationship with the character (in her case personally). QP instead looks at the texts first and thinks about how they play into the external factors that matter to him (like believability and continuity). Those readings are really forced though.

                        S6E15 - 28 Pranks Later

                        Yeah, this one was solid. The title kind of gave it away, but it was still a pretty drastic turning point when MLP became a zombie movie. It's also didn't pass me by that it hinted at the infamous Cupcakes fanfic at times, but not too heavily. Overall, the callback of Rainbow's prank habit and the sudden turn towards very unorthodox genre territory created a really clever mix of familiar and surprising that made the effect of the contrast that much bigger. I'm no horror expert, but I feel like the invasion of the strange into the familiar is a cornerstone of the horror genre, and they really built that up in this episode.

                        This is the kind of shit that would really scare me when I was young, and I wouldn't have liked it then because I was wuss. Other kids would enjoy the sense of thrill and the catharsis that it brought, and I absolutely think this is a good episode for kids and adults alike.

                                            S6E16 - The Times They Are A Changeling

                                            Okay, that was a funny title.

                                            How do I put this... you know that feeling you get sometimes of "is this My Little Pony?!" or "oh my god, only in FiM"? Like when Twilight had a DBZ fight with Tirek or when there was a whole episode devoted to background characters and fandom memes?                                             This episode was the opposite of that. The only time when I felt a little spark of interest was when Spike rejected Thorax the first time, but that was over in a moment. The theme was also a bit confused: most of the episode was focused on prejudice, but then Spike's song was about how a "changeling can change", which made it sound like Thorax had done something wrong and was seeking redemption. Which he might have done previously, but not towards the Crystal Empire.

                                            I don't want to say it was a bad episode. The theme was strong and important. But it was also formulaic, lacking that edge that I know MLP has. For once, I felt like I might not be the target audience here. That's bound to happen sometimes.

                                                    S6E19 - The Fault in Our Cutie Marks

                                                    So, I came into this expecting a story about a teenage filly with terminal cancer meeting the colt of her life, also with cancer, and them having a more fulfilling year together than most of the mares and stallions around them because love is infinite no matter how big your infinity is. I'm only slightly disappointed.

                                                    "What the apples" is now the cutest way of not swearing on air.

                                                    Yeah, I really liked this. Gabby was delightful, and I liked that they adressed one of the issues I have with cutie marks; they are often portrayed as something you get when you find the right situation and which then dictates your job, kind of like a lottery where you have to search for the ticket. They're actually supposed to be an external representation of your inclinations, which in fact allows for great flexibility in your life choices. Main example is Rarity: she's all about fashion, craftmanship and beauty, but her actual cutie mark relates to finding precious stones.

                                                    I thought the whole episode was high-energy fun with a subtle theme of cultural inclusivity. Really solid.
