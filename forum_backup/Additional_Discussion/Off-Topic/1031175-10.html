                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        Out of curiosity, who's identity is harmed by using the word "vagina" instead of the term "female sex organ"?"
                        I would think "nobody." But then again, I would also think that "sex is female, gender identity is male, expression is male" (AKA S:F/G:M/E:M) is a harmless-yet-comprehensive way to describe someone. Apparently not for everyone?...

                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        Oh, certainly not. Our identities are constantly being shaped by all manner of influences, including our physical forms.
                        ...
                        No, but I do believe that we are all the ultimate authorities on our own identities. No one can know how you feel better than you do, so whether or not saying you are X makes you X, it would be wrong of someone else to say, "no, you're Y."
                        I disagree, for two reasons.
                        1. Just because I am the ultimate authority on myself doesn't mean my capacity for self-awareness is any good.
                        2. As a participant in a society of people, and as a creature whose creation was not of my own will, neither my form nor my function is decided wholly by me.

                        To some extent, my being is defined by external forces. I feel it is only reasonable to accept that who and what I am is not for me to determine alone. If someone or something tells me "You're Y!" when I think I'm X, then I might be in the position of needing to "prove" that I am X. Or maybe they need to prove that I'm Y. Context changes how that exchange goes, but in any case I can't take my self-identity for granted or insist that other people accept it without question. I need to be prepared to either assert my identity against resistance, or accept a different definition for myself.

                        As far as this applies to language, my thinking is that transpeople need to do one of two things:
                        1. Change the definitions of existing words until they fit easily within those definitions.
                        2. Find different words to define themselves, and assert those words until they become commonly accepted.

                        ...I suspect that this is all a bit more abstract than what you were originally aiming for, which was "Transmen/women should be acknowledged as real men/women." If so, I apologize for the tangent.
