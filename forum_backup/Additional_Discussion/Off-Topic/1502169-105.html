                                            Like any media genre part of the problem is that the definitions are going to be fuzzy. If you only watch "classic" noir... of course they're all in black and white. If you include later "neo-noir" films you're going to get color films. That's just when the movies where made and what the available technology (or at least budgets) was.

                                            So, by point:

                                            1 - the main period of noir films was still during the black and white era of film making. While color film existed, it was extremely expensive and the costs didn't come down until after the initial genre heyday. Modern noir films (or whatever sub-noir label you want to apply) may or may not keep to their aesthetic. Nolan's Memento is kind of the perfect example of a neo-noir as it is half black and white, and half color.

                                            2 - The minority of film noirs are actually PI lead stories. Undercover/plain clothes police and overt criminals are more common protagonists in the genre. Femme fatales are common protagonists during a period where female lead movies were not common. The PI lead is more present in modern references to specific genre hits being icons for the genre rather than being what the genre was actually like. Imagine in 60 years people treating the first Iron Man movie as the prototypical superhero movie, and thus all references to superhero films are about rich geniuses in power armor despite how obvious it is to us now that's really not what the genre is like.

                                            3 - Monologues are definitely part of the genre because it's meant to highlight the isolation and alienation experienced by people on the edges of society. The whole point is that these are people with nobody to talk to that would understand them and the hard life they've been pushed into. Like any genre it's something that can be done well, or not. If you hate jump scares you're not going to like the horror genre much, but that doesn't mean it's hard to grasp the role of the technique and how it appeals to people that do.

                                            4 - Lots of noir protagonists don't die... not really sure where that's coming from. But the name "noir" comes from the idea that these are films with dark themes and gritty plots. It's a genre that mixed the hopeless of people that didn't recover well from the Great Depression with an influx of German creatives fleeing from the rise of Nazism. So, yeah, it's not a genre where heros win or even survive as a default.

                                            Like, if you don't like the genre, that's cool, there's plenty of them, but it doesn't sound like you've gotten access to the variety within it.
