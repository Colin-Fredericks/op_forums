                                                            So, listening to the glass cannon podcast of the PF2 playtest rules.

                                                            I really like the new action economy. Making everything an action and giving you three actions creates a lot of interesting tradeoffs every turn of combat, without bogging the game down in a million different categories of action. I also love some of the things they're playing around with, with certain spells or maneuvers getting stronger if you spend more than one action on them. I don't love going back to movement being an action instead of its own separate resource like in 5e, but the idea of things like the number of magic missiles you fire being determined by how many actions you use when casting it is, I think, worth that trade-off.

                                                            On a similar note, I think I like that raising a shield is an action. Makes shield-use a bit of a tradeoff instead of an always-on AC bonus. But I am concerned about if this will cause sword-and-board to be inferior to dual-wielding or 2-handed weapons. We'll see.

                                                            I'm iffy about adding level as a bonus to so many things. Adding half-level to everything was one of the things I really disliked in 4e, and was why bounded accuracy was such a strong selling point to me in 5e. I find purely numerical advancement boring, but acceptable, and artificial advancement detestable. I can't tell yet if this +level is indeed what I would consider artificial advancement, but it's definitely a red flag for me.

                                                            I like the sound of Class Feats. Very little to go on so far, but it is seeming like something that would appeal to my design sensibilities so far.

                                                            I'm unsure about the whole "encounter mode, exploration mode, downtime mode" thing. It doesn't seem bad necessarily, but it seems to be unnecessarily mechanizing something I think should be handled organically by a DM.

                                                            I'm ambivalent about the new handling of Initiative. I do like that you can apply a variety of different skills to Initiative depending on what you're doing when combat starts. In concept, I think that's great. But I'm a little worried that it will end up telegraphing ambushes when the DM asks, "for no particular reason, let's just go around the table and have everyone say what their character is doing and how alert they are."

                                                            I like the slightly higher starting HP. That was something I enjoyed in 4e (even if HP did bloat too much too fast after that). And I really like that some portion of that higher starting HP comes from raceancestry. That gives me hope that ancestry might have a bigger impact on how a character plays in this edition. I would love it if a Dwarf Fighter actually played meaningfully differently than a Human Fighter, rather than just having slightly different bonuses and penalties that don't end up making much difference in the long run.

                                                            I'm curious about the idea of "bulk" as an encumbrance system. I'm thrilled that they're doing away with counting individual pounds. But it does still sound like ultimately a means of counting weight, where I would prefer something a bit more abstract. Interested to see more specifics on how this will work.

                                                            Kinda neat that illusion magic can fool divination magic.

                                                            "That exceeded my armor class by 10, which means it's a critical hit. Double damage."

                                                            Apparently "expert craft" on a weapon means it has +1 to hit, "master craft" means it has +2, and "legendary craft" means +3. All of this unrelated to magic. I like that. I've been suggesting exactly such a change in D&D for a long time, so nice to see PF going there.

                                                            Weakness is still rated in terms of "Weakness (Damage Type) X", with each hit dealing damage of the appropriate type doing X additional damage. Presumably resistance will work the same way. Not unexpected, but it is a hint of the fiddlyness that I don't care for in 3.5 and PF1.
