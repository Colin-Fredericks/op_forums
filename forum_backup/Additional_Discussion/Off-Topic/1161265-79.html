                                Originally posted by Camilla View Post
                                I do own two retro-clones of 0e, Full Metal Plate Mail (a near-identical replica of the original boxed set) and White Box Fantasy Medieval Adventure Game (a more modernized yet still simple clone) and I am thinking of buying the PDF's of the original material from DTRPG in the near future. I'll buy the core boxed set, along with all four supplements and maybe even Chainmail as the PDF's themselves are fairly low-priced.
                                To put this in its context with other retro clones of 0E.

                                Delving Deeper, Full Metal Plate Mail and White Box FMAG differ in how much of the Greyhawk supplement they use. This supplement was released in 1975, one year after the core books.

                                Delving Deeper goes purely by the core books, meaning it does not use ability modifiers much outside Dexterity, Constitution and Charisma and for the first two they only go to +- 1. Also it doesn't use the combat system introduced in Greyhawk, instead it uses Chainmail's rules, which are more complex and give the game more of a single unit exploratory wargame feel, i.e. more like an actual traditional "game" than an RPG. It also doesn't use any RPG language or concepts not known in 1974, meaning some things take longer to explain than you might think. (They can't reference them as special cases of more general rules in common knowledge)

                                Full Metal Plate Mail is similar to Delving Deeper, but uses modern RPG language and the combat system from Greyhawk, that is the ancestor of the combat system in later editions.

                                White Box FMAG uses modern language, the d20 combat system and adds the Thief from Greyhawk. It also lists some house rules common at the time, such as the combat order from Dr. John Holmes and the race-as-class Elf (the race-as-class Elf would become an official rule in B/X and BECMI D&D)

                                Swords and Wizardry White box is very similar to White Box FMAG, it just drops the thief, simplifies saving scores and isn't written quite as well in my opinion.

                                Swords and Wizardry Core is 0E core and Greyhawk with a heavy dose of the author's favourite house rules built in.

                                Swords and Wizardry Complete is a retro clone of 0E core and all four supplements, with some of the author's house rules.

                                Seven Voyages of Zylarthen is actually an odd combination of 0E core and Greyhawk, removing the core Cleric and adding the Greyhawk thief, as well as altering the monster list to fit more with what the author thought was best about the feel of 0E.

                                            Off hand it seems like the Alchemist is going to have a bit less utlity in PF2 than in PF1. In PF1 the Alchemist had a ton of versatility and utility, depending on how you built them. For example, you could easily build an Alchemist to be a primary healer by picking up healing bombs, some healing extracts and maybe an archetype to push your healing a bit more, while still having all of the other fun stuff alchemists get. In PF2 however, Alchemists are losing extracts which is going to seriously cut down on their overall utility. Of course, all we've gotten is a preview, and it's very much a work-in-progress, so it's hard to say for sure how things will pan out. For example, if most of the things that previously required extracts to perform are now capable of being done via alchemist tools, then the Alchemist won't have lost much overall.

                                            No word on the witch, only the Alchemist and the Oracle. The witch is a very popular class though, so I wouldn't be surprised if it appears in the corebook.

                                                            So, my first Starfinder session happened and it went well. We all enjoyed it and only one player tried to kill a child, which counts as a triumph with the group I have. I still like the premises and I think everyone is on board with them and got interested by what I put in front of them.

                                                            The two most notable hacks, at least to the D20's standards, is that I have not asked the players to write an alignment nor to pick a god. The alignment is just a matter of personal preference and awareness that it would have mattered little, but I'm interested about discussing the deities, gods and how you implement them in game. It's not as if I don't like the pantheon the book presents (some I actually love, though I had to change others since I made up my own setting), but I'd rather introduce the players to the various faiths of the galaxy (or whatever, depending on what and where you play) through game and let them decide if they want to worship one or more.

                                                            It might be only my experience, which admittedly is not the best when it comes to playing D&D/Pathfinder, but DMs were always quite strict about gods and characters, and it always looked more as a nuisance than a boon to me. I haven't changed the setting much, at least when it comes to planar entities, so it's not like the gods are not there, but I see no reason to make them important to characters by default, either in Starfinder or elsewhere (unless the relationship between the character and a faith is not important because of background, of course)

                                                            Thoughts?

                                                            EDIT: with "thoughts?" I mean I'd like to hear how you deal with gods in your game. Could use some perspective and stories from more experienced people
