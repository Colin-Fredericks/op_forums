                                                    Minecraft is not a game about colonialism.

                                                    And yet.


                                                    I agree with Dan(on the whole, but I'm gonna dig into the particular of it happening)-no one intended for that narrative to emerge-but it happens.

                                                    Thankfully, Minecraft's accidental colonialist message is not a deal breaker or major message, and there are a lot of media pieces that have similar problems that are also not going to direly affect the way people think about the world-but a lot of other unintended messages are harmfully propagated wide and far before someone takes catches it as well.

                                                    The accidental nature of that messaging, in either case, is more problematic than a deliberate implementation of it. A deliberate implementation happens either because a) a person who deliberately puts forward a problematic concept in media/um often does so because their point is to highlight it and comment on it, and ultimately use the media/um as a way to ultimately demonstrate the problems of that concept, or b) legitimately believes in it's implementation, and has it's sincerity show up on it's sleeves in it's own way in such a way that blood and bone will clearly find their own.

                                                    Accidentally messaged things, by contrast, is hard to deal with because it's harder for creators or readers to admit to and own because the other points come across more strongly, and the ignorance of basically everyone makes it harder to admit that a problematic narrative emerged out of something else. It is not as apparent even when it happens. So people can walk away with a reinforcement of any number of values, many of which can be toxic or otherwise negative, without ever confronting it, and when confronted with it have an easy time just dismissing it, or even getting critically defensive of it.

                                                    Now once that's done, okay, it happens, people make mistakes, things slip through the cracks. It's bad, but in an ideal situation, the creators will own the problem and course correct in some way going forward, or at least, you know, apologize for it. The end goal is to get participants in (a) media/um, both creators and consumers, to examine their biases that they might not otherwise have credited affecting their work or reading, and at least decide for themselves how they let the reading go from there.

                                                    The problem, then, is when creators don't do any of that, and when the fans of a property go long and far in defying the acknowledgment of any such flaws in media. When given the chance to take their passive participation in the creative and consumptive process, they instead lash out and allow what is basically a societal poison or disease to spread because the alternative is somehow uncomfortable.

                                                    As a more at-home example, I love the fuck out of Beast, and I always have-but it started off with some really bad messaging that was not intended, and part of what it is now is because Beast still has some uncomfortable ideas at play in it's narrative. The primary difference is that Beast has owned a lot of the really bad unintended-but-emergent narrative that presented, worked hard to erase the most unneccesary elements of it, and has refined the rest of it into part of it's point as playing a villain for fun and psychologically healthy reason while still keeping the meta-narrative clear that it's current problems are to be seen as problems and not, you know, something to praise and defend.

                                                    If Beast had continued onward with it's original draft and just dug in deep to that, I couldn't defend it now that I'm aware of what the hell I was missing in that first draft, and probably wouldn't touch it. But I stepped back, learned from those who had gripes with it, and saw a better Beast than the one I originally read. So far, Beast since has been working towards that, and heaven knows I'm gonna keep it up where I'm involved.

                                                    But anyways, yeah, people absolutely write messages they didn't intend, and we as the audience absolutely ingest it as well.

                                                    The average person eats 8 spiders while they sleep. Passive media creation and consumption is much the same thing-taking in something you might not want to while you just sleep-watch a show. With responsible media creation and consumption, you want people to at least have the option of knowing what they're eating (and possibly telling other people to eat) as much as possible, at the least.*
