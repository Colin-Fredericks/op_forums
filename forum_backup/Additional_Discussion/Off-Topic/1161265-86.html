    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Speaking of D&D as a survival game, I’ve always wanted to give Torchbearer a try. But it’s the kind of game I don’t expect to be able to find other people for. While a game that aims to simulate what a truly miserable experience dungeon delving would actually be sounds like a delightful time to me, I expect most people would not find it very fun.

    Burning Wheel in general seems neat, but Torchbearer in particular appeals to me for its focus on the smaller details that get brushed over in heroic fantasy, like not only “how much weight are you carrying?” but “where did you pack this item?”, or the lingering effects of exhaustion, hunger, thirst, and trauma that would build up over the course of a delve.
    If I can ask: what’s the appeal in this? I know that we have pretty divergent tastes, but I’m just not seeing much of the appeal at all of something so wrapped up in minutiae and grit.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

            If I can ask: what’s the appeal in this? I know that we have pretty divergent tastes, but I’m just not seeing much of the appeal at all of something so wrapped up in minutiae and grit.
            It wouldn’t be in a traditional heroic fantasy story. But that’s not the kind of story Torchbearer is about. Torchbearer is to D&D as Darkest Dungeon is to Dragon Age. It’s not about the epic quest to save the kingdom, it’s about the day to day struggles of people trying to survive as dungeon delvers. I generally prefer smaller scale stories. I’m more interested in “what will you have to do to make it through one more day?” than in “will our heroes defeat the evil necromamcer?” Torchbearer is specifically tailored to facilitate the former.

            That’s also why, as I mentioned in the other thread, Fallout has never been satisfying to me, because what I want out of a post-apocalyptic game is a focus on the harrowing experiences. I want to be trading my last magazine of bullets for despately needed medical supplies, not a thriving bottle cap based economy and neuclear-powered exo-suits.

            But, like, I totally get that I’m the weird one for finding that level of grit appealing at all.
