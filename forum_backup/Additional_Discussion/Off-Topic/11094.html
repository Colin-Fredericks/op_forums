    Game Ideas You'll Never Use

    11-15-2013, 03:13 AM
    Well, "never" doesn't always turn out to be true. Still, I think most of us have dreamed up a few ideas - perhaps a thematic and longrunning campaign arc, a bizarre new setting for adventures, or a character who instantly takes on a life of their own - which we have then had to keep shelved indefinitely. Usually for some stupid reason like "it's my turn to run the game", or "the group really doesn't feel like playing that", or "you don't have a group, remember, that's why you're talking to yourself about this." :P

    So in the spirit of listing out my dusty ideas and rummaging magpie-like through yours: what unrealized dreams do you have for your games?

    (Two examples of mine to get started, nothing important lies past this point):

    - I'd like to run a Promethean campaign which questions whether the Pilgrimage is a good thing and leaves the answer open-ended. Maybe I'd even offer the players the choice to cheat the Pilgrimage, instead of just completing it or rejecting it.

    - I'd like to play a fantasy setting where humans aren't given any special central role. They're present, but are of no relative importance, and nobody pays them any special attention.

                          I've had an idea for a WtA game for a while. The idea is that instead of the typical fight against the apocalypse until it happens they intentionally cause it, kill the wyrm and have the weaver take his place until they can return the wyrm uncorrupted to the balance. Unfortunately when I first had this idea I was in a group that if they didn't have combat going on every 20 minutes they would get bored and I didn't want the game to just be combat. And yes, I know this flies in the face of some of the metaplot. I was reminded a hundred times by the Encyclopedia guy we had in the group. You know the guy, the guy who whenever you try to do something that doesn't follow the metaplot he pipes up and says something.

                          I have also had an idea for a Hunter game involving the Bale Hounds. Was basically just go around, investigating these strange murders. then you find out that a Bale Hound pack is helping a murder spirit and terror spirit do their thing and just terrorize a populace. I got a couple of sessions of that one under my belt and dropped it because everyone thought playing hunters meant shooting everything that moved.

                                                  I'm not sure what system this would be for, probably one of the Mages or Changelings.

                                                  You know how periodically there are those songs that kind of just catch on? "Let it Go", "Father and Son", even "Tik Tok". The songs might not necessarily be good, but for some reason you can't turn around without hearing them, and people are invoking lyrics as arguments for or against certain points. That's not an accident; these stories are signals from an entity trapped too many eons ago to count, and it's taken this long to learn how to reach out even that little. The songs literally shape how people think; tiny amounts individually, but slowly slowly the entity is bringing about the conditions required to release it.

                                                  Course, there's a deeper problem: people have become dependent on these stimulations. After so many centuries of being slowly engineered by these songs, peoples ability to direct themselves, previously humanities greatest power, has weakened. If they were just stopped, then so would humanity. Unfortunately, if they aren't stopped, the entity will be released, and risk the whole of reality.
