                                        Equating the DSA and Justice Dems to the alt-right is some pretty questionable stuff.

                                        The problem is more simple really: The majority of Democratic voters, and for various reasons this concentrates further the higher up you go, are institutionalists: they consider having a functional imperfect government more important than the risk of such radical change that the system break before it can be made into a system that better reflects the ideals of the majority of the Democratic coalition. That's why centrist and incrementalists do so well in the party. Their end goals are largely the same as the farther left elements of the party (and the far left doesn't have anyone else to seriously partner with, so the party just needs to keep the big picture together to keep most of them in the tent), but doing things "slow" via the norms and projecting competent governance keeps the very strong protectionist wing of the party from getting too nervous about losing gains by going big.

                                        The Republicans have gone in the opposite direction. Their strategy has been to let the government collapse if they can't get their way. The reason it's worked so well despite lots of moderate/centrist Republicans that should counteract the far right like what happens with the Democrats is simple: the so-called conservative movement isn't actually fighting to protect legislative gains for their voters. They're not trying to protect rights, government services, and so on that they fought hard to get. They're fighting to protect power. The power of the Trump-age Republican party is that the whole coalition is solidified around fighting for a binary: being in power or sabotaging anything anyone that tries to have more power than them does. The GOP didn't lose a particularly large number of voters this year, the Democrats turned out more.

                                        AOC could replace Pelosi tomorrow, and the rhetoric might change but the political reality wouldn't. Point to whichever politicians you can name, they're a product of that reality, and they can't not be.

There are also Democrats that look at the state of the economy and don't think that the GND is the right sort of stimulus to fix the COVID-19 disaster, and are going to want to deal with that first to get the economy back to zero, and then will get behind the GND to start really growing it again.
