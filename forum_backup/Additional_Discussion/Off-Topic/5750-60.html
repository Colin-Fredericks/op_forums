        I also saw Spy, with Melissa McCarthy, who I've only seen before in her appearances on SNL. This movie was also sort of misrepresented, and I think it did a disservice. The, admittedly few, ads that I saw for it seemed to suggest that the protagonist Susan, played by McCarthy, was going to be a sort of bumbling idiot who stumbles their way through an espionage mission similar to Maxwell Smart or how Mr. Bean might do it. 'Ha ha, look at the fat lady trying to be a spy.' But she's not that way at all in the movie. She's certainly placed her life on hold to assist another man, and she doesn't have in the field experience, but she's a CIA analyst and very good at her job, and she's also prepared for field work. It's something she's long wanted to do before being won over by agent Bradley Fine, who appreciated her skills and sniped her before she could become a field agent, tasking her eternally to be his back-up.

        Just saw the Spielberg movie JAWS all the way through for the first time.

        Holy cow, people. If you're wondering how Spielberg became a household name, watch this movie. Good Lord.
        Just wait till you get to the 3D one!

        But yeah, Jaws was pretty much THE original Summer Blockbuster, barely predating Star Wars, which solidified the concept (summer used to be a dumping ground for movies the studios didn't expect much from, with winter/Christmas being reserved for the big hits). The impact of both of those films on pop culture, and how they helped to shape the cinematic landscape of the 80s, cannot be underestimated!
