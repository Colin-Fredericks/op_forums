    I'd like to know which Necronomicon you're reading.

    But Lovecraft does make for a good reading. I'm particularly fond of At The Mountains of Madness, but he has a lot of other wonderful works. You only have to be wary of his many problematic stances, as they're all over the place in his works. The Call of Cthulhu isn't a classic for nothing and is a short read, a must read, but it's also especially riddled with racism to the point that I know Lovecraft fans that couldn't finish reading it due to sheer disgust.

    On the bright side of things, Lovecraft was part of a literary movement that prized the free sharing of ideas, and never claimed ownership of anything he created. In a sense he was one of the earliest adopters of the concept of Creative Commons. So you can enjoy his creations in all kinds of flavors and variations in works that date back to the time of the originals.

                            Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                            did Poe want to impress his readers with his decoding and ciphering?
                            Actually, yes, that was precisely that, and they knew. He specifically wrote The Gold-Bug after publishing a work on secret codes and the public's response showing there was a big interest on the topic.

                            Notice also that cryptography was little known and seen as almost magical at the time. We have a lot more familiarity with it today, but his public was really impressed even by the simplest ciphers. Just to be clear, this is not a "past" thing, this is specifically a "19th century" thing, as the use and understanding of ciphers waxed and waned through history.

                            Originally posted by Iguazu View Post

                                If anyone is interested in the development of watches, I highly recommend Dava Sobel's book Longitude, about the quest to develop proper maritime chronometers. (Being able to accurately know the time of both your current location and that of the port you left from is necessary for determining longitude, and the older pendulum or water powered clocks don't work properly on ships. The Portuguese, Spanish, French, and English governments were offering massive amounts of money to anyone who could solve the problem as far back as the 1500s.)
