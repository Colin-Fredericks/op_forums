    It's still a pretty apples and oranges comparison. The View, which is a more comparable US show to The Social, pays its hosts in a similar range as what Melissa Grelio makes. Daytime shows are seen as less prestigious, and bring in less viewers, than prime time news shows do... so it's less well paying (also since they tend to have multiple hosts that all demand relatively large paychecks instead of one personality, it's only functional if they all take "smaller" salaries).

    Does Rachel Maddow make an obscene amount of money? Sure. But she's an outlier at the top of her industry (and even then, she's a prime time host, a podcaster, and regularly writes out non-fiction books, so she's putting in a lot of hours compared her bosses that get paid even more for making decisions like "our core audience likes Rachel Maddow, keep her well paid so she stays on the air," and then consider that a days work.

    Income disparities in the US are horrific, but the tiny fraction of individuals that actually work hard and get big paychecks for it are not the problem compared to all the corporate execs that draw huge salaries while not doing any hard work. Do we pay the top tier of entertainers too much? Probably. Do we let entertainment executives demand ridiculous compensation packages despite not even doing any of the work that makes their companies profitable? Yes. Is the second the bigger problem? Very much yes.

            Salary caps are something sports implement of their own volition because it's good for their bottom line: they don't have to pay players as much, and avoiding richer teams from buying up the contracts of all the best players helps ensure seasons are competitive so audiences have investment and don't disengage because a few teams win everything predictably.

            There's no reason for that in other industries.

            The problem is that this is a feature, not a bug, in capitalist systems. If this isn't happening, a capitalism based system would be collapsing. Since we're reaching the limits of Keynesian economic's ability to restrain capitalism via mixed economy checks via socialist backstops, the inevitable thing that needs to happen is a new economic system; likely a socialism basis with capitalist policies layered on top (because people don't really like pure socialism... people like a bit of inequality because they want to feel like people that work harder get more) instead of our current approach. But who knows what'll happen.

                                    There's another problem really: Star Wars is a franchise that's been stuck in one continuity for too long.

                                    Something I watched a bit ago about the Halo video game franchise is that having one long on going continuity for 20+ years only hurts it in the long run, and Star Wars is basically in the same position despite the Disney Legacy cutting back of old canon. When everyone is wed to the original creator(s)'s vision and continuity in perpetuity, well after they're not working on it any more, it's a creative trap. If you stray too far from canon to make something you want to do, fans hate it for not being true to the franchise. If you stick to things as they've always been, it gets stale as you're just copying someone else's ideas without being them with their peculiar and individual passions and quirks.

                                    In franchises where reboots/resets/whatever are quickly established as normal, and fans are used to it, each new creative team has a lot more freedom to make the best iteration of the franchise they can do. It might still suck, it might be divisive, it might be beloved. But you only get beloved when new teams get a chance do to their own spin on things instead of having to take 20 or 30 or more years worth of lore and make sure you stick to that script. If you're a fan of Spider-man or Batman, you might not like the current blockbuster movie, but there's probably a TV show or cartoon right up your alley, because both characters get fresh takes all the time. Or worst case you just wait a bit and eventually there's going to be a new movie or show with a new team and a new take that's your speed.

                                    I think that's why most of the best Star Wars stuff right now comes out of the grab bag that is the Disney streaming shows. Each show's creative team gets a lot more leeway to tell their Star Wars story without worrying about what it means for the big Cinematic Universe that's been building off of the original movie. Some of the shows to focus on that part of it, but others basically get away with having nothing to do with the Skywalkers and their epic space opera.

                                    Or, another example that's the source of my handle and avatar: the Gundam franchise has a "core" continuity in the Universal Century that has, by far, the most stuff set in it. But they toss completely new settings with radically different premises into the mix on a regular basis. If a story doesn't work as a UC story, you just make a new timeline that it does work in. Again, some are duds, and some are masterpieces, but the room to experiment nets a better overall result than if everything had to stick to the UC canon.
