                                                        I think it's a lot to ask of a superhero movies. It's easy to setup a scenario that's black & white on the moral compass and all, but how does a superhero monitor the US clandestine intelligence services, and their relationship with the military, to know which drone strike hit a civilian target with a purely retroactive justification, and which recklessly endangered bystanders to strike at least a target that puts it in the "arguable" category? How does this story work into a big action blockbuster?

                                                        It just turns into the Civil War (more comics really) plot line all over again where governments decide they need to enforce rules on supers so the superheroes don't get to write their own rules for how people should act, while superheroes feel like they can do a lot of good without those restrictions.

                                                        The only hero I can even imagine getting a serious go at this sort of plot line would be Martian Manhunter because he's not tightly associated with any specific human group like Superman, and has the powers that could actually put him in a place to confront these things head on. Superman was raised by humans, and Americans for that matter, and has already grown accustomed to how humans justify their actions. Martian Manhunter is a fully formed adult alien. He can more honestly challenge government actions with, "explain how this is acceptable to me."
