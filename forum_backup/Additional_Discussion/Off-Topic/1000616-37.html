                                Whereas before al-Assad was (understandably) pretty confident in his ability to use chemical weapons without worrying about a response from the US or the rest of the world, now he's probably rather less certain that he can use a chemical weapon, free from facing some kind of retribution for doing so.

                                Now, is making al-Assad and other dictators think twice before unleashing chemical weapons against civilian populations worth 80 million dollars? Personally I would say absolutely. Missiles are made to be used after all, and if we can use these missiles, in a way that doesn't actually kill anyone and helps ensure that chemical weapons aren't used to slaughter civilians, then I feel that's a fairly good way to use a missile all told. At the least it's a somewhat more moral use of a missile than using it to blow a bunch of dudes up.

                                Some people, of course, disagree. I've seen people elsewhere essentially say that if you spend money on a missile and then fire it in a way that doesn't kill other people, or that doesn't dish out massive property damage, then you've "wasted" the money you invested in the weapon. But well, different people will have different judgments when it comes to cost/benefit analysis that involves human lives.
