    There are a lot of interesting conversations going in this thread, but I wanted to go ahead and say what makes my gender/orientation identity because I don't get to talk about it too much.

    In the shortest form, I'm cissex male, gender-neutral, pansexual, and (maybe) biromantic.

    To break that down ...

    Cissex male: I feel comfortable being physically male or, in other words, having boy parts and male secondary sexual features like facial hair, larger hands/feet/joints, etc. That feels correct to me. However . . .

    Gender neutral: . . . I don't have any inward internalization of any of that. Put another way, I have no inner sense of maleness or any kind of male identity. This means a lot of things, but most practically, I don't have any instant sympathy for other men nor instant friction with other women. There's just no substance to that kind of identity. (As a strange aside, sexism didn't make sense to me for a long time because I was all, ". . . but we're all people. Why would gender identity matter?" I'm still that way to a significant degree.)

    Pansexual: I'm sexually attracted to people of nearly every gender identity and sexual feature configuration you can think of: pre-op transwomen, men, women, androgynous biomales ... Not androgynous biofemales, though, go figure. There is a lot more gradation and specifics within each of those categories, but that's the short version.

    Biromantic: This means that I have had romantic feelings for and could pursue romantic relationships with both men and women. I put "maybe" with this because I have emotional hang-ups about dating men that I'm working to get over, I've only dated women so far, and I have yet to meet a nonbinary person for whom I felt romantically.

    Hope that all made sense!

                                        Gay. Male. A "Bear" if you are familiar with that group...

                                        I came out to myself when I started going to college. I secretly knew I was gay all through high school, and some of my classmates picked up on it (even though I am not particularly effeminate, I do tend to wear my emotions on my sleeve and the school bullies picked up on that. I denied it to myself and everyone else until I was able to get myself out of that environment.

I tried my damnedest to be as anonymous as possible, and I suppose I was scared that I might get even more attention and negative... I don't know...

                                        It took me a long time to come out to my gaming group. I knew they would approve, but the fact that I hadn't gotten the courage to do so already in its own way just made it harder. Plus, when it was game time it was all business, so it was kind of hard to find a moment to say... "Awesome, natural 20! By the way, I prefer to have sex with other men, if anyone was wondering."

                                        I met my BF, who I will refer to as CMS to protect the guilty, shortly before that. He was the one that actually introduced me to the whole "bear" thing.

                                        Basically, Bears are kind of a gay male subculture, which instead of preferring men who are smooth/shaven, thin, or athletically built (what our culture as a whole holds up as what an attractive male is supposed to look like) prefer men who have body hair, beards, body fat and/or muscles. We have lots of stupid names for lots of different body shapes and types

                                        My experience with Bears has actually been somewhere between positive and negative.

                                        On the positive side, I think bears have actually changed what I see as attractive to a degree? Or perhaps just invited me to explore what I felt was attractive about other body types that I hadn't considered before... It's actually pretty hard to tell, as it was so gradual, and makes me wonder how much control the media really has over what we find attractive. While I think most people would put what we find attractive strictly in the realm of biology, I think media and culture plays a much larger part than most people would guess.

                                        Sorry, tangent.

                                        Also, it was the first time in my entire life someone had told me I was attractive.

                                        On the negative side, it seems the Bear community, or the bear community that is easy to find, lean heavily toward polyamory and open relationships, whereas I am more of a monogamous kind of guy.

                                        My experience with bears in person has been somewhat negative as well... When CMS and I started dating, he took me to a local bear mens group. My experiences with that group were less than positive, such as the organizer's boyfriend who couldn't keep his hands to himself, and a trip to a cabin in Des Moines that made me feel out of place, like I had stepped out of my life and into somebody else's... Being in my early 20's, surrounded by much older men, all over one another... one guy (who I didn't know) noticed I was tense, and tried to make me feel more comfortable by giving me a shoulder massage... It didn't help. That night I couldn't sleep, over the sounds of sex downstairs... they had even given CMS and I the best bed in the house, but that just made me feel even more awkward and out of place. I would have left, but my car was pinned in, and I don't think CMS ever did understood how I felt that night. I was upset and he was upset that I was upset, not to mention that I never wanted to go on that trip in the first place, but only went because CMS basically begged me to go...

                                        So, the whole Bear thing has been both a blessing and a curse... Great idea, but not a fan of the community as a whole.

                                        CMS and I were together for 10 years. We had a good 5 years and a bad 5 years. Right there in the middle I discovered CMS had been cybering with another guy (CMS had wanted an open relationship from the start, but I insisted on monogamy) and I was left to try to pick up the pieces. Any time I brought the issue up, he deflected the whole issue by talking about how the guy he cyber cheated on with and I needed to "make up and be friends again." Which I never understood because he and I were never friends to begin with. He forced a confrontation, and I ended up saying some rather cruel things to said "friend".

                                        Nothing ended up getting resolved, but I did end up giving CMS my trust again, because I loved him, but the relationship felt broken from then on. He even asked me to marry him at one point, and I turned him down. Years later CMS did it again, and feeling hollow inside, I ended the relationship.

CMS has found someone new, and I am happy for him, but also a little jealous... he found a great guy who was able to give him the open relationship he wanted, while I am stuck alone without prospects trying to figure out what my next move is going to be. I know it's time to live for me, but I guess I haven't figured out what that entails yet...
