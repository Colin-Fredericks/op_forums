    And of course, as a player, you are not aware of the chances. I'm not going to tell you how hard it is to tame a tarrasque or persuade the tyrant or assassinate a well guarded lich king.                                         Okay, as somebody who's only experience with D&D and Pathfinder comes from video games, rather than table top, there's something about the game that has always confused me.

                                        That something being Meta Magic!

                                        Whenever I hear people review the Sorcerer class for the table top version, they always bring up Meta Magic as if it is a special ability that is exclusive to Sorcerers. But in every video game I've played, Meta Magic was a feat available to casters of all types. Sorcerers, Wizards, Clerics, Druids, Warlocks, Bards, Rangers, Paladins, they could all learn to use Meta Magic!

                                        So what is so great about the Sorcerer class' access to Meta Magic that makes them stand out?

                                        Or is making it a universal feat only something found in the digital version of the game?
