                            This whole attitude is one of the reasons why we have things like this consent form in the first place. The general RPG world wants to claim their more than adult enough to not need these tools, but then want to wave incredibly immature thinking in other people's faces.

                            We need consent tools because you sound - whether you intend to or not, or care or not - like the kind of person that would subject their players without warning or consent, to the graphic depictions of children being raped and then cannibalized, and then when everyone gets upset, you'd insist everyone but you is the problem.

                            Someone that seriously says, "there are no themes, stop reading into stuff," is someone who's only goal is to cause others emotional harm; and I absolutely believe that's true about the South Park creators. Because they said so. They just claim causing harm to as many people as possible somehow makes it OK.


                            1 - of course I'm not using it as an excuse for players to be ass holes to other people. That's absolutely ridiculous! If somebody at your table is spewing hate speech towards somebody in real life, then go ahead and deal with them. But if they're restricting such behavior to in character actions, and directing it towards other in game characters then that's perfectly fine.

                            2 - Bull shit, humans have been using the term "race" for thousands of years. What would you suggest we use to refer to each other as? Should we start identifying humans by various "breeds?" Because that sounds even worse!

                            3 - I'm against the idea of them being nuanced on a large scale. But I don't have any particular qualms about there being a rare outlier among their kind appearing once in a while. There's plenty of stories throughout fictional media that involve that very concept, usually with said outlier becoming an enemy of their own kind and fighting to oppose the evil they represent. As I said, when I use the word "evil" for describing a race, I do so in a context where evilness is ingrained into their very being. They can struggle against it, but they can never truly conquer that side of themselves.

                            Take the dhampir, Blade for example. No matter how many vampires he destroys over the course of his life, he still has that craving for human blood, and only manages to suppress it through a daily use of special drugs. He might stand on the side of humanity, and he might hate the undead with a burning passion. But he is and will always be a monster, one who's inherent nature will always threaten the very people he tries to protect.


                            4 - No, it's not stupid, and anybody who follows the rule of Show Don't Tell is likely to agree. I'm not sure if you're aware of the controversy that surrounded the first episode of Goblin Slayer, but I was checking around for videos talking about it. What I found was one discussion in which they go on about how many stories often claim that the enemy of the story is an objective threat which needs to be destroyed, but they often shy away from proving that to be the case. Sure, the villains may be depicted as doing evil things from time to time, but such behavior fails to drive home the idea that this threat needs to be eradicated.

                            Goblin Slayer addresses this idea, by going into graphic detail about why the protagonist has absolutely no empathy for the little green monsters. Those who haven't read the manga were probably shocked when he began clubbing baby Goblins to death in front of the Priestess. But during the Goblin Lord chapter, we see that even as infants, Goblins are just as prone to evil and chaos as their adult counterparts. The Goblin Lord was spared by a heroine who decided to take pity on him, when she saw him crying in terror as an infant. And the very second she turned her back to him and began to leave, the infant Goblin bashed her skull in with a rock!

                            Once again, claiming that your villain is evil and expecting people to take it on faith is kind of a weak sell. You have to prove to people that your villain is evil, and explain why they can not be reasoned with. Having an established lore which depicts them as being Inherently malicious from birth is a good way to go about accomplishing that.

                            Unless the narrative of the story is specifically focused on exploring philosophical world views, such as doing the wrong thing for the right reasons, I do not want to sympathize with the enemy. If they are my enemy, then the only thing I'm going to be focusing on is finding a way to bring them down, and then celebrating afterwards. If you want peaceful coexistence, send a diplomat with a gift basket. Not a party of adventurers who are armed to the teeth and looking for riches.
