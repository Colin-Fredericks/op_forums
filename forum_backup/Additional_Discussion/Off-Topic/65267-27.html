    Since we're in a recommending mood right now I'd like to suggest Hexed. It's the story of Lucifer, a woman who acts as a sort of occult-oriented Catwoman, without the body suit or cleavage. The characters first appearance was way back in the fantastic Fall of Cthulhu series, then she had her own 4 issue mini, called Hexed, back in 2009 where she was hired to steal an angels wings back for it and we were introduced to her patron, a woman with a mysterious past who is also heavily involved with the occult. Recently she's got an ongoing, also called Hexed, where she's dealing with the fallout of some of the things that happened in the four issue mini and her patron's new intern, who was sort of shanghai'd into the occult underworld without knowing anything about it.

    For fans of American Vampire, they're trying to ride on the success of that with Wolf Moon, a comic about golems. No, of course not, it's about werewolves. Sort of. The mythology behind them isn't like that of World of Darkness. I don't believe it's as good as American Vampire, personally, I think they made an error focusing more on the werewolf hunter than the werewolf itself, but that does sort of get explained (and changed) later. But if you're a werewolf fan it might be your thing. The werewolf is a particularly monstrous and violent thing.

    I'll also second Crossed, specifically recommending Badlands (which is sort of a series of different stories by different writers and artists about the same 'world') and Wish You Were Here (which is just one story). Alan Moore's currently doing an interesting short series in the Crossed universe right now, showing the future of humanity without the civilization we currently have. And I'll suggest Stitched, which apparently spawned from Garth Ennis' first film, and starts an interesting story.

    Oh, and Sam & Twitch. It's about a pair of detectives and while it Spawned from a superhero-ish comic, said antihero only appears in, I think, two frames throughout the entirety of the series, though, and it sticks to mostly down to earth detective stuff.

                            I like new Thor, and I find Titania being written out of character there. On the previous page Carl Creel, the man Titania hits, her husband, says some remarks that are probably similar to something the writer's heard in response to the change to a woman Thor.

                            As for the pushing a character they like, it's no different from Batman hugging Tim Drake in Robin and saying Tim's going to be better than him one day, and urging him to ignore Cassandra Cain, she's just crazy. Or all the Avengers agreeing that Spider-man is going to be a smarter and better hero than all of them when he matures. Or after Tony Stark's memory got erased, Thor offering him access to the treasures of Asgard to become rich again. Or Quentin Quire, the 'world's worst terrorist,' being handed over to Wolverine so he can go to school with other mutants like him and become the star of the comic.

                            In regards to dealing with real issues, there's nothing wrong with comics getting into that, especially when something is as topical as women in comics. It would be nice if it was handled more subtly or in character but that doesn't always happen.
