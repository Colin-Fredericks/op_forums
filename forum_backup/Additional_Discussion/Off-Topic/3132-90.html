                                I used to care about whether I was a good person or not. I am now starting to realize no one else does, because it's a selfish standard to apply. How righteous I am in my conscience is a thing that can only ever benefit me. What people care about is whether I am useful; useful to them, mostly. Whether I have skills or abilities that can benefit them somehow. That statement seems perhaps condemnatory, but I don't mean it that way. Our friends are our friends because we get something out of being friends with them, be that companionship or enjoyment or more tangible physical benefits, not because they're good people.

                                As I look back at all the friends I've lost, particularly friends from this website I drove away, I am forced to evaluate my skills and abilities, and realize that I have been so concerned with being a good person I have put no effort into learning to be a useful one. I have almost nothing to offer people except an example of how to fail that they can profit from studying in order to avoid being like me.

                                I am not crying because of the friends I've lost. That is a pain that will not heal, but is too old for me to weep over any more. Nor am I crying for self-pity, exactly, though I certainly feel enough of it right now.

                                I'm crying for my innocence. I still want to believe that putting in the effort to make compassionate choices is enough, but it isn't, and it never will be. Being good to people does not earn me any points, and the childish part of me that believes the world should be fair has taken a terrible blow.
