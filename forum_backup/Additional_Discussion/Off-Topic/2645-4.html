                I’ve mentioned elsewhere (I think) how I consider there to be four distinct Star Trek universes, which I call Classic Trek, the Star Fleet Universe, the NextGen retcon, and the Abrams-Trek reboot. With the official abandonment by Disney of the Star Wars Expanded Universe, I now consider there to be two Star Wars universes as well: and I’m quite comfortable thinking of Episodes I-III being exclusive to the New Star Wars. Heck, I’ll even be willing to say that there’s some divergence in the two universes’ versions of Episodes IV-VI: in the Classic Star Wars universe, Han shot first; in the New Star Wars universe, Greedo was an incompetent boob who couldn’t even shoot straight. We have never seen Classic Star Wars’ Episodes I-III, although we’ve seen hints about it in Episode VI deleted scenes and in the Thawn Trilogy: for instance, Owen Lars had no relation to Anikan Skywalker; he was Obi-Wan’s brother(-in-law). Anikan never set foot on Tatooine until Episode IV. And so on.

                There are a few novels that came out after Episode I that are slightly problematic because they attempt to reconcile the EU with the new revelations in the prequel series; I have no problem excising such connective tissue as needed.

                There are now two Star Wars; and I intend to judge each on its own merits.

                                            I've been thinking a lot about Star Wars recently (more so than usual, even!)...And I'm moving my Anticipation-meter to "Cautiously Optimistic". I still wish they would leave well enough alone, but that ship has sailed, so let's deal with what's coming. Or, even more fun, speculate how we'd do it!

                                            One night, Abrams, Kennedy, Lucas and whoever else is involved turns up at your door, confesses that they just can't get this thing right, and since you're the biggest SW geek they can find, they need *your help*. What decisions will you make?

                                            For me, the biggest problem is the overall story: SW is *not* Science-Fiction, it's Science-Fantasy, or Space Opera, if you prefer. It's a fairy tale, a pulp fable in skiffy trappings. It's not deep or complex, it's just telling a basic story of Good Vs. Evil. The original trilogy told the straightforward tale of how Good rose up to defeat Evil. The Prequels expanded this by showing how the Evil was originally Good that fell into Evil, only to be redeemed later.

                                            But, that tale is done. Where else can the story go, without repeating the same story all over again?

                                            I said that SW isn't "deep", but it does borrow from Western and Eastern sources. Ultimately, however, it is a Western tale, and one of the big differences in Western and Eastern philosophies are their take on the role of Evil in Life. Greatly oversimplifying here, but *in general*, the Western idea is that Evil is fundamentally *wrong*, it is a twisted perversion of the natural order that never should have existed, and Good can only win once it destroys Evil utterly (the paradox is that one must often use Evil's methods to do so); Eastern religion/philosophy, otoh, allows for there to be a place for Evil in the natural order of things. It may be unpleasant and undesirable, and it certainly can still be opposed, but at the end of the day, it is Right that it exists for it has a role to play.

                                            In six movies, SW has told the Western version of Good triumphing over Evil. IF the series must continue, then I would expand it into an Eastern direction. The upcoming movies would examine the true nature of both sides of the Force and what exactly it means to have them in "Balance" (think of the Yin-Yang). This would necessitate the revival of the Sith (or at least, some Dark Side users), and their eventually establishing some sort of working relationship with the new Jedi Order Skywalker founds. The easiest way to bring that about would be by introducing a new, third element to threaten both sides...maybe an invasion, like the Yuuzahn Vong, or a cosmic menace, like Abeloth, though obviously not those themselves.

                                            So, the broadest outline: Movie 1, catch up with Luke and introduce his new Jedi who discover a new Sith threat; ends with suggestions that there's something else out there; Movie 2, Jedi and Sith go into all out war on each other, while the new threat is revealed to the audience as manipulating both. Movie 3, Jedi and Sith realize both have been duped, and reluctantly join forces to defeat new threat. This done, both warily call a cease-fire, which hopefully will build into a lasting peace...

                                            (Also, aside from the droids, I would ONLY have Luke back...and he dies at the end of part 1 saving the youngsters. The circle is now complete!)

                                            That's the basic story I would tell, the rest is all details. It may not be the most original or profound idea, but then SW never was, that was never its strength! Of course, I also see that as a Final Ending, and I know Disney is going to want to keep driving this horse until it dies and no one wants to see it anymore, but this is about as far as I care to take it.

                                            Thoughts?

                                                Where would I want to take the story? Away from the Jedi.

                                                One mistake I think that Star Wars made with increasing regularity was that Force users came to be increasingly central to everything, to the point that the galaxy ended up (figuratively) revolving around them. Make the central protagonist of the new Trilogy be a smuggler, or a politician, or a soldier or spy or bounty hunter or any number of other occupations; as long as he or she is not a Jedi, or Sith, or any other sort of Force user. Don’t make him or her strong in the Force; and avoid the temptation to recruit him or her into the ranks of the Jedi as the series progresses. Place all Jedi characters and the like in supporting roles.
