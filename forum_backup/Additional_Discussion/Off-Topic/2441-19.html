                    Originally posted by Second Chances View Post
                    The thing that gets me when I go back and watch Naruto is the sexism. I just can't unsee it and while I can overlook a lot in the name of mindless entertainment, I can't get past that
                    Yeah, sadly that's an issue with Shounen series in general. For the most part in Shounen series, female characters are just eye candy, a love interest or only there to assist the male characters. When you look at most Shonen series (Naruto, One Piece, Bleach, Hunter x Hunter, Kenshin, etc) you find this pattern in the treatment of female characters over and over again.

                    Of course, there are some exceptions, like in One Piece we had Big Mom who is one of the most powerful people in the world. Or Fairy Tail which at least had Erza as the token badass female and who was probably the strongest and most interesting person in the series.

                    But Shounen series like Medaka Box, with a number of very strong, very important female characters who are well fleshed out in their motivations, personalities and receive as much screen time as the male characters are incredibly rare. Honestly I can't think of many others off-hand - Binbougami Ga, maybe Soul Eater, that's honestly about it.

                    Sadly if you're looking for well written female characters you're just not really going to find them in most Shounen series.

                                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                                Was Naruto plotted to be an ongoing story telling tales in the life of Naruto, or with a mostly fleshed out beginning, middle and end?
                                An ongoing story. Pretty much anything published in Shonen Jump magazine has to be an ongoing story, as they do weekly serials that continue based on popularity. So as long as your series keeps getting votes, you need to keep writing chapters. It’s actually a huge problem in the manga industry that mangaka are pressured to produce content at an absurd schedule with no definite end point. And it’s why all the best manga go on way too long and end up a mess, because the author is pretty much just winging it week to week and getting no downtime.

                                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                I heard a rumor years ago that Naruto was originally supposed to be a story about wizards and that Sasuke wasn't even planned to be a character. But the creator later changed it to ninjas and added in a rival character for Naruto because his editors gave him the Toriyama treatment or something.
                                Sort of. You’re actually conflating two different stories. The original concept that eventually became Naruto was a cooking manga about a boy who worked at a ramen shop. Pretty much everything about that concept ended up getting scrapped except the main character’s name and brash personality. But that’s why Naruto is obsessed with ramen.

                                He also had a separate concept for a manga about a boy (who also had a similar design and personality to Naruto) who went to a school for wizards. But then Harry Potter came out and he (or maybe his editors) decided not to go forward with that idea because it would likely be viewed as imitating Harry Potter.

                                The Toriyama treatment is pretty much industry standard, especially for shonen jump and shojo beat manga.

                                            04-29-2018, 03:40 PM
                                            The artists have partial control over the IP, but only for the manga. If it gets an anime or video game adaptation the publisher generally controls that part, which can make things pretty tricky. Manga artists sometimes have mixed feelings about adaptations. There are also some horror stories that have arisen due to this system.

                                            For example, the series Polar Bear's Cafe. One evening the manga author was watching TV, flipped the channel and saw that her manga had been turned into an anime, and then saw that this was episode 20. Nobody had told her, not the editor, the publisher or anyone else that they had greenlit and produced an anime of her manga, let alone that they had already produced 20 episodes. Needless to say she was incredibly upset, and there was a lot of blowback from the community (even those who had never heard of the series) who were angry at how the author had been completely left out of something so important.

                                            Another series, Negima, was basically cancelled after the publisher openly threatened/bragged aboit taking all the rights away from the manga artist.

                                            JUMP tends to treat their authors fairly well, all things considered and compared to other publishers but there are a lot of huge problems with the manga industry.
