                    I feel like it.

                    One of the things about my depression is that facts which should carry with them absolutely no emotional weight can become horrible. For instance, on satellite radio there is a station called Pop2K, which only plays pop music from the years 2000-2009. Whenever they play a song on that station, there's a two-digit number in parentheses behind the song's title to indicate the year it came from, presumably because they think people will change the station if they are not given textual assurance that Nickelback's "Photographs" debuted in 2005. That Pop2K has this policy should have no impact on anyone at all except possibly as fodder for dumb jokes like the previous one, but when I get depressed, that fact casts a sinister, grasping shadow in my mind, like a vampire's profile cast in moonlight through the terrace windows. That the station would document and broadcast such information is sad for reasons I can't explain.

                    Don't even get me started on the time I had to leave Walmart because I saw they had double-tall cans of Mountain Dew with a hunter camouflage print on the can and large red letters along the top near the rim explaining that the can is camouflaged. That one was a bad one.

                    When a thing that shouldn't matter matters, that in and of itself hurts. It hurts worse that I can generally tell when something oughtn't to matter and I judge myself for it, hating myself for being so weak as to let pointless things prick me. I wouldn't judge any of you if you feel the same way; if it was any of you, I'd make sympathetic noises and say, "You have an injury in your brain, and what's going on up there is no more your fault than a stroke victim being unable to lift her left arm after the connections have gone down," but when it's me, it's unacceptable. That should tell you everything you need to know about my ability to keep a sense of perspective.
