    Actually, you know what, I do have another thing to say.

    Dialogue. Holy shit, the dialogue.

    RWBY has never had terrible dialogue, but I can't accuse it of always being...interesting. A lot of the time, things were being said, and they were good to know, but that was about it. Best Day Ever has fantastic dialogue, with great point-counter point timing and characteristic styling. This shines again really nicely with the scene before the fight, because the humor runs quick and steady, but the villain scenes are so much fun to watch because the dialogue is just so on point with it's wording and it's delivery. It's fun to listen to even the more mundane parts of the episode(not it has much in the way of that). So, yeah, holy shit that dialogue.

    Also, while I still don't have a lot ot say, I really like how the thing with the White Fang from the last episode is weighing on Blake's mind. I really just want to give the poor girl a hug.

                    So it's come to my attention that the Exalted Forums have an extensive RWBY Forum over there. To that, I have to say: Shame on you. I thought when they shunted both the old RWBY and the MLP threads over into here the lesson was learned.

                    And no, I'm not just bitter because they have way more conversation going on than we do. Even though I am.

                    So, RWBY Volume 2, Episode 2: Welcome to Beacon.

                    Hey, look! The plot is both thickening and moving!

                    It also makes zero sense at this point. But let's get technicals out of the way first.

                    So the animation has gone back into more humor filled territory-er, more of the standard material, rather than a comedic fight of ages, as it were- and....I'm not sure why it works, but it works. THe usual issues with a lack of stretch and squash and handling of snap didn't come in here, and I think a large part of that came from how they decided to handle it. The scene with Weiss in particular felt like an embrace of the medium's issues, and when it's used as an effect rather than a byproduct, it...works. I feel really weird saying that. I'm not saying i's great, but it does work.

                    I don't have much to say on the audio, so....characters.

                    Before individuals, I feel like I must say that things feels more like the result of relationships rather than individuals now, which makes going through the large list of characters already here a bit of a struggle. It's an improvement in general from last volume-not because the characterization was bad there, but because this is much more layered and real. THe verisimilitude is here as peanut butter mortar, is what I'm saying.

                    Ozpin has been a favorite for a while now, and largely remains so here. He's been given a little bit more to work with, and the man is starting to be fleshed out. I know some key moments in his career are sure toe come up, but what I love about him is that so far he hasn't needed those pin points-it's enough to say that he has led many, seen many die, and killed much in his life. It's been great to see him walk with that and to talk with that, but now we see how he responds to it, and interestingly enough, he comes off as a competent Cornelius Fudge from Harry Potter. He just wants peace, and while he's not closing his eyes to it, he's not about to start riling people up over it yet because he wants peace, and he wants people to enjoy peace. He's not wrong....but he might not be right either. It's a nifty twist.

                    Glinda's role as the hardass comes more and more into perspective with this context. Ozpin and Ironwood(more on him in a second) are both fairly thoughtful men, and even though Ironwood is more active, I never got the impression he hadn't carefully gauged his options-but Glinda exists in context of Ozpin, so let's get back to that. Where Ozpin debates and struggles, Glinda has to be the one who draws lines. She does so at Ozpin's behest, but I think her being a hardass and turning the path he takes into iron edicts is just as much for his sake as it is a sign for her loyalty. Ozpin is mutable in his own way, and he needs someone who at least looks like iron to back him, and Glinda does that. She doesn't waver anywhere, because he's always got an eye somewhere, and if he saw her not be the strong pillar, it might allow his more questioning nature lead to inaction and/or reversal of action.

                    Ironwood strikes me as a middle of the road to these two. A man of sorrows like Ozpin, he also thinks long and hard about what to do, and probably doesn't see any clear answers either. Lacking a Glinda in his life though, he's had to become the one who makes the push in his life-he's had be his own iron when he plays more like Ozpin's wet clay. People like that often feel pushed into decisions because of the need to do something, and they often make bad choices because of the sense of haste. It doesn't help he's got some serious positions. He may be an academy leader, but he's also openly a general-unlike Ozpin who chose to be a clay shaper, Ironwood is a metalshaper, he has to force things into place hard. THe conclusion he's made feels organic to what I think is his nature. He's not making the right choice either, but it's not wrong either. Both he and Ozpin are going to have their choices bite them in the ass in their own ways-to be fair, it's not a situation that has an answer that doesn't bite.

                    The entire Risk board game scene was cute, and fun, and I love seeing RWBY have fun as a group...the problem being that it's not, but more on that in a second. For now, Ruby and Yang's sisterly dynamics are awesome to watch and, again, true to life. Weiss's involvment with the game is also familiar to me, as I've been there on several games of Magic the Gathering, and seeing two people who know go at it while the third one ambitiously tries to win but has no clue what they're doing is just true to life and adorable to boot. JNPR didn't do much, but their contributions were funny all the same. By the way, shout out to X-Ray and Vav, who will be getting their own series later on, and a few other jokes I can't remember right now.

                    So remember how I was going on about how Jaune never made himself appealing in the last scene, and thus Pyrrha's interest in him couldn't be romantic? While I conceeded that it might have been romantic, I still stand by that, and Neptune(the real reason I bring this up) is the reason why. I grant that he was not amazingly charming, but he was smoother and more respectable, so when he goes "snow angel" to Weiss and she doesn't go nutso, I buy it. I don't think she's taken with him(although that may change and build), but she's not offended by him either, and at least finds it minorly endearing. I'm not sold that he's as smooth and suave as I think the show wants me to believe, but the man has earned what he's put out, and I wouldn't be surprised if he came to be more so. Do I think he's up to something? No, I think he's just a player, but I think he'll be fun to watch.

                    More Sun. Now. I find it particularly cute how he's trying to warm up to Blake, and that's that.

                    So, onto Team RWBY proper. Gotta start with Blake.

                    Oh Blake. Oh Blake.

                    Once again, it's great that out of everyone, she's the one carrying the baggage from the last season, because of course she does-she thinks wider than everyone else and sits on things a lot more(With the exception of maybe Weiss, but she at least keeps going with something while brooding), and I love how her instincts are still to run and hide. She doesn't trust, well, anyone-Ozpin's interview shows that much. I like that she doesn't share anything with him for a lot of reasons.

                    That said, you stupid, stupid person.

                    I don't say that as a slam on her, it makes sense-but this is really a situation where showing your cards helps. I get why, but it is still mildly frustrating that she does it She needed to let out that explosion of concern, if nothing else. Passing on her htughts and knowledge would have been a big help. I'm also a little appalled at her arrogance-maybe no one knows the White Fang like she does, but that's crass to assume Ozpin doesn't know a hefty helping. More to the point, if people don't know, tell them!

                    Thankfully, Weiss is here to call bullshit.

                    I seem to recall bitching a tong about Weiss last volume. I really wanted to like her, and her intro made that really hard. Still, she is probably my second favorite character, and it's for stuff like this that has been creeping out over time-in fact, now it's in full bloom. I'm so glad it was Weiss who called Blake out, particularly given their last conversation. Weiss has her bag of issues, but she's self-aware in a way that Yang and Ruby just aren't(although not as much as Blake, Weiss clearly shows blindspots) and really just aware in a way many of them aren't. She cuts to the point, with others and herself, and she makes her points solidly(With a great chair balancing act to boot).

                    I also kind of like how she's the cautious one(HOORAY FOR TAKING RESPONSIBILITY AS A MAGE!). She's book smart in ways where the others are street smart, and while her education was not gentle in the way book-smart normally works, her objectivity on most of the world is still from the viewpoint of a glass tower. She is calculated without a great sense of what really happens in the situations those calculations are based on. Where Blake feels the concern and Ruby and Yang feel the drive to do something about it, Weiss reins them in a bit and keeps them grounded. Her role as second lead to RWBY is coming in nicely, and I like that, even if it is a problem in this scenario.

                    Speaking of Ruby, this is her story, right? Yeah, it is. Where Blake worried about a problem bigger than her and Weiss enforced that, Ruby wisely said "Screw that, we know, therefore we do"(not an actual quote). They might not be able to do much, but they can do something.

                    Which doesn't include talking to Ozpin, but we'll let that slide for now.

                    Again, Ruby is taking charge, and it's cool the see that. The team really is coming together, and I'm curious how it'll go.

                    Yang. Remember last volume how I said with Yang "What you see is what you get"? I'm getting more of the same here. Yang isn't showing more or growing like the others are, and I still can't decide if that's a good thing or a bad thing. We'll see. Otherwise? Hilarious as always.

                    And that leaves me with the stinger.

                    ...

                    ...

                    ...No.

                    Not for a second.

                    Don't buy it.

                    Here's the thing-I don't believe you could ever pass off Cinder as a student. Maybe Mercury and Emerald? But not Cinder. Cinder's just too...mature.

                    I know how that sounds, shut up. What I mean is that, while we are dealing with young adults, the characters are still children in their way. Cinder isn't. Cinder so isn't. Anyone looking with eyes can see that-anyone with a brain knows that. This entire charade doesn't work in my head.

                    And in fact, I think it's crazy to think that Qrow, Ozpin, and Ironwood can talk about their enemies(Queens has pawns, anyone) without knowing what they look like! Or at the least, what Cinder looks like!

                    As for the plot, well...I don't like where it's saying it's going, but I'll let it slide. Rant rant, bitch bitch later if I'm vindicated.

                    All in all though, good episode, I loved it.

                                            So, RWBY Volume 2 Episode 3: A Little Hiccup.

                                            In which the little hiccup is never addressed or explained, but I think Akatsuki has it right.

                                            So since the animation, soundtrack, sound effects, and voice acting are all staying solid save for some of the weird clothes issues RWBY has always had, I'm gonna take a some time to discuss RWBY's time format and aesthetic. And by aesthetic, I mean I wanna squee and talk about the clothing, but more on that in a second.

                                            RWBY's episodes in Volume 1 were, well, irritating in their breaks. We spent weeks getting half an episode, leading to a certain clunkiness that left episodes feeling unsatisfying. Putting them together didn't really prove to be satisfying(10 episodes of roughly 12 minutes, by the way), because they still felt like they were designed to handle their short presentations on the internet. Add in some stock storytelling and it was always a little frustrating.

                                            That just makes Volume 2 even more impressive though. Each episode has been a neat balance of dense and loose. The series breathes and takes it's time as needed, but is interested in getting on quickly with that breathing pace. I feel like there's a lot of nifty character bits thrown in here that builds on each other, and since the characters are finally being realized as full and fun characters, that's rewarding as hell. The plot is going, but it's not eager to run, but given that it's so neat ot hang out with these people, it's refreshing. In short, I feel like Monty, Kerry, and Miles have really learned how to manage their time, and it has paid off.

                                            Now onto the pretty clothes. *deep breath* SQUEEEEEEEE!

                                            Let me get one thing out of the way, right now: You can buy Weiss's coat, so if you got a girlfriend you're trying to get into cosplay, try bribing her with this. I legitimately think it's a cute coat, so might not even need the precedent. And I wouldn't be surprised if you could find all of the other styles for sale on DHGate.

                                            If I'm really honest, I like these better than the normal outfits. Weiss's original stuff has an style I rather lie that makes me feel more neutral in regards to her, but I like how modern these are, and how more normal they seem. I realize unusual attire is part of the anime schtick and for RWBY, but seeing Blake in an outfit I legitimately thought she would wear brought so much joy to me I had a little mini-cheer. I think these are very flattering and very cool designs. I also have to agree with Akatsuki in regards to the variety factor. Anime studio budgets usually limit them to one or two designs, which is part of the reason the high school thing is as popular as it is-because one design fits all. For all of that though, we see so much of it it that we never see people realistically, well, live outside the clothes. I feel like seeing more clothing styles breathes life into the world and the characters. And if nothing else, I really do like these outfits a lot better.

                                            And no, I am not lusting after under-aged animated flesh, I really like the clothes that much.

                                            So, with that said. The events and the highlighted characters.

                                            I loved the opening classroom scene, of course. I rather like that Professor Port is like an openly mocked Gilderoy Lockhart character who's lessons are literally just self-aggrandizing stories that hide discussion of Grim creatures weaknesses and tactics against them, and I like a subtle touch of character growth on Jaune's part of trying to pick up Weiss-Old Jaune might have continued trying to overblow himself, here he tries to just appeal with activities. It's a small touch that shows that he's grown up and calmed down in some ways. One day kid, one day.

                                            The scene with the plan discussion(AWESOME CLOTHINGS SQUEEEEEEEEEEEE!) is actually fairly solid and interesting. It places Yang's trailer as squarely happening before the series(I know Monty's mentioned they all happened prior to the series, but I never believed it really), and builds on that as giving each of them solid leads to go and poke in for information. And that's another thing, these are really good ideas that these characters can take advantage of with their established backgrounds. THe new clothes set the tone for confronting these situations as well.

                                            Sun and Neptune continue to be awesome as hell. These two alone really make the sort of cool kids club you wish you could be a part of when you were in school. Neptune's balance of cool factor with legitimate, humanizing concern over his circumstances is just great. In fact, that's something you don't get a lot of with media-believably cool people. When you come across the cool kids in media, it's often so because the show tells you they're cool, but Sun and Neptune legitimately have earned it. The fact Neptune has done so with so little screen presence astounds me. Weiss is totally within her right to want to spend time with this guy. On that front, holy shit, they showed that right as well-humorous but not overblown, both feeling true to life and completely hilarious.

                                            The initial scene was, I think, a set up for future elements of the story-the Schnee's communications and transportation networks feel important in a Wild West sort of sense in that a shit ton of infrastructure seems to have been built on it. I also liked how Weiss's and Ruby's friendship has shaped out-it seems like they annoy each other, but that's part and parcel for the game, and Ruby has come to, in a way, appreciate Weiss's knowledge, and Weiss Ruby's enthusiasm.

                                            And then there was Penny, and Penny was a revelation.

                                            I didn't like Penny in Volume 1. I didn't get why people liked her so much. It boggled me. But then I saw her here and her initial claims of not knowing Ruby, and I was instantly "Oh God no". And the way the characterized her from there was still stylistically that "Main Street, USA from Disneyland presents an AI girl", but much more sympathetic. I'm not sure if the setup she had helped or not, but I found I could invest and care for Penny where before I didn't care.

                                            The entire scene with Weiss was to be expected, but damn did I love the delivery. I loved that we spent time with Weiss trying to reconcile with the call and how much she wanted to avoid her family. I like how much of it was strictly in her face, and that when it came to the call, it fit with the masks we've seen Weiss wear before-and now we know they're masks. I'm curious about her relationship with Winter.

                                            The subsequent scene with Ruby and Penny and the chase had a lot of great little moments, from Penny going through the the group as she listed who she wasn't allowed to contact, to casting some doubt on the relationships she has(she answers to her father and Ironwood, and that raises some questions in my head), to her stopping that truck, and Ruby's confusion and desperation, the entire thing played out like a charm.

                                            All in all, I really liked this episode, and this convinces me that Volume 2 is going to be spectaular, which is what I always wanted from RWBY.

                                            Sometimes it's okay to get what you wished for.
