                        I think what you're referring to is what happens if the reactors get shut down (which normally drastically reduces risks during disasters and so on). Normally, nuclear reactors power their own cooling systems, but if you shut one down, they still need cooling. So you need something else powering the process to cool them.

                        What I've read from experts that have discussed it, is that they're worried about Russian attacks on the rest of the Ukraine's infrastructure combined with their refusal to honor humanitarian and disaster corridors to allow non-fighters to deal with these things in the middle of a war zone. Basically, modern reactors are designed to endure acute disasters and incidents, that only last a day or two before emergency crews can restore certain safety features. They're not designed for a siege where their emergency diesel generators for their cooling systems can't get refueled on a regular basis after the reactor is shut down and the plant can't access the main grid for that power.

                        The concern around staff is that understandably Ukrainians don't want to go to work in a war zone. In the places Russia has taken over the nuclear facilities already, they're reported to be forcing the Ukrainian staff to continue to work instead of trying to replace them with Russians (which probably isn't too hard to do in the short term considering the consequences, but I'm sure those Ukrainians are worried that it's just a matter of time before the Russians are familiar enough with the reactors to run them themselves). So that's even more worries that the staff in other facilities are going to flee rather than stay and operate the reactors as safely as possible.
