                Working dogs are not supposed to be showered with affection when "on the clock". This results in things like missed cues, which can mean that either some substance doesn't get caught or an important cue that a service animal was supposed to pick up on (like the prelude to a seizure) gets through the cracks. Seeing-eye dogs are perhaps the most direct demonstration of this principle, because they're trained to be a blind human's eyes, which is a full time job.

                And, of course, all animals have some sense of personal space and territory. Even dogs, frozen in wolf neoteny as they are, aren't quite permanent puppies in truth, and if they have an established sense of territory, the number one rule is Beware of Dog.

                No, K-9 units (both civilian and military) tend to use dogs for their sharp senses and capability to find something of import via training. Humans tend to frown on training attack dogs because the dogs in question end up kind of in the same boat that military personnel do, with a lot of PTSD and the capacity to turn off whatever typical behavioral restrictions keep them from performing violence. Keep in mind that social animals have a weird relationship with violence, and a lot of them were sculpted by selection pressures to have a lot of ways to avoid using it among their own.

                        The fact that we have to put signs on working dogs so people don't go up and screw with them is the problem. People's presumption that all dogs are their friends and they have the right to all dog's physical space is the biggest cause of dog behavioral problems, and that leads to most dog bites, and thus most dogs that get put down for no other reason than humans giving them impossible rules to follow.

                        Consider what it's like to work with rescue animals. If you never have, maybe you should. All of that "dogs love me" energy you have? That gets you bit by dogs in the middle of massive traumatic problems. You can have the most positive energy ever. You're not a piece of shit. You're not haunted by an evil spirit. You're trying to help a dog that's been betrayed by the people that were supposed to treat it's love with healthy love in response. It needs patience. It needs calm. It needs low energy situations were trust can be rebuilt because you don't assume anything about your right to be near them and wait for them to let you in so they learn you will listen where others didn't.

                        I have had dogs in my personal life get court ordered death sentences because the simple rule of ask the humans with the dog first wasn't followed. I've worked shelters with owners killing themselves with stress because they will not put animals down, but finding potential owners that are willing to overcome our stupid stereotypes about breeds and actually engage with the dogs as individuals is so damned hard.

                        I'm going to say this again, because this is really serious to me:

                        If you go around saying any dog is there for you to force your desire for physical contact on - not matter how much flowery language about love and good tempers you put around it - you are actively putting dogs in danger. Dogs, innocent loving dogs breed to imprint on us, are killed by us all the time because these stereotypes are bad; even if you say them as positively as possible.

                        You want to know why we kill dogs for our own mistakes? The same reason you feel comfortable walking up to a dog you don't know and putting your hands all over them. In both cases, if you did that to a human, you'd be evil. If you do it to a dog, you're asserting that humans are the masters to the dogs. Humans are social creatures. We put human rights over other animals' rights. We didn't spend thousands of years breeding dogs to be our equals. So when humans do things that push dogs into misbehaving, the dogs suffer for being less than us; and the only thing we can do to protect dogs is to make preach to as many as possible the need to be responsible for the animals we've bred to be the way they are. Because we're going to - as a species - always view dogs as lesser. But we can choose to mean that we own them responsible treatment as the ones in charge over choosing to mean that we get to do whatever we want to them.
