                                                Having lived on a kibbutz for a bit (an Israeli socialist commune), I can talk a bit about what it's actually like living in a "pure" socialist community (there's no such thing since you still end up interacting with non-socialist communities).

                                                And for the most part? Life wasn't really much different. I got a weekly sum of money as my regular extra expenses money... so a paycheck. Smaller than I'd get doing the same work normally, but for good reasons: no local taxes, no water bill, no sewage bill, no trash bills. All that stuff was factored in. I could eat three meals a day from the communal cafeteria without having to pay anything. I lived in the equivalent of an apartment with my rent already covered as well. So, my routine was: wake up, go eat breakfast at the cafeteria, work, eat lunch at the cafeteria (sometimes we'd arrange for bagged lunches if we wanted to stay out in the field rather than coming in), work until work was done, generally have time to shower and hang out since we usually finished around 4 PM, run to the kibbutz store to spend some of my money in various ways (treats, ordering stuff the store didn't normally carry, rent movies/etc. since this was way before streaming) eat dinner at the cafeteria, relax, sleep, repeat.

                                                Kinda normal life really. Less mass produced entertainment, but we still had a well stocked library, radio, TV, and computers, but those were more shared (but that was more generally true at the time). Lots of people used their free time for art... we had multiple clubs for different types of artists/writers/etc. People put on plays and musicals; some locally written too.

                                                If there was a special family occasion we'd gather at one of the houses (those were saved for married couples with children), use our money to get nicer things to cook than the cafeteria food and buy gifts or whatever the event was about, and celebrate. Us teenagers would grab a bus to the nearest city (~20 minutes), go clubbing, see movies, go shopping with whatever cash we still had, and generally be teenagers before heading back.

                                                I didn't really feel like I was missing out having the cafeteria vs. having a dozen fast food places to pick from... and the cafeteria was better food than that. I kinda missed the variety of entertainment, but I had a budget for my hobbies and I didn't have to worry about bills. Nobody was getting rich... but nobody was going poor either. I didn't live there long enough to quite get past the idea that promotions and raises and fancy cars (we had a shared motor pool rather than bothering to own cars), but I never really felt like I was missing something just because if I stayed longer I wouldn't have been making more money working the same job but with seniority.
