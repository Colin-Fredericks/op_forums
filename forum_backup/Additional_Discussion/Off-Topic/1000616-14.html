                                Also, the electors don't "change" their votes. They refuse to vote for who the people voted for (which is actually their constitutional right if they feel the need); they're not actually supposed to decide ahead of time (though nothing legally stops them from it and some states mandate that the decision is made for them anyway).

                                Though Hillary doesn't need to get to 270, she still needs the majority of the counted votes (which is why Florida essentially abstaining by doing anything but voting for Trump works, it reduces the number of elector votes from 538 to 509 and thus the needed number to win to 255). It's not at all likely, but it remains a legal possibility as well as an action that actually reflects the desires of the authors of the EC; that it act a a check against exactly this sort of popular wave upsetting the system they designed.

                                Though Charlaquin is off a bit in that if none of the candidates have a majority of the cast ballots it doesn't go to the people, it goes to the House for President and Senate for Vice President (and they could opt to split the ticket as well, which is a whole different set of strangeness).

                                                            Originally posted by haren View Post
                                                            Edit: The Founding Fathers were elitist assholes who didn't want the general population to screw things up, and therefore made the electoral college and such so they could keep the power where they felt it belonged.
                                                            The creation of the Electoral College was more complicated than that.

                                                            First, keep in mind that most of them were incredibly weary of a strong federal government, which they were afraid would quickly take control of the country at the expense of the individual states and colonies. That's why their first attempt at a government, via the Articles of Confederation, created a federal government so weak that it was actually incapable of doing much of anything. The Constitution was written in reaction to the weakness of the original federal government, but the authors still took great pains to keep the federal government from being so strong it would be able to usurp states rights.

                                                            Part of the idea with the Electoral College was that less populated states would still have a stronger say in the election of the president - that the president wouldn't only need to have to win the votes in 3 or 4 most populated states states and then get to ignore all the rest. It was a way to ensure that individual states retained a voice in the presidential election and, in fact, when one looks at elections such as this where one candidate wins the popular vote but loses the election because they weren't able to convince enough people in enough states to vote for them, that is the Electoral College performing exactly as it was intended to.

                                                            Of course, there were also other ideas wrapped up in the Electoral College, like the worry that Americans might elect a demagogue - many of the framers of the Constitution were all very aware of the kind of mortal damage that demagogues such as Alcibiades or Cleon inflicted on ancient Athens and the Electoral College was one method by which they hoped would that sort of event would be prevented from happening in America.

                                                            But the idea that the Electoral College was just created to fuck over the common man isn't really very accurate. There were a lot of reasons that it helped bring about its inception and dicking over the common man only had a tiny part to do with it.
